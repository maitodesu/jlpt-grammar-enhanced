# いちばん

**Meaning** · the most; the best; number one

Japanese adjectives have no `-est` ending. `いちばん` supplies it: put this one
word in front of an adjective and it becomes a superlative. Literally "number
one", and it is written either as `いちばん` or with the kanji
一番(いちばん).

## Usage

`いちばん` is an adverb, so it goes **directly in front of the word it makes
superlative** - an adjective, a な-adjective, or an adverb - and nothing comes
between them.

- Before an い-adjective: いちばん高(たか)い, いちばん新(あたら)しい
- Before a な-adjective: いちばん静(しず)か（な）, いちばん便利(べんり)（な）
- Before an adverb: いちばん早(はや)く来(き)ました, "arrived earliest"
- Before <code>好(す)き</code>, which behaves as a な-adjective:
  いちばん好(す)きな色(いろ)

It can also stand alone as a noun meaning "the top one" - 
日本(にほん)で一番(いちばん)になりたい, "I want to be number one in Japan" - 
and it feeds the ordinal 一番目(いちばんめ), "the first".

The set of things being compared is usually left unsaid and understood from
context. When you do want to state it explicitly, Japanese wraps `いちばん` in the
frame <code>～の中(なか)で～がいちばん…</code>, which has its own page.

## Examples

<pre>
この店(みせ)のケーキが<b>いちばん</b>おいしいです。
The cake at this shop is the best.
</pre>

<pre>
私(わたし)は夏(なつ)が<b>いちばん</b>好(す)きです。
Summer is my favourite season.
</pre>

<pre>
駅(えき)までは、この道(みち)が<b>いちばん</b>近(ちか)いですよ。
This road is the shortest way to the station.
</pre>

<pre>
試験(しけん)の前(まえ)は、よく寝(ね)るのが<b>いちばん</b>大切(たいせつ)だと思(おも)います。
Before an exam, I think sleeping well is the most important thing of all.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and extremely frequent in ordinary speech. Nothing about it
marks age, gender or formality.

In kanji it is 一番(いちばん), and both spellings are normal; textbooks and
signage tend to use the kanji, casual writing the kana. The reading never changes.

The formal written equivalent is <code>最(もっと)も</code>. A newspaper writes
最(もっと)も高(たか)い山(やま); a person talking to you says
いちばん高(たか)い山(やま). Using <code>最(もっと)も</code> in conversation sounds
like you are reading from a report.

Do not confuse `いちばん` with `もっと`. `もっと`
means "more" - a comparative, not a superlative - so
もっと安(やす)い店(みせ) is "a cheaper shop" while
いちばん安(やす)い店(みせ) is "the cheapest shop". The two are one syllable
apart and constantly mixed up.

Spoken Japanese also uses `いちばん` loosely as a rough intensifier of preference - 
「やっぱりこれがいちばん」, "this really is the best" - where no real ranking has
been carried out. That usage is casual and warm, and perfectly standard.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この山(やま)は高(たか)いいちばんです。 → ✅ この山(やま)が<b>いちばん高(たか)い</b>です。
  `いちばん` goes *in front of* the adjective, never after it. Word order carries
  the whole meaning here.

- ❌ 犬(いぬ)より<b>いちばん</b>好(す)きです。 → ✅ 犬(いぬ)より猫(ねこ)の方(ほう)が好(す)きです。
  `より` compares exactly two things, and a superlative needs three or more. The
  two patterns cannot be combined in one sentence.

- ❌ いちばん<b>の</b>好(す)きな食(た)べ物(もの) → ✅ <b>いちばん好(す)きな</b>食(た)べ物(もの)
  No `の` between `いちばん` and the adjective. `いちばん` is an adverb here, not a
  noun.

- ❌ この部屋(へや)がいちばん静(しず)かなです。 → ✅ この部屋(へや)が<b>いちばん静(しず)か</b>です。
  The `な` of a な-adjective appears only before a noun. `いちばん` changes nothing
  about how the adjective itself is conjugated.

## Practice

**1.** クラスで山田(やまだ)さんが____足(あし)が速(はや)いです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

クラスで山田(やまだ)さんが<b>いちばん</b>足(あし)が速(はや)いです。

`いちばん` marks the top of the group. It sits in front of the part being ranked,
and the person holding that rank is marked with `が`, not `は`.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: この辞書(じしょ)はいちばんの便利(べんり)です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この辞書(じしょ)が<b>いちばん便利(べんり)</b>です。

Delete the `の`: `いちばん` attaches straight onto the adjective. Changing `は` to
`が` is also natural, because picking out the top item of a set is exactly the job
`が` does.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) もっと大(おお)きいかばんがほしいです。
(b) いちばん大(おお)きいかばんがほしいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a comparative: the speaker has seen a bag and wants a bigger one than
that. No ranking of the whole shop is implied.

(b) is a superlative: out of everything available, the speaker wants the single
biggest one. `もっと` moves you one step up the scale; `いちばん` takes you to the
top of it.

</details>

# ～のなかで～がいちばん

**Meaning** · of all ～, ～ is the most ～ - naming the top of a stated group

This is the frame that makes a superlative explicit. The first slot names the
field of comparison, the second names the winner. Japanese marks that winner with
`が`, never `は`, because the sentence exists precisely to single one item out.

## Formation

Two halves that go together: <code>～の中(なか)で</code> sets the field,
<code>～がいちばん + adjective</code> announces the winner.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Group noun | Noun + の中(なか)で | クラス → クラスの中(なか)で |
| Category noun | Noun + の中(なか)で | スポーツ → スポーツの中(なか)で |
| Listed items | A と B と C + の中(なか)で | 赤(あか)と青(あお)と黒(くろ)の中(なか)で |
| Place or period | Noun + で（no 中(なか)） | 世界(せかい)で／一年(いちねん)で |
| Winner | Noun + が + いちばん + adjective | 夏(なつ)がいちばん暑(あつ)いです |
| Question (people) | だれが いちばん …か | だれがいちばん強(つよ)いですか |
| Question (closed set) | どれが いちばん …か | どれがいちばん安(やす)いですか |
| Question (place / time) | どこが／いつが いちばん …か | どこがいちばん寒(さむ)いですか |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the winner takes `が`. Using `は` there
（❌夏(なつ)はいちばん暑(あつ)いです）turns the sentence into a topic
statement and loses the "out of all of them" force. `が` selects; `は` merely
comments.

</div>

The question word matches the field: <b>だれ</b> for people, <b>どれ</b> for a closed
set of listed things, <b>なに</b> for an open category, <b>どこ</b> for places, <b>いつ</b>
for times.

## Examples

<pre>
果物(くだもの)<b>の中(なか)で</b>、りんご<b>がいちばん</b>好(す)きです。
Of all fruit, I like apples best.
</pre>

<pre>
クラス<b>の中(なか)で</b>、林(はやし)さん<b>がいちばん</b>背(せ)が高(たか)いです。
Hayashi is the tallest in the class.
</pre>

<pre>
家族(かぞく)<b>の中(なか)で</b>、だれ<b>がいちばん</b>早(はや)く起(お)きますか。
Who gets up earliest in your family?
</pre>

<pre>
一年(いちねん)<b>で</b>、八月(はちがつ)<b>がいちばん</b>暑(あつ)いです。
August is the hottest month of the year.
</pre>

<pre>
この三(みっ)つ<b>の中(なか)で</b>、どれ<b>がいちばん</b>軽(かる)いですか。
Which of these three is the lightest?
</pre>

<pre>
兄弟(きょうだい)<b>の中(なか)で</b>、妹(いもうと)<b>がいちばん</b>ピアノが上手(じょうず)です。
Of us siblings, my little sister is the best at the piano.
</pre>

<pre>
この店(みせ)のメニュー<b>の中(なか)で</b>、この定食(ていしょく)<b>がいちばん</b>人気(にんき)があります。
Of everything on this restaurant's menu, this set meal is the most popular.
</pre>

<pre>
今(いま)まで見(み)た映画(えいが)<b>の中(なか)で</b>、あの作品(さくひん)<b>がいちばん</b>おもしろかったです。
Of all the films I've seen, that one was the most interesting.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral. The whole frame works unchanged in plain speech - just drop
`です` - and it is one of the first things a learner is asked in conversation
practice: 日本(にほん)の食(た)べ物(もの)の中(なか)で、何(なに)がいちばん好(す)きですか。

The <code>中(なか)</code> is often left out when the field is a **place or a period of
time**, because those are already containers:
世界(せかい)でいちばん高(たか)い山(やま), 一年(いちねん)でいちばん寒(さむ)い日(ひ),
クラスでいちばん元気(げんき)な人(ひと). Saying
世界(せかい)の中(なか)で is not wrong, but it is heavier than a native
speaker would usually bother with. With a category noun - fruit, sports, films - 
the <code>の中(なか)で</code> is the normal choice.

In writing you will see <code>最(もっと)も</code> in place of `いちばん`, and
<code>～のうちで</code> in place of <code>～の中(なか)で</code>. Both are stiffer. Neither
changes the grammar.

Answering such a question, you normally repeat the frame only if it is not
already obvious. Asked
季節(きせつ)の中(なか)でいつがいちばん好(す)きですか, you answer simply
秋(あき)がいちばん好(す)きです - or just 秋(あき)です.

## Contrast

### ～の中(なか)で～がいちばん vs ～より～のほうが

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - count the candidates. Two → `より`. Three or more → `いちばん`.

</div>

<pre>
スポーツ<b>の中(なか)で</b>、サッカー<b>がいちばん</b>好(す)きです。
Of all sports, I like football best.
</pre>

<pre>
サッカーより野球(やきゅう)の方(ほう)が好(す)きです。
I like baseball more than football.
</pre>

Mixing the two produces sentences that are simply broken:
❌ サッカーよりいちばん好(す)きです. If a `より` phrase is present, the
comparison is between two named things and there is no top of any ranking to
point at.

### が vs は in the winner slot

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - this pattern answers "which one?", and answers to "which
one?" always take `が`.

</div>

<pre>
四人(よにん)<b>の中(なか)で</b>、田中(たなか)さん<b>がいちばん</b>若(わか)いです。
Tanaka is the youngest of the four.
</pre>

Replace `が` with `は` and the sentence stops selecting: it would be read as "as
for Tanaka, he is the youngest", which is a comment about Tanaka rather than the
result of comparing four people. The same logic governs the question - the
question word `だれ` cannot take `は` at all.

`は` does appear one step earlier in the sentence, on the field, when the field is
being contrasted: 日本(にほん)の山(やま)の中(なか)では…, "in Japan at
least, …".

### の中(なか)で vs で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with a **group**, keep <code>の中(なか)で</code>. With a **place
or a stretch of time**, the plain `で` is enough.

</div>

<pre>
日本(にほん)<b>で</b>富士山(ふじさん)<b>がいちばん</b>高(たか)いです。
Mt. Fuji is the highest mountain in Japan.
</pre>

<pre>
日本(にほん)の山(やま)<b>の中(なか)で</b>富士山(ふじさん)<b>がいちばん</b>高(たか)いです。
Of all the mountains in Japan, Mt. Fuji is the highest.
</pre>

Both are correct. The first treats Japan as a location; the second explicitly
assembles the set of Japanese mountains and ranks it. What you must not do is drop
the particle altogether: ❌日本(にほん)富士山(ふじさん)がいちばん高(たか)いです.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 果物(くだもの)の中(なか)で、みかん<b>は</b>いちばん安(やす)いです。 → ✅ 果物(くだもの)の中(なか)で、みかん<b>が</b>いちばん安(やす)いです。
  The item being crowned takes `が`. This is the error learners make most often
  with the pattern, because English "oranges are the cheapest" looks like a topic
  sentence.

- ❌ スポーツの中(なか)<b>に</b>、テニスがいちばん好(す)きです。 → ✅ スポーツの中(なか)<b>で</b>、テニスがいちばん好(す)きです。
  `で` marks the domain within which the comparison is carried out. `に` would
  mark a location of existence, which is not what is going on.

- ❌ 世界(せかい)の中(なか)でいちばん大(おお)きい国(くに) → ✅ 世界(せかい)でいちばん大(おお)きい国(くに)
  Not ungrammatical, but a place name plus <code>の中(なか)で</code> is clumsy.
  Places and time spans take the bare `で`.

- ❌ この三(みっ)つの中(なか)で、<b>何(なに)</b>がいちばんいいですか。 → ✅ この三(みっ)つの中(なか)で、<b>どれ</b>がいちばんいいですか。
  When the candidates have been listed, the question word is `どれ`. `なに` asks
  about an open field with no stated members.

- ❌ 兄弟(きょうだい)の中(なか)で、弟(おとうと)がいちばん元気(げんき)<b>な</b>です。 → ✅ 兄弟(きょうだい)の中(なか)で、弟(おとうと)がいちばん元気(げんき)です。
  `いちばん` does not change how the adjective ends. A な-adjective keeps its `な`
  only in front of a noun.

## Practice

**1.** 飲(の)み物(もの)の中(なか)で、お茶(ちゃ)____いちばん好(す)きです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

飲(の)み物(もの)の中(なか)で、お茶(ちゃ)<b>が</b>いちばん好(す)きです。

`が` marks the item selected out of the group. It is also the particle
<code>好(す)き</code> normally takes for the thing liked, so the two requirements
agree here.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 春(はる)と秋(あき)と（どちら・どれ）がいちばん好(す)きですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

春(はる)と秋(あき)と<b>どちら</b>が好(す)きですか。

With exactly two candidates you use `どちら` - and then `いちばん` has to go, since
there is no ranking of three or more to head. `どれ` needs at least three listed
options.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Which season do you like best?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

季節(きせつ)の中(なか)で、<b>いつがいちばん</b>好(す)きですか。
（also natural: どの季節(きせつ)がいちばん好(す)きですか。）

Seasons are times, so `いつ` fits; <code>どの + 季節(きせつ)</code> is the other
standard way to ask. Either way the question word carries `が`, never `は`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 日本(にほん)の町(まち)の中(なか)に、京都(きょうと)はいちばんきれいだと思(おも)います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 日本(にほん)の町(まち)の中(なか)<b>で</b>、京都(きょうと)<b>が</b>いちばんきれいだと思(おも)います。

Two particles are wrong. The field of comparison takes `で`, not `に`, and the
selected item takes `が`, not `は`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この店(みせ)のパンはおいしいです。
(b) この辺(へん)の店(みせ)の中(なか)で、この店(みせ)のパンがいちばんおいしいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is an unranked judgement - the bread here is good, and nothing is said about
anywhere else.

(b) sets up a field (the shops around here), compares them, and awards first
place. The listener now knows the speaker has tried the others. That explicit
field is exactly what the pattern adds over a bare adjective.

</details>

# ～より～のほうが～

**Meaning** · ～ is more ～ than ～ - comparing exactly two things

Japanese adjectives have no comparative form: 高(たか)い covers both "tall" and
"taller". The comparison is carried entirely by the particles. `より` marks the
loser, <code>の方(ほう)が</code> marks the winner, and the adjective stays exactly as
it is.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (winner) | Noun + の方(ほう)が | 猫(ねこ) → 猫(ねこ)の方(ほう)が |
| Noun (loser) | Noun + より | 犬(いぬ) → 犬(いぬ)より |
| Verb | plain form + 方(ほう)が（no の） | 歩(ある)く → 歩(ある)く方(ほう)が |
| い-adjective | plain form + 方(ほう)が | 安(やす)い → 安(やす)い方(ほう)が |
| な-adjective | stem + な + 方(ほう)が | 静(しず)かだ → 静(しず)かな方(ほう)が |
| Question | AとBと、どちらが + adjective + か | どちらが安(やす)いですか |
| Answer | Bの方(ほう)が + adjective + です | Bの方(ほう)が安(やす)いです |
| Short form | Aより + adjective | 去年(きょねん)より寒(さむ)いです |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the order is free. AよりBの方(ほう)が高(たか)いです and
Bの方(ほう)がAより高(たか)いです say the same thing. What is fixed is which
particle goes on which noun: `より` on the one that loses, <code>の方(ほう)が</code>
on the one that wins. Swap the particles and you reverse the sentence.

</div>

Either half can also stand alone. <code>去年(きょねん)より寒(さむ)いです</code>
("colder than last year") and <code>こちらの方(ほう)が安(やす)いです</code> ("this
one is cheaper") are both complete, ordinary sentences.

## Examples

<pre>
犬(いぬ)<b>より</b>猫(ねこ)<b>の方(ほう)が</b>好(す)きです。
I like cats more than dogs.
</pre>

<pre>
バス<b>より</b>電車(でんしゃ)<b>の方(ほう)が</b>速(はや)いです。
The train is faster than the bus.
</pre>

<pre>
今日(きょう)は昨日(きのう)<b>より</b>暖(あたた)かいですね。
It's warmer today than yesterday, isn't it.
</pre>

<pre>
東京(とうきょう)と大阪(おおさか)と、どちら<b>の方(ほう)が</b>人(ひと)が多(おお)いですか。
Which has more people, Tokyo or Osaka?
</pre>

<pre>
肉(にく)<b>より</b>魚(さかな)<b>の方(ほう)を</b>よく食(た)べます。
I eat fish more often than meat.
</pre>

<pre>
私(わたし)<b>の方(ほう)が</b>姉(あね)<b>より</b>少(すこ)し背(せ)が高(たか)いです。
I'm slightly taller than my older sister.
</pre>

<pre>
車(くるま)で行(い)く<b>より</b>、歩(ある)く<b>方(ほう)が</b>早(はや)いですよ。
It's quicker to walk than to go by car.
</pre>

<pre>
冬(ふゆ)の北海道(ほっかいどう)は、思(おも)っていた<b>より</b>ずっと寒(さむ)かったです。
Hokkaido in winter was far colder than I had expected.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral. In casual speech <code>の方(ほう)が</code> is often reduced to
<code>の方(ほう)</code> with the `が` dropped - こっちの方(ほう)安(やす)いよ - and
the whole `より` half is regularly left out when it is obvious what the comparison
is against.

Degree adverbs slot in before the adjective and are very common:
`ずっと` (far, by a long way), `もっと` (more), <code>少(すこ)し</code> and `ちょっと` (a
little). 去年(きょねん)よりずっと忙(いそが)しいです.

The question form is <code>AとBと、どちらが…</code>. Note three things about it: `どちら`
not `どれ` (two candidates, not three); `が` not `は`; and the polite
<code>どちら</code> has a casual counterpart `どっち` that you will hear constantly in
conversation - 紅茶(こうちゃ)とコーヒー、どっちがいい?

<code>の方(ほう)</code> literally means "side, direction", and that image is worth
keeping: you are pointing at one side of a pair. It is why the pattern feels wrong
with three or more items - there is no "other side" to point away from.

Finally, `より` has a second life in formal writing and announcements where it
means "from": 九時(くじ)より開(ひら)きます, "opening from nine". Same
character, unrelated job.

## Contrast

### ～より～の方(ほう)が vs ～の中(なか)で～がいちばん

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - two candidates take `より`; three or more take `いちばん`. They
never appear in the same clause.

</div>

<pre>
コーヒー<b>より</b>紅茶(こうちゃ)<b>の方(ほう)が</b>好(す)きです。
I prefer tea to coffee.
</pre>

<pre>
飲(の)み物(もの)の中(なか)で紅茶(こうちゃ)がいちばん好(す)きです。
Of all drinks, tea is my favourite.
</pre>

The first ranks two named things and says nothing about anything else - the
speaker may dislike both. The second crowns a winner over a whole field.

### comparative `より` vs "from" `より`

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if `より` is followed by an adjective, it is comparing. If it
is followed by a verb of starting, opening or beginning, it means "from" and is a
formal replacement for `から`.

</div>

<pre>
この映画(えいが)は前(まえ)の作品(さくひん)<b>より</b>長(なが)いです。
This film is longer than the previous one.
</pre>

<pre>
受付(うけつけ)は十時(じゅうじ)より始(はじ)まります。
Reception opens from ten o'clock.
</pre>

The second belongs to notices and announcements. In speech you would say
十時(じゅうじ)から.

### ～の方(ほう)が vs ～方(ほう)がいい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same word <code>方(ほう)</code>, two different jobs. With an
adjective it compares; with `いい` and a verb it advises.

</div>

<pre>
新(あたら)しいパソコン<b>の方(ほう)が</b>軽(かる)いです。
The new laptop is lighter.
</pre>

<pre>
新(あたら)しいパソコンを買(か)った方(ほう)がいいですよ。
You'd better buy a new laptop.
</pre>

The advice pattern normally takes the た-form of the verb and ends in `いい`. If
you find yourself saying <code>方(ほう)がいい</code> when you only meant "more", you
have accidentally told someone what to do.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 猫(ねこ)<b>の方(ほう)が</b>犬(いぬ)<b>が</b>好(す)きです。 → ✅ 犬(いぬ)<b>より</b>猫(ねこ)<b>の方(ほう)が</b>好(す)きです。
  The losing item needs `より`. Without it there is nothing to compare against and
  the sentence has two competing `が`.

- ❌ 歩(ある)く<b>の</b>方(ほう)が早(はや)いです。 → ✅ 歩(ある)く方(ほう)が早(はや)いです。
  Nouns take `の` before <code>方(ほう)</code>; verbs and い-adjectives attach
  directly. Only な-adjectives insert anything, and what they insert is `な`.

- ❌ 猫(ねこ)方(ほう)が好(す)きです。 → ✅ 猫(ねこ)<b>の</b>方(ほう)が好(す)きです。
  The mirror-image error. After a noun the `の` is compulsory.

- ❌ コーヒーと紅茶(こうちゃ)と、どちらが<b>いちばん</b>好(す)きですか。 → ✅ コーヒーと紅茶(こうちゃ)と、どちらが好(す)きですか。
  `いちばん` needs three or more candidates. With two, `どちら` alone already asks
  the question.

- ❌ 今日(きょう)は昨日(きのう)より<b>もっと暖(あたた)かいの方(ほう)が</b>です。 → ✅ 今日(きょう)は昨日(きのう)より暖(あたた)かいです。
  The adjective itself never changes shape, and the two halves of the pattern do
  not both attach to it. Comparison lives in the particles, not in the adjective.

## Practice

**1.** 飛行機(ひこうき)は新幹線(しんかんせん)____速(はや)いです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

飛行機(ひこうき)は新幹線(しんかんせん)<b>より</b>速(はや)いです。

`より` attaches to the thing being beaten. The winner here is already the topic,
marked with `は`, so <code>の方(ほう)が</code> is not needed - though
新幹線(しんかんせん)より飛行機(ひこうき)の方(ほう)が速(はや)いです is
equally correct.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 春(はる)と夏(なつ)と、（どちら・どれ）が好(す)きですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

春(はる)と夏(なつ)と、<b>どちら</b>が好(す)きですか。

Two options means `どちら` (casually `どっち`). `どれ` requires a set of at least
three, and choosing it here would leave the listener looking for a third season.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "This room is quieter than that one."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

あの部屋(へや)<b>より</b>この部屋(へや)<b>の方(ほう)が</b>静(しず)かです。

Both halves are nouns, so both take their own particle, and
<code>静(しず)かです</code> stays in its ordinary form - there is no comparative
ending to add. Reversing the two halves is also fine.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 私(わたし)は兄(あに)の方(ほう)が背(せ)が高(たか)いです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 私(わたし)<b>より</b>兄(あに)の方(ほう)が背(せ)が高(たか)いです。

As written, `は` and <code>の方(ほう)が</code> are fighting over which noun is the
subject. Marking the speaker with `より` makes it clear who is being compared with
whom - and, incidentally, who is taller.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この辞書(じしょ)の方(ほう)が便利(べんり)です。
(b) この辞書(じしょ)を使(つか)った方(ほう)がいいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a comparison. Some other dictionary is in play, and this one is the more
useful of the two. The speaker is describing, not instructing.

(b) is advice: you should use this dictionary. The た-form plus
<code>方(ほう)がいい</code> is a recommendation, and depending on tone it can sound
quite firm. Same noun <code>方(ほう)</code>, entirely different social act.

</details>
