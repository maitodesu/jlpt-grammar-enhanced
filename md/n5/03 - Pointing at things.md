# N5 - Pointing at things

# これ・それ・あれ・どれ

**Meaning** · this one / that one / that one over there / which one - the four words that stand in for a *thing* without naming it

These four are **pronouns**. Each one *is* a noun: it takes particles, it sits
where a noun sits, and it can be the entire answer to a question. Which one you
pick depends on whose territory the thing is in - yours (`これ`), your listener's
(`それ`), or neither of yours (`あれ`) - and `どれ` asks the listener to choose one
out of several.

## Formation

Nothing attaches to these words; they *are* the noun. The table shows what each
one picks out and how it slots into a sentence.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing - これ is itself a noun | これ = a thing in the speaker's reach | これ、おいしいですね |
| Nothing | それ = a thing in the listener's reach, or one just mentioned | それを三(みっ)つください |
| Nothing | あれ = a thing away from both of you | あれは何(なん)ですか |
| Nothing | どれ = which one, out of **three or more** | どれが安(やす)いですか |
| Particles | これ／それ／あれ／どれ + は・が・を・に・で・と | それと同(おな)じものをください |
| A following noun | ❌ これ + Noun → use この + Noun | ❌ これかばん → ✅ このかばん |
| Two choices only | どれ → どちら（casual どっち） | コーヒーとお茶(ちゃ)、どちらにしますか |
| Polite equivalent | これ／それ／あれ／どれ → こちら／そちら／あちら／どちら | こちらが新(あたら)しいメニューです |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if a noun follows, you need `この・その・あの・どの`. If nothing
follows, you need `これ・それ・あれ・どれ`. A word in this series is never sitting
directly in front of the thing it points at.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
<b>これ</b>は姉(あね)のかさです。
This is my older sister's umbrella.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>それ</b>、ちょっと見(み)せてください。
Could you let me see that for a second?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>あれ</b>は駅(えき)のビルです。
That building over there is the station.
</pre>

<pre>
メニューの中(なか)で<b>どれ</b>が一番(いちばん)安(やす)いですか。
Which is the cheapest thing on the menu?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>それ</b>はいい考(かんが)えですね。
That's a good idea.
</pre>

<pre>
すみません、<b>これ</b>と<b>それ</b>をください。
Excuse me - I'll take this one and that one.
</pre>

<pre>
去年(きょねん)京都(きょうと)で買(か)ったカップ、<b>あれ</b>はどこにしまいましたか。
That cup we bought in Kyoto last year - where did you put it away?
</pre>

<pre>
説明(せつめい)は分(わ)かりましたが、<b>それ</b>が本当(ほんとう)かどうかは別(べつ)の話(はなし)です。
I follow the explanation, but whether it is true is another matter.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The series itself is register-neutral. <code>これは…です</code> is the first full
sentence most learners ever build, and it stays usable for life.

Three things are worth knowing before you aim one of these words at a person.

**Never point at a human with これ／それ／あれ.** They are for objects. For people,
use <code>この人(ひと)</code> at the blunt end and `こちら` when you are being polite - 
<code>こちらは田中(たなか)さんです</code> is the standard way to introduce someone.
`こいつ`／`そいつ`／`あいつ` also exist for people and they are rough: tolerable
between close friends about an absent third party, insulting anywhere else.

**こちら・そちら・あちら・どちら are the polite upgrade**, and they double as the
two-item version of `どれ`. In a shop you will hear `こちら` where a friend would
say `これ` - <code>こちらでよろしいでしょうか</code>, "will this one be all right?"

**`あれ` on its own is also an interjection** meaning "huh?" - the noise you make
when something is not where you left it: <code>あれ、かぎがない</code>. Same word on
the page, completely different job, and context makes it obvious.

In speech the particle is routinely dropped: `これ、いくら?` rather than
<code>これはいくらですか</code>. Keep the particle in writing and in ます-speech.

## Contrast

### これ・それ・あれ・どれ vs この・その・あの・どの

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the れ-series replaces the noun; the の-series introduces it.
`これ` = "this one". `この` = "this ___", and the blank is compulsory.

</div>

This is the commonest error in the whole topic, because English uses one word - 
"this" - for both jobs.

<pre>
<b>これ</b>は重(おも)いです。
This one is heavy.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>この</b>はこは重(おも)いです。
This box is heavy.
</pre>

You cannot mix them: ❌<code>これはこ</code> and ❌<code>このは重(おも)いです</code> are
both broken. And unlike the place words, the れ-series does not take `の` in front
of a noun either - ❌<code>これの本(ほん)</code> is not how you say "this book";
<code>この本(ほん)</code> is.

### それ vs あれ - pointing back at the conversation

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for something *said* rather than seen: `それ` picks up what one
of you has just mentioned; `あれ` picks up something you both already know.

</div>

Once nothing is physically in the room, distance stops being about metres. `それ`
handles information that has only just entered the conversation, from one side or
the other.

<pre>
「来週(らいしゅう)、試験(しけん)があるよ。」「えっ、<b>それ</b>、本当(ほんとう)?」
"There's an exam next week." "Wait - is that true?"
</pre>

`あれ` reaches instead for shared memory: something you have both experienced,
which is why it can turn up with no antecedent in the sentence at all.

<pre>
去年(きょねん)二人(ふたり)で見(み)た映画(えいが)、<b>あれ</b>はよかったね。
That film the two of us saw last year - that was a good one, wasn't it.
</pre>

Use `あれ` when the listener does *not* share the memory and you will sound as if
you are talking to yourself.

### どれ vs どちら・どっち

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `どれ` needs **three or more** candidates. With exactly two,
Japanese switches to `どちら`, or `どっち` in casual speech.

</div>

English "which" does not care how many there are, so this has to be learned on
purpose.

<pre>
この三(みっ)つのうち、<b>どれ</b>にしますか。
Which of these three will you have?
</pre>

<pre>
赤(あか)と青(あお)、<b>どちら</b>がいいですか。
Red or blue - which would you prefer?
</pre>

`どちら` is also simply the polite form of `どれ`, so a shop assistant may use it
even facing a whole shelf of options. `どっち` is casual and extremely frequent in
speech; keep it out of email.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>これ</b>かばんは私(わたし)のです。 → ✅ <b>この</b>かばんは私(わたし)のです。
  A noun follows, so the の-series is required. `これ` can only stand alone.

- ❌ <b>これの</b>本(ほん)を読(よ)みました。 → ✅ <b>この</b>本(ほん)を読(よ)みました。
  `の` does not rescue it. Unlike <code>ここの店(みせ)</code>, which is perfectly
  normal, <code>これの本(ほん)</code> is not a thing.

- ❌ 紅茶(こうちゃ)とコーヒー、<b>どれ</b>がいいですか。 → ✅ 紅茶(こうちゃ)とコーヒー、<b>どちら</b>がいいですか。
  Two candidates means `どちら`／`どっち`. `どれ` starts at three.

- ❌ <b>あれ</b>は山田(やまだ)さんです。 → ✅ <b>あちら</b>は山田(やまだ)さんです。
  `あれ` is for objects. Aimed at a person it is genuinely rude; `あちら`
 - or <code>あの人(ひと)</code> among friends - is what you want.

- ❌ 「これは何(なん)ですか。」「はい、<b>これ</b>はペンです。」 → ✅ 「…」「<b>それ</b>はペンです。」
  Once the object is in your *listener's* hands it has moved into their sphere, so
  the answer flips to `それ`. Replying with `これ` claims you are the one holding it.

## Practice

**1.** すみません、____をください。（you are pointing at a cake inside the display case, out of reach of both of you）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

すみません、<b>あれ</b>をください。

The cake is behind glass, away from both you and the shop assistant, so it sits in
neither person's sphere - which is exactly what `あれ` is for. <code>こちら</code>
would be the staff's word, not yours.

</details>

**2.** 靴(くつ)が四(よん)足(そく)並(なら)んでいます。（どれ・どちら）が好(す)きですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>どれ</b>が好(す)きですか。

Four pairs means three or more candidates, so `どれ`. `どちら` would tell the
listener you are offering a choice between exactly two - or that you are being
markedly polite.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Is that yours? - No, this one is mine."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>それ</b>はあなたのですか。 - いいえ、<b>これ</b>が私(わたし)のです。

The thing asked about is near the listener, hence `それ`; the speaker then points
at the one in their own reach, hence `これ`. The answer uses `が` because "this
one" is being singled out as the correct one among several.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: これのシャツは小(ちい)さいですから、大(おお)きいのをください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ <b>この</b>シャツは小(ちい)さいですから、大(おお)きいのをください。
"This shirt is too small, so please give me a bigger one."

<code>これの</code> is not a possible modifier. When a noun follows, switch to the
の-series. The `の` later in the sentence is a different `の` - the pronoun "one",
standing in for シャツ.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 昨日(きのう)の話(はなし)、<b>それ</b>はもう終(お)わりました。
(b) 昨日(きのう)の話(はなし)、<b>あれ</b>はもう終(お)わりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) treats yesterday's matter as something that has just been raised in this
conversation - typically the listener brought it up and you are answering their
words. It is the neutral, businesslike choice.

(b) treats it as a file you both already have open: shared history you do not need
to re-explain. It sounds warmer and more familiar, and it would be strange if the
listener had no idea what you were referring to.

</details>

# この・その・あの・どの

**Meaning** · this ~ / that ~ / that ~ over there / which ~ - pointing words that modify a noun instead of replacing it

`この` and its siblings are **adnominals**: they do nothing on their own and must be
followed immediately by a noun. <code>この本(ほん)</code> is "this book", never "this". The
こ／そ／あ／ど split is the same one the rest of the series uses - the speaker's
side, the listener's side, away from both, and the question.

## Formation

Nothing attaches *to* these words. They attach to the noun on their right, with
nothing allowed in between except an adjective.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | この + Noun - in the speaker's territory | この傘(かさ) |
| Noun | その + Noun - in the listener's territory, or just mentioned | その話(はなし) |
| Noun | あの + Noun - away from both, or shared memory | あの店(みせ) |
| Noun | どの + Noun - which ~, out of **three or more** | どの電車(でんしゃ) |
| Adjective + Noun | この + Adj + Noun | この赤(あか)いくつ |
| The particle | この + Noun **+ particle** - never この + particle | この傘(かさ)は私(わたし)のです |
| Nothing | ❌ この on its own → use これ | ❌ このは大(おお)きい → ✅ これは大(おお)きい |
| Two choices only | どの → どちらの（casual どっちの） | どちらの席(せき)がいいですか |
| Polite equivalent | この／その／あの → こちらの／そちらの／あちらの | そちらの方(かた)にお聞(き)きください |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `この` is a hook with nothing on it. Something must hang from it,
and that something is a noun. If you have no noun to supply, you wanted
`これ・それ・あれ・どれ` instead.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
<b>この</b>ペンは父(ちち)のです。
This pen is my father's.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>その</b>いすに座(すわ)ってください。
Please sit on that chair.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>あの</b>白(しろ)い建物(たてもの)は図書館(としょかん)です。
That white building over there is the library.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>どの</b>バスが空港(くうこう)へ行(い)きますか。
Which bus goes to the airport?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>その</b>話(はなし)はもう聞(き)きました。
I have already heard that story.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>あの</b>とき、もっと早(はや)く出(で)ればよかった。
I should have left earlier that time.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>この</b>辺(へん)は夜(よる)になると静(しず)かですから、勉強(べんきょう)するのにいいですよ。
This area goes quiet after dark, so it's a good place to study.
</pre>

<pre>
注文(ちゅうもん)は<b>あの</b>背(せ)の高(たか)い店員(てんいん)さんにお願(ねが)いしました。
I gave my order to that tall member of staff over there.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Neutral in itself, and unavoidable - you cannot get through a paragraph of
Japanese without it.

**`あの` is also the standard hesitation noise**, usually stretched to `あのう`. It
opens a request to a stranger the way "excuse me…" does in English:
<code>あのう、すみません、ちょっとよろしいですか</code>. Beginners hear it constantly
and assume it is the demonstrative; it is not, and it points at nothing.

**`その` is the workhorse of written and business Japanese.** In an email,
<code>その件(けん)について</code> ("regarding that matter") refers back to whatever
the previous sentence or the previous email established. Anaphoric `その` is far
more common in writing than the physical "near you" sense you learn first.

**Politeness rises through `こちらの`.** <code>あの人(ひと)</code> is fine among
friends but too blunt about a customer or a superior; <code>あちらの方(かた)</code>
is the safe formal version. `あいつ` and friends are the rough end of the same
scale and take a `の` too: `あいつの` - avoid in polite company.

**Casual speech shortens `どの` to `どっちの` when there are two**, and drops the
noun altogether when it can be guessed: 「どっち?」rather than
<code>どちらの席(せき)がいいですか</code>.

## Contrast

### この・その・あの・どの vs これ・それ・あれ・どれ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `この` modifies, `これ` replaces. `この` must be followed by a
noun; `これ` must **not** be.

</div>

They are the same four distances packaged for two different grammatical slots, and
mixing them up is the single most frequent mistake in this topic.

<pre>
<b>その</b>時計(とけい)はいくらですか。
How much is that watch?
</pre>

<pre>
それはいくらですか。
How much is that one?
</pre>

Both are correct; the first names the object, the second assumes you can see which
object is meant. What you may never write is ❌<code>それ時計(とけい)</code> or
❌<code>この、いくらですか</code>.

There is one asymmetry worth memorising: the place words *do* take `の` before a
noun (<code>ここの店(みせ)</code>, "the shop here"), but the thing pronouns do not.
❌<code>これの店(みせ)</code> is wrong; <code>この店(みせ)</code> is right.

### その vs あの

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `その` = it is on your listener's side, or it entered the
conversation a moment ago. `あの` = it is on nobody's side, or we both already
know it.

</div>

Physically the split is easy. In conversation, where nothing is visible, it is
about who owns the information.

<pre>
「新(あたら)しいカフェができたよ。」「へえ、<b>その</b>カフェ、どこ?」
"A new café has opened." "Oh - where is that café?"
</pre>

The speaker has never seen it. It exists for them only because the listener just
said so, so `その`.

<pre>
学生(がくせい)のとき、よく二人(ふたり)で行(い)ったよね。<b>あの</b>カフェ、まだあるかな。
We used to go together as students, didn't we. I wonder if that café is still there.
</pre>

Here the café is in both memories, so `あの`. Using `あの` for something the
listener has never heard of sounds as though you are muttering to yourself - which
is precisely why writers use it for that effect.

### どの vs どんな

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `どの` picks one out of a known set. `どんな` asks what kind of
thing it is, with no set in front of you.

</div>

Both sit in front of a noun and both come out as "what/which" in English, so they
collapse together unless you separate them deliberately.

<pre>
棚(たな)の本(ほん)のうち、<b>どの</b>本(ほん)が一番(いちばん)役(やく)に立(た)ちますか。
Of the books on the shelf, which book is the most useful?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>どんな</b>本(ほん)をよく読(よ)みますか。
What kind of books do you usually read?
</pre>

Answer the first with a book you can point at; answer the second with a category - 
歴史(れきし)の本(ほん), 小説(しょうせつ). A quick test: if the English is
"which of these", it is `どの`; if it is "what sort of", it is `どんな`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>この</b>は私(わたし)のかばんです。 → ✅ <b>これ</b>は私(わたし)のかばんです。
  `この` cannot stand alone. Either supply a noun (<code>このかばんは私(わたし)のです</code>)
  or switch to `これ`.

- ❌ <b>それ</b>本(ほん)を取(と)ってください。 → ✅ <b>その</b>本(ほん)を取(と)ってください。
  A noun follows, so the の-series is required. `それ` never sits in front of a noun.

- ❌ <b>あのの</b>人(ひと)は先生(せんせい)です。 → ✅ <b>あの</b>人(ひと)は先生(せんせい)です。
  `この・その・あの・どの` already end in the `の` they need. Adding a second one is
  doubling up.

- ❌ 肉(にく)と魚(さかな)、<b>どの</b>料理(りょうり)にしますか。 → ✅ 肉(にく)と魚(さかな)、<b>どちらの</b>料理(りょうり)にしますか。
  Two options means `どちらの`. `どの` needs three or more to choose between.

- ❌ <b>どの</b>音楽(おんがく)が好(す)きですか。（meaning "what kind of music do you like?"）→ ✅ <b>どんな</b>音楽(おんがく)が好(す)きですか。
  `どの` would be asking the listener to point at one specific piece of music from a
  list in front of them. For a genre, you want `どんな`.

## Practice

**1.** ____建物(たてもの)は百年(ひゃくねん)前(まえ)に建(た)てられました。（the building is a long way off, visible from where you both stand）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>あの</b>建物(たてもの)は百年(ひゃくねん)前(まえ)に建(た)てられました。

It is away from both of you, so `あの`. A noun follows, so it must be the の-series
and not `あれ`.

</details>

**2.** 会議室(かいぎしつ)が五(いつ)つあります。（どの・どんな）部屋(へや)を使(つか)いますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>どの</b>部屋(へや)を使(つか)いますか。

There is a defined set of five rooms and you are asking the listener to pick one
out of it. <code>どんな部屋(へや)</code> would be asking what sort of room they want - big,
quiet, with a projector - which is a different question.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I don't know that word. Could you write it on the board?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>その</b>言葉(ことば)は知(し)りません。ホワイトボードに書(か)いていただけますか。

The word came from the listener's mouth, so it belongs to their side of the
conversation: `その`. `あの` would wrongly imply you both already know it, which is
the opposite of what you are saying.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: すみません、これシャツはいくらですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ すみません、<b>この</b>シャツはいくらですか。
"Excuse me, how much is this shirt?"

シャツ is a noun and it follows immediately, so the modifying form `この` is
required. The alternative repair is to drop the noun: <code>これはいくらですか</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) <b>その</b>映画(えいが)は面白(おもしろ)かったですか。
(b) <b>あの</b>映画(えいが)は面白(おもしろ)かったですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) refers to a film the listener has just brought up, and which the speaker knows
nothing about beyond that mention. It is the natural reply to "I went to the
cinema yesterday."

(b) presupposes that both of you already have a particular film in mind - one you
discussed before, or one everybody in the room has heard of. Said out of nowhere it
would leave the listener asking 「どの映画(えいが)?」.

</details>

# ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ

**Meaning** · here / there / over there / where - the four words that name a *place* without naming it

These are place pronouns. Like `これ` they stand alone and take particles, but they
denote locations, which changes which particles they attract: `に` for existence and
destination, `で` for where something happens, `から`／`まで` for the ends of a route.
Note the shape of the third one - it is `あそこ`, not `あこ`. That is the one
irregularity in the whole こそあど system.

## Formation

Nothing attaches to these words; they *are* the place noun. What follows is a
particle.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing - ここ is itself a noun | ここ = where the speaker is | ここで待(ま)ちます |
| Nothing | そこ = where the listener is, or a place just mentioned | そこに置(お)いてください |
| Nothing | あそこ = a place away from both of you | あそこまで歩(ある)きましょう |
| Nothing | どこ = where | どこに住(す)んでいますか |
| Particle に | Place + に - existence or destination | ここに名前(なまえ)を書(か)きます |
| Particle で | Place + で - where an action happens | あそこで会(あ)いましょう |
| から／まで | Place + から／まで - route ends | ここから駅(えき)まで十分(じゅっぷん) |
| **Noun modification** | ここ／そこ／あそこ／どこ **+ の +** Noun | ここの水(みず)はおいしい |
| Polite equivalent | ここ／そこ／あそこ／どこ → こちら／そちら／あちら／どちら | どちらにお住(す)まいですか |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `に` puts something *at* the place, `で` makes something *happen*
at the place. <code>ここに本(ほん)がある</code> versus
<code>ここで本(ほん)を読(よ)む</code>. Getting this pair wrong is the single most
audible error learners make with place words.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
かばんは<b>ここ</b>に置(お)きます。
I'll put my bag here.
</pre>

<pre>
出口(でぐち)は<b>どこ</b>ですか。
Where is the exit?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>そこ</b>、ちょっと危(あぶ)ないですよ。
It's a bit dangerous where you're standing.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>あそこ</b>の角(かど)を右(みぎ)に曲(ま)がってください。
Turn right at that corner over there.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>ここ</b>のパンは朝(あさ)六時(ろくじ)に焼(や)き上(あ)がります。
The bread here comes out of the oven at six in the morning.
</pre>

<pre>
旅行(りょこう)の写真(しゃしん)、<b>どこ</b>でとったんですか。
Where did you take these holiday photos?
</pre>

<pre>
駅(えき)を出(で)たら、<b>そこ</b>から三(みっ)つ目(め)の信号(しんごう)を左(ひだり)に曲(ま)がってください。
Once you leave the station, turn left at the third set of lights from there.
</pre>

<pre>
子(こ)どものころ住(す)んでいた町(まち)に戻(もど)ったが、<b>あそこ</b>はもうすっかり変(か)わってしまった。
I went back to the town I lived in as a child, but the place has changed completely.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Neutral, and used constantly. Three extensions are worth knowing.

**They point at abstract "places" too.** In an explanation or an argument, `ここ`
means "this point in what I am saying" and `そこ` means "that point you just made":
<code>ここが大切(たいせつ)です</code> ("this is the important bit"),
<code>そこが問題(もんだい)なんです</code> ("that is exactly the problem"). This use is
extremely common and completely idiomatic; a learner who only knows the physical
sense will misread it.

**`ここ` also stretches over time.** <code>ここ一週間(いっしゅうかん)</code> means
"these last/next seven days", and <code>ここ数年(すうねん)</code> "these past few
years". `そこ` and `あそこ` do not do this.

**`こちら`・`そちら`・`あちら`・`どちら` are the polite register**, and in service or
business Japanese they replace the plain words wholesale. Asked for your address you
will hear <code>どちらにお住(す)まいですか</code>, never
<code>どこに住(す)んでいますか</code>. `どちら` is also how you ask a customer where
they are from. Meanwhile `ここら`／`そこら` ("round about here / there") is the casual
end of the scale.

One caution on `そこ` with people: <code>そこの人(ひと)</code>, shouted, is the
Japanese equivalent of "oi, you there" - accurate grammar, terrible manners.

## Contrast

### ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ vs これ・それ・あれ and この・その・あの

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - three parallel series, three different jobs. `これ` is a *thing*
standing alone, `この` modifies a following noun, and `ここ` is a *place* standing
alone.

</div>

Everything in the こそあど system shares the same four-way distance split; the
series you pick is decided by what grammatical slot you need to fill.

<pre>
<b>ここ</b>は銀行(ぎんこう)です。
This place is a bank. (You are standing in it.)
</pre>

<pre>
これは銀行(ぎんこう)のかぎです。
This is the key to the bank. (An object in your hand.)
</pre>

The place words have one privilege the thing pronouns lack: **they take `の` before
a noun.** <code>ここの店員(てんいん)</code> ("the staff here") and
<code>どこの会社(かいしゃ)</code> ("which company / a company from where") are both
normal, whereas ❌<code>これの店員(てんいん)</code> is impossible - you would have to
say <code>この店員(てんいん)</code>. So `ここの` is a genitive, and `この` is a
demonstrative; they are not interchangeable.

### ここに vs ここで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `に` marks the place a thing *is* or *ends up*; `で` marks the
stage on which an action is performed.

</div>

English "here" and "there" carry no such distinction, so this must be chosen
consciously every time.

<pre>
<b>ここに</b>自転車(じてんしゃ)があります。
There is a bicycle here.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>ここで</b>自転車(じてんしゃ)を直(なお)します。
I fix bicycles here.
</pre>

Existence verbs and verbs of arriving or putting take `に`; everything active takes
`で`. The test that catches most cases: if you can rephrase the English as "there
is / it goes onto", use `に`; if it is "the activity takes place at", use `で`.

### どこ vs どちら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same question, two registers. `どこ` is neutral, `どちら` is
polite, and `どちら` additionally means "which of two".

</div>

<pre>
トイレは<b>どこ</b>ですか。
Where's the toilet?
</pre>

<pre>
お国(くに)は<b>どちら</b>ですか。
Where are you from? (polite)
</pre>

Asking a customer, a client or an older stranger <code>どこですか</code> is not
ungrammatical, just noticeably plain. In the other direction, `どっち` is the casual
contraction and belongs with friends. Note that `どちら` doing double duty - "where"
and "which of two" - is resolved by context alone; nobody finds it ambiguous in
practice.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>あこ</b>に公園(こうえん)があります。 → ✅ <b>あそこ</b>に公園(こうえん)があります。
  The あ-form of the place series is irregular. `これ→あれ` and `この→あの` drop the
  そ, but `ここ→あそこ` keeps it.

- ❌ <b>ここで</b>花(はな)があります。 → ✅ <b>ここに</b>花(はな)があります。
  `で` marks the setting of an action. Existing is not an action, so it takes `に`.

- ❌ <b>ここに</b>働(はたら)いています。 → ✅ <b>ここで</b>働(はたら)いています。
  The mirror image: working *is* an action, so the place takes `で`.

- ❌ <b>これ</b>は入口(いりぐち)です。（pointing at a doorway you are standing in）→ ✅ <b>ここ</b>が入口(いりぐち)です。
  A location needs the place series, not the thing series. `これ` would mean you
  were holding the entrance.

- ❌ レストランが三(さん)軒(げん)あります。<b>どこの</b>店(みせ)にしますか。 → ✅ <b>どの</b>店(みせ)にしますか。
  <code>どこの店(みせ)</code> asks which *area or chain* a shop belongs to. To pick
  one out of a set in front of you, use `どの`.

## Practice

**1.** すみません、この荷物(にもつ)は____に置(お)けばいいですか。

すみません、この荷物(にもつ)は<b>どこ</b>に置(お)けばいいですか。
"Excuse me, where should I put this luggage?"

<code>置(お)く</code> is a verb of putting, so the landing place takes `に`, not
`で`. The question word for a place is `どこ`; in a formal setting you could raise it
to `どちら`.

</details>

**2.** 図書館(としょかん)（に・で）宿題(しゅくだい)をします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Doing homework is an activity, and the library is the stage it happens on, so `で`.
You would use `に` only for existence or arrival - for example
<code>図書館(としょかん)に行(い)きます</code>.

</details>

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Place words take `の` to modify a following noun, which is how you say "the coffee

</details>

has just raised - "that, what you just mentioned, is the hard part" - so it
acknowledges their contribution.

(b) points at what the *speaker* is currently explaining: "this bit here, the bit I
am showing you now, is the hard part." A teacher at a whiteboard uses (b); someone
agreeing with a colleague's diagnosis uses (a).

</details>

# Question words

**Meaning** · the words that ask what, who, when, where, how, why, what kind and how much

Japanese does not rearrange a sentence to ask a question. The question word simply
occupies the slot the answer will occupy, the word order stays exactly as it was,
and `か` goes on the end. <code>田中(たなか)さんが来(き)ます</code> becomes
<code>誰(だれ)が来(き)ますか</code> - one word swapped, nothing moved.

## Formation

One row per word. Each sits in the slot its answer would fill.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun slot | 何(なに)／何(なん) - what | これは何(なん)ですか |
| Noun slot | 誰(だれ) - who（polite どなた） | 誰(だれ)が来(き)ますか |
| Noun slot | いつ - when（never takes `に`） | 試験(しけん)はいつですか |
| Noun slot | どこ - where（polite どちら） | 受付(うけつけ)はどこですか |
| Adverb slot | どう - how, in what state | コーヒーはどうですか |
| Adverb slot | どうして - why（neutral, spoken and written） | どうして遅(おく)れたんですか |
| Adverb slot | なぜ - why（formal, mostly written） | なぜ人(ひと)は働(はたら)くのか |
| **Before a noun** | どんな + Noun - what kind of | どんな音楽(おんがく)が好(す)きですか |
| Noun slot | いくら - how much（price, amount） | これはいくらですか |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a question word that is the subject takes `が`, never `は`.
`は` marks something already known, and the whole point of a question word is that
it is not known. ✅<code>誰(だれ)が作(つく)りましたか</code> ／
❌<code>誰(だれ)は作(つく)りましたか</code>. The reply keeps the `が` too:
<code>母(はは)が作(つく)りました</code>.

</div>

<div class="rule">

**Reading rule for 何(なに)** - it is read **なん** before a sound in the た／だ／な
rows (で・と・に・だ・です・の) and before every counter; it is read **なに** everywhere
else. So 何(なん)ですか, 何(なん)の本(ほん), 何人(なんにん),
何時(なんじ) - but 何(なに)が, 何(なに)を, 何(なに)か, 何(なに)も.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
このマークは<b>何(なん)</b>ですか。
What is this symbol?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>誰(だれ)</b>がピアノを弾(ひ)きますか。
Who is going to play the piano?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>いつ</b>日本(にほん)へ来(き)ましたか。
When did you come to Japan?
</pre>

<pre>
この漢字(かんじ)は<b>どう</b>読(よ)みますか。
How do you read this kanji?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>どんな</b>映画(えいが)をよく見(み)ますか。
What sort of films do you watch a lot?
</pre>

<pre>
地下鉄(ちかてつ)の乗(の)り換(か)えは<b>どこ</b>でしますか。
Where do you change subway lines?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>どうして</b>昨日(きのう)学校(がっこう)を休(やす)んだんですか。
Why were you off school yesterday?
</pre>

<pre>
送料(そうりょう)も入(い)れると、全部(ぜんぶ)で<b>いくら</b>になりますか。
Including postage, how much does it come to altogether?
</pre>

<pre>
報告書(ほうこくしょ)には、<b>なぜ</b>この方法(ほうほう)を選(えら)んだのかを書(か)いてください。
In the report, please write why you chose this method.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The words themselves are neutral; what varies is politeness and how blunt the
question sounds.

**Politeness ladder.** <code>誰(だれ)</code> → `どなた` and
<code>どこ</code> → `どちら` are the two upgrades you will actually need.
「失礼(しつれい)ですが、どなたですか」is how you ask a stranger who they are;
<code>誰(だれ)ですか</code> at the same moment is abrupt. `いくら` has a formal
partner too - <code>おいくらですか</code>, and in shops
<code>おいくらになりますか</code>.

**Softening with ん／の.** A bare <code>どうして来(こ)なかったんですか</code> is
softer than <code>どうして来(こ)ませんでしたか</code>, because the `ん` frames the
question as asking for an explanation you are ready to accept rather than demanding
one. Without it, "why" questions can land as accusations in Japanese just as in
English.

**Three words for "why".** `なんで` is the casual spoken one, `どうして` the
everyday neutral one, `なぜ` the formal and written one. `なんで` from a subordinate
to a boss sounds childish; `なぜ` between friends sounds like a lecture.

**Rising intonation replaces `か` in casual speech**, and the question word carries
the stress: 「どこ行(い)くの?」「何(なに)食(た)べる?」 Adding `か` to a plain
form - ❌<code>どこ行(い)くか?</code> - sounds rough or interrogational, so leave
`か` for the ます-form.

Finally, note that all of these words also serve as the building blocks for
indefinites: add `か` and <code>何(なに)</code> becomes "something", add `も` with a
negative and it becomes "nothing". Those are separate patterns with their own pages.

## Contrast

### どうして vs なぜ vs なんで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - all three mean "why". `なんで` is casual speech, `どうして` is
the neutral default, `なぜ` is formal and belongs mainly to writing.

</div>

There is no difference in meaning, only in register, which is why choosing wrongly
is so noticeable.

<pre>
<b>なんで</b>遅(おく)れたの?
How come you're late?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>なぜ</b>事故(じこ)が起(お)きたのか、まだ分(わ)かっていません。
It is still not understood why the accident occurred.
</pre>

`どうして` sits comfortably in both of those situations and is the safe choice if
you are unsure. One extra wrinkle: `どうして` can also mean "by what means, how" in
older or set expressions, and `なんで` written in kanji as
<code>何(なん)で</code> is ambiguous with "by what means" - see the reading section
below.

### どう vs どんな

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `どう` asks about a state or a manner and stands alone;
`どんな` asks what *kind* of thing and must be followed by a noun.

</div>

Both come out as "how" or "what" in English and both are asking for a description,
so they blur together fast.

<pre>
新(あたら)しい仕事(しごと)は<b>どう</b>ですか。
How's the new job?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>どんな</b>仕事(しごと)ですか。
What kind of job is it?
</pre>

The first invites an evaluation - busy, fun, tiring. The second asks for the
category - sales, teaching, night shifts. Grammatically, ❌<code>どう仕事(しごと)</code>
is impossible, because `どう` is an adverb and cannot modify a noun; and
❌<code>仕事(しごと)はどんなですか</code> is impossible, because `どんな` cannot stand
alone. If you want the adjective-like form on its own, it is <code>どんな感(かん)じ</code>.

### 何(なに) vs 何(なん) - reading the same kanji two ways

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - **なん** before で・と・に・だ・です・の and before any counter;
**なに** everywhere else, including on its own.

</div>

The kanji never changes, so this is invisible on the page and audible the moment
you speak.

<pre>
何時(なんじ)に何(なに)を食(た)べましたか。
What did you eat, and at what time?
</pre>

Both readings in one sentence: `なんじ` because a counter follows, `なに` because
`を` follows. Further examples: 何人(なんにん), 何回(なんかい),
何日(なんにち), 何曜日(なんようび) all take なん - but
何色(なにいろ) ("what colour") and 何語(なにご) ("what language") take なに,
because those are not counters.

The pair that actually causes trouble is <code>何(なん)で</code> versus
<code>何(なに)で</code>.

<pre>
<b>何(なん)で</b>会社(かいしゃ)をやめたんですか。
Why did you quit the company?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>何(なに)で</b>会社(かいしゃ)まで来(き)ましたか。
What did you come to the office by?
</pre>

Same three characters, two readings, two meanings: なんで is "why", なにで is "by
what means". In speech people avoid the ambiguity by saying `どうして` for the first
and <code>何(なに)に乗(の)って</code> for the second.

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>何(なに)に</code> is the one place the rule wobbles. Following the
な-row rule it should be なんに, and 「何(なん)になさいますか」("what will you
have?") is indeed なん. But 「何(なに)に使(つか)うの?」("what's it for?") is
normally なに. Both readings are heard; treat this cell as genuinely variable
rather than as a rule you have got wrong.

</div>

## Common mistakes

- ❌ これは<b>なに</b>ですか。 → ✅ これは<b>なん</b>ですか。
  `です` begins with a だ-row sound, so the reading is なん. The kanji is identical,
  which is why this survives for years unnoticed.

- ❌ 誰(だれ)<b>は</b>この絵(え)をかきましたか。 → ✅ 誰(だれ)<b>が</b>この絵(え)をかきましたか。
  `は` presents known information. A question word is by definition unknown, so it
  takes `が`.

- ❌ <b>どう</b>人(ひと)が好(す)きですか。 → ✅ <b>どんな</b>人(ひと)が好(す)きですか。
  `どう` is an adverb and cannot sit in front of a noun. To ask "what kind of", you
  need `どんな`.

- ❌ 駅(えき)まで<b>いくら</b>かかりますか。（asking about *time*）→ ✅ 駅(えき)まで<b>どのくらい</b>かかりますか。
  `いくら` asks about money or quantity. For duration and distance the word is
  `どのくらい`／`どれくらい`. With <code>かかる</code> both are possible, so state
  which you mean.

- ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、<b>なんで</b>この案(あん)にしたんですか。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、<b>どうして</b>この案(あん)にされたんですか。
  Not a grammar error but a register one: `なんで` to a superior sounds like a child
  demanding an explanation.

**1.** ____がこのケーキを作(つく)りましたか。

"Who made this cake?"

The missing element is the subject, so the question word goes straight into the
subject slot with `が` - not `は`, because an unknown person cannot be a known
topic.

</details>

**2.** 週末(しゅうまつ)は（どう・どんな）でしたか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

週末(しゅうまつ)は<b>どう</b>でしたか。
"How was your weekend?"

`どんな` cannot stand alone in front of `です`; it always needs a noun after it. To
use `どんな` here you would have to say
<code>どんな週末(しゅうまつ)でしたか</code>, which asks what sort of weekend it was

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, formally, for a written report: "Why did the number of customers fall?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

`なぜ` is the register the task asks for - `どうして` would be acceptable but plainer,
and `なんで` would be wrong in a report. <code>のでしょうか</code> keeps the tone
measured rather than accusatory.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: すみません、この荷物(にもつ)は何(なに)ですか。何(なん)を入(い)れましたか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ すみません、この荷物(にもつ)は<b>何(なん)</b>ですか。<b>何(なに)</b>を入(い)れましたか。
"Excuse me, what is this parcel? What did you put in it?"

The two readings are swapped. `です` triggers なん; `を` triggers なに. The written
sentence looks identical either way, so only reading it aloud exposes the error.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) <b>何(なん)で</b>東京(とうきょう)へ行(い)きますか。
(b) <b>何(なに)で</b>東京(とうきょう)へ行(い)きますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

They are the same three characters and differ only in reading. (a), read なんで,
asks *why* you are going to Tokyo - it is the casual equivalent of `どうして`.

(b), read なにで, asks *by what means* - train, plane, car. Because writing cannot
distinguish them, careful writers avoid <code>何(なん)で</code> altogether and use
`どうして` for the reason and
<code>何(なに)に乗(の)って</code> for the method.

</details>

# 何か・誰か・どこか

**Meaning** · something / someone / somewhere - an unspecified one of a kind, rather than a request to identify it

Add `か` to a question word and it stops asking and starts referring. <code>何(なに)</code>
demands "what?"; <code>何(なに)か</code> says "some thing or other, and I am not
saying which - possibly because I do not know, possibly because it does not matter".
The reading is **なにか**, not なんか.

## Formation

The `か` fuses onto the question word. What comes after it is an ordinary predicate.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 何(なに) | 何(なに) + か → 何(なに)か - something, anything | 何(なに)か食(た)べる? |
| 誰(だれ) | 誰(だれ) + か → 誰(だれ)か - someone（polite どなたか） | 誰(だれ)か呼(よ)んでください |
| どこ | どこ + か → どこか - somewhere（polite どちらか） | どこかで休(やす)みましょう |
| いつ | いつ + か → いつか - sometime, one day | いつか話(はな)します |
| どれ | どれ + か → どれか - one of them（three or more） | どれか選(えら)んでください |
| どちら | どちら + か → どちらか - one of the two | どちらかに決(き)めてください |
| Particles が・を・は | **dropped** | ❌何(なに)かを買(か)う → ✅何(なに)か買(か)う |
| Particles に・で・へ・と・から | **kept, after the か** | 誰(だれ)かに聞(き)く／どこかで会(あ)った |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a `か`-word makes the sentence a **yes/no** question, not a
content question. <code>何(なに)か買(か)いましたか</code> is answered はい or
いいえ. <code>何(なに)を買(か)いましたか</code> demands a list of purchases.
Same English word "what", opposite conversations.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
<b>何(なに)か</b>飲(の)みますか。
Would you like something to drink?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>誰(だれ)か</b>いますか。
Is anybody there?
</pre>

<pre>
冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)に<b>何(なに)か</b>残(のこ)っていますか。
Is there anything left in the fridge?
</pre>

<pre>
週末(しゅうまつ)、<b>どこか</b>へ行(い)きませんか。
Shall we go somewhere at the weekend?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>誰(だれ)か</b>に道(みち)を聞(き)いたほうがいいですね。
We had better ask somebody for directions.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>いつか</b>北海道(ほっかいどう)で馬(うま)に乗(の)ってみたい。
One day I want to try riding a horse in Hokkaido.
</pre>

<pre>
さっきから<b>どこか</b>で電話(でんわ)が鳴(な)っているが、誰(だれ)も出(で)ない。
A phone has been ringing somewhere for a while now, but nobody is picking up.
</pre>

<pre>
会議(かいぎ)の前(まえ)に<b>何(なに)か</b>確認(かくにん)しておきたいことはありますか。
Is there anything you would like to check before the meeting?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and very high frequency in speech, largely because Japanese
prefers a vague offer to a specific one.

**The particle drop is the natural form.** <code>何(なに)かを食(た)べる</code>
is not exactly ungrammatical, but it is stiff and rarely said;
<code>何(なに)か食(た)べる</code> is what people actually produce. The same goes
for `が` - <code>誰(だれ)か来(き)たよ</code>, not
<code>誰(だれ)かが来(き)たよ</code> in ordinary speech, although the `が` version
does appear in writing when the subject needs emphasis. `は` never attaches at all,
because `は` announces a known topic and a `か`-word is by definition unknown.

**Vagueness is politeness.** Offering <code>何(なに)か飲(の)みますか</code> lets
the guest steer, whereas naming a drink pushes them into accepting or refusing that
drink. The same softening drives <code>どこか行(い)きたいところある?</code> when
planning a day out.

**`なんか` is a different word.** Written the same way in kanji, but `なんか`
functions as "or something / like / kind of":
<code>コーヒーなんかどうですか</code> ("how about a coffee or something?"), or as pure
filler - <code>なんか、疲(つか)れた</code>. It is casual and, used as filler,
slightly juvenile. Do not read <code>何(なに)か</code> as なんか.

**<code>何(なに)か</code> can also mean "somehow, somewhat"** in front of an
adjective: <code>何(なに)かおかしい</code>, "something's not right". No noun is
being referred to at all.

**Polite upgrades** exist and are worth having: `どなたか` for
<code>誰(だれ)か</code>, and `どちらか` for `どこか` when you mean a place. On the
phone: <code>どなたかいらっしゃいますか</code>.

## Contrast

### 何(なに)か・誰(だれ)か・どこか vs 何(なに)も・誰(だれ)も・どこも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `か` is the positive indefinite and works with any predicate;
`も` is the negative one and **requires** a negative verb. They are a matched pair,
and a question in `か` is usually answered in `も`.

</div>

This is the pairing to drill, because the two sets are one syllable apart and mean
opposite things.

<pre>
昼(ひる)ごはん、<b>何(なに)か</b>食(た)べましたか。
Did you eat anything for lunch?
</pre>

<pre>
いいえ、いそがしくて何(なに)も食(た)べませんでした。
No - I was busy and didn't eat anything.
</pre>

Note that the も-answer cannot be positive: ❌<code>何(なに)も食(た)べました</code>
is broken Japanese. And the か-question cannot be negated into a "nothing":
❌<code>誰(だれ)か来(き)ませんでした</code> does not mean "nobody came" - for that
you need <code>誰(だれ)も来(こ)ませんでした</code>.

### 何(なに)か食(た)べましたか vs 何(なに)を食(た)べましたか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with `か` you are asking **whether**; with `を` you are asking
**what**. The first expects はい／いいえ, the second expects a noun.

</div>

English "did you eat something / anything" versus "what did you eat" makes the same
split, but learners import the `を` version by default and end up interrogating
people.

<pre>
朝(あさ)、<b>何(なに)か</b>飲(の)みましたか。
Did you have anything to drink this morning?
</pre>

<pre>
朝(あさ)、何(なに)を飲(の)みましたか。
What did you drink this morning?
</pre>

The second presupposes that you drank; it is fine between friends and pushy from a
stranger. Use the `か` form when you do not yet know whether the event happened at
all.

### 何(なに)か vs 何(なん)でも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `か` means "some one thing, unspecified". `でも` means "any at
all, take your pick". `か` narrows to one; `でも` opens the whole set.

</div>

Both land on English "anything", which hides the difference completely.

<pre>
<b>何(なに)か</b>質問(しつもん)はありますか。
Are there any questions?
</pre>

<pre>
何(なん)でも聞(き)いてください。
Ask me anything you like.
</pre>

The first asks whether at least one question exists. The second grants permission
across the entire range. So <code>誰(だれ)か手伝(てつだ)って</code> means "could
someone help", while <code>誰(だれ)でも手伝(てつだ)える</code> means "anyone at
all is able to help".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ reading <code>何(なに)か</code> as **なんか** → ✅ **なにか**
  `なんか` is a separate colloquial word meaning "or something / kind of". The
  indefinite pronoun is なにか, always.

- ❌ <b>誰(だれ)か</b>来(き)ませんでした。 → ✅ <b>誰(だれ)も</b>来(こ)ませんでした。
  Negating a `か`-word does not give you "nobody". The か-sentence would mean
  something closer to "it wasn't the case that someone came", which is not what you
  want; switch to the も-series.

- ❌ <b>誰(だれ)かは</b>この漢字(かんじ)が読(よ)めますか。 → ✅ <b>誰(だれ)か</b>この漢字(かんじ)が読(よ)めますか。
  `は` marks a known topic and cannot attach to an indefinite. `が` and `を` are also
  dropped after a か-word.

- ❌ <b>いつかに</b>食事(しょくじ)でもしましょう。 → ✅ <b>いつか</b>食事(しょくじ)でもしましょう。
  `いつか` is already adverbial, like `いつ` itself. Time words of this kind take no
  `に`.

## Practice

**1.** 部屋(へや)が暗(くら)いですね。____電気(でんき)をつけてくれませんか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>誰(だれ)か</b>電気(でんき)をつけてくれませんか。
"Could somebody turn the light on?"

You are addressing a room rather than a named person, so you need the indefinite
"someone". No particle follows `か` here, because the subject marker `が` drops.

</details>

**2.** 夏休(なつやす)みに（どこか・どこも）旅行(りょこう)に行(い)きたいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

夏休(なつやす)みに<b>どこか</b>旅行(りょこう)に行(い)きたいです。

The predicate is positive - you *want* to go - so the か-series is required. `どこも`
only works with a negative, and would give "I don't want to travel anywhere", the
opposite meaning.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I think I've met him somewhere before."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

彼(かれ)には前(まえ)に<b>どこか</b>で会(あ)ったことがあると思(おも)います。

`どこか` supplies the vague place, and `で` stays on because meeting is an action
that happens somewhere. Note the particle sits *after* the `か`, never before it.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: すみません、何(なに)かをお願(ねが)いしたいことがあるんですが。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ すみません、<b>何(なに)か</b>お願(ねが)いしたいことがあるんですが。
"Excuse me, there's something I'd like to ask you to do."

The `を` after `か` has to go. Object and subject particles are dropped after a
か-word; only `に`, `で`, `へ`, `と` and `から` survive.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 週末(しゅうまつ)、<b>何(なに)か</b>しましたか。
(b) 週末(しゅうまつ)、何(なに)をしましたか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a yes/no question: did anything happen at all? It leaves the listener free to
say 「いいえ、何(なに)もしませんでした」without feeling they have failed to answer.

(b) assumes the weekend contained activities and asks for them by name. Between
friends that is perfectly friendly; asked of someone you barely know it can feel
like it is prying. This is why (a) is the safer opener with a new colleague.

</details>

# 何も・誰も・どこも

**Meaning** · nothing / no one / nowhere - a question word plus `も`, finished off with a **negative** verb

Japanese builds "nothing" out of two halves. <code>何(なに)も</code> supplies the
"any", and the negative ending on the verb supplies the "not". Neither half works
alone: <code>何(なに)も食(た)べません</code> is "I don't eat anything", while
❌<code>何(なに)も食(た)べます</code> is not a sentence. English puts the negation
in the pronoun; Japanese puts it in the verb, every time.

## Formation

The negative sits on the predicate at the end of the clause, however far away that
is.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 何(なに) | 何(なに) + も + **negative** - nothing | 何(なに)も見(み)えない |
| 誰(だれ) | 誰(だれ) + も + **negative** - no one | 誰(だれ)も知(し)らない |
| どこ | どこ + も + **negative** - nowhere | どこも開(あ)いていない |
| どれ | どれ + も + **negative** - none of them | どれも気(き)に入(い)らない |
| Particles が・を・は | **dropped** | ❌何(なに)もを → ✅何(なに)も |
| Particles に・で・へ・と | **inserted before the も** | 誰(だれ)<b>に</b>も／どこ<b>へ</b>も／誰(だれ)<b>と</b>も |
| Emphatic | 何(なに)も → 何(なん)にも（なんにも） | 何(なん)にも残(のこ)っていない |
| Positive predicate | どこも／どれも + affirmative = **every** | どこも混(こ)んでいる |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the particle goes **inside**, not outside. It is
<code>誰(だれ)にも言(い)わない</code>, not ❌<code>誰(だれ)もに言(い)わない</code>.
Think of it as `question word + particle + も`, with `が`, `を` and `は` simply
deleted at that first slot.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
朝(あさ)から<b>何(なに)も</b>食(た)べていません。
I haven't eaten anything since this morning.
</pre>

<pre>
教室(きょうしつ)には<b>誰(だれ)も</b>いません。
There is no one in the classroom.
</pre>

<pre>
昨日(きのう)は<b>どこへも</b>行(い)きませんでした。
I didn't go anywhere yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
店(みせ)に入(はい)ったが、<b>何(なに)も</b>買(か)わなかった。
I went into the shop but didn't buy anything.
</pre>

<pre>
この話(はなし)は<b>誰(だれ)にも</b>言(い)わないでください。
Please don't tell anyone about this.
</pre>

<pre>
三(さん)着(ちゃく)試(ため)してみたが、<b>どれも</b>サイズが合(あ)わなかった。
I tried three of them on, but none of them fitted.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>誰(だれ)も</b>手(て)を上(あ)げなかったので、先生(せんせい)は困(こま)った顔(かお)をしていた。
Nobody put their hand up, so the teacher looked stuck.
</pre>

<pre>
連休(れんきゅう)中(ちゅう)は<b>どこも</b>人(ひと)でいっぱいだから、家(いえ)でゆっくりしようと思(おも)う。
Everywhere is packed over the long weekend, so I think I'll take it easy at home.
</pre>

<pre>
引(ひ)っ越(こ)してきたばかりで、この町(まち)には知(し)り合(あ)いが<b>誰(だれ)も</b>いない。
I've only just moved here, so I don't know a soul in this town.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Neutral in register; what varies is force.

**`なんにも` is the emphatic version.** Written
<code>何(なん)にも</code>, it is <code>何(なに)も</code> with feeling - 
<code>冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)に何(なん)にもない</code> means the fridge is *completely*
empty. Spoken, slightly whiny, very common. Its counterpart for people is
<code>誰(だれ)一人(ひとり)…ない</code>, which is the *formal* intensifier:
<code>誰(だれ)一人(ひとり)反対(はんたい)しなかった</code>, "not a single person
objected".

**Not every も-word is negative.** This is the trap. `いつも` means "always" and
takes a positive verb. `どこも` and `どれも` swing both ways: with a negative they
mean "nowhere" and "none", with a positive they mean "everywhere" and "every one of
them" - <code>どれもおいしかった</code>, "they were all delicious". Only
<code>何(なに)も</code> is reliably negative-only.
<code>誰(だれ)も</code> is negative in ordinary sentences, but
<code>誰(だれ)もが</code>, with `が` added, flips to "everyone" and belongs to
formal writing: <code>誰(だれ)もが知(し)っている話(はなし)</code>.

**It is the standard answer to a `か`-question.** 「何(なに)か買(か)った?」
「ううん、何(なに)も」 - the negative verb can even be left off entirely when the
context supplies it, and trailing off after <code>何(なに)も</code> is completely natural in
speech.

**Word order is flexible but the negative is not.** You can move
<code>誰(だれ)にも</code> around the sentence freely; what you cannot do is let the
verb come out positive.

## Contrast

### 何(なに)も・誰(だれ)も・どこも vs 何(なに)か・誰(だれ)か・どこか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `か` is the positive indefinite, `も` the negative one. `か`
sentences end in a positive verb; `も` sentences end in a negative one.

</div>

They are the same nine question words wearing opposite hats, and a `か`-question is
normally answered with a `も`-sentence.

<pre>
部屋(へや)に誰(だれ)かいますか。
Is there anybody in the room?
</pre>

<pre>
いいえ、<b>誰(だれ)も</b>いません。
No, there's no one.
</pre>

The two cannot be crossed. ❌<code>誰(だれ)かいません</code> does not mean "there is
nobody" - it is at best a strange way of denying that someone is present. And
❌<code>誰(だれ)もいます</code> is simply ungrammatical.

### 何(なに)も vs 何(なん)でも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `も` with a negative = "not any". `でも` with a positive =
"any at all, whichever". One closes the set, the other throws it open.

</div>

English "anything" covers both, so the two get swapped constantly.

<pre>
昨日(きのう)は<b>何(なに)も</b>しませんでした。
I did nothing yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
明日(あした)なら何(なん)でもできます。
Tomorrow I can do anything you like.
</pre>

The same split runs through the whole set: <code>誰(だれ)も来(こ)ない</code>
("nobody comes") versus <code>誰(だれ)でも来(こ)られる</code> ("anybody can
come"); <code>どこにも売(う)っていない</code> ("it isn't sold anywhere") versus
<code>どこでも売(う)っている</code> ("it's sold everywhere").

### どこも with a negative vs どこも with a positive

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `どこも` and `どれも` take their meaning from the verb.
Negative verb → "nowhere / none". Positive verb → "everywhere / all of them".

</div>

This is the one place where the "も means negative" shortcut misleads you, and the
resulting misreadings are total reversals of meaning.

<pre>
正月(しょうがつ)は<b>どこも</b>開(あ)いていなかった。
Nothing was open over New Year.
</pre>

<pre>
正月(しょうがつ)は<b>どこも</b>静(しず)かだった。
Everywhere was quiet over New Year.
</pre>

<code>何(なに)も</code> does not behave this way - it stays negative - and
<code>誰(だれ)も</code> only turns positive in the fixed written pattern
<code>誰(だれ)もが</code>. So the swing is a property of `どこも` and `どれも`
specifically. Read to the end of the sentence before deciding what a も-word means.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 昨日(きのう)は<b>何(なに)も</b>食(た)べました。 → ✅ 昨日(きのう)は<b>何(なに)も</b>食(た)べませんでした。
  <code>何(なに)も</code> carries no negation of its own. The verb has to supply it.

- ❌ パーティーで<b>誰(だれ)もと</b>話(はな)さなかった。 → ✅ パーティーで<b>誰(だれ)とも</b>話(はな)さなかった。
  The particle goes between the question word and `も`, not after it. Likewise
  <code>誰(だれ)にも</code>, <code>どこへも</code>, <code>どこでも</code>.

- ❌ 夏休(なつやす)みは<b>どこも</b>行(い)きませんでした。 → ✅ 夏休(なつやす)みは<b>どこへも</b>（or <b>どこにも</b>）行(い)きませんでした。
  With a verb of motion the destination particle is required. Bare `どこも` before
  <code>行(い)く</code> is heard casually but reads as sloppy, and it collides with
  the "everywhere" sense.

- ❌ お皿(さら)に<b>何(なに)もを</b>残(のこ)さないでください。 → ✅ お皿(さら)に<b>何(なに)も</b>残(のこ)さないでください。
  `を`, `が` and `は` are all deleted after a も-word. Only `に`, `で`, `へ` and `と`
  survive, and they go inside.

- ❌ reading <code>何(なに)も</code> as **なんも** → ✅ **なにも**
  `も` is not in the た／だ／な rows, so <code>何(なに)</code> keeps its なに
  reading. `なんも` exists in some regional speech but is not standard.

## Practice

**1.** 財布(さいふ)を開(あ)けたが、中(なか)には____入(はい)っていなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

中(なか)には<b>何(なに)も</b>入(はい)っていなかった。
"I opened my wallet, but there was nothing in it."

The verb is already negative, so the slot needs the も-series, not the か-series.
<code>何(なに)か</code> here would produce a strange denial rather than "nothing".

</details>

**2.** すみません、この質問(しつもん)について（誰(だれ)か・誰(だれ)も）知(し)っている人(ひと)はいませんか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>誰(だれ)か</b>知(し)っている人(ひと)はいませんか。

Although the sentence ends in a negative, that negative belongs to
<code>いませんか</code>, which is a polite *invitation* - "isn't there anyone who…?"
You are hoping the answer is yes, so the positive indefinite `か` is correct.
<code>誰(だれ)も</code> would flatly assert that no such person exists.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Don't say anything to anyone."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>誰(だれ)にも何(なに)も</b>言(い)わないでください。

Two も-words can share one negative verb without any conflict - Japanese has no
double-negative rule. `に` sits inside <code>誰(だれ)にも</code> because it marks
the person spoken to, while <code>何(なに)も</code> drops its `を`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 連休(れんきゅう)に温泉(おんせん)へ行(い)ったが、どれもホテルが空(あ)いていました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 連休(れんきゅう)に温泉(おんせん)へ行(い)ったが、<b>どのホテルも</b>空(あ)いていませんでした。
"I went to a hot spring over the long weekend, but none of the hotels had rooms."

Two problems. `どれも` cannot be followed by the noun it refers to - attach `も` to
the noun instead: <code>どのホテルも</code>. And the intended meaning is "none", so
the verb must be negative; as written, the sentence claimed every hotel was free.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 店(みせ)は<b>どこも</b>込(こ)んでいた。
(b) 店(みせ)は<b>どこも</b>込(こ)んでいなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) has a positive verb, so `どこも` reads as "every one of them": every shop was
crowded. (b) has a negative verb, so the same `どこも` reads as "not any of them":
no shop was crowded anywhere.

The two sentences are identical up to the ending, which is why you must read a
も-word all the way to the verb before deciding what it means. With
<code>何(なに)も</code> this ambiguity never arises - it is negative-only.

</details>
