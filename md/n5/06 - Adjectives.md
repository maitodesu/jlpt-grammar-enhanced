# い-adjective conjugation

**Meaning** · how い-adjectives change for negative, past, and linking - 
くない, かった, くなかった, くて

An い-adjective is a word like 高(たか)い, 新(あたら)しい or
楽(たの)しい: it ends in い in its dictionary form, and - this is the point - 
it **conjugates by itself**. It does not lean on the copula the way a noun does.
高(たか)い carries its own past tense, 高(たか)かった, with no `でした`
anywhere in sight. Learners who treat an い-adjective like a noun produce
❌高(たか)いでした, and it is the single most recognisable beginner error in
the language.

## Formation

Chop the final い off and replace it. Everything below is one operation on that
stem.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective (plain non-past) | ～い | 安(やす)い |
| い-adjective (polite non-past) | ～い + です | 安(やす)いです |
| い-adjective (negative) | い → くない | 安(やす)くない |
| い-adjective (past) | い → かった | 安(やす)かった |
| い-adjective (past negative) | い → くなかった | 安(やす)くなかった |
| い-adjective (て-form) | い → くて | 安(やす)くて |
| い-adjective (adverb) | い → く | 安(やす)く買(か)う |
| い-adjective (before a noun) | ～い + Noun | 安(やす)い店(みせ) |

Politeness is added afterwards, by putting `です` on the finished form - it never
changes the form itself:

| Plain | Polite (everyday) | Polite (formal) |
|---|---|---|
| 寒(さむ)い | 寒(さむ)いです | 寒(さむ)いです |
| 寒(さむ)くない | 寒(さむ)くないです | 寒(さむ)くありません |
| 寒(さむ)かった | 寒(さむ)かったです | 寒(さむ)かったです |
| 寒(さむ)くなかった | 寒(さむ)くなかったです | 寒(さむ)くありませんでした |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the past tense lives **inside the adjective**, never on
`です`. 寒(さむ)かったです is correct; ❌寒(さむ)いでした is not. `です`
here is a politeness marker only, and it does not inflect.

</div>

**いい is irregular.** It is the one い-adjective that does not conjugate on its
own stem: every form except the dictionary form is built on its older shape
**よい**.

| Form | いい / よい |
|---|---|
| Plain non-past | いい（よい） |
| Negative | よくない |
| Past | よかった |
| Past negative | よくなかった |
| て-form | よくて |
| Adverb | よく |
| Polite past | よかったです |

There is no ❌いくない and no ❌いかった. The irregularity spreads to every
compound built on いい: かっこいい → かっこよかった, ちょうどいい →
ちょうどよくない, 都合(つごう)がいい → 都合(つごう)がよかった.

<div class="note">

**Note** - a handful of words end in い but are **not** い-adjectives. きれい,
有名(ゆうめい), 嫌(きら)い are な-adjectives; the negative of きれい is
きれいじゃない, not ❌きれくない. One partial test: a genuine い-adjective never
ends in **えい**, so きれい and 有名(ゆうめい) are ruled out on sight.
嫌(きら)い ends in あい and passes the test, so that one simply has to be
memorised. Going the other way, 大(おお)きい and 小(ちい)さい really are
い-adjectives, though they also have the special prenominal forms
大(おお)きな and 小(ちい)さな.

</div>

The summer holidays were so long when I was a child, and now I only get a week off.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

い-adjectives are register-neutral in themselves; the register is set by what
you put after them. Plain 寒(さむ)い to a friend, 寒(さむ)いです to
anyone else.

The negative has a genuine register split that learners are rarely told about.
`～くないです` and `～くありません` mean the same thing, but `～くありません`
sounds noticeably more formal and a little stiff - it belongs to announcements,
written notices, and careful speech. In ordinary conversation
高(たか)くないです is what people actually say. The past is more lopsided
still: 高(たか)くなかったです is normal speech, while
高(たか)くありませんでした sounds like a customer-service script.

A bare い-adjective is a complete sentence, and Japanese uses them that way
constantly: 寒(さむ)い! 痛(いた)い! おいしい! Adding `です` to
these exclamations makes them oddly formal - nobody yelps ❌痛(いた)いです
when they stub a toe.

Two things to be careful of:

- `です` after an い-adjective is **not** the copula doing its usual work; it is
  purely a politeness ending. That is why it cannot inflect for tense, and why
  ❌高(たか)いでした fails.
- Adjectives describing another person's inner state - 寂(さび)しい,
  欲(ほ)しい, 痛(いた)い - cannot be asserted flatly about a third person.
  You need そう, ようだ, or `～がる`: 弟(おとうと)は寂(さび)しがっている.

Finally, `～く` is the adverb, and it does far more work than English learners
expect: 早(はや)く起(お)きる "get up early", 大(おお)きく書(か)く
"write it big", よく寝(ね)た "slept well".

## Contrast

### い-adjective vs な-adjective

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - an い-adjective inflects **itself**; a な-adjective leaves
the work to the copula. So the tense marker moves: 高(たか)かった against
静(しず)かだった.

</div>

<pre>
去年(きょねん)のアパートは<b>狭(せま)かった</b>。
Last year's flat was cramped.
</pre>

<pre>
去年(きょねん)のアパートは<b>静(しず)かだった</b>。
Last year's flat was quiet.
</pre>

Both sentences are past tense; the machinery differs completely. In the first,
い became かった inside the word. In the second, the adjective did not change at
all and `だ` became `だった`. This is why the negatives are also
狭(せま)くない against 静(しず)かじゃない - and why mixing them up gives
❌狭(せま)いじゃない or ❌静(しず)かくない.

### ～く vs ～に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both make an adverb, but the ending follows the adjective's
class: い-adjectives take **く**, な-adjectives take **に**.

</div>

<pre>
字(じ)をもっと<b>大(おお)きく</b>書(か)いてください。
Please write the characters bigger.
</pre>

<pre>
もう少(すこ)し<b>静(しず)かに</b>話(はな)してください。
Please speak a bit more quietly.
</pre>

The same split covers "becoming": 暖(あたた)かくなる against
元気(げんき)になる, and "making": 短(みじか)くする against きれいにする.
Get into the habit of storing every new adjective together with its adverb
form - it removes the guesswork permanently.

### ～くないです vs ～くありません

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning. `くないです` is conversational,
`くありません` is formal and a bit starchy. Neither is wrong; the mismatch is
with the situation, not the grammar.

</div>

<pre>
この問題(もんだい)はそんなに<b>難(むずか)しくないです</b>。
This question isn't all that hard. (talking to a classmate)
</pre>

<pre>
当店(とうてん)のサービスは決(けっ)して<b>高(たか)くありません</b>。
Our service is by no means expensive. (a shop's notice)
</pre>

The rule extends to the past: `くなかったです` in speech,
`くありませんでした` in formal contexts. If you only want to remember one
column, remember the `です` one - it is never wrong, only occasionally casual.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 寒(さむ)いでした → ✅ <b>寒(さむ)かったです</b>
  The past tense goes inside the adjective. `です` never inflects after an
  い-adjective.

- ❌ 寒(さむ)いじゃない → ✅ <b>寒(さむ)くない</b>
  `じゃない` is the negative for nouns and な-adjectives. い-adjectives swap い
  for くない.

- ❌ いくない／いかった → ✅ <b>よくない</b>／<b>よかった</b>
  いい is irregular: everything except the dictionary form is built on よ. The
  same applies to かっこいい → かっこよかった.

- ❌ 新(あたら)しいの車(くるま) → ✅ <b>新(あたら)しい車(くるま)</b>
  An い-adjective attaches straight to the noun. No `の`, and no `な` either.

- ❌ 高(たか)いくない → ✅ <b>高(たか)くない</b>
  Remove the い before adding くない. Keeping both is the same slip as
  ❌食(た)べるない with verbs.

## Practice

**1.** 昨日(きのう)のパーティーはとても楽(たの)し____です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

楽(たの)<b>しかった</b>です

昨日(きのう) puts the sentence in the past, and an い-adjective carries its
own past tense: い → かった. `です` is then added unchanged, purely for
politeness.

</details>

**2.** この部屋(へや)は（きれくない・きれいじゃない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この部屋(へや)は<b>きれいじゃない</b>。

きれい ends in い but is a な-adjective, so it negates with `じゃない`. The
giveaway is that it ends in **えい**, a shape no genuine い-adjective has.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The film wasn't interesting, but the music was good."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

映画(えいが)は<b>面白(おもしろ)くなかった</b>けど、音楽(おんがく)は<b>よかった</b>です。

Two い-adjectives, both carrying their own tense. Note that いい becomes
よかった - never ❌いかった - and that only the last clause takes `です`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この町(まち)は昔(むかし)、あまり大(おお)きいじゃなかったです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この町(まち)は昔(むかし)、あまり<b>大(おお)きくなかった</b>です。

大(おお)きい is an い-adjective despite looking like it might be a
な-adjective, so the past negative is built by stacking い-adjective endings:
い → くない → くなかった.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この辺(へん)は夜(よる)、あまり明(あか)るくないです。
(b) この辺(へん)は夜(よる)、あまり明(あか)るくありません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The meaning is the same: neither area is well lit. What differs is how formal
the speaker is being. (a) is the everyday polite negative - what you would say
to a neighbour or a colleague.

(b) is the older, more formal construction. It sounds appropriate in a written
notice, a news report, or an official explanation, and slightly starchy in
casual conversation.

</details>

# な-adjective conjugation

**Meaning** · how な-adjectives work - the な before a noun, and じゃない,
だった, で

A な-adjective is a word like 静(しず)か, 便利(べんり), 好(す)き or
きれい. The name comes from the one place it differs visibly from a noun: put it
in front of a noun and it needs a な: <b>静(しず)かな町(まち)</b> "a
quiet town".

The important structural fact is what な-adjectives *don't* do: they do not
conjugate. The word itself never changes. All the tense and negation happens on
the copula behind it - 静(しず)かだ → 静(しず)かだった →
静(しず)かじゃない. If you can handle 学生(がくせい)だった, you can
already handle 静(しず)かだった, because they are the same machinery.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| な-adjective (before a noun) | ～な + Noun | 便利(べんり)な店(みせ) |
| な-adjective (plain non-past) | ～だ | 便利(べんり)だ |
| な-adjective (polite non-past) | ～です | 便利(べんり)です |
| な-adjective (negative) | ～じゃない／ではない | 便利(べんり)じゃない |
| な-adjective (past) | ～だった | 便利(べんり)だった |
| な-adjective (past negative) | ～じゃなかった | 便利(べんり)じゃなかった |
| な-adjective (て-form) | ～で | 便利(べんり)で |
| な-adjective (adverb) | ～に | 便利(べんり)になる |

Politeness, as with nouns, is a separate layer:

| Plain | Polite (everyday) | Polite (formal) |
|---|---|---|
| 元気(げんき)だ | 元気(げんき)です | 元気(げんき)です |
| 元気(げんき)じゃない | 元気(げんき)じゃないです | 元気(げんき)ではありません |
| 元気(げんき)だった | 元気(げんき)でした | 元気(げんき)でした |
| 元気(げんき)じゃなかった | 元気(げんき)じゃなかったです | 元気(げんき)ではありませんでした |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `な` appears **only** directly before a noun. Everywhere else
it is not there. So 静(しず)かな部屋(へや) but 部屋(へや)は
静(しず)かです - never ❌静(しず)かなです.

</div>

Set the two adjective classes side by side and the whole system falls out of one
question: does the word inflect, or does the copula do it for you?

| | い-adjective (高(たか)い) | な-adjective (静(しず)か) |
|---|---|---|
| Before a noun | 高(たか)い山(やま) | 静(しず)かな山(やま) |
| Plain non-past | 高(たか)い | 静(しず)かだ |
| Negative | 高(たか)くない | 静(しず)かじゃない |
| Past | 高(たか)かった | 静(しず)かだった |
| Past negative | 高(たか)くなかった | 静(しず)かじゃなかった |
| て-form | 高(たか)くて | 静(しず)かで |
| Adverb | 高(たか)く | 静(しず)かに |

<div class="note">

**Note** - な-adjectives behave almost exactly like nouns, and historically that
is what many of them are. 元気(げんき), 親切(しんせつ) and
健康(けんこう) can all appear as nouns in their own right. The **only**
systematic difference is what goes in front of a noun: a な-adjective takes
`な`, a noun takes `の`. 病気(びょうき)の人(ひと) "a sick person" (noun),
元気(げんき)な人(ひと) "a lively person" (な-adjective). Everything else - 
だ, だった, じゃない, で, でした - is shared.

</div>

Words that catch people out:

- Ending in い but な-adjectives: きれい, 嫌(きら)い, 有名(ゆうめい),
  嫌(いや), 得意(とくい). きれいじゃない, not ❌きれくない.
- 同(おな)じ is a な-adjective that drops the な before a noun:
  同(おな)じ人(ひと), never ❌同(おな)じな人(ひと). But the な comes
  back before ので and のに: 同(おな)じなので.
- 好(す)き, 嫌(きら)い, 上手(じょうず), 下手(へた) are adjectives in
  Japanese although they translate as English verbs, and their object takes
  `が`: 魚(さかな)が好(す)きです.

## Nuance & register

The word itself is register-neutral; the copula after it is where register
lives. `だ` is plain and slightly assertive, `です` is polite, and in casual
speech `だ` is very often dropped altogether:
この店(みせ)、静(しず)か - a complete, natural sentence. Women's casual
speech in particular tends to omit `だ`; keeping it can sound blunt or
declamatory.

The negative comes in three shapes, and they form a clear ladder:

- **じゃない** - casual, spoken. A contraction of ではない.
- **じゃないです／じゃありません** - polite, everyday.
- **ではありません／ではございません** - formal, written, and service Japanese.

`ではない` in speech sounds like a lecture or a debate; `じゃない` in a written
report looks careless. That mismatch, rather than any grammatical error, is what
makes learners' Japanese sound off.

One more thing worth knowing early: `じゃない` with rising intonation is a
tag question, not a negative - きれいじゃない？ means "isn't it pretty?", the
opposite of what the words look like. Context and intonation carry it.

Some な-adjectives are almost never used about oneself. Saying
わたしは上手(じょうず)です about your own Japanese is boastful; the modest
answer is まだまだです. And 大丈夫(だいじょうぶ) has drifted in modern
speech into a soft refusal - offered a plastic bag, 大丈夫(だいじょうぶ)です
means "no thanks", not "that's fine".

## Contrast

### な-adjective vs い-adjective - telling them apart

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ignore the last letter and look at the **negative**. If it
takes じゃない, it is a な-adjective. If it takes くない, it is an い-adjective.
The dictionary form lies; the negative never does.

</div>

<pre>
この部屋(へや)は<b>きれいじゃない</b>。
This room isn't clean.
</pre>

<pre>
この部屋(へや)は<b>汚(きたな)くない</b>。
This room isn't dirty.
</pre>

きれい and 汚(きたな)い both end in い, and they belong to different classes.
The reliable signal is the syllable before the final い: an い-adjective never
ends in **えい**, so きれい (kirei) and 有名(ゆうめい) (yuumei) give
themselves away. 嫌(きら)い does not, and just has to be learned. When you
meet a new adjective, learn it with its な attached - きれいな, 好(す)きな - 
and the problem disappears.

### な vs の before a noun

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - な-adjectives take `な`; nouns take `の`. Everything after
the noun is identical, so this one particle is the whole visible difference
between the two word classes.

</div>

<pre>
親切(しんせつ)な<b>店員(てんいん)</b>さんに道(みち)を教(おし)えてもらった。
A kind shop assistant gave me directions.
</pre>

<pre>
日本語(にほんご)の<b>先生(せんせい)</b>に手紙(てがみ)を書(か)きました。
I wrote a letter to my Japanese teacher.
</pre>

A few words sit in both camps and change meaning accordingly.
病気(びょうき)の人(ひと) is "a person with an illness" (noun +の);
元気(げんき)な人(ひと) is "a lively person" (な-adjective). If you are
unsure which class a word is in, `の` is the safer guess for anything that
names a thing, and `な` for anything that describes a quality.

### ～な vs ～に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `な` modifies a **noun**; `に` modifies a **verb**. Same
adjective, two different jobs.

</div>

<pre>
<b>静(しず)かな</b>人(ひと)が好(す)きです。
I like quiet people.
</pre>

<pre>
ドアを<b>静(しず)かに</b>閉(し)めてください。
Please close the door quietly.
</pre>

`に` is also the form used with なる and する:
上手(じょうず)になる "get good at something", きれいにする "make something
clean". This `に` is the な-adjective's equivalent of the
い-adjective's `く`, and the two never cross over:
❌静(しず)かくする, ❌安(やす)にする.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 静(しず)かの部屋(へや) → ✅ <b>静(しず)かな部屋(へや)</b>
  静(しず)か is a な-adjective, so it takes `な` before a noun. `の` is for
  nouns modifying nouns.

- ❌ きれいくない → ✅ <b>きれいじゃない</b>
  きれい ends in い but is a な-adjective. It negates with `じゃない`, like a
  noun.

- ❌ 元気(げんき)なです → ✅ <b>元気(げんき)です</b>
  The `な` only appears immediately before a noun. Before `です` there is
  nothing.

- ❌ 有名(ゆうめい)かった → ✅ <b>有名(ゆうめい)でした</b>／<b>有名(ゆうめい)だった</b>
  な-adjectives have no past tense of their own; the copula supplies it.
  `かった` belongs to い-adjectives.

- ❌ 上手(じょうず)に人(ひと) → ✅ <b>上手(じょうず)な人(ひと)</b>
  `に` goes with verbs, `な` with nouns. 上手(じょうず)に話(はな)す is
  fine; 上手(じょうず)な人(ひと) is the noun version.

## Practice

**1.** 田中(たなか)さんは____人(ひと)です。（しんせつ）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

田中(たなか)さんは<b>親切(しんせつ)な</b>人(ひと)です。

親切(しんせつ) is a な-adjective, and it is standing directly in front of the
noun 人(ひと), which is exactly the environment that requires `な`.

</details>

**2.** この問題(もんだい)は（簡単(かんたん)くない・簡単(かんたん)じゃない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この問題(もんだい)は<b>簡単(かんたん)じゃない</b>。

簡単(かんたん) is a な-adjective, so its negative is made with the copula:
じゃない (or the more formal ではありません). `くない` is reserved for
い-adjectives.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The hotel was clean and quiet, but it wasn't cheap."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ホテルは<b>きれいで</b>静(しず)かでしたが、安(やす)くなかったです。

Two な-adjectives linked by the て-form, which for a な-adjective is simply `で`.
Only the last one carries tense. The final adjective 安(やす)い is an
い-adjective, so it makes its own past negative.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 昨日(きのう)のコンサートはとてもすてきかったです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 昨日(きのう)のコンサートはとても<b>すてきでした</b>。

すてき is a な-adjective, and な-adjectives never take `かった`. The past is
carried by the copula: だった in plain style, でした in polite style.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 彼(かれ)はギターが上手(じょうず)です。
(b) 彼(かれ)はギターを上手(じょうず)に弾(ひ)きます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) uses 上手(じょうず) as an adjective describing him, so the guitar is
marked with `が` - it is a statement about what kind of player he is.

(b) uses the adverbial `に` form to describe how he performs the action of
playing, so the guitar returns to being an ordinary object with `を`. (a) is a
judgement about ability; (b) is a description of a performance.

</details>
