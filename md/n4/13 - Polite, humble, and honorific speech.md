# N4 - Polite, humble, and honorific speech


---

# お○○になる

**Meaning** · the general-purpose honorific frame - describes *someone else's* action while raising them

Japanese politeness runs on three separate systems. 丁寧(ていねい)語(ご) - 
です／ます - makes your *speech* polite to whoever is listening.
尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) raises the **person you are talking about**.
謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご) lowers **you**. `お○○になる` is the workhorse of the
second group: take almost any verb, and it becomes a respectful description of
what your customer, your teacher or your boss did.

## Formation

Take the ます-stem, put `お` in front and `になる` behind. `になる` then conjugates
as an ordinary う-verb (五段(ごだん), Group 1).

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | お + ます-stem + になる | 書(か)きます → お書(か)きになる |
| Verb (る-verb) | お + ます-stem + になる | 調(しら)べます → お調(しら)べになる |
| する-verb, Chinese-derived root | ご + noun + になる | 利用(りよう)する → ご利用(りよう)になる |
| する-verb, native root | お + noun + になる | 問(と)い合(あ)わせする → お問(と)い合(あ)わせになる |
| Polite / past | お + stem + になります／になりました | お使(つか)いになりました |
| Verb with its own honorific | frame **not** used | 食(た)べる → 召(め)し上(あ)がる |
| One-mora ます-stem | frame **not** used | 見(み)る → ご覧(らん)になる、寝(ね)る → お休(やす)みになる |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if the ます-stem is only one mora long
（見(み)、着(き)、寝(ね)、来(き)、し）, the frame will not carry it.
Those verbs all have a dedicated honorific word instead:
ご覧(らん)になる、お召(め)しになる、お休(やす)みになる、いらっしゃる、なさる.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
先生(せんせい)は何時(なんじ)に<b>お帰(かえ)りになります</b>か。
What time will you be leaving, sensei?
</pre>

<pre>
この本(ほん)をもう<b>お読(よ)みになりました</b>か。
Have you read this book already?
</pre>

<pre>
部長(ぶちょう)は昼(ひる)すぎに<b>お出(で)かけになりました</b>。
The department head went out in the early afternoon.
</pre>

<pre>
お客(きゃく)様(さま)が<b>お選(えら)びになった</b>のは、こちらの色(いろ)でよろしいでしょうか。
Is this the colour you chose, madam?
</pre>

<pre>
課長(かちょう)が<b>お決(き)めになった</b>方法(ほうほう)で進(すす)めます。
We'll go ahead with the method the section chief settled on.
</pre>

<pre>
エレベーターを<b>ご利用(りよう)になる</b>方(かた)は、右(みぎ)の通路(つうろ)へお回(まわ)りください。
Those using the lift, please go round to the right-hand corridor.
</pre>

<pre>
皆(みな)様(さま)が<b>お集(あつ)まりになる</b>時間(じかん)は午後(ごご)二時(にじ)でよろしいでしょうか。
Would two o'clock suit as the time for everyone to gather?
</pre>

<pre>
新(あたら)しい計画(けいかく)について、社長(しゃちょう)はどう<b>お考(かんが)えになっている</b>のでしょうか。
I wonder what the president makes of the new plan.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is business and service language. In a shop, a hospital, a hotel, a
会議(かいぎ)室(しつ) or a classroom it is completely ordinary; among friends
it is not. Saying お食(た)べになりますか to a classmate is not "extra polite",
it is strange - it puts a counter between the two of you. Overusing
敬語(けいご) socially is a real mistake, not a safe default.

Three things follow from the fact that `お○○になる` is
尊敬(そんけい)語(ご):

- **Never about yourself.** ❌私(わたし)がお作(つく)りになりました is the
  single commonest 敬語(けいご) error learners make. You cannot raise your own
  head. Your own actions take 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご) - お作(つく)りしました
 - or plain ます.
- **Never doubled.** ❌お読(よ)みになられる stacks the frame on top of the
  `られる` honorific. That is 二重(にじゅう)敬語(けいご), and it sounds
  overcooked rather than extra respectful. One layer is enough.
- **Not for your own side.** Speaking to a customer about a colleague, even your
  own boss, you drop the honorific:
  田中(たなか)はただいま席(せき)をはずしております - never
  お席(せき)をはずしになっています.

On the politeness ladder, `お○○になる` sits above `れる／られる` and below the set
honorific verbs. Treat that as a tendency rather than a measurement: in most
offices お書(か)きになる and 書(か)かれる are simply two acceptable choices,
and which one a person reaches for depends on the workplace and on how far above
them the other person stands.

## Contrast

### お○○になる vs ～れる／～られる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are honorific. `られる` is the light, everyday office
form; `お○○になる` is heavier, more deferential, and unambiguous.

</div>

<pre>
社長(しゃちょう)はもう<b>帰(かえ)られました</b>。
The president has already left for the day.
</pre>

<pre>
社長(しゃちょう)はもう<b>お帰(かえ)りになりました</b>。
The president has already left for the day.
</pre>

The second is the more respectful of the two, and it is what you would say to an
outside visitor; the first is what one colleague says to another. The practical
advantage of `お○○になる` is that it cannot be misread. <code>帰(かえ)られる</code> is also
the passive and the potential, so 先生(せんせい)に来(こ)られました can be
read two ways, while 先生(せんせい)がお越(こ)しになりました cannot.

### お○○になる vs お○○する

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same stem, opposite direction. `になる` raises the other
person; `する` lowers you.

</div>

<pre>
先生(せんせい)が荷物(にもつ)を<b>お持(も)ちになります</b>。
The teacher is going to carry the bags himself.
</pre>

<pre>
先生(せんせい)の荷物(にもつ)を<b>お持(も)ちします</b>。
I'll carry your bags for you, sensei.
</pre>

One says the teacher does the carrying; the other offers to do it for him.
Swapping them reverses who is being honoured, which is why the pair is worth
drilling until it is automatic.

### お○○になる vs the set honorific verbs

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if a verb owns a dedicated honorific word, the frame is
closed to it. Learn that short list; use the frame for everything else.

</div>

<pre>
先生(せんせい)はもうお昼(ひる)を<b>召(め)し上(あ)がりました</b>か。
Has sensei had lunch yet?
</pre>

<pre>
資料(しりょう)はもう<b>ご覧(らん)になりました</b>か。
Have you had a look at the documents yet?
</pre>

<code>食(た)べる</code>, <code>見(み)る</code>, <code>言(い)う</code>,
`する`, <code>行(い)く</code>, <code>来(く)る</code>, `いる` and
<code>寝(ね)る</code> all belong to that closed set. Note that
<code>ご覧(らん)になる</code> ends in `になる` but is not built from a ます-stem - 
<code>ご覧(らん)</code> is a noun, and the word has to be memorised whole.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 私(わたし)が資料(しりょう)を<b>お作(つく)りになりました</b>。 → ✅ 私(わたし)が資料(しりょう)を<b>お作(つく)りしました</b>。
  Honorific language points away from the speaker. Describing your own work with
  <code>お○○になる</code> is the error a Japanese listener notices fastest.

- ❌ 地図(ちず)を<b>お見(み)になりました</b>か。 → ✅ 地図(ちず)を<b>ご覧(らん)になりました</b>か。
  <code>見(み)る</code> has a one-mora stem and its own honorific verb. The
  frame does not build on it.

- ❌ 先生(せんせい)が<b>お話(はな)しになられました</b>。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)が<b>お話(はな)しになりました</b>。
  Two honorific layers on one verb is 二重(にじゅう)敬語(けいご). Pick one.

- ❌ 課長(かちょう)が<b>お説明(せつめい)になりました</b>。 → ✅ 課長(かちょう)が<b>ご説明(せつめい)になりました</b>。
  A Chinese-derived する-verb root takes `ご`: ご利用(りよう)、
  ご出発(しゅっぱつ)、ご記入(きにゅう).

- ❌ ねえ、この漫画(まんが)<b>お読(よ)みになった</b>? → ✅ ねえ、この漫画(まんが)読(よ)んだ?
  Grammatical, wrong for the situation. Full 敬語(けいご) aimed at a friend
  reads as sarcasm or as sudden distance.

## Practice

**1.** 先生(せんせい)は毎朝(まいあさ)新聞(しんぶん)を____読(よ)____になります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

先生(せんせい)は毎朝(まいあさ)新聞(しんぶん)を<b>お読(よ)みになります</b>。

The frame is `お` + ます-stem + `になる`. The stem of
<code>読(よ)みます</code> is <code>読(よ)み</code>, so `お` goes in front of it
and `になります` behind.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 係(かかり)の者(もの)が（ご案内(あんない)になります・ご案内(あんない)します）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

係(かかり)の者(もの)が<b>ご案内(あんない)します</b>。

<code>係(かかり)の者(もの)</code> is a member of your own side, so the
sentence has to lower rather than raise. `ご○○になる` would honour your own staff
in front of a customer, which is exactly backwards.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "What time will the president be arriving?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

社長(しゃちょう)は何時(なんじ)に<b>お着(つ)きになります</b>か。

<code>着(つ)きます</code> gives the stem <code>着(つ)き</code>, hence
お着(つ)きになります. ご到着(とうちゃく)になりますか works too, since
到着(とうちゃく) is a Chinese-derived root and takes `ご`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 明日(あした)、私(わたし)は十時(じゅうじ)にお越(こ)しになります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 明日(あした)、私(わたし)は十時(じゅうじ)に参(まい)ります。

Two faults in one clause. The verb describes you, so it needs
謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご), not 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご). And
<code>来(く)る</code> is outside the frame anyway: the honorific for someone
else coming is お越(こ)しになる or いらっしゃる, while the humble word for your
own coming is 参(まい)る.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 田中(たなか)様(さま)はいつ出発(しゅっぱつ)されますか。
(b) 田中(たなか)様(さま)はいつご出発(しゅっぱつ)になりますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are honorific and both are correct. (a) uses the `される` form, which is
light and quick - the register of a colleague, or of a routine business email.

(b) is the heavier frame, and it is what a hotel clerk or a receptionist would
say to a guest. It sounds more careful and more deliberate. The gap is one of
degree, not of grammar: choosing (a) with a customer would not offend anyone, it
would only sound a little brisk.

</details>

# お○○ください

**Meaning** · "please do ○○" - an honorific request, the form used to customers, guests and audiences

`お○○ください` asks the listener to do something while raising them. It is the
尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) counterpart of `～てください`: same job, but the listener
is honoured rather than simply addressed politely. Every announcement you hear on
a Japanese train, in a lift and in a shop is built out of this frame.

## Formation

`ください` is the imperative of `くださる`, the honorific of `くれる`. Put it after
`お` + the ます-stem.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | お + ます-stem + ください | 待(ま)ちます → お待(ま)ちください |
| Verb (る-verb) | お + ます-stem + ください | 調(しら)べます → お調(しら)べください |
| する-verb, Chinese-derived root | ご + noun + ください | 確認(かくにん)する → ご確認(かくにん)ください |
| する-verb, native root | お + noun + ください | 申(もう)し込(こ)みする → お申(もう)し込(こ)みください |
| Set honorific verb | fixed form | 見(み)る → ご覧(らん)ください |
| Extra-polite service form | + ませ | お待(ま)ちくださいませ |
| Negative ("please don't") | frame **not** used | ご遠慮(えんりょ)ください／～ないでください |

<div class="note">

**Note** - one-mora stems are shut out here for the same reason as in
`お○○になる`: ❌お見(み)ください、❌お来(き)ください do not exist. Use
ご覧(らん)ください and お越(こ)しください.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
こちらで<b>お待(ま)ちください</b>。
Please wait here.
</pre>

<pre>
どうぞ<b>おかけください</b>。
Please have a seat.
</pre>

<pre>
この線(せん)の内側(うちがわ)まで<b>お下(さ)がりください</b>。
Please step back behind this line.
</pre>

<pre>
温(あたた)かいうちに<b>お召(め)し上(あ)がりください</b>。
Please eat it while it's still warm.
</pre>

<pre>
足元(あしもと)に<b>ご注意(ちゅうい)ください</b>。
Please mind your step.
</pre>

<pre>
順番(じゅんばん)にご案内(あんない)しますので、こちらに<b>お並(なら)びください</b>。
We'll show you through in turn, so please queue here.
</pre>

<pre>
ご不明(ふめい)な点(てん)がございましたら、いつでも<b>お問(と)い合(あ)わせください</b>。
If anything is unclear, please get in touch at any time.
</pre>

<pre>
会場(かいじょう)にお入(はい)りの際(さい)は、受付(うけつけ)でこちらの用紙(ようし)を<b>お出(だ)しください</b>。
When you enter the hall, please hand this form in at reception.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This frame is service and announcement language, and it carries a faint flavour of
*instruction*. A station guard, a shop assistant, a nurse calling a patient and a
website's help page all use it. Between friends it is bizarre - you do not tell a
classmate ❌ちょっとお待(ま)ちください unless you are joking.

The instructional flavour matters when you want a personal favour. `ください` is
still an imperative underneath, so a bare お書(か)きください to your boss can
land like a directive from below. For asking a superior for something, shift to a
question form:

- お書(か)きいただけますか - "could you write it?"
- お書(か)きいただけませんでしょうか - the softest, and standard in email.
- 書(か)いていただけると助(たす)かります - "it would help me if you would."

`ませ` on the end（お待(ま)ちくださいませ、いらっしゃいませ）belongs to shop and
hotel speech specifically. It is warm and formal at once, and it is heard far more
from staff than from anyone else.

Two words about お召(め)し上(あ)がりください and お求(もと)めください.
Strictly, 召(め)し上(あ)がる is already honorific, so adding `お` makes a
double layer; usage guides nonetheless list these as established by custom, and
you will hear them constantly. Treat them as fixed phrases rather than as models
for building new ones.

One more tendency worth knowing: many speakers avoid お座(すわ)りください in
favour of おかけください, because お座(すわ)り on its own is what you say to a
dog. Plenty of hospitals and offices say お座(すわ)りください anyway, so it is a
preference rather than a rule - but おかけください is never the wrong choice.

## Contrast

### お○○ください vs ～てください

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～てください` is polite; `お○○ください` is polite **and**
honorific. The first addresses the listener courteously, the second raises them.

</div>

<pre>
ここに名前(なまえ)を<b>書(か)いてください</b>。
Please write your name here.
</pre>

<pre>
こちらにお名前(なまえ)を<b>お書(か)きください</b>。
Please write your name here.
</pre>

The first is what a teacher says to a class, or a friend to a friend. The second
is what a bank clerk says to a customer - notice how `ここ` becomes `こちら` and
名前(なまえ) takes its prefix. 敬語(けいご) rarely arrives alone; the whole
sentence shifts register together.

### お○○ください vs お○○いただけますか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ください` tells; `いただけますか` asks. When the favour is for
*your* benefit, ask.

</div>

<pre>
明日(あした)までに<b>ご返事(へんじ)ください</b>。
Please reply by tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
明日(あした)までに<b>ご返事(へんじ)いただけますでしょうか</b>。
Might I ask you to reply by tomorrow?
</pre>

Both are polite. The first states what needs to happen and suits a notice or a
form; the second leaves the decision with the listener and suits a request to a
client or a superior. `いただく` is 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご), so the second
sentence lowers you at the same time as it raises them - which is why it feels so
much softer.

### お○○ください vs ご遠慮(えんりょ)ください

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the frame has no negative. To ask someone *not* to do
something, use a different verb: ご遠慮(えんりょ)ください or
お控(ひか)えください.

</div>

<pre>
車内(しゃない)でのお電話(でんわ)は<b>ご遠慮(えんりょ)ください</b>。
Please refrain from making phone calls on board.
</pre>

<pre>
写真(しゃしん)の撮影(さつえい)は<b>お控(ひか)えください</b>。
Please refrain from taking photographs.
</pre>

There is no ❌お話(はな)しにならないでください in ordinary use. Japanese public
notices prefer to name a polite verb of refraining rather than to negate the
request - it reads as an appeal to the listener's discretion instead of a ban.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 明日(あした)会社(かいしゃ)に<b>お来(き)ください</b>。 → ✅ 明日(あした)会社(かいしゃ)に<b>お越(こ)しください</b>。
  <code>来(く)る</code> has no place in the frame. Use お越(こ)しください,
  おいでください or いらしてください.

- ❌ こちらのボタンを<b>ご確認(かくにん)してください</b>。 → ✅ こちらのボタンを<b>ご確認(かくにん)ください</b>。
  `ご○○する` is the *humble* frame, so <code>ご確認(かくにん)してください</code> tells the
  listener to humble themselves. Drop the `して`.

- ❌ メニューを<b>お見(み)ください</b>。 → ✅ メニューを<b>ご覧(らん)ください</b>。
  One-mora stem, dedicated honorific verb. The same goes for
  お食(た)べください → お召(め)し上(あ)がりください.

- ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、この書類(しょるい)に<b>おサインください</b>。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、この書類(しょるい)に<b>ご署名(しょめい)いただけますでしょうか</b>。
  Two problems: loanwords do not take the prefix, and a direct `ください` to your
  own boss for your own paperwork is too flat. Ask with `いただけますか`.

## Practice

**1.** 危(あぶ)ないですので、白線(はくせん)の内側(うちがわ)まで____下(さ)がり____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

危(あぶ)ないですので、白線(はくせん)の内側(うちがわ)まで<b>お下(さ)がりください</b>。

`お` + ます-stem + `ください`. <code>下(さ)がります</code> gives the stem
<code>下(さ)がり</code>. This is the standard platform announcement, and it is
honorific because it is addressed to passengers.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 資料(しりょう)を（ご覧(らん)ください・拝見(はいけん)ください）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

資料(しりょう)を<b>ご覧(らん)ください</b>。

<code>拝見(はいけん)する</code> is 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご) - it lowers the
person doing the looking. Telling the listener to 拝見(はいけん) orders them to
be humble towards you, which is the opposite of what a request should do.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Please use the lift on the left."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

左(ひだり)のエレベーターを<b>ご利用(りよう)ください</b>。

利用(りよう) is a Chinese-derived する-verb root, so it takes `ご` and the
`する` disappears entirely: ご利用(りよう)ください, not
❌ご利用(りよう)してください.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 館内(かんない)ではおタバコをお吸(す)いにならないでください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 館内(かんない)での喫煙(きつえん)は<b>ご遠慮(えんりょ)ください</b>。

Two faults. Loanwords do not take the polite prefix, and the honorific frame has
no natural negative - Japanese notices name a verb of refraining instead.
館内(かんない)ではタバコをお吸(す)いにならないでください is understandable but
sounds clumsy next to the fixed phrase.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 少(すこ)々お待(ま)ちください。
(b) 少(すこ)々お待(ま)ちいただけますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the standard service line - a receptionist, a phone operator, a waiter. It
is honorific and perfectly courteous, but it *tells* the listener what will
happen, which is fine when the waiting is part of the process.

(b) turns the same content into a question, so the listener is free to say no. It
is what you use when the delay is your fault, or when you are asking a favour of
someone senior. Between colleagues (a) would sound oddly official; from a shop
assistant (b) can sound over-apologetic. Match the form to who owns the delay.

</details>

# いらっしゃる

**Meaning** · the honorific verb covering いる, 行(い)く and 来(く)る all at once

`いらっしゃる` is 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご): it raises the person it describes, so
it is used about your teacher, your client, your guest - never about yourself.
Three ordinary verbs collapse into it, and Japanese speakers do not find that
ambiguous, because the situation always says which one is meant.

## Formation

| Plain verb | Honorific | Example |
|---|---|---|
| いる | いらっしゃる | 先生(せんせい)は部屋(へや)にいらっしゃいます |
| 行(い)く | いらっしゃる | どちらへいらっしゃいますか |
| 来(く)る | いらっしゃる | よくいらっしゃいました |
| ～ている | ～ていらっしゃる | 待(ま)っていらっしゃいます |
| Noun + です (about a person) | ～でいらっしゃる | 社長(しゃちょう)でいらっしゃいます |

It is a う-verb, but its ます-form is irregular - the `り` you expect becomes `い`.
Four verbs share this quirk, and they are all 敬語(けいご):

| Dictionary | ます-form | Never |
|---|---|---|
| いらっしゃる | いらっしゃいます | ❌いらっしゃります |
| おっしゃる | おっしゃいます | ❌おっしゃります |
| なさる | なさいます | ❌なさります |
| くださる | くださいます | ❌くださります |

| Other forms | Shape |
|---|---|
| Negative polite | いらっしゃいません |
| Plain negative | いらっしゃらない |
| て-form | いらっしゃって（contracted: いらして） |
| Request | いらしてください／お越(こ)しください |
| Greeting form | いらっしゃい／いらっしゃいませ |

## Examples

<pre>
先生(せんせい)は教室(きょうしつ)に<b>いらっしゃいます</b>か。
Is the teacher in the classroom?
</pre>

<pre>
どちらへ<b>いらっしゃいます</b>か。
Where are you off to?
</pre>

<pre>
遠(とお)いところ、よく<b>いらっしゃいました</b>。
Thank you for coming all this way.
</pre>

<pre>
明日(あした)の会(かい)には何名(なんめい)ほど<b>いらっしゃいます</b>か。
Roughly how many people will be coming to tomorrow's gathering?
</pre>

<pre>
部長(ぶちょう)は今(いま)、会議室(かいぎしつ)には<b>いらっしゃいません</b>。
The department head isn't in the meeting room at the moment.
</pre>

<pre>
山田(やまだ)先生(せんせい)は来週(らいしゅう)大阪(おおさか)から<b>いらっしゃる</b>そうです。
I hear Professor Yamada is coming up from Osaka next week.
</pre>

<pre>
お客(きゃく)様(さま)が先(さき)ほどから受付(うけつけ)で<b>待(ま)っていらっしゃいます</b>。
The customer has been waiting at reception for a little while now.
</pre>

<pre>
失礼(しつれい)ですが、田中(たなか)様(さま)で<b>いらっしゃいます</b>か。
Excuse me - would you be Mr Tanaka?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`いらっしゃる` is the first 敬語(けいご) verb most learners can actually use,
because it turns up everywhere: shop doorways, phone calls, introductions,
reception desks. It belongs to business, service and formal social speech. Saying
it about a friend - ❌ゆうべ友(とも)だちが家(いえ)にいらっしゃった - is not
extra-polite, it is odd, because you are honouring someone who is on your own
level.

Some texture:

- **いらっしゃいませ** is not an invitation to come. It is a shop and restaurant
  greeting, roughly "welcome", and it expects no reply at all.
- **いらっしゃい** on its own is warm and informal - a host to a guest, an adult to
  a child, a market stall to passers-by.
- **いらして／いらしてください** is the contracted て-form. It is softer and a
  little lighter than いらっしゃって, and it leans towards polite conversational
  speech rather than announcements.
- **お越(こ)しになる, おいでになる, お見(み)えになる** are alternatives for
  the "come" sense, each a shade more formal than plain いらっしゃる.

The trap that catches every English speaker is the **in-group rule**. Japanese
politeness is not about rank in the abstract; it is about which side of a line the
person stands on. Inside your company, 部長(ぶちょう) outranks you and takes
尊敬(そんけい)語(ご). The moment you speak to somebody *outside* the company
 - a client, a supplier, a caller - your whole company becomes your in-group, and
your boss is lowered along with you:

- To a colleague: 部長(ぶちょう)はもうお帰(かえ)りになりました。
- To a client: 田中(たなか)は本日(ほんじつ)外出(がいしゅつ)しております。

No title, no `さん`, and `おります` rather than `いらっしゃいます`. It feels
disloyal to an English speaker; in Japanese it is exactly the loyal thing to do,
because lowering your own side is how you raise the customer.

## Contrast

### いらっしゃる vs おる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `いらっしゃる` for people you are raising; `おる` for yourself
and for anyone on your own side of the line.

</div>

<pre>
社長(しゃちょう)は三階(さんがい)に<b>いらっしゃいます</b>。
The president is on the third floor.
</pre>

<pre>
私(わたし)どもは明日(あした)も店(みせ)に<b>おります</b>。
We'll be at the shop tomorrow as well.
</pre>

The same phone call can need both, one clause apart. An outside caller asking
田中(たなか)さんはいらっしゃいますか is right - from where they stand,
田中(たなか) is an outsider and deserves the honorific. Your reply,
田中(たなか)はただいま席(せき)をはずしております, is also right, because from
where *you* stand he is family.

### いらっしゃる vs 参(まい)る・伺(うかが)う

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `いらっしゃる` is them going or coming; <code>参(まい)る</code> and
<code>伺(うかが)う</code> are you going or coming.

</div>

<pre>
先生(せんせい)は三時(さんじ)に<b>いらっしゃいます</b>。
Sensei will be arriving at three.
</pre>

<pre>
三時(さんじ)に研究室(けんきゅうしつ)へ<b>伺(うかが)います</b>。
I'll come to your office at three.
</pre>

<code>伺(うかが)う</code> is used when the place you are going belongs to somebody you
respect; <code>参(まい)る</code> is the plainer humble word and works even when nobody in
particular is being honoured - 電車(でんしゃ)が参(まい)ります on a platform
announcement is about a train, not a person.

### いらっしゃる vs おいでになる・お見(み)えになる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - all three are honorific. `いらっしゃる` is the default;
`おいでになる` is more formal; <code>お見(み)えになる</code> only ever means "come".

</div>

<pre>
会長(かいちょう)が会場(かいじょう)に<b>おいでになりました</b>。
The chairman has arrived at the venue.
</pre>

<pre>
お約束(やくそく)の方(かた)が<b>お見(み)えになりました</b>。
Your appointment has arrived.
</pre>

Because `いらっしゃる` covers three meanings, the alternatives are sometimes chosen
simply for clarity: お見(み)えになりました can only be an arrival, so nobody has
to work out whether the person came, went or is present.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 先生(せんせい)は明日(あした)<b>いらっしゃります</b>。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)は明日(あした)<b>いらっしゃいます</b>。
  The `り` becomes `い` in the ます-form. The same applies to おっしゃいます,
  なさいます and くださいます.

- ❌ 明日(あした)、私(わたし)が会社(かいしゃ)に<b>いらっしゃいます</b>。 → ✅ 明日(あした)、私(わたし)が会社(かいしゃ)に<b>参(まい)ります</b>。
  尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) about yourself is the classic error. You cannot raise
  your own head - use the humble verb.

- ❌ （to a client）弊社(へいしゃ)の社長(しゃちょう)は今(いま)<b>いらっしゃいません</b>。 → ✅ 社長(しゃちょう)の佐藤(さとう)は本日(ほんじつ)<b>おりません</b>。
  The in-group rule. Talking to an outsider, your own president is lowered like
  everyone else on your side - and loses the `さん`.

- ❌ こちらの資料(しりょう)は<b>いらっしゃいます</b>か。 → ✅ こちらの資料(しりょう)は<b>ございます</b>か。
  `いらっしゃる` honours a person. Objects take `ある` and its polite form
  `ございます`; there is nobody there to raise.

## Practice

**1.** 校長(こうちょう)先生(せんせい)は職員室(しょくいんしつ)に____か。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

校長(こうちょう)先生(せんせい)は職員室(しょくいんしつ)に<b>いらっしゃいます</b>か。

`いる` about someone you are honouring becomes `いらっしゃる`, and its polite form
is いらっしゃいます - not ❌いらっしゃります.

</details>

**2.** Choose: （部長(ぶちょう)はいらっしゃいません・部長(ぶちょう)の中村(なかむら)はおりません） - said on the phone to a customer.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>部長(ぶちょう)の中村(なかむら)はおりません</b>。

Speaking to somebody outside the company, your own department head belongs to your
in-group. He is referred to without `さん` and with the humble `おる`. The first
version honours your own side in front of a customer, which sounds arrogant rather
than respectful.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Which floor are you going to?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

何階(なんがい)へ<b>いらっしゃいます</b>か。

Here `いらっしゃる` carries the 行(い)く sense. Nothing in the form changes; the
question word and the `へ` are what tell the listener that going, rather than
being, is meant.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 私(わたし)は来月(らいげつ)、京都(きょうと)にいらっしゃる予定(よてい)です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 私(わたし)は来月(らいげつ)、京都(きょうと)に<b>参(まい)る</b>予定(よてい)です。

The subject is 私(わたし), so an honorific verb is impossible. Use
参(まい)る, or simply 行(い)きます if the situation does not call for
謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご) at all.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) いらっしゃいませ。
(b) いらっしゃい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is service language. A shop assistant, a waiter or a hotel receptionist says
it as you walk in, and it is not really addressed to you as an individual - no
reply is expected.

(b) is the same word without the `ませ`, and dropping `ませ` drops the formality.
It is what a host says opening the door to a guest, or a parent to a child's
friend: friendly and personal. A market trader shouting いらっしゃい, いらっしゃい
is using it as a call to passers-by, which is friendlier still.

</details>

# なさる

**Meaning** · the honorific of する - "to do", said about someone you are raising

`なさる` is 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご). Because `する` combines with hundreds of
nouns, `なさる` gives you an honorific for every one of them at a stroke:
勉強(べんきょう)なさる、運転(うんてん)なさる、ご結婚(けっこん)なさる. It
is also the verb behind the two most useful service questions in Japanese,
どうなさいますか and いかがなさいましたか.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| する | なさる | する → なさる |
| Noun + する | Noun + なさる | 運転(うんてん)する → 運転(うんてん)なさる |
| Noun + する, with prefix | ご／お + Noun + なさる | 心配(しんぱい)する → ご心配(しんぱい)なさる |
| ～ている | ～なさっている | 研究(けんきゅう)している → 研究(けんきゅう)なさっている |
| Choosing between options | Noun + に + なさる | コーヒーにする → コーヒーになさる |

Like `いらっしゃる`, it is a う-verb with an irregular ます-form:

| Form | Shape |
|---|---|
| Polite | なさいます（❌なさります） |
| Past polite | なさいました |
| Negative | なさらない／なさいません |
| て-form | なさって |
| Negative request | ～なさらないでください |
| Softened command | ます-stem + なさい（食(た)べなさい） |

<div class="note">

**Note** - `～なさい` is built from this verb but does **not** honour anyone. It is
a gentle order aimed *downward*: a parent to a child, a teacher to a class, a test
paper to a candidate. おやすみなさい and ごめんなさい are fossilised leftovers of
the same form.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
どう<b>なさいました</b>か。
What seems to be the trouble?
</pre>

<pre>
コーヒーと紅茶(こうちゃ)、どちらに<b>なさいます</b>か。
Coffee or tea - which would you like?
</pre>

<pre>
先生(せんせい)は毎朝(まいあさ)散歩(さんぽ)を<b>なさいます</b>。
Sensei goes for a walk every morning.
</pre>

<pre>
どうぞご心配(しんぱい)<b>なさらないで</b>ください。
Please don't worry about it.
</pre>

<pre>
社長(しゃちょう)は週末(しゅうまつ)にゴルフを<b>なさる</b>そうです。
I gather the president plays golf at weekends.
</pre>

<pre>
明日(あした)の集合(しゅうごう)は何時(なんじ)に<b>なさいます</b>か。
What time would you like to meet tomorrow?
</pre>

<pre>
先生(せんせい)が長年(ながねん)<b>研究(けんきゅう)なさっている</b>テーマについて伺(うかが)いたいと思(おも)っております。
I'd like to ask you about the subject you have been researching for so many years.
</pre>

<pre>
部長(ぶちょう)はこの件(けん)をどのように処理(しょり)<b>なさる</b>おつもりでしょうか。
How does the department head intend to handle this matter?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`なさる` is squarely business, service and formal-social language. Shop staff,
receptionists, doctors, flight attendants and hotel clerks use it constantly,
mostly in the fixed openers どうなさいますか（"what would you like to do?"）,
いかがなさいますか（the more formal version）and どうなさいましたか（"what's
wrong?"）. To a friend, ❌何(なに)をなさっているんですか would be comic.

Because it is 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) it obeys the same two hard limits as the
rest of the system. It never describes your own action - that is `いたす` - and it
never describes your own side when you are speaking to an outsider. On the phone
to a customer, ❌担当(たんとう)の者(もの)が確認(かくにん)なさいます honours
your own colleague; the sentence you want is
担当(たんとう)の者(もの)が確認(かくにん)いたします.

`される` is the lighter alternative: 部長(ぶちょう)はどうされますか is
perfectly polite and rather more everyday than
部長(ぶちょう)はどうなさいますか. As a tendency, `なさる` is felt as warmer and
more attentive, and it is heard more often from service staff and in careful
formal speech; `される` is the workhorse of ordinary office Japanese. Neither is
wrong in the other's setting.

Do not stack layers. ❌おなさりになる and ❌なさられる are
二重(にじゅう)敬語(けいご); `なさる` on its own is already the honorific.

## Contrast

### なさる vs いたす

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なさる` for what *they* do; `いたす` for what *you* do. Same
verb, opposite directions.

</div>

<pre>
お客(きゃく)様(さま)はどちらに<b>なさいます</b>か。
Which will you have, madam?
</pre>

<pre>
私(わたし)どもがご用意(ようい)<b>いたします</b>。
We'll get it ready for you.
</pre>

This pair is the cleanest illustration of the whole three-way system. Both
sentences are polite to the customer; the first gets there by lifting her, the
second by lowering the speaker. Using `なさる` about yourself does not merely sound
odd - it claims a status you have just given to somebody else.

### なさる vs される

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are honorific. `される` is lighter and unmarked;
`なさる` is more attentive and more distinctly 敬語(けいご).

</div>

<pre>
課長(かちょう)はいつ出張(しゅっちょう)<b>されます</b>か。
When are you going on the business trip, 課長(かちょう)?
</pre>

<pre>
会長(かいちょう)はいつ出張(しゅっちょう)<b>なさいます</b>か。
When will the chairman be going on his trip?
</pre>

There is one practical reason to reach for `なさる`: `される` is also the passive.
注意(ちゅうい)されました can mean "you kindly took care" or "I got told off",
and 注意(ちゅうい)なさいました can only mean the first.

### なさる vs ～なさい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なさる` looks up; `～なさい` looks down. They come from the
same root and point in opposite social directions.

</div>

<pre>
宿題(しゅくだい)を先(さき)に<b>しなさい</b>。
Do your homework first.
</pre>

<pre>
宿題(しゅくだい)はもう<b>なさいました</b>か。
Have you done your homework yet? (said respectfully)
</pre>

`～なさい` is softer than the bare imperative しろ, which is why parents and
teachers like it - but softness is not respect. Aimed upward it is an insult, so
❌先生(せんせい)、こちらに書(か)きなさい must be
先生(せんせい)、こちらにお書(か)きください.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 社長(しゃちょう)は何時(なんじ)に出発(しゅっぱつ)<b>なさりますか</b>。 → ✅ 社長(しゃちょう)は何時(なんじ)に出発(しゅっぱつ)<b>なさいますか</b>。
  The `り` turns into `い` before ます. Same irregularity as いらっしゃいます and
  くださいます.

- ❌ 私(わたし)がご説明(せつめい)<b>なさいます</b>。 → ✅ 私(わたし)がご説明(せつめい)<b>いたします</b>。
  尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) pointed at yourself. Your own actions take
  謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご).

- ❌ お客(きゃく)様(さま)、どう<b>しましたか</b>。 → ✅ お客(きゃく)様(さま)、どう<b>なさいましたか</b>。
  Grammatically fine, socially thin. `しましたか` is only
  丁寧(ていねい)語(ご) - polite speech with no honorific in it - and a
  customer expects to be raised, not merely addressed politely.

- ❌ 先生(せんせい)が説明(せつめい)<b>なさられました</b>。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)が説明(せつめい)<b>なさいました</b>。
  `なさる` plus `られる` is two honorifics on one verb. One is the ceiling.

## Practice

**1.** お飲(の)み物(もの)は何(なに)に____か。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お飲(の)み物(もの)は何(なに)に<b>なさいます</b>か。

<code>何(なに)にする</code> - "go for which one" - becomes
何(なに)になさる when the chooser is the person you are serving. This is the
standard restaurant line.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 明日(あした)は私(わたし)が（案内(あんない)なさいます・ご案内(あんない)いたします）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

明日(あした)は私(わたし)が<b>ご案内(あんない)いたします</b>。

The subject is 私(わたし), so the sentence must lower rather than raise.
`なさる` would put the honorific on your own action. Note also that
案内(あんない) is a Chinese-derived root, so it takes `ご`.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Does the teacher drive?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

先生(せんせい)は運転(うんてん)を<b>なさいます</b>か。

運転(うんてん)する is a noun + `する`, so the honorific is made simply by
swapping `する` for `なさる`. 運転(うんてん)されますか is equally correct and a
little lighter.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 部長(ぶちょう)、この書類(しょるい)を確認(かくにん)しなさい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、この書類(しょるい)をご確認(かくにん)いただけますでしょうか。

`～なさい` is a command directed downward - fine from a teacher to a pupil,
unacceptable to your own department head. A request upward needs the honorific
frame, and softening it into a question softens it further.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 先生(せんせい)は何(なに)を研究(けんきゅう)していますか。
(b) 先生(せんせい)は何(なに)を研究(けんきゅう)なさっていますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are polite in form, and neither is rude. (a) uses only
丁寧(ていねい)語(ご): the `ます` makes your speech courteous, but the verb
itself says nothing about the teacher's standing. It is what a student might say
to a lecturer they know well.

(b) adds 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) on top, so the teacher is lifted by the verb as
well as addressed politely. It suits a first meeting, an interview or a formal
setting. The difference is not politeness versus rudeness - it is distance and
deference.

</details>

# 敬語の動詞

**Meaning** · the closed set of verbs that have their own honorific and humble replacements

A dozen or so of the commonest Japanese verbs do not use the
お○○になる／お○○する frames at all. They have **separate words** instead - one
that raises the person you are talking about, and one that lowers you. Learn them
as pairs and most of 敬語(けいご) is done.

## Formation

These verbs are not built; they are swapped in. The plain verb disappears and a
different word takes its place.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb with a set honorific | plain verb → 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) word | 食(た)べる → 召(め)し上(あ)がる |
| Verb with a set humble | plain verb → 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご) word | 見(み)る → 拝見(はいけん)する |
| Noun + する (honorific) | ご／お + noun + になる・なさる | 説明(せつめい)する → ご説明(せつめい)になる |
| Noun + する (humble) | ご／お + noun + する・いたす | 説明(せつめい)する → ご説明(せつめい)いたす |
| ～ている (honorific) | ～ていらっしゃる | 待(ま)っている → 待(ま)っていらっしゃる |
| ～ている (humble) | ～ておる | 待(ま)っている → 待(ま)っております |

The set itself, laid out as the mirror it is. The middle column is for the other
person; the right-hand column is for you and for anyone on your side.

| Plain verb | 尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) - raises them | 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご) - lowers you |
|---|---|---|
| いる | いらっしゃる／おいでになる | おる |
| 行(い)く | いらっしゃる／お越(こ)しになる | 参(まい)る／伺(うかが)う |
| 来(く)る | いらっしゃる／お見(み)えになる | 参(まい)る／伺(うかが)う |
| する | なさる | いたす |
| 言(い)う | おっしゃる | 申(もう)す／申(もう)し上(あ)げる |
| 食(た)べる・飲(の)む | 召(め)し上(あ)がる | いただく |
| 見(み)る | ご覧(らん)になる | 拝見(はいけん)する |
| 聞(き)く・尋(たず)ねる | お聞(き)きになる | 伺(うかが)う／承(うけたまわ)る |
| 知(し)っている | ご存(ぞん)じだ | 存(ぞん)じている／存(ぞん)じ上(あ)げる |
| もらう | - | いただく／頂戴(ちょうだい)する |
| あげる | - | さし上(あ)げる |
| くれる | くださる | - |
| 会(あ)う | お会(あ)いになる | お目(め)にかかる |
| 着(き)る | お召(め)しになる | - |
| 寝(ね)る | お休(やす)みになる | - |
| 借(か)りる | - | 拝借(はいしゃく)する |

Four of the honorific verbs conjugate irregularly before ます. This is the detail
that most often gives a learner away:

| Dictionary | ます-form | Never |
|---|---|---|
| いらっしゃる | いらっしゃいます | ❌いらっしゃります |
| おっしゃる | おっしゃいます | ❌おっしゃります |
| なさる | なさいます | ❌なさります |
| くださる | くださいます | ❌くださります |

## Nuance & register

Hold the three-way system in mind and none of this is guesswork.
丁寧(ていねい)語(ご)（です・ます）makes your speech polite to the listener.
尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) raises the subject of the verb.
謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご) lowers the subject of the verb, which raises whoever
the action is aimed at. Choosing a form is therefore a question about *who is
doing the verb*, not about how polite you feel.

Two refinements worth having early:

**The humble column has two halves.** 伺(うかが)う,
申(もう)し上(あ)げる, 拝見(はいけん)する and お目(め)にかかる need a
specific person on the receiving end who deserves the respect. 参(まい)る,
申(もう)す and おる do not - they simply lower and formalise, which is why
電車(でんしゃ)が参(まい)ります works on a platform announcement even though a
train respects nobody. That second group is sometimes labelled
丁重(ていちょう)語(ご), and it is the group you use for your own side in
front of an outsider.

**In-group before rank.** English speakers expect politeness to track seniority.
Japanese tracks the boundary of your group first. Talking to a client, your own
部長(ぶちょう) is lowered exactly like you: not
❌部長(ぶちょう)がおっしゃっていました but
部長(ぶちょう)の中村(なかむら)が申(もう)しておりました - no `さん`, humble
verb, and the title placed before the surname rather than after it. Inside the
office, with only colleagues listening, おっしゃいました is right again. Nothing
about the boss changed; the audience did.

Some smaller points:

- `いただく` does double duty - humble "eat/drink" and humble "receive". The
  context separates them.
- 存(ぞん)じ上(あ)げる is used about **people**
  （お名前(なまえ)は存(ぞん)じ上(あ)げております）, and
  存(ぞん)じる about **facts**.
- 承(うけたまわ)る is heavy formal business language - order-taking, phone
  messages - and is rare in speech otherwise.
- お伺(うかが)いします is strictly a double layer, but it is so established that
  usage guides let it stand. Do not extend the pattern to new verbs.

And the honest register note: this is the language of work and of service. It is
not a politer version of everyday Japanese that you can switch on at will. Using
it with friends puts a formal wall between you, and Japanese speakers read that
wall as coldness or as mockery.

## Contrast

### 召(め)し上(あ)がる vs いただく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - 召(め)し上(あ)がる is *them* eating; いただく is *you*
eating. Both mean "eat", and they are never interchangeable.

</div>

<pre>
冷(さ)めないうちに<b>召(め)し上(あ)がって</b>ください。
Do please eat before it gets cold.
</pre>

<pre>
おいしいお菓子(かし)を<b>いただきました</b>。
I had some lovely cakes.
</pre>

The commonest single 敬語(けいご) error learners make is turning the honorific
on themselves, and this is where it happens most:
❌私(わたし)はもう召(め)し上(あ)がりました. You have just told the room you
consider yourself the honoured guest. The word for your own eating is
いただきました - the same word as in the いただきます said before a meal.

### おっしゃる vs 申(もう)す・申(もう)し上(あ)げる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - おっしゃる is their speaking; 申(もう)す is yours.
申(もう)し上(あ)げる adds a person you are speaking *to* and deserves.

</div>

<pre>
先生(せんせい)は何(なに)か<b>おっしゃいました</b>か。
Did sensei say anything?
</pre>

<pre>
はじめまして、鈴木(すずき)と<b>申(もう)します</b>。
How do you do - my name is Suzuki.
</pre>

Introducing yourself with ❌鈴木(すずき)とおっしゃいます would mean "I am called
Suzuki, the honourable gentleman". The reverse error is just as common: asking a
customer ❌何(なん)と申(もう)しますか lowers the customer.

### 伺(うかが)う vs 参(まい)る

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are humble "go/come". <code>伺(うかが)う</code> requires a
person at the destination whom you respect; <code>参(まい)る</code> does not.

</div>

<pre>
来週(らいしゅう)、先生(せんせい)のお宅(たく)へ<b>伺(うかが)います</b>。
I'll visit your home next week, sensei.
</pre>

<pre>
三番(さんばん)線(せん)に電車(でんしゃ)が<b>参(まい)ります</b>。
A train is arriving at platform three.
</pre>

Announcements, weather reports and formal self-description use 参(まい)る
freely; you cannot substitute 伺(うかが)う into any of them, because there is
nobody to honour. Note too that 伺(うかが)う has a second job - humble "ask" - 
so ちょっと伺(うかが)いますが is "may I ask you something?"

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 私(わたし)は会議(かいぎ)の資料(しりょう)を<b>ご覧(らん)になりました</b>。 → ✅ 私(わたし)は会議(かいぎ)の資料(しりょう)を<b>拝見(はいけん)しました</b>。
  尊敬(そんけい)語(ご) about yourself. Looking at somebody's documents is
  your own action, so it takes the humble word.

- ❌ （to a customer）社長(しゃちょう)さんが<b>そうおっしゃいました</b>。 → ✅ 社長(しゃちょう)の佐藤(さとう)が<b>そう申(もう)しておりました</b>。
  The in-group rule. Your own president is lowered in front of an outsider,
  referred to by bare surname, with the title in front.

- ❌ 先生(せんせい)のお話(はなし)を<b>お聞(き)きになりました</b>。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)のお話(はなし)を<b>伺(うかが)いました</b>。
  Same error with a different verb. When *you* are the listener, the humble verb
  is 伺(うかが)う.

- ❌ その方(かた)のお名前(なまえ)は<b>存(ぞん)じております</b>。 → ✅ その方(かた)のお名前(なまえ)は<b>存(ぞん)じ上(あ)げております</b>。
  Knowing *a person* takes 存(ぞん)じ上(あ)げる;
  存(ぞん)じる on its own is for facts and things.

- ❌ ねえ、もう<b>召(め)し上(あ)がった</b>? → ✅ ねえ、もう食(た)べた?
  Correct grammar, wrong world. Full 敬語(けいご) between friends reads as a
  joke at the listener's expense.

**1.** どうぞ、温(あたた)かいうちに____ください。

The person eating is your guest, so the verb must raise them. There is no such

fixed extra-polite variant of the same thing.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 明日(あした)、部長(ぶちょう)のお宅(たく)へ（いらっしゃいます・伺(うかが)います）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

明日(あした)、部長(ぶちょう)のお宅(たく)へ<b>伺(うかが)います</b>。

The speaker is the one travelling, so the verb lowers the speaker.
伺(うかが)う rather than 参(まい)る because the destination belongs to a

</details>

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

今月(こんげつ)のカタログはもう<b>ご覧(らん)になりました</b>か。

The customer is doing the looking, so 見(み)る becomes the honorific

</details>

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 部長(ぶちょう)の山田(やまだ)は今(いま)席(せき)を外(はず)して<b>おります</b>が、戻(もど)りましたらご連絡(れんらく)<b>いたします</b>。

`いらっしゃる` and `なさる` both honour your own department head in front of a
customer. Lower him instead: humble `おる` and `いたす`, bare surname, and the
title moved in front of the name.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 明日(あした)大阪(おおさか)へ参(まい)ります。
(b) 明日(あした)大阪(おおさか)へ伺(うかが)います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both lower the speaker, so both are humble. (a) simply makes the statement formal:
it works whether you are visiting a client, going to a conference or moving house,
because 参(まい)る does not point at anybody.

(b) says there is a particular person in Osaka whom you are going to see and whom
you respect - a client, a teacher, a customer. Said about a purely private trip
it sounds off, because the respect in the verb has nowhere to land.

</details>

# お○○する

**Meaning** · the general-purpose humble frame - describes *your own* action while lowering you

`お○○する` is the mirror image of `お○○になる`. Same prefix, same ます-stem, but
`する` on the end instead of `になる`, and the whole thing points the other way: it
lowers the speaker, and by lowering the speaker it raises the person the action is
done for. This is 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご), and it is the half of
敬語(けいご) that learners most often leave out.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | お + ます-stem + する | 持(も)ちます → お持(も)ちする |
| Verb (る-verb) | お + ます-stem + する | 届(とど)けます → お届(とど)けする |
| する-verb, Chinese-derived root | ご + noun + する | 説明(せつめい)する → ご説明(せつめい)する |
| する-verb, native root | お + noun + する | 手伝(てつだ)いする → お手伝(てつだ)いする |
| More humble | お／ご + stem + いたす | お持(も)ちいたします |
| Most formal (written) | お／ご + stem + 申(もう)し上(あ)げる | ご案内(あんない)申(もう)し上(あ)げます |
| Ongoing / standing state | お／ご + stem + しております | お待(ま)ちしております |
| Verb with a set humble word | frame **not** used | 見(み)る → 拝見(はいけん)する |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the frame needs a **recipient**: somebody the action is done
to, for, or in front of, whom you respect. お持(も)ちします works because you
carry it *for them*. ❌お寝(ね)します and ❌お散歩(さんぽ)します do not,
because nobody is on the receiving end.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
お荷物(にもつ)を<b>お持(も)ちします</b>。
Let me take your bag.
</pre>

<pre>
この傘(かさ)を<b>お貸(か)しします</b>。
I'll lend you this umbrella.
</pre>

<pre>
使(つか)い方(かた)を<b>ご説明(せつめい)します</b>。
I'll explain how it works.
</pre>

<pre>
ロビーで<b>お待(ま)ちしております</b>。
I'll be waiting in the lobby.
</pre>

<pre>
書類(しょるい)は明日(あした)までに<b>お届(とど)けします</b>。
I'll get the documents to you by tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
少(すこ)し<b>お尋(たず)ねしたい</b>ことがあるのですが、よろしいでしょうか。
There's something I'd like to ask you, if I may.
</pre>

<pre>
受付(うけつけ)から会場(かいじょう)まで、係(かかり)の者(もの)が<b>ご案内(あんない)します</b>。
A member of staff will show you from reception through to the hall.
</pre>

<pre>
新(あたら)しいサービスにつきましては、あらためて資料(しりょう)を<b>お送(おく)りします</b>ので、ご確認(かくにん)いただければ幸(さいわ)いです。
I'll send you the material on the new service separately, so I'd be grateful if you would take a look.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Everything here belongs to work, service and formal dealings with strangers. In a
shop, on a client call, in a business email it is the normal way to describe what
you are about to do; between friends it is not, and お貸(か)ししようか to a
classmate sounds like you are role-playing a hotel clerk.

The ladder inside the frame is worth knowing:

- **お送(おく)りします** - standard humble. Fine for almost everything.
- **お送(おく)りいたします** - `いたす` is itself the humble `する`, so this is
  one notch lower and one notch more formal. Common in business speech.
- **お送(おく)り申(もう)し上(あ)げます** - ceremonial and written. Notices,
  formal letters, wedding invitations.

Treat that as a gradient rather than three fixed rungs: most speakers move between
the first two without thinking about it, and the third is rare enough that using
it in conversation would draw attention.

`しております` is the humble `～ている`, and it is everywhere in service Japanese
because so much of that language describes standing states rather than single
acts - 
お待(ま)ちしております,
ご連絡(れんらく)をお待(ま)ちしております,
お問(と)い合(あ)わせをお受(う)けしております.

Two limits to keep straight. First, the frame never describes somebody else's
action: ❌先生(せんせい)がお話(はな)しします turns your teacher into your
servant. Second, an action with no human on the other end will not take it, which
is why "I'll go home early today" cannot be ❌早(はや)くお帰(かえ)りします - 
say 失礼(しつれい)します or
早(はや)く帰(かえ)らせていただきます instead.

## Contrast

### お○○する vs お○○になる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical up to the last word. `する` = my action, lowered.
`になる` = their action, raised.

</div>

<pre>
先生(せんせい)に新(あたら)しい住所(じゅうしょ)を<b>お知(し)らせします</b>。
I'll let sensei know my new address.
</pre>

<pre>
先生(せんせい)が新(あたら)しい住所(じゅうしょ)を<b>お知(し)らせになりました</b>。
Sensei let us know his new address.
</pre>

Get the pair backwards and you say the opposite of what you mean. It is worth
practising them as a drill: pick a verb, build both, and say out loud who is doing
which.

### お○○する vs ～させていただく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `お○○する` says "I'll do it for you"; `～させていただく` says
"I'll take the liberty, with your permission".

</div>

<pre>
来週(らいしゅう)の予定(よてい)を<b>ご連絡(れんらく)します</b>。
I'll let you know next week's schedule.
</pre>

<pre>
本日(ほんじつ)は早(はや)めに<b>閉(し)めさせていただきます</b>。
We'll be closing early today.
</pre>

`させていただく` frames the action as something you are permitted to do, so it fits
things that affect the listener without being *for* them - closing early, taking
leave, declining. It has spread so far in recent business Japanese that it is
regularly criticised as overused; where the action really is a service you are
performing, `お○○する` is the cleaner choice.

### お○○する vs plain ます

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ます` is 丁寧(ていねい)語(ご) only: polite speech, no
humility. `お○○する` adds the humility.

</div>

<pre>
明日(あした)資料(しりょう)を<b>送(おく)ります</b>。
I'll send the material tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
明日(あした)資料(しりょう)を<b>お送(おく)りします</b>。
I'll send the material to you tomorrow.
</pre>

Both are polite; neither is rude. The first is what you write to a colleague, the
second to a customer. This is the distinction to keep hold of when people say
敬語(けいご) is "polite Japanese": です／ます politeness and
謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご) humility are separate dials, and you can turn one
without the other.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)が会議(かいぎ)の内容(ないよう)を<b>ご説明(せつめい)します</b>。（to a colleague）→ ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)が会議(かいぎ)の内容(ないよう)を<b>ご説明(せつめい)になります</b>。
  The frame lowers whoever is doing the verb. Applied to your department head in
  front of colleagues, it demotes him.

- ❌ 私(わたし)が<b>お説明(せつめい)します</b>。 → ✅ 私(わたし)が<b>ご説明(せつめい)します</b>。
  説明(せつめい) is a Chinese-derived root, so the prefix is `ご`. Compare
  ご連絡(れんらく)します, ご報告(ほうこく)します, ご案内(あんない)します.

- ❌ 先生(せんせい)の論文(ろんぶん)を<b>お見(み)しました</b>。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)の論文(ろんぶん)を<b>拝見(はいけん)しました</b>。
  One-mora stems are shut out of the frame, and 見(み)る has its own humble
  verb.

- ❌ 今日(きょう)は用事(ようじ)があるので、早(はや)く<b>お帰(かえ)りします</b>。 → ✅ 今日(きょう)は用事(ようじ)があるので、<b>お先(さき)に失礼(しつれい)します</b>。
  Going home is not done *for* anybody, so there is no recipient for the humility
  to reach. Worse, お帰(かえ)り is strongly associated with the honorific, so
  the sentence sounds as if you are honouring yourself.

that if you were saying this to an outside audience about your own company's
president, you would drop the honorific again and say
社長(しゃちょう)の佐藤(さとう)がご説明(せつめい)いたします.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 窓口(まどぐち)でお預(あず)かりします。
(b) 窓口(まどぐち)で預(あず)かります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both mean the counter will hold the item, and both are polite in form. (a) uses
the humble frame, so the speaker is lowered and the customer raised; it is the
sentence a bank clerk or a cloakroom attendant says, and the courtesy is aimed at
a specific person standing in front of them.

(b) is only 丁寧(ていねい)語(ご). It reports the arrangement neutrally, the
way a colleague explains a procedure to another colleague, or a sign states a
rule. Nothing in it honours the listener.

</details>

# いたす

**Meaning** · the humble of する - "to do", said about your own action

`いたす` is 謙譲(けんじょう)語(ご). It replaces `する` when the person doing
the verb is you, or anyone on your side of the group boundary, and by lowering
that person it raises whoever you are speaking to. It is the direct opposite
number of `なさる`, and between them the two cover every `する` verb in the
language.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| する | いたす | する → いたす |
| Noun + する | Noun + いたす | 失礼(しつれい)する → 失礼(しつれい)いたす |
| Noun + する, with prefix | ご／お + Noun + いたす | 案内(あんない)する → ご案内(あんない)いたす |
| Verb (humble frame) | お + ます-stem + いたす | 届(とど)けます → お届(とど)けいたす |
| ～ている | いたしております | 準備(じゅんび)している → 準備(じゅんび)いたしております |

Unlike `なさる` and `いらっしゃる`, `いたす` is a completely regular う-verb:

| Form | Shape |
|---|---|
| Polite | いたします |
| Past polite | いたしました |
| Negative polite | いたしません |
| Plain negative | いたさない |
| て-form | いたして／いたしまして |

<div class="note">

**Note** - in practice you will almost never meet the plain form in speech.
`いたす` lives in the ます-form; a plain いたす turns up mainly in written notices
and in fixed compounds.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
その仕事(しごと)は私(わたし)が<b>いたします</b>。
I'll take care of that job.
</pre>

<pre>
失礼(しつれい)<b>いたします</b>。
Excuse me. / I'll be on my way.
</pre>

<pre>
どう<b>いたしまして</b>。
Not at all - you're very welcome.
</pre>

<pre>
準備(じゅんび)は私(わたし)どもが<b>いたします</b>ので、ご安心(あんしん)ください。
We'll handle the preparations, so please don't worry.
</pre>

<pre>
ただいま担当(たんとう)の者(もの)が確認(かくにん)<b>いたします</b>。
Someone from the relevant team will check that right now.
</pre>

<pre>
承知(しょうち)<b>いたしました</b>。すぐに手配(てはい)いたします。
Understood. I'll arrange it straight away.
</pre>

<pre>
本日(ほんじつ)は貴重(きちょう)なお時間(じかん)をいただき、感謝(かんしゃ)<b>いたします</b>。
Thank you for giving us your valuable time today.
</pre>

<pre>
来週(らいしゅう)の打(う)ち合(あ)わせにつきましては、あらためてメールでご連絡(れんらく)<b>いたします</b>。
As for next week's meeting, I'll be in touch again by email.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`いたします` is one of the most-used words in Japanese working life, largely
because it is welded into a handful of phrases you will say every day:

- **よろしくお願(ねが)いいたします** - closes almost every business email.
- **失礼(しつれい)いたします** - entering a room, leaving a room, ending a call.
- **承知(しょうち)いたしました** - "understood", to a customer or a superior.
- **どういたしまして** - a frozen phrase built on this verb, used to wave away
  thanks.

There is a useful register ordering for acknowledging an instruction, though it is
a tendency rather than a rule: かしこまりました is the most deferential and belongs
to service staff, 承知(しょうち)いたしました is the business standard,
わかりました is neutral, and 了解(りょうかい)しました is felt by many people to
be too casual to send upward. Plenty of workplaces use
了解(りょうかい)です internally without anyone minding; to a client it is worth
avoiding.

`いたす` also has a slightly different flavour depending on what it is attached to.
In ご連絡(れんらく)いたします it is aimed at a specific person you are serving.
In 失礼(しつれい)いたします or
開店(かいてん)時間(じかん)を変更(へんこう)いたしました it simply makes the
statement formal and self-effacing, with nobody in particular being honoured - the
same job that 参(まい)る and 申(もう)す do. Both uses are humble; only the
second works in an announcement.

Finally, the limit that defines the word: `いたす` never describes somebody else's
action when you are honouring them. ❌先生(せんせい)が説明(せつめい)いたしました
lowers your teacher. The exception is your own in-group in front of an outsider,
where lowering is exactly right:
部長(ぶちょう)の中村(なかむら)がご説明(せつめい)いたします.

## Contrast

### いたす vs なさる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `いたす` for what I do, `なさる` for what you or they do. One
verb, two directions.

</div>

<pre>
お席(せき)はどちらに<b>なさいます</b>か。
Which table would you like?
</pre>

<pre>
それでは、三十分(さんじゅっぷん)ほどで<b>お持(も)ちいたします</b>。
In that case, I'll bring it to you in about thirty minutes.
</pre>

One restaurant exchange, two verbs. Everything the customer does takes `なさる`;
everything the staff do takes `いたす`. A single conversation swings between them
constantly, and the swing follows the **subject of each verb**, not the overall
tone of the conversation.

### いたす vs する

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `します` is polite speech. `いたします` is polite speech
**plus** humility. They are not two levels of the same dial.

</div>

<pre>
明日(あした)の朝(あさ)、電話(でんわ)<b>します</b>。
I'll phone tomorrow morning.
</pre>

<pre>
明日(あした)の朝(あさ)、お電話(でんわ)<b>いたします</b>。
I'll telephone you tomorrow morning.
</pre>

The first is perfectly polite and entirely normal to a colleague or a friend's
parent. The second lowers you in front of the listener and belongs to a customer,
a client or a professor. Reaching for `いたします` with a friend does not make you
sound respectful; it makes you sound like a call centre.

### お○○いたす vs お○○する

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are humble. `いたす` is one notch more formal, because
the humble frame and the humble verb are working together.

</div>

<pre>
資料(しりょう)を<b>お渡(わた)しします</b>。
I'll hand you the material.
</pre>

<pre>
資料(しりょう)を<b>お渡(わた)しいたします</b>。
I'll hand you the material.
</pre>

Strictly this doubles up, but it is long-established and nobody objects to it - 
unlike ❌お読(よ)みになられる on the honorific side, which does draw comment.
Use `お○○します` in ordinary business speech and `お○○いたします` when you are
being formal: a first meeting, a customer, a public announcement.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 先生(せんせい)が説明(せつめい)<b>いたしました</b>。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)が説明(せつめい)<b>なさいました</b>。
  A humble verb applied to the person you mean to honour. It says your teacher
  demeaned himself in order to explain.

- ❌ お客(きゃく)様(さま)、どう<b>いたしますか</b>。 → ✅ お客(きゃく)様(さま)、どう<b>なさいますか</b>。
  The customer is doing the deciding, so the verb must raise. どういたしましょうか
  is fine, because there the speaker is offering to act.

- ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)、会議(かいぎ)に出席(しゅっせき)<b>いたしますか</b>。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)、会議(かいぎ)に出席(しゅっせき)<b>なさいますか</b>。
  Inside the company your department head is above you and takes
  尊敬(そんけい)語(ご). Only in front of an outsider does he get lowered.

- ❌ （to a client）了解(りょうかい)しました。 → ✅ 承知(しょうち)<b>いたしました</b>。
  Not a grammar error, a register one. 了解(りょうかい) reads as flat and
  slightly casual coming from the serving side of a business relationship.

## Practice

**1.** ご質問(しつもん)には私(わたし)どもが責任(せきにん)をもってお答(こた)え____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ご質問(しつもん)には私(わたし)どもが責任(せきにん)をもって<b>お答(こた)えいたします</b>。

The subject is 私(わたし)ども, so the verb is humble. お + ます-stem +
`いたす` is the more formal of the two humble frames, which suits a written
undertaking like this one. お答(こた)えします would also be correct.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 社長(しゃちょう)は明日(あした)大阪(おおさか)へ（出張(しゅっちょう)いたします・出張(しゅっちょう)なさいます） - said to a colleague.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

社長(しゃちょう)は明日(あした)大阪(おおさか)へ<b>出張(しゅっちょう)なさいます</b>。

The listener is inside the company, so the president is honoured normally.
出張(しゅっちょう)いたします would lower him. Said to a client, however, the
humble version becomes correct - and he would lose his `さん` too.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, as an email to a client: "I'll send you the details tomorrow."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

詳(くわ)しい内容(ないよう)は明日(あした)<b>ご連絡(れんらく)いたします</b>。

連絡(れんらく) is a Chinese-derived root, so it takes `ご`, and the humble
`いたします` replaces `します` because the sender is the one acting.
お知(し)らせいたします is an equally good alternative.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 恐(おそ)れ入(い)りますが、こちらの用紙(ようし)にご記入(きにゅう)いたしてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 恐(おそ)れ入(い)りますが、こちらの用紙(ようし)に<b>ご記入(きにゅう)ください</b>。

A request tells the *listener* to act, so it cannot contain a humble verb - 
ご記入(きにゅう)いたしてください orders them to lower themselves. Drop the
`いたして` and the honorific request form appears on its own.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 明日(あした)伺(うかが)います。
(b) 明日(あした)訪問(ほうもん)いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are humble, and either would be understood as "I'll visit you tomorrow".
(a) uses the dedicated humble verb for visiting a person you respect. It is
warmer and more personal, and it implies the visit is to *you*.

(b) is built on a Chinese-derived noun plus `いたす`, which makes it flatter and
more procedural - the register of a written schedule or a formal notification.
Neither is more polite than the other; (a) sounds like a person speaking, (b) like
a company writing.

</details>

# ございます

**Meaning** · the very polite form of ある - "there is", "we have"

`ございます` does the same work as `あります`, one register higher. It is
丁寧(ていねい)語(ご): it lifts the *tone of your speech*, not the subject of
the sentence. That is why you can use it about the listener's questions and about
your own shop's stock without contradicting yourself - nobody is being raised or
lowered, only the language.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ある | ございます | 時間(じかん)がある → 時間(じかん)がございます |
| ない | ございません | 席(せき)がない → 席(せき)がございません |
| Past | ございました／ございませんでした | 連絡(れんらく)がございました |
| Conditional | ございましたら | ご希望(きぼう)がございましたら |
| い-adjective + ございます | sound change on the く-form | ありがたい → ありがとうございます |
| Noun + です | でございます | 受付(うけつけ)でございます |

The い-adjective pattern is worth seeing once, because three of the commonest
greetings in the language come out of it:

| Adjective | く-form | Sound change | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| ありがたい | ありがたく | ～あく → ～おう | ありがとうございます |
| おめでたい | おめでたく | ～あく → ～おう | おめでとうございます |
| 早(はや)い | 早(はや)く | ～あく → ～おう | お早(はよ)うございます |
| うれしい | うれしく | ～しく → ～しゅう | うれしゅうございます |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the plain form ござる has dropped out of modern speech; you will meet it
only in samurai films. Treat ございます as a word without a dictionary form, and
note that it shares the irregular ます-form of いらっしゃる and なさる:
❌ござります does not exist.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
お手洗(てあら)いは二階(にかい)に<b>ございます</b>。
The washrooms are on the second floor.
</pre>

<pre>
時間(じかん)はまだ<b>ございます</b>。
There is still time.
</pre>

<pre>
ご質問(しつもん)は<b>ございません</b>か。
Are there any questions?
</pre>

<pre>
あいにく在庫(ざいこ)が<b>ございません</b>。
I'm afraid we're out of stock.
</pre>

<pre>
会員(かいいん)の方(かた)には特別(とくべつ)な割引(わりびき)が<b>ございます</b>。
There is a special discount for members.
</pre>

<pre>
恐(おそ)れ入(い)りますが、喫煙(きつえん)できるお席(せき)は<b>ございません</b>。
I'm sorry, but we have no seats where smoking is allowed.
</pre>

<pre>
何(なに)かご要望(ようぼう)が<b>ございましたら</b>、フロントまでお申(もう)し付(つ)けください。
If there is anything you need, please let the front desk know.
</pre>

<pre>
車内(しゃない)にお忘(わす)れ物(もの)の<b>ございません</b>よう、ご注意(ちゅうい)ください。
Please take care not to leave anything behind on the train.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is service language above all. Shops, hotels, restaurants, station
announcements, formal speeches and business correspondence run on `ございます`; a
conversation between friends does not. Saying
❌冷蔵庫(れいぞうこ)にビールがございます to a flatmate is not politer, it is
theatrical.

Because it is 丁寧(ていねい)語(ご) and not
尊敬(そんけい)語(ご), `ございます` never honours anybody by itself. That has
one practical consequence worth knowing: strictly, asking about something the
listener possesses calls for the honorific おありです - 
ご質問(しつもん)はおありでしょうか. In real service Japanese,
ご質問(しつもん)はございませんか is what you will hear almost every time, and
it is not treated as an error. Use whichever you like; recognise both.

Three details:

- **ありがとうございます／ありがとうございました.** The present form thanks for
  something still going on or just now done; the past form closes it off. A shop
  says ありがとうございました as you leave with the bag.
- **申(もう)し訳(わけ)ございません** is the standard business apology. A
  minority of usage guides object to it on the grounds that
  申(もう)し訳(わけ)ない is a single adjective that should not be split, and
  prefer 申(もう)し訳(わけ)なく存(ぞん)じます. In practice the objection
  has lost; you will hear ございません everywhere, and using it will not mark you.
- **The い-adjective form is dated.** よろしゅうございます, うれしゅうございます
  and 大(おお)きゅうございます are grammatical, but they belong to older, very
  formal or self-consciously refined speech. The frozen greetings survive; the
  productive pattern has largely gone.

## Contrast

### ございます vs あります

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different altitude. `あります` is polite;
`ございます` is polite and formal, and it announces that you are in a service or
ceremonial situation.

</div>

<pre>
地下(ちか)に駐車場(ちゅうしゃじょう)が<b>あります</b>。
There's a car park in the basement.
</pre>

<pre>
地下(ちか)に駐車場(ちゅうしゃじょう)が<b>ございます</b>。
There is a car park in the basement.
</pre>

Nothing about the car park has changed. The second sentence tells you the speaker
is behind a counter. Reach for `あります` in ordinary conversation, in class and
with colleagues, and switch to `ございます` when you are serving somebody or
speaking formally to a group.

### ございます vs いらっしゃいます

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ございます` is for things and facts; `いらっしゃいます` is for
people you are honouring.

</div>

<pre>
受付(うけつけ)に案内図(あんないず)が<b>ございます</b>。
There is a floor plan at reception.
</pre>

<pre>
受付(うけつけ)に係(かかり)の方(かた)が<b>いらっしゃいます</b>。
There is a member of staff at reception.
</pre>

The mistake goes one way: learners reach for `ございます` about a person because it
feels like the most polite word available. It is not more polite about a person - 
it is wrong, because it treats them as an object. Note that your **own** side does
take a non-honorific verb, but the word is おります, not ございます:
担当(たんとう)の者(もの)が受付(うけつけ)におります.

### ございます vs でございます

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ございます` replaces `あります`; `でございます` replaces
`です`. Existence versus identity.

</div>

<pre>
三階(さんがい)に喫茶店(きっさてん)が<b>ございます</b>。
There is a coffee shop on the third floor.
</pre>

<pre>
こちらが喫茶店(きっさてん)<b>でございます</b>。
This is the coffee shop.
</pre>

The `で` is the giveaway. If the English is "there is a…" or "we have a…", you
want the bare form; if it is "X is Y", you want `でございます`. Mixing them
produces ❌こちらが喫茶店(きっさてん)ございます, which is simply ungrammatical.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ご用件(ようけん)は何(なに)か<b>ござりますか</b>。 → ✅ ご用件(ようけん)は何(なに)か<b>ございますか</b>。
  The `り` becomes `い` before ます - the same irregularity as いらっしゃいます,
  おっしゃいます, なさいます and くださいます.

- ❌ お客(きゃく)様(さま)は二階(にかい)に<b>ございます</b>。 → ✅ お客(きゃく)様(さま)は二階(にかい)に<b>いらっしゃいます</b>。
  A person is not a thing. `ございます` about a customer sounds as though they are
  stock on a shelf.

- ❌ ねえ、消(け)しゴム<b>ございます</b>? → ✅ ねえ、消(け)しゴム<b>ある</b>?
  A register error rather than a grammar one, and the kind that makes people
  laugh. Service Japanese aimed at a classmate is comic.

- ❌ <b>ありがとうでございます</b>。 → ✅ <b>ありがとうございます</b>。
  `ございます` follows the adjective form directly. The `で` belongs only after a
  noun.

## Practice

**1.** 申(もう)し訳(わけ)ありません、本日(ほんじつ)のチケットは売(う)り切(き)れて席(せき)が____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

申(もう)し訳(わけ)ありません、本日(ほんじつ)のチケットは売(う)り切(き)れて席(せき)が<b>ございません</b>。

The negative of `ございます` is `ございません` - there is no ❌ございないです. The
whole sentence is service register, so ありません would sit a step lower than the
rest of it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 田中(たなか)様(さま)は会議室(かいぎしつ)に（ございます・いらっしゃいます）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

田中(たなか)様(さま)は会議室(かいぎしつ)に<b>いらっしゃいます</b>。

`ございます` is the polite form of `ある`, which is used for things. A person you
are honouring takes the honorific of `いる`. If 田中(たなか) were your own
colleague and you were speaking to a client, the answer would change again - to
おります.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, as a hotel receptionist: "There is a convenience store next door."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

隣(となり)にコンビニが<b>ございます</b>。

The subject is a shop, so the verb of existence is `ある`, raised to service
register as `ございます`. Note that no honorific prefix is needed on コンビニ - 
loanwords do not take one.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: こちらが新(あたら)しいパンフレットございます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ こちらが新(あたら)しいパンフレット<b>でございます</b>。

This sentence identifies one thing as another, so it needs the polite copula, not
the polite verb of existence. Without the `で` there is nothing linking the noun
to the sentence at all.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ご意見(いけん)はありませんか。
(b) ご意見(いけん)はございませんか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are polite and both are correct. (a) is ordinary polite Japanese - a chair
asking a familiar committee, or a teacher asking a class. (b) lifts the whole
utterance into formal register, and suits a public meeting, a shareholders'
briefing or a customer.

Neither honours the listener grammatically, since `ございます` is
丁寧(ていねい)語(ご). If you want to raise the listener as well, the
honorific of `ある` is おありです: ご意見(いけん)はおありでしょうか. That is the
strictly correct form, though (b) is far more often heard.

</details>

# でございます

**Meaning** · the formal copula - "is", at the register of a hotel desk or a station announcement

`でございます` replaces `です`. Like `ございます`, it is
丁寧(ていねい)語(ご): it raises the formality of the sentence without
honouring anybody in it. That is why you can say
山田(やまだ)でございます about yourself, and why you must **not** say it about
the person you are talking to.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + でございます | 受付(うけつけ)です → 受付(うけつけ)でございます |
| な-adjective | な-adj + でございます | 結構(けっこう)です → 結構(けっこう)でございます |
| Negative | ではございません | ちがいます → ではございません |
| Past | でございました | 以上(いじょう)でございました |
| Tentative | でございましょう | そのとおりでございましょう |
| い-adjective | frame **not** used | よろしいです → よろしゅうございます |
| About a person you honour | でいらっしゃいます | 社長(しゃちょう)でいらっしゃいます |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `でございます` for things, places, prices, facts, and for
**yourself**. `でいらっしゃいます` for the listener and for anyone else you are
raising.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
こちらが会議室(かいぎしつ)<b>でございます</b>。
This is the meeting room.
</pre>

<pre>
はい、山田(やまだ)<b>でございます</b>。
Yes, Yamada speaking.
</pre>

<pre>
お会計(かいけい)は三千(さんぜん)円(えん)<b>でございます</b>。
That comes to three thousand yen.
</pre>

<pre>
次(つぎ)は終点(しゅうてん)、東京(とうきょう)<b>でございます</b>。
The next stop is the last one, Tokyo.
</pre>

<pre>
こちらのお席(せき)はすべて禁煙(きんえん)<b>でございます</b>。
All of the seats in this section are non-smoking.
</pre>

<pre>
申(もう)し訳(わけ)ございませんが、その商品(しょうひん)は当店(とうてん)の取(と)り扱(あつか)い<b>ではございません</b>。
I'm sorry, but that isn't a product we carry.
</pre>

<pre>
担当(たんとう)の者(もの)は本日(ほんじつ)休(やす)み<b>でございます</b>ので、明日(あした)あらためてご連絡(れんらく)いたします。
The person handling this is off today, so we'll be in touch again tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
本日(ほんじつ)はお忙(いそが)しいところお越(こ)しいただき、ありがとうございます。司会(しかい)を務(つと)めます佐藤(さとう)<b>でございます</b>。
Thank you for coming today despite your busy schedules. I'm Sato, and I'll be chairing.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Announcements, counters, telephones and formal speeches. In a shop or a hotel it
is the default copula and nobody notices it; in a conversation with a friend it
would be as odd as answering the door in a uniform. Between those extremes, a
business meeting will use it for set pieces - introductions, figures, conclusions
 - and drop back to `です` for ordinary discussion.

The single most useful thing to keep straight is that `でございます` does not
honour. Saying ❌田中(たなか)様(さま)でございますか to a visitor is a real
error, not merely a stiff one: it equates the guest with a price or a room number.
The honorific copula is `でいらっしゃいます`, and it is used the same way for
descriptions: お元気(げんき)でいらっしゃいますか,
社長(しゃちょう)の奥様(おくさま)でいらっしゃいます.

Some notes on the edges:

- **Negatives.** ではございません is the formal negative. In quick speech people
  reduce it to じゃございません, which is a mixed register and is best avoided in
  writing.
- **い-adjectives.** There is no ❌よろしいでございますか. The forms that exist are
  よろしゅうございますか, which sounds old-fashioned, and よろしいでしょうか, which
  is what you should actually say.
- **Placement.** `でございます` closes the sentence. Inside a sentence you use the
  て-form でございまして, which is heavy and almost entirely a written or
  speech-making form.
- **Do not stack it on 敬語(けいご) you have already used.**
  ご覧(らん)になるお品(しな)でございます is fine, but
  ❌ご覧(らん)になられるお品(しな)でございます piles two honorific layers
  under the formal copula.

## Contrast

### でございます vs です

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different height. `です` is polite; `でございます`
is polite and formal, and it signals a service or ceremonial setting.

</div>

<pre>
これは去年(きょねん)の資料(しりょう)<b>です</b>。
This is last year's material.
</pre>

<pre>
こちらが昨年度(さくねんど)の資料(しりょう)<b>でございます</b>。
This is last year's material.
</pre>

Notice how much moves with the copula. `これ` becomes `こちら`,
去年(きょねん) becomes 昨年度(さくねんど). Register is carried by the whole
sentence, and a lone `でございます` on top of casual vocabulary sounds like a joke.

### でございます vs でいらっしゃいます

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `でございます` about yourself and about things;
`でいらっしゃいます` about the person you are honouring. Getting this backwards is
the classic 敬語(けいご) error.

</div>

<pre>
失礼(しつれい)ですが、ご予約(よやく)の方(かた)<b>でいらっしゃいます</b>か。
Excuse me - do you have a reservation with us?
</pre>

<pre>
受付(うけつけ)の木村(きむら)<b>でございます</b>。
I'm Kimura from reception.
</pre>

The pair shows the whole three-way system in two lines. The first raises the
guest; the second simply formalises a statement about the speaker. Swap them and
you have honoured yourself while treating the guest as an object - which is why
❌私(わたし)が担当(たんとう)でいらっしゃいます is such a jarring sentence.

### でございます vs ～になります

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `になる` means "becomes". For "is", the formal copula is
`でございます`.

</div>

<pre>
こちらがお飲(の)み物(もの)のメニュー<b>でございます</b>。
Here is the drinks menu.
</pre>

<pre>
合計(ごうけい)で二千(にせん)円(えん)<b>でございます</b>。
That's two thousand yen altogether.
</pre>

You will hear こちらメニューになります and
二千(にせん)円(えん)になります constantly in Japanese shops. It is common
enough to have its own nickname - マニュアル敬語(けいご), "manual keigo" - and
a large number of speakers consider it wrong, on the grounds that nothing is
turning into anything. `になります` is correct when there really is a change
（四月(しがつ)から新(あたら)しい料金(りょうきん)になります）. For a plain
identification, `でございます` is the safe choice.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 田中(たなか)様(さま)<b>でございます</b>か。 → ✅ 田中(たなか)様(さま)<b>でいらっしゃいます</b>か。
  `でございます` does not raise anybody. Asked of a guest, it puts them in the same
  grammatical box as the room key.

- ❌ 私(わたし)は鈴木(すずき)<b>でいらっしゃいます</b>。 → ✅ 私(わたし)は鈴木(すずき)<b>でございます</b>。
  The mirror error, and the one learners make when they decide
  `でいらっしゃいます` is simply "the politest version".

- ❌ お席(せき)はこちらで<b>よろしいでございますか</b>。 → ✅ お席(せき)はこちらで<b>よろしいでしょうか</b>。
  An い-adjective cannot take `でございます`. Either use でしょうか, or the
  old-fashioned よろしゅうございますか.

- ❌ こちら、お釣(つ)り<b>になります</b>。 → ✅ こちら、お釣(つ)り<b>でございます</b>。
  The change is nothing to do with becoming. Very widely heard, and very widely
  disliked.

## Practice

**1.** お電話(でんわ)ありがとうございます。山(やま)の上(うえ)ホテル、予約(よやく)係(がかり)____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お電話(でんわ)ありがとうございます。山(やま)の上(うえ)ホテル、予約(よやく)係(がかり)<b>でございます</b>。

You are identifying yourself and your own department, so the formal copula is
right. `でいらっしゃいます` here would honour yourself, which is the one thing
敬語(けいご) never lets you do.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 失礼(しつれい)ですが、部長(ぶちょう)の奥様(おくさま)（でございます・でいらっしゃいます）か。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

失礼(しつれい)ですが、部長(ぶちょう)の奥様(おくさま)<b>でいらっしゃいます</b>か。

The sentence identifies the person in front of you, so it needs the honorific
copula. `でございます` would be merely formal, and asking a guest
❌奥様(おくさま)でございますか sounds as though you are reading a label.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, as a shop assistant: "This one is this year's newest model."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

こちらが今年(ことし)の最新(さいしん)モデル<b>でございます</b>。

A product is a thing, so the formal copula is the right choice, and `こちら`
rather than `これ` keeps the whole sentence at the same height. ❌モデルになります
would say the item is turning into a model.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 田中(たなか)様(さま)、こちらがご注文(ちゅうもん)の品(しな)になります。合計(ごうけい)で千五百(せんごひゃく)円(えん)になります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 田中(たなか)様(さま)、こちらがご注文(ちゅうもん)の品(しな)<b>でございます</b>。合計(ごうけい)で千五百(せんごひゃく)円(えん)<b>でございます</b>。

Neither the goods nor the total is *becoming* anything, so `になります` has no
work to do. Both clauses are plain identifications and take the formal copula.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 受付(うけつけ)は一階(いっかい)です。
(b) 受付(うけつけ)は一階(いっかい)でございます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are correct and neither honours anyone - `でございます` is
丁寧(ていねい)語(ご), so the difference is formality, not respect.

(a) is ordinary polite Japanese: a colleague telling a visiting friend where to
go, or a sign in a small office. (b) places the speaker on the serving side of a
counter. Said by a hotel employee it is exactly right; said by one student to
another it sounds like a performance.

</details>
