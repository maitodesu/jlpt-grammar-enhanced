# N1 - Formal ways to say based on, in line with, and by means of


---

# ～に則って

**Meaning** · in accordance with; in conformity with - what was done followed the letter of a rule, a law, a ceremony's established form or a precedent

<code>則(のっと)る</code> is a verb meaning "to take something as one's standard and
follow it". What stands in front of it is always a **codified norm**: a statute, a
set of regulations, the traditional order of a ceremony, a long-standing custom, an
earlier case treated as binding. The sentence then asserts that the procedure was
correct - not merely sensible, but *proper*. This is the language of legal texts,
company rules and formal ceremonies. Nobody says it about their afternoon.

## Formation

<code>則(のっと)る</code> is a う-verb (五段(ごだん)/godan/Group 1) and takes its
norm with `に`, never `を`. The pattern attaches to a **noun** only.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + に則(のっと)って | 法律(ほうりつ) → 法律(ほうりつ)に則(のっと)って |
| Noun (stiffer, documents) | Noun + に則(のっと)り | 規約(きやく) → 規約(きやく)に則(のっと)り |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + に則(のっと)った + Noun | 伝統(でんとう) → 伝統(でんとう)に則(のっと)った儀式(ぎしき) |
| Same, non-past shape | Noun + に則(のっと)る + Noun | 手(て)続(つづ)き → 手(て)続(つづ)きに則(のっと)る処理(しょり) |
| Negative | Noun + に則(のっと)らない + Noun | 前例(ぜんれい) → 前例(ぜんれい)に則(のっと)らない判断(はんだん) |
| Written in kana (common) | Noun + にのっとって | ルール → ルールにのっとって |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the reading. <code>則</code> is ソク in 規則(きそく) and
原則(げんそく), but the verb
則(のっと)る is **のっとる**, and the ～って ending is okurigana outside the kanji:
則(のっと)って. Learners who guess そくって or きそくって from the compound reading
give themselves away instantly.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
ルールに<b>のっとって</b>試合(しあい)が行(おこな)われた。
The match was played by the rules.
</pre>

<pre>
会社(かいしゃ)の規則(きそく)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>処理(しょり)します。
We will handle it in accordance with company regulations.
</pre>

<pre>
式(しき)は昔(むかし)からの作法(さほう)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>進(すす)められた。
The ceremony proceeded according to the old forms.
</pre>

<pre>
憲法(けんぽう)<b>に則(のっと)り</b>、国会(こっかい)が召集(しょうしゅう)された。
The Diet was convened in accordance with the constitution.
</pre>

<pre>
選考(せんこう)は募集(ぼしゅう)要項(ようこう)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>公平(こうへい)に行(おこな)われます。
Selection is carried out fairly, in accordance with the terms of the call for applications.
</pre>

<pre>
遺言(ゆいごん)が残(のこ)されていなかったため、民法(みんぽう)の定(さだ)め<b>に則(のっと)って</b>遺産(いさん)が分(わ)けられた。
As no will had been left, the estate was divided according to the provisions of the civil code.
</pre>

<pre>
新(あたら)しい制度(せいど)とはいえ、運用(うんよう)はこれまでの前例(ぜんれい)<b>に則(のっと)った</b>形(かたち)になるでしょう。
New system though it is, in practice it will be run along the lines of existing precedent.
</pre>

<pre>
当社(とうしゃ)は個人(こじん)情報(じょうほう)保護(ほご)法(ほう)<b>に則(のっと)り</b>、お客様(きゃくさま)の情報(じょうほう)を管理(かんり)しております。
We manage customer information in accordance with the Personal Information Protection Act.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This belongs to contracts, official notices, ceremonies and formal speeches, and
almost nowhere else. You will meet it in a statute, a set of 定款(ていかん), a
press release, a wedding or funeral programme, a sports federation's rulebook. In
ordinary conversation a Japanese speaker says
<code>ルール<b>どおりに</b></code> or <code>決(き)まりに従(したが)って</code>. Say
<code>夕食(ゆうしょく)は母(はは)の方針(ほうしん)に則(のっと)って作(つく)った</code>
and you will get a laugh, because you have dressed a family kitchen in the clothes
of a statute.

The `に則り` shape is if anything the more frequent one in written notices; `に則って`
is what you hear read aloud at a ceremony. Neither has a casual form, and there is
no contraction.

<div class="note">

**Note** - written in kana, `のっとる` is identical to 乗(の)っ取(と)る, "to
hijack, to take over". <code>会社(かいしゃ)をのっとる</code> and
<code>規則(きそく)にのっとる</code> are unrelated words that sound the same; the
particle (`を` versus `に`) is what separates them, and it is one reason writers of
formal documents keep the kanji <code>則</code>.

</div>

The pattern carries a faint note of self-justification. Announcing that you acted
<code>規則(きそく)に則(のっと)って</code> is a way of saying that whatever the
outcome, no one can fault the process - which is exactly why organisations reach for
it when something has gone wrong.

## Contrast

### ～に則(のっと)って vs ～に基(もと)づいて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に基(もと)づいて</code> takes anything that can serve as
**grounds**, including data and facts. <code>～に則(のっと)って</code> takes only a
**norm you conform your conduct to**, and it comments on the *manner* of doing.

</div>

<pre>
調査(ちょうさ)結果(けっか)<b>に基(もと)づいて</b>対策(たいさく)を決(き)めた。
We decided on countermeasures on the basis of the survey findings.
</pre>

<pre>
所定(しょてい)の手(て)順(じゅん)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>対策(たいさく)を決(き)めた。
We decided on countermeasures by following the prescribed procedure.
</pre>

The first says where the content of the decision came from; the second says nothing
about the content and everything about the process. Findings, figures, evidence and
experience are all fine before <code>基(もと)づいて</code> and all impossible
before <code>則(のっと)って</code>, because you cannot *obey* a number.

### ～に則(のっと)って vs ～に従(したが)って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に従(したが)って</code> is the everyday word for
following, and its object can be a person. <code>～に則(のっと)って</code> is its
ceremonial counterpart, and its object must be an impersonal norm.

</div>

<pre>
係(かかり)の人(ひと)の指示(しじ)<b>に従(したが)って</b>外(そと)に出(で)てください。
Please go outside, following the instructions of the staff.
</pre>

<pre>
避難(ひなん)訓練(くんれん)は消防法(しょうぼうほう)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>実施(じっし)されます。
The evacuation drill is conducted in accordance with the fire services act.
</pre>

<code>従(したが)う</code> works at every level of the language: a child follows a
parent's word, a driver follows a sign. <code>則(のっと)る</code> works only where
the thing followed has been written down or handed down. Replace
<code>係(かかり)の人(ひと)の指示(しじ)</code> with
<code>則(のっと)って</code> and it sounds as though the staff member's off-hand
remark had the standing of a statute.

### ～に則(のっと)って vs ～に沿(そ)って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に沿(そ)って</code> keeps to a line without wandering
off it, and the line can be soft - a plan, a theme, somebody's wishes.
<code>～に則(のっと)って</code> demands that the line be a **rule**.

</div>

<pre>
ご要望(ようぼう)<b>に沿(そ)って</b>日程(にってい)を組(く)み直(なお)しました。
We have rearranged the schedule in line with your wishes.
</pre>

<pre>
表彰(ひょうしょう)式(しき)は例年(れいねん)の次第(しだい)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>行(おこな)います。
The award ceremony will be held according to the customary order of proceedings.
</pre>

A wish is not a rule, so ❌<code>ご要望(ようぼう)に則(のっと)って</code> is wrong.
Going the other way is merely weaker rather than wrong:
<code>次第(しだい)に沿(そ)って</code> is possible and means the ceremony broadly
tracked the programme, where <code>則(のっと)って</code> insists it was done
properly.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>進(すす)めます。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)の指示(しじ)<b>に従(したが)って</b>進(すす)めます。
  A person cannot be a norm. Either follow the person with
  <code>従(したが)って</code>, or make the norm explicit - their instruction, their
  policy - and then <code>則(のっと)る</code> still sounds too grand for one manager.

- ❌ データ<b>に則(のっと)って</b>結論(けつろん)を出(だ)す → ✅ データ<b>に基(もと)づいて</b>結論(けつろん)を出(だ)す
  Data are grounds, not rules. Nothing that merely *informs* a decision can stand
  before <code>則(のっと)って</code>.

- ❌ 規則(きそく)<b>を則(のっと)って</b> → ✅ 規則(きそく)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>
  The verb is intransitive and marks its norm with `に`. Writing `を` here also
  collides with 乗(の)っ取(と)る, which really does take `を`.

- ❌ 規則(きそく)に<b>則(そく)って</b> → ✅ 規則(きそく)に<b>則(のっと)って</b>
  The compound reading ソク does not carry over to the verb. Note too that the
  okurigana って sits outside the furigana group: the kanji alone is のっと.

## Practice

**1.** 開票(かいひょう)は公職(こうしょく)選挙(せんきょ)法(ほう)____行(おこな)われた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

公職(こうしょく)選挙(せんきょ)法(ほう)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>行(おこな)われた。

A statute is the model case for this pattern, and counting votes is exactly the kind
of procedure that has to be certified as properly done. <code>に則(のっと)り</code>
would be equally correct and slightly stiffer.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 過去(かこ)三年(さんねん)の売(う)り上(あ)げ（に則(のっと)って・に基(もと)づいて）来年度(らいねんど)の目標(もくひょう)を立(た)てる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

過去(かこ)三年(さんねん)の売(う)り上(あ)げ<b>に基(もと)づいて</b>

Sales figures are grounds you reason from, not a rule you obey. Only
<code>基(もと)づいて</code> accepts data. Swap in a target-setting *procedure* laid
down by head office and <code>則(のっと)って</code> becomes possible again.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The wedding was held in accordance with Shinto tradition."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

結婚式(けっこんしき)は神道(しんとう)の伝統(でんとう)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>行(おこな)われた。

A religious tradition is a handed-down norm with a fixed order of acts, which is
precisely what this pattern wants. The noun phrase
<code>伝統(でんとう)に則(のっと)った結婚式(けっこんしき)</code> works just as well.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 審査(しんさ)は委員(いいん)長(ちょう)の気持(きも)ちに則(のっと)って進(すす)められた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 審査(しんさ)は審査(しんさ)基準(きじゅん)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>進(すす)められた。

A feeling is not a codified norm, and the original sentence accidentally says the
opposite of what such a report wants to say - that the process was subjective. If
the chair's view really was decisive, write
<code>委員(いいん)長(ちょう)の意向(いこう)に沿(そ)って</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 会員(かいいん)規約(きやく)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>退会(たいかい)の手(て)続(つづ)きをした。
(b) 会員(かいいん)規約(きやく)<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>退会(たいかい)の手(て)続(つづ)きをした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the withdrawal was carried out the way the membership rules prescribe: the
rules dictated each step, and the sentence is a claim that nothing was skipped.

(b) says the rules were read and taken into account before acting, which leaves open
the possibility that the person did something the rules do not actually cover. In a
dispute, (a) is the sentence you want on your side.

</details>

# ～に即して

**Meaning** · in keeping with; fitted to - the action or judgement is shaped to match a reality as it actually stands, rather than applied from a template

<code>即(そく)する</code> means "to be right up against something, to fit it
exactly, to leave no gap". The noun in front is a **state of affairs**:
実情(じつじょう), 実態(じったい), 現状(げんじょう), 事実(じじつ),
時代(じだい), the particular case in hand. The sentence then says that what was
done was cut to the shape of that reality. The unspoken contrast is always with
something rigid and off-the-shelf - a rule applied blindly, a plan drawn up in an
office far from the site.

## Formation

<code>即(そく)する</code> is a する-verb and marks the reality it fits with `に`.
The pattern takes a **noun** only; a clause has to be packaged into one first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + に即(そく)して | 実情(じつじょう) → 実情(じつじょう)に即(そく)して |
| Noun (stiffer, documents) | Noun + に即(そく)し | 現状(げんじょう) → 現状(げんじょう)に即(そく)し |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + に即(そく)した + Noun | 実態(じったい) → 実態(じったい)に即(そく)した運用(うんよう) |
| Same, non-past shape | Noun + に即(そく)する + Noun | 時代(じだい) → 時代(じだい)に即(そく)する教育(きょういく) |
| As the predicate | Noun + に即(そく)している | 事実(じじつ) → 事実(じじつ)に即(そく)している |
| Negative | Noun + に即(そく)さない + Noun | 実情(じつじょう) → 実情(じつじょう)に即(そく)さない規則(きそく) |
| Nominalised clause | ～という + Noun + に即(そく)して | 事実(じじつ) → 人口(じんこう)が減(へ)ったという事実(じじつ)に即(そく)して |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the spelling. <code>即</code> is the kanji for fitting reality:
事実(じじつ)に即(そく)した, 現場(げんば)に即(そく)した. Some publishers'
style guides use <code>則</code> - the kanji of 規則(きそく) - when the thing fitted
is a law or a norm rather than a reality, and the reading stays そくして either way.
Unless you are copy-editing to a house style, write <code>即</code> for realities and use
<code>に則(のっと)って</code> when you mean obeying a rule; that keeps the two
apart cleanly.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
現状(げんじょう)<b>に即(そく)して</b>計画(けいかく)を見(み)直(なお)す。
We will revise the plan to fit the situation as it now stands.
</pre>

<pre>
その説明(せつめい)は事実(じじつ)<b>に即(そく)していない</b>。
That account does not match the facts.
</pre>

<pre>
法律(ほうりつ)は時代(じだい)<b>に即(そく)した</b>形(かたち)に改(あらた)められるべきだ。
The law ought to be amended into a form that fits the times.
</pre>

<pre>
地域(ちいき)の実情(じつじょう)<b>に即(そく)した</b>支援(しえん)が求(もと)められている。
What is called for is support tailored to conditions in each locality.
</pre>

<pre>
規則(きそく)をそのまま当(あ)てはめるのではなく、個々(ここ)の事情(じじょう)<b>に即(そく)して</b>判断(はんだん)します。
Rather than applying the rule as it stands, we judge each case on its actual circumstances.
</pre>

<pre>
現場(げんば)の実態(じったい)<b>に即(そく)さない</b>指示(しじ)は、結局(けっきょく)守(まも)られない。
Instructions that do not match conditions on the ground end up being ignored.
</pre>

<pre>
お客様(きゃくさま)のご事情(じじょう)<b>に即(そく)して</b>、返済(へんさい)計画(けいかく)を組(く)み直(なお)すことも可能(かのう)です。
We can also rebuild the repayment plan around your individual circumstances.
</pre>

<pre>
今回(こんかい)の報告書(ほうこくしょ)は、現地(げんち)で見(み)てきたこと<b>に即(そく)して</b>書(か)かれている。
This report is written to match what was actually seen on site.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Like its neighbours in this group, `に即して` lives in official prose: policy papers,
committee reports, administrative guidance, contracts, the speech a director gives
when announcing a change of course. It is not conversational. Where a report writes
<code>実情(じつじょう)に即(そく)した対応(たいおう)</code>, a colleague in the
corridor says <code>実際(じっさい)の様子(ようす)を見(み)て決(き)める</code>
or simply <code>現場(げんば)に合(あ)わせて</code>. That last one -
<code>～に合(あ)わせて</code> - is the everyday equivalent to reach for.

The pattern carries an argumentative edge that the dictionary gloss hides. Saying
<code>実態(じったい)に即(そく)した制度(せいど)が必要(ひつよう)だ</code> is a
polite way of saying the present system is detached from reality. It is a standard
move in Japanese policy writing, and a reader hears the criticism even though no one
has been named.

The `に即し` form belongs to written documents; `に即して` is what gets read aloud.
There is no casual contraction, and no form of this that sounds natural in speech
between friends.

## Contrast

### ～に即(そく)して vs ～に則(のっと)って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に即(そく)して</code> fits itself to a **reality**.
<code>～に則(のっと)って</code> obeys a **rule**. Reality can be untidy; a rule
cannot.

</div>

<pre>
処分(しょぶん)は就業(しゅうぎょう)規則(きそく)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>決(き)めた。
The disciplinary measure was decided in accordance with the work rules.
</pre>

<pre>
処分(しょぶん)は本人(ほんにん)の事情(じじょう)<b>に即(そく)して</b>決(き)めた。
The disciplinary measure was decided in the light of his actual circumstances.
</pre>

These two sentences describe opposite defences. The first says the outcome was not
the speaker's choice - the rulebook produced it. The second says the rulebook was
not applied mechanically. Notice that they can even conflict:
<code>実情(じつじょう)に即(そく)して</code> is often precisely a decision *not*
to follow the standard procedure to the letter.

### ～に即(そく)して vs ～に基(もと)づいて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に基(もと)づいて</code> **derives** the result from an
authority or a body of evidence. <code>～に即(そく)して</code> **shapes** the result
so that it fits a situation.

</div>

<pre>
調査(ちょうさ)結果(けっか)<b>に基(もと)づいて</b>新(あたら)しい基準(きじゅん)を作(つく)った。
We drew up the new standard on the basis of the survey findings.
</pre>

<pre>
現場(げんば)の状況(じょうきょう)<b>に即(そく)した</b>基準(きじゅん)に作(つく)り直(なお)した。
We rewrote the standard so that it fits conditions on the ground.
</pre>

The first sentence tells you where the content came from; the second tells you what
the content now matches. That is why <code>基(もと)づいて</code> naturally takes
data, laws and agreements, and <code>即(そく)して</code> naturally takes
実情(じつじょう), 実態(じったい) and 現状(げんじょう). Neither list is a hard
boundary, but the direction of the relation is.

### ～に即(そく)して vs ～に応(おう)じて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に応(おう)じて</code> **varies** as the noun varies:
change the input, change the output. <code>～に即(そく)して</code> stays
**faithful** to one situation, without any implication of a scale.

</div>

<pre>
年齢(ねんれい)<b>に応(おう)じて</b>料金(りょうきん)が変(か)わります。
The fee varies according to age.
</pre>

<pre>
利用者(りようしゃ)の実態(じったい)<b>に即(そく)して</b>料金(りょうきん)体系(たいけい)を見(み)直(なお)しました。
We revised the fee structure to match how users actually use the service.
</pre>

<code>応(おう)じて</code> needs something that comes in degrees or types - age,
income, the season, the size of an order. <code>即(そく)して</code> needs a single
concrete reality to be true to. They overlap in phrases like
<code>場合(ばあい)に応(おう)じて</code> and
<code>ケースに即(そく)して</code>, but even there the first means "it depends" and
the second means "we look at what the case actually is".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 部長(ぶちょう)のご希望(きぼう)<b>に即(そく)して</b>日程(にってい)を決(き)めます。 → ✅ 部長(ぶちょう)のご希望(きぼう)<b>に沿(そ)って</b>日程(にってい)を決(き)めます。
  A wish is not a state of affairs. Wishes, plans and policies are lines you keep to,
  which is <code>沿(そ)って</code>.

- ❌ 実情(じつじょう)<b>を即(そく)して</b> → ✅ 実情(じつじょう)<b>に即(そく)して</b>
  <code>即(そく)する</code> is intransitive here and takes `に`. The `を` version is
  a straightforward particle slip, and it is the most common one on this pattern.

- ❌ 昔話(むかしばなし)<b>に即(そく)して</b>映画(えいが)を作(つく)った。 → ✅ 昔話(むかしばなし)<b>をもとに</b>映画(えいが)を作(つく)った。
  A folk tale is raw material you rework, not a reality you conform to. Use
  <code>をもとに</code> when something new is being built out of the noun.

- ❌ 現状(げんじょう)に<b>即(そっ)して</b> → ✅ 現状(げんじょう)に<b>即(そく)して</b>
  Before し the reading is そく, with no gemination. <code>即</code> does geminate in
  即決(そっけつ) and 即興(そっきょう) - before k-sounds - which is where the
  wrong reading comes from.

## Practice

**1.** 外国人(がいこくじん)住民(じゅうみん)が増(ふ)えているので、窓口(まどぐち)の対応(たいおう)も地域(ちいき)の実情(じつじょう)____変(か)えていく必要(ひつよう)がある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

地域(ちいき)の実情(じつじょう)<b>に即(そく)して</b>変(か)えていく

実情(じつじょう) is the archetypal noun for this pattern, and the sentence is
arguing that the current counter service does not fit what the area is actually like.
<code>に即(そく)した対応(たいおう)に変(か)えていく</code> would also work.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この判決(はんけつ)は事実(じじつ)（に即(そく)している・に応(おう)じている）とは言(い)えない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

事実(じじつ)<b>に即(そく)している</b>とは言(い)えない。

The complaint is that the ruling does not match what actually happened - a question
of faithfulness to one reality, not of varying with a scale.
<code>応(おう)じて</code> would need something gradable like the severity of the
offence.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Rules drawn up thirty years ago no longer fit the way people work today."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

三十年(さんじゅうねん)前(まえ)に作(つく)られた規則(きそく)は、もう今(いま)の働(はたら)き方(かた)<b>に即(そく)していない</b>。

The predicate form <code>に即(そく)していない</code> is the natural way to say
that something has come adrift from reality, and it is a stock phrase in reports
arguing for reform.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 会社(かいしゃ)の規則(きそく)に即(そく)して処分(しょぶん)が決(き)められたので、文句(もんく)は言(い)えない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 会社(かいしゃ)の規則(きそく)<b>に則(のっと)って</b>処分(しょぶん)が決(き)められたので、文句(もんく)は言(い)えない。

The second half - "so you cannot complain" - only follows if the rulebook was
*obeyed*, and that is <code>則(のっと)って</code>. With
<code>即(そく)して</code> the sentence would be saying the rules were bent to fit
the case, which is exactly what someone might complain about.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 現場(げんば)の意見(いけん)<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>制度(せいど)を設計(せっけい)した。
(b) 現場(げんば)の実態(じったい)<b>に即(そく)して</b>制度(せいど)を設計(せっけい)した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the views of the people on the ground were heard and taken into account
before designing the system. It is a claim about process, and it leaves open how much
of what they said survived into the design.

(b) says the finished system matches what actually happens on the ground. It is a
claim about the result. You can honestly say (a) about a system that turned out to be
unworkable; you cannot say (b) about one.

</details>

# ～に照らして

**Meaning** · in the light of; measured against - holds the matter up against a law, a standard or a body of past experience, and reaches a verdict

<code>照(て)らす</code> is "to shine a light on something". The noun in front is the
**yardstick** you shine: a statute, a set of criteria, previous cases, what you
personally have seen over the years. What follows is almost always a judgement -
違法(いほう)だ, 妥当(だとう)だ, 認(みと)められない,
問題(もんだい)がある, 判断(はんだん)する. The pattern does not describe an action
being carried out; it describes something being assessed.

## Formation

<code>照(て)らす</code> is a う-verb (五段(ごだん)/godan/Group 1). The yardstick
is marked with `に`, and the pattern takes a **noun** only.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + に照(て)らして | 法(ほう) → 法(ほう)に照(て)らして |
| Noun (stiffer, documents) | Noun + に照(て)らし | 規定(きてい) → 規定(きてい)に照(て)らし |
| Conditional | Noun + に照(て)らすと / に照(て)らせば | 判例(はんれい) → 判例(はんれい)に照(て)らせば |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + に照(て)らした + Noun | 基準(きじゅん) → 基準(きじゅん)に照(て)らした評価(ひょうか) |
| Cross-checking two things | A を B に照(て)らし合(あ)わせる | 証言(しょうげん) → 証言(しょうげん)を記録(きろく)に照(て)らし合(あ)わせる |
| Set phrase | 経験(けいけん)に照(て)らして | - → 経験(けいけん)に照(て)らして言(い)えば |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>照(て)らし合(あ)わせる</code> in the fifth row is a related but
separate verb, and it is the one you want when two documents are being compared with
each other rather than one thing being judged against a standard. It is also far more
usable in ordinary working Japanese than <code>に照(て)らして</code> itself.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
その行為(こうい)は法(ほう)<b>に照(て)らして</b>明(あき)らかに違法(いほう)だ。
Measured against the law, that act is plainly illegal.
</pre>

<pre>
過去(かこ)の例(れい)<b>に照(て)らすと</b>、来月(らいげつ)には落(お)ち着(つ)くはずだ。
Judging by past cases, things should settle down next month.
</pre>

<pre>
私(わたし)の経験(けいけん)<b>に照(て)らして</b>言(い)えば、この方法(ほうほう)はうまくいかない。
Speaking from my own experience, this approach will not work.
</pre>

<pre>
評価(ひょうか)基準(きじゅん)<b>に照(て)らして</b>、今回(こんかい)の申請(しんせい)は認(みと)められません。
Measured against the assessment criteria, this application cannot be approved.
</pre>

<pre>
社内(しゃない)の規定(きてい)<b>に照(て)らし</b>、処分(しょぶん)の内容(ないよう)を決(き)めた。
The content of the disciplinary measure was determined against the internal regulations.
</pre>

<pre>
証言(しょうげん)を当時(とうじ)の記録(きろく)<b>に照(て)らし合(あ)わせた</b>ところ、食(く)い違(ちが)いが見(み)つかった。
When the testimony was checked against the records of the time, discrepancies emerged.
</pre>

<pre>
判例(はんれい)<b>に照(て)らせば</b>、この契約(けいやく)条項(じょうこう)は無効(むこう)と判断(はんだん)される可能性(かのうせい)が高(たか)い。
In the light of precedent, this contract clause is likely to be held void.
</pre>

<pre>
当時(とうじ)の常識(じょうしき)<b>に照(て)らして</b>考(かんが)えれば、彼(かれ)の判断(はんだん)だけを責(せ)めることはできない。
Judged by the accepted thinking of the day, his decision alone cannot be blamed.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is courtroom, committee-room and audit-report Japanese. You meet it in
judgements, in disciplinary findings, in inspection reports, in the part of a formal
speech where a decision is being justified. Outside those settings it sounds
theatrical: nobody says
<code>時計(とけい)に照(て)らして遅刻(ちこく)だ</code>. In conversation the same
thought comes out as <code>規則(きそく)で考(かんが)えると</code>,
<code>前(まえ)の例(れい)から見(み)ると</code> or just
<code>今(いま)までの感(かん)じだと</code>.

One shape does survive into ordinary speech:
<code>経験(けいけん)に照(て)らして</code>, and especially
<code>経験(けいけん)に照(て)らして言(い)えば</code>. A senior colleague will
use it in a meeting to introduce an opinion, and it lends the opinion the weight of
years. Said by someone in their twenties it tends to sound as though they are
imitating a manager - a tendency, not a rule, but worth knowing before you try it.

Because the pattern promises a verdict, a sentence that opens with it and then fails
to deliver one feels unfinished. The sentence
<code>基準(きじゅん)に照(て)らして書類(しょるい)を集(あつ)めた</code> reads
oddly; collecting documents is not a judgement.

## Contrast

### ～に照(て)らして vs ～に基(もと)づいて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に基(もと)づいて</code> takes its authority as the
**source** of the action. <code>～に照(て)らして</code> takes it as the
**measuring stick** and delivers a verdict.

</div>

<pre>
条例(じょうれい)<b>に基(もと)づいて</b>指導(しどう)を行(おこな)った。
Guidance was issued under the ordinance.
</pre>

<pre>
条例(じょうれい)<b>に照(て)らして</b>問題(もんだい)があると判断(はんだん)した。
Measured against the ordinance, it was judged to be problematic.
</pre>

In the first, the ordinance empowers the action; in the second, it grades it. This is
why <code>基(もと)づいて</code> is followed by verbs of *doing* -
処分(しょぶん)する, 作成(さくせい)する, 支給(しきゅう)する - and
<code>照(て)らして</code> by verbs and adjectives of *assessing*. The two can
appear in the same paragraph without contradiction, because judging a case and then
acting on the finding are different steps.

### ～に照(て)らして vs ～に即(そく)して

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the direction is reversed.
<code>～に即(そく)して</code> bends the **standard** to fit the reality;
<code>～に照(て)らして</code> measures the **reality** against a fixed standard.

</div>

<pre>
現場(げんば)の実態(じったい)<b>に即(そく)した</b>基準(きじゅん)を作(つく)る。
We will draw up a standard that fits conditions on the ground.
</pre>

<pre>
基準(きじゅん)<b>に照(て)らして</b>現場(げんば)の実態(じったい)を評価(ひょうか)する。
We will evaluate conditions on the ground against the standard.
</pre>

The same two nouns, the same relationship, opposite ends of it. If the standard is
the thing that has to give, you want <code>即(そく)して</code>. If the standard is
fixed and the situation is on trial, you want <code>照(て)らして</code>.

### ～に照(て)らして vs ～と比(くら)べて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～と比(くら)べて</code> compares two things of the same
kind and reports a difference. <code>～に照(て)らして</code> requires the noun to be
a **criterion**, and reports a judgement.

</div>

<pre>
去年(きょねん)<b>と比(くら)べて</b>売(う)り上(あ)げが落(お)ちた。
Sales are down compared with last year.
</pre>

<pre>
目標(もくひょう)額(がく)<b>に照(て)らして</b>売(う)り上(あ)げは十分(じゅうぶん)とは言(い)えない。
Measured against the target figure, sales cannot be called adequate.
</pre>

Last year's sales are a fact you sit beside this year's; the target is a bar the
figure either clears or does not. That is why
❌<code>弟(おとうと)に照(て)らして背(せ)が高(たか)い</code> is impossible - a
brother is not a criterion - while
<code>平均(へいきん)身長(しんちょう)に照(て)らして</code> is at least
conceivable, if stiff.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 弟(おとうと)<b>に照(て)らして</b>背(せ)が高(たか)い。 → ✅ 弟(おとうと)<b>と比(くら)べて</b>背(せ)が高(たか)い。
  The noun before this pattern has to be a standard, not just the other item in a
  comparison.

- ❌ 法律(ほうりつ)<b>に照(て)らして</b>許可(きょか)を出(だ)した。 → ✅ 法律(ほうりつ)<b>に基(もと)づいて</b>許可(きょか)を出(だ)した。
  Issuing a permit is an action taken *under* the law, not a verdict measured against
  it. Only <code>基(もと)づいて</code> fits.

- ❌ 基準(きじゅん)<b>を照(て)らして</b> → ✅ 基準(きじゅん)<b>に照(て)らして</b>
  The standard is marked with `に`. `を` belongs to the literal verb -
  <code>懐中(かいちゅう)電灯(でんとう)で足元(あしもと)を照(て)らす</code>,
  shining a torch on your feet.

- ❌ 規定(きてい)に<b>照(しょう)らして</b> → ✅ 規定(きてい)に<b>照(て)らして</b>
  <code>照</code> is ショウ in 参照(さんしょう) and 照明(しょうめい), but the verb is
  照(て)らす, and らして is okurigana outside the furigana group.

## Practice

**1.** 今回(こんかい)の発言(はつげん)は、社内(しゃない)の行動(こうどう)指針(ししん)____不適切(ふてきせつ)だと判断(はんだん)された。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

行動(こうどう)指針(ししん)<b>に照(て)らして</b>不適切(ふてきせつ)だと判断(はんだん)された

A code of conduct is a criterion, and the sentence delivers a verdict on the remark.
<code>に照(て)らし</code> would fit an internal document just as well.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 就業(しゅうぎょう)規則(きそく)（に照(て)らして・に基(もと)づいて）残業(ざんぎょう)代(だい)を支給(しきゅう)する。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

就業(しゅうぎょう)規則(きそく)<b>に基(もと)づいて</b>支給(しきゅう)する。

Paying overtime is an action the rules authorise, not a judgement about whether
something measures up. Change the verb to
<code>支給(しきゅう)対象(たいしょう)かどうかを判断(はんだん)する</code> and
<code>照(て)らして</code> becomes the natural choice.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "In the light of past cases, a decision is unlikely to come this year."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

過去(かこ)の事例(じれい)<b>に照(て)らせば</b>、今年(ことし)中(じゅう)に結論(けつろん)が出(で)ることはないだろう。

The conditional <code>に照(て)らせば</code> is the usual shape when the yardstick
supports a prediction rather than a ruling. Note that in 今年(ことし)中(じゅう) the character
<code>中</code> is じゅう, not ちゅう.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 基準(きじゅん)に照(て)らして、必要(ひつよう)な書類(しょるい)を集(あつ)めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 基準(きじゅん)<b>に照(て)らして</b>、書類(しょるい)が足(た)りているかどうかを確(たし)かめた。

The original stops short of a judgement, and collecting documents is an ordinary
action - <code>基準(きじゅん)に従(したが)って集(あつ)めた</code> would say
that. This pattern needs an assessment on the other side of it.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 時代(じだい)の流(なが)れ<b>に即(そく)して</b>校則(こうそく)を見(み)直(なお)す。
(b) 今(いま)の常識(じょうしき)<b>に照(て)らして</b>校則(こうそく)を見(み)直(なお)す。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the school rules are to be reshaped so that they fit how the world now is.
The rules are the thing being altered, and the times are simply the shape they must
take.

(b) says today's common sense is being used as a measuring stick, and each rule will
be tested against it - some passing, some failing. (b) implies a rule-by-rule
verdict; (a) implies a redesign.

</details>

# ～を踏まえて

**Meaning** · taking X as the footing; in the light of - X is something already established, and whatever comes next was decided with X firmly underfoot

<code>踏(ふ)まえる</code> is literally "to plant your feet on". What goes in front is
a settled thing you cannot now ignore: last meeting's discussion, a survey result, a
change in the law, the lesson of a failure. The sentence then says that the next
step was taken **with that in view**. It does not say the result was made *out of*
X, and it does not say the result is *authorised* by X. It says X was taken fully
into account.

## Formation

<code>踏(ふ)まえる</code> is a る-verb (一段(いちだん)/ichidan/Group 2) and is
transitive, so its object is marked with `を`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + を踏(ふ)まえて | 結果(けっか) → 結果(けっか)を踏(ふ)まえて |
| Noun (stiffer, documents) | Noun + を踏(ふ)まえ | 議論(ぎろん) → 議論(ぎろん)を踏(ふ)まえ |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + を踏(ふ)まえた + Noun | 反省(はんせい) → 反省(はんせい)を踏(ふ)まえた計画(けいかく) |
| Emphasising the step order | Noun + を踏(ふ)まえた上(うえ)で | 意見(いけん) → ご意見(いけん)を踏(ふ)まえた上(うえ)で |
| Clause | ～という点(てん) / こと + を踏(ふ)まえて | - → 人手(ひとで)が足(た)りないことを踏(ふ)まえて |
| Polite (spoken, business) | Noun + を踏(ふ)まえまして | ご指摘(してき) → ご指摘(してき)を踏(ふ)まえまして |
| Negative | Noun + を踏(ふ)まえずに | 実情(じつじょう) → 実情(じつじょう)を踏(ふ)まえずに |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the `を` is not optional and not interchangeable.
❌<code>議論(ぎろん)に踏(ふ)まえて</code> is the single most common error on this
pattern, produced by analogy with its `に` neighbours
<code>に基(もと)づいて</code> and <code>に沿(そ)って</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
前回(ぜんかい)の議論(ぎろん)<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>、案(あん)を直(なお)しました。
I revised the proposal in the light of last time's discussion.
</pre>

<pre>
アンケートの結果(けっか)<b>を踏(ふ)まえ</b>、メニューを変更(へんこう)します。
Taking the survey results into account, we are changing the menu.
</pre>

<pre>
昨年(さくねん)の失敗(しっぱい)<b>を踏(ふ)まえた</b>計画(けいかく)になっている。
The plan has been built with last year's failure in mind.
</pre>

<pre>
お客様(きゃくさま)のご意見(いけん)<b>を踏(ふ)まえまして</b>、受付(うけつけ)時間(じかん)を延(の)ばすことにいたしました。
In response to customer feedback, we have decided to extend our reception hours.
</pre>

<pre>
法(ほう)改正(かいせい)<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>、社内(しゃない)規定(きてい)を見(み)直(なお)す必要(ひつよう)があります。
In view of the amendment to the law, we need to review our internal regulations.
</pre>

<pre>
現場(げんば)の声(こえ)<b>を踏(ふ)まえた上(うえ)で</b>決(き)めたことなので、反対(はんたい)は少(すく)ないだろう。
It was decided only after the views from the shop floor had been taken in, so there should be little opposition.
</pre>

<pre>
人口(じんこう)が減(へ)り続(つづ)けているという事実(じじつ)<b>を踏(ふ)まえずに</b>、将来(しょうらい)の計画(けいかく)は立(た)てられない。
You cannot draw up plans for the future while ignoring the fact that the population keeps falling.
</pre>

<pre>
以上(いじょう)申(もう)し上(あ)げた点(てん)<b>を踏(ふ)まえまして</b>、来年度(らいねんど)の方針(ほうしん)をご説明(せつめい)いたします。
On the basis of the points I have just made, I will now explain next year's policy.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`を踏まえて` is the most usable member of this group. It belongs to the same world -
committee papers, official notices, formal speeches, the minutes of a board meeting -
but unlike <code>に則(のっと)って</code> or <code>に照(て)らして</code> it is
genuinely spoken. In a Japanese office you will hear
<code>前回(ぜんかい)のご指摘(してき)を踏(ふ)まえまして</code> out loud several
times a week, and it is worth being able to produce.

What it is not is casual. Between friends the same idea is
<code>そのへんも考(かんが)えて</code>,
<code>前(まえ)の話(はなし)も入(い)れて</code> or simply
<code>参考(さんこう)にして</code>. Say
<code>昨日(きのう)の話(はなし)を踏(ふ)まえて飲(の)みに行(い)こう</code>
and you have turned a drink into an agenda item.

Two things it quietly signals. First, respect: naming what somebody said and then
saying you built on it is a standard way of crediting them, which is why
<code>ご意見(いけん)を踏(ふ)まえまして</code> works so well as a reply to
criticism. Second, sequence - the noun came first, the decision came after. The
<code>上(うえ)で</code> variant makes that ordering explicit, and it is the shape
to reach for when you want to insist that you did not decide before listening.

## Contrast

### ～を踏(ふ)まえて vs ～をもとに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>をもとに</code> names the **material** something new was
made out of. <code>を踏(ふ)まえて</code> names a **consideration** that was kept in
view; the result is not made of it.

</div>

<pre>
実話(じつわ)<b>をもとに</b>映画(えいが)が作(つく)られた。
The film was made from a true story.
</pre>

<pre>
試写会(ししゃかい)の反応(はんのう)<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>結末(けつまつ)を変(か)えた。
They changed the ending in the light of the preview audience's reaction.
</pre>

The true story is in the film; the audience reaction is not. That is the whole test.
❌<code>実話(じつわ)を踏(ふ)まえた映画(えいが)</code> sounds as though the film
merely bore the true story in mind while being about something else. Conversely
❌<code>反応(はんのう)をもとに結末(けつまつ)を変(か)えた</code> suggests the new
ending was assembled out of audience comments.

### ～を踏(ふ)まえて vs ～に基(もと)づいて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に基(もと)づいて</code> means the result **answers to**
its base and may not depart from it. <code>を踏(ふ)まえて</code> means the base was
weighed - and you may still have decided otherwise.

</div>

<pre>
契約(けいやく)書(しょ)<b>に基(もと)づいて</b>費用(ひよう)を請求(せいきゅう)します。
We will invoice the costs under the terms of the contract.
</pre>

<pre>
契約(けいやく)書(しょ)の内容(ないよう)<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>今後(こんご)の対応(たいおう)を考(かんが)えます。
We will work out how to proceed with the contract's terms in mind.
</pre>

The first is a legal claim; if the invoice departs from the contract it is simply
wrong. The second promises only that the contract will be read. This is exactly why a
company under pressure writes
<code>ご意見(いけん)を踏(ふ)まえて検討(けんとう)します</code> rather than
<code>ご意見(いけん)に基(もと)づいて</code> - the weaker verb commits it to
nothing.

### ～を踏(ふ)まえて vs ～を前提(ぜんてい)に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を前提(ぜんてい)に</code> **assumes** X to be true and
builds on it; if X turns out false the whole plan collapses.
<code>を踏(ふ)まえて</code> merely **factors X in**.

</div>

<pre>
値上(ねあ)げ<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>来年度(らいねんど)の予算(よさん)を組(く)んだ。
We built next year's budget on the assumption of a price rise.
</pre>

<pre>
値上(ねあ)げの可能性(かのうせい)<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>来年度(らいねんど)の予算(よさん)を組(く)んだ。
We built next year's budget with the possibility of a price rise in view.
</pre>

If the rise never happens, the first budget has to be rewritten and the second does
not. Note also the tense difference hiding in the two: a 前提(ぜんてい) can be
something that has not happened yet, while what you 踏(ふ)まえる is normally
already on the record.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 議論(ぎろん)<b>に踏(ふ)まえて</b> → ✅ 議論(ぎろん)<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>
  The verb is transitive. Its `に` neighbours in this group pull learners the wrong
  way, but <code>踏(ふ)まえる</code> never takes `に`.

- ❌ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)る<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>日程(にってい)を決(き)めた。 → ✅ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)る<b>ことを踏(ふ)まえて</b>日程(にってい)を決(き)めた。
  A clause has to be nominalised with こと or repackaged as
  <code>可能性(かのうせい)</code>, <code>点(てん)</code> or
  <code>事実(じじつ)</code> before it can be an object.

- ❌ 実話(じつわ)<b>を踏(ふ)まえた</b>小説(しょうせつ) → ✅ 実話(じつわ)<b>をもとにした</b>小説(しょうせつ)
  Source material takes <code>をもとに</code>. <code>踏(ふ)まえる</code> would
  say the novel is about something else and merely remembered the true story.

- ❌ 週末(しゅうまつ)の天気(てんき)<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>、遊(あそ)びに行(い)こうよ。 → ✅ 週末(しゅうまつ)の天気(てんき)<b>を見(み)て</b>、遊(あそ)びに行(い)こうよ。
  Correct grammar, wrong register. Inviting a friend out is not a policy decision,
  and the formal verb turns the invitation into a memo.

## Practice

**1.** 今回(こんかい)の事故(じこ)____、安全(あんぜん)点検(てんけん)の回数(かいすう)を増(ふ)やすことにした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

今回(こんかい)の事故(じこ)<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>、回数(かいすう)を増(ふ)やすことにした。

The accident is an established fact that forced a rethink, and the decision was taken
with it in view. <code>を踏(ふ)まえ</code> is the shape a written notice would use.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 祖母(そぼ)の日記(にっき)（を踏(ふ)まえて・をもとに）一冊(いっさつ)の本(ほん)を書(か)いた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

祖母(そぼ)の日記(にっき)<b>をもとに</b>一冊(いっさつ)の本(ほん)を書(か)いた。

The diary is the material the book was made out of, so <code>をもとに</code> is the
only natural choice. <code>を踏(ふ)まえて</code> would mean the writer had read the
diary and borne it in mind while writing about something else. Note the counter:
一冊(いっさつ), with gemination.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Taking your comments into account, we will submit a revised proposal next week."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

いただいたご意見(いけん)<b>を踏(ふ)まえまして</b>、来週(らいしゅう)修正(しゅうせい)案(あん)を提出(ていしゅつ)いたします。

<code>を踏(ふ)まえまして</code> is the polite spoken form and is exactly what this
sentence wants - a business reply that credits the other side. The plain
<code>を踏(ふ)まえて</code> would be fine in an email.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 市民(しみん)の要望(ようぼう)に踏(ふ)まえて、新(あたら)しい公園(こうえん)を作(つく)ることになった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 市民(しみん)の要望(ようぼう)<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>、新(あたら)しい公園(こうえん)を作(つく)ることになった。

The particle is `を`. If you want a `に` pattern here,
<code>要望(ようぼう)に応(こた)えて</code> works and is warmer - it says the
request was granted, not merely considered.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ご指摘(してき)<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>検討(けんとう)いたします。
(b) ご指摘(してき)<b>に基(もと)づいて</b>修正(しゅうせい)いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) promises that the criticism will be taken into account during consideration. It
is courteous and it commits to nothing: the outcome may be no change at all.

(b) says the correction will be made to match what was pointed out. The criticism now
functions as the specification. In Japanese business correspondence the gap between
these two sentences is the difference between a polite holding reply and an
undertaking.

</details>

# ～をもって - by means of

**Meaning** · by means of; with - names the formal method, instrument or quality with which something is carried out

`もって` is the て-form of an old verb of holding, written 以(もっ)て on the rare
occasions it is written in kanji at all. What it does is exactly what the particle
`で` does - it names the means - but it does it in the register of a notice board, a
contract and a formal address. Two kinds of noun sit in front of it: a **method**
(書面(しょめん), 投票(とうひょう), 抽選(ちゅうせん)) and a **quality you bring
to the task** (誠意(せいい), 全力(ぜんりょく), 責任(せきにん)).

## Formation

The pattern takes a **noun** only, marked with `を`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (method) | Noun + をもって | 書面(しょめん) → 書面(しょめん)をもって |
| Noun (quality, attitude) | Noun + をもって | 誠意(せいい) → 誠意(せいい)をもって |
| Polite, announcements | Noun + をもちまして | 抽選(ちゅうせん) → 抽選(ちゅうせん)をもちまして |
| Written in kanji | Noun + を以(もっ)て | 書面(しょめん) → 書面(しょめん)を以(もっ)て |
| "with X you could…" | Noun + をもってすれば | 実力(じつりょく) → 彼(かれ)の実力(じつりょく)をもってすれば |
| "even with X" | Noun + をもってしても | 技術(ぎじゅつ) → 最新(さいしん)の技術(ぎじゅつ)をもってしても |
| Set phrase | 身(み)をもって + Verb | - → 身(み)をもって知(し)る |

<div class="note">

**Note** - do not confuse this with 持(も)って, the て-form of
<code>持(も)つ</code>, "to hold". <code>ペンを持(も)って</code> means a pen is in
your hand. The `もって` of this pattern is never written with that kanji and never
means physically carrying anything, which is why it is usually left in kana.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
結果(けっか)は書面(しょめん)<b>をもって</b>お知(し)らせします。
The result will be communicated in writing.
</pre>

<pre>
誠意(せいい)<b>をもって</b>対応(たいおう)いたします。
We will deal with the matter in good faith.
</pre>

<pre>
役員(やくいん)は会員(かいいん)の投票(とうひょう)<b>をもって</b>選(えら)ばれる。
Officers are chosen by a vote of the membership.
</pre>

<pre>
災害(さいがい)の恐(おそ)ろしさを身(み)<b>をもって</b>知(し)った。
I learned how terrible a disaster can be through my own experience of one.
</pre>

<pre>
彼女(かのじょ)の語学力(ごがくりょく)<b>をもってすれば</b>、通訳(つうやく)も十分(じゅうぶん)務(つと)まるだろう。
With her command of languages, she could certainly manage as an interpreter.
</pre>

<pre>
最新(さいしん)の設備(せつび)<b>をもってしても</b>、この部品(ぶひん)は作(つく)れない。
Even with the latest equipment, this part cannot be made.
</pre>

<pre>
当選者(とうせんしゃ)の発表(はっぴょう)は賞品(しょうひん)の発送(はっそう)<b>をもって</b>代(か)えさせていただきます。
Winners will be announced by the dispatch of the prizes rather than by any other notice.
</pre>

<pre>
微力(びりょく)ではございますが、全力(ぜんりょく)<b>をもって</b>務(つと)めさせていただく所存(しょぞん)です。
My powers are small, but I intend to devote everything I have to the role.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is the vocabulary of contracts, official notices, competition rules and
ceremonial speech. The last two examples above are close to fixed formulas: the
prize-draw sentence appears on countless Japanese entry forms, and the
全力(ぜんりょく)をもって line belongs to the speech a newly appointed person gives.
Nothing here is conversational. In ordinary speech the same idea is the plain
particle `で` - <code>メールで知(し)らせる</code>,
<code>本気(ほんき)でやる</code> - and a learner who reaches for `をもって` at a
dinner table will sound like a court summons.

Two shapes are worth separating out. <code>をもってすれば</code> means "given X, it
can be done", and <code>をもってしても</code> means "even given X, it cannot" -
they are a matched pair, and the second is far more common, because the interesting
sentence is usually the one about a resource that was not enough.

<code>身(み)をもって</code> is a frozen idiom with a life of its own: it means
learning something by living through it, and it pairs with
知(し)る, 感(かん)じる, 体験(たいけん)する, 示(しめ)す. It is the one form
of this pattern that turns up in ordinary written Japanese - essays, memoirs,
newspaper columns - and it is worth learning as a unit.

Note finally that `をもって` cannot name a person. The doer of an action is
`によって`; this pattern names only the instrument.

## Contrast

### ～をもって vs で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same job, different register. `で` is the everyday means
particle. `をもって` is its formal counterpart, and it needs an **abstract or
institutional** means; physical tools are out.

</div>

<pre>
はし<b>で</b>食(た)べます。
I eat with chopsticks.
</pre>

<pre>
審査(しんさ)結果(けっか)はメール<b>をもって</b>ご連絡(れんらく)いたします。
The outcome of the review will be conveyed by email.
</pre>

❌<code>はしをもって食(た)べます</code> is not merely stiff; it is wrong, because
chopsticks are an object in the hand rather than a formal channel. The test is
whether the means is the sort of thing an organisation would specify in writing. A
document, a vote, a lottery, an email, a payment - yes. A fork, a bicycle, a hammer -
no.

### ～をもって vs ～によって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `によって` is the wide-ranging one: means, cause, agent of a
passive, and "depending on". `をもって` does **means only**, in a formal register.

</div>

<pre>
この橋(はし)は外国(がいこく)の技術者(ぎじゅつしゃ)<b>によって</b>設計(せっけい)された。
This bridge was designed by a foreign engineer.
</pre>

<pre>
支払(しはら)いは銀行(ぎんこう)振込(ふりこみ)<b>をもって</b>完了(かんりょう)とします。
Payment shall be deemed complete upon bank transfer.
</pre>

Swapping them in the first sentence fails outright -
❌<code>技術者(ぎじゅつしゃ)をもって設計(せっけい)された</code> - because a person
can never be the noun here.
Swapping them in the second merely loosens it: `によって` would be acceptable but
would lose the contractual ring that makes the sentence sound like a clause in an
agreement.

### ～をもって - means vs ～をもって - as of

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - look at the **noun**, then at the **verb**. A method or a
quality plus an ordinary verb gives the means reading; a date, a time or これ plus a
verb of ending or taking effect gives the effective-date reading.

</div>

<pre>
投票(とうひょう)<b>をもって</b>会長(かいちょう)を決(き)める。
The chair is decided by a vote.
</pre>

<pre>
本日(ほんじつ)<b>をもって</b>受付(うけつけ)を終了(しゅうりょう)します。
Applications close as of today.
</pre>

The form is identical and the two readings almost never compete, because
投票(とうひょう) is not a point in time and 本日(ほんじつ) is not a method. The
one genuinely ambiguous noun is これ: <code>これをもって</code> can mean "by this
means" or "with this, we finish", and only the following verb tells you which.
Because closing speeches are so common, a listener's first guess is the time reading.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ はし<b>をもって</b>食(た)べる → ✅ はし<b>で</b>食(た)べる
  A physical tool takes `で`. This pattern needs an institutional or abstract means.

- ❌ この本(ほん)は有名(ゆうめい)な作家(さっか)<b>をもって</b>書(か)かれた。 → ✅ この本(ほん)は有名(ゆうめい)な作家(さっか)<b>によって</b>書(か)かれた。
  The agent of a passive is `によって`. `をもって` never takes a person.

- ❌ ペン<b>をもって</b>書(か)いてください。 → ✅ ペン<b>で</b>書(か)いてください。
  Here the writer has probably reached for 持(も)って, "holding". Both spellings
  land you somewhere you did not intend; the sentence you want is the plain `で` one.

- ❌ 全力(ぜんりょく)<b>にもって</b>頑張(がんば)ります。 → ✅ 全力(ぜんりょく)<b>をもって</b>取(と)り組(く)みます。
  The particle is `を`. And since the pattern is ceremonial, it wants a formal verb
  after it: 頑張(がんば)る is too everyday to sit beside it comfortably.

## Practice

**1.** 合否(ごうひ)の結果(けっか)は郵送(ゆうそう)____お知(し)らせいたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

郵送(ゆうそう)<b>をもって</b>お知(し)らせいたします。

Postal delivery is a formal channel specified by an institution, which is exactly
what this pattern names. <code>をもちまして</code> would be even more polite, and is
what you would hear read out at a briefing.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この作品(さくひん)は若(わか)い画家(がか)（をもって・によって）描(えが)かれた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

若(わか)い画家(がか)<b>によって</b>描(えが)かれた。

The painter is the agent of a passive, and only `によって` can mark a person.
`をもって` is restricted to instruments and methods.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Even with all his experience, he could not repair it."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

長年(ながねん)の経験(けいけん)<b>をもってしても</b>、彼(かれ)にはそれを直(なお)すことができなかった。

<code>をもってしても</code> is the fixed "even with X" shape, and it always leads to
a negative result. The plain <code>をもって</code> here would flatten the sentence
into "he repaired it using his experience".

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 自転車(じてんしゃ)をもって駅(えき)まで行(い)きました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 自転車(じてんしゃ)<b>で</b>駅(えき)まで行(い)きました。

A bicycle is a physical means of transport, so it takes `で`. Written as
持(も)って it would say you carried the bicycle to the station, which is a
different and much less likely sentence.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 抽選(ちゅうせん)<b>をもって</b>当選者(とうせんしゃ)を決(き)めます。
(b) 抽選(ちゅうせん)<b>で</b>当選者(とうせんしゃ)を決(き)めます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the wording of the official rules. The pattern signals that the draw is the
sole, formally stipulated method, and that no other route to winning exists. It is
what you read in small print.

(b) says the same thing as plain information - the winner will be picked by lot. It
is what a staff member would say out loud to a customer who asked. The propositional
content is identical; only the standing of the statement differs.

</details>

# ～をもって - as of; with effect from

**Meaning** · as of; with this - marks the exact point at which something ends, begins or takes effect

The same `もって` as the "by means of" pattern, but with a **point in time** in front
of it instead of a method. What follows is a change of state: something closes, is
abolished, comes into force, is deemed complete. This is the wording of closure
notices, resignation announcements, contract clauses and the last sentence of a
ceremony, and it is one of the few N1 patterns you will hear spoken aloud almost
every week in Japan - at the end of an event, in the form
<code>これをもちまして</code>.

## Formation

The pattern takes a **noun** - a date, a time, a deadline, or a demonstrative
standing for the event just concluded.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Date | Noun + をもって | 本日(ほんじつ) → 本日(ほんじつ)をもって |
| Time | Noun + をもって | 午後(ごご)五時(ごじ) → 午後(ごご)五時(ごじ)をもって |
| Demonstrative | これ / 以上(いじょう) + をもって | これ → これをもって |
| Polite, spoken announcements | Noun + をもちまして | これ → これをもちまして |
| Written in kanji | Noun + を以(もっ)て | 本日(ほんじつ) → 本日(ほんじつ)を以(もっ)て |
| Deadline noun | Noun + をもって締(し)め切(き)る | 月末(げつまつ) → 月末(げつまつ)をもって締(し)め切(き)る |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the pattern needs a verb of **transition** after it: 終了(しゅうりょう),
閉店(へいてん), 退職(たいしょく), 廃止(はいし), 施行(しこう),
適用(てきよう), 無効(むこう)となる. It cannot mark the time of an ordinary event.
<code>三時(さんじ)をもって会議(かいぎ)を始(はじ)めましょう</code> is wrong -
you want <code>三時(さんじ)から</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
本日(ほんじつ)<b>をもって</b>閉店(へいてん)いたします。
We close for good as of today.
</pre>

<pre>
これ<b>をもちまして</b>、説明会(せつめいかい)を終(お)わります。
That brings the briefing to a close.
</pre>

<pre>
三月(さんがつ)末日(まつじつ)<b>をもって</b>退職(たいしょく)することになりました。
I will be leaving the company with effect from the end of March.
</pre>

<pre>
受付(うけつけ)は午後(ごご)五時(ごじ)<b>をもって</b>締(し)め切(き)らせていただきます。
Reception closes at five in the afternoon.
</pre>

<pre>
新(あたら)しい料金(りょうきん)は四月(しがつ)一日(ついたち)<b>をもって</b>適用(てきよう)されます。
The new charges take effect on the first of April.
</pre>

<pre>
今回(こんかい)の発表(はっぴょう)<b>をもって</b>、長(なが)く続(つづ)いた議論(ぎろん)にようやく決着(けっちゃく)がついた。
With this announcement, the long-running argument was finally settled.
</pre>

<pre>
当(とう)サービスは来年(らいねん)三月(さんがつ)三十一日(さんじゅういちにち)<b>をもって</b>終了(しゅうりょう)させていただきます。長年(ながねん)のご愛顧(あいこ)に感謝(かんしゃ)申(もう)し上(あ)げます。
This service will end on the thirty-first of March next year. We are grateful for your custom over so many years.
</pre>

<pre>
以上(いじょう)<b>をもちまして</b>、新郎(しんろう)新婦(しんぷ)のご紹介(しょうかい)を終(お)わらせていただきます。
That concludes the introduction of the bride and groom.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This belongs to notices, contracts and ceremonies, and it does not travel outside
them. Announcing to a friend
<code>今日(きょう)をもってバイトを辞(や)める</code> is comic; you say
<code>今日(きょう)でバイト辞(や)める</code>. The plain particle `で` is the
everyday equivalent, and it covers every case:
<code>今日(きょう)で終(お)わり</code>,
<code>三月(さんがつ)いっぱいで辞(や)める</code>.

<code>をもちまして</code> is the form to learn for listening. It is what a master of
ceremonies, a station announcer, a shop manager or a conference chair says, and
<code>これをもちまして、○○を終了(しゅうりょう)させていただきます</code> is close to
a fixed sentence. Learn to hear it and you will know a Japanese event is over before
anyone stands up.

Two points of meaning. First, the boundary is **inclusive** when the verb is one of
ending: <code>本日(ほんじつ)をもって閉店(へいてん)</code> means the shop is open
today and shut tomorrow, not that it shut this morning. Second, whether the point is
an ending or a beginning comes entirely from the verb -
<code>廃止(はいし)する</code> ends something,
<code>施行(しこう)する</code> starts it - and the pattern itself is neutral about
direction. This is why translations swing between "as of", "with effect from" and
"as from" depending on the sentence.

Because the phrasing is so official, using it about a small personal matter carries
an unmistakable note of ceremony, and Japanese speakers exploit that for humour:
<code>本日(ほんじつ)をもって禁煙(きんえん)します</code> said to friends is a joke
about the solemnity of the resolution, and it works precisely because the register is
so far from the occasion.

## Contrast

### ～をもって vs で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `で` marks the same boundary in ordinary Japanese. `をもって` is
the formal register of the identical statement, and it is the one that appears in
writing and in announcements.

</div>

<pre>
この店(みせ)、今週(こんしゅう)<b>で</b>終(お)わりなんだって。
Apparently this place is closing at the end of the week.
</pre>

<pre>
当店(とうてん)は今週(こんしゅう)末(まつ)<b>をもって</b>営業(えいぎょう)を終了(しゅうりょう)いたします。
This store will cease trading at the end of this week.
</pre>

These two sentences report the same closure. The first is what a customer says to
another customer; the second is what is printed on the door. Neither is more correct.
If you are unsure which to use in the moment, `で` is never wrong in speech, whereas
`をもって` in speech is wrong whenever the occasion is not formal.

### ～をもって vs ～限(かぎ)りで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>限(かぎ)りで</code> means "this is the last one",
looking at the final instance of a series. `をもって` marks a **point on the
calendar** at which a state changes.

</div>

<pre>
この番組(ばんぐみ)は今回(こんかい)<b>限(かぎ)りで</b>終(お)わります。
This is the last episode of the programme.
</pre>

<pre>
この番組(ばんぐみ)は三月(さんがつ)末(まつ)<b>をもって</b>終了(しゅうりょう)いたします。
This programme will end at the close of March.
</pre>

<code>限(かぎ)りで</code> counts instances and carries a faint regret - it is the
word for a farewell performance. `をもって` counts dates and carries none: it is
administrative. They overlap in <code>本日(ほんじつ)限(かぎ)りで</code> versus
<code>本日(ほんじつ)をもって</code>, where the first still sounds like the end of a
run and the second like the end of a contract.

### ～をもって - as of vs ～をもって - by means of

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the noun decides. A **point in time** gives this page's reading;
a **method or a quality** gives the instrument reading.

</div>

<pre>
三月(さんがつ)末(まつ)<b>をもって</b>契約(けいやく)は終了(しゅうりょう)します。
The contract terminates at the end of March.
</pre>

<pre>
書面(しょめん)<b>をもって</b>契約(けいやく)を解除(かいじょ)します。
The contract will be cancelled by written notice.
</pre>

Same particle, same verb-phrase, different noun, and no native reader hesitates for a
moment. The overlap is real only with an abstract event noun that could be read
either way: <code>支払(しはら)いをもって完了(かんりょう)とする</code> can be
heard as "by means of payment" or "at the moment of payment", and in a contract it
comfortably means both at once.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 三時(さんじ)<b>をもって</b>会議(かいぎ)を始(はじ)めましょう。 → ✅ 三時(さんじ)<b>から</b>会議(かいぎ)を始(はじ)めましょう。
  The pattern needs a formal change of state, not an ordinary scheduled event - and
  never a volitional invitation.

- ❌ 明日(あした)<b>をもって</b>会(あ)いましょう。 → ✅ 明日(あした)会(あ)いましょう。
  A time when something *happens* is not a boundary at which something *ends*. This
  is the commonest way learners overreach with the pattern.

- ❌ 今日(きょう)<b>をもって</b>部活(ぶかつ)やめるわ。 → ✅ 今日(きょう)<b>で</b>部活(ぶかつ)やめるわ。
  Grammatical, but the ceremonial register collides with the casual わ ending. Keep
  `をもって` out of casual speech unless you are being deliberately theatrical.

- ❌ 本日(ほんじつ)<b>にもちまして</b>終了(しゅうりょう)いたします。 → ✅ 本日(ほんじつ)<b>をもちまして</b>終了(しゅうりょう)いたします。
  The particle is `を`, not `に`, in both the plain and the polite form.

## Practice

**1.** 当社(とうしゃ)の大阪(おおさか)支店(してん)は九月(くがつ)三十日(さんじゅうにち)____閉鎖(へいさ)いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

九月(くがつ)三十日(さんじゅうにち)<b>をもって</b>閉鎖(へいさ)いたします。

A dated closure announced by a company is the textbook case. Note the reading
九月(くがつ), not きゅうがつ, and 三十日(さんじゅうにち) for the date.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 会長(かいちょう)は全会員(ぜんかいいん)の投票(とうひょう)（をもって・をもちまして）選出(せんしゅつ)される。Which reading of `をもって` is this - time or means?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

投票(とうひょう)<b>をもって</b>選出(せんしゅつ)される。This is the **means**
reading.

A vote is a method, not a point in time, so the sentence says the chair is chosen *by*
a ballot. <code>をもちまして</code> is the spoken-announcement variant and would sit
oddly in what is clearly a written rule.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The old membership cards become invalid as of the end of this month."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

古(ふる)い会員証(かいいんしょう)は今月(こんげつ)末(まつ)<b>をもって</b>無効(むこう)となります。

<code>無効(むこう)となる</code> is a change of state on a date, which is exactly
what this pattern marks. In speech you would say
<code>今月(こんげつ)末(まつ)で使(つか)えなくなります</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 来週(らいしゅう)の月曜日(げつようび)をもって新(あたら)しい店長(てんちょう)に会(あ)う予定(よてい)です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 来週(らいしゅう)の月曜日(げつようび)<b>に</b>新(あたら)しい店長(てんちょう)に会(あ)う予定(よてい)です。

Meeting someone is an event, not a transition, so the plain time particle `に` is
what the sentence needs. Keep <code>をもって</code> for endings, starts and things
taking effect.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 本日(ほんじつ)<b>をもって</b>営業(えいぎょう)を終了(しゅうりょう)いたします。
(b) 本日(ほんじつ)限(かぎ)り営業(えいぎょう)いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) announces a permanent closure taking effect at the end of today. Today is the
last day of trading, and tomorrow the business no longer exists.

(b) says the opposite kind of thing: the shop is open **only** today, as a one-off -
a pop-up, a special opening on a normal closing day. <code>限(かぎ)り</code>
restricts the scope of the trading rather than marking its end, and confusing the two
would put a customer outside a locked door.

</details>

# ～にまつわる

**Meaning** · connected with; surrounding - the stories, rumours, memories and troubles that have gathered around something

`まつわる` is an old verb meaning "to twine around, to cling to". What clings, in this
pattern, is never a regulation or a technical detail; it is the human material that
collects around a thing over time - a legend attached to a lake, the gossip around a
famous name, the arguments that money brings, the memories a ring carries. The noun it
modifies is almost always 話(はなし),
伝説(でんせつ), 噂(うわさ), エピソード, 思(おも)い出(で),
トラブル: words for things that get told.

## Formation

The pattern takes a **noun** with `に`, and it almost always **modifies a following
noun**. There is no adjective or verb attachment.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun, modifying a noun | Noun + にまつわる + Noun | 城(しろ) → 城(しろ)にまつわる伝説(でんせつ) |
| Nouns that typically follow | にまつわる + 話(はなし) / 噂(うわさ) / 思(おも)い出(で) | お金(かね) → お金(かね)にまつわる噂(うわさ) |
| Adverbial (uncommon) | Noun + にまつわって | 遺産(いさん) → 遺産(いさん)にまつわって争(あらそ)いが起(お)きた |
| Stiff written variant | Noun + にまつわり | 土地(とち) → 土地(とち)にまつわり語(かた)られてきた話(はなし) |
| Set noun phrase | Noun + にまつわるあれこれ | お金(かね) → お金(かね)にまつわるあれこれ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>まつわる</code> is written in kana in almost all modern text. A kanji
spelling exists, but the character is outside the everyday set and using it makes a
sentence look affected rather than learned.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
この湖(みずうみ)<b>にまつわる</b>伝説(でんせつ)を聞(き)いたことがある。
I have heard the legend attached to this lake.
</pre>

<pre>
祖母(そぼ)は指輪(ゆびわ)<b>にまつわる</b>話(はなし)をしてくれた。
My grandmother told me the story behind the ring.
</pre>

<pre>
お金(かね)<b>にまつわる</b>トラブルは友人(ゆうじん)関係(かんけい)を壊(こわ)す。
Trouble over money destroys friendships.
</pre>

<pre>
その画家(がか)<b>にまつわる</b>噂(うわさ)は、どれも確(たし)かめようがない。
None of the rumours that surround that painter can be verified.
</pre>

<pre>
番組(ばんぐみ)では、各地(かくち)の祭(まつ)り<b>にまつわる</b>習慣(しゅうかん)を紹介(しょうかい)している。
The programme introduces the customs that have grown up around festivals in different regions.
</pre>

<pre>
引(ひ)っ越(こ)し<b>にまつわる</b>手(て)続(つづ)きは、思(おも)っていたよりずっと面倒(めんどう)だった。
The various procedures that come with moving house were far more trouble than I had expected.
</pre>

<pre>
創業者(そうぎょうしゃ)<b>にまつわる</b>エピソードは、いまも社内(しゃない)で語(かた)り継(つ)がれている。
The stories about the founder are still handed down inside the company.
</pre>

<pre>
あの事件(じけん)<b>にまつわる</b>記録(きろく)は、長(なが)い間(あいだ)公開(こうかい)されずにきた。
The records surrounding that incident went unreleased for many years.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`にまつわる` is written Japanese, but it is not the same kind of written Japanese as
the rest of this group. Where <code>に則(のっと)って</code> and
<code>に照(て)らして</code> belong to statutes and findings, `にまつわる` belongs to
feature articles, book titles, television programmes and museum captions - places
where somebody is about to tell you an interesting story. It is faintly literary and
distinctly warm. You will not find it in a contract, and if you do, someone has
chosen the wrong word.

In speech it is uncommon but not impossible; a person telling an anecdote may reach
for it, and it sounds a little bookish in a pleasant way. The ordinary spoken
equivalent is a plain の or について: <code>この湖(みずうみ)の話(はなし)</code>,
<code>お金(かね)のことでもめる</code>.

Two limits are worth fixing in memory. First, the noun after it must be the kind of
thing that *accumulates*: stories, rumours, customs, memories, disputes, hardships.
❌<code>契約(けいやく)にまつわる条項(じょうこう)</code> is wrong because clauses
do not gather around a contract; they are written into it. Second, the pattern is
essentially adnominal. A bare predicate -
❌<code>この話(はなし)は祭(まつ)りにまつわる</code> - sounds unfinished, and you
want <code>祭(まつ)りにまつわる話(はなし)だ</code> instead.

## Contrast

### ～にまつわる vs ～に関(かん)する

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に関(かん)する</code> is the neutral "concerning", and it
works for documents, rules and data. `にまつわる` needs **human material that has
gathered** around the topic.

</div>

<pre>
個人(こじん)情報(じょうほう)の扱(あつか)い<b>に関(かん)する</b>規定(きてい)を改(あらた)めた。
We revised the regulations concerning the handling of personal data.
</pre>

<pre>
この土地(とち)<b>にまつわる</b>言(い)い伝(つた)えを集(あつ)めた本(ほん)。
A book collecting the legends attached to this land.
</pre>

Swapping them exposes the difference at once.
<code>この土地(とち)に関(かん)する言(い)い伝(つた)え</code> is possible but
flat, the wording of a catalogue entry.
❌<code>扱(あつか)いにまつわる規定(きてい)</code> is simply wrong, because
regulations are drafted rather than accumulated. Whenever the following noun is
規定(きてい), 情報(じょうほう), 報告(ほうこく) or 資料(しりょう), use
<code>関(かん)する</code>.

### ～にまつわる vs ～について

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `について` introduces a **topic you are going to treat**, and it
happily precedes a verb. `にまつわる` names a **halo of associations** and normally
precedes a noun.

</div>

<pre>
環境(かんきょう)問題(もんだい)<b>について</b>話(はな)し合(あ)いましょう。
Let's discuss environmental problems.
</pre>

<pre>
環境(かんきょう)問題(もんだい)<b>にまつわる</b>誤解(ごかい)が広(ひろ)がっている。
Misconceptions surrounding environmental problems are spreading.
</pre>

❌<code>環境(かんきょう)問題(もんだい)にまつわって話(はな)し合(あ)う</code>
does not work; the adverbial form is rare and never means "discuss the topic of". Note
also that `について` is level-neutral and usable everywhere, while `にまつわる` colours
whatever follows it with the sense of a story being told.

### ～にまつわる vs ～をめぐる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `をめぐる` puts **parties in conflict** around the noun - a
dispute, a controversy, competing claims. `にまつわる` puts **stories and feelings**
around it, with nobody necessarily fighting.

</div>

<pre>
遺産(いさん)<b>をめぐる</b>争(あらそ)いが十年(じゅうねん)続(つづ)いた。
The dispute over the inheritance went on for ten years.
</pre>

<pre>
遺産(いさん)<b>にまつわる</b>思(おも)い出(で)を姉(あね)と語(かた)り合(あ)った。
My sister and I talked over the memories attached to what our parents left.
</pre>

The two are close enough that a headline can use either, and the choice tells the
reader what kind of article follows. <code>をめぐる</code> promises news: sides,
positions, an unresolved question. `にまつわる` promises a feature: anecdotes,
background, colour.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 契約(けいやく)<b>にまつわる</b>条項(じょうこう) → ✅ 契約(けいやく)<b>に関(かん)する</b>条項(じょうこう)
  Clauses are drafted into a contract, not gathered around it. This pattern needs a
  noun that collects stories, not one that states rules.

- ❌ この話(はなし)は祭(まつ)り<b>にまつわる</b>。 → ✅ これは祭(まつ)り<b>にまつわる</b>話(はなし)だ。
  The pattern modifies a noun. Left as a bare predicate it sounds like a sentence
  someone stopped writing halfway.

- ❌ 健康(けんこう)<b>にまつわって</b>調(しら)べています。 → ✅ 健康(けんこう)<b>について</b>調(しら)べています。
  Researching a topic is `について`. The adverbial <code>にまつわって</code> exists but
  is rare, and never carries this meaning.

- ❌ 彼(かれ)<b>にまつわっています</b>。 → ✅ 彼(かれ)<b>にまつわる</b>噂(うわさ)が多(おお)い。
  There is no natural progressive use. If you want to say a person attracts stories,
  say that the stories are many.

## Practice

**1.** 京都(きょうと)の古(ふる)い寺(てら)____話(はなし)を集(あつ)めた本(ほん)を買(か)った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

古(ふる)い寺(てら)<b>にまつわる</b>話(はなし)を集(あつ)めた本(ほん)

Temples and the tales that have grown up around them are exactly what this pattern is
for, and 話(はなし) is its most typical partner. <code>についての</code> would also
be grammatical but would suggest a book of factual descriptions instead.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 新(あたら)しい制度(せいど)（にまつわる・に関(かん)する）説明(せつめい)資料(しりょう)をお配(くば)りします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

新(あたら)しい制度(せいど)<b>に関(かん)する</b>説明(せつめい)資料(しりょう)

Explanatory documents are produced, not accumulated, and the setting is
administrative. <code>にまつわる</code> would promise anecdotes about the new system,
which is not what a handout contains.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "There are all sorts of superstitions surrounding this mountain."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この山(やま)<b>にまつわる</b>言(い)い伝(つた)えは数(かぞ)え切(き)れないほどある。

Folklore clinging to a place is the core use. 迷信(めいしん) would also work for
"superstitions"; 言(い)い伝(つた)え is the warmer word and fits the register of
the pattern better.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 会社(かいしゃ)の歴史(れきし)にまつわって発表(はっぴょう)します。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 会社(かいしゃ)の歴史(れきし)<b>にまつわる</b>話(はなし)を発表(はっぴょう)します。

The adverbial form cannot introduce the topic of a presentation. Either supply a noun
for <code>にまつわる</code> to modify, or switch to
<code>歴史(れきし)について発表(はっぴょう)します</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 土地(とち)の売買(ばいばい)<b>をめぐる</b>記事(きじ)
(b) 土地(とち)の売買(ばいばい)<b>にまつわる</b>記事(きじ)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) leads you to expect news: a disagreement about a land sale, with parties on
either side and something still to be decided.

(b) leads you to expect a feature: the peculiar cases, the local customs, the stories
people tell about buying and selling land. Nothing in (b) implies a conflict, and a
reader who wanted the dispute would feel misled.

</details>

# ～をいいことに

**Meaning** · taking advantage of; on the excuse that - somebody exploits a convenient circumstance to do something the speaker disapproves of

Literally "treating X as a good thing". What sits in front is a circumstance that
happens to leave the door open: nobody is watching, the boss is away, the other
person is too polite to refuse. The clause after it describes what the person then
did with that opening - and the disapproval is not optional. `をいいことに` is a
criticism built into the grammar, and a sentence using it always has a target.

## Formation

The pattern takes a **noun** or a clause nominalised with `の`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + をいいことに | 留守(るす) → 留守(るす)をいいことに |
| Verb | plain form + のをいいことに | 見(み)ていない → 誰(だれ)も見(み)ていないのをいいことに |
| い-adjective | plain form + のをいいことに | 優(やさ)しい → 優(やさ)しいのをいいことに |
| な-adjective | ～な + のをいいことに | 静(しず)か → 周(まわ)りが静(しず)かなのをいいことに |
| Noun as a clause | ～な + のをいいことに | 素人(しろうと) → 相手(あいて)が素人(しろうと)なのをいいことに |
| Fuller variant | ～をいいことにして | 留守(るす) → 留守(るす)をいいことにして |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the `の` in the verb and adjective rows is not optional.
❌<code>優(やさ)しいをいいことに</code> is ungrammatical: <code>いいこと</code> is a
noun and needs a noun-shaped thing before the `を`, which is what `の` supplies.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
親(おや)がいない<b>のをいいことに</b>、夜中(よなか)まで遊(あそ)んでいた。
With his parents out of the house, he stayed out playing until the middle of the night.
</pre>

<pre>
誰(だれ)も見(み)ていない<b>のをいいことに</b>、ごみを捨(す)てていく人(ひと)がいる。
There are people who dump their rubbish here because nobody is watching.
</pre>

<pre>
彼(かれ)は私(わたし)が断(ことわ)れない<b>のをいいことに</b>、仕事(しごと)を押(お)し付(つ)けてくる。
He keeps dumping work on me, knowing full well I can't say no.
</pre>

<pre>
店長(てんちょう)の留守(るす)<b>をいいことに</b>、アルバイトたちは休(やす)んでばかりいた。
With the manager away, the part-timers did nothing but take breaks.
</pre>

<pre>
言葉(ことば)が通(つう)じない<b>のをいいことに</b>、ひどい値段(ねだん)をふっかける店(みせ)もある。
There are shops that take advantage of the language barrier to charge outrageous prices.
</pre>

<pre>
相手(あいて)が素人(しろうと)なの<b>をいいことに</b>、必要(ひつよう)のない工事(こうじ)まで勧(すす)めていたらしい。
Apparently they exploited the customer's lack of knowledge to push work that was not needed at all.
</pre>

<pre>
規則(きそく)に書(か)かれていない<b>のをいいことに</b>、平気(へいき)で列(れつ)に割(わ)り込(こ)む客(きゃく)が後(あと)を絶(た)たない。
There is no end of customers who push into the queue without a qualm, on the grounds that no rule forbids it.
</pre>

<pre>
上司(じょうし)が優(やさ)しい<b>のをいいことに</b>、締(し)め切(き)りを何度(なんど)も延(の)ばしてもらっていた。
He kept getting his deadline pushed back, trading on how easy-going his boss was.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This one is written Japanese rather than contract Japanese: newspaper columns,
essays, novels, and the part of a report where the writer stops being neutral. Unlike
<code>に則(のっと)って</code> and <code>をもって</code>, it has no place in a
statute, because a statute does not accuse anyone of taking liberties. It can be
spoken - a person complaining about a colleague may well produce it - but it lands as
noticeably bookish, and the plain spoken version is
<code>いないからって</code>, <code>優(やさ)しいからって</code>,
<code>優(やさ)しさに甘(あま)えて</code>.

The disapproval is the load-bearing part. A sentence that ends in something harmless
sounds broken: ❌<code>休(やす)みをいいことに、ゆっくり寝(ね)た</code> asks the
listener to condemn a lie-in and gets no purchase. What follows must be something the
speaker thinks the person should not have done.

Two consequences follow. First, the subject is nearly always somebody else; turned on
yourself the pattern becomes a confession, and reads as self-reproach rather than as
plain description. Second, the circumstance in front must be a **standing permission**:
something that stays true long enough to be exploited. A single momentary opening is
the territory of <code>に乗(じょう)じて</code>.

## Contrast

### ～をいいことに vs ～に乗(じょう)じて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に乗(じょう)じて</code> rides a **momentary opening** -
confusion, darkness, a disaster. `をいいことに` exploits a **standing condition** that
leaves you free to misbehave.

</div>

<pre>
停電(ていでん)の混乱(こんらん)<b>に乗(じょう)じて</b>店(みせ)に入(はい)り込(こ)んだ者(もの)がいる。
Someone got into the shop under cover of the confusion caused by the power cut.
</pre>

<pre>
防犯(ぼうはん)カメラがない<b>のをいいことに</b>、品物(しなもの)を持(も)ち去(さ)っていた。
He was walking off with goods, safe in the knowledge that there were no security cameras.
</pre>

<code>乗(じょう)じる</code> is about timing: the chance is fleeting and the actor
seizes it. `をいいことに` is about a state of affairs: it was true yesterday and it
will be true tomorrow, which is why the misbehaviour is often repeated.
<code>乗(じょう)じて</code> also sits comfortably in crime reporting, where
`をいいことに` sounds more like moral commentary.

### ～をいいことに vs ～を口実(こうじつ)に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を口実(こうじつ)に</code> is an excuse the person
**states out loud**. `をいいことに` is a circumstance **silently exploited**; nobody
has to say anything.

</div>

<pre>
体調(たいちょう)不良(ふりょう)<b>を口実(こうじつ)に</b>会議(かいぎ)を欠席(けっせき)した。
He used feeling unwell as an excuse to miss the meeting.
</pre>

<pre>
会議(かいぎ)に出(で)なくてもいい<b>のをいいことに</b>、一日中(いちにちじゅう)席(せき)を外(はず)していた。
Since he was not required at the meeting, he took the opportunity to be away from his desk all day.
</pre>

A 口実(こうじつ) is offered to somebody; it is a piece of speech, and it may well be
false. The circumstance behind `をいいことに` need not be false at all - the boss really
is away, the rule really is silent. That is why 口実(こうじつ) pairs with a stated
reason and this pattern pairs with a state of affairs.

### ～をいいことに vs ～からといって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>からといって</code> is the neutral, everyday way to say
"just because X". `をいいことに` says the same thing with the accusation already
made.

</div>

<pre>
安(やす)い<b>からといって</b>買(か)いすぎるのはよくない。
Just because something is cheap is no reason to buy too much of it.
</pre>

<pre>
値段(ねだん)が安(やす)い<b>のをいいことに</b>、必要(ひつよう)のないものまで買(か)い込(こ)んでいる。
Because the prices are low, she is buying up things she does not even need.
</pre>

<code>からといって</code> typically introduces an argument - it is usually followed by
a negative judgement about the reasoning itself. `をいいことに` skips the argument and
reports the behaviour, already judged. In conversation
<code>安(やす)いからって買(か)いすぎだよ</code> is what you would actually say;
save `をいいことに` for writing.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 優(やさ)しい<b>をいいことに</b> → ✅ 優(やさ)しい<b>のをいいことに</b>
  Adjectives and verbs need `の` before it. The pattern is built on the noun
  <code>いいこと</code> and cannot take a bare predicate.

- ❌ 留守(るす)<b>にいいことに</b> → ✅ 留守(るす)<b>をいいことに</b>
  The particle is `を`. The `に` at the end of the pattern already belongs to
  <code>いいことに（する）</code>, and doubling it is a common slip.

- ❌ 休(やす)み<b>をいいことに</b>、一日中(いちにちじゅう)ゆっくり寝(ね)た。 → ✅ 休(やす)みなので、一日中(いちにちじゅう)ゆっくり寝(ね)た。
  Nothing here is being exploited and nobody is at fault, so the criticism the pattern
  carries has nothing to attach to.

- ❌ 彼(かれ)<b>をいいことに</b>仕事(しごと)を任(まか)せた。 → ✅ 彼(かれ)が断(ことわ)らない<b>のをいいことに</b>仕事(しごと)を任(まか)せた。
  A bare person cannot be the circumstance. Say what it is about them that leaves the
  door open.

## Practice

**1.** 先生(せんせい)が教室(きょうしつ)にいない____、生徒(せいと)たちは大声(おおごえ)で騒(さわ)ぎ始(はじ)めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

いない<b>のをいいことに</b>、騒(さわ)ぎ始(はじ)めた

The teacher's absence is a standing condition the pupils exploit, and the sentence
disapproves. Note the `の`: the verb <code>いない</code> has to be nominalised before
`を`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 地震(じしん)の混乱(こんらん)（をいいことに・に乗(じょう)じて）空(あ)き巣(す)に入(はい)る者(もの)がいた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

混乱(こんらん)<b>に乗(じょう)じて</b>空(あ)き巣(す)に入(はい)る

The chaos after an earthquake is a fleeting opening seized at the moment, which is
<code>乗(じょう)じて</code>. `をいいことに` would suggest the confusion was a
lasting state of affairs the burglars settled into.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He took advantage of the fact that I don't know much about cars and sold me a wreck."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

彼(かれ)は私(わたし)が車(くるま)に詳(くわ)しくない<b>のをいいことに</b>、ひどい中古車(ちゅうこしゃ)を売(う)りつけた。

The buyer's ignorance is a standing condition, the seller exploits it, and the speaker
is plainly accusing him - all three ingredients of the pattern.
<code>売(う)りつける</code> carries the same disapproval and pairs with it well.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 土曜日(どようび)が休(やす)みをいいことに、友(とも)だちと映画(えいが)を見(み)に行(い)った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 土曜日(どようび)が休(やす)みなので、友(とも)だちと映画(えいが)を見(み)に行(い)った。

Two problems. Grammatically a noun clause needs `な` before the `の`
(<code>休(やす)みなのをいいことに</code>), but the real fault is that going to the
cinema on your day off is not something to be criticised for, so the pattern has
nothing to bite on.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 忙(いそが)しい<b>のをいいことに</b>、彼(かれ)は連絡(れんらく)を返(かえ)してこない。
(b) 忙(いそが)しい<b>ので</b>、彼(かれ)は連絡(れんらく)を返(かえ)してこない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) accuses him. Being busy is a shield he is hiding behind: the speaker believes he
could reply if he wanted to, and is using the busyness as cover.

(b) simply reports a cause and excuses him. The two sentences describe identical
behaviour and take opposite views of the person, which is exactly why this pattern
should not be used unless you mean the criticism.

</details>

# ～を前提に

**Meaning** · on the premise that; on the assumption of - X is treated as settled, and everything that follows is built on top of it

前提(ぜんてい) is "premise" in the logician's sense: the thing you take as given
before you start. <code>～を前提(ぜんてい)に</code> announces that assumption
openly. The point of saying it aloud is that everyone can then see what the plan
rests on - and what would collapse if the assumption turned out to be false. That
makes it the language of contracts, negotiations, budgets and project proposals.

## Formation

The pattern takes a **noun**; a clause is packaged with こと or という前提(ぜんてい).

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + を前提(ぜんてい)に | 参加(さんか) → 参加(さんか)を前提(ぜんてい)に |
| Noun (stiffer, documents) | Noun + を前提(ぜんてい)として | 値上(ねあ)げ → 値上(ねあ)げを前提(ぜんてい)として |
| Fuller variant | Noun + を前提(ぜんてい)にして | 結婚(けっこん) → 結婚(けっこん)を前提(ぜんてい)にして |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + を前提(ぜんてい)とした + Noun | 在宅(ざいたく)勤務(きんむ) → 在宅(ざいたく)勤務(きんむ)を前提(ぜんてい)とした制度(せいど) |
| Same, non-past shape | Noun + を前提(ぜんてい)とする + Noun | 返済(へんさい) → 返済(へんさい)を前提(ぜんてい)とする契約(けいやく) |
| Clause | ～こと + を前提(ぜんてい)に | 来(く)る → 全員(ぜんいん)が来(く)ることを前提(ぜんてい)に |
| Clause, spoken | ～という前提(ぜんてい)で | 届(とど)く → 明日(あした)届(とど)くという前提(ぜんてい)で |

<div class="note">

**Note** - after a **bare noun** the particle is `に` or `として`, not `で`:
❌<code>結婚(けっこん)を前提(ぜんてい)で</code>. `で` appears only in the clause
pattern <code>～という前提(ぜんてい)で</code>, where 前提(ぜんてい) is a full noun
of its own.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
結婚(けっこん)<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>付(つ)き合(あ)っています。
We are seeing each other with a view to marriage.
</pre>

<pre>
参加(さんか)<b>を前提(ぜんてい)として</b>話(はなし)を進(すす)めます。
We will proceed on the assumption that you are taking part.
</pre>

<pre>
この見(み)積(つ)もりは、材料(ざいりょう)費(ひ)が上(あ)がらないこと<b>を前提(ぜんてい)にしている</b>。
This estimate assumes that material costs will not rise.
</pre>

<pre>
在宅(ざいたく)勤務(きんむ)<b>を前提(ぜんてい)とした</b>制度(せいど)に作(つく)り直(なお)す必要(ひつよう)がある。
The system needs to be rebuilt around the assumption of working from home.
</pre>

<pre>
返(かえ)すこと<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>貸(か)したのに、一年(いちねん)経(た)っても連絡(れんらく)がない。
I lent it on the understanding that it would come back, and a year on I have heard nothing.
</pre>

<pre>
全員(ぜんいん)集(あつ)まる<b>という前提(ぜんてい)で</b>会場(かいじょう)を押(お)さえてしまった。
We went ahead and booked the venue assuming everyone would be there.
</pre>

<pre>
値上(ねあ)げ<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>話(はなし)が進(すす)んでいることに、現場(げんば)は納得(なっとく)していない。
The staff on the ground are unhappy that the discussion is proceeding on the assumption of a price rise.
</pre>

<pre>
将来(しょうらい)人口(じんこう)が増(ふ)えること<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>作(つく)られた道路(どうろ)が、いまはほとんど使(つか)われずに残(のこ)っている。
Roads built on the premise of future population growth now stand almost unused.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This belongs with the rest of the group in contracts, official notices and formal
meetings - a clause reading
<code>支払(しはら)いを前提(ぜんてい)として</code> is doing real legal work - but
it is more usable than its neighbours, because negotiating is something people do out
loud. <code>それを前提(ぜんてい)に考(かんが)えます</code> is ordinary business
speech, and <code>という前提(ぜんてい)で</code> is common enough in a working
conversation to be worth producing.

One phrase has escaped into everyday life altogether:
<code>結婚(けっこん)を前提(ぜんてい)に付(つ)き合(あ)う</code>. It is a fixed
expression, it is said in ordinary conversation, and it is heard as sincere rather
than legalistic. Outside that phrase, the casual equivalents are
<code>～つもりで</code> and <code>～ということにして</code>:
<code>来(く)るつもりで準備(じゅんび)しておく</code>.

What the pattern is quietly doing is limiting liability. Announcing the premise means
that if it fails, the plan can be reopened without anyone being at fault. That is why
it turns up so often in the awkward parts of a negotiation, and why a Japanese
counterpart who says
<code>来年度(らいねんど)の予算(よさん)がつくことを前提(ぜんてい)に</code>
has just told you, politely, that nothing is guaranteed.

## Contrast

### ～を前提(ぜんてい)に vs ～を踏(ふ)まえて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a 前提(ぜんてい) is **assumed and may not yet be true**; if it
fails, everything built on it falls. What you <code>踏(ふ)まえる</code> is
**already established** and is merely taken into account.

</div>

<pre>
補助金(ほじょきん)が出(で)ること<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>計画(けいかく)を立(た)てた。
We drew up the plan assuming the subsidy would come through.
</pre>

<pre>
補助金(ほじょきん)の審査(しんさ)結果(けっか)<b>を踏(ふ)まえて</b>計画(けいかく)を立(た)てた。
We drew up the plan in the light of the outcome of the subsidy review.
</pre>

The first plan has a hole in it if the money does not arrive; the second was made
after the answer was known. Notice the tense hidden in each: a premise usually points
forward, while what you 踏(ふ)まえる has already happened.

### ～を前提(ぜんてい)に vs ～に基(もと)づいて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に基(もと)づいて</code> stands on something that
**exists** - a law, a contract, a set of figures.
<code>を前提(ぜんてい)に</code> stands on something **granted for the sake of
argument**.

</div>

<pre>
契約(けいやく)書(しょ)<b>に基(もと)づいて</b>費用(ひよう)を負担(ふたん)します。
We will bear the costs in accordance with the contract.
</pre>

<pre>
契約(けいやく)が結(むす)ばれること<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>準備(じゅんび)を進(すす)めます。
We will get on with preparations on the assumption that a contract will be signed.
</pre>

In the first, the contract is signed and sitting on the desk. In the second, it is
not, and the sentence says so. Learners often reach for
<code>に基(もと)づいて</code> when the thing does not exist yet, which quietly
overstates the case; if it has not happened, you want
<code>を前提(ぜんてい)に</code>.

### ～を前提(ぜんてい)に vs ～という条件(じょうけん)で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a 条件(じょうけん) is a **requirement imposed on the other
party**, and failing it has consequences for them. A 前提(ぜんてい) is a **shared
assumption** the plan is built around, with nobody obliged to deliver it.

</div>

<pre>
来月(らいげつ)までに返(かえ)すという<b>条件(じょうけん)で</b>貸(か)した。
I lent it on condition that it be returned by next month.
</pre>

<pre>
来月(らいげつ)までに返(かえ)ってくること<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>次(つぎ)の予定(よてい)を組(く)んだ。
I arranged what comes next assuming it would be back by next month.
</pre>

The first sentence makes a demand of the borrower; the second only describes how the
lender planned. They are often both true at once, which is why they get confused - but
only <code>条件(じょうけん)</code> gives you grounds for complaint.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 結婚(けっこん)<b>を前提(ぜんてい)で</b>付(つ)き合(あ)う → ✅ 結婚(けっこん)<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>付(つ)き合(あ)う
  A bare noun takes `に` or `として`. `で` belongs to
  <code>～という前提(ぜんてい)で</code>, where the noun is the head of its own
  phrase.

- ❌ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)る<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>準備(じゅんび)する → ✅ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)る<b>ことを前提(ぜんてい)に</b>準備(じゅんび)する
  A clause has to be nominalised with こと before `を`.

- ❌ 去年(きょねん)の売(う)り上(あ)げ<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>計算(けいさん)した。 → ✅ 去年(きょねん)の売(う)り上(あ)げ<b>に基(もと)づいて</b>計算(けいさん)した。
  Last year's figures are a fact, not an assumption. Reserve
  <code>前提(ぜんてい)</code> for things that are not yet settled.

- ❌ 明日(あした)<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>会(あ)いましょう。 → ✅ 明日(あした)会(あ)えるという<b>前提(ぜんてい)で</b>準備(じゅんび)しておきます。
  A bare time word is not a premise. The assumption has to be a proposition - that
  something will be the case.

## Practice

**1.** 工事(こうじ)が三月(さんがつ)までに終(お)わること____、引(ひ)っ越(こ)しの日(ひ)を決(き)めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

終(お)わること<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>、引(ひ)っ越(こ)しの日(ひ)を決(き)めた。

The building work is not finished yet, so it is an assumption rather than a fact, and
the moving date depends on it entirely. Note the こと: the clause must be nominalised.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 去年(きょねん)の実績(じっせき)（を前提(ぜんてい)に・に基(もと)づいて）今年(ことし)の目標(もくひょう)を決(き)めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

去年(きょねん)の実績(じっせき)<b>に基(もと)づいて</b>決(き)めた。

Last year's results are on the record. A premise is something granted in advance
because it is not yet known, and there is nothing unknown about last year.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The budget was drawn up on the assumption that the number of visitors would double."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

来場者(らいじょうしゃ)数(すう)が倍(ばい)になること<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>予算(よさん)が組(く)まれた。

A forecast that may or may not come true is a textbook 前提(ぜんてい).
<code>を前提(ぜんてい)として</code> would suit a written budget document even
better.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 来週(らいしゅう)届(とど)くを前提(ぜんてい)で作業(さぎょう)を進(すす)めています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 来週(らいしゅう)届(とど)くという<b>前提(ぜんてい)で</b>作業(さぎょう)を進(すす)めています。

Two repairs in one. A clause cannot sit directly before `を`, and after a bare noun
the particle would have to be `に`. Wrapping the clause in
<code>という前提(ぜんてい)で</code> fixes both at once, and it is the shape you
would actually hear in a meeting.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 全員(ぜんいん)出席(しゅっせき)すること<b>を前提(ぜんてい)に</b>会場(かいじょう)を選(えら)んだ。
(b) 全員(ぜんいん)出席(しゅっせき)するという<b>条件(じょうけん)で</b>会場(かいじょう)を借(か)りた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is about the planner's own reasoning: the venue was chosen for a full turnout, and
if half the people stay away the room is simply too big. Nobody has been asked to
promise anything.

(b) is about a requirement someone imposed. Full attendance is a term of the booking,
and failing it means the arrangement can be withdrawn. (a) risks embarrassment; (b)
risks losing the hall.

</details>
