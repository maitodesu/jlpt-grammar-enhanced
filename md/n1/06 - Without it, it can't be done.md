# ～にかかっている

**Meaning** · rests on; hinges on; is entirely up to - names the one thing that will decide an outcome

The literal picture is a weight hanging from a single hook: take the hook away and
everything comes down. So <code>にかかっている</code> does not merely say that A
influences B. It says B's whole fate is suspended from A, and that if A gives way
there is nothing else holding B up.

## Formation

Two slots matter. The **deciding factor** takes に; the **thing that depends** on it
is the topic or subject of the sentence, marked は or が. Either slot can be a noun
or a nominalised clause.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (the deciding factor) | Noun + にかかっている | 君(きみ)の覚悟(かくご) → 君(きみ)の覚悟(かくご)にかかっている |
| Embedded question as the factor | 疑問詞(ぎもんし) … か + にかかっている | どう動(うご)くか → どう動(うご)くかにかかっている |
| かどうか as the factor | ～かどうか + にかかっている | 間(ま)に合(あ)うかどうか → 間(ま)に合(あ)うかどうかにかかっている |
| What depends (noun) | Noun + は … にかかっている | 成否(せいひ) → 成否(せいひ)は準備(じゅんび)にかかっている |
| What depends (clause) | ～かどうか + は … にかかっている | 勝(か)てるかどうか → 勝(か)てるかどうかは…にかかっている |
| Polite | ～にかかっています | 協力(きょうりょく) → 皆(みな)さんの協力(きょうりょく)にかかっています |
| Past | ～にかかっていた | 一言(ひとこと) → あの一言(ひとこと)にかかっていた |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the pattern lives in the <code>ている</code> form. Plain
<code>にかかる</code> is not the everyday shape of this meaning; you will meet it
only in headlines and slogans, where verbs are routinely stripped back.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
試合(しあい)の結果(けっか)は、明日(あした)の天気(てんき)<b>にかかっている</b>。
The result of the match depends on tomorrow's weather.
</pre>

<pre>
あとは君(きみ)の覚悟(かくご)<b>にかかっている</b>よ。
From here on it's down to how serious you are.
</pre>

<pre>
決勝(けっしょう)に進(すす)めるかどうかは、この一球(いっきゅう)<b>にかかっています</b>。
Whether they reach the final hangs on this one pitch.
</pre>

<pre>
工場(こうじょう)の再開(さいかい)は、部品(ぶひん)がいつ届(とど)くか<b>にかかっている</b>。
Restarting the plant depends on when the parts arrive.
</pre>

<pre>
この計画(けいかく)が実現(じつげん)するかどうかは、資金(しきん)が集(あつ)まるか<b>にかかっています</b>。
Whether this plan happens at all rests on whether we can raise the money.
</pre>

<pre>
会社(かいしゃ)の将来(しょうらい)は若(わか)い社員(しゃいん)の育(そだ)て方(かた)<b>にかかっている</b>と思(おも)います。
I believe the future of this company rests on how we bring on our younger staff.
</pre>

<pre>
条約(じょうやく)が結(むす)ばれるかどうかは、両国(りょうこく)がどこまで歩(あゆ)み寄(よ)れるか<b>にかかっている</b>。
Whether a treaty gets signed depends on how far the two countries can move towards each other.
</pre>

<pre>
小(ちい)さな店(みせ)が生(い)き残(のこ)れるかどうかは、常連(じょうれん)のお客(きゃく)さんをどれだけ大切(たいせつ)にできるか<b>にかかっている</b>。
Whether a small shop survives comes down to how well it looks after its regulars.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral in form: <code>にかかっている</code> and
<code>にかかっています</code> both fit a conversation, a meeting, a newspaper column
and a coach's team talk. What is *not* neutral is the weight. The expression is
dramatic by nature, and it wants a real outcome behind it - a contract, a season, a
harvest, someone's recovery. Used of a small choice it sounds like a joke:
昼(ひる)ご飯(はん)をどこにするかは気分(きぶん)にかかっている is comic
overstatement, and 気分(きぶん)次第(しだい)だ is what people actually say.

Because the outcome is suspended from one named thing, the sentence usually lands
as **pressure or responsibility**. 「あとは君(きみ)にかかっている」 is
encouragement and a burden in the same breath, and speakers use it deliberately for
that effect - in speeches, in sports commentary, at the end of a briefing. Softening
it is done by naming a group rather than a person:
皆(みな)さんの協力(きょうりょく)にかかっています spreads the load.

In formal writing the factor is often abstract and the sentence a little grand -
地域(ちいき)の未来(みらい)は教育(きょういく)にかかっている. There is also a
frozen literary variant, ～の双肩(そうけん)にかかっている, "rests on the
shoulders of", which turns up in editorials and ceremonial speech and nowhere else.

## Contrast

### ～にかかっている vs ～次第(しだい)だ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>次第(しだい)だ</code> simply names the **variable** that
settles the matter, at any level of importance. <code>にかかっている</code> adds that
something is **hanging in the balance**.

</div>

<pre>
出発(しゅっぱつ)するかどうかは天気(てんき)<b>次第(しだい)です</b>。
Whether we set off depends on the weather.
</pre>

<pre>
登山(とざん)隊(たい)の安全(あんぜん)は天気(てんき)<b>にかかっています</b>。
The safety of the climbing party hangs on the weather.
</pre>

Both are correct, and the first would be odd with <code>にかかっている</code> only
because a day trip is not weighty enough. Note also the grammar:
<code>次第(しだい)</code> bolts straight onto the noun with no particle, while
this pattern needs に.

### ～にかかっている vs ～によって決(き)まる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>によって決(き)まる</code> states a **rule**: this is the
factor a value is calculated from. <code>にかかっている</code> is about **one
outcome, undecided right now**.

</div>

<pre>
料金(りょうきん)は距離(きょり)<b>によって決(き)まります</b>。
The fare is determined by the distance.
</pre>

<pre>
この交渉(こうしょう)の行方(ゆくえ)は次(つぎ)の一手(いって)<b>にかかっている</b>。
Where this negotiation goes hangs on the next move.
</pre>

Swap them and the first becomes strange: a fare table is not in suspense. Where a
standing rule, a formula or a price list is involved, only
<code>によって決(き)まる</code> works.

### ～にかかっている vs ～にかかわる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - に**かかっている** = X **decides** the outcome.
に**かかわる** = the matter **touches on** X, X is what is at stake.

</div>

<pre>
これは命(いのち)<b>にかかわる</b>問題(もんだい)です。
This is a matter of life and death.
</pre>

<pre>
患者(かんじゃ)の命(いのち)は手術(しゅじゅつ)の成功(せいこう)<b>にかかっている</b>。
The patient's life depends on the operation succeeding.
</pre>

They are near-twins with opposite structure. Watch the particle in a third, related
shape: <code>命(いのち)がかかっている</code> - with が, not に - means "a life is
on the line", i.e. the life is the thing at stake, not the thing deciding.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 成功(せいこう)は努力(どりょく)<b>がかかっている</b>。 → ✅ 成功(せいこう)は努力(どりょく)<b>にかかっている</b>。
  The deciding factor takes に. With が the sentence flips: it would say that the
  effort is the thing at stake.

- ❌ 結果(けっか)は天気(てんき)<b>にかかる</b>。 → ✅ 結果(けっか)は天気(てんき)<b>にかかっている</b>。
  The pattern is the <code>ている</code> form. Plain <code>かかる</code> in speech is
  heard as the ordinary verb - time, money, a phone call.

- ❌ 優勝(ゆうしょう)できるかどうかは体調(たいちょう)次第(しだい)<b>にかかっている</b>。 → ✅ 優勝(ゆうしょう)できるかどうかは体調(たいちょう)<b>にかかっている</b>。
  One or the other. <code>次第(しだい)だ</code> and
  <code>にかかっている</code> do the same job, and stacking them is redundant.

- ❌ 今日(きょう)のおやつは母(はは)の機嫌(きげん)<b>にかかっている</b>。 → ✅ 今日(きょう)のおやつは母(はは)の機嫌(きげん)<b>次第(しだい)だ</b>。
  Not ungrammatical, but the stakes are wrong. Save the pattern for outcomes that
  genuinely hang in the balance.

## Practice

**1.** 来年(らいねん)大会(たいかい)を開(ひら)けるかどうかは、市(し)の支援(しえん)____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

市(し)の支援(しえん)<b>にかかっています</b>

The city's backing is the single thing the whole event is suspended from, and a
public event is weighty enough for the expression. Note the に in front of かかる.

</details>

**2.** Choose: タクシー代(だい)は距離(きょり)と時間(じかん)（にかかっている・によって決(き)まる）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

距離(きょり)と時間(じかん)<b>によって決(き)まる</b>。

A fare is calculated from a standing rule, so nothing is in suspense.
<code>にかかっている</code> needs an outcome that could still go either way.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Whether the village survives depends on whether young families come back."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

村(むら)が残(のこ)れるかどうかは、若(わか)い家族(かぞく)が戻(もど)ってくるか<b>どうかにかかっている</b>。

Both slots are clauses here: <code>かどうか</code> for the thing that depends, and a
second <code>かどうか</code> plus に for the factor. The high stakes make the
expression a natural fit.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この事故(じこ)は会社(かいしゃ)の信用(しんよう)にかかっている問題(もんだい)だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この事故(じこ)は会社(かいしゃ)の信用(しんよう)<b>にかかわる</b>問題(もんだい)だ。
"This accident is a matter that goes to the company's reputation."

The reputation is what is at stake, not what decides the outcome, so the verb is
<code>かかわる</code>. Compare
<code>会社(かいしゃ)の信用(しんよう)は対応(たいおう)の速(はや)さにかかっている</code>,
where speed of response really is the deciding factor.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 収穫(しゅうかく)量(りょう)は夏(なつ)の雨(あめ)<b>次第(しだい)だ</b>。
(b) 今年(ことし)の農家(のうか)の暮(く)らしは夏(なつ)の雨(あめ)<b>にかかっている</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a flat statement of what the yield varies with, the sort of line an
agricultural handbook would carry. It has no emotion and no urgency in it.

(b) puts people's livelihoods on the line and leaves them there. The listener hears
that if the rain fails the farmers are in trouble - the sentence is about suspense
and consequence, not about correlation.

</details>

# ～あっての

**Meaning** · exists only because of; would be nothing without - A is what makes B possible at all, and take A away and B goes with it

<code>あっての</code> is the て-form of ある plus の, so
<b>AあってのB</b> is literally "a B that exists because there is an A". The claim is
stronger than "A helps B". It says B has no independent existence: no customers, no
shop; no health, no career. Almost every use is a compliment paid to A.

## Formation

Both slots are **nouns**. Nothing conjugates and no particle intervenes.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun + Noun | A + あっての + B | お客(きゃく)様(さま) → お客(きゃく)様(さま)あっての商売(しょうばい) |
| As a full sentence | A + あっての + B + だ／です | 健康(けんこう) → 健康(けんこう)あっての仕事(しごと)です |
| No noun to hand | A + あってのことだ | ご協力(きょうりょく) → ご協力(きょうりょく)あってのことです |
| Same, with もの | A + あってのものだ | 信頼(しんらい) → 信頼(しんらい)あってのものだ |
| する-noun as A | Noun (not the verb) + あっての | 練習(れんしゅう)する → 練習(れんしゅう)あっての上達(じょうたつ) |
| Proverb | fixed | 命(いのち)あっての物種(ものだね) |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the order is fixed and it is the reverse of the English one.
The **precondition comes first**: お客(きゃく)様(さま)あっての商売(しょうばい),
never ❌商売(しょうばい)あってのお客(きゃく)様(さま).

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>Aあっての</code> is a noun-modifying phrase, so it cannot end a
sentence on its own. Where English would stop at "it is all thanks to your support",
Japanese needs a noun for の to attach to, and the default filler is
<code>こと</code>: ご支援(しえん)あってのことです.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
お客(きゃく)様(さま)<b>あっての</b>商売(しょうばい)です。
Our business exists because our customers do.
</pre>

<pre>
読者(どくしゃ)<b>あっての</b>雑誌(ざっし)だ。
A magazine is nothing without its readers.
</pre>

<pre>
健康(けんこう)<b>あっての</b>仕事(しごと)だから、無理(むり)はしないでね。
Your job depends on your health, so don't push yourself.
</pre>

<pre>
今日(きょう)の優勝(ゆうしょう)は、家族(かぞく)の支(ささ)え<b>あってのこと</b>です。
Today's win is entirely down to my family's support.
</pre>

<pre>
信頼(しんらい)<b>あっての</b>関係(かんけい)だから、嘘(うそ)だけはつきたくない。
It's a relationship built on trust, so lying is the one thing I won't do.
</pre>

<pre>
農家(のうか)の方々(かたがた)の苦労(くろう)<b>あっての</b>一杯(いっぱい)のご飯(はん)だと思(おも)う。
I think that bowl of rice exists because of the work farmers put in.
</pre>

<pre>
うちの店(みせ)が30年(ねん)続(つづ)いたのは、近所(きんじょ)の皆(みな)さんの応援(おうえん)<b>あってのこと</b>です。
The only reason our shop has lasted thirty years is the support of everyone nearby.
</pre>

<pre>
自由(じゆう)な議論(ぎろん)<b>あっての</b>民主主義(みんしゅしゅぎ)であって、批判(ひはん)を封(ふう)じてしまえば中身(なかみ)は残(のこ)らない。
Democracy exists only where argument is free; shut down criticism and nothing is left of it.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is the grammar of **thanks and of mottoes**. It turns up in shop creeds,
acceptance speeches, retirement remarks, company philosophies and the closing lines
of a formal message. It is polite without being stiff, and it works in ordinary
conversation as long as the sentiment is sincere - saying
健康(けんこう)あっての仕事(しごと)だよ to a colleague who is overworking is
perfectly natural spoken Japanese.

Two things follow from what it means. First, **A has to be something valued**. The
sentence is a tribute, so a neutral cause sounds wrong in the slot: an accident is
the reason for a traffic jam, but ❌事故(じこ)あっての渋滞(じゅうたい) is
bizarre, because it thanks the accident. Second, the A slot in practice draws from a
fairly small family of nouns - お客(きゃく)様(さま), 読者(どくしゃ),
ファン, 命(いのち), 健康(けんこう), 信頼(しんらい),
皆(みな)さんの支(ささ)え, ご協力(きょうりょく). Something unexpected there
does not read as creative; it reads as a slogan that has come out wrong.

The pattern is also the standard way to **deflect praise**. Asked how the project
succeeded, a Japanese manager will very often answer
現場(げんば)の皆(みな)さんの力(ちから)あってのことです rather than accept
the credit, and the phrase is heard as modest rather than falsely humble.

One proverb worth knowing on sight: 命(いのち)あっての物種(ものだね) - "while
there's life there's hope", or more bluntly, nothing matters if you are dead. It is
the usual thing said to someone taking a physical risk.

## Contrast

### ～あっての vs ～のおかげで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>おかげで</code> credits A with **a particular good
outcome**. <code>あっての</code> makes A a **standing precondition** for B's very
existence.

</div>

<pre>
薬(くすり)<b>のおかげで</b>熱(ねつ)が下(さ)がりました。
The fever came down thanks to the medicine.
</pre>

<pre>
ファン<b>あっての</b>チームだということを忘(わす)れてはいけない。
We must not forget that the team only exists because of its fans.
</pre>

Swap them and the first collapses: ❌薬(くすり)あっての熱(ねつ) would claim
the fever cannot exist without the medicine. The grammar differs too -
<code>おかげで</code> takes a whole clause
(手伝(てつだ)ってくれたおかげで), while <code>あっての</code> takes a bare noun
and nothing else.

### ～あっての vs ～なくしては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same claim from opposite ends. <code>あっての</code> states
it **positively and warmly**; <code>なくしては</code> states it **negatively** and
demands a negative predicate.

</div>

<pre>
支援(しえん)<b>あっての</b>復興(ふっこう)です。
Recovery is possible only because of the help we received.
</pre>

<pre>
支援(しえん)<b>なくしては</b>復興(ふっこう)はあり得(え)ない。
Without support, recovery is out of the question.
</pre>

The register gap is wide. The first is something a mayor says to volunteers; the
second belongs to an editorial or a policy paper. If you want to thank someone,
<code>あっての</code> is the one you want.

### ～あっての vs ～てこそ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>あっての</code> joins two **nouns**. When the precondition
is an **action**, use <code>てこそ</code> - or nominalise and keep
<code>あっての</code>.

</div>

<pre>
毎日(まいにち)続(つづ)け<b>てこそ</b>力(ちから)がつく。
Only by keeping at it every day do you build any strength.
</pre>

<pre>
地道(じみち)な練習(れんしゅう)<b>あっての</b>上達(じょうたつ)だ。
Progress comes out of patient practice and nothing else.
</pre>

Both sentences make the same point, and the choice is purely structural: if what you
have is a verb, ❌続(つづ)けるあっての is not available, so either switch to
<code>てこそ</code> or turn the verb into a noun as the second example does.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 努力(どりょく)する<b>あっての</b>成功(せいこう) → ✅ 努力(どりょく)<b>あっての</b>成功(せいこう)
  Only a noun goes in front. A する-verb has a noun sitting inside it - use that.

- ❌ 商売(しょうばい)<b>あっての</b>お客(きゃく)様(さま) → ✅ お客(きゃく)様(さま)<b>あっての</b>商売(しょうばい)
  Reversed. English puts the dependent thing first ("a business built on its
  customers"); Japanese puts the precondition first.

- ❌ 皆(みな)さんのご協力(きょうりょく)<b>あってです</b>。 → ✅ 皆(みな)さんのご協力(きょうりょく)<b>あってのことです</b>。
  The の has to modify something. With no noun in the sentence, insert
  <code>こと</code> or <code>もの</code>.

- ❌ 事故(じこ)<b>あっての</b>渋滞(じゅうたい)だ。 → ✅ 事故(じこ)による渋滞(じゅうたい)だ。
  Grammatical and still wrong. The pattern honours the thing in the A slot, so a
  plain cause needs <code>による</code> instead.

## Practice

**1.** 選手(せんしゅ)一人(ひとり)一人(ひとり)の努力(どりょく)____今回(こんかい)の記録(きろく)です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

努力(どりょく)<b>あっての</b>今回(こんかい)の記録(きろく)です

The record has no existence apart from the work the athletes put in, and the
sentence is paying them a compliment - both hallmarks of this pattern.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 新(あたら)しい薬(くすり)（あっての・のおかげで）、祖母(そぼ)は歩(ある)けるようになった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

新(あたら)しい薬(くすり)<b>のおかげで</b>、祖母(そぼ)は歩(ある)けるようになった。

This reports one good outcome that followed from the medicine, which is
<code>おかげで</code>. <code>あっての</code> cannot be used here at all: it has to
link two nouns, and there is no second noun for it to modify.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "A library is nothing without the people who use it."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

利用(りよう)する人(ひと)<b>あっての</b>図書館(としょかん)です。

The users are the precondition, so they go first; the library goes after の. Note
that a relative clause modifying the A noun is fine - what is banned is a verb
sitting directly in front of <code>あっての</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 会社(かいしゃ)がここまで来(こ)られたのは、社員(しゃいん)の皆(みな)さんあってだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 会社(かいしゃ)がここまで来(こ)られたのは、社員(しゃいん)の皆(みな)さん<b>あってのことだ</b>。
"The company got this far only because of its staff."

<code>あって</code> plus <code>の</code> must land on a noun. When the sentence has
none, <code>こと</code> fills the slot. (The potential of 来(く)る is
来(こ)られる - the こ stem, as in 来(こ)ない.)

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 部下(ぶか)の頑張(がんば)りのおかげで、目標(もくひょう)を達成(たっせい)できました。
(b) 部下(ぶか)の頑張(がんば)り<b>あっての</b>達成(たっせい)です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a straightforward thank-you: they worked hard and the target was reached. It
leaves open that the speaker contributed too.

(b) is a stronger and more formal statement. It says the achievement had no other
source - remove the team's effort and there is no achievement left to talk about.
This is the shape a manager reaches for in a speech, precisely because it hands over
all of the credit.

</details>

# ～なくして

**Meaning** · without; in the absence of - and if it is absent, the thing in question is simply not possible

<code>なくして</code> is 無(な)く plus して, an old adverbial form of ない frozen
into a conjunction. It names a **precondition** and then denies the rest of the
sentence: no A, therefore no B. It is a written form. Recognise it in editorials,
manifestos and speeches; say <code>がなければ</code> or
<code>なしでは</code> yourself.

## Formation

Nothing here conjugates. <code>なくして</code> has no tense, no polite form and no
negative of its own, and the predicate at the end of the sentence must be negative
or express impossibility.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + なくして + negative | 支援(しえん) → 支援(しえん)なくして再建(さいけん)はない |
| Noun (usual shape) | Noun + なくしては + negative | 理解(りかい) → 理解(りかい)なくしては進(すす)まない |
| Clause | Verb (plain) + こと + なくして | 話(はな)し合(あ)う → 話(はな)し合(あ)うことなくして |
| Aphorism frame | A + なくして + B + なし | 自由(じゆう) → 自由(じゆう)なくして平和(へいわ)なし |
| Required predicate | ～ない／～あり得(え)ない／不可能(ふかのう)だ | 実現(じつげん)できない, 語(かた)れない |
| Modern equivalent | ～がなければ／～なしでは | 支援(しえん)がなければ再建(さいけん)できない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the sentence must end in a **negative**.
❌<code>君(きみ)なくして寂(さび)しい</code> is not a stylistic slip, it is
ungrammatical. The whole job of <code>なくして</code> is to set up a denial.

</div>

## Examples

These are the registers the form actually lives in: editorial prose, formal
addresses, campaign lines and mottoes.

<pre>
努力(どりょく)<b>なくして</b>成長(せいちょう)はない。
There is no growth without effort.
</pre>

<pre>
信頼(しんらい)<b>なくして</b>協力(きょうりょく)はあり得(え)ない。
Cooperation is impossible in the absence of trust.
</pre>

<pre>
地元(じもと)の理解(りかい)<b>なくしては</b>、この事業(じぎょう)を進(すす)めることはできません。
Without the understanding of local people, this project cannot go forward.
</pre>

<pre>
徹底(てってい)した調査(ちょうさ)<b>なくして</b>、再発(さいはつ)防止(ぼうし)は語(かた)れない。
Talk of preventing a recurrence is empty without a thorough investigation.
</pre>

<pre>
安全(あんぜん)の確保(かくほ)<b>なくして</b>運転(うんてん)の再開(さいかい)はないというのが、住民(じゅうみん)側(がわ)の立場(たちば)である。
No restart without guaranteed safety - that is the residents' position.
</pre>

<pre>
多(おお)くの方(かた)の寄付(きふ)<b>なくしては</b>、この図書館(としょかん)は建(た)たなかっただろう。
Without the donations of a great many people, this library would never have been built.
</pre>

<pre>
過去(かこ)を見(み)つめ直(なお)すこと<b>なくして</b>、真(しん)の和解(わかい)はあり得(え)ない。
There can be no genuine reconciliation without an honest look at the past.
</pre>

<pre>
表現(ひょうげん)の自由(じゆう)<b>なくして</b>、健全(けんぜん)な社会(しゃかい)は成(な)り立(た)たない。
A healthy society cannot stand without freedom of expression.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Read it; do not produce it.** <code>なくして</code> belongs to written argument -
newspaper editorials, policy papers, party slogans, the openings of formal
addresses, chapter titles, and the Japanese of translated aphorisms. In conversation
it does not sound merely formal; it sounds like a quotation, as though the speaker
had switched into declaiming. The everyday equivalents are
<code>がなければ</code> and <code>なしでは</code>, and those are what you should
use.

The clearest signal of how frozen it is: the pattern's most productive use is not
a sentence at all but a **motto frame**, <b>AなくしてBなし</b> - three or four words,
no verb, the shape of a banner. 「努力(どりょく)なくして成功(せいこう)なし」 is
the specimen everybody knows, and new ones are coined on that model constantly for
campaign posters and company slogans. Outside that frame the form appears mainly in
prose that is arguing a case.

There is also an important homograph problem. The everyday word
無(な)くして in 「鍵(かぎ)を無(な)くしてしまった」 is the transitive verb
無(な)くす, "to lose", and has nothing to do with this pattern. Kana spelling
does not disambiguate; only the structure does. If a noun sits in front with no
particle and the sentence ends in a negative, it is this one.

Two mechanical facts to hold onto. It attaches to **nouns**, so a verbal idea has to
be nominalised with <code>こと</code>. And it is invariant - there is no
❌なくしました, no ❌なくしてです.

## Contrast

### ～なくして vs ～がなければ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning, different worlds.
<code>がなければ</code> is the **ordinary conditional** you should actually say;
<code>なくして</code> is its formal-written counterpart.

</div>

<pre>
君(きみ)の助(たす)け<b>がなかったら</b>、あの仕事(しごと)は終(お)わらなかったよ。
If it hadn't been for your help, that job would never have got finished.
</pre>

<pre>
関係(かんけい)各国(かっこく)の協調(きょうちょう)<b>なくして</b>、この問題(もんだい)の解決(かいけつ)はない。
Without coordination among the countries involved, there is no solving this problem.
</pre>

Both sentences say "without X, no Y". The first is what one person says to another.
The second is what a leader column says about the world. Put
<code>なくして</code> into the first and the speaker sounds as if they are addressing
a conference; put <code>がなかったら</code> into the second and the editorial goes
slack.

### ～なくして vs ～なしに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>なしに</code> says an action went ahead **unaccompanied**
by X. <code>なくして</code> says X is a **precondition**, and then denies the
outcome.

</div>

<pre>
説明(せつめい)も<b>なしに</b>、会議(かいぎ)が中止(ちゅうし)になった。
The meeting was cancelled with no explanation at all.
</pre>

<pre>
十分(じゅうぶん)な説明(せつめい)<b>なくして</b>、住民(じゅうみん)の同意(どうい)は得(え)られない。
Without a proper explanation, the residents' agreement cannot be obtained.
</pre>

The first reports what happened, and its predicate is perfectly positive. The second
could not be written with a positive predicate at all. That test separates them
reliably: if the sentence ends affirmatively, only <code>なしに</code> is possible.

### ～なくして vs ～あっての

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same claim from opposite ends.
<code>あっての</code> is **positive and grateful**; <code>なくして</code> is
**negative and argumentative**.

</div>

<pre>
皆(みな)さんの支(ささ)え<b>あっての</b>今日(きょう)です。
Today exists because of everyone's support.
</pre>

<pre>
市民(しみん)の支(ささ)え<b>なくして</b>、この活動(かつどう)は続(つづ)けられない。
This work cannot continue without the backing of the public.
</pre>

Choose by what you are doing. Thanking someone takes <code>あっての</code>, which is
warm and can be spoken. Pressing a case - warning what will fail if the support is
withdrawn - takes <code>なくして</code>, and lands as written argument.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 君(きみ)<b>なくして</b>寂(さび)しいよ。 → ✅ 君(きみ)がいないと寂(さび)しいよ。
  Two faults at once: the predicate is not negative, and a personal remark in this
  register sounds like a stage line.

- ❌ 許可(きょか)<b>なくして</b>中(なか)に入(はい)った。 → ✅ 許可(きょか)<b>なしに</b>中(なか)に入(はい)った。
  "Went in without permission" reports a completed action, so it needs
  <code>なしに</code>. <code>なくして</code> would require something like
  入(はい)れない at the end.

- ❌ 練習(れんしゅう)する<b>なくして</b>上達(じょうたつ)はない。 → ✅ 練習(れんしゅう)<b>なくして</b>上達(じょうたつ)はない。
  It attaches to a noun. Either use the bare noun or nominalise with
  <code>こと</code>: 練習(れんしゅう)することなくして.

- ❌ ご協力(きょうりょく)<b>なくしません</b>でした。 → ✅ ご協力(きょうりょく)<b>なくしては</b>不可能(ふかのう)でした。
  The form is invariant. It has no polite form, no past and no negative; the
  conjugating verb 無(な)くす is a different word.

**1.** Where would you expect to meet this sentence, and who is speaking?
対話(たいわ)<b>なくして</b>解決(かいけつ)はない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A newspaper editorial, a political speech, a placard or a headline - and the speaker
is arguing a case in public, not talking to a friend.

The clues are the bare noun with no particle, the missing verb at the end, and the
slogan-length rhythm. Nobody says this over lunch.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: 皆(みな)の協力(きょうりょく)なくしては、この計画(けいかく)は成功(せいこう)しない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

皆(みな)が協力(きょうりょく)して<b>くれなければ</b>、この計画(けいかく)は成功(せいこう)しない。

The plain conditional carries the whole meaning. <code>がなければ</code> and
<code>なしでは</code> would both work equally well here; all three say the same thing
with none of the platform manner.

</details>

**3.** Fill the blank in this campaign slogan: 安全(あんぜん)____成長(せいちょう)なし。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

安全(あんぜん)<b>なくして</b>成長(せいちょう)なし。

This is the motto frame AなくしてBなし, where the pattern is most at home. Note that
the final なし is itself the classical form of ない, which is why the two halves
match in register.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 新(あたら)しい技術(ぎじゅつ)なくして、生産(せいさん)量(りょう)が増(ふ)えた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 新(あたら)しい技術(ぎじゅつ)<b>なしに</b>、生産(せいさん)量(りょう)が増(ふ)えた。
"Output rose without any new technology."

The predicate is affirmative, so <code>なくして</code> cannot be used. If you want to
keep it, the sentence has to be turned round:
新(あたら)しい技術(ぎじゅつ)なくして増産(ぞうさん)はあり得(え)ない.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in register:
(a) お金(かね)がなければ留学(りゅうがく)はできない。
(b) 資金(しきん)<b>なくして</b>留学(りゅうがく)は実現(じつげん)しない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is ordinary spoken Japanese - a friend, a parent or a teacher stating a practical
fact about money and study abroad.

(b) reads as a line from a policy document or a fundraising appeal. The vocabulary
has shifted up as well (資金(しきん) rather than お金(かね),
実現(じつげん)しない rather than できない), which is typical: the pattern pulls
everything around it into formal written style. Said aloud to a friend, it would
sound like a parody.

</details>

# ～なしに

**Meaning** · without; with no X at all - something went ahead although X, which should have come first, never happened

<code>なしに</code> is the noun 無(な)し with に. It attaches to a **noun** and says
the main action took place unaccompanied by it. What the pattern adds over a plain
negative is a faint charge of impropriety: the nouns that fill the slot -
予告(よこく), 断(ことわ)り, 許可(きょか), 連絡(れんらく),
説明(せつめい) - are things somebody was owed and did not get.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + なしに | 予告(よこく) → 予告(よこく)なしに |
| Noun (emphatic) | 何(なん)の + Noun + もなしに | 説明(せつめい) → 何(なん)の説明(せつめい)もなしに |
| As a precondition | Noun + なしには + negative | 辞書(じしょ) → 辞書(じしょ)なしには読(よ)めない |
| Before a noun | Noun + なしの + Noun | 予約(よやく) → 予約(よやく)なしの来店(らいてん) |
| Clause | Verb (plain) + こと + なしに | 休(やす)む → 休(やす)むことなしに |
| Neutral counterpart | Noun + なしで | 砂糖(さとう) → 砂糖(さとう)なしで |
| Sentence-final | Noun + なしだ | 問題(もんだい) → 問題(もんだい)なしだ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>なしに</code> takes a noun and nothing else. To put a verb in front
of it you must nominalise first, which produces
<code>ことなしに</code> - a related but noticeably heavier pattern with rules of its
own. For a plain "without doing", ordinary
<code>～ないで</code> or <code>～ずに</code> is what a Japanese speaker reaches for.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
彼(かれ)は何(なん)の連絡(れんらく)も<b>なしに</b>休(やす)んだ。
He took the day off without a word to anyone.
</pre>

<pre>
予告(よこく)<b>なしに</b>料金(りょうきん)が変(か)わることがあります。
Charges may change without notice.
</pre>

<pre>
断(ことわ)り<b>なしに</b>人(ひと)の引(ひ)き出(だ)しを開(あ)けないでください。
Please don't open other people's drawers without asking.
</pre>

<pre>
前触(まえぶ)れ<b>なしに</b>停電(ていでん)になり、作業(さぎょう)が止(と)まってしまった。
The power went out with no warning at all and the work came to a halt.
</pre>

<pre>
この資料(しりょう)を許可(きょか)<b>なしに</b>複製(ふくせい)することを禁(きん)じます。
Reproduction of this material without permission is prohibited.
</pre>

<pre>
専門(せんもん)用語(ようご)が多(おお)いので、辞書(じしょ)<b>なしには</b>この論文(ろんぶん)は読(よ)めない。
There is so much jargon that you can't read this paper without a dictionary.
</pre>

<pre>
挨拶(あいさつ)も<b>なしに</b>帰(かえ)ってしまうなんて、いつもの彼(かれ)らしくない。
Leaving without so much as a goodbye isn't like him at all.
</pre>

<pre>
会社(かいしゃ)は従業員(じゅうぎょういん)への説明(せつめい)<b>なしに</b>、工場(こうじょう)の閉鎖(へいさ)を決(き)めてしまった。
The company decided to close the plant without explaining anything to its workforce.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>なしに</code> leans **written and formal**. Its natural homes are notices,
contracts, terms of service, warning signs and reported complaints - anywhere an
obligation was skipped and somebody is putting that on the record.
予告(よこく)なしに and 許可(きょか)なしに are close to fixed expressions in
that kind of text.

It works in speech, but only where the speaker is being pointed. 「何(なん)の
説明(せつめい)もなしに決(き)められた」 is a complaint, and it is heard as one.
For a neutral "he left without eating", speech uses
<code>食(た)べないで</code> or <code>食(た)べずに</code>, and
<code>なしに</code> would sound as though you were filing a grievance about the
missed meal.

The emphatic frame 何(なん)の…もなしに is worth learning as a unit. The も is
not optional decoration - it is what produces "not so much as a", and dropping it
leaves the sentence sounding half-built.

Register-wise, <code>なしで</code> is the everyday sibling and
<code>抜(ぬ)きで</code> the casual one; <code>なしに</code> sits above both. In a
negative sentence, <code>なしには</code> and <code>なしでは</code> both occur and
overlap heavily - 辞書(じしょ)なしには読(よ)めない and
辞書(じしょ)なしでは読(よ)めない are both good Japanese, with the first a
little more written.

## Contrast

### ～なしに vs ～ないで／～ずに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>なしに</code> takes a **noun**; <code>ないで</code> and
<code>ずに</code> take a **verb**. Where both are possible,
<code>なしに</code> is the more formal, and hints that the omission was improper.

</div>

<pre>
朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べ<b>ないで</b>出(で)かけた。
I went out without eating breakfast.
</pre>

<pre>
上司(じょうし)への報告(ほうこく)<b>なしに</b>契約(けいやく)を結(むす)んだ。
He signed the contract without reporting it to his manager.
</pre>

Notice that the first sentence is neutral - nobody is at fault for skipping
breakfast - and that swapping in 朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)なしに would make it sound
oddly grave. The second could be rewritten as
報告(ほうこく)しないで, which would soften it into a plain description of what
happened.

### ～なしに vs ～なしで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>なしで</code> is about doing without a **thing**: a tool,
an ingredient, a companion. <code>なしに</code> is about proceeding without an
**act** that ought to have come first.

</div>

<pre>
息子(むすこ)は補助(ほじょ)輪(りん)<b>なしで</b>自転車(じてんしゃ)に乗(の)れるようになった。
My son can ride a bike without training wheels now.
</pre>

<pre>
住民(じゅうみん)の同意(どうい)<b>なしに</b>工事(こうじ)が始(はじ)まった。
Construction started without the residents' consent.
</pre>

Reverse them and both go wrong in the same way. ❌補助(ほじょ)輪(りん)なしに
treats a piece of equipment as a missed procedure; ❌同意(どうい)なしで is not
impossible, but it flattens a serious omission into a shrug. In negative sentences
the two draw much closer together, and there the choice is register alone.

### ～なしに vs ～抜(ぬ)きで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>抜(ぬ)きで</code> means X was **deliberately left
out**, usually as a choice the speaker approves of. <code>なしに</code> simply
reports that X did not happen.

</div>

<pre>
今日(きょう)はお世辞(せじ)<b>抜(ぬ)きで</b>話(はな)しましょう。
Let's talk today with the flattery left out.
</pre>

<pre>
十分(じゅうぶん)な検討(けんとう)<b>なしに</b>結論(けつろん)が出(だ)された。
A conclusion was reached without adequate consideration.
</pre>

<code>抜(ぬ)きで</code> is casual and cheerful - ワサビ抜(ぬ)きで,
冗談(じょうだん)抜(ぬ)きで - and it implies an agent choosing to omit
something. <code>なしに</code> is neutral about who chose, which is exactly why it
suits the language of complaints and contracts.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 説明(せつめい)する<b>なしに</b>帰(かえ)った。 → ✅ 説明(せつめい)<b>なしに</b>帰(かえ)った。
  A verb cannot sit in front of it. Use the bare noun, or nominalise:
  説明(せつめい)することなしに.

- ❌ 砂糖(さとう)<b>なしに</b>コーヒーを飲(の)む。 → ✅ 砂糖(さとう)<b>なしで</b>コーヒーを飲(の)む。
  A missing ingredient is a thing, not a skipped obligation, so
  <code>なしで</code> is the fit.

- ❌ 予約(よやく)<b>なしに</b>のお客(きゃく)様(さま) → ✅ 予約(よやく)<b>なしの</b>お客(きゃく)様(さま)
  In front of a noun the form is <code>なしの</code>. に is for modifying a verb.

- ❌ 何(なん)の連絡(れんらく)<b>なしに</b>来(こ)なかった。 → ✅ 何(なん)の連絡(れんらく)<b>もなしに</b>来(こ)なかった。
  The emphatic frame is 何(なん)の…も…なしに. Without the も it reads as an
  unfinished sentence.

## Practice

**1.** 工事(こうじ)の音(おと)がひどい。管理人(かんりにん)から何(なん)の____始(はじ)まった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

何(なん)の知(し)らせ<b>もなしに</b>始(はじ)まった

The complaint frame is 何(なん)の + noun + も + なしに, and a notice from the
building manager is exactly the kind of owed act this pattern marks as missing.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 彼女(かのじょ)は眼鏡(めがね)（なしに・なしで）新聞(しんぶん)を読(よ)んでいる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

眼鏡(めがね)<b>なしで</b>新聞(しんぶん)を読(よ)んでいる。

Glasses are a piece of equipment, not a procedure somebody skipped, so this is
<code>なしで</code>. <code>なしに</code> would imply she was supposed to put her
glasses on first and improperly failed to.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "They cut down the trees without consulting anyone in the neighbourhood."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

近所(きんじょ)の人(ひと)への相談(そうだん)<b>なしに</b>、木(き)が切(き)られてしまった。

相談(そうだん) is an action-noun and consultation was owed here, which is the
core case for <code>なしに</code>. The passive plus しまう carries the speaker's
disapproval, matching the pattern's tone.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 会社(かいしゃ)は社員(しゃいん)に知(し)らせるなしに制度(せいど)を変(か)えた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 会社(かいしゃ)は社員(しゃいん)に<b>知(し)らせることなしに</b>制度(せいど)を変(か)えた。
（or 社員(しゃいん)への通知(つうち)<b>なしに</b>）

<code>なしに</code> needs a noun. Either add <code>こと</code> to nominalise the
verb, or replace the verb with the corresponding noun, which is the more natural
choice in written Japanese.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 彼(かれ)は何(なに)も言(い)わ<b>ずに</b>部屋(へや)を出(で)て行(い)った。
(b) 彼(かれ)は一言(ひとこと)の説明(せつめい)も<b>なしに</b>部屋(へや)を出(で)て行(い)った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain narration. He said nothing and left; the sentence does not judge him,
and it could equally describe someone who was upset or simply in a hurry.

(b) states that an explanation was owed and was not given. The noun
説明(せつめい), the emphatic 一言(ひとこと)の…も, and the formality of
<code>なしに</code> all point the same way, and the listener hears a grievance.

</details>

# ～ことなしに

**Meaning** · without doing A - and the point is that if you skip A, B is not going to happen

<code>ことなしに</code> is the verb version of <code>なしに</code>:
<code>こと</code> turns the verb into a noun, and に attaches it to the main clause.
It is not a neutral "without". It names A as an **unavoidable step** and then denies
the outcome - no honest look at the problem, no fix. Almost every real example ends
in a negative.

## Formation

The verb goes in the plain **dictionary form**. Nothing before
<code>ことなしに</code> is ever past or negative.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | Dictionary form + ことなしに | 認(みと)める → 認(みと)めることなしに |
| Verb (usual shape) | Dictionary form + ことなしには + negative | 変(か)わる → 変(か)わることなしには解決(かいけつ)しない |
| する-verb | Noun + する + ことなしに | 努力(どりょく)する → 努力(どりょく)することなしに |
| Polite predicate | ～ことなしに … ません | 見(み)る → 見(み)ることなしに判断(はんだん)できません |
| Noun in the slot instead | Noun + なしに（no こと） | 説明(せつめい) → 説明(せつめい)なしに |
| Positive-predicate relative | Dictionary form + ことなく | 休(やす)む → 休(やす)むことなく働(はたら)く |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the sentence needs a **negative or impossibility** at the end:
～ない, ～できない, ～あり得(え)ない, ～難(むずか)しい. If the main clause is
plainly affirmative, what you want is <code>ことなく</code> or
<code>ずに</code>, not this.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
人(ひと)と話(はな)す<b>ことなしに</b>、会話(かいわ)は上達(じょうたつ)しない。
Your conversation will not improve unless you talk to people.
</pre>

<pre>
失敗(しっぱい)する<b>ことなしに</b>、新(あたら)しい技術(ぎじゅつ)は生(う)まれない。
No new technology comes into being without failures along the way.
</pre>

<pre>
現場(げんば)を自分(じぶん)の目(め)で見(み)る<b>ことなしには</b>、正(ただ)しい判断(はんだん)はできません。
You cannot judge this correctly without seeing the site with your own eyes.
</pre>

<pre>
痛(いた)みを伴(ともな)う<b>ことなしに</b>改革(かいかく)を進(すす)めることはできない。
Reform cannot be carried through without pain.
</pre>

<pre>
長年(ながねん)の習慣(しゅうかん)を見直(みなお)す<b>ことなしに</b>、残業(ざんぎょう)を減(へ)らすのは無理(むり)だろう。
Cutting overtime is hopeless unless the habits of decades are reconsidered.
</pre>

<pre>
自分(じぶん)の非(ひ)を認(みと)める<b>ことなしに</b>、失(うしな)った信頼(しんらい)を取(と)り戻(もど)すことはできない。
Trust once lost cannot be recovered without admitting your own fault.
</pre>

<pre>
農薬(のうやく)に頼(たよ)る<b>ことなしに</b>大量(たいりょう)の野菜(やさい)を育(そだ)てるのは、想像(そうぞう)以上(いじょう)に難(むずか)しい。
Growing vegetables at scale without relying on chemicals is harder than people imagine.
</pre>

<pre>
誰(だれ)かを傷(きず)つける<b>ことなしに</b>真実(しんじつ)を語(かた)ることは、できないのかもしれない。
Perhaps the truth cannot be told without hurting somebody.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Formal and **written**. Its home is argument: editorials, essays, policy papers,
academic prose, the serious part of a business proposal. What it contributes is a
rhetorical shape - it sets up a condition the reader would rather avoid, and then
closes the door. That is why the A slot so often holds something unwelcome:
失敗(しっぱい)する, 痛(いた)みを伴(ともな)う, 非(ひ)を
認(みと)める, 犠牲(ぎせい)を払(はら)う.

In speech the same idea is expressed with <code>～ないで</code>,
<code>～ずに</code> or a plain conditional. 「練習(れんしゅう)しないと
上手(じょうず)にならないよ」 is what a teacher says to a student;
練習(れんしゅう)することなしに上達(じょうたつ)はしない is what the same
teacher writes in a preface. Using it over coffee about a small matter -
傘(かさ)を持(も)つことなしに出(で)かけた - is not wrong so much as absurdly
heavy, like saying "absent the provision of an umbrella".

Two mechanical points that catch learners. The verb is always in the **dictionary
form**, however far in the past the situation is: 調(しら)べることなしに
結論(けつろん)を出(だ)してしまった is correct, and ❌調(しら)べたこと
なしに is not. And the pattern is for **verbs only**. Where you have a plain noun -
許可(きょか), 予告(よこく), 説明(せつめい) - drop the
<code>こと</code> and use <code>なしに</code>.

## Contrast

### ～ことなしに vs ～ことなく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ことなく</code> is a plain adverbial, "without ever doing",
and happily takes a **positive** predicate. <code>ことなしに</code> sets A up as a
**necessary condition** and denies what follows.

</div>

<pre>
彼女(かのじょ)は一度(いちど)も振(ふ)り返(かえ)る<b>ことなく</b>歩(ある)き去(さ)った。
She walked away without once looking back.
</pre>

<pre>
過去(かこ)を振(ふ)り返(かえ)る<b>ことなしに</b>、同(おな)じ失敗(しっぱい)は防(ふせ)げない。
The same mistakes cannot be prevented without looking back at the past.
</pre>

This is the pair to get straight. The first sentence simply reports how she left, and
<code>ことなしに</code> would be wrong there because nothing is being denied. The
second could not use <code>ことなく</code> without losing the argument: the point is
not that people fail to look back, it is that not looking back makes prevention
impossible.

### ～ことなしに vs ～なしに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same pattern, different part of speech.
<code>ことなしに</code> takes a **verb**; <code>なしに</code> takes a **noun**.

</div>

<pre>
住民(じゅうみん)への説明(せつめい)<b>なしに</b>計画(けいかく)が決(き)まった。
The plan was settled with no explanation to residents.
</pre>

<pre>
住民(じゅうみん)に説明(せつめい)する<b>ことなしに</b>計画(けいかく)を進(すす)めるべきではない。
The plan should not be pushed forward without explaining it to residents.
</pre>

Many nouns can go either way, since 説明(せつめい) and
説明(せつめい)する are the same word in two coats. Choose by what the rest of the
clause needs: a full verb phrase with an object and a particle forces
<code>ことなしに</code>, while a bare noun is lighter with <code>なしに</code>.

### ～ことなしに vs ～ないで／～ずに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the meanings overlap; the **register does not**.
<code>ずに</code> is everyday Japanese for any omission at all;
<code>ことなしに</code> is written argument about an omission that matters.

</div>

<pre>
傘(かさ)を持(も)た<b>ずに</b>出(で)かけて、ずぶ濡(ぬ)れになった。
I went out without an umbrella and got soaked.
</pre>

<pre>
原因(げんいん)を突(つ)き止(と)める<b>ことなしに</b>対策(たいさく)を立(た)てても意味(いみ)がない。
There is no point drawing up measures without first pinning down the cause.
</pre>

The umbrella sentence cannot take <code>ことなしに</code>: the omission is trivial
and the predicate is not a denial. Going the other way is possible -
原因(げんいん)を突(つ)き止(と)めずに would be understood - but the
sentence loses the formal, argued tone the writer was after.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 努力(どりょく)した<b>ことなしに</b>成功(せいこう)はない。 → ✅ 努力(どりょく)する<b>ことなしに</b>成功(せいこう)はない。
  The verb stays in the dictionary form. Tense is carried by the main clause only.

- ❌ 練習(れんしゅう)する<b>ことなしに</b>上手(じょうず)になった。 → ✅ 練習(れんしゅう)する<b>ことなく</b>上手(じょうず)になった。
  With a positive predicate the pattern is <code>ことなく</code>.
  <code>ことなしに</code> exists to deny the second half.

- ❌ 説明(せつめい)<b>ことなしに</b>決(き)めてしまった。 → ✅ 説明(せつめい)<b>なしに</b>決(き)めてしまった。
  A bare noun takes <code>なしに</code>. <code>こと</code> is only there to
  nominalise a verb that is not one already.

- ❌ 昨日(きのう)は朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べる<b>ことなしに</b>家(いえ)を出(で)た。 → ✅ 昨日(きのう)は朝(あさ)ご飯(はん)を食(た)べ<b>ずに</b>家(いえ)を出(で)た。
  Grammatical, but the weight is wrong. Everyday omissions belong to
  <code>ずに</code> and <code>ないで</code>.

## Practice

**1.** 信頼(しんらい)関係(かんけい)を築(きず)く____、本音(ほんね)を引(ひ)き出(だ)すことはできない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

築(きず)く<b>ことなしに</b>

Building trust is presented as the step you cannot skip, and the main clause is a
denial of possibility - the standard shape for this pattern.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 彼(かれ)は三十年(さんじゅうねん)間(かん)、一日(いちにち)も休(やす)む（ことなく・ことなしに）店(みせ)を開(あ)け続(つづ)けた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

一日(いちにち)も休(やす)む<b>ことなく</b>店(みせ)を開(あ)け続(つづ)けた。

The main clause is affirmative - he kept the shop open - so the plain adverbial
<code>ことなく</code> is required. <code>ことなしに</code> needs something to deny.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "You cannot understand a country without learning something of its history."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

その国(くに)の歴史(れきし)を学(まな)ぶ<b>ことなしに</b>、その国(くに)を理解(りかい)することはできない。

Learning the history is the unavoidable step; understanding is denied without it.
Note the dictionary form 学(まな)ぶ and the negative
できない at the end - both are obligatory.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 十分(じゅうぶん)に議論(ぎろん)したことなしに、結論(けつろん)を急(いそ)ぐべきではない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 十分(じゅうぶん)に議論(ぎろん)<b>することなしに</b>、結論(けつろん)を急(いそ)ぐべきではない。
"We should not rush to a conclusion without a full discussion."

The past form 議論(ぎろん)した cannot appear here. <code>こと</code> attaches to
the dictionary form, and the tense of the sentence lives in the final predicate.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 誰(だれ)にも相談(そうだん)せ<b>ずに</b>会社(かいしゃ)を辞(や)めた。
(b) 周(まわ)りに相談(そうだん)する<b>ことなしに</b>正(ただ)しい決断(けつだん)はできない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) narrates one person's action. He quit, and he consulted nobody; the sentence is
neutral about whether that was wise, and the register is ordinary conversation.

(b) makes a general claim in written style: consulting others is a condition for
deciding well, and without it good decisions are impossible. It states a principle
rather than an event, and it would sound oddly grand as a remark about a friend's
resignation.

</details>

# ～もしないで

**Meaning** · without even doing; without so much as - the bare minimum was skipped, and the speaker is annoyed about it

Take the ます-stem, wedge in a も, and finish with <code>しないで</code>:
見(み)ないで becomes 見(み)もしないで. That も is the "even" of
水(みず)も飲(の)まない. So the pattern does not merely report an omission - it
says the person did not do the one obvious minimum thing, and then went ahead
anyway. It is a complaint almost every time.

## Formation

The base is the **ます-stem**, and も sits between the stem and しないで.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + もしないで | 謝(あやま)る → 謝(あやま)りもしないで |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + もしないで | 調(しら)べる → 調(しら)べもしないで |
| Verb (来(く)る) | **き** + もしないで | 来(く)る → 来(き)もしないで |
| する-verb | Noun + もしないで | 掃除(そうじ)する → 掃除(そうじ)もしないで |
| Formal / written | ます-stem + もせずに | 読(よ)む → 読(よ)みもせずに |
| Ending the sentence | ます-stem + もしない | 見(み)る → 見(み)もしない |
| Contrastive sibling | ます-stem + はしない | 見(み)る → 見(み)はしない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - う-verb / る-verb are the two regular classes（五段(ごだん) /
一段(いちだん), Group 1 / Group 2）. 来(く)る is irregular and its stem here is
**き**, the same stem as 来(き)ます - not こ, which belongs to
来(こ)ない and 来(こ)られる.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
彼(かれ)は謝(あやま)り<b>もしないで</b>帰(かえ)って行(い)った。
He left without so much as an apology.
</pre>

<pre>
中身(なかみ)を見(み)<b>もしないで</b>捨(す)てないでよ。
Don't throw it out without even looking inside.
</pre>

<pre>
自分(じぶん)では何(なに)もやり<b>もしないで</b>、文句(もんく)ばかり言(い)っている。
He does nothing himself and yet complains non-stop.
</pre>

<pre>
うちの息子(むすこ)は部屋(へや)の掃除(そうじ)<b>もしないで</b>、ゲームばかりしている。
My son plays games all day and doesn't even tidy his room.
</pre>

<pre>
説明書(せつめいしょ)を読(よ)み<b>もせずに</b>組(く)み立(た)てるから、こういうことになる。
This is what happens when you assemble it without even reading the instructions.
</pre>

<pre>
事情(じじょう)を確(たし)かめ<b>もしないで</b>人(ひと)を疑(うたが)うのはよくない。
Suspecting someone without even checking the facts is not on.
</pre>

<pre>
挨拶(あいさつ)に来(き)<b>もしないで</b>、いきなり条件(じょうけん)の話(はなし)を始(はじ)めた。
Without even coming over to say hello, he launched straight into terms.
</pre>

<pre>
口(くち)をつけ<b>もしないで</b>「まずい」と決(き)めつけるのは、さすがに失礼(しつれい)だと思(おも)う。
Writing the food off as bad without so much as tasting it is, I have to say, rude.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The form is register-neutral - it fits casual speech and ordinary polite speech
alike, and <code>もせずに</code> covers formal writing. What is heavily marked is the
**attitude**. This construction accuses. It sets up a minimum standard, reports that
it was not met, and then names the thing the person did instead, which is usually
worse: complaining, deciding, throwing something away, suspecting somebody.

Because of that, it is rarely used about oneself except in self-reproach -
調(しら)べもしないで買(か)ってしまった, "I bought it without even checking,
idiot that I am". Used about a listener it is direct criticism, and softening
particles do not really rescue it; if you want to be gentle, drop the も and say
調(しら)べないで.

The も is doing real work. 読(よ)まないで just says the thing was not read;
読(よ)みもしないで says not one line was read, when reading it was the obvious
first step. Note also the sentence-final use, which is the same construction
standing alone: 「あの人(ひと)は謝(あやま)りもしない」 - "he doesn't even
apologise". The near-twin <code>はしない</code>（謝(あやま)りはしない）swaps the
"even" for a contrast: he does other things, but apologising is not among them.

One structural point. With a する-verb, も goes straight after the noun -
掃除(そうじ)もしないで, 連絡(れんらく)もしないで,
確認(かくにん)もしないで. This looks like a different pattern and is not; the noun
is simply standing where the ます-stem would.

## Contrast

### ～もしないで vs ～ないで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ないで</code> reports the omission.
<code>もしないで</code> adds **"not even"**, and with it a judgement.

</div>

<pre>
傘(かさ)を持(も)た<b>ないで</b>出(で)かけた。
I went out without taking an umbrella.
</pre>

<pre>
天気(てんき)予報(よほう)を見(み)<b>もしないで</b>出(で)かけるから、いつも濡(ぬ)れるんだ。
You get soaked every time because you go out without even checking the forecast.
</pre>

The first is a fact about the morning. The second is a scolding, and the も is what
makes it one. If you find yourself wanting to add "even" in the English, the も
belongs in the Japanese.

### ～もしないで vs ～もせずに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical in meaning. <code>しないで</code> is **spoken**,
<code>せずに</code> is **written and formal**. The disapproval is the same in both.

</div>

<pre>
下調(したしら)べ<b>もしないで</b>面接(めんせつ)に行(い)ったの。
You went to the interview without doing any research at all?
</pre>

<pre>
現地(げんち)を確認(かくにん)<b>もせずに</b>報告書(ほうこくしょ)をまとめたことが問題(もんだい)である。
The problem is that the report was compiled without even inspecting the site.
</pre>

Note that <code>せずに</code> uses the old negative stem せ of する - never
❌しずに. Everything else about the two forms is the same, so pick by the register
of the surrounding text.

### ～もしないで vs ろくに～ないで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>もしないで</code> says the act happened **not at all**.
<code>ろくに～ないで</code> says it happened but **not properly**.

</div>

<pre>
説明(せつめい)を聞(き)き<b>もしないで</b>判断(はんだん)した。
He made up his mind without hearing the explanation at all.
</pre>

<pre>
説明(せつめい)を<b>ろくに</b>聞(き)か<b>ないで</b>判断(はんだん)した。
He made up his mind having barely listened to the explanation.
</pre>

In the first he did not listen for a second; in the second he sat through some of it
with half an ear. Both disapprove, which is why they feel interchangeable, but they
make different factual claims - and if the person genuinely did nothing at all,
ろくに understates it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 謝(あやま)る<b>もしないで</b> → ✅ 謝(あやま)り<b>もしないで</b>
  The base is the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.

- ❌ 来(こ)<b>もしないで</b> → ✅ 来(き)<b>もしないで</b>
  来(く)る is irregular. Its ます-stem is き; こ belongs to
  来(こ)ない and 来(こ)よう.

- ❌ 調(しら)べ<b>もしなくて</b>買(か)った。 → ✅ 調(しら)べ<b>もしないで</b>買(か)った。
  <code>なくて</code> gives a reason ("because it wasn't"); "without doing" is
  <code>ないで</code>.

- ❌ 彼女(かのじょ)は化粧(けしょう)<b>もしないで</b>きれいだ。 → ✅ 彼女(かのじょ)は化粧(けしょう)をし<b>なくても</b>きれいだ。
  The pattern is not "even without doing X". It is "without even doing X", and it
  needs something the speaker disapproves of in the second half.

## Practice

**1.** 値段(ねだん)を確(たし)かめ____買(か)ってしまい、あとで後悔(こうかい)した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

確(たし)かめ<b>もしないで</b>

Checking the price is the obvious minimum step, and skipping it led to something the
speaker regrets - the standard shape, here turned on the speaker himself.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 彼(かれ)はメールを（読(よ)まないで・読(よ)みもしないで）削除(さくじょ)したらしい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

メールを<b>読(よ)みもしないで</b>削除(さくじょ)したらしい。

Deleting a message unread is precisely the "not even" case, and the speaker is
clearly not impressed. 読(よ)まないで would be a flat report with no comment in it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "She threw the letter away without even opening it."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

彼女(かのじょ)はその手紙(てがみ)を開(あ)け<b>もしないで</b>捨(す)ててしまった。

Opening it is the minimum act that was skipped, so も goes after the ます-stem
開(あ)け. しまう adds the sense that this was a waste or a shame.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 部長(ぶちょう)は報告書(ほうこくしょ)を見(み)るもせずに、会議(かいぎ)で批判(ひはん)を始(はじ)めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 部長(ぶちょう)は報告書(ほうこくしょ)を<b>見(み)もせずに</b>、会議(かいぎ)で批判(ひはん)を始(はじ)めた。
"The department head started criticising it in the meeting without even looking at the report."

The dictionary form 見(み)る cannot appear before もせずに. The base is the

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 連絡(れんらく)<b>しないで</b>欠席(けっせき)した。
(b) 連絡(れんらく)<b>もしないで</b>欠席(けっせき)した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) states what happened: he was absent and did not get in touch. It could be said by
someone simply passing on information.

(b) says a phone call was the least he could have done and he did not even manage
that. The も turns a fact into an accusation, and a listener would take the speaker
to be annoyed with him.

</details>

# ～ともなく

**Meaning** · without meaning to; not really doing it on purpose - the action happened, but no intention was behind it

The なく misleads almost everybody. <code>ともなく</code> does **not** say the action
failed to occur. 見(み)るともなく見(み)ていた means the person *was* looking -
at nothing in particular, without having decided to look. What is denied is the
intention, not the act. This is a narrator's form: recognise it in fiction and
essays, and say <code>なんとなく</code> yourself.

## Formation

Two shapes, and they behave differently. The first takes a **verb**, usually the
same verb twice; the second takes a **question word**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | Dictionary form + ともなく + (same verb) | 聞(き)く → 聞(き)くともなく聞(き)いていた |
| Verb (fuller variant) | Dictionary form + ともなしに | 見(み)る → 見(み)るともなしに見(み)ていた |
| Question word + particle | どこから／誰(だれ)から + ともなく | どこから → どこからともなく |
| Question word alone | いつ／どちら + ともなく | どちら → どちらからともなく |
| Tense | carried by the **second** verb only | 待(ま)つともなく待(ま)っていた |
| Modern equivalent (verb use) | なんとなく／ぼんやり + ～ていた | 見(み)る → なんとなく見(み)ていた |
| Modern equivalent (question use) | 疑問詞(ぎもんし) + か | どこから → どこからか |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the verb in front of <code>ともなく</code> is always the plain
**dictionary form**, and the sentence always ends **affirmatively**. If the second
half is negative, you are not looking at this pattern.

</div>

## Examples

These are the registers the form really occurs in - narration, description, and the
handful of question-word phrases that have survived into ordinary prose.

<pre>
見(み)る<b>ともなく</b>窓(まど)の外(そと)を眺(なが)めていた。
He was gazing out of the window without really looking at anything.
</pre>

<pre>
ラジオを聞(き)く<b>ともなしに</b>聞(き)いていたら、懐(なつ)かしい曲(きょく)が流(なが)れてきた。
The radio was on in the background when a song I hadn't heard in years came on.
</pre>

<pre>
どこから<b>ともなく</b>、金木犀(きんもくせい)の香(かお)りが漂(ただよ)ってきた。
The scent of osmanthus drifted in from somewhere.
</pre>

<pre>
誰(だれ)から<b>ともなく</b>拍手(はくしゅ)が起(お)こり、やがて会場(かいじょう)全体(ぜんたい)に広(ひろ)がった。
Applause began without anyone starting it and spread through the whole hall.
</pre>

<pre>
待(ま)つ<b>ともなく</b>駅(えき)のベンチに座(すわ)っていると、見覚(みおぼ)えのある顔(かお)が通(とお)り過(す)ぎた。
As he sat on the station bench, waiting for nothing in particular, a familiar face went past.
</pre>

<pre>
どちらから<b>ともなく</b>手(て)を差(さ)し出(だ)し、二人(ふたり)は静(しず)かに握手(あくしゅ)をした。
Neither of them quite began it; a hand went out, and the two shook quietly.
</pre>

<pre>
言(い)う<b>ともなしに</b>もらした一言(ひとこと)が、後(あと)になって大(おお)きな問題(もんだい)になった。
A remark he let slip without thinking became a serious problem later on.
</pre>

<pre>
いつから<b>ともなく</b>、この路地(ろじ)は「猫(ねこ)通(どお)り」と呼(よ)ばれるようになっていた。
At some point nobody could name, this alley had come to be called Cat Lane.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**This is narration.** The verb use - 見(み)るともなく見(み)る,
聞(き)くともなしに聞(き)く, 待(ま)つともなく待(ま)つ - belongs to
novels, essays, memoirs and the descriptive parts of a screenplay. Said in
conversation it does not sound merely formal, it sounds as though the speaker were
narrating their own life aloud. The everyday equivalents are
<code>なんとなく</code>, <code>ぼんやり</code> and <code>無意識(むいしき)に</code>:
「なんとなくテレビ見(み)てた」 is what a person actually says.

The question-word use is a different matter and much more alive.
どこからともなく, 誰(だれ)からともなく and どちらからともなく turn up in ordinary
journalism, in descriptive writing and occasionally in speech, and they are best
learned as fixed phrases rather than as an application of a rule.

The trap is worth repeating, because it catches nearly every learner. **なく here
does not negate the action.** 聞(き)くともなく聞(き)いていた describes someone
who heard the whole thing; they simply were not trying to. If you want "without
listening", that is 聞(き)かずに or 聞(き)かないで, and those are entirely
different sentences.

Two smaller points. The verb before <code>ともなく</code> takes no tense - the past
lives in the second verb. And the two verbs are usually the same one; where they
differ, the second is a near relative
(見(み)るともなく眺(なが)める), never something unrelated.

## Contrast

### ～ともなく vs なんとなく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different century. <code>なんとなく</code> is the
**ordinary modern** way to say it; <code>ともなく</code> is the **literary** way, and
it also requires you to name the verb twice.

</div>

<pre>
きのうは<b>なんとなく</b>テレビを見(み)ていた。
I was just watching TV yesterday, not really for any reason.
</pre>

<pre>
彼(かれ)は見(み)る<b>ともなく</b>テレビの画面(がめん)に目(め)をやっていた。
He had his eyes on the television screen without really watching it.
</pre>

The first is what you say; the second is how a novel describes it. Note also that
<code>なんとなく</code> modifies any verb freely and can attach to feelings as well
（なんとなく不安(ふあん)だ）, while <code>ともなく</code> needs a concrete verb of
perception or waiting and cannot describe a state.

### ～ともなく vs ～ずに／～ないで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ともなく</code> = the action **happened**, unintentionally.
<code>ずに</code> = the action **did not happen** at all.

</div>

<pre>
音楽(おんがく)を聞(き)か<b>ずに</b>勉強(べんきょう)した。
I studied without listening to music.
</pre>

<pre>
音楽(おんがく)を聞(き)く<b>ともなく</b>聞(き)きながら、机(つくえ)に向(む)かっていた。
Music was playing half-heard as he sat at his desk.
</pre>

In the first there was no music in his ears; in the second there was, and he could
probably tell you which album. This is the single most useful thing to remember
about the pattern, because the なく invites exactly the wrong reading.

### 疑問詞(ぎもんし)＋ともなく vs 疑問詞(ぎもんし)＋か

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>どこからか</code> means the speaker **doesn't know where**.
<code>どこからともなく</code> means there **is no particular where** - the thing
seems to come from nowhere in particular.

</div>

<pre>
どこから<b>か</b>子供(こども)の声(こえ)が聞(き)こえる。
I can hear a child's voice from somewhere.
</pre>

<pre>
どこから<b>ともなく</b>人(ひと)が集(あつ)まり、広場(ひろば)はいっぱいになった。
People gathered from all directions and the square filled up.
</pre>

The first sentence implies a real source that the speaker has not located. The second
does not: nobody arrived from any one place, and the effect is of something welling
up on its own. That difference is why 誰(だれ)からともなく拍手(はくしゅ)が
起(お)こる is not the same claim as 誰(だれ)かが拍手(はくしゅ)を
始(はじ)めた.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 見(み)る<b>ともなく</b>、外(そと)を見(み)なかった。 → ✅ 見(み)る<b>ともなく</b>外(そと)を見(み)ていた。
  The pattern is not a negative. The looking happened; only the intention is denied.

- ❌ 見(み)た<b>ともなく</b> → ✅ 見(み)る<b>ともなく</b>
  The first verb is always the plain dictionary form. Tense belongs to the second
  verb.

- ❌ 明日(あした)は読(よ)む<b>ともなく</b>本(ほん)を読(よ)むつもりだ。 → ✅ 明日(あした)はのんびり本(ほん)でも読(よ)もうと思(おも)う。
  You cannot plan to do something unintentionally. The pattern looks back at what
  already happened.

- ❌ 「きのう見(み)るともなくテレビ見(み)てたんだよね」（to a friend） → ✅ 「きのうなんとなくテレビ見(み)てた」
  Grammatical but theatrical. In conversation this is
  <code>なんとなく</code> or <code>ぼんやり</code>.

## Practice

**1.** Did the action happen or not? 彼女(かのじょ)は聞(き)く<b>ともなく</b>隣(となり)の会話(かいわ)を聞(き)いていた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It happened. She heard the neighbouring conversation - she simply was not trying to.

The なく denies the intention only. A learner who reads this as "she did not listen"
has the sentence backwards, which is the classic error with this form.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: 待(ま)つともなく駅(えき)の前(まえ)に立(た)っていた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>なんとなく</b>駅(えき)の前(まえ)に立(た)っていた。（or ぼんやり立(た)っていた）

<code>なんとなく</code> carries the whole meaning without the literary doubling of
the verb, and it is what a speaker would actually use about their own afternoon.

</details>

**3.** Fill the blank: 会場(かいじょう)が静(しず)まると、誰(だれ)から____歌(うた)が始(はじ)まった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

誰(だれ)から<b>ともなく</b>歌(うた)が始(はじ)まった

Nobody in particular started it; the singing simply arose. 誰(だれ)かが would
claim there was a specific person, which is the opposite of what the sentence
describes.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 彼(かれ)は考(かんが)えるともなく考(かんが)えなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 彼(かれ)は考(かんが)える<b>ともなく</b>考(かんが)えていた。
"He was turning it over in his mind without meaning to."

The sentence has to end affirmatively. Written with a negative it says nothing at
all - the pattern already contains everything it is going to deny.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference:
(a) どこか<b>から</b>いい匂(にお)いがしてきた。
(b) どこから<b>ともなく</b>いい匂(にお)いがしてきた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) assumes a real source somewhere - a kitchen, a stall - that the speaker has not
identified yet. You could plausibly go and find it.

(b) refuses to locate the source at all. The smell seems to be simply in the air,
arriving from no direction in particular, and the phrasing is doing atmospheric work
of the kind you meet in descriptive writing rather than in a conversation about
lunch.

</details>

# (よ)うにも～ない

**Meaning** · I would if I could, but I can't - the will is there and the circumstances block it

The volitional says "let me do it"; にも concedes that; the potential negative shuts
it down. So 帰(かえ)ろうにも帰(かえ)れない is not "I didn't go home" but "I
wanted to go home and there was no way to". The obstacle is outside the speaker, and
it is normally named in the same sentence.

## Formation

The **same verb twice**: volitional first, potential negative second.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | Volitional + にも + potential negative | 休(やす)む → 休(やす)もうにも休(やす)めない |
| Verb (る-verb) | ～よう + にも + ～られない | 出(で)る → 出(で)ようにも出(で)られない |
| Verb (する) | **しよう**にも + **できない** | 連絡(れんらく)する → 連絡(れんらく)しようにもできない |
| Verb (来(く)る) | <b>来(こ)よう</b>にも <b>来(こ)られない</b> | 来(く)る → 来(こ)ようにも来(こ)られない |
| Polite | ～ようにも … ません | 買(か)う → 買(か)おうにも買(か)えません |
| Past | ～ようにも … なかった | 下(お)りる → 下(お)りようにも下(お)りられなかった |
| With the reason attached | ～て／～ので + ～ようにも … ない | 熱(ねつ)がある → 熱(ねつ)があって起(お)きようにも起(お)きられない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - う-verb / る-verb are the two regular classes（五段(ごだん) /
一段(いちだん), Group 1 / Group 2）. Watch the two irregulars: する pairs
しよう with できない, not ❌されない, and 来(く)る gives
来(こ)ようにも来(こ)られない - the こ stem in both halves.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
熱(ねつ)があって、起(お)き<b>ようにも起(お)きられない</b>。
I've got a fever - I can't get up however much I want to.
</pre>

<pre>
終電(しゅうでん)が行(い)ってしまって、帰(かえ)ろ<b>うにも帰(かえ)れない</b>。
The last train's gone, so there's no getting home now.
</pre>

<pre>
電話(でんわ)番号(ばんごう)を知(し)らないので、連絡(れんらく)し<b>ようにもできない</b>。
I don't have his number, so I can't get in touch even if I want to.
</pre>

<pre>
忙(いそが)しすぎて、休(やす)も<b>うにも休(やす)めない</b>日(ひ)が続(つづ)いている。
It's been one day after another where I couldn't take a break even if I tried.
</pre>

<pre>
雪(ゆき)で道(みち)がふさがれ、山(やま)を下(お)り<b>ようにも下(お)りられなかった</b>。
Snow had blocked the path and there was no way down off the mountain.
</pre>

<pre>
部品(ぶひん)がもう作(つく)られていないので、直(なお)そ<b>うにも直(なお)せない</b>状態(じょうたい)です。
The parts aren't made any more, so it simply can't be repaired.
</pre>

<pre>
停電(ていでん)でエレベーターが止(と)まり、上(うえ)の階(かい)へ上(あ)がろ<b>うにも上(あ)がれなかった</b>。
The power cut stopped the lifts, and there was no getting up to the floors above.
</pre>

<pre>
記録(きろく)がすべて処分(しょぶん)されていて、当時(とうじ)のことを調(しら)べ<b>ようにも調(しら)べられない</b>。
The records were all destroyed, so there is no way to investigate what happened then.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and genuinely useful: it fits conversation, a business email and a
news report equally. The feeling it carries is **frustration** - the speaker is
willing, sometimes desperate, and something outside them has closed the door. That
is why a reason clause almost always sits in front of it
(熱(ねつ)があって, 終電(しゅうでん)が行(い)ってしまって,
雪(ゆき)で). A bare
帰(かえ)ろうにも帰(かえ)れない with no explanation sounds unfinished.

It follows that the pattern cannot be used when you simply do not want to do the
thing. ❌行(い)きたくないから行(い)こうにも行(い)けない contradicts itself;
if the reluctance is internal, the sentence is just 行(い)かない or
行(い)きたくない. The same goes for a plain lack of skill - the block has to be
circumstantial. 緊張(きんちょう)して話(はな)そうにも話(はな)せなかった is
fine, because nerves are treated as something that happened to the speaker.

The second half is normally the **potential negative of the same verb**. A closely
related negative clause is also idiomatic where repeating the verb would be odd -
買(か)おうにも売(う)っていない, "I'd buy one if anyone sold them" - but the
repeated-verb version is the safe default and the one tested.

Two forms to keep straight in speech: う-verbs give the volitional in お-う
（休(やす)もう, 帰(かえ)ろう, 買(か)おう）, and their potential is the
え-stem（休(やす)める, 帰(かえ)れる, 買(か)える）. Getting one right and the
other wrong is the usual slip.

## Contrast

### (よ)うにも～ない vs ～たくても～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>たくても</code> foregrounds the **wish**;
<code>(よ)うにも</code> foregrounds the **attempt**, and is a little more formal.

</div>

<pre>
会(あ)い<b>たくても</b>、もう会(あ)え<b>ない</b>。
I'd love to see her, but I can't any more.
</pre>

<pre>
書類(しょるい)がそろわず、申(もう)し込(こ)も<b>うにも申(もう)し込(こ)めない</b>。
The paperwork isn't complete, so there's no applying even if we try.
</pre>

The overlap is large and both are correct in most sentences.
<code>たくても</code> is the everyday choice and needs a subject with feelings, which
is why it fits the first sentence so well. <code>(よ)うにも</code> works equally for
an organisation or an impersonal situation, where "wanting" would be strange.

### (よ)うにも～ない vs ～ようがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ようがない</code> says there is **no method**, full stop.
<code>(よ)うにも～ない</code> says **this particular obstacle** is in the way, and
usually names it.

</div>

<pre>
ここまで壊(こわ)れると、もう直(なお)し<b>ようがない</b>。
Broken this badly, there's no fixing it.
</pre>

<pre>
工具(こうぐ)を全部(ぜんぶ)貸(か)してしまって、直(なお)そ<b>うにも直(なお)せない</b>。
I've lent out all my tools, so I can't repair it even though I'd like to.
</pre>

The two are easy to confuse because both attach to a verb stem and both end in ない.
<code>ようがない</code> takes the **ます-stem**（直(なお)し-）and makes a flat
statement about impossibility; this pattern takes the **volitional**
（直(なお)そう-）and implies a thwarted intention. If nobody in the world could do
it, use <code>ようがない</code>.

### (よ)うにも～ない vs ～わけにはいかない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>(よ)うにも～ない</code> = **cannot**, physically or
practically. <code>わけにはいかない</code> = **could, but must not** - duty, custom
or conscience forbids it.

</div>

<pre>
大事(だいじ)な会議(かいぎ)だから、休(やす)む<b>わけにはいかない</b>。
It's an important meeting, so I can't very well take the day off.
</pre>

<pre>
代(か)わりの人(ひと)がいなくて、休(やす)も<b>うにも休(やす)めない</b>。
There's nobody to cover for me, so taking a day off is out of the question.
</pre>

In the first the speaker could stay home and has decided that would be wrong. In the
second staying home is not available at all. Learners reach for
<code>わけにはいかない</code> when they mean simple impossibility; the test is whether
anyone would be annoyed with you if you did it anyway.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 行(い)こうにも行(い)か<b>ない</b>。 → ✅ 行(い)こうにも行(い)け<b>ない</b>。
  The second verb must be the **potential** negative. With the plain negative the
  sentence just says you didn't go.

- ❌ 運動(うんどう)する<b>ようにも</b> → ✅ 運動(うんどう)し<b>ようにも</b>
  The volitional of する is しよう. The dictionary form cannot appear here.

- ❌ 連絡(れんらく)しようにも連絡(れんらく)<b>されない</b>。 → ✅ 連絡(れんらく)しようにも<b>できない</b>。
  The potential of する is できる. されない is the passive and says something quite
  different.

- ❌ 行(い)きたくないから、行(い)こうにも行(い)けない。 → ✅ 行(い)きたくないから、行(い)かない。
  The pattern requires that you *want* to and are stopped. An internal reluctance
  makes it self-contradictory.

## Practice

**1.** パスポートを忘(わす)れてきたので、飛行機(ひこうき)に乗(の)ろ____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

乗(の)ろ<b>うにも乗(の)れない</b>

The forgotten passport is the external obstacle, and the speaker plainly wants to
board. Note the shape: volitional 乗(の)ろう, then the potential negative
乗(の)れない.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 部長(ぶちょう)に頼(たの)まれた仕事(しごと)だから、断(ことわ)る（わけにはいかない・ようにもできない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

断(ことわ)る<b>わけにはいかない</b>。

Refusing is perfectly possible here; it would just be unacceptable. That is
<code>わけにはいかない</code>. The other pattern is for cases where refusing is not
available at all.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The road was flooded, so we couldn't get to the hospital however hard we tried."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

道(みち)が水(みず)に浸(つ)かってしまい、病院(びょういん)へ行(い)こ<b>うにも行(い)けなかった</b>。

The flood is the named obstacle, the will is obvious, and the past tense goes on the
final negative - 行(い)こう never changes.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 資料(しりょう)がないので、説明(せつめい)しようにも説明(せつめい)しられない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 資料(しりょう)がないので、説明(せつめい)し<b>ようにもできない</b>。
"Without the materials, I can't explain it even if I try."

❌しられない is not a form. する has the suppletive potential できる, so the second
half is できない - or, if you prefer to keep the verb visible,
説明(せつめい)のしようがない.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 忙(いそが)しくて旅行(りょこう)に行(い)け<b>ない</b>。
(b) 忙(いそが)しくて旅行(りょこう)に行(い)こ<b>うにも行(い)けない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply reports the fact. Work is busy and the trip is not happening; the speaker
may or may not mind.

(b) puts the wanting into the sentence. It says the speaker has been trying to make
the trip work and keeps running into the same wall, and it invites sympathy in a way
that (a) does not.

</details>

# とても～ない

**Meaning** · cannot possibly; there is no way - the thing is simply beyond what is achievable

This is not the とても of とてもおいしい. In front of a negative, とても reverts to its
older sense, "by any means at all", and the sentence says the thing is out of reach:
とても信(しん)じられない is "I couldn't believe that if I tried". The frame is
fixed - lose the negative and the word snaps back to meaning "very".

## Formation

The predicate must express **inability or insufficiency**. In practice that means a
potential negative, 無理(むり)だ, or one of a small set of verbs about being
enough or being in time.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (potential) | とても + ～(ら)れない | 信(しん)じる → とても信(しん)じられない |
| Verb (する) | とても + できない | 想像(そうぞう)する → とても想像(そうぞう)できない |
| Verbs of sufficiency | とても + plain negative | 足(た)りる → とても足(た)りない |
| な-adjective / noun | とても + 無理(むり)だ／不可能(ふかのう)だ | 無理(むり) → とても無理(むり)だ |
| Polite | とても + ～ません | 覚(おぼ)える → とても覚(おぼ)えられません |
| Emphatic (spoken) | とてもじゃないが／とてもじゃないけど + ～ない | 払(はら)う → とてもじゃないが払(はら)えない |
| Formal variant | 到底(とうてい) + ～ない | 及(およ)ぶ → 到底(とうてい)及(およ)ばない |
| Set phrase | とても + 手(て)が出(で)ない | 値段(ねだん) → とても手(て)が出(で)ない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the negative is not optional decoration, it is half the pattern.
とても on its own in front of an ordinary verb -
❌<code>とても行(い)かない</code> - does not mean "definitely won't go"; it means
nothing at all.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
そんな話(はなし)、<b>とても</b>信(しん)じ<b>られない</b>。
I can't possibly believe a story like that.
</pre>

<pre>
この値段(ねだん)では、<b>とても</b>手(て)が出(で)<b>ない</b>。
At this price it's completely out of my reach.
</pre>

<pre>
一週間(いっしゅうかん)で全部(ぜんぶ)覚(おぼ)えろと言(い)われても、<b>とても</b>無理(むり)です。
Being told to memorise it all in a week - that's just not possible.
</pre>

<pre>
今(いま)から走(はし)っても、開演(かいえん)には<b>とても</b>間(ま)に合(あ)わ<b>ない</b>。
Even running from here, there's no way we make the curtain.
</pre>

<pre>
あの穏(おだ)やかな人(ひと)が怒鳴(どな)ったなんて、<b>とても</b>想像(そうぞう)でき<b>ない</b>。
I simply cannot picture that gentle man shouting at anyone.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>とてもじゃないが</b>、この量(りょう)を一人(ひとり)では片付(かたづ)け<b>られない</b>。
There is honestly no way one person gets through this much.
</pre>

<pre>
半年(はんとし)分(ぶん)の貯金(ちょきん)では、留学(りゅうがく)の費用(ひよう)には<b>とても</b>足(た)り<b>ません</b>。
Six months of savings comes nowhere near covering the cost of studying abroad.
</pre>

<pre>
現場(げんば)の様子(ようす)は、写真(しゃしん)や言葉(ことば)では<b>とても</b>伝(つた)え切(き)れ<b>ない</b>。
Photographs and words cannot begin to convey what the site was like.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and very common. It fits a friend's kitchen and a company report
alike, and the only thing that shifts with formality is which member of the family
you pick: <code>とてもじゃないが</code> or <code>とてもじゃないけど</code> in
exasperated speech, plain <code>とても</code> anywhere, and
<code>到底(とうてい)</code> in writing and formal argument.

<code>とてもじゃないが</code> deserves attention because textbooks skip it and
speakers use it constantly. It is the version you reach for when you are throwing up
your hands: 「とてもじゃないけど終(お)わらないよ」. Grammatically it behaves
exactly like <code>とても</code>; the extra じゃないが is pure emphasis, and the が or
けど is part of the phrase rather than a real "but".

The historical note is worth knowing because it explains the oddity. とても began as
an adverb used only with negatives - roughly "by any means" - and the familiar "very"
sense is a comparatively recent development. Modern Japanese kept both, and the
predicate does all the disambiguating. That is also why the negative here has to be
about **capacity**. とても信(しん)じられない and とても無理(むり)だ are
idiomatic; ❌とても行(い)かない is not, because not going is a choice rather than
an impossibility.

One trap in the other direction: with an い-adjective, とても…ない does not give
"not very". ❌とても難(むずか)しくない is not how to say "it isn't very hard" -
that is <code>あまり難(むずか)しくない</code>.

## Contrast

### とても～ない vs とても (affirmative)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one word, two jobs, and the **predicate decides**. Affirmative =
"very". Negative of capacity = "not possibly".

</div>

<pre>
この料理(りょうり)は<b>とても</b>おいしいですね。
This dish is really good.
</pre>

<pre>
量(りょう)が多(おお)すぎて、<b>とても</b>食(た)べ切(き)れ<b>ません</b>。
There's far too much - I couldn't possibly finish it.
</pre>

The same adverb is doing opposite work in the two sentences, and nothing but the
ending signals which. This is why the "not very" error is so persistent: English
speakers assume they can negate the "very" reading, and Japanese does not let them.
For "not very", use <code>あまり</code>.

### とても～ない vs 全然(ぜんぜん)～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>全然(ぜんぜん)</code> denies the **fact**: it didn't
happen, not at all. <code>とても</code> denies the **possibility**: it couldn't.

</div>

<pre>
彼(かれ)の説明(せつめい)は<b>全然(ぜんぜん)</b>分(わ)から<b>なかった</b>。
I didn't understand his explanation at all.
</pre>

<pre>
専門(せんもん)用語(ようご)だらけで、<b>とても</b>理解(りかい)でき<b>なかった</b>。
It was so full of jargon that there was no understanding it.
</pre>

The first is a report on the outcome; the second says the task was beyond the
speaker. That is also why <code>全然(ぜんぜん)</code> is happy with a plain
negative while <code>とても</code> wants the potential - "at all" measures the
result, "possibly" measures the ability.

### とても～ない vs 到底(とうてい)～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same meaning at three heights.
<code>とてもじゃないが</code> is **casual and exasperated**, <code>とても</code> is
**neutral**, <code>到底(とうてい)</code> is **formal and written**.

</div>

<pre>
<b>とてもじゃないけど</b>、この家賃(やちん)は払(はら)え<b>ない</b>よ。
Honestly, there's no way I can pay this rent.
</pre>

<pre>
現状(げんじょう)の設備(せつび)では、需要(じゅよう)に<b>到底(とうてい)</b>応(こた)え<b>られない</b>。
With the present facilities, demand cannot conceivably be met.
</pre>

Both sentences would be correct with plain <code>とても</code>. What changes is who
is speaking and where: the first belongs to a conversation about moving flat, the
second to a report or a board meeting. <code>到底(とうてい)</code> in casual
speech sounds stiff, and <code>とてもじゃないけど</code> in a written report sounds
like the writer lost their temper.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この本(ほん)は<b>とても</b>読(よ)ま<b>ない</b>。 → ✅ この本(ほん)は<b>とても</b>読(よ)め<b>ない</b>。
  The predicate has to express inability, which normally means the potential form.
  読(よ)まない is a choice, not an impossibility.

- ❌ この問題(もんだい)は<b>とても</b>難(むずか)しく<b>ない</b>。（meaning "not very hard"） → ✅ この問題(もんだい)は<b>あまり</b>難(むずか)しく<b>ない</b>。
  とても cannot be negated to give "not very". Use <code>あまり</code>.

- ❌ <b>とてもじゃない</b>、終(お)わらない。 → ✅ <b>とてもじゃないが</b>、終(お)わらない。
  The が or けど is part of the fixed phrase. Dropping it leaves the sentence
  sounding cut off.

- ❌ そんなことは<b>とても</b>あり得(え)る。 → ✅ そんなことは<b>とても</b>あり得(え)<b>ない</b>。
  Without the negative, とても is simply "very", and the sentence ends up asserting
  the opposite of what was intended.

## Practice

**1.** 新車(しんしゃ)が欲(ほ)しいけれど、今(いま)の給料(きゅうりょう)では____買(か)えない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>とても</b>買(か)えない（or とてもじゃないが買(か)えない）

The salary puts the car out of reach, which is inability rather than choice - exactly
what this frame is for. The potential negative 買(か)えない is what makes it work.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 昨日(きのう)の映画(えいが)は（とても・あまり）面白(おもしろ)くなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

昨日(きのう)の映画(えいが)は<b>あまり</b>面白(おもしろ)くなかった。

"Not very interesting" is <code>あまり～ない</code>. とても in front of a negated
い-adjective does not produce that meaning, and native speakers do not use it that
way.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "A beginner could not possibly climb that mountain in a day."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

初心者(しょしんしゃ)にあの山(やま)を一日(いちにち)で登(のぼ)るのは<b>とても無理(むり)だ</b>。

無理(むり)だ is the most natural predicate when the English is "could not
possibly" about a whole undertaking. とても登(のぼ)れない would also be correct.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 忙(いそが)しすぎて、とても休(やす)まない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 忙(いそが)しすぎて、とても<b>休(やす)めない</b>。
"I'm far too busy to take any time off."

休(やす)まない says the speaker chooses not to rest. The pattern needs the
potential negative 休(やす)めない, which says resting is not available.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) その話(はなし)は<b>全然(ぜんぜん)</b>信(しん)じ<b>ていない</b>。
(b) その話(はなし)は<b>とても</b>信(しん)じ<b>られない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) states the speaker's position: they give the story no credence whatever. It is a
report about their own attitude, and it is flat and slightly dismissive.

(b) says the story is beyond belief - the speaker might even want to believe it and
find that they cannot. It points at the story rather than at the speaker, and it
often carries surprise or dismay rather than dismissal.

</details>

# どうにも～ない

**Meaning** · there is nothing to be done about it - no course of action would change this, so the speaker gives up

どう + に + も is "in any way at all", and the negative removes every one of those
ways. What the pattern expresses is **helplessness**: the speaker has looked at the
situation, or at their own feelings, and found no lever to pull. It is close to a
sigh in grammatical form.

## Formation

どうにも is an adverb and needs a negative predicate. The negative is very often a
potential.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (potential) | どうにも + ～(ら)れない | 眠(ねむ)る → どうにも眠(ねむ)れない |
| Verb (する) | どうにも + できない | 我慢(がまん)する → どうにも我慢(がまん)できない |
| Verb (plain negative) | どうにも + ～ない | 直(なお)る → どうにも直(なお)らない |
| Polite | どうにも + ～ません | 納得(なっとく)する → どうにも納得(なっとく)できません |
| Frozen: nothing can be done | ～は + どうにもならない | 過去(かこ) → 過去(かこ)はどうにもならない |
| Frozen: I can do nothing about it | ～を + どうにもできない | 自分(じぶん)の性格(せいかく) → 自分(じぶん)の性格(せいかく)をどうにもできない |
| Emphatic (casual) | どうにもこうにも + ～ない | 進(すす)む → どうにもこうにも進(すす)まない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the two frozen phrases divide by particle.
<code>どうにもならない</code> is intransitive and takes が or は: the situation is
beyond help, with nobody named. <code>どうにもできない</code> takes を and names
someone who cannot fix it. ❌<code>彼(かれ)をどうにもならない</code> mixes the two.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
暑(あつ)くて、夜(よる)は<b>どうにも</b>眠(ねむ)れ<b>ない</b>。
It's so hot that I simply can't sleep at night.
</pre>

<pre>
この癖(くせ)は<b>どうにも</b>直(なお)ら<b>ない</b>ね。
This habit of mine just won't go away.
</pre>

<pre>
隣(となり)の工事(こうじ)の音(おと)が、<b>どうにも</b>我慢(がまん)でき<b>ない</b>。
The noise from the building work next door is more than I can stand.
</pre>

<pre>
理屈(りくつ)は分(わ)かるのだが、<b>どうにも</b>納得(なっとく)でき<b>ない</b>。
I follow the logic, and still I can't bring myself to accept it.
</pre>

<pre>
この翻訳(ほんやく)は意味(いみ)こそ通(とお)るが、<b>どうにも</b>しっくり来(こ)<b>ない</b>。
The translation makes sense, but something about it just doesn't sit right.
</pre>

<pre>
部品(ぶひん)が一(ひと)つ足(た)りず、<b>どうにもこうにも</b>組(く)み立(た)てられ<b>なかった</b>。
One part was missing and there was absolutely no way to put it together.
</pre>

<pre>
一度(いちど)失(うしな)った信用(しんよう)は、今(いま)となっては<b>どうにも</b>取(と)り返(かえ)しがつか<b>ない</b>。
Trust once lost cannot be recovered at this stage, whatever anyone does.
</pre>

<pre>
上司(じょうし)と部下(ぶか)の間(あいだ)に入(はい)ってはみたが、私(わたし)の力(ちから)では<b>どうにも</b>でき<b>なかった</b>。
I did try to get between my boss and his staff, but there was nothing I could do.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Everyday Japanese, common in speech and in writing that has a voice.
What it adds is **resignation**, and often a note of complaint. The speaker is not
neutrally reporting an impossibility; they have already run through the options and
come up empty, and the sentence carries that weariness.

A large share of real uses are about the speaker's own feelings refusing to
cooperate - どうにも気(き)が進(すす)まない, どうにも好(す)きになれない,
どうにも落(お)ち着(つ)かない, どうにも気(き)になる. The situation there is
internal, which is exactly the point: even your own mind is not something you can
simply decide about.

<code>どうにもこうにも</code> is the emphatic doubling and it is **casual**. It works
in conversation and in a chatty blog and looks out of place in a report, where the
neutral どうにも or a rephrasing is safer.

<code>どうにもならない</code> deserves separate mention because it has hardened into
a fixed phrase used on its own: 「もうどうにもならない」, "there's nothing for it
now". You will meet it after a て-form as well - 悔(く)やんでも
どうにもならない - where it becomes a way of telling someone to stop dwelling on
something.

## Contrast

### どうにも～ない vs どうしても～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>どうしても</code> is about **effort**: I tried hard and
still could not. <code>どうにも</code> is about **options**: there is no move
available at all.

</div>

<pre>
名前(なまえ)が<b>どうしても</b>思(おも)い出(だ)せ<b>ない</b>。
I just cannot remember his name, however hard I try.
</pre>

<pre>
今(いま)の人手(ひとで)では、<b>どうにも</b>回(まわ)ら<b>ない</b>。
With the staff we have, it simply doesn't work, and nothing will make it.
</pre>

The first has a person straining at a task; the second has a situation with no way
out. There is also a use <code>どうにも</code> cannot copy:
<code>どうしても</code> takes affirmative predicates for strong determination -
どうしても行(い)きたい, どうしても会(あ)わせたい - and
❌どうにも行(い)きたい is not Japanese.

### どうにも～ない vs なんとも～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>どうにも</code> denies that anything can be **done**.
<code>なんとも</code> denies that anything can be **said or felt** about it.

</div>

<pre>
結果(けっか)が出(で)るまでは<b>なんとも</b>言(い)え<b>ません</b>。
Until the results are in I can't say anything either way.
</pre>

<pre>
台風(たいふう)が来(く)るなら、予定(よてい)は<b>どうにも</b>変(か)えられ<b>ない</b>。
If a typhoon is coming, the schedule is beyond changing.
</pre>

<code>なんとも</code> lives with verbs of speaking, thinking and feeling -
なんとも言(い)えない, なんとも思(おも)わない - and with the frozen
なんともない, "no problem, no harm done". It is about judgement.
<code>どうにも</code> is about action, which is why it pairs so naturally with
できない and ならない.

### どうにも～ない vs とても～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>とても</code> says the thing is **beyond reach**, usually
in scale or degree. <code>どうにも</code> says **no approach works**, whatever the
scale.

</div>

<pre>
この値段(ねだん)は、学生(がくせい)には<b>とても</b>払(はら)え<b>ない</b>。
A student couldn't possibly pay that price.
</pre>

<pre>
値上(ねあ)げの話(はなし)は決(き)まってしまい、<b>どうにも</b>止(と)められ<b>なかった</b>。
The price rise had already been decided and there was no stopping it.
</pre>

<code>とても</code> measures a gap - the price against the wallet.
<code>どうにも</code> reports that every avenue has been closed. Where a quantity or
a degree is in view, とても is the better fit; where the speaker has been casting
around for a way to act, どうにも is.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 会議(かいぎ)が<b>どうにも</b>長引(ながび)いた。 → ✅ 会議(かいぎ)が<b>どうにも</b>終(お)わら<b>なかった</b>。
  どうにも requires a negative. In an affirmative sentence it has no meaning to
  contribute.

- ❌ <b>どうにも</b>優勝(ゆうしょう)したい。 → ✅ <b>どうしても</b>優勝(ゆうしょう)したい。
  Strong positive determination is <code>どうしても</code>. どうにも cannot express
  wanting.

- ❌ 彼(かれ)の性格(せいかく)を<b>どうにもならない</b>。 → ✅ 彼(かれ)の性格(せいかく)を<b>どうにもできない</b>。
  <code>ならない</code> is intransitive and takes は or が; with an を object the verb
  must be <code>できない</code>.

- ❌ （in a written report）状況(じょうきょう)は<b>どうにもこうにも</b>改善(かいぜん)しません。 → ✅ 状況(じょうきょう)は改善(かいぜん)の見通(みとお)しが立(た)ちません。
  <code>どうにもこうにも</code> is conversational. In a formal document it reads as
  though the writer had given up mid-sentence.

## Practice

**1.** 隣(となり)の部屋(へや)の話(はな)し声(ごえ)が気(き)になって、____集中(しゅうちゅう)できない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>どうにも</b>集中(しゅうちゅう)できない

The speaker has presumably tried moving, closing the door and putting up with it, and
nothing helps - the helplessness this adverb carries. Note the obligatory negative
predicate.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 来週(らいしゅう)の試合(しあい)には（どうにも・どうしても）勝(か)ちたい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

来週(らいしゅう)の試合(しあい)には<b>どうしても</b>勝(か)ちたい。

This is determination, and the predicate is affirmative. どうにも works only with a
negative, so it cannot appear here at all.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I understand why she's angry, but there is nothing I can do about it now."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

彼女(かのじょ)が怒(おこ)る理由(りゆう)は分(わ)かるが、今(いま)となっては<b>どうにもならない</b>。

"There is nothing I can do about it now" is the frozen
<code>どうにもならない</code>. If you want to name yourself as the one who is
powerless, use 私(わたし)にはどうにもできない.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この問題(もんだい)をどうにもならないので、専門家(せんもんか)に相談(そうだん)した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この問題(もんだい)を<b>どうにもできない</b>ので、専門家(せんもんか)に相談(そうだん)した。
（or この問題(もんだい)<b>は</b>どうにもならないので…）

<code>ならない</code> cannot take an を object. Either switch the verb to
<code>できない</code> or switch the particle to は and let the problem be the subject.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この靴(くつ)は<b>とても</b>履(は)け<b>ない</b>。
(b) この靴(くつ)は<b>どうにも</b>履(は)け<b>ない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) puts the shoes out of range. They are far too small, or so impractical that
wearing them is not a serious proposition - the sentence measures a gap.

(b) has the speaker fighting with them. They have loosened the laces, tried a thinner
sock, sat down and tried again, and the shoes still will not go on. The frustration
of having exhausted the options is what どうにも contributes.

</details>

# ～やしない

**Meaning** · doesn't ... at all; certainly won't - a flat, blunt negative with feeling behind it

<code>やしない</code> is <code>はしない</code> with the は worn down to や, the same
softening that turns やはり into やっぱり. The meaning is an ordinary negative plus
emphasis and irritation: 分(わ)かりやしない is "he hasn't the first idea". It is a
**voice** rather than a register - blunt, informal, and noticeably dated. Recognise
it in fiction and say the plain negative yourself.

## Formation

The base is the **ます-stem**, as with <code>はしない</code>.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + やしない | 分(わ)かる → 分(わ)かりやしない |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + やしない | 食(た)べる → 食(た)べやしない |
| Verb (する) | し + やしない | 気(き)にする → 気(き)にしやしない |
| Verb (来(く)る) | **き** + やしない | 来(く)る → 来(き)やしない |
| ある | ありやしない → **ありゃしない** | ある → ありゃしない |
| Further contraction | ～りやしない → ～りゃしない | 知(し)る → 知(し)りゃしない |
| い-adjective | ～く + もありゃしない | 痛(いた)い → 痛(いた)くもありゃしない |
| Neutral source form | ます-stem + はしない | 分(わ)かる → 分(わ)かりはしない |
| Modern equivalent | plain ～ない／～なんか～ない | 分(わ)かる → 分(わ)からない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - there is **no polite form**. ❌<code>やしません</code> does not
exist, and neither does a past ❌<code>やしなかった</code> in normal use. The
expression only appears in plain, casual speech, and that is a fact about its
character rather than a gap in the paradigm.

</div>

## Examples

These are the kinds of line the form really occurs in - dialogue in a novel, a manga
panel, a dubbed film, a comic monologue.

<pre>
そんなこと、おれは知(し)り<b>やしない</b>よ。
I don't know a thing about it.
</pre>

<pre>
あいつの話(はなし)なんか、誰(だれ)も信(しん)じ<b>やしない</b>。
Nobody's going to believe a word he says.
</pre>

<pre>
待(ま)っていたって、どうせ誰(だれ)も来(き)<b>やしない</b>。
You can wait all you like - nobody's coming.
</pre>

<pre>
金(かね)なんか、これっぽっちも<b>ありゃしない</b>。
Money? I haven't got two coins to rub together.
</pre>

<pre>
こんな給料(きゅうりょう)じゃ、やって<b>られやしない</b>。
On wages like these, you can't be expected to carry on.
</pre>

<pre>
今更(いまさら)謝(あやま)ったって、あの人(ひと)は許(ゆる)し<b>やしない</b>さ。
Apologise now if you like - she won't forgive you.
</pre>

<pre>
「大丈夫(だいじょうぶ)か」「平気(へいき)だよ。こんな傷(きず)、痛(いた)く<b>もありゃしない</b>」
"Are you all right?" "Fine. A scratch like this doesn't hurt a bit."
</pre>

<pre>
昔(むかし)の苦労(くろう)を話(はな)したところで、今(いま)の若(わか)い者(もの)には分(わ)か<b>りゃしない</b>だろう。
Tell young people today about the old hardships and they won't understand a word of it.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Read it; do not say it.** <code>やしない</code> is the emphatic negative of blunt,
informal speech, and in the modern language it survives mainly on the page and the
screen: manga, novels, 落語(らくご), period drama, and the dubbing of foreign
films where a translator wants a character to sound gruff. Its associations run
towards older speakers, working-class 下町(したまち) dialogue, and male speech -
tendencies, not rules, and brash characters of any age or gender get given it. Used
by a learner in a real conversation it does not read as fluent; it reads as an
impression of a character.

The modern equivalents are ordinary. For a plain denial, use
<code>～ない</code>. For emphasis, <code>～なんか～ない</code>
（知(し)らないよ, そんなの信(しん)じないよ, 誰(だれ)も来(こ)ないよ）or
<code>全然(ぜんぜん)～ない</code>. All of these carry the same content with none of
the costume.

Two corners of the pattern are more alive than the rest.
<code>ありゃしない</code> is common in the frame
～ったらありゃしない - 「うるさいったらありゃしない」, "the noise, honestly" - which
adults do still use half-jokingly. And やってられやしない, "I can't be expected to
put up with this", turns up in genuinely irritated speech. Even these sit at the
theatrical end of casual, and neither is something to reach for at work.

The contraction ladder is worth being able to hear.
<code>はしない</code> is the neutral ancestor and is still perfectly usable;
<code>やしない</code> is the casual descendant; and where the stem ends in り, it
contracts once more to <code>りゃしない</code>
（知(し)りゃしない, 分(わ)かりゃしない, ありゃしない）. Each step down is a step
rougher.

## Contrast

### ～やしない vs plain ～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same denial. <code>ない</code> is **what you say**;
<code>やしない</code> adds emphasis and a strongly marked, dated speaking voice.

</div>

<pre>
そんな話(はなし)は信(しん)じ<b>ない</b>よ。
I don't believe that.
</pre>

<pre>
そんな話(はなし)、信(しん)じ<b>やしない</b>よ。
I don't believe a word of that.
</pre>

The content is identical. What changes is the speaker: the first could be anyone, the
second is a character. If you want the emphasis without the costume,
信(しん)じるもんか or 全然(ぜんぜん)信(しん)じない will do the job in modern
speech.

### ～やしない vs ～はしない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>はしない</code> is the **neutral** emphatic negative and is
usable in real life, including in writing. <code>やしない</code> is its **casual and
rough** descendant.

</div>

<pre>
心配(しんぱい)する必要(ひつよう)はない。この数字(すうじ)は変(か)わり<b>はしない</b>。
There is no need to worry. These figures are not going to change.
</pre>

<pre>
どうせ何(なに)も変(か)わり<b>やしない</b>さ。
Nothing's going to change anyway.
</pre>

<code>はしない</code> is contrastive - it says "that particular thing, no" and often
implies something else is true. It is at home in careful prose and measured speech.
<code>やしない</code> keeps the emphasis but loses the composure, which is why the
second sentence needs the さ at the end to sound natural and the first does not.

### ～やしない vs ～ものか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>やしない</code> denies a **fact**: it isn't so.
<code>ものか</code> refuses a **course of action**: I'm certainly not going to.

</div>

<pre>
あんな店(みせ)、二度(にど)と行(い)く<b>もんか</b>。
I am never setting foot in that place again.
</pre>

<pre>
一人(ひとり)で運(はこ)べ<b>やしない</b>よ、こんな重(おも)いもの。
There's no carrying this on your own - it's far too heavy.
</pre>

Both are emphatic, both are casual, and both are marked. The difference is what they
deny. <code>ものか</code> is a declaration about the speaker's own will and is
therefore first-person by nature; <code>やしない</code> makes a claim about how
things are and can be said of anybody.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 分(わ)かる<b>やしない</b> → ✅ 分(わ)かり<b>やしない</b>
  The base is the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.

- ❌ 来(こ)<b>やしない</b> → ✅ 来(き)<b>やしない</b>
  来(く)る is irregular. Its ます-stem is き, as in 来(き)ます; こ belongs to
  来(こ)ない.

- ❌ そのようなことは存(ぞん)じ<b>やしません</b>。 → ✅ そのようなことは存(ぞん)じ<b>ません</b>。
  There is no polite form. Attaching ます to a deliberately rough colloquialism
  produces something no speaker would say.

- ❌ （in a report）今回(こんかい)の対策(たいさく)では効果(こうか)が出(で)やしない。 → ✅ 今回(こんかい)の対策(たいさく)では効果(こうか)は期待(きたい)できない。
  Fiction dialogue in a document. Written argument uses ordinary negatives, or
  <code>はしない</code> at most.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to meet this line, and what sort of speaker says it?
「あんな奴(やつ)の言(い)うことなんか、信(しん)じ<b>やしない</b>」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In fiction - a manga panel, a novel, a film subtitle - and from a blunt, informal
speaker, often an older or deliberately rough-talking character.

The markers are the pejorative 奴(やつ), the dismissive なんか, and
<code>やしない</code> itself. In real conversation a modern speaker would just say
信(しん)じないよ.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: 誰(だれ)も手伝(てつだ)ってくれやしない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

誰(だれ)も手伝(てつだ)って<b>くれない</b>。（or 誰(だれ)も手伝(てつだ)ってなんかくれない）

The plain negative carries the whole meaning. If the complaint needs emphasis,
なんか or 全然(ぜんぜん) supplies it without the theatrical voice.

</details>

**3.** Fill the blank with the contracted form: そんな昔(むかし)のこと、もう覚(おぼ)えて____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

覚(おぼ)えて<b>やしない</b>（or 覚(おぼ)えちゃいない）

<code>やしない</code> attaches happily to a て-form plus い-verb here, since
覚(おぼ)えている gives the stem 覚(おぼ)えてい-. Note that the very similar
ちゃいない comes from ては→ちゃ and is a separate contraction.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: あの人(ひと)は絶対(ぜったい)に来(こ)やしませんよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ あの人(ひと)は絶対(ぜったい)に<b>来(き)ませんよ</b>。（casual: 来(き)やしないよ）

Two faults. The stem of 来(く)る here is き, not こ; and
<code>やしない</code> has no polite form, so ます cannot be bolted onto it. If the
sentence needs to be polite, the whole expression has to go.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in register:
(a) 彼(かれ)の話(はなし)を信(しん)じ<b>てはいない</b>。
(b) 彼(かれ)の話(はなし)なんか信(しん)じ<b>やしない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is measured and could appear in a report or in careful speech. The は marks a
contrast - the speaker doubts his account specifically, and may accept other things
he has said.

(b) is dialogue. The なんか is contemptuous, <code>やしない</code> adds heat, and the
whole line sounds like a character in a story. A learner who produces it in a
meeting will get a startled look rather than a fluent one.

</details>

# ～でも何でもない

**Meaning** · not X at all; nothing of the sort - a flat rebuttal of a label somebody has just applied

The frame is the ordinary "neither A nor B" negative with 何(なん) standing in for
B: "not X, and not anything else either". That is why it lands as a **rebuttal**
rather than a description. Somebody has called the thing a joke, an accident, an
apology - and this construction says no, it is not, and here is what it really is.

## Formation

Nouns and な-adjectives take でも何(なん)でもない. い-adjectives take a different
frame, ～くも何(なん)ともない, and the pairing of く with とも is fixed.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + でも何(なん)でもない | 冗談(じょうだん) → 冗談(じょうだん)でも何(なん)でもない |
| な-adjective | Stem + でも何(なん)でもない | 失礼(しつれい) → 失礼(しつれい)でも何(なん)でもない |
| い-adjective | ～く + も何(なん)ともない | 面白(おもしろ)い → 面白(おもしろ)くも何(なん)ともない |
| Verb (needs a nominaliser) | ～わけ／～つもり + でも何(なん)でもない | 断(ことわ)る → 断(ことわ)るわけでも何(なん)でもない |
| Polite | ～でも何(なん)でもありません | 秘密(ひみつ) → 秘密(ひみつ)でも何(なん)でもありません |
| Past | ～でも何(なん)でもなかった | 偶然(ぐうぜん) → 偶然(ぐうぜん)でも何(なん)でもなかった |
| Mid-sentence | ～でも何(なん)でもなく、… | 結果(けっか) → 結果(けっか)でも何(なん)でもなく |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the reading is **なん**, not なに. 何(なん) takes なん before で, だ
and の（何(なん)でも, 何(なん)だ, 何(なん)の）and なに before が and か
（何(なに)が, 何(なに)か）. Said aloud the tail of the phrase runs
でもなんでもない, and the い-adjective version runs くもなんともない.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
あれは冗談(じょうだん)<b>でも何(なん)でもない</b>。本気(ほんき)で言(い)ったんだ。
That was not a joke. I meant it.
</pre>

<pre>
彼(かれ)の話(はなし)は面白(おもしろ)<b>くも何(なん)ともなかった</b>。
His story wasn't the least bit interesting.
</pre>

<pre>
「秘密(ひみつ)なの?」「秘密(ひみつ)<b>でも何(なん)でもない</b>よ。聞(き)かれなかっただけ」
"Is it a secret?" "Not in the slightest. Nobody asked me, that's all."
</pre>

<pre>
偶然(ぐうぜん)<b>でも何(なん)でもない</b>。最初(さいしょ)から仕組(しく)まれていたのだ。
This was no coincidence. It was set up from the very beginning.
</pre>

<pre>
彼女(かのじょ)は専門家(せんもんか)<b>でも何(なん)でもない</b>のに、平気(へいき)で人(ひと)に指示(しじ)を出(だ)す。
She's no expert of any kind, and yet she happily tells everyone what to do.
</pre>

<pre>
あんなものは芸術(げいじゅつ)<b>でも何(なん)でもない</b>と、彼(かれ)は吐(は)き捨(す)てるように言(い)った。
That is not art or anything like it, he said, almost spitting the words out.
</pre>

<pre>
今回(こんかい)の決定(けってい)は現場(げんば)の声(こえ)を聞(き)いた結果(けっか)<b>でも何(なん)でもなく</b>、上(うえ)が勝手(かって)に決(き)めたことだ。
This decision was in no sense the result of listening to the people on the ground; it was handed down from above.
</pre>

<pre>
事故(じこ)のあとに会社(かいしゃ)が出(だ)した文書(ぶんしょ)は、謝罪(しゃざい)<b>でも何(なん)でもありませんでした</b>。
What the company issued after the accident was not an apology in any shape or form.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The **form** is register-neutral - the polite
でも何(なん)でもありません is entirely at home in a formal complaint or a written
statement, and the plain form in conversation. What is marked is the **stance**. This
is a contradiction, and it presupposes that a label is on the table. Somebody called
it a joke, or the newspaper called it a coincidence, or the letter was described as
an apology; the sentence rejects that description outright.

That presupposition is a real constraint. Out of the blue, with nothing to push back
against, the expression falls flat - ❌今日(きょう)は雨(あめ)でも
何(なん)でもない said to someone looking out of a window is simply odd, because no
one suggested it was raining. A useful test: if you could preface the English with
"That is not…" or "It was no…", the pattern fits.

Because it contradicts, it usually comes with a **correction attached**. The typical
shape is two clauses - でも何(なん)でもない, then ただの… or
実(じつ)は… saying what the thing actually is. Left without the second half it
can sound curt, which is sometimes exactly the effect wanted.

The い-adjective member of the family, ～くも何(なん)ともない, is worth learning
separately. 痛(いた)くも何(なん)ともない, 悔(くや)しくも
何(なん)ともない, 怖(こわ)くも何(なん)ともない are all common, and all deny a
feeling somebody has attributed to the speaker.

## Contrast

### ～でも何(なん)でもない vs ～ではない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ではない</code> is a **statement**: X is not the case.
<code>でも何(なん)でもない</code> is a **rebuttal**: X is not the case, and don't
suggest otherwise.

</div>

<pre>
これは私(わたし)の本(ほん)<b>ではありません</b>。
This is not my book.
</pre>

<pre>
これは私(わたし)の責任(せきにん)<b>でも何(なん)でもありません</b>。
This is not my responsibility, and it's no use pretending it is.
</pre>

The first identifies whose book it is not, and carries no heat. The second answers an
accusation. Note that <code>ではない</code> will go anywhere, including in neutral
description, while the longer form needs something to be arguing with.

### ～でも何(なん)でもない vs 何(なん)でもない (on its own)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with a noun in front it means "not X at all". **Standing alone**,
何(なん)でもない means "it's nothing, never mind" - about a situation, not a label.

</div>

<pre>
「どうしたの?」「ううん、<b>何(なん)でもない</b>」
"What's wrong?" "Nothing. Never mind."
</pre>

<pre>
怪我(けが)といっても、大(たい)したこと<b>でも何(なん)でもない</b>。
It's called an injury, but it's nothing serious at all.
</pre>

The two look almost identical on the page and do quite different work. The bare
何(なん)でもない deflects concern; the full pattern rejects a description. Listen
for whether a noun precedes it.

### ～でも何(なん)でもない vs ～なんかじゃない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same rebuttal at two levels.
<code>なんかじゃない</code> is **casual speech**;
<code>でも何(なん)でもない</code> is **stronger and register-flexible**.

</div>

<pre>
友達(ともだち)<b>なんかじゃない</b>よ、ただのクラスメイト。
He's not a friend or anything - just someone in my class.
</pre>

<pre>
これは値引(ねび)き<b>でも何(なん)でもなく</b>、元(もと)の値段(ねだん)に戻(もど)しただけです。
This is not a discount of any kind; the price has simply gone back to what it was.
</pre>

<code>なんか</code> carries a dismissive edge and belongs to conversation; putting it
in a written statement would sound flippant. The longer form keeps the force and
loses the flippancy, which is why it is the one that appears in complaints,
editorials and formal denials.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 面白(おもしろ)い<b>でも何(なん)でもない</b> → ✅ 面白(おもしろ)<b>くも何(なん)ともない</b>
  い-adjectives do not take で. They use the く form plus も何(なん)ともない.

- ❌ 痛(いた)くも何(なん)<b>でも</b>ない → ✅ 痛(いた)くも何(なん)<b>とも</b>ない
  Within the い-adjective frame the pair is く … とも. Mixing the two frames is the
  single most common slip with this pattern.

- ❌ 彼(かれ)が悪(わる)い人(ひと)だと言(い)う<b>でも何(なん)でもない</b>。 → ✅ 彼(かれ)が悪(わる)いと言(い)っている<b>わけでも何(なん)でもない</b>。
  A verb cannot attach directly. Nominalise it first with
  <code>わけ</code>, <code>つもり</code> or <code>こと</code>.

- ❌ （unprompted, looking at the sky）今日(きょう)は雨(あめ)<b>でも何(なん)でもない</b>。 → ✅ 今日(きょう)は雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っていない。
  There is nothing to rebut. The pattern needs a claim in the air; without one it
  sounds as if the speaker is arguing with themselves.

## Practice

**1.** 「あの人(ひと)、君(きみ)の彼氏(かれし)?」「____よ。兄(あに)の友達(ともだち)」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

彼氏(かれし)<b>でも何(なん)でもない</b>よ

A label has just been proposed and the speaker is throwing it out - the core use.
Note the correction that follows, which is how the pattern usually arrives.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 注射(ちゅうしゃ)は（痛(いた)いでも何(なん)でもなかった・痛(いた)くも何(なん)ともなかった）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

注射(ちゅうしゃ)は<b>痛(いた)くも何(なん)ともなかった</b>。

痛(いた)い is an い-adjective, so it takes the く frame paired with とも. The
でも frame belongs to nouns and な-adjectives only.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "What he wrote was not a report at all - it was an advertisement."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

彼(かれ)が書(か)いたものは報告(ほうこく)<b>でも何(なん)でもなく</b>、ただの宣伝(せんでん)だった。

The mid-sentence form なく joins the rebuttal to the correction, which is the shape
this pattern most often takes. ただの reinforces the dismissal.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この結果(けっか)は運(うん)がよかったでも何(なん)でもない。準備(じゅんび)の差(さ)だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この結果(けっか)は運(うん)<b>でも何(なん)でもない</b>。準備(じゅんび)の差(さ)だ。
（or 運(うん)がよかった<b>わけでも何(なん)でもない</b>）

A full clause cannot sit in front of でも. Either reduce it to the bare noun
運(うん), or nominalise with <code>わけ</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) それは問題(もんだい)<b>ではありません</b>。
(b) それは問題(もんだい)<b>でも何(なん)でもありません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a calm assessment. The speaker has looked at the matter and judges that it
does not constitute a problem; a manager could say it reassuringly.

(b) pushes back. It assumes someone has been calling the thing a problem, and it
denies the label with some force - roughly "that is not a problem, nor anything
resembling one". The listener will hear disagreement, not reassurance.

</details>

# ～そうにもない

**Meaning** · shows no sign of happening; doesn't look at all likely - judged from what the speaker can see in front of them

This is the negative of the "looks like it" そうだ, the one that attaches to the
ます-stem. 止(や)みそうにもない is a verdict on the sky: not "it will not stop" but
"there is nothing in the look of it that suggests stopping". The も is emphasis - the
plain form is そうにない, and adding も takes it from *unlikely* to *no sign
whatsoever*.

## Formation

The base is the **ます-stem**. That is what makes it the appearance そうだ rather
than the hearsay one.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + そうにもない | 止(や)む → 止(や)みそうにもない |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + そうにもない | 晴(は)れる → 晴(は)れそうにもない |
| Verb (する) | し + そうにもない | 回復(かいふく)する → 回復(かいふく)しそうにもない |
| Verb (来(く)る) | **き** + そうにもない | 来(く)る → 来(き)そうにもない |
| Potential verb | potential stem + そうにもない | 勝(か)てる → 勝(か)てそうにもない |
| Weaker variants | ～そうにない／～そうもない | 終(お)わる → 終(お)わりそうにない |
| Polite | ～そうにもありません | 間(ま)に合(あ)う → 間(ま)に合(あ)いそうにもありません |
| Before a noun | ～そうにない + Noun | 終(お)わる → 終(お)わりそうにない作業(さぎょう) |
| い-adjective (different form) | ～く + なさそうだ | おいしい → おいしくなさそうだ |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - three forms, one meaning, rising emphasis:
<code>そうにない</code> → <code>そうもない</code> → <code>そうにもない</code>. All
three are correct and interchangeable in most sentences; the last is the one you
reach for when you want to sound resigned.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
雨(あめ)は当分(とうぶん)止(や)み<b>そうにもない</b>。
This rain doesn't look like stopping any time soon.
</pre>

<pre>
この仕事(しごと)、今日中(きょうじゅう)には終(お)わり<b>そうにない</b>な。
There's no way this job is getting finished today by the look of it.
</pre>

<pre>
渋滞(じゅうたい)がひどくて、待(ま)ち合(あ)わせの時間(じかん)には間(ま)に合(あ)い<b>そうにもありません</b>。
The traffic is dreadful and I don't see us making the meeting time.
</pre>

<pre>
熱(ねつ)が下(さ)がらず、明日(あした)も出社(しゅっしゃ)でき<b>そうにない</b>。
My temperature isn't coming down; it doesn't look as though I'll make it in tomorrow either.
</pre>

<pre>
あの様子(ようす)では、彼(かれ)のほうから謝(あやま)り<b>そうにもない</b>ね。
Judging by the look on his face, an apology from him isn't coming.
</pre>

<pre>
相手(あいて)が強(つよ)すぎて、この調子(ちょうし)では勝(か)て<b>そうにもない</b>。
They're too strong; at this rate we haven't a hope of winning.
</pre>

<pre>
議論(ぎろん)は平行線(へいこうせん)のままで、今日(きょう)のうちに結論(けつろん)が出(で)<b>そうにありません</b>。
The discussion is going in circles and there is no sign of a conclusion today.
</pre>

<pre>
交渉(こうしょう)は続(つづ)いているものの、来月(らいげつ)までに話(はなし)がまとまり<b>そうにもない</b>。
Talks are continuing, but there is no prospect at all of an agreement by next month.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and extremely common. It fits a text message, a weather bulletin and
a business report equally, and the polite そうにもありません is standard in an email
explaining that you will be late.

What it always carries is **evidence in view**. The speaker is looking at the sky,
the queue, the pile of documents, the other person's expression. That is why it does
not work for your own intentions: ❌明日(あした)は行(い)きそうにない about a
decision you have made is odd, because you are not inferring your own plans from the
outside. It works fine for your own **uncontrollable** states -
眠(ねむ)れそうにない, 治(なお)りそうにない, 間(ま)に合(あ)いそうにない -
because those are things happening to you.

The も is doing what も usually does. 終(お)わりそうにない is a flat prediction;
終(お)わりそうにもない adds a shrug to it. That is a tendency rather than a rule,
and in fast speech people use whichever comes out, but the emphatic version pairs
naturally with resigned particles - ね, な, よ - and with adverbs like
当分(とうぶん) and この調子(ちょうし)では.

One structural point worth holding onto: the same そう has two completely separate
grammars. **ます-stem + そう** is appearance; **plain form + そう** is hearsay.
降(ふ)りそうだ means "it looks like rain"; 降(ふ)るそうだ means "I hear it will
rain". The negatives diverge accordingly, and mixing them is the classic error.

## Contrast

### ～そうにもない vs ～ないそうだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>そうにもない</code> is what the speaker **sees**.
<code>ないそうだ</code> is what the speaker **heard**. Different base form, different
source of knowledge.

</div>

<pre>
天気(てんき)予報(よほう)によると、明日(あした)は降(ふ)ら<b>ないそうです</b>。
According to the forecast, it isn't going to rain tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
空(そら)を見(み)る限(かぎ)り、今日(きょう)は降(ふ)り<b>そうにもありません</b>。
Looking at the sky, there's no sign of rain today.
</pre>

The first cites a source and would be false if the speaker made it up; the second
reports an impression and can be wrong without anybody having lied. Check the base:
降(ふ)ら**ない**そう is a plain form, so hearsay; 降(ふ)り**そう** is a
ます-stem, so appearance.

### ～そうにもない vs ～なさそうだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for **verbs**, the idiomatic negative of the appearance そうだ is
<code>そうにない</code>. <code>なさそうだ</code> is the form for **adjectives and
ある**.

</div>

<pre>
この料理(りょうり)、あまりおいし<b>くなさそう</b>だね。
This dish doesn't look very appetising.
</pre>

<pre>
部長(ぶちょう)は今日中(きょうじゅう)に戻(もど)り<b>そうにない</b>。
The department head doesn't look like getting back today.
</pre>

Verbs do also produce 戻(もど)らなさそう in casual speech, and you will hear it;
そうにない is the neutral, unmarked choice and the one to write. Going the other way
is not available - ❌おいしそうにない is not how Japanese negates an adjective's
appearance.

### ～そうにもない vs ～はずがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>そうにもない</code> reads the **signs**, and could turn out
wrong. <code>はずがない</code> reasons from **what must be true**, and claims the
thing is impossible.

</div>

<pre>
鍵(かぎ)をかけたのだから、誰(だれ)かが入(はい)った<b>はずがない</b>。
I locked the door, so nobody can possibly have got in.
</pre>

<pre>
この人手(ひとで)では、今週(こんしゅう)のうちに終(お)わり<b>そうにもない</b>。
With this few people there's no sign of it being done this week.
</pre>

The first is a deduction and admits no exceptions; the second is an estimate and
could be overturned if three more people turned up on Thursday. Learners reach for
はずがない when they mean "it doesn't look like it", which overstates the case
considerably.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 終(お)わる<b>そうにない</b> → ✅ 終(お)わり<b>そうにない</b>
  The appearance そう takes the ます-stem. With the dictionary form you have built
  the hearsay そうだ instead, and then negated it in a way that does not work.

- ❌ 来(こ)<b>そうにない</b> → ✅ 来(き)<b>そうにない</b>
  来(く)る is irregular. Its ます-stem is き, as in 来(き)ます.

- ❌ この問題(もんだい)は難(むずか)し<b>そうにない</b>。 → ✅ この問題(もんだい)は難(むずか)し<b>くなさそうだ</b>。
  い-adjectives use the く form plus なさそう. そうにない is for verbs.

- ❌ 明日(あした)は会社(かいしゃ)に行(い)き<b>そうにない</b>。（about your own plan） → ✅ 明日(あした)は会社(かいしゃ)を休(やす)むつもりだ。
  You do not infer your own decisions from evidence. The pattern needs something you
  are observing rather than choosing.

**1.** 前(まえ)の人(ひと)の列(れつ)が全然(ぜんぜん)進(すす)まない。開演(かいえん)までに入(はい)れ____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

入(はい)れ<b>そうにもない</b>

The queue in front of the speaker is the visible evidence, and the emphatic も
matches the frustration. Note the potential stem 入(はい)れ- before そう.

</details>

**2.** Choose: ニュースによると、台風(たいふう)は上陸(じょうりく)（しそうにない・しないそうだ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

台風(たいふう)は上陸(じょうりく)<b>しないそうだ</b>。

The sentence names a source - ニュースによると - so this is hearsay, which takes the
plain form plus そうだ. しそうにない would mean the speaker had looked at the sky and
formed their own impression.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He's still asleep; it doesn't look as though he'll be up in time."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

まだ寝(ね)ている。時間(じかん)までに起(お)き<b>そうにない</b>。

The speaker is looking at him, so this is the appearance そう on the ます-stem
起(お)き-. Adding も（起(お)きそうにもない）would sharpen it into "no chance
whatsoever".

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 工事(こうじ)は来年(らいねん)まで終(お)わるそうにもない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 工事(こうじ)は来年(らいねん)まで<b>終(お)わりそうにもない</b>。
"The building work shows no sign of finishing before next year."

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 雨(あめ)は止(や)み<b>そうにない</b>。
(b) 雨(あめ)は止(や)み<b>そうにもない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain reading of the weather: on the evidence, the rain will continue. It is
the neutral form and would be fine in a forecast.

(b) adds the も, and with it a shrug. The speaker has been watching for a while,
sees nothing at all promising, and has given up expecting it to clear. The difference
is emphasis and mood rather than fact, and it is a tendency rather than a rule - but
(b) is the one you would say while putting your umbrella back up.

</details>

# ～てもどうにもならない

**Meaning** · even if you do it, nothing will change - the situation is past mending, and this action is not what would mend it

<code>どうにもならない</code> on its own means "there is nothing to be done". Put a
て-form in front of it and you name the particular thing that will not help:
悔(く)やんでもどうにもならない says regret is not a lever, because the situation
has stopped responding to levers. It is the grammar of a shrug, and very often the
grammar of talking someone out of something.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + もどうにもならない | 謝(あやま)る → 謝(あやま)ってもどうにもならない |
| い-adjective | ～くて + もどうにもならない | 悔(くや)しい → 悔(くや)しくてもどうにもならない |
| な-adjective / Noun | ～でもどうにもならない | 残念(ざんねん) → 残念(ざんねん)でもどうにもならない |
| Polite | ～てもどうにもなりません | 急(いそ)ぐ → 急(いそ)いでもどうにもなりません |
| Past | ～てもどうにもならなかった | 頼(たの)む → 頼(たの)んでもどうにもならなかった |
| Without a ても clause | ～は／～が + どうにもならない | 過去(かこ) → 過去(かこ)はどうにもならない |
| Naming who is powerless | ～には + どうにもならない | 私(わたし) → 私(わたし)にはどうにもならない |
| Transitive counterpart | ～を + どうにもできない | 状況(じょうきょう) → 状況(じょうきょう)をどうにもできない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>ならない</code> is intransitive: what cannot be changed is the
subject, marked は or が, and a person who is powerless is marked
には. If you want an を object, the verb has to be
<code>できない</code>. ❌過去(かこ)をどうにもならない mixes the two.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
今(いま)さら悔(く)やん<b>でもどうにもならない</b>。
There's no use regretting it at this stage.
</pre>

<pre>
ここで怒(おこ)っ<b>てもどうにもならない</b>よ。
Losing your temper here isn't going to change anything.
</pre>

<pre>
水道(すいどう)が止(と)まっているのだから、蛇口(じゃぐち)を回(まわ)し<b>てもどうにもならない</b>。
The water's been cut off, so turning the tap achieves precisely nothing.
</pre>

<pre>
過(す)ぎたことを責(せ)め<b>てもどうにもならない</b>から、次(つぎ)をどうするか考(かんが)えよう。
Blaming each other over what's done won't help, so let's think about what comes next.
</pre>

<pre>
一人(ひとり)で抱(かか)え込(こ)ん<b>でもどうにもなりません</b>。誰(だれ)かに相談(そうだん)してください。
Carrying it alone will get you nowhere. Please talk to somebody.
</pre>

<pre>
期限(きげん)を過(す)ぎてから申(もう)し込(こ)ん<b>でもどうにもならない</b>と窓口(まどぐち)で言(い)われた。
I was told at the counter that applying after the deadline gets you nowhere.
</pre>

<pre>
値段(ねだん)については、この場(ば)で私(わたし)が頭(あたま)を下(さ)げ<b>てもどうにもなりません</b>。
As for the price, my apologising here and now will not move it an inch.
</pre>

<pre>
建物(たてもの)の老朽化(ろうきゅうか)が進(すす)み、表面(ひょうめん)を塗(ぬ)り直(なお)し<b>てもどうにもならない</b>段階(だんかい)に来(き)ている。
The building has deteriorated to a point where repainting the surface no longer helps at all.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Everyday Japanese, plain or polite as required, and equally at home in speech and in
writing that has a person behind it. The feeling is **resignation** - the speaker has
concluded that the situation has hardened and no longer responds to effort.

It is very often **advice**. 「心配(しんぱい)してもどうにもならないよ」 to a
worried friend is not a philosophical observation; it is a way of saying stop. That
is why the pattern so frequently sits in front of から or ので and a suggestion:
first the door is closed, then an alternative is offered.

Two adverbs pull it along almost by reflex. 今(いま)さら points at a window that
has shut - 今(いま)さら謝(あやま)ってもどうにもならない - and いくら
measures wasted effort - いくら待(ま)ってもどうにもならなかった. Both are worth
learning as part of the shape.

There is a register warning attached to the bare
<code>どうにもなりません</code>. Said to a customer or a client, it lands as "I have
given up on you", however politely it is conjugated. Business Japanese prefers
対応(たいおう)いたしかねます or
お力(ちから)になれず申(もう)し訳(わけ)ございません, which decline
without announcing that the situation is hopeless.

## Contrast

### ～てもどうにもならない vs ～ても始(はじ)まらない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>どうにもならない</code> says **the situation** will not
move. <code>始(はじ)まらない</code> says **this activity gets you nowhere**, so
do something else instead.

</div>

<pre>
悩(なや)ん<b>でも始(はじ)まらない</b>。まず手(て)を動(うご)かそう。
Fretting won't get us anywhere. Let's start doing something.
</pre>

<pre>
契約(けいやく)はもう結(むす)ばれていて、反対(はんたい)し<b>てもどうにもならない</b>。
The contract is already signed; objecting now will change nothing.
</pre>

The two overlap widely and either would pass in many sentences. Where they part
company is scope. <code>始(はじ)まらない</code> is about the futility of a
particular activity - almost always worrying, complaining, arguing, hesitating - and
looks forward to a better use of the time.
<code>どうにもならない</code> is about the situation itself, takes any verb at all,
and looks at a door that has closed.

### ～てもどうにもならない vs ～てもしかたがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>しかたがない</code> evaluates the **act**: doing it is
pointless. <code>どうにもならない</code> evaluates the **outcome**: nothing you do
lands.

</div>

<pre>
終(お)わったことを気(き)にし<b>てもしかたがない</b>よ。
There's no sense worrying about something that's over.
</pre>

<pre>
部品(ぶひん)が生産(せいさん)中止(ちゅうし)になった以上(いじょう)、修理(しゅうり)に出(だ)し<b>てもどうにもならない</b>。
Now that the part is out of production, sending it for repair achieves nothing.
</pre>

They are close cousins and often interchangeable.
<code>しかたがない</code> carries a gentler, more consoling tone and is the one used
to reassure someone. <code>どうにもならない</code> is more final. Note also that
<code>しかたがない</code> has a second life meaning "it can't be helped, so accept
it" （雨(あめ)だからしかたがない）, which <code>どうにもならない</code> does not.

### ～てもどうにもならない vs ～ても無駄(むだ)だ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>無駄(むだ)だ</code> calls the effort **a waste**, and it
is blunt. <code>どうにもならない</code> reports that the situation will not shift,
and is softer.

</div>

<pre>
何度(なんど)説明(せつめい)し<b>ても無駄(むだ)だ</b>。聞(き)く気(き)がないんだから。
Explaining it again is a waste of breath. He isn't listening.
</pre>

<pre>
何度(なんど)説明(せつめい)し<b>てもどうにもならなかった</b>ので、書面(しょめん)で伝(つた)えることにした。
Repeated explanations got us nowhere, so we decided to put it in writing.
</pre>

<code>無駄(むだ)だ</code> passes judgement on the person making the effort, which
is why it stings; used to someone's face about their own attempt it is close to an
insult. <code>どうにもならない</code> puts the blame on circumstances and leaves
everybody's dignity intact.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 悔(く)やん<b>でもどうにもしない</b>。 → ✅ 悔(く)やん<b>でもどうにもならない</b>。
  The verb is <code>なる</code>. <code>する</code> would need an agent and an を
  object.

- ❌ 過去(かこ)を<b>どうにもならない</b>。 → ✅ 過去(かこ)は<b>どうにもならない</b>。（or 過去(かこ)を<b>どうにもできない</b>）
  <code>ならない</code> is intransitive. Match the particle to the verb.

- ❌ 急(いそ)い<b>でもどうにもならない</b>ので、走(はし)りましょう。 → ✅ 急(いそ)い<b>でもどうにもならない</b>ので、ゆっくり行(い)きましょう。
  The pattern closes a door, so what follows must accept that. Concluding "therefore
  let's hurry" contradicts the first clause.

- ❌ （to a customer）「その件(けん)は<b>どうにもなりません</b>」 → ✅ 「申(もう)し訳(わけ)ございませんが、対応(たいおう)いたしかねます」
  Polite in form, hopeless in content. Announcing that a customer's problem cannot be
  solved is not how a refusal is delivered in business Japanese.

**1.** 試験(しけん)はもう終(お)わったんだから、答(こた)えを調(しら)べ____。

The exam is over, so the door has closed; checking the answers cannot affect the
outcome. This is the 今(いま)さら flavour the pattern specialises in.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 迷(まよ)っ（てもどうにもならない・ても始(はじ)まらない）から、そろそろ決(き)めよう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Dithering is exactly the kind of unproductive activity
<code>始(はじ)まらない</code> targets, and the sentence goes on to propose the
alternative. Both are acceptable here, but <code>始(はじ)まらない</code> is the idiomatic fit.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "There's no point complaining to me - I don't set the prices."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The speaker is powerless over the matter, which is the core case. The polite

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この状況(じょうきょう)を、私(わたし)一人(ひとり)ではどうにもなりません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この状況(じょうきょう)<b>は</b>、私(わたし)一人(ひとり)ではどうにもなりません。
（or この状況(じょうきょう)を、私(わたし)一人(ひとり)では<b>どうにもできません</b>）

<code>なりません</code> does not take an を object. Either make the situation the
subject with は, or switch the verb to <code>できない</code>, which does take one.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 泣(な)い<b>てもしかたがない</b>よ。
(b) 泣(な)い<b>てもどうにもならない</b>よ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is consoling. It tells the person that crying will not help and, underneath,
that nobody is blaming them for the situation - the sort of thing said with a hand on
someone's shoulder.

(b) is flatter and more final. It says the situation has passed the point where
anything, crying included, would make a difference. Said gently it is still comfort;
said briskly it is an instruction to pull yourself together.

</details>

# ～ても始まらない

**Meaning** · it's no use doing that; that won't get us anywhere - the activity produces no forward movement, so stop and do something else

Literally: even if you do it, **nothing starts**. That word 始(はじ)まる is the
whole character of the expression. It does not say the act is stupid or that the
world is hopeless; it says no progress comes out of it, and it therefore points
straight at whatever should be done instead. In practice it is almost always
encouragement.

## Formation

The て-form of a **verb**, plus も, plus 始(はじ)まらない. Adjectives and nouns do
not enter this pattern.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + も始(はじ)まらない | 悩(なや)む → 悩(なや)んでも始(はじ)まらない |
| する-verb | ～しても始(はじ)まらない | 心配(しんぱい)する → 心配(しんぱい)しても始(はじ)まらない |
| Verb phrase | Noun + を + て-form + も… | 文句(もんく)を言(い)う → 文句(もんく)を言(い)っても始(はじ)まらない |
| With てみる | ～てみても始(はじ)まらない | 考(かんが)える → 考(かんが)えてみても始(はじ)まらない |
| Polite | ～ても始(はじ)まりません | 焦(あせ)る → 焦(あせ)っても始(はじ)まりません |
| Usual continuation | …から／…ので + a suggestion | 迷(まよ)う → 迷(まよ)っても始(はじ)まらないから、決(き)めよう |
| Not available | い-adjective, な-adjective, noun | ❌寒(さむ)くても始(はじ)まらない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the verb slot is narrow. It holds **unproductive mental and
verbal activity**: 悩(なや)む, 考(かんが)える, 心配(しんぱい)する,
焦(あせ)る, 迷(まよ)う, 慌(あわ)てる, 後悔(こうかい)する,
文句(もんく)を言(い)う, 責(せ)める, 泣(な)く. A genuinely productive
action in that slot sounds odd, because such an action *would* start something.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
一人(ひとり)で考(かんが)え<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>。
Sitting there thinking about it on your own won't get you anywhere.
</pre>

<pre>
今(いま)になって後悔(こうかい)し<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>よ。
Regretting it now isn't going to move anything forward.
</pre>

<pre>
文句(もんく)を言(い)っ<b>ても始(はじ)まりません</b>から、できることからやりましょう。
Complaining gets us nowhere, so let's start with what we can actually do.
</pre>

<pre>
焦(あせ)っ<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>。落(お)ち着(つ)いてもう一度(いちど)読(よ)んでみて。
Panicking won't help. Calm down and read it through once more.
</pre>

<pre>
互(たが)いに責任(せきにん)を押(お)し付(つ)け合(あ)っ<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>。
Pushing the blame back and forth is getting us precisely nowhere.
</pre>

<pre>
数字(すうじ)を眺(なが)め<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>。現場(げんば)に行(い)って話(はなし)を聞(き)いてこよう。
Staring at the figures won't get us anywhere. Let's go and talk to the people on site.
</pre>

<pre>
ここで立(た)ち止(ど)まって迷(まよ)っ<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>ので、とりあえず申(もう)し込(こ)んでみることにした。
Standing here dithering wasn't going to achieve anything, so I decided to just apply.
</pre>

<pre>
過去(かこ)の失敗(しっぱい)を悔(く)やん<b>でも始(はじ)まらない</b>と頭(あたま)では分(わ)かっているが、なかなか割(わ)り切(き)れない。
I know perfectly well that dwelling on past mistakes leads nowhere, and still I can't quite let go.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Conversational and neutral. The plain form is what friends and colleagues say to each
other, and 始(はじ)まりません fits a polite meeting without sounding stiff. What
is distinctive is the **direction it faces**: forward. Because the literal claim is
that nothing gets started, the sentence naturally hands over to a proposal, and in
real speech it very often arrives glued to one -
「悩(なや)んでも始(はじ)まらないから、明日(あした)聞(き)いてみよう」.

That makes it a **kind** thing to say. Told that your worrying is useless, you might
bristle; told that worrying is not going to start anything and that here is the next
step, you generally do not. Speakers use it to break a stalemate - in a meeting that
has turned into recrimination, at a kitchen table at two in the morning.

The verb restriction is the main thing to internalise. The pattern wants an activity
that spins on the spot: fretting, hesitating, arguing, apportioning blame. Put a
productive verb in and the sentence becomes strange, because
掃除(そうじ)しても始(はじ)まらない seems to claim that cleaning starts nothing,
which is false unless you mean cleaning is beside the point. Where you want to
dismiss a productive action, ～ても意味(いみ)がない or
～てもどうにもならない is the tool.

Two form notes. The も is not optional: 悩(なや)んで始(はじ)まらない, without
it, reads as a cause-and-effect statement and makes no sense. And the pattern is
normally in the present tense, because it states a general judgement rather than
reporting an outcome; the past
始(はじ)まらなかった exists but is rare and usually means something more literal.

## Contrast

### ～ても始(はじ)まらない vs ～てもどうにもならない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>始(はじ)まらない</code> says **this activity produces
nothing**, and expects a better activity next.
<code>どうにもならない</code> says **the situation itself will not shift**.

</div>

<pre>
言(い)い争(あらそ)っ<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>。条件(じょうけん)を一(ひと)つずつ見(み)ていこう。
Arguing gets us nowhere. Let's go through the terms one at a time.
</pre>

<pre>
工場(こうじょう)はもう閉(と)じられていて、今(いま)から抗議(こうぎ)し<b>てもどうにもならない</b>。
The factory has already closed; protesting at this point will change nothing.
</pre>

The first assumes there is still a way forward and is looking for it. The second
reports that the way forward is gone. That is also why
<code>どうにもならない</code> accepts any verb at all while
<code>始(はじ)まらない</code> keeps to its narrow list: one is about the world,
the other is about how you are spending your time.

### ～ても始(はじ)まらない vs ～てもしかたがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>しかたがない</code> **consoles**: there is no help for it,
so don't blame yourself. <code>始(はじ)まらない</code> **prods**: this isn't
moving us, so let's do something.

</div>

<pre>
終(お)わったことだから、落(お)ち込(こ)ん<b>でもしかたがないよ</b>。
It's over and done with - there's no point being down about it.
</pre>

<pre>
落(お)ち込(こ)ん<b>でも始(はじ)まらない</b>から、そろそろ次(つぎ)の準備(じゅんび)をしよう。
Being down about it isn't getting us anywhere, so let's start getting ready for the next one.
</pre>

Both are said to the same dejected person, and the difference is what the speaker
wants from them. <code>しかたがない</code> asks for acceptance;
<code>始(はじ)まらない</code> asks for movement. If the next sentence out of your
mouth is a suggestion, this is the one you want.

### ～ても始(はじ)まらない vs ～ても意味(いみ)がない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>意味(いみ)がない</code> judges the act **meaningless**
in itself, and applies to anything. <code>始(はじ)まらない</code> says only that
it **produces no progress**.

</div>

<pre>
答(こた)えを写(うつ)すだけでは、宿題(しゅくだい)をやっ<b>ても意味(いみ)がない</b>。
Just copying the answers means there's no point doing the homework at all.
</pre>

<pre>
結論(けつろん)の出(で)ない話(はなし)を続(つづ)け<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>。
There is no sense going on with a discussion that isn't reaching a conclusion.
</pre>

<code>意味(いみ)がない</code> is the broader and blunter of the two. It can be
said of an object, a rule, a whole plan, and it can sound dismissive of the person
who proposed it. <code>始(はじ)まらない</code> criticises no one; it just observes
that the clock is running.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 悩(なや)ん<b>で始(はじ)まらない</b>。 → ✅ 悩(なや)ん<b>でも始(はじ)まらない</b>。
  The も is part of the pattern. Without it the sentence reads as cause and effect -
  "it doesn't start because you worry" - which means nothing.

- ❌ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っ<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>。 → ✅ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っ<b>てもどうにもならない</b>。
  The slot holds something a person is *doing* pointlessly. Weather is not an
  activity anyone can be talked out of.

- ❌ 寒(さむ)く<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>。 → ✅ 寒(さむ)くても仕方(しかた)がない。
  Adjectives and nouns do not enter this pattern at all. It takes verbs only.

- ❌ 部屋(へや)を掃除(そうじ)し<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>。 → ✅ 部屋(へや)を掃除(そうじ)し<b>ても意味(いみ)がない</b>。
  Cleaning is productive, so saying it starts nothing is close to a contradiction.
  For dismissing a real action, use 意味(いみ)がない or どうにもならない.

## Practice

**1.** 結果(けっか)は来週(らいしゅう)まで分(わ)からないのだから、今(いま)から心配(しんぱい)し____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

心配(しんぱい)し<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>（よ）

Worrying is the archetypal verb for this pattern, and the sentence is comforting
someone rather than dismissing them. Note the する-verb form
心配(しんぱい)しても.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 契約(けいやく)はもう終(お)わっているので、今(いま)から条件(じょうけん)を変(か)えてほしいと言(い)っ（ても始(はじ)まらない・てもどうにもならない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

言(い)っ<b>てもどうにもならない</b>。

The contract is closed - the situation itself has stopped moving, which is
<code>どうにもならない</code>. <code>始(はじ)まらない</code> would imply there is
still somewhere better to direct the effort.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "There's no use blaming each other. Let's work out what to do next."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お互(たが)いを責(せ)め合(あ)っ<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>。次(つぎ)にどうするか考(かんが)えよう。

Mutual blame is exactly the wheel-spinning this pattern names, and the suggestion
that follows is the shape it almost always comes in.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 準備(じゅんび)が足(た)りなくても始(はじ)まらないので、あきらめました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 準備(じゅんび)が足(た)りず、どうにもならなかったので、あきらめました。
"We weren't ready enough and there was nothing to be done, so we gave up."

Two problems. 足(た)りない is a state rather than a futile activity, and the
conclusion is surrender - but <code>始(はじ)まらない</code> exists to point at a
next step, so it clashes with あきらめました.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 後悔(こうかい)し<b>てもしかたがない</b>。
(b) 後悔(こうかい)し<b>ても始(はじ)まらない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) lets the listener off. It says the regret is understandable but pointless, and
invites them to stop being hard on themselves. Nothing is asked of them.

(b) is asking for something. It says the regret is not moving anybody forward, and
the listener will expect the speaker's next sentence to be a proposal. Same comfort,
different destination.

</details>
