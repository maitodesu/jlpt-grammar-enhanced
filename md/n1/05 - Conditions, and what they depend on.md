# N1 - Conditions, and what they depend on


---

# ～とあれば

**Meaning** · if it really is a case of ~; if it's for ~ - a weighty circumstance is granted, and the speaker says what they will do about it

The `と` is the quotative particle and `あれば` is the conditional of `ある`, so the
literal shape is "if it is (the case) that ~". That is why the pattern does not
float a hypothesis - it **accepts** one, usually one both people already know
about, and the second half then states a resolve, an obligation the speaker takes
on, or something treated as unavoidable.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + とあれば | 急用(きゅうよう) → 急用(きゅうよう)とあれば |
| な-adjective | stem + とあれば | 必要(ひつよう)な → 必要(ひつよう)とあれば |
| い-adjective | plain form + とあれば | 忙(いそが)しい → 忙(いそが)しいとあれば |
| Verb | plain form + とあれば | 行(い)く → 行(い)くとあれば |
| Verb (past) | plain past + とあれば | 決(き)まった → 決(き)まったとあれば |
| Purpose | Noun + のため + とあれば | 家族(かぞく) → 家族(かぞく)のためとあれば |
| Emphatic doubling | Noun + が + Noun + とあれば | 事情(じじょう) → 事情(じじょう)が事情(じじょう)とあれば |

<div class="note">

**Note** - な-adjectives normally drop the `な` here:
<code>必要(ひつよう)とあれば</code> is the usual shape, not
<code>必要(ひつよう)なとあれば</code>. The fuller
<code>必要(ひつよう)だとあれば</code> exists but is much less common.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
必要(ひつよう)<b>とあれば</b>、いつでも伺(うかが)います。
If it's needed, I'll come over any time.
</pre>

<pre>
君(きみ)の頼(たの)み<b>とあれば</b>、断(ことわ)れないな。
If it's you asking, I can hardly say no.
</pre>

<pre>
子(こ)どものため<b>とあれば</b>、親(おや)はどんな無理(むり)でもするものだ。
When it's for their children, parents will put up with anything.
</pre>

<pre>
社長(しゃちょう)の指示(しじ)<b>とあれば</b>、従(したが)うほかない。
If those are the president's instructions, there is nothing to do but follow them.
</pre>

<pre>
急(きゅう)を要(よう)する<b>とあれば</b>、夜間(やかん)でも対応(たいおう)いたします。
Should the matter be urgent, we will attend to it even at night.
</pre>

<pre>
事情(じじょう)が事情(じじょう)<b>とあれば</b>、今回(こんかい)だけは大目(おおめ)に見(み)よう。
Given the circumstances - and they are what they are - I'll let it go this once.
</pre>

<pre>
恩師(おんし)の最後(さいご)の願(ねが)い<b>とあれば</b>、何(なん)としても叶(かな)えてさしあげたい。
If it is my old teacher's last wish, I want to see it granted whatever it takes.
</pre>

<pre>
地元(じもと)の祭(まつ)りを守(まも)るため<b>とあれば</b>、住民(じゅうみん)は時間(じかん)も費用(ひよう)も惜(お)しまなかった。
Where it was a question of saving the local festival, the residents grudged neither time nor money.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`とあれば` is **formal and written-leaning**. Its natural homes are narration in
fiction, speeches, editorials, and the more ceremonious end of business speech,
where <code>必要(ひつよう)とあれば</code> and <code>ご希望(きぼう)とあれば</code>
are close to fixed expressions. Between friends it sounds theatrical: saying
<code>ビールがないとあれば、買(か)いに行(い)くしかない</code> is a joke about
one's own seriousness, and hearers will take it as one.

Two things the pattern needs. First, the condition has to carry some **weight** -
a duty, an emergency, somebody's request, a person's welfare, money on the line.
Everyday weather and timetables are too small for it. Second, the main clause has
to be a **response**, not an observation. The endings that keep it company are
<code>どんな…も</code>, <code>…ほかない</code>, <code>…わけにはいかない</code>,
<code>…も辞(じ)さない</code> and plain volitionals.

The doubling trick <code>事情(じじょう)が事情(じじょう)</code>
("the circumstances being the circumstances") is worth learning as a unit. It lets
a speaker grant that something is exceptional without spelling out what it was,
which is tactful, and it appears with a small set of nouns -
事情(じじょう), 相手(あいて), 時期(じき). In workplace Japanese you will
meet it most often with <code>だけに</code> or <code>なので</code> rather than
with `とあれば`: <code>事情(じじょう)が事情(じじょう)だけに、強(つよ)く言(い)えない</code>.

## Contrast

### ～とあれば vs ～なら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なら` takes up any condition at all, at any register.
`とあれば` takes up a condition that has been **granted as significant**, and
raises the register several notches.

</div>

<pre>
雨(あめ)<b>なら</b>、試合(しあい)は中止(ちゅうし)だね。
If it rains the match is off, then.
</pre>

<pre>
選手(せんしゅ)の安全(あんぜん)にかかわること<b>とあれば</b>、中止(ちゅうし)もやむを得(え)ない。
Where the players' safety is at stake, calling it off cannot be avoided.
</pre>

Swap them and the first sentence becomes pompous about drizzle, while the second
loses the sense that the speaker has weighed the matter. A useful test: if the
English would tolerate "if it's a question of ~", `とあれば` fits.

### ～とあれば vs ～とすれば・～としたら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `とすれば` **supposes** something for the sake of argument and
works out what would follow. `とあれば` **accepts** something as so and states
what will be done.

</div>

<pre>
彼(かれ)の話(はなし)が本当(ほんとう)だ<b>とすれば</b>、計算(けいさん)が合(あ)わない。
If we suppose his account is true, the numbers don't add up.
</pre>

<pre>
彼(かれ)の話(はなし)が本当(ほんとう)<b>とあれば</b>、すぐに手(て)を打(う)たねばならない。
If his account really is true, we have to act at once.
</pre>

The first sentence ends in an inference; the second ends in a decision. That is the
whole difference, and it is why `とすれば` is the natural partner of
<code>はずだ</code> and <code>ことになる</code>, while `とあれば` pairs with verbs
of doing.

### ～とあれば vs ～ともなると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `とあれば` is a **circumstance that justifies an action**.
`ともなると` is a **level or stage reached**, and what follows is what naturally
comes with it.

</div>

<pre>
部長(ぶちょう)の命令(めいれい)<b>とあれば</b>、週末(しゅうまつ)も出社(しゅっしゃ)する。
If it's an order from the department head, I'll come in at the weekend too.
</pre>

<pre>
部長(ぶちょう)<b>ともなると</b>、週末(しゅうまつ)も落(お)ち着(つ)かないものらしい。
Once you are a department head, apparently even weekends are not your own.
</pre>

The first is a one-off response to an order. The second is a general truth about
the rank. `ともなると` cannot be used for a single occasion, and `とあれば` cannot
introduce a generalisation about a status.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 明日(あした)晴(は)れ<b>とあれば</b>、洗濯(せんたく)をします。 → ✅ 明日(あした)晴(は)れる<b>なら</b>、洗濯(せんたく)をします。
  Fine weather and the laundry are too ordinary. `とあれば` promises the listener
  that something serious is coming, and then nothing does.

- ❌ 必要(ひつよう)な<b>とあれば</b> → ✅ 必要(ひつよう)<b>とあれば</b>
  な-adjectives lose the `な` in front of this `と`. The `と` is quotative, and a
  quotative `と` takes the bare stem.

- ❌ 会(あ)えない<b>とあれば</b>、残念(ざんねん)です。 → ✅ 会(あ)えない<b>とあれば</b>、日(ひ)を改(あらた)めるしかありません。
  The main clause has to be a response. A bare statement of feeling leaves the
  pattern's promise of action unpaid.

- ❌ 事情(じじょう)が事情(じじょう)<b>とあった</b>ので、欠席(けっせき)した。 → ✅ 事情(じじょう)が事情(じじょう)<b>とあって</b>、欠席(けっせき)した。
  `とあれば` is the conditional; the "since it was so" partner is
  <code>とあって</code>. ❌<code>とあった</code> is not used as a connector here.

## Practice

**1.** お急(いそ)ぎ____、特急(とっきゅう)料金(りょうきん)をいただきますが明日(あした)お届(とど)けできます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お急(いそ)ぎ<b>とあれば</b>

The customer's urgency is accepted as a real and significant condition, and the
second half is what the shop will do about it. The polite noun お急(いそ)ぎ
attaches directly, with no `だ`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 来週(らいしゅう)台風(たいふう)が来(く)る（とあれば・とすれば）、出発(しゅっぱつ)を早(はや)めた方(ほう)がいいかもしれない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

来(く)る<b>とすれば</b>

かもしれない makes this a piece of reasoning about a typhoon nobody has confirmed,
which is `とすれば` territory. `とあれば` would mean the typhoon is now established
fact and the speaker is committing to a change of plan, which sits badly with the
hedge at the end.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If it is for the sake of the patient, the doctor will not begrudge the time."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

患者(かんじゃ)のため<b>とあれば</b>、医師(いし)は時間(じかん)を惜(お)しまない。

"For the sake of ~" is <code>のため</code>, and <code>のためとあれば</code> is one
of the pattern's commonest shapes. The main clause is a willing act, which is
exactly what `とあれば` asks for.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 大切(たいせつ)な客(きゃく)だとあれば、社長(しゃちょう)自(みずか)ら挨拶(あいさつ)に出(で)るだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 大切(たいせつ)な客(きゃく)<b>とあれば</b>、社長(しゃちょう)自(みずか)ら挨拶(あいさつ)に出(で)るだろう。

A noun links to this quotative `と` bare. The `だ` is not impossible in every
context, but with `とあれば` the plain noun is overwhelmingly the normal form, and
<code>客(きゃく)だとあれば</code> reads as clumsy.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 部下(ぶか)の頼(たの)み<b>なら</b>、聞(き)いてやろう。
(b) 部下(ぶか)の頼(たの)み<b>とあれば</b>、聞(き)いてやろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is an ordinary conditional. It could be said lightly, about a small favour, and
sounds like everyday spoken Japanese.

(b) treats the request as carrying obligation: because this person works under me,
I owe them a hearing. The register lifts, the speaker sounds as though they have
weighed the matter, and the sentence would fit a novel or a speech more comfortably
than a corridor conversation.

</details>

# ～ともなると・～ともなれば

**Meaning** · once you get to ~; when it comes to being ~ - at this level or stage, the thing that follows is only to be expected

The `も` is doing the work. <code>となると</code> would simply say "when it comes to
that"; the `も` adds "even, no less than", so the phrase marks the noun as a
**notable level** - an age, a rank, a season, a quantity, a scale of undertaking.
The second half then reports what naturally comes with that level, usually as a
general truth rather than a one-off event.

## Formation

The clause in front is a noun or a plain-form verb. Nothing changes shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (status, rank) | Noun + ともなると | 管理職(かんりしょく) → 管理職(かんりしょく)ともなると |
| Noun (age, stage) | Noun + ともなれば | 中学生(ちゅうがくせい) → 中学生(ちゅうがくせい)ともなれば |
| Noun (season, time) | Noun + ともなると | 真冬(まふゆ) → 真冬(まふゆ)ともなると |
| Number, quantity | Number + ともなると | 百人(ひゃくにん) → 百人(ひゃくにん)ともなると |
| Verb | plain form + ともなると | 出場(しゅつじょう)する → 出場(しゅつじょう)するともなると |
| Written variant | ～ともなれば | 名門校(めいもんこう)ともなれば |

<div class="note">

**Note** - `ともなると` and `ともなれば` are interchangeable in almost every
sentence. `ともなると` states the level as a fact and is the commoner of the two;
`ともなれば` leans very slightly towards the hypothetical ("supposing you got that
far") and turns up a little more in writing. Nothing else separates them.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
冬(ふゆ)<b>ともなると</b>、この町(まち)は雪(ゆき)で静(しず)かになる。
Come winter, the snow makes this town go quiet.
</pre>

<pre>
高校生(こうこうせい)<b>ともなれば</b>、自分(じぶん)のことは自分(じぶん)でやるものだ。
Once they're in high school, children look after themselves.
</pre>

<pre>
連休(れんきゅう)の最終日(さいしゅうび)<b>ともなると</b>、高速道路(こうそくどうろ)は昼過(ひるす)ぎから渋滞(じゅうたい)する。
On the last day of a long weekend the motorway starts backing up from early afternoon.
</pre>

<pre>
課長(かちょう)<b>ともなると</b>、部下(ぶか)の失敗(しっぱい)まで背負(せお)わされる。
Once you make section chief you end up carrying your subordinates' mistakes as well.
</pre>

<pre>
標高(ひょうこう)三千(さんぜん)メートル<b>ともなれば</b>、夏(なつ)でも息(いき)が白(しろ)くなる。
At three thousand metres you can see your breath even in summer.
</pre>

<pre>
桜(さくら)の季節(きせつ)<b>ともなると</b>、この公園(こうえん)には近隣(きんりん)の市(し)からも人(ひと)が押(お)し寄(よ)せる。
When cherry-blossom season comes round, people pour into this park from the neighbouring cities too.
</pre>

<pre>
世界大会(せかいたいかい)に出場(しゅつじょう)する<b>ともなれば</b>、練習量(れんしゅうりょう)も覚悟(かくご)もこれまでとは比(くら)べものにならない。
Once it is a matter of competing at the world championships, the training load and the resolve required are on another scale entirely.
</pre>

<pre>
三十年(さんじゅうねん)勤(つと)めた職人(しょくにん)<b>ともなると</b>、機械(きかい)の音(おと)を聞(き)いただけで不具合(ふぐあい)が分(わ)かるらしい。
A craftsman with thirty years behind him can apparently tell there's a fault just from the sound of the machine.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is **formal, but not stiff**. It is at home in journalism, weather and travel
copy, commentary, essays and any spoken register above the chatty - a presentation,
an interview, a teacher explaining something. In relaxed conversation the same idea
comes out as <code>中学生(ちゅうがくせい)になると</code> or
<code>中学生(ちゅうがくせい)にもなれば</code>.

The main clause is where learners go wrong, so it is worth stating flatly: it must
be a **generalisation**. `ともなると` sets up a level and then reports what is
always, typically, or inevitably true at that level. That is why the endings
<code>ものだ</code>, <code>らしい</code>, <code>ようだ</code>,
<code>わけにはいかない</code> and plain non-past statements fit it so well, and why
a request, an invitation or a report of one particular afternoon does not.

There is a mild rhetorical colour to it. Very often the speaker is registering that
the level in question is impressive, burdensome, or beyond the ordinary - "at that
rank, no wonder", "at that altitude, of course". Used of something unremarkable the
sentence sounds inflated, which is exactly why it is also good for gentle irony:
「課長(かちょう)<b>ともなると</b>コピーも自分(じぶん)で取(と)らないんですね」
is a dig, not a compliment.

## Contrast

### ～ともなると vs ～となると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `となると` covers **any** topic once it comes up.
`ともなると` needs the noun to be a **notable level** on some scale, and adds
"even at that level".

</div>

<pre>
引(ひ)っ越(こ)し<b>となると</b>、考(かんが)えることが多(おお)い。
Once moving house is on the table, there's a lot to think about.
</pre>

<pre>
海外(かいがい)への引(ひ)っ越(こ)し<b>ともなると</b>、準備(じゅんび)に半年(はんとし)はかかる。
A move abroad, no less, takes a good six months to prepare.
</pre>

The first simply raises the subject. The second sorts moves into ordinary ones and
big ones and puts this one at the top end. Where there is no scale to be at the top
of, `ともなると` has nothing to add and `となると` is the right choice.

### ～ともなると vs ～になると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `になると` is the neutral "when it becomes / when it gets to".
`ともなると` is the same event **presented as a threshold**, with a comment
attached.

</div>

<pre>
七月(しちがつ)<b>になると</b>、市民(しみん)プールが開放(かいほう)される。
The public pool opens to swimmers in July.
</pre>

<pre>
八月(はちがつ)<b>ともなると</b>、昼間(ひるま)は外(そと)を歩(ある)く気(き)にもならない。
By the time August comes round, you can't even face walking outside during the day.
</pre>

Both are grammatical in both sentences, but the first is a timetable and the second
is a remark about how far things have gone. `になると` is neutral and can carry a
plain schedule; `ともなると` always implies "and that is saying something".

### ～ともなると vs ～とあれば

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ともなると` = a **level**, and the consequence follows
naturally. `とあれば` = a **circumstance granted**, and the consequence is
something the speaker decides to do.

</div>

<pre>
新人(しんじん)の指導(しどう)<b>とあれば</b>、喜(よろこ)んで引(ひ)き受(う)けます。
If it's a matter of training the new intake, I'll gladly take it on.
</pre>

<pre>
新人(しんじん)の指導(しどう)を任(まか)される立場(たちば)<b>ともなると</b>、自分(じぶん)の仕事(しごと)は後回(あとまわ)しになる。
Once you're in the position of training the new intake, your own work goes to the back of the queue.
</pre>

The `とあれば` sentence is a personal undertaking made on one occasion. The
`ともなると` sentence is a description of what the role does to anybody who holds
it. Volition belongs to the first; inevitability to the second.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 来週(らいしゅう)の水曜日(すいようび)<b>ともなると</b>、会議(かいぎ)がある。 → ✅ 来週(らいしゅう)の水曜日(すいようび)<b>には</b>、会議(かいぎ)がある。
  A particular date is not a level on a scale. The pattern needs a stage that
  things reach, not a square on the calendar.

- ❌ 社会人(しゃかいじん)<b>ともなると</b>、時間(じかん)を守(まも)ってください。 → ✅ 社会人(しゃかいじん)<b>ともなると</b>、時間(じかん)に厳(きび)しくなるものだ。
  The second half has to be a generalisation. A request or an order cannot follow
  `ともなると`, because the pattern is describing what is so, not telling anyone
  what to do.

- ❌ 大学生(だいがくせい)に<b>ともなれば</b> → ✅ 大学生(だいがくせい)<b>ともなれば</b>
  No `に`. Learners insert one by analogy with
  <code>大学生(だいがくせい)になれば</code>, but the `と` here already fills
  that slot.

- ❌ 昨夜(さくや)の決勝(けっしょう)<b>ともなると</b>、会場(かいじょう)は満員(まんいん)だった。 → ✅ 決勝(けっしょう)<b>ともなると</b>、会場(かいじょう)は満員(まんいん)になる。
  The pattern reports what is typical, so a single completed event in the past
  clashes with it. Drop the date and make the verb non-past and it works.

## Practice

**1.** 師走(しわす)____、商店街(しょうてんがい)は夜(よる)まで人(ひと)が絶(た)えない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

師走(しわす)<b>ともなると</b>（or ともなれば）

December is a stage the year reaches, and the crowded shopping street is what
always comes with it. The bare noun attaches directly, with no `に` and no `だ`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 来月(らいげつ)の引(ひ)っ越(こ)し（となると・ともなると）、荷物(にもつ)をどうするか決(き)めないといけない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

引(ひ)っ越(こ)し<b>となると</b>

This is simply the topic coming up for discussion - there is no scale on which
"next month's move" counts as a high point. `ともなると` would suggest moves come
in sizes and this one is at the impressive end, which the rest of the sentence does
not support.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Once you are the head of a household, you can't just spend money as you please."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

一家(いっか)の主(あるじ)<b>ともなれば</b>、好(す)きなように金(かね)を使(つか)うわけにはいかない。

A status noun plus `ともなれば`, and the main clause is a general consequence of
holding that status. <code>わけにはいかない</code> is one of the endings this
pattern most often takes.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 一流(いちりゅう)のホテルにともなると、タオル一枚(いちまい)も質(しつ)が違(ちが)います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 一流(いちりゅう)のホテル<b>ともなると</b>、タオル一枚(いちまい)も質(しつ)が違(ちが)います。

Delete the `に`. The pattern is <code>Noun + ともなると</code>; the `に` of
<code>になると</code> has no place once `と` is there.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 部長(ぶちょう)<b>になると</b>、会議(かいぎ)が増(ふ)える。
(b) 部長(ぶちょう)<b>ともなると</b>、会議(かいぎ)が増(ふ)える。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a flat statement of what changes at that rank. It could come from an HR
briefing and carries no attitude at all.

(b) presents the rank as a considerable one and the meetings as what you would
expect at that height - "at *that* level, naturally". Depending on the delivery it
can read as respect or as a light dig, but it is never neutral the way (a) is.

</details>

# さもないと

**Meaning** · otherwise; or else; if you don't - looks back at an instruction or a condition and names the bad thing that follows if it is not met

Pull it apart and it is transparent: `さ` is the old "so, that way" that survives in
そう and さよう, `も` is emphatic, `ない` negates, `と` makes the conditional. "And
if that is **not** how it goes ~". Everything after it is unwelcome; that is not a
tendency but a fixed part of the word.

## Usage

<code>さもないと</code> is a conjunctive adverb. It opens the clause that carries
the consequence, either after a full stop or after a comma, and it never changes
form and never attaches to anything.

What must come **before** it is a directive or a requirement: an imperative, a
request, a <code>べきだ</code>, a <code>なければならない</code>, a
<code>ほうがいい</code>, or a suggestion with <code>よう</code>. The word is
pointing back at a course of action and saying "take it".

What must come **after** it is an outcome the listener would rather avoid, and it
is usually non-past: <code>遅(おく)れる</code>,
<code>失(うしな)う</code>, <code>無効(むこう)になる</code>,
<code>…ことになる</code>, <code>…てしまう</code>.

It has two sisters with the same meaning and a different weight:

- <code>さもないと</code> - the plainest of the three, and the only one you might
  hear said aloud.
- <code>さもなければ</code> - the standard written form; commonest in print.
- <code>さもなくば</code> - old and literary. Fiction, slogans, translated
  dialogue. Do not put it in an email.

<div class="note">

**Note** - `さも` also lives in a completely separate expression,
<code>さも…そうに</code> / <code>さも…ように</code>, meaning "just as if,
for all the world like": <code>さも当然(とうぜん)のように席(せき)に座(すわ)った</code>.
Same little `さ`, unrelated job. Do not read one as the other.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
早(はや)く出(で)よう。<b>さもないと</b>終電(しゅうでん)に間(ま)に合(あ)わない。
Let's leave now. Otherwise we won't make the last train.
</pre>

<pre>
傘(かさ)を持(も)って行(い)きなさい。<b>さもないと</b>ずぶ濡(ぬ)れになるよ。
Take an umbrella. If you don't you'll get soaked.
</pre>

<pre>
期日(きじつ)までにご連絡(れんらく)ください。<b>さもないと</b>お申(もう)し込(こ)みは無効(むこう)となります。
Please contact us by the closing date; failing that, your application will be void.
</pre>

<pre>
今(いま)のうちに設備(せつび)を更新(こうしん)すべきだ。<b>さもないと</b>、数年後(すうねんご)には同業(どうぎょう)他社(たしゃ)に太刀打(たちう)ちできなくなる。
We should replace the equipment while we still can. Otherwise, in a few years we will be in no position to compete.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>さもないと</code> is **formal and written-leaning**, and it is worth being
blunt about that, because its English translation is entirely ordinary. "Otherwise"
is neutral in English; <code>さもないと</code> is not neutral in Japanese. Its home
turf is notices, terms and conditions, safety warnings, editorials, subtitles and
dubbed dialogue, and the kind of speech that has been prepared in advance. A parent
or a boss may say it in earnest, and it lands as a genuine ultimatum.

In everyday conversation the same job is done by <code>そうしないと</code>,
<code>でないと</code>, <code>じゃないと</code> or by hanging `ないと` straight off
the verb. A friend telling you to hurry says
「早(はや)く行(い)かないと遅(おく)れるよ」, not
「早(はや)く行(い)きなさい。さもないと…」.

Two consequences follow from the register. First, because it is stern, it carries
an implicit threat when the speaker controls the outcome: 「静(しず)かにしろ。<b>さもないと</b>出(で)て行(い)ってもらう」
is a warning, not advice. Second, in fiction it is a stock marker of the villain,
the drill sergeant and the ultimatum, so overusing it in ordinary writing makes the
prose sound like a translation.

## Contrast

### さもないと vs そうしないと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register.
<code>そうしないと</code> is what people actually say; <code>さもないと</code> is
what gets written down.

</div>

<pre>
薬(くすり)は毎日(まいにち)飲(の)んでね。<b>そうしないと</b>効(き)かないよ。
Take the medicine every day, all right? It won't work otherwise.
</pre>

<pre>
服用(ふくよう)は毎日(まいにち)決(き)まった時間(じかん)に。<b>さもないと</b>十分(じゅうぶん)な効果(こうか)が得(え)られません。
Take the dose at the same time each day; otherwise the full effect will not be obtained.
</pre>

There is one grammatical difference behind the register gap.
<code>そうしないと</code> contains する, so it needs the preceding clause to be an
**action**. <code>さもないと</code> has no verb in it and can follow a state as
well: 「室温(しつおん)は二十度(にじゅうど)以下(いか)でなければならない。<b>さもないと</b>機械(きかい)が止(と)まる」.

### さもないと vs さもなければ・さもなくば

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one meaning, three heights.
<code>さもないと</code> < <code>さもなければ</code> < <code>さもなくば</code>,
from merely formal to frankly literary.

</div>

<pre>
全員(ぜんいん)の同意(どうい)が要(い)る。<b>さもないと</b>話(はなし)が前(まえ)に進(すす)まない。
We need everyone to agree. Otherwise the discussion goes nowhere.
</pre>

<pre>
全員(ぜんいん)の同意(どうい)を得(え)なければならない。<b>さもなければ</b>、計画(けいかく)は白紙(はくし)に戻(もど)る。
Unanimous agreement must be obtained; failing that, the plan goes back to square one.
</pre>

<code>さもなくば</code> belongs with it, one step further up:
<code>降伏(こうふく)せよ、さもなくば滅(ほろ)びる</code> reads as a line from
a historical drama, which is where you will meet it. Recognise it; do not write it.

### さもないと vs ～ないと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>さもないと</code> refers back to a **whole preceding
sentence**. Plain `ないと` negates **one named verb** inside its own sentence.

</div>

<pre>
急(いそ)が<b>ないと</b>遅(おく)れるよ。
You'll be late if you don't hurry.
</pre>

<pre>
急(いそ)いだ方(ほう)がいい。<b>さもないと</b>遅(おく)れるよ。
You'd better hurry. Otherwise you'll be late.
</pre>

The two are not interchangeable in shape: `ないと` is a suffix and needs a verb
stem in front of it, while <code>さもないと</code> is a free word and needs a
sentence behind it. Trying to use the second as a suffix -
❌<code>急(いそ)ぐさもないと</code> - is the single most common error with it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 毎日(まいにち)練習(れんしゅう)しなさい。<b>さもないと</b>上手(じょうず)になりますよ。 → ✅ 毎日(まいにち)練習(れんしゅう)しなさい。<b>さもないと</b>上手(じょうず)になりませんよ。
  What follows <code>さもないと</code> has to be bad news. A desirable outcome
  contradicts the word itself.

- ❌ 走(はし)る<b>さもないと</b>遅(おく)れる。 → ✅ 走(はし)ろう。<b>さもないと</b>遅(おく)れる。
  It is an adverb, not a suffix. Finish the first sentence, then start the next one
  with it.

- ❌ この部屋(へや)は狭(せま)い。<b>さもないと</b>問題(もんだい)はない。 → ✅ この部屋(へや)は狭(せま)い。<b>それ以外(いがい)に</b>問題(もんだい)はない。
  English "otherwise" has a second sense, "in other respects", and
  <code>さもないと</code> does not share it. Use
  <code>それ以外(いがい)は</code> or <code>その他(ほか)は</code>.

- ❌ 昨日(きのう)は雨(あめ)だった。<b>さもないと</b>試合(しあい)があった。 → ✅ 昨日(きのう)は雨(あめ)だった。雨(あめ)でなければ試合(しあい)があったはずだ。
  <code>さもないと</code> needs a directive in front of it and points forward to a
  consequence. It cannot rewrite the past as a counterfactual.

## Practice

**1.** 荷物(にもつ)から目(め)を離(はな)さないでください。____盗(ぬす)まれても責任(せきにん)は負(お)いかねます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>さもないと</b>

The first sentence is an instruction and the second is an unwelcome consequence of
ignoring it - exactly the frame this word sits in. The formal
<code>負(お)いかねます</code> at the end confirms that the notice register fits.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 「ちょっと、早(はや)く準備(じゅんび)して。（さもないと・そうしないと）置(お)いて行(い)くよ」と妹(いもうと)に言(い)った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>そうしないと</b>

The frame is a line spoken casually to a younger sister, and
<code>さもないと</code> would sound as though she were being read the terms of a
contract. Between family members the ordinary spoken forms
<code>そうしないと</code>, <code>でないと</code> and plain `ないと` are what get
used.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 時間(じかん)を守(まも)ってください。<b>さもないと</b>参加(さんか)をお断(ことわ)りします。
(b) 時間(じかん)を守(まも)ってくださいね。守(まも)らないと参加(さんか)できなくなっちゃいますよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a rule being stated. The register is that of a printed notice or an official
speaking on the record, and <code>お断(ことわ)りします</code> makes the
consequence a decision the speaker will take. It is close to an ultimatum.

(b) is the same information delivered as a friendly warning. The `ね` and `よ`, the
contraction <code>ちゃいます</code>, and the potential
<code>参加(さんか)できなくなる</code> all shift the blame onto circumstances
rather than the speaker. Nobody would feel threatened by it.

</details>

# ～ほうがましだ

**Meaning** · I'd sooner ~; ~ is the lesser evil - of two unattractive options, this one is less bad

The whole page hangs on one word. <code>まし</code> does not mean "good"; it means
"less objectionable". A sentence with <code>ほうがましだ</code> in it is therefore
never a recommendation and never a compliment - it is a grudging choice between
things the speaker dislikes, and the option not chosen is being called worse.

## Formation

<code>まし</code> is a な-adjective, so it takes `だ` / `です` and modifies nouns
with `な` (<code>ましな案(あん)</code>). The clause in front of
<code>ほう</code> is in noun-modifying shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (non-past) | plain + ほうがましだ | 歩(ある)く → 歩(ある)くほうがましだ |
| Verb (negative) | ないほうがましだ | 行(い)かない → 行(い)かないほうがましだ |
| Verb (past) | たほうがましだ | 断(ことわ)った → 断(ことわ)ったほうがましだ |
| い-adjective | plain + ほうがましだ | 安(やす)い → 安(やす)いほうがましだ |
| な-adjective | ～な + ほうがましだ | 不便(ふべん)な → 不便(ふべん)なほうがましだ |
| Noun | ～の + ほうがましだ | 徒歩(とほ) → 徒歩(とほ)のほうがましだ |
| Polite | ～ほうがましです | 待(ま)つほうがましです |
| Standard frame | ～くらいなら + ～ほうがましだ | 謝(あやま)るくらいなら黙(だま)っているほうがましだ |
| With まだ | ～ほうがまだましだ | 歩(ある)くほうがまだましだ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>まし</code> is written 増(ま)し in kanji, from
増(ま)す "to increase", but in this expression it is written in kana virtually
without exception. Writing it as 増(ま)し looks archaic.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
こんな雨(あめ)の中(なか)を歩(ある)くくらいなら、タクシーの<b>ほうがましだ</b>。
Rather than walk in rain like this, I'd sooner take a taxi.
</pre>

<pre>
まずい料理(りょうり)を食(た)べるより、何(なに)も食(た)べない<b>ほうがましだ</b>。
Better to eat nothing at all than to eat bad food.
</pre>

<pre>
あんな人(ひと)に頭(あたま)を下(さ)げるくらいなら、辞(や)めた<b>ほうがましだ</b>。
If it means bowing to a man like that, I'd rather quit.
</pre>

<pre>
待(ま)たされるだけなら、自分(じぶん)でやってしまう<b>ほうがましです</b>。
If all I'm going to do is be kept waiting, I'd sooner just do it myself.
</pre>

<pre>
中途半端(ちゅうとはんぱ)に謝(あやま)られるより、黙(だま)っていてくれた<b>ほうがまだましだ</b>。
A half-hearted apology is worse than nothing; I'd rather he had just said nothing.
</pre>

<pre>
残業代(ざんぎょうだい)も出(で)ないのなら、給料(きゅうりょう)が安(やす)くても定時(ていじ)で帰(かえ)れる職場(しょくば)の<b>ほうがましだ</b>。
If they don't even pay overtime, I'd take a lower salary and getting out on time.
</pre>

<pre>
嘘(うそ)をついて信用(しんよう)を失(うしな)うくらいなら、正直(しょうじき)に話(はな)して叱(しか)られる<b>ほうがましだろう</b>。
Rather than lie and lose people's trust, you're better off owning up and taking the telling-off.
</pre>

<pre>
設備(せつび)が古(ふる)いのは承知(しょうち)しているが、使(つか)い方(かた)も分(わ)からない新型(しんがた)を入(い)れられるより、この機械(きかい)のままの<b>ほうがまだましだ</b>。
I know the equipment is old, but I'd still rather keep this machine than have some new model dumped on us that nobody knows how to operate.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The form itself is register-neutral - <code>ほうがましです</code> is perfectly
usable in polite speech - but the **attitude** never is. Every use of the pattern
is a complaint about the alternative, and often about the situation that forces the
choice. That is why it turns up so much in grumbling, in arguing a point, and in
reluctant decisions, and why it is out of place in a recommendation.

Be careful with it about people and about things somebody owns. 「前(まえ)の店長(てんちょう)の<b>ほうがましだった</b>」
does not mean the previous manager was good; it means both were poor and this one
is worse. Said to the wrong person that is a serious insult, and it is meant to be.

Three details worth having:

- <code>くらいなら</code> is the pattern's usual partner:
  <code>Aくらいなら、Bほうがましだ</code>. It marks A as the option being rejected
  and carries the same distaste, so the two reinforce each other.
- <code>まだ</code> is often inserted - <code>まだましだ</code> - meaning "at
  least comparatively". It softens nothing; it simply admits that the chosen option
  is not good either.
- In casual speech the pattern is a workhorse of hyperbole:
  <code>そんな恥(は)ずかしい思(おも)いをするくらいなら死(し)んだほうがましだ</code>.
  Extremely common, and understood as exaggeration - but exaggeration about death
  lands badly with some listeners, so recognise it rather than reach for it.

## Contrast

### ～ほうがましだ vs ～ほうがいい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ほうがいい</code> says the option is **good**, and is
advice. <code>ほうがましだ</code> says the option is **less bad**, and is a
verdict on a poor situation.

</div>

<pre>
風邪(かぜ)のときは早(はや)く寝(ね)た<b>ほうがいい</b>よ。
When you've got a cold you should get to bed early.
</pre>

<pre>
熱(ねつ)があるのに働(はたら)かされるくらいなら、休(やす)んで給料(きゅうりょう)を引(ひ)かれる<b>ほうがましだ</b>。
If the alternative is being made to work with a fever, I'd sooner take the day and lose the pay.
</pre>

Give the second sentence to someone as advice and it fails, because
<code>まし</code> concedes that the recommended course is also unpleasant. The
practical test: if you would be happy for the listener to end up in the situation
you are describing, you want <code>ほうがいい</code>.

### ～ほうがましだ vs むしろ～

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>むしろ</code> reverses an expectation: "if anything, the
opposite". <code>ほうがましだ</code> compares two bad options and grades them.

</div>

<pre>
静(しず)かな部屋(へや)より、<b>むしろ</b>少(すこ)し音(おと)があるほうが集中(しゅうちゅう)できる。
I actually concentrate better with a bit of noise than in a silent room.
</pre>

<pre>
工事(こうじ)の音(おと)を一日中(いちにちじゅう)聞(き)かされるくらいなら、外(そと)のカフェで働(はたら)く<b>ほうがましだ</b>。
Rather than listen to building work all day, I'd sooner go and work in a café.
</pre>

The <code>むしろ</code> sentence is content: a bit of noise is genuinely what the
speaker prefers. The <code>まし</code> sentence is not: the café is a retreat.
They combine happily - <code>むしろ…ほうがましだ</code> - but only when the
"opposite" being preferred is itself unwelcome.

### ～ほうがましだ vs ～に越(こ)したことはない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に越(こ)したことはない</code> names the **best**
outcome: nothing beats it. <code>ほうがましだ</code> names the **least bad** one.

</div>

<pre>
準備(じゅんび)は早(はや)めにする<b>に越(こ)したことはない</b>。
There's nothing to beat getting your preparation done early.
</pre>

<pre>
直前(ちょくぜん)になって慌(あわ)てるくらいなら、不完全(ふかんぜん)でも早(はや)く出(だ)す<b>ほうがましだ</b>。
Rather than panic at the last minute, I'd sooner hand it in early and imperfect.
</pre>

Both are about doing something early, and they judge it in opposite directions. The
first is an ideal; the second is damage limitation. Note also that
<code>に越(こ)したことはない</code> is a general maxim and takes no specific
subject, while <code>ほうがましだ</code> is nearly always the speaker's own
choice in a concrete bind.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ こちらの商品(しょうひん)の<b>ほうがましです</b>よ。（to a customer） → ✅ こちらの商品(しょうひん)の<b>ほうがおすすめです</b>よ。
  Recommending something with <code>まし</code> tells the customer that everything
  on the shelf is disappointing and this is the least disappointing. It is the
  commonest way learners cause offence with this pattern.

- ❌ 静(しず)か<b>ほうがましだ</b> → ✅ 静(しず)か<b>なほうがましだ</b>
  <code>ほう</code> is a noun, so a な-adjective needs its `な`.

- ❌ 電車(でんしゃ)<b>ほうがましだ</b> → ✅ 電車(でんしゃ)<b>のほうがましだ</b>
  A plain noun links to <code>ほう</code> with `の`.

- ❌ 今日(きょう)は天気(てんき)がいいから、散歩(さんぽ)する<b>ほうがましだ</b>。 → ✅ 今日(きょう)は天気(てんき)がいいから、散歩(さんぽ)する<b>ほうがいい</b>。
  Good weather and a walk are not two evils. With nothing bad to compare against,
  <code>まし</code> has no work to do and the sentence sounds sour for no reason.

## Practice

**1.** こんな安(やす)い給料(きゅうりょう)で働(はたら)かされるくらいなら、辞(や)めて職(しょく)を探(さが)す____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

探(さが)す<b>ほうがましだ</b>

The <code>くらいなら</code> clause has already marked the current job as
intolerable, and job-hunting is not exactly pleasant either - two bad options, so
<code>まし</code> is the fit. The verb attaches in plain non-past form.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 夜(よる)よく眠(ねむ)れないなら、寝(ね)る前(まえ)にスマホを見(み)ない（ほうがいい・ほうがましだ）ですよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

見(み)ない<b>ほうがいい</b>ですよ

This is advice, and the outcome being recommended - sleeping properly - is simply
good. <code>ほうがましだ</code> would imply that putting the phone down is itself
a sacrifice the listener will regret, which is not what a piece of health advice
means to say.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Rather than go to a party where I don't know anyone, I'd sooner stay home on my own."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

知(し)り合(あ)いが一人(ひとり)もいないパーティーに行(い)くくらいなら、一人(ひとり)で家(いえ)にいる<b>ほうがましだ</b>。

"Rather than" plus a distasteful option is <code>くらいなら</code>, and the
speaker's choice is not being called enjoyable either - a lonely evening at home
is only the lesser evil. That is precisely <code>まし</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: あんな派手(はで)な服(ふく)を着(き)せられるくらいなら、地味(じみ)ほうがましだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ あんな派手(はで)な服(ふく)を着(き)せられるくらいなら、地味(じみ)<b>なほうがましだ</b>。

<code>ほう</code> is a noun, and 地味(じみ) is a な-adjective, so the `な` is
required. The meaning is right otherwise: neither loud clothes nor dull ones
please the speaker, and dull is merely the lesser evil.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この案(あん)の<b>ほうがいいと思(おも)います</b>。
(b) この案(あん)の<b>ほうがまだましだと思(おも)います</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) backs the proposal. The speaker thinks it is a good plan and is putting their
name to it.

(b) backs nothing. It says the other proposals are worse and this one is the least
harmful of a poor set, with <code>まだ</code> underlining that even this one is not
liked. In a meeting (b) is a criticism of the whole agenda, and everyone present
will hear it that way.

</details>

# ～いかんでは・～いかんによっては

**Meaning** · depending on how ~ turns out; according to the state of ~ - the outcome named in the second half is one possibility among several

<code>いかん</code> is the noun 如何(いかん), "how a thing stands, how it turns
out". <code>結果(けっか)のいかん</code> is therefore "the how-it-turns-out of
the result", and <code>いかんでは</code> hands the second half of the sentence over
to it. The `は` matters: it singles out *some* states of affairs out of all the
possible ones, so the main clause is always a **contingency**, never a certainty.

## Formation

<code>いかん</code> is a noun, so it takes `の` after the noun it depends on -
though in set phrases the `の` is often dropped.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + の + いかんでは | 結果(けっか) → 結果(けっか)のいかんでは |
| Noun (の dropped) | Noun + いかんでは | 事情(じじょう) → 事情(じじょう)いかんでは |
| Longer variant | Noun + の + いかんによっては | 成績(せいせき) → 成績(せいせき)のいかんによっては |
| Neutral, no は | ～いかんで / ～いかんによって | 天候(てんこう)のいかんで |
| Sentence-final | ～いかんだ | すべては本人(ほんにん)のやる気(き)のいかんだ |
| Opposite sense | ～いかんによらず / ～いかんを問(と)わず | 理由(りゆう)のいかんによらず |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the kanji 如何(いかん) is the same one as in
<code>如何(いかが)ですか</code>, read differently. In this pattern it is
written in kana almost without exception. Do not confuse it with the homophone
遺憾(いかん) "regrettable", which turns up in the same formal documents:
<code>遺憾(いかん)に存(ぞん)じます</code> is an apology, not a condition.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
結果(けっか)<b>のいかんでは</b>、計画(けいかく)を見直(みなお)すこともある。
Depending on the result, the plan may have to be revised.
</pre>

<pre>
天候(てんこう)<b>のいかんによっては</b>、出発(しゅっぱつ)を遅(おく)らせます。
Depending on the weather, we will delay the departure.
</pre>

<pre>
検査(けんさ)結果(けっか)<b>のいかんによっては</b>、入院(にゅういん)していただく場合(ばあい)がございます。
Depending on the test results, we may need to admit you to hospital.
</pre>

<pre>
事情(じじょう)<b>いかんでは</b>、受験料(じゅけんりょう)を返還(へんかん)することがあります。
In certain circumstances the examination fee may be refunded.
</pre>

<pre>
交渉(こうしょう)<b>のいかんによっては</b>、工場(こうじょう)の閉鎖(へいさ)もあり得(う)る。
Depending on how the negotiations go, closing the plant is a real possibility.
</pre>

<pre>
世論(よろん)の動向(どうこう)<b>のいかんでは</b>、法案(ほうあん)は来年(らいねん)に持(も)ち越(こ)される可能性(かのうせい)が高(たか)い。
Depending on which way public opinion moves, the bill is likely to be carried over to next year.
</pre>

<pre>
ご提出(ていしゅつ)いただいた資料(しりょう)の内容(ないよう)<b>のいかんによっては</b>、追加(ついか)の説明(せつめい)を求(もと)めることがございます。
Depending on the content of the materials you submit, we may ask for further explanation.
</pre>

<pre>
今後(こんご)の為替(かわせ)の動(うご)き<b>いかんでは</b>、来期(らいき)の業績(ぎょうせき)予想(よそう)を下方(かほう)修正(しゅうせい)せざるを得(え)ない。
Depending on how exchange rates move from here, we may have no choice but to revise next term's forecast downwards.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is **hard written Japanese**. Contracts, terms of use, school regulations,
public notices, medical consent forms, annual reports, and the spoken registers
that read from such documents - an announcement, a press conference, a formal
briefing. Nobody uses it deciding where to have lunch.

The everyday equivalent is <code>次第(しだい)では</code>, and one step below
that <code>によっては</code>. Substituting either into any of the examples above
costs nothing but formality:
<code>天候(てんこう)次第(しだい)では出発(しゅっぱつ)を遅(おく)らせます</code>
is the same announcement in ordinary Japanese.

Two habits go with the register. First, the main clause is nearly always hedged -
<code>…こともある</code>, <code>…場合(ばあい)がございます</code>,
<code>…かねません</code>, <code>あり得(う)る</code>,
<code>…可能性(かのうせい)がある</code> - because the whole point is that the
outcome is conditional. Second, the noun in front is usually an abstract one whose
state is not yet settled: 結果(けっか), 事情(じじょう), 状況(じょうきょう),
内容(ないよう), 成績(せいせき), 天候(てんこう), 回答(かいとう).

Drop the `は` and you get <code>いかんで</code>, which is not a contingency but a
dependency: <code>成否(せいひ)は準備(じゅんび)のいかんで決(き)まる</code>,
"success is determined by the preparation". Same noun, slightly different job -
worth noticing, because the `は` is doing all of it.

## Contrast

### ～いかんでは vs ～次第(しだい)では

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different height.
<code>次第(しだい)では</code> is ordinary Japanese;
<code>いかんでは</code> is document Japanese.

</div>

<pre>
返事(へんじ)<b>次第(しだい)では</b>、こちらも考(かんが)え方(かた)を変(か)えます。
Depending on their answer, we'll change our approach too.
</pre>

<pre>
ご回答(かいとう)<b>のいかんでは</b>、契約(けいやく)を解除(かいじょ)させていただきます。
Depending on your response, we may exercise our right to terminate the contract.
</pre>

There is a small structural difference as well.
<code>次第(しだい)</code> attaches to the bare noun with no `の`, and it also
has a second life meaning "as soon as" with the ます-stem
(<code>届(とど)き次第(しだい)</code>). <code>いかん</code> takes `の` and
has no such second sense.

### ～いかんでは vs ～によっては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>によっては</code> picks out **some members of a set**
("in some cases, with some people"). <code>いかんでは</code> depends on **how one
particular thing turns out**.

</div>

<pre>
人(ひと)<b>によっては</b>、この薬(くすり)で眠(ねむ)くなることがあります。
With some people this medicine causes drowsiness.
</pre>

<pre>
経過(けいか)<b>のいかんでは</b>、薬(くすり)を変(か)えることがあります。
Depending on how you respond, we may change the medication.
</pre>

The first sentence surveys a population; the second watches one unfolding
situation. That is why <code>人(ひと)によっては</code> is natural and
❌<code>人(ひと)のいかんでは</code> is not - a person is not a thing that
"turns out".

### ～いかんでは vs ～いかんによらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one word, two opposite jobs. <code>いかんでは</code> = the
outcome **depends on** it. <code>いかんによらず</code> = the outcome is **not
affected** by it.

</div>

<pre>
理由(りゆう)<b>のいかんでは</b>、再(さい)提出(ていしゅつ)を認(みと)めることがあります。
Depending on the reason given, resubmission may be permitted.
</pre>

<pre>
理由(りゆう)<b>のいかんによらず</b>、一度(いちど)納(おさ)めた費用(ひよう)は返金(へんきん)いたしません。
Fees once paid are not refunded, whatever the reason.
</pre>

These two sit next to each other on real forms, sometimes in adjoining clauses, and
they say opposite things. Read the tail, not the いかん: `では` opens a door,
<code>によらず</code> and <code>を問(と)わず</code> shut it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 結果(けっか)に<b>よるいかんでは</b> → ✅ 結果(けっか)<b>のいかんでは</b>
  <code>いかんによっては</code> already contains よる. Adding another produces a
  doubled construction that native readers stumble over.

- ❌ どうなる<b>いかんでは</b>、中止(ちゅうし)します。 → ✅ 状況(じょうきょう)<b>のいかんでは</b>、中止(ちゅうし)することもあります。
  <code>いかん</code> is a noun and takes a noun in front of it. A verb clause
  cannot attach directly.

- ❌ 天候(てんこう)<b>のいかんでは</b>、昨日(きのう)の試合(しあい)は中止(ちゅうし)になりました。 → ✅ 天候(てんこう)不良(ふりょう)のため、昨日(きのう)の試合(しあい)は中止(ちゅうし)になりました。
  The pattern looks forward to something not yet settled. A completed event has no
  contingency left in it, so the two halves fight each other.

- ❌ お昼(ひる)どうする？ 気分(きぶん)<b>のいかんでは</b>ラーメンかな。 → ✅ お昼(ひる)どうする？ 気分(きぶん)次第(しだい)かな。
  Not a grammar error - a register error, and the kind that gets laughed at. Legal
  language over a lunch decision sounds like a parody of a company announcement.

## Practice

**1.** 工事(こうじ)の進(すす)み具合(ぐあい)____、開店(かいてん)が一(いっ)か月(げつ)遅(おく)れることもございます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

進(すす)み具合(ぐあい)<b>のいかんによっては</b>（or <b>のいかんでは</b>）

The polite <code>ございます</code> and the hedge
<code>…こともございます</code> mark this as an announcement to customers, which is
where this pattern belongs. The noun takes `の` before
<code>いかん</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 店(みせ)（によっては・のいかんでは）、同(おな)じ商品(しょうひん)が半額(はんがく)で売(う)られている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

店(みせ)<b>によっては</b>

The sentence surveys a set of shops and reports that some of them do this.
<code>いかん</code> would mean "depending on how the shop turns out", which is
not what a shop does - and the plain observational ending is far too casual for the
pattern anyway.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Depending on the content of the inquiry, it may take several days for us to reply."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お問(と)い合(あ)わせの内容(ないよう)<b>のいかんによっては</b>、ご返答(へんとう)までに数日(すうじつ)いただく場合(ばあい)がございます。

A standard line from a company contact page, so the formal
<code>のいかんによっては</code> is at home. Note that the main clause is hedged
with <code>場合(ばあい)がございます</code>; a flat
<code>数日(すうじつ)かかります</code> would contradict the contingency.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 審査(しんさ)の結果(けっか)のいかんでは、ご入会(にゅうかい)をお断(ことわ)りします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 審査(しんさ)の結果(けっか)<b>のいかんでは</b>、ご入会(にゅうかい)をお断(ことわ)り<b>する場合(ばあい)がございます</b>。

The grammar of the first half is fine; the second half is the problem. With
<code>いかんでは</code> the outcome is one possibility among several, so a flat
<code>お断(ことわ)りします</code> reads as though everyone will be refused.
Hedging it repairs the sentence.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 準備(じゅんび)<b>のいかんで</b>結果(けっか)が決(き)まる。
(b) 準備(じゅんび)<b>のいかんでは</b>結果(けっか)が変(か)わることもある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) states a dependency as a general law: the result is a function of the
preparation, full stop. Without the `は` the sentence asserts the relationship
itself.

(b) picks out particular states of preparation and says that in those cases things
may go differently. The `は` turns the dependency into a contingency, and the
hedged ending follows from it. (a) is a claim about how the world works; (b) is a
warning about one specific outcome.

</details>

# ～いかんによらず

**Meaning** · regardless of ~; irrespective of ~ - whatever the state of the thing named, the rule that follows applies unchanged

<code>いかん</code> is the noun 如何(いかん), "how a thing stands", and
<code>によらず</code> is "not depending on". Put together they close a door:
whatever the reason, whatever the result, whatever the weather, this is what
happens. It is the exact mirror of <code>いかんでは</code>, which opens one.

## Formation

<code>いかん</code> is a noun, so it takes `の` after the noun it governs; in
frozen phrases the `の` is often dropped.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + の + いかんによらず | 理由(りゆう) → 理由(りゆう)のいかんによらず |
| Noun (の dropped) | Noun + いかんによらず | 事情(じじょう) → 事情(じじょう)いかんによらず |
| Variant | ～いかんにかかわらず | 結果(けっか)のいかんにかかわらず |
| Variant | ～いかんを問(と)わず | 経験(けいけん)のいかんを問(と)わず |
| Sentence-final | ～いかんによらない | 合否(ごうひ)は年齢(ねんれい)のいかんによらない |
| Opposite sense | ～いかんでは / ～いかんによっては | 理由(りゆう)のいかんでは |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the three variants <code>によらず</code>,
<code>にかかわらず</code> and <code>を問(と)わず</code> are
interchangeable here. <code>を問(と)わず</code> ("without asking about") is
the commonest of the three in job adverts and entry rules;
<code>にかかわらず</code> is commonest in contracts.

</div>

## Examples

These are the environments the pattern actually lives in: rules, clauses, notices
and prepared speech.

<pre>
年齢(ねんれい)<b>のいかんによらず</b>、どなたでもご応募(おうぼ)いただけます。
Applications are welcome from anyone, regardless of age.
</pre>

<pre>
事情(じじょう)<b>のいかんによらず</b>、遅刻(ちこく)は欠席(けっせき)扱(あつか)いとする。
Whatever the circumstances, late arrival shall be treated as absence.
</pre>

<pre>
当日(とうじつ)の天候(てんこう)<b>のいかんによらず</b>、大会(たいかい)は予定(よてい)どおり実施(じっし)いたします。
The event will be held as scheduled regardless of the weather on the day.
</pre>

<pre>
受賞(じゅしょう)<b>のいかんにかかわらず</b>、応募(おうぼ)作品(さくひん)は返却(へんきゃく)いたしません。
Entries will not be returned, whether or not they receive an award.
</pre>

<pre>
国籍(こくせき)や性別(せいべつ)<b>のいかんを問(と)わず</b>、採用(さいよう)の機会(きかい)は平等(びょうどう)に与(あた)えられる。
Employment opportunities are given equally, irrespective of nationality or gender.
</pre>

<pre>
審査(しんさ)結果(けっか)<b>のいかんによらず</b>、選考(せんこう)過程(かてい)に関(かん)するお問(と)い合(あ)わせにはお答(こた)えいたしかねます。
Regardless of the outcome, we are unable to answer enquiries concerning the selection process.
</pre>

<pre>
会員(かいいん)が規約(きやく)に違反(いはん)した場合(ばあい)、当社(とうしゃ)は理由(りゆう)<b>のいかんによらず</b>、事前(じぜん)の通知(つうち)なく利用(りよう)を停止(ていし)することができるものとする。
Where a member breaches these terms, the Company may suspend their use of the service without prior notice and irrespective of the reason.
</pre>

<pre>
勝敗(しょうはい)<b>のいかんによらず</b>、最後(さいご)まで全力(ぜんりょく)を尽(つ)くした選手(せんしゅ)諸君(しょくん)に拍手(はくしゅ)を送(おく)りたい。
Win or lose, I should like us to applaud the players who gave everything to the end.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise this one; do not say it.** <code>いかんによらず</code> belongs to
written documents and to speech that has been written first. You will meet it in
terms and conditions, refund and cancellation policies, school and company
regulations, application guidelines, contracts, official notices, and in the
prepared parts of a ceremonial address. It does not appear in conversation at any
level of politeness, and a learner who produces it at a meeting or in an email will
sound as though they are reading out a clause at somebody.

What to use instead: <code>に関係(かんけい)なく</code> in ordinary speech and
writing, <code>を問(と)わず</code> in notices and adverts, and
<code>…がどうであれ</code> or <code>…に関係(かんけい)なく</code> when the
thing being dismissed is a whole clause. Every one of the examples above can be
rewritten with <code>に関係(かんけい)なく</code> and will lose only its
starch:
<code>年齢(ねんれい)に関係(かんけい)なく、どなたでも応募(おうぼ)できます</code>.

Two further points of recognition. First, the noun in front is nearly always drawn
from a small set - 理由(りゆう), 事情(じじょう), 結果(けっか),
内容(ないよう), 年齢(ねんれい), 経験(けいけん), 天候(てんこう),
勝敗(しょうはい), 合否(ごうひ) - so those collocations are worth reading as
units. Second, the main clause is a **rule or a policy**, and it is stated
flatly, often with the legal endings <code>…ものとする</code>,
<code>…とする</code>, <code>いたしかねます</code> or
<code>…しないものとします</code>. If the second half is a personal preference or
a single event, the pattern has been misused.

The one place you might reasonably say it out loud is the set phrase
<code>勝敗(しょうはい)のいかんによらず</code> or
<code>結果(けっか)のいかんによらず</code> in a speech - a coach, a principal,
a company president addressing a room. Even there, it is a register choice, not a
neutral option.

## Contrast

### ～いかんによらず vs ～に関係(かんけい)なく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning; opposite ends of the register scale.
<code>に関係(かんけい)なく</code> is what you should write and say;
<code>いかんによらず</code> is what you should be able to read.

</div>

<pre>
経験(けいけん)<b>に関係(かんけい)なく</b>、やる気(き)のある人(ひと)なら大歓迎(だいかんげい)です。
Experience aside, anyone with drive is very welcome.
</pre>

<pre>
実務(じつむ)経験(けいけん)<b>のいかんによらず</b>、所定(しょてい)の研修(けんしゅう)を受(う)けるものとする。
Irrespective of practical experience, employees shall undergo the prescribed training.
</pre>

Notice what changes with the register: the second sentence has no speaker in it. It
is a rule that applies to a category of people, and the ending
<code>ものとする</code> confirms it. The first has a human being behind it. When
you are the human being, take the first.

### ～いかんによらず vs ～を問(と)わず・～にかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - these are the same family at slightly lower altitude. Drop
<code>いかん</code> and attach the tail straight to the noun, and you have a form
that is formal but usable.

</div>

<pre>
昼夜(ちゅうや)<b>を問(と)わず</b>、受付(うけつけ)をしております。
We take enquiries day and night.
</pre>

<pre>
昼夜(ちゅうや)のいかん<b>を問(と)わず</b>、警備(けいび)員(いん)を常駐(じょうちゅう)させるものとする。
A guard shall be stationed on site at all times, day or night.
</pre>

<code>昼夜(ちゅうや)を問(と)わず</code> is at home on a shop sign;
<code>昼夜(ちゅうや)のいかんを問(と)わず</code> is at home in the
maintenance contract behind it. Inserting <code>いかん</code> does not change the
meaning at all - it only raises the register, which is a good way to remember what
the word is for.

### ～いかんによらず vs ～いかんによっては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same noun, pointing opposite ways.
<code>によらず</code> = it makes **no difference**.
<code>によっては</code> = it makes **all the difference**.

</div>

<pre>
申請(しんせい)理由(りゆう)<b>のいかんによっては</b>、期限(きげん)の延長(えんちょう)を認(みと)めることがあります。
Depending on the reason given, an extension of the deadline may be granted.
</pre>

<pre>
申請(しんせい)理由(りゆう)<b>のいかんによらず</b>、期限(きげん)の延長(えんちょう)は認(みと)めません。
No extension of the deadline will be granted, whatever the reason given.
</pre>

These two clauses can appear on the same page of the same form, and confusing them
is expensive. The instruction is simple: ignore <code>いかん</code>, which is the
same in both, and read the tail. <code>では</code> and
<code>によっては</code> leave room; <code>によらず</code>,
<code>にかかわらず</code> and <code>を問(と)わず</code> do not.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 理由(りゆう)を<b>いかんによらず</b> → ✅ 理由(りゆう)<b>のいかんによらず</b>
  <code>いかん</code> is a noun modified by another noun, so the link is `の`, or
  nothing at all. `を` belongs only to the variant
  <code>のいかんを問(と)わず</code>, where it is the object of
  問(と)う.

- ❌ どんな理由(りゆう)がある<b>いかんによらず</b> → ✅ どんな理由(りゆう)が<b>あろうと</b>／理由(りゆう)<b>のいかんによらず</b>
  A verb clause cannot attach to <code>いかん</code>. If you want a clause, the
  ordinary options are <code>…であれ</code>, <code>…であろうと</code> or
  <code>…かどうかに関係(かんけい)なく</code>.

- ❌ 「天気(てんき)<b>のいかんによらず</b>、明日(あした)は行(い)くよ」と友(とも)だちに言(い)った。 → ✅ 「天気(てんき)<b>に関係(かんけい)なく</b>、明日(あした)は行(い)くよ」と友(とも)だちに言(い)った。
  Not a grammar error. It is contract language dropped into a conversation with a
  friend, and it will be heard as a joke or as showing off.

- ❌ 値段(ねだん)<b>のいかんによらず</b>、この服(ふく)が欲(ほ)しい。 → ✅ 値段(ねだん)<b>に関係(かんけい)なく</b>、この服(ふく)が欲(ほ)しい。
  The main clause has to be a rule or a policy, not one person's wish. With a
  personal want behind it the formal frame has nothing to enforce.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to read this sentence? 理由(りゆう)のいかんによらず、キャンセル料(りょう)は返金(へんきん)いたしません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In the cancellation policy of a hotel, an airline or a ticket seller - the small
print on a booking page, or the terms attached to a contract.

The giveaways are <code>のいかんによらず</code> itself, the humble
<code>いたしません</code>, and a main clause that states a blanket rule rather
than anything about a particular customer. Nobody says this sentence out loud.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese, keeping the meaning: 経験(けいけん)のいかんによらず、全員(ぜんいん)に研修(けんしゅう)を受(う)けてもらいます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

経験(けいけん)<b>に関係(かんけい)なく</b>、全員(ぜんいん)に研修(けんしゅう)を受(う)けてもらいます。

<code>に関係(かんけい)なく</code> is the everyday equivalent and attaches
straight to the noun with no <code>いかん</code>. In a job advert
<code>経験(けいけん)を問(と)わず</code> would also be idiomatic; in speech,
<code>経験(けいけん)があってもなくても</code>.

</details>

**3.** Choose the form the document needs: 提出(ていしゅつ)書類(しょるい)に不備(ふび)があった場合(ばあい)、事情(じじょう)（のいかんによっては・のいかんによらず）受(う)け付(つ)けないものとする。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

事情(じじょう)<b>のいかんによらず</b>

<code>受(う)け付(つ)けないものとする</code> is an absolute rule with no
exceptions in it, so the clause has to be the closing one.
<code>によっては</code> would promise that some circumstances are accepted, which
directly contradicts the ending.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 当選(とうせん)するいかんによらず、ご応募(おうぼ)いただいた方(かた)全員(ぜんいん)にご連絡(れんらく)いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 当選(とうせん)<b>のいかんによらず</b>、ご応募(おうぼ)いただいた方(かた)全員(ぜんいん)にご連絡(れんらく)いたします。

<code>いかん</code> is a noun and takes a noun in front of it, so the verb
当選(とうせん)する has to become the plain noun 当選(とうせん). Writing
<code>当選(とうせん)したかどうかに関係(かんけい)なく</code> would also fix
it, at a lower register.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference between these two, and say where each belongs:
(a) 理由(りゆう)<b>のいかんによらず</b>、入場(にゅうじょう)をお断(ことわ)りする場合(ばあい)がございます。
(b) 理由(りゆう)<b>に関係(かんけい)なく</b>、入場(にゅうじょう)をお断(ことわ)りすることがあります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The meaning is the same. What differs is the frame around it.

(a) is written to be read: printed on a ticket, posted at an entrance, or set out
in the conditions of admission. The formality of <code>のいかんによらず</code>
matches <code>ございます</code>, and the whole sentence reads as an institution
speaking.

(b) is the same policy explained by a person - a member of staff at the door, or a
line in an ordinary email. It is perfectly polite; it just does not pretend to be a
legal document. For a learner, (b) is the sentence to be able to produce and (a)
the one to be able to read.

</details>

# ～によらず

**Meaning** · regardless of ~; not determined by ~ - the thing named is not what decides the matter

<code>による</code> means "depends on, is due to, is by means of".
<code>によらず</code> is simply its negative, left hanging so the sentence can
continue: "not depending on ~, ...". Which of the three senses of
<code>よる</code> is being negated decides how the phrase reads - most often
"not decided by", sometimes "not by means of", and in one very common set phrase,
"not as it appears".

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + によらず | 年齢(ねんれい) → 年齢(ねんれい)によらず |
| Noun (means) | Noun + によらず | 手術(しゅじゅつ) → 手術(しゅじゅつ)によらず |
| Set phrase | 見(み)かけによらず | 見(み)かけによらず力持(ちからも)ちだ |
| Set phrase | 何事(なにごと)によらず | 何事(なにごと)によらず慎重(しんちょう)だ |
| Sentence-final | ～によらない | 人(ひと)は見(み)かけによらない |
| Formal variant | ～のいかんによらず | 理由(りゆう)のいかんによらず |

<div class="note">

**Note** - no `の` before <code>によらず</code>. The `の` belongs to
<code>いかん</code>, which is a noun: <code>理由(りゆう)のいかんによらず</code>
but <code>理由(りゆう)によらず</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
人(ひと)は<b>見(み)かけによらない</b>ものだ。
People aren't what they look like.
</pre>

<pre>
彼(かれ)は<b>見(み)かけによらず</b>力持(ちからも)ちだ。
He's a lot stronger than he looks.
</pre>

<pre>
年齢(ねんれい)<b>によらず</b>、どなたでもご参加(さんか)いただけます。
Anyone may take part, whatever their age.
</pre>

<pre>
この奨学金(しょうがくきん)は国籍(こくせき)<b>によらず</b>申(もう)し込(こ)むことができる。
This scholarship is open to applicants of any nationality.
</pre>

<pre>
何事(なにごと)<b>によらず</b>、下調(したしら)べをしておくに越(こ)したことはない。
Whatever the matter in hand, there is nothing to beat doing your homework first.
</pre>

<pre>
当店(とうてん)は天候(てんこう)<b>によらず</b>、年中(ねんじゅう)無休(むきゅう)で営業(えいぎょう)しております。
We are open all year round whatever the weather.
</pre>

<pre>
彼女(かのじょ)は相手(あいて)の肩書(かたが)き<b>によらず</b>、誰(だれ)に対(たい)しても同(おな)じ態度(たいど)で接(せっ)する。
She treats everybody the same way, no matter what their title happens to be.
</pre>

<pre>
新(あたら)しい治療法(ちりょうほう)は、手術(しゅじゅつ)<b>によらず</b>薬(くすり)だけで症状(しょうじょう)を抑(おさ)えられる点(てん)が画期的(かっきてき)だ。
What is groundbreaking about the new treatment is that it controls the symptoms with drugs alone, without surgery.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>によらず</code> is a **written form** for the "regardless of" sense. It is
the ordinary register of notices, adverts, prospectuses, company statements and
reports, and it is comfortable in prepared speech. It is a step milder than
<code>のいかんによらず</code>, which is document language proper, and a step
stiffer than <code>に関係(かんけい)なく</code>, which is what you would say to
a colleague.

The set phrase <code>見(み)かけによらず</code> is the exception and belongs to
everyday speech, so it is worth separating out. It does **not** mean "regardless of
appearance". It means "contrary to appearances", and it always introduces something
surprising about the person or thing described:
<code>見(み)かけによらず辛(から)いですよ</code>, said of a mild-looking
curry. The proverb form is <code>人(ひと)は見(み)かけによらない</code> or,
more old-fashioned, <code>人(ひと)は見(み)かけによらぬもの</code>. Note
that it is a comment on somebody, and a fairly personal one, so it is friendly
between equals and rude upwards.

The "not by means of" sense - <code>力(ちから)によらず</code>,
<code>手術(しゅじゅつ)によらず</code>,
<code>従来(じゅうらい)の方法(ほうほう)によらず</code> - is the most formal
of the three and the rarest in speech. It reads as technical or literary, and
often has a `で` phrase after it naming the means actually used.

<code>何事(なにごと)によらず</code> is a small fixed adverbial: "in anything
you care to name". Slightly old-fashioned in flavour, common in the mouths of older
speakers and in essays.

## Contrast

### ～によらず vs ～にかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>によらず</code> takes a noun naming a **criterion**
that might have decided the matter. <code>にかかわらず</code> is happiest with a
**pair or a yes-or-no**, and can take a clause.

</div>

<pre>
出席(しゅっせき)するしない<b>にかかわらず</b>、返信(へんしん)をお願(ねが)いします。
Please reply whether or not you plan to attend.
</pre>

<pre>
経験(けいけん)の有無(うむ)<b>にかかわらず</b>、研修(けんしゅう)は全員(ぜんいん)受(う)けていただきます。
Everyone takes the training, experienced or not.
</pre>

<pre>
成績(せいせき)<b>によらず</b>、全員(ぜんいん)に修了証(しゅうりょうしょう)が渡(わた)される。
Every participant receives a certificate, whatever their marks.
</pre>

<code>成績(せいせき)にかかわらず</code> is also possible; what does not work
is the reverse - ❌<code>出席(しゅっせき)するしないによらず</code> is
awkward, because <code>によらず</code> wants a single noun standing for a scale.
Where a scale exists (marks, age, weather, price), take
<code>によらず</code>; where the choice is binary, take
<code>にかかわらず</code>.

### ～によらず vs ～を問(と)わず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を問(と)わず</code> means "we do not **ask** about
it", so it fits eligibility and categories. <code>によらず</code> means "it is
not what **decides** the outcome", so it fits how a judgement is made.

</div>

<pre>
学歴(がくれき)・経験(けいけん)<b>を問(と)わず</b>、やる気(き)のある方(かた)を募集(ぼしゅう)します。
Education and experience immaterial - we are looking for motivated people.
</pre>

<pre>
採用(さいよう)は学歴(がくれき)<b>によらず</b>、面接(めんせつ)での受(う)け答(こた)えで判断(はんだん)します。
Hiring is not based on educational background; we judge on how candidates answer at interview.
</pre>

The first sentence sets the entry conditions. The second explains the basis of the
decision, and it is followed by what the basis actually *is* - a very common shape
for <code>によらず</code>, and one <code>を問(と)わず</code> does not take
naturally.

### ～によらず vs ～によっては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same verb <code>よる</code>, opposite polarity.
<code>によっては</code> = **in some cases it does** decide.
<code>によらず</code> = it does **not** decide, in any case.

</div>

<pre>
季節(きせつ)<b>によっては</b>、この道(みち)は通(とお)れないことがある。
At some times of year this road is impassable.
</pre>

<pre>
季節(きせつ)<b>によらず</b>、この道(みち)は一年中(いちねんじゅう)通(とお)れる。
The road is open all year round, whatever the season.
</pre>

Learning them as a pair is the efficient move, because the noun in front is
identical and only the tail changes. If you can hear the difference between "some
seasons" and "no season makes a difference", the whole family - including
<code>のいかんでは</code> and <code>のいかんによらず</code> - follows the same
split.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 参加(さんか)する<b>によらず</b> → ✅ 参加(さんか)する<b>かどうかにかかわらず</b>
  <code>によらず</code> takes a noun. For a clause, use
  <code>かどうかにかかわらず</code> or
  <code>かどうかに関係(かんけい)なく</code>.

- ❌ 見(み)かけ<b>によらず</b>採用(さいよう)します。 → ✅ 外見(がいけん)<b>に関係(かんけい)なく</b>採用(さいよう)します。
  <code>見(み)かけによらず</code> is frozen with the meaning "contrary to
  appearances". Used for "we don't care what you look like" it says something quite
  different, and a little strange.

- ❌ 成績(せいせき)<b>のによらず</b> → ✅ 成績(せいせき)<b>によらず</b>
  No `の`. Learners add one after meeting
  <code>のいかんによらず</code>, where the `の` is there because
  <code>いかん</code> is a noun.

- ❌ 雨(あめ)<b>によらず</b>、試合(しあい)は行(おこな)われる。 → ✅ 天候(てんこう)<b>によらず</b>、試合(しあい)は行(おこな)われる。
  The noun has to name a variable - something with a range of possible values.
  雨(あめ) is one particular condition, not a scale, so the natural forms are
  <code>雨(あめ)でも</code> or <code>雨天(うてん)にかかわらず</code>.

## Practice

**1.** この図書館(としょかん)は市内(しない)にお住(す)まいかどうか____、どなたでもご利用(りよう)になれます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

かどうか<b>に関係(かんけい)なく</b>（or かどうか<b>にかかわらず</b>）

The trap is that the blank follows a <code>かどうか</code> clause, and
<code>によらず</code> does not attach to one. With a yes-or-no in front, the
natural tails are <code>にかかわらず</code> and
<code>に関係(かんけい)なく</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 給料(きゅうりょう)は年齢(ねんれい)（によらず・によっては）、仕事(しごと)の成果(せいか)で決(き)まる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

年齢(ねんれい)<b>によらず</b>

The second half names what pay *is* decided by, so the first half has to say what
it is *not* decided by. <code>によっては</code> would claim that age does decide
it in some cases, which the rest of the sentence then contradicts.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He looks delicate, but in fact he hardly ever gets ill."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

彼(かれ)は<b>見(み)かけによらず</b>丈夫(じょうぶ)で、めったに病気(びょうき)をしない。

"Looks ~ but in fact" is exactly what the set phrase
<code>見(み)かけによらず</code> carries, and it goes in front of the
surprising quality rather than after it. Note that the English "but" disappears -
the contrast is already inside the phrase.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この制度(せいど)は収入(しゅうにゅう)のによらず、子育(こそだ)て世帯(せたい)すべてが対象(たいしょう)となります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この制度(せいど)は収入(しゅうにゅう)<b>によらず</b>、子育(こそだ)て世帯(せたい)すべてが対象(たいしょう)となります。

Delete the `の`. <code>によらず</code> attaches straight to the noun; the `の`
would only be correct in front of <code>いかん</code>, as in
<code>収入(しゅうにゅう)のいかんによらず</code>, which is the same sentence in
document register.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 彼女(かのじょ)は<b>見(み)かけによらず</b>よく食(た)べる。
(b) 彼女(かのじょ)は見(み)かけ<b>に関係(かんけい)なく</b>よく食(た)べる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is idiomatic and means she eats far more than you would guess from looking at
her. The speaker is registering a mild surprise, and the sentence is a compliment
of sorts among friends.

(b) is grammatical but nearly nonsensical. It says her appetite is not affected by
her appearance - as though the two might have been connected and turned out not to
be. The lesson is that <code>見(み)かけによらず</code> is a fixed expression
with its own meaning, not a free combination of 見(み)かけ and
<code>によらず</code>.

</details>

# ～ようによっては

**Meaning** · depending on how you do it; looked at another way - the same thing comes out differently according to the manner of doing or seeing

The <code>よう</code> here is the noun 様(よう), "manner, way", the one that
gives <code>使(つか)いよう</code> "a way of using" and
<code>言(い)いよう</code> "a way of putting it". Hang
<code>によっては</code> on it and you get "depending on the way of ~ing". The
second half then offers a reading of the situation that was not the obvious one -
sometimes a consolation, sometimes a warning.

## Formation

Take the ます-stem of the verb, add <code>よう</code>, then
<code>によっては</code>.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + ようによっては | 使(つか)う → 使(つか)いようによっては |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + ようによっては | 考(かんが)える → 考(かんが)えようによっては |
| Verb (する) | し / やり + ようによっては | やる → やりようによっては |
| Compound する-verb | ～の + しようによっては | 説明(せつめい)する → 説明(せつめい)のしようによっては |
| Without は | ～ようによって | 言(い)いようによって |
| Sentence-final | ～ようだ / ～ようがある | 考(かんが)えようがある |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the ます-stem is the point of attachment, not the dictionary form.
<code>考(かんが)えようによっては</code>, never
❌<code>考(かんが)えるようによっては</code>. Two of these forms collide with
the volitional in spelling - <code>見(み)よう</code>,
<code>しよう</code> - but the grammar is unrelated, and the following
<code>によっては</code> makes the reading unambiguous.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
考(かんが)え<b>ようによっては</b>、これはいい機会(きかい)だ。
Looked at another way, this is a good opportunity.
</pre>

<pre>
見(み)<b>ようによっては</b>、この雲(くも)は魚(さかな)の形(かたち)に見(み)える。
Depending on how you look at it, that cloud is shaped like a fish.
</pre>

<pre>
その言(い)い方(かた)は、取(と)り<b>ようによっては</b>失礼(しつれい)に聞(き)こえるよ。
Put like that, it could be taken as rude.
</pre>

<pre>
古(ふる)い道具(どうぐ)も、使(つか)い<b>ようによっては</b>新(あたら)しい物(もの)より役(やく)に立(た)つ。
Handled the right way, an old tool can be more use than a new one.
</pre>

<pre>
失敗(しっぱい)も、考(かんが)え<b>ようによっては</b>次(つぎ)への準備(じゅんび)期間(きかん)だと言(い)える。
Failure too, seen in a certain light, is preparation for what comes next.
</pre>

<pre>
予算(よさん)は少(すく)ないが、やり<b>ようによっては</b>十分(じゅうぶん)面白(おもしろ)いものが作(つく)れるはずだ。
The budget is small, but done the right way it should still produce something interesting.
</pre>

<pre>
同(おな)じデータでも、示(しめ)し<b>ようによっては</b>まったく逆(ぎゃく)の印象(いんしょう)を与(あた)えてしまう。
The very same figures can leave the opposite impression, depending on how they are presented.
</pre>

<pre>
人(ひと)に物(もの)を頼(たの)むときは、言(い)い<b>ようによって</b>相手(あいて)の受(う)け取(と)り方(かた)がまるで変(か)わってくる。
When you ask someone for a favour, the way you phrase it changes how they take it entirely.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This one is **register-neutral and genuinely useful in speech**, which sets it
apart from most of the "depending on" family.
<code>考(かんが)えようによっては</code> in particular is everyday Japanese and
one of the commonest ways to offer somebody a different perspective on bad news.
<code>見(み)ようによっては</code>, <code>取(と)りようによっては</code>,
<code>言(い)いようによっては</code> and <code>やりようによっては</code> are
close behind. The less common verbs sound more considered and fit writing better,
but nothing about the pattern is formal in itself.

What the second half does is worth noticing, because it is what learners leave out.
The main clause almost always presents an **alternative** reading, and it is
usually hedged to show that it is one option among several:
<code>…とも言(い)える</code>, <code>…かもしれない</code>,
<code>…に見(み)える</code>, <code>…こともある</code>,
<code>…はずだ</code>. A flat assertion after
<code>ようによっては</code> sounds as though the speaker forgot how they started.

The pattern turns in both directions. Facing a setback it consoles -
<code>考(かんが)えようによっては、運(うん)がよかった</code> - and facing a
draft email it warns:
<code>書(か)きようによっては誤解(ごかい)されますよ</code>. The warning use
is a standard piece of workplace advice and is worth having ready.

<code>言(い)いよう</code> has a life of its own in the proverb
<code>ものは言(い)いよう</code>, "it's all in how you say it" - the same noun,
with no <code>によっては</code> attached.

## Contrast

### ～ようによっては vs ～方(かた)によっては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - almost interchangeable.
<code>方(かた)</code> is the plainer, more concrete "way of doing";
<code>よう</code> is slightly more abstract and slightly more literary.

</div>

<pre>
使(つか)い方(かた)<b>によっては</b>、この薬(くすり)は危険(きけん)です。
Used the wrong way, this drug is dangerous.
</pre>

<pre>
使(つか)い<b>ようによっては</b>、失敗(しっぱい)の経験(けいけん)も強(つよ)みになる。
Handled well, even the experience of failing becomes a strength.
</pre>

The difference shows up at the edges. <code>方(かた)</code> points at an
actual method you could describe or teach, which is why it fits instructions and
manuals. <code>よう</code> can point at something as vague as a frame of mind,
which is why <code>考(かんが)えようによっては</code> is the set phrase for
"looked at another way", while
<code>考(かんが)え方(かた)によっては</code> - not wrong, and used - leans
towards there being an actual method of thinking under discussion.

### ～ようによっては vs ～によっては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>によっては</code> alone hangs off a **noun** and varies
by which case you are in. <code>ようによっては</code> hangs off a **verb** and
varies by how the thing is done.

</div>

<pre>
店(みせ)<b>によっては</b>、同(おな)じ本(ほん)が安(やす)く買(か)える。
At some shops you can buy the same book cheaper.
</pre>

<pre>
探(さが)し<b>ようによっては</b>、同(おな)じ本(ほん)がもっと安(やす)く見(み)つかる。
Search the right way and you'll find the same book cheaper still.
</pre>

The first sentence sorts shops; the second sorts search techniques. What varies in
the first is out in the world, and what varies in the second is what the person
does - which is exactly why the second is often encouraging and the first is not.

### ～ようによっては vs ～ようでは

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - different patterns that look alike.
<code>ようによっては</code> attaches to the **ます-stem** and means "depending on
how". <code>ようでは</code> attaches to the **plain form** and means "if things
are going like this", always with a bad outcome.

</div>

<pre>
そんなに慌(あわ)てる<b>ようでは</b>、本番(ほんばん)が心配(しんぱい)だ。
If you panic like that, I worry about the real thing.
</pre>

<pre>
慌(あわ)て<b>ようによっては</b>、かえって時間(じかん)がかかる。
Panic in the wrong way and it actually takes longer.
</pre>

Two things separate them on sight: the attachment (<code>慌(あわ)てる</code>
versus <code>慌(あわ)て</code>) and the tone.
<code>ようでは</code> is a criticism of how things currently stand;
<code>ようによっては</code> is neutral about what has happened and interested in
the method.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 考(かんが)える<b>ようによっては</b> → ✅ 考(かんが)え<b>ようによっては</b>
  The base is the ます-stem, not the dictionary form. This is the error to watch,
  because <code>考(かんが)えるよう</code> is a perfectly good sequence in other
  patterns.

- ❌ する<b>ようによっては</b>結果(けっか)が変(か)わる。 → ✅ <b>やりようによっては</b>結果(けっか)が変(か)わる。（or しようによっては）
  する has the ます-stem し, so the form is <code>しようによっては</code>. In
  practice speakers reach for <code>やりよう</code> far more often, and for
  compound verbs the natural shape is
  <code>説明(せつめい)のしようによっては</code>.

- ❌ 天気(てんき)の<b>ようによっては</b>、中止(ちゅうし)します。 → ✅ 天気(てんき)<b>によっては</b>、中止(ちゅうし)します。
  A noun takes <code>によっては</code> on its own. Inserting
  <code>のよう</code> turns it into "resembling the weather", which is a different
  word altogether.

- ❌ 考(かんが)え<b>ようによっては</b>、明日(あした)は月曜日(げつようび)だ。 → ✅ 考(かんが)え<b>ようによっては</b>、明日(あした)から新(あたら)しく始(はじ)められる。
  The main clause has to be something that could be seen otherwise. A bare fact has
  only one reading, so the frame promises a new angle and then does not supply one.

## Practice

**1.** 狭(せま)い部屋(へや)だが、家具(かぐ)の置(お)き____広(ひろ)く感(かん)じられる。

置(お)き<b>ようによっては</b>

置(お)く → ます-stem 置(お)き → <code>置(お)きよう</code> "a way of

the verb-based form is the one needed, and the concessive が in front of it sets up
exactly the reframing this pattern delivers.

</details>

<code>見(み)ようによっては</code> would be about the manner of looking and

</details>

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<code>言(い)いよう</code> is the natural noun here and is half of the proverb

</details>

report and the same writer, but only (a) leaves the writer somewhere to go.

</details>

# 場合によっては

**Meaning** · depending on the circumstances; in some cases; if it comes to it - flags what follows as one possibility among several, not as the plan

場合(ばあい) is the noun "case, occasion", and <code>によっては</code> picks
out some of those cases rather than all of them. The phrase therefore does not
predict; it reserves. Whoever says it is telling you that something might happen,
and quietly asking you not to be surprised if it does.

## Usage

<code>場合(ばあい)によっては</code> is a fixed adverbial phrase. It never
changes form, nothing attaches to it, and it takes no noun in front of it. It sits
at the head of the clause it qualifies, or just after the topic:
<code>この薬(くすり)は、場合(ばあい)によっては…</code>.

Its partner is a **hedged** main clause, and the hedge is not optional in practice.
The endings that go with it are <code>…かもしれません</code>,
<code>…こともあります</code>, <code>…場合(ばあい)がございます</code>,
<code>…ことになります</code>, <code>…可能性(かのうせい)があります</code>. A
bare assertion after it reads as a contradiction, because the phrase has just said
the outcome is uncertain.

Three variants, same meaning, different height:

- <code>場合(ばあい)によっては</code> - the standard form, usable anywhere.
- <code>場合(ばあい)により</code> / <code>場合(ばあい)によりましては</code> -
  the announcement and customer-notice form. You hear it on trains and read it on
  websites.
- <code>場合(ばあい)によったら</code> - colloquial, and noticeably softer.

<div class="note">

**Note** - drop the `は` and the meaning shifts.
<code>場合(ばあい)によって対応(たいおう)が異(こと)なります</code> means
"the response varies from case to case" - a statement about variation.
<code>場合(ばあい)によっては</code> singles out particular cases and warns
about them. The `は` is doing all the work.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
<b>場合(ばあい)によっては</b>、明日(あした)休(やす)むかもしれない。
Depending on how things go, I might take tomorrow off.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>場合(ばあい)によっては</b>、警察(けいさつ)に連絡(れんらく)することになります。
If it comes to it, we will have to involve the police.
</pre>

<pre>
工事(こうじ)は夜間(やかん)も続(つづ)きますので、<b>場合(ばあい)によっては</b>騒音(そうおん)が気(き)になることがあるかと存(ぞん)じます。
The work continues overnight, so in some instances you may find the noise intrusive.
</pre>

<pre>
しばらく様子(ようす)を見(み)ますが、<b>場合(ばあい)によりましては</b>専門医(せんもんい)をご紹介(しょうかい)いたします。
We'll keep an eye on it for now, but should it prove necessary I will refer you to a specialist.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The phrase itself is **register-neutral** - it works in a text message and in an
annual report - but it earns its keep in polite and business Japanese, where it is
one of the standard ways of introducing bad news before it happens. A supplier
writing <code>場合(ばあい)によっては納期(のうき)が遅(おく)れる可能性(かのうせい)がございます</code>
is not predicting a delay; they are making sure that a delay, if it comes, was
foreseen. Reading it as a mere maybe underestimates it. Very often the speaker
already suspects the thing will happen.

It also works as a **softener on a threat**. 「場合(ばあい)によっては法的(ほうてき)な対応(たいおう)も検討(けんとう)いたします」
is a serious sentence dressed as a contingency, and everyone involved knows it. The
phrase gives both sides room to retreat, which is exactly why it is chosen.

In casual speech it is common and unremarkable -
<code>場合(ばあい)によっては行(い)けないかも</code> - though friends will
just as often say <code>もしかしたら</code> or
<code>ひょっとすると</code>. The polite
<code>場合(ばあい)によりましては</code> is worth recognising rather than
producing: it belongs to station announcements, airline notices and formal customer
correspondence, and in ordinary email it sounds over-dressed.

## Contrast

### 場合(ばあい)によっては vs ことによると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>場合(ばあい)によっては</code> says the outcome
**depends on how things develop**. <code>ことによると</code> says the speaker
**suspects something may already be true**.

</div>

<pre>
<b>ことによると</b>、彼(かれ)はもう知(し)っているのかもしれない。
It's just possible he already knows.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>場合(ばあい)によっては</b>、彼(かれ)にも説明(せつめい)する必要(ひつよう)が出(で)てくる。
Depending on how it goes, we may need to explain it to him as well.
</pre>

<code>ことによると</code> is a guess about a fact and looks at the present or the
past; it is close to <code>もしかすると</code>.
<code>場合(ばあい)によっては</code> looks forward at branching outcomes.
There is a middle form, <code>ことによっては</code>, which does overlap with
<code>場合(ばあい)によっては</code> - but it is less common and slightly more
bookish.

### 場合(ばあい)によっては vs 時(とき)には

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>時(とき)には</code> is about **frequency**:
sometimes, from time to time. <code>場合(ばあい)によっては</code> is about
**contingency**: in certain circumstances, if things fall that way.

</div>

<pre>
<b>時(とき)には</b>厳(きび)しいことを言(い)わなければならない。
There are times when you have to say something harsh.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>場合(ばあい)によっては</b>厳(きび)しいことを言(い)わなければならないかもしれない。
Depending on the situation, I may have to say something harsh.
</pre>

The first reports what happens periodically and is comfortable in the plain
non-past. The second is about one situation that has not resolved yet, and it needs
its hedge. A quick test: if you could add <code>ときどき</code>, you want
<code>時(とき)には</code>.

### 場合(ばあい)によっては vs 場合(ばあい)によって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with `は`, a **warning** about particular cases. Without `は`,
a **description** of how things vary across cases.

</div>

<pre>
料金(りょうきん)は季節(きせつ)や人数(にんずう)など、<b>場合(ばあい)によって</b>異(こと)なります。
Prices vary by case - season, number of people and so on.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>場合(ばあい)によっては</b>追加(ついか)料金(りょうきん)をいただくことがございます。
In certain cases an additional charge may apply.
</pre>

The first sentence is neutral information a customer would expect on a price page.
The second is a caution, and it names a specific unwelcome possibility. Using the
first where the second is meant makes a warning sound like a footnote; using the
second where the first is meant makes ordinary variation sound ominous.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>場合(ばあい)によっては</b>、必(かなら)ず中止(ちゅうし)します。 → ✅ <b>場合(ばあい)によっては</b>、中止(ちゅうし)することもあります。
  必(かなら)ず and the phrase cancel each other out. If the outcome is certain,
  the contingency frame has nothing to do.

- ❌ <b>場合(ばあい)によっては</b>対応(たいおう)が違(ちが)います。 → ✅ <b>場合(ばあい)によって</b>対応(たいおう)が違(ちが)います。
  Plain variation across cases needs no `は`. With `は` the sentence promises to
  single out a particular case and then does not.

- ❌ 雨(あめ)の<b>場合(ばあい)によっては</b>、中止(ちゅうし)です。 → ✅ 雨(あめ)の<b>場合(ばあい)は</b>、中止(ちゅうし)です。
  Nothing attaches in front of the phrase. Once you have named the case - rain -
  the thing you want is <code>…場合(ばあい)は</code>.

- ❌ 昨日(きのう)は<b>場合(ばあい)によっては</b>中止(ちゅうし)になりました。 → ✅ 昨日(きのう)は雨(あめ)のため中止(ちゅうし)になりました。
  The phrase reserves a possibility, so it cannot describe something that has
  already been settled. A completed past event leaves nothing open.

## Practice

**1.** 台風(たいふう)が接近(せっきん)しております。____、一部(いちぶ)の列車(れっしゃ)が運休(うんきゅう)となる可能性(かのうせい)がございます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>場合(ばあい)によりましては</b>（or <b>場合(ばあい)によっては</b>）

The surrounding <code>ございます</code> and the hedge
<code>可能性(かのうせい)があります</code> mark this as a public announcement,
which is the natural home of the extra-polite
<code>によりましては</code>. The plain
<code>場合(ばあい)によっては</code> is equally correct, just a shade less
formal.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 子育(こそだ)てというのは、（時(とき)には・場合(ばあい)によっては）叱(しか)ることも必要(ひつよう)だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>時(とき)には</b>

This is a general remark about raising children over the years, so what is at issue
is how often, not what a particular situation may turn into.
<code>場合(ばあい)によっては</code> would point at one unresolved incident and
warn about it, which does not fit a statement of principle in the plain non-past.

</details>

(a) 納品(のうひん)は来週(らいしゅう)末(まつ)になります。
(b) 納品(のうひん)は来週(らいしゅう)末(まつ)の予定(よてい)ですが、<b>場合(ばあい)によっては</b>数日(すうじつ)遅(おく)れることもございます。

(b) keeps the same date but buys insurance against it. The sender is signalling that
a delay is a live possibility - very often that they already expect one - and doing
so now so that it will have been announced rather than confessed. In Japanese
business correspondence this is the polite thing to do, and a reader who takes (b)
as merely (a) with padding has missed the message.

</details>

# ～たら～たで・～なら～で

**Meaning** · if it turns out that way, then that way has its own story - repeating the predicate says the situation is not settled just because it went the way it went

English has no clean equivalent, which is why this one takes a moment. You say the
same predicate twice: once as a condition, once again with `で` after it.
<code>降(ふ)ったら降(ふ)ったで</code> is "if it rains, then in the
rains-case ~". The doubling is the message. It concedes the outcome and then
immediately says that the outcome brings something of its own - a fresh problem, or
a way of coping that is fine after all.

## Formation

The rule is mechanical: **conditional form + the same predicate again + で**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | ～たら + ～た + で | 降(ふ)る → 降(ふ)ったら降(ふ)ったで |
| Verb (negative) | ～なかったら + ～なかった + で | 来(く)る → 来(こ)なかったら来(こ)なかったで |
| ある | あれば + あった + で | あればあったで |
| ない | なければ + ない + で | なければないで |
| い-adjective | ～ければ + ～い + で | 安(やす)い → 安(やす)ければ安(やす)いで |
| い-adjective (past) | ～かったら + ～かった + で | 忙(いそが)しかったら忙(いそが)しかったで |
| な-adjective | ～なら + stem + で | 暇(ひま)な → 暇(ひま)なら暇(ひま)で |
| Noun | ～なら + Noun + で | 一人(ひとり) → 一人(ひとり)なら一人(ひとり)で |
| Shortened repeat | ～たら + した + で | 結婚(けっこん)したらしたで |
| Paired (both branches) | Aば A で、B ば B で | あればあったで、なければないで |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the `で` is the て-form of the copula `だ`, connecting the concession to
what follows. That is why it never becomes `て`, and why it does not change for
tense: the tense is already carried by the repeated predicate.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
雨(あめ)が<b>降(ふ)ったら降(ふ)ったで</b>、家(いえ)で映画(えいが)でも見(み)よう。
And if it rains, well, we'll watch a film at home or something.
</pre>

<pre>
お金(かね)は、<b>あればあったで</b>使(つか)ってしまう。
Money is money - if you have it, you spend it.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>なければないで</b>、人(ひと)は何(なん)とかするものだ。
And if you haven't got it, somehow people manage.
</pre>

<pre>
一人(ひとり)<b>なら一人(ひとり)で</b>、気(き)ままに暮(く)らせばいいじゃないか。
If you're on your own, then be on your own and live as you please.
</pre>

<pre>
部屋(へや)が狭(せま)い<b>なら狭(せま)いで</b>、掃除(そうじ)が楽(らく)だという利点(りてん)はある。
If the flat is small, then a small flat has its compensations - it's easy to clean.
</pre>

<pre>
子(こ)どもは<b>小(ちい)さければ小(ちい)さいで</b>手(て)がかかるし、大(おお)きくなったら大(おお)きくなったでお金(かね)がかかる。
Small children take your time, and once they're grown they take your money.
</pre>

<pre>
連絡(れんらく)が<b>来(こ)なかったら来(こ)なかったで</b>、こちらから確認(かくにん)すればいいだけの話(はなし)だ。
And if they don't get back to us, then we just chase them ourselves - that's all there is to it.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>結婚(けっこん)したらしたで</b>気(き)を使(つか)うことが増(ふ)えるし、<b>しなければしないで</b>周(まわ)りがうるさい。
Get married and there's more to worry about; don't, and everyone around you has an opinion.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Colloquial.** This lives in conversation, in blog posts, in dialogue, in the kind
of writing that sounds like somebody talking. It has no place in a report, a
contract or a formal announcement, and there is no polite version of the pattern
itself - you can end the sentence in ます or です, but the doubled middle stays as
it is.

The tone is **resigned, wry, and faintly world-weary**. The speaker has thought
about both branches and found that neither is clean. That is why it goes so
naturally with <code>困(こま)る</code>, <code>大変(たいへん)だ</code>,
<code>文句(もんく)を言(い)う</code>, and equally with
<code>何(なん)とかなる</code>, <code>それなりに</code>,
<code>仕方(しかた)がない</code> and <code>いいじゃないか</code>.

Two directions to hear it in. The **complaint** direction: whichever way it falls,
there is trouble - and the paired form
<code>あればあったで、なければないで</code> makes that explicit. The
**acceptance** direction: all right, that is how it is, and we will live with it -
<code>来(こ)られないなら来(こ)られないで、こちらは進(すす)めます</code>.
Nothing in the grammar distinguishes them; the main clause does. If the second half
names a problem it is the complaint; if it names a way forward it is the acceptance.

A small but useful habit: the repeat is very often **shortened**.
<code>結婚(けっこん)したら結婚(けっこん)したで</code> almost always comes
out as <code>結婚(けっこん)したらしたで</code>, and
<code>暇(ひま)になったらなったで</code> drops the noun the same way. Learners
who only ever write the full form sound careful rather than fluent.

## Contrast

### ～たら～たで vs ～ても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ても` says the main clause holds **regardless** of the
condition. `たら～たで` says the condition **creates a new situation**, which the
main clause then describes.

</div>

<pre>
雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っ<b>ても</b>、試合(しあい)は行(おこな)われます。
The match will go ahead even if it rains.
</pre>

<pre>
雨(あめ)が<b>降(ふ)ったら降(ふ)ったで</b>、客(きゃく)の数(かず)が読(よ)めなくなる。
And if it does rain, then we can no longer predict the turnout.
</pre>

The `ても` sentence brushes the rain aside. The doubled sentence does the opposite:
it lets the rain in and reports what the rainy world looks like. A quick test - if
you could translate with "even if", you want `ても`; if you would translate with
"and if it does, then ~", you want the doubling.

### ～たら～たで vs plain ～たら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - plain `たら` is a neutral condition. The repeat adds "granted,
and even so" - a concession the speaker has already made before the main clause
starts.

</div>

<pre>
時間(じかん)が<b>あったら</b>、映画(えいが)を見(み)に行(い)きます。
If I have time I'll go and see a film.
</pre>

<pre>
時間(じかん)が<b>あったらあったで</b>、結局(けっきょく)寝(ね)てしまうんですよ。
And when I do have time, I just end up sleeping, funnily enough.
</pre>

Notice that the second sentence cannot be about a genuinely open condition. It
assumes the case will occur, or has occurred before, and comments on it - which is
why it so often carries よ, ね, んです and the other markers of shared experience.
The first sentence is a plan; the second is a rueful observation.

### ～なら～で vs ～なりに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>なりに</code> says the thing is doing well **by its own
standards**. <code>なら～で</code> concedes the situation first and then says what
comes with it, good or bad.

</div>

<pre>
新人(しんじん)は新人(しんじん)<b>なりに</b>努力(どりょく)している。
The new staff are trying hard in their own way.
</pre>

<pre>
新人(しんじん)<b>なら新人(しんじん)で</b>、教(おし)える側(がわ)の手間(てま)も増(ふ)える。
If they're new, then being new means more work for whoever trains them.
</pre>

The two combine happily -
<code>狭(せま)いなら狭(せま)いで、狭(せま)いなりに工夫(くふう)する</code> -
because they are doing different jobs. <code>なりに</code> evaluates; the doubling
concedes. Only the doubling can lead into a complaint.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 雨(あめ)が<b>降(ふ)ったら止(や)んだで</b> → ✅ 雨(あめ)が<b>降(ふ)ったら降(ふ)ったで</b>
  The same predicate has to appear twice. Two different verbs make the sentence
  unreadable, because the doubling is what carries the meaning.

- ❌ 暇(ひま)だったら暇(ひま)<b>で</b>困(こま)る。 → ✅ 暇(ひま)<b>なら暇(ひま)で</b>困(こま)る。（or 暇(ひま)だったら暇(ひま)<b>だったで</b>）
  Match the two halves. Either use `なら` with the bare stem, or use `だったら`
  with `だった`; mixing the two leaves the sentence half-formed.

- ❌ 試験(しけん)に<b>受(う)かったら受(う)かったで</b>嬉(うれ)しい。 → ✅ 試験(しけん)に<b>受(う)かったら受(う)かったで</b>、引(ひ)っ越(こ)しの準備(じゅんび)が大変(たいへん)だ。
  Straightforward good news defeats the pattern. The doubling promises a
  complication or a compromise, and simple happiness supplies neither.

- ❌ 納品(のうひん)が<b>遅(おく)れたら遅(おく)れたで</b>、別途(べっと)ご案内(あんない)申(もう)し上(あ)げます。 → ✅ 納品(のうひん)が遅(おく)れる場合(ばあい)は、別途(べっと)ご案内(あんない)申(もう)し上(あ)げます。
  A register error rather than a grammatical one. The doubling is chatty and a
  little fatalistic, and it clashes badly with the humble language of a business
  notice.

## Practice

**1.** 車(くるま)は、あったらあったで維持費(いじひ)がかかるし、____買(か)い物(もの)に不便(ふべん)だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>なければないで</b>

The first half has already opened the paired frame, so the second branch has to
mirror it. With ある the negative partner is <code>なければないで</code> - note
that ない stays non-past here, unlike the あった of the first half.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 会議(かいぎ)が中止(ちゅうし)に（なっても・なったらなったで）、準備(じゅんび)した資料(しりょう)は来月(らいげつ)使(つか)えばいい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

中止(ちゅうし)に<b>なったらなったで</b>

The second half is not a result that holds regardless - it is what the speaker will
do *in the cancelled world*, and the tone is "fine, we'll cope". <code>ても</code>
would say the materials are usable next month whether or not the meeting is
cancelled, which is not quite the same thought.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "A big house is a lot of cleaning, and a small one has nowhere to put anything. Either way it's something."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

家(いえ)は<b>広(ひろ)ければ広(ひろ)いで</b>掃除(そうじ)が大変(たいへん)だし、<b>狭(せま)ければ狭(せま)いで</b>物(もの)の置(お)き場所(ばしょ)に困(こま)る。

Two い-adjectives, each doubled in the <code>～ければ～いで</code> shape, joined
by し. "Either way it's something" needs no separate translation - the paired
doubling already says it, which is exactly what makes the pattern hard to render in
English.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 息子(むすこ)が独立(どくりつ)したらしたで寂(さび)しいものだが、しないならしないで心配(しんぱい)だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 息子(むすこ)が<b>独立(どくりつ)したらしたで</b>寂(さび)しいものだが、<b>しなければしないで</b>心配(しんぱい)だ。

The first half is fine. In the second, `なら` goes with a noun or a な-adjective;
a verb takes <code>なければ</code> (or <code>しなかったらしなかったで</code>).
Keeping the two branches parallel in form is part of what makes the paired version
work.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 来(こ)られない<b>なら</b>、早(はや)めに連絡(れんらく)してください。
(b) 来(こ)られない<b>なら来(こ)られないで</b>、こちらだけで始(はじ)めます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is an ordinary conditional request. It treats the person's coming as still
undecided and asks for notice either way.

(b) has already accepted that they may not come. The doubling grants the case
before the main clause begins, and the main clause then reports what the speaker
will do inside it. Depending on tone it can be generous - "don't worry about it, we
will manage" - or pointed, a way of saying that the meeting will go on without
them. Only (b) carries that second layer, and only (b) sounds like speech.

</details>
