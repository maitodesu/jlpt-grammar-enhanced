# N2 - Regardless of, apart from, and except

# ～にかかわらず

**Meaning** · regardless of; irrespective of - the thing named makes no difference to what follows

`にかかわらず` declares a variable **irrelevant**. Whichever way it turns out - rain
or shine, young or old, yes or no - the main clause holds unchanged. It is the
voice of notices, rules and application forms, and it is the negative of the verb
<code>関(かか)わる</code> ("to bear on"): *not being connected to*. Add one `も`
and you get `にもかかわらず`, which does almost the opposite job.

## Formation

`にかかわらず` attaches to a noun, or to something that has been shaped into a
two-way choice.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + にかかわらず | 天候(てんこう) → 天候(てんこう)にかかわらず |
| Paired-opposite noun | 有無(うむ)・大小(だいしょう)・多少(たしょう) + にかかわらず | 経験(けいけん)の有無(うむ)にかかわらず |
| Verb (affirmative + negative) | V + V-ない + にかかわらず | 行(い)く → 行(い)く行(い)かないにかかわらず |
| Verb + かどうか | plain form + かどうか + にかかわらず | 参加(さんか)する → 参加(さんか)するかどうかにかかわらず |
| い-adjective (antonym pair) | ～い + ～い + にかかわらず | 高(たか)い → 高(たか)い安(やす)いにかかわらず |
| Written variant | Noun + にかかわりなく | 年齢(ねんれい) → 年齢(ねんれい)にかかわりなく |

<div class="note">

**Note** - in kanji this is 関(かか)わらず, occasionally 拘(かか)わらず. Public
notices very often write it in kana precisely because the two kanji spellings are
not fully settled. Either spelling is safe to read; kana is safe to write.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
天気(てんき)<b>にかかわらず</b>、試合(しあい)は行(おこな)われます。
The match will go ahead regardless of the weather.
</pre>

<pre>
年齢(ねんれい)<b>にかかわらず</b>、どなたでも申(もう)し込(こ)めます。
Anyone can apply, regardless of age.
</pre>

<pre>
参加(さんか)するかどうか<b>にかかわらず</b>、返事(へんじ)は金曜(きんよう)までにください。
Whether you're joining or not, please reply by Friday.
</pre>

<pre>
経験(けいけん)の有無(うむ)<b>にかかわらず</b>、研修(けんしゅう)は全員(ぜんいん)受(う)けてもらいます。
Experienced or not, everyone has to take the training.
</pre>

<pre>
買(か)う買(か)わない<b>にかかわらず</b>、まず試着(しちゃく)してみてください。
Try it on first, whether you end up buying it or not.
</pre>

<pre>
この保険(ほけん)は国籍(こくせき)<b>にかかわらず</b>加入(かにゅう)できます。
You can take out this insurance whatever your nationality.
</pre>

<pre>
金額(きんがく)の大小(だいしょう)<b>にかかわらず</b>、領収書(りょうしゅうしょ)は必(かなら)ず保管(ほかん)しておいてください。
Keep every receipt, however large or small the amount.
</pre>

<pre>
雨(あめ)が降(ふ)る降(ふ)らない<b>にかかわらず</b>、朝(あさ)の散歩(さんぽ)だけは欠(か)かさないことにしている。
Rain or no rain, the one thing I never skip is my morning walk.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`にかかわらず` is formal and written-leaning. Its natural home is the sentence that
sets out a rule for everybody: recruitment ads, ticket conditions, school
regulations, insurance terms. In those settings it is completely neutral - no
politeness is built into the form, and it takes whatever ending the surrounding
register calls for (<code>同(おな)じです</code>, <code>同(おな)じである</code>,
<code>同(おな)じだ</code>).

Spoken casually it sounds like you are reading from a leaflet. Between friends the
same idea is carried by a doubled `でも` or by `ても`:
<code>雨(あめ)でも晴(は)れでも行(い)くよ</code>. Save `にかかわらず` for
writing, presentations and announcements, where it is exactly right.

`にかかわりなく` is a slightly stiffer written variant with the same meaning, and
`によらず` covers similar ground in legal and administrative prose. All three are
neutral as to the speaker's feelings: unlike `もかまわず`, nobody is being accused
of ignoring anything. The variable is simply not part of the rule.

One structural habit worth absorbing: because `にかかわらず` needs a *range*, a
single verb will not do. Japanese builds the range explicitly, either by pairing a
verb with its own negative (<code>来(く)る来(こ)ないにかかわらず</code>) or by
wrapping the clause in `かどうか`. The pair pattern with `する` is
<code>するしないにかかわらず</code>.

## Contrast

### ～にかかわらず vs ～にもかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `にかかわらず` says the thing **makes no difference**.
`にもかかわらず` says the thing **was true, and the result is surprising anyway**.

</div>

This one `も` is the single most expensive character in the batch. `にかかわらず`
sweeps a whole range aside; `にもかかわらず` picks out one real fact and concedes it.

<pre>
平日(へいじつ)か週末(しゅうまつ)か<b>にかかわらず</b>、料金(りょうきん)は同(おな)じです。
The price is the same whether it's a weekday or the weekend.
</pre>

<pre>
平日(へいじつ)<b>にもかかわらず</b>、店(みせ)は満席(まんせき)だった。
Even though it was a weekday, the restaurant was packed.
</pre>

There is also a formation tell. `にもかかわらず` happily follows a single completed
fact - a plain past verb, an い-adjective, a noun. `にかかわらず` cannot, because one
fact is not a range.

### ～にかかわらず vs ～を問(と)わず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を問(と)わず</code> wants a **bare noun** or a fixed pair.
`にかかわらず` will also take a clause.

</div>

The meanings are close enough that in a job advertisement either would pass.

<pre>
経験(けいけん)<b>を問(と)わず</b>採用(さいよう)しています。
We are hiring regardless of experience.
</pre>

<pre>
経験(けいけん)があるかないか<b>にかかわらず</b>採用(さいよう)しています。
We are hiring whether you have experience or not.
</pre>

<code>を問(と)わず</code> is at its best with a short noun that names a category -
男女(だんじょ), 昼夜(ちゅうや), 国籍(こくせき), 経験(けいけん)の有無(うむ)
- and it grows heavy when a whole clause is stacked in front of it. When the thing
you are dismissing is an action or a decision rather than a category, reach for
`にかかわらず`.

### ～にかかわらず vs たとえ～ても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ても` concedes **one case**; `にかかわらず` dismisses **the
whole range at once**.

</div>

<pre>
たとえ雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っても、予定(よてい)どおり出発(しゅっぱつ)します。
Even if it rains, we'll leave as planned.
</pre>

<pre>
天候(てんこう)<b>にかかわらず</b>、予定(よてい)どおり出発(しゅっぱつ)します。
We'll leave as planned regardless of the weather.
</pre>

The first names the bad case and says it will not stop us; you can hear that rain
is the worry. The second refuses to look at the variable at all, and is the form an
announcement would use. When a learner wants to sound institutional rather than
personally determined, `にかかわらず` is the switch.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 安(やす)い<b>にかかわらず</b>買(か)わなかった。 → ✅ 安(やす)かった<b>にもかかわらず</b>買(か)わなかった。
  One established fact that you are conceding needs the `も`. Without it the
  sentence promises a range and never delivers one.

- ❌ 参加(さんか)する<b>にかかわらず</b> → ✅ 参加(さんか)するかどうか<b>にかかわらず</b>（or 参加(さんか)する参加(さんか)しない<b>にかかわらず</b>）
  A bare verb is a single case. Turn it into a two-way choice with `かどうか` or by
  pairing it with its own negative.

- ❌ 天気(てんき)<b>をかかわらず</b> → ✅ 天気(てんき)<b>にかかわらず</b>
  The verb is <code>関(かか)わる</code>, "to relate **to**", so the particle is
  `に`. `を` belongs to <code>を問(と)わず</code>, a different verb.

- ❌ 年齢(ねんれい)にかかわらず<b>の</b>人(ひと)が集(あつ)まった。 → ✅ 年齢(ねんれい)にかかわらず、いろいろな人(ひと)が集(あつ)まった。
  `にかかわらず` modifies the verb, not a noun. To modify a noun directly, use
  <code>年齢(ねんれい)を問(と)わない募集(ぼしゅう)</code> instead.

## Practice

**1.** この美術館(びじゅつかん)は、季節(きせつ)____同(おな)じ時間(じかん)に開館(かいかん)しています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

季節(きせつ)<b>にかかわらず</b>同(おな)じ時間(じかん)に開館(かいかん)しています。

The season is being declared irrelevant to the opening hours, which is exactly the
job of `にかかわらず`. <code>季節(きせつ)を問(と)わず</code> would also be
natural here, since 季節(きせつ) is a short category noun.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 大雪(おおゆき)（にかかわらず・にもかかわらず）、彼(かれ)は時間(じかん)どおりに来(き)た。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

大雪(おおゆき)<b>にもかかわらず</b>、彼(かれ)は時間(じかん)どおりに来(き)た。

The heavy snow actually happened, and his arriving on time is surprising *because*
of it. That concessive reading needs the `も`. Without it the sentence would claim
that snow makes no difference to him, which is a different - and stranger - thing
to say.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Members and non-members alike pay the same fee."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

会員(かいいん)かどうか<b>にかかわらず</b>、料金(りょうきん)は同(おな)じです。

"Whether or not" is the `かどうか` shape, and it slots straight in front of
`にかかわらず`. <code>会員(かいいん)の有無(うむ)</code> would be wrong here -
有無(うむ) is for whether a *thing* exists, not for what someone is.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 来(く)るにかかわらず、席(せき)は用意(ようい)しておきます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 来(く)る来(こ)ないにかかわらず、席(せき)は用意(ようい)しておきます。
"I'll set a place for you whether you come or not."

A single verb gives `にかかわらず` nothing to sweep aside. The fix is the
affirmative-plus-negative pair. Note the two stems of 来(く)る: the dictionary
form is くる and the negative is こない - the vowel changes, so the furigana cannot
be copied from one to the other.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 値段(ねだん)<b>にかかわらず</b>、質(しつ)のいいものを選(えら)びます。
(b) 値段(ねだん)<b>にもかかわらず</b>、質(しつ)のいいものを選(えら)びます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a policy: price is not one of my criteria, so I buy quality however much it
costs. This is the normal, natural sentence.

(b) is odd, and it is worth understanding why. `にもかかわらず` needs a specific
fact to concede, and a bare 値段(ねだん) is not a fact - it is a category. To make
(b) work you would have to say what the price actually was:
<code>値段(ねだん)が高(たか)いにもかかわらず</code>, "even though it's expensive".
The `も` demands a completed statement in front of it.

</details>

# ～にかかわる

**Meaning** · to have a bearing on; to be bound up with; to be involved in

`にかかわる` is the positive half of the verb whose negative gives you
`にかかわらず`. It says that two things are **connected in a way that matters**.
In its commonest use the connection is a threat - <code>命(いのち)にかかわる</code>,
"it's a matter of life and death" - and the noun in front of it is something
precious that could be damaged.

## Formation

<code>かかわる</code> is an ordinary う-verb (五段(ごだん) / Group 1), so it conjugates
normally: かかわらない, かかわった, かかわって.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (as predicate) | Noun + にかかわる | 命(いのち) → 命(いのち)にかかわる |
| Noun (modifying a noun) | Noun + にかかわる + Noun | 教育(きょういく) → 教育(きょういく)にかかわる仕事(しごと) |
| Continuous / involvement | Noun + にかかわっている | 開発(かいはつ) → 開発(かいはつ)にかかわっている |
| Past | Noun + にかかわった | 事件(じけん) → 事件(じけん)にかかわった人物(じんぶつ) |
| Negative | Noun + にかかわらない | 政治(せいじ) → 政治(せいじ)にはかかわらない |
| て-form, listing a reason | Noun + にかかわるので／から | 信用(しんよう) → 信用(しんよう)にかかわるので |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the kanji is 関(かか)わる; 係(かか)わる is also seen, and older texts
use 拘(かか)わる. The okurigana is わる, so the furigana sits on the single
character: 関(かか)わる, not the whole word.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
それは命(いのち)<b>にかかわる</b>問題(もんだい)です。
That is a matter of life and death.
</pre>

<pre>
会社(かいしゃ)の信用(しんよう)<b>にかかわる</b>から、軽(かる)く考(かんが)えないでほしい。
It affects the company's reputation, so please don't take it lightly.
</pre>

<pre>
彼(かれ)は長年(ながねん)、難民(なんみん)の支援(しえん)<b>にかかわって</b>きた。
He has been involved in refugee support work for many years.
</pre>

<pre>
教育(きょういく)<b>にかかわる</b>仕事(しごと)がしたくて、大学院(だいがくいん)に進(すす)んだ。
I went on to graduate school because I wanted a job connected with education.
</pre>

<pre>
この決定(けってい)は全社員(ぜんしゃいん)の待遇(たいぐう)<b>にかかわります</b>。
This decision has a direct bearing on every employee's conditions.
</pre>

<pre>
睡眠(すいみん)不足(ぶそく)は事故(じこ)に直接(ちょくせつ)<b>かかわる</b>ので、運転手(うんてんしゅ)の勤務(きんむ)時間(じかん)は厳(きび)しく管理(かんり)されている。
Lack of sleep feeds straight into accidents, so drivers' hours are tightly controlled.
</pre>

<pre>
名誉(めいよ)<b>にかかわる</b>ことですから、事実(じじつ)かどうかを確(たし)かめてから発言(はつげん)してください。
Someone's good name is at stake, so check the facts before you speak.
</pre>

<pre>
報告(ほうこく)が一日(いちにち)遅(おく)れただけで人命(じんめい)<b>にかかわる</b>場合(ばあい)もある。
There are cases where a single day's delay in reporting costs lives.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Two senses live in this one form, and telling them apart is the whole page.

The **stakes** sense - <code>命(いのち)にかかわる</code>,
<code>信用(しんよう)にかかわる</code>, <code>名誉(めいよ)にかかわる</code> - is
close to fixed. The nouns that appear in front of it are a short list of things you
can lose: life, health, reputation, honour, someone's future, the survival of the
company. The sentence is a warning, and it is why the pattern turns up in safety
notices and in the moment before a manager tells you to take something seriously.
It is register-neutral in itself but the content is always grave; you would not use
it about a small inconvenience.

The **involvement** sense is usually in the ている form:
<code>プロジェクトにかかわっている</code>, "I'm working on that project." Here the
tone is plain and slightly modest - it says you are one of the people connected to
the work without claiming to run it. <code>事件(じけん)にかかわる</code> in the
past tense is the newspaper phrase for being implicated in an incident, so mind the
company the word keeps: saying <code>あの件(けん)にかかわっていた</code> about
someone can sound accusatory.

The negative <code>かかわらない</code> is a live, useful verb meaning "keep out of
it": <code>あの人(ひと)にはかかわらないほうがいい</code> - "better not get
involved with him." Do not confuse this with `にかかわらず`, which is frozen and no
longer functions as a verb form in the sentence.

## Contrast

### ～にかかわる vs ～に関(かん)する・～について

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に関(かん)する</code> means "on the topic of".
`にかかわる` means "with consequences for".

</div>

Both can end up as "related to" in a dictionary, and that is the trap.

<pre>
環境(かんきょう)に関(かん)する報告書(ほうこくしょ)を読(よ)んだ。
I read a report on the environment.
</pre>

<pre>
環境(かんきょう)<b>にかかわる</b>決定(けってい)は慎重(しんちょう)にすべきだ。
Decisions that affect the environment should be made carefully.
</pre>

The first report is *about* the environment; swapping in `にかかわる` would suggest
the report itself has an impact on the environment, which is not what you meant.
The second is about consequences; swapping in <code>に関(かん)する</code> would
weaken it to "decisions on environmental topics".

There is also a hard structural difference. <code>に関(かん)する</code> only
modifies nouns - <code>に関(かん)する問題(もんだい)</code> - and cannot end a
sentence. `にかかわる` can do both.

### ～にかかわる vs ～にかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same verb, opposite verdicts. `にかかわる` says the thing
**matters**; `にかかわらず` says it **does not**.

</div>

<pre>
健康(けんこう)<b>にかかわる</b>ことなので、自分(じぶん)で決(き)めてください。
It concerns your health, so please decide for yourself.
</pre>

<pre>
健康(けんこう)状態(じょうたい)<b>にかかわらず</b>、全員(ぜんいん)検査(けんさ)を受(う)けてください。
Everyone must be tested, whatever the state of their health.
</pre>

`にかかわらず` is the negative <code>かかわらない</code> in its old ず form, frozen
into a set phrase. Knowing that the two are one verb is the fastest way to stop
mixing them up: if the sentence is claiming a connection, no `ず`; if it is denying
one, `ず`.

### ～にかかわる vs ～に影響(えいきょう)する

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に影響(えいきょう)する</code> reports a measurable
**effect**. `にかかわる` says the thing is **at stake**.

</div>

<pre>
円安(えんやす)は輸入(ゆにゅう)価格(かかく)に影響(えいきょう)する。
A weak yen affects import prices.
</pre>

<pre>
この判断(はんだん)は会社(かいしゃ)の存続(そんぞく)<b>にかかわる</b>。
This decision could determine whether the company survives.
</pre>

<code>影響(えいきょう)する</code> is analytical and can be small: prices moved a
little. `にかかわる` is not gradable - a thing either is or is not at stake - and it
carries an implicit "so be careful". That is why
<code>少(すこ)し命(いのち)にかかわる</code> is not a sentence anyone says.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この事故(じこ)は命(いのち)<b>をかかわる</b>。 → ✅ この事故(じこ)は命(いのち)<b>にかかわる</b>。
  <code>かかわる</code> is intransitive: the thing it connects to is marked with
  `に`, never `を`.

- ❌ 環境(かんきょう)<b>にかかわる</b>本(ほん)を読(よ)んだ。（meaning "a book about the environment"） → ✅ 環境(かんきょう)<b>に関(かん)する</b>本(ほん)を読(よ)んだ。
  For a plain topic, use <code>に関(かん)する</code>. `にかかわる` would claim the
  book has consequences for the environment.

- ❌ 天気(てんき)<b>にかかわる</b>、試合(しあい)は行(おこな)われる。 → ✅ 天気(てんき)<b>にかかわらず</b>、試合(しあい)は行(おこな)われる。
  Dropping the `ず` reverses the meaning and leaves the clause dangling. The
  "regardless" pattern is the negative form.

- ❌ ちょっと命(いのち)<b>にかかわる</b>問題(もんだい)です。 → ✅ 命(いのち)<b>にかかわる</b>問題(もんだい)です。
  The stakes sense is absolute. Softening it with ちょっと sounds like a joke, which
  is occasionally the point but rarely what a learner intends.

## Practice

**1.** 安全(あんぜん)に____ことですから、面倒(めんどう)でも手順(てじゅん)を守(まも)ってください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

安全(あんぜん)に<b>かかわる</b>ことですから…

安全(あんぜん) is exactly the kind of noun the stakes sense takes - something that
can be lost. The plain form modifies こと directly, with no `の` in between.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 日本(にほん)の経済(けいざい)（にかかわる・に関(かん)する）統計(とうけい)をまとめた資料(しりょう)です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

日本(にほん)の経済(けいざい)<b>に関(かん)する</b>統計(とうけい)

The statistics are *on the topic of* the economy; they do not have consequences for
it. <code>に関(かん)する</code> is the neutral "concerning". `にかかわる` here
would oddly suggest the figures themselves move the economy.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "She has been involved in this research since she was a student."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

彼女(かのじょ)は学生(がくせい)のころからこの研究(けんきゅう)に<b>かかわって</b>います。

The involvement sense wants the ている form, because the connection is ongoing.
<code>かかわってきました</code> is also natural and adds the sense of a long stretch
of time leading up to now.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 会社(かいしゃ)の将来(しょうらい)にかかわらず問題(もんだい)なので、全員(ぜんいん)で話(はな)し合(あ)いましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 会社(かいしゃ)の将来(しょうらい)に<b>かかわる</b>問題(もんだい)なので、全員(ぜんいん)で話(はな)し合(あ)いましょう。
"It's a problem that bears on the company's future, so let's all discuss it."

The intended meaning is that the future *is* at stake, so the affirmative form is
needed. `にかかわらず` also cannot modify 問題(もんだい) directly - it is
adverbial, and would need a comma and a full clause after it.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 彼(かれ)はその計画(けいかく)に<b>かかわっている</b>。
(b) 彼(かれ)はその事件(じけん)に<b>かかわっている</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is neutral work vocabulary: he is one of the people on the project. It is what
you would say introducing a colleague, and it makes no claim about how central he
is.

(b) is not neutral. With 事件(じけん) - an incident, a scandal, a crime - the same
verb becomes the language of news reporting and means "he is implicated". The
grammar has not changed at all; the noun in front has dragged the whole sentence
into a different register. This is worth remembering before using
<code>かかわっている</code> about a person and a problem.

</details>

# ～を問わず

**Meaning** · regardless of; without regard to - this is not something we ask about

<code>問(と)う</code> means "to ask, to call into question", so
<code>を問(と)わず</code> literally says *we do not ask*. That is its flavour: the
speaker is an institution announcing which boxes it will **not** be ticking. Age,
sex, nationality, experience - named and then waved through.

## Formation

<code>を問(と)わず</code> takes a **noun**, and the noun is nearly always a short
one naming a category or a two-way split.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + を問(と)わず | 経験(けいけん) → 経験(けいけん)を問(と)わず |
| Paired nouns, written together | Noun + Noun + を問(と)わず | 男女(だんじょ)・昼夜(ちゅうや)・国内外(こくないがい) を問(と)わず |
| Two nouns joined by か | Noun + か + Noun + かを問(と)わず | 新品(しんぴん)か中古(ちゅうこ)かを問(と)わず |
| Noun + の有無(うむ) | Noun + の有無(うむ) + を問(と)わず | 資格(しかく)の有無(うむ)を問(と)わず |
| Modifying a noun | Noun + を問(と)わない + Noun | 年齢(ねんれい)を問(と)わない募集(ぼしゅう) |
| Written / listing form | Noun + を問(と)わず、… | in notices, before a comma |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>を問(と)わず</code> is a fixed adverbial phrase, but
<code>問(と)う</code> underneath it is a live verb, which is why the
noun-modifying form is <code>を問(と)わない</code> and the polite predicate form is
<code>を問(と)いません</code>: <code>経験(けいけん)は問(と)いません</code>,
"experience not required".

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
経験(けいけん)<b>を問(と)わず</b>募集(ぼしゅう)しています。
We are recruiting regardless of experience.
</pre>

<pre>
男女(だんじょ)<b>を問(と)わず</b>応募(おうぼ)できます。
Men and women alike may apply.
</pre>

<pre>
この店(みせ)は年中(ねんじゅう)無休(むきゅう)で、昼夜(ちゅうや)<b>を問(と)わず</b>営業(えいぎょう)している。
The shop never closes - it trades day and night alike.
</pre>

<pre>
季節(きせつ)<b>を問(と)わず</b>楽(たの)しめる温泉(おんせん)として知(し)られています。
It is known as a hot spring you can enjoy in any season.
</pre>

<pre>
大人(おとな)子(こ)ども<b>を問(と)わず</b>、この映画(えいが)には泣(な)かされる。
Adult or child, this film gets you.
</pre>

<pre>
受賞(じゅしょう)歴(れき)の有無(うむ)<b>を問(と)わず</b>、どなたでも出品(しゅっぴん)していただけます。
Anyone may submit a piece, whether or not they have won awards before.
</pre>

<pre>
国内外(こくないがい)<b>を問(と)わず</b>、優(すぐ)れた研究(けんきゅう)には同(おな)じ基準(きじゅん)で助成金(じょせいきん)を出(だ)す方針(ほうしん)です。
Our policy is to fund outstanding research on the same criteria at home and abroad.
</pre>

<pre>
正社員(せいしゃいん)か派遣(はけん)か<b>を問(と)わず</b>、同(おな)じ仕事(しごと)には同(おな)じ賃金(ちんぎん)を払(はら)うべきだ。
Permanent or agency, the same work should be paid the same wage.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is public-notice Japanese. It belongs to job ads, competition rules, opening
hours, museum leaflets and terms of service, and its effect is to sound official
and even-handed. Nothing about it is rude in conversation, but it is stiff: a
friend who asked whether your local café is open on Sundays would find
<code>曜日(ようび)を問(と)わず営業(えいぎょう)しています</code> a strange
answer, where <code>毎日(まいにち)やってるよ</code> is what you would actually say.

The nouns that go in front are conventionalised, and learning the stock list is
more useful than learning the rule: 年齢(ねんれい), 性別(せいべつ), 国籍(こくせき), 経験(けいけん), 資格(しかく),
学歴(がくれき), 季節(きせつ), 昼夜(ちゅうや), 男女(だんじょ),
新旧(しんきゅう), 大小(だいしょう), 理由(りゆう). Notice how many are
two-kanji compounds that already contain both poles of a contrast.
昼夜(ちゅうや) is not "daytime"; it is "day-and-night", and that built-in pairing
is why it fits so neatly.

Because <code>問(と)う</code> is a real verb, the phrase carries a faint agent -
somebody who would have been doing the asking. That makes
<code>を問(と)わず</code> natural where there is a screening process to waive, and
slightly odd where there is nobody to do any asking. For a plain fact of nature -
the tide comes in whatever you think - `にかかわらず` sits better.

## Contrast

### ～を問(と)わず vs ～にかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を問(と)わず</code> waives a **criterion**;
`にかかわらず` dismisses a **variable**. Only `にかかわらず` takes a clause
comfortably.

</div>

<pre>
学歴(がくれき)<b>を問(と)わず</b>採用(さいよう)します。
We hire without regard to educational background.
</pre>

<pre>
大学(だいがく)を出(で)ている出(で)ていない<b>にかかわらず</b>採用(さいよう)します。
We hire whether or not you have been to university.
</pre>

The mechanical difference is the one to hold on to. `にかかわらず` will follow a
verb pair or a `かどうか` clause; <code>を問(と)わず</code> wants a noun, and a
long clause in front of it reads like a badly drafted form. Where both are
possible, <code>を問(と)わず</code> is the crisper, more formulaic choice - it is
what the printed advertisement says.

### ～を問(と)わず vs ～を除(のぞ)いて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を問(と)わず</code> says the category is
**irrelevant**; <code>を除(のぞ)いて</code> says one member is **carved out**.

</div>

This is the sharpest line in this whole group of patterns, and learners blur it
because both English translations start with a dismissive gesture.

<pre>
年齢(ねんれい)<b>を問(と)わず</b>入場(にゅうじょう)できます。
Anyone can come in, whatever their age.
</pre>

<pre>
小学生(しょうがくせい)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>入場(にゅうじょう)できます。
Everyone can come in except primary-school children.
</pre>

In the first, age never enters the decision - the door is open to all. In the
second, one named group is standing outside it. <code>を問(と)わず</code> takes an
*axis* (年齢(ねんれい), 国籍(こくせき)); <code>を除(のぞ)いて</code> takes a
*member* (小学生(しょうがくせい), 田中(たなか)さん). If the noun could answer
"which one?", you want <code>除(のぞ)く</code>.

### ～を問(と)わず vs ～もかまわず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を問(と)わず</code> is **neutral policy**;
`もかまわず` is somebody **ignoring what they should have minded**.

</div>

<pre>
時間(じかん)<b>を問(と)わず</b>ご相談(そうだん)をお受(う)けしています。
We take enquiries at any hour.
</pre>

<pre>
時間(じかん)<b>もかまわず</b>電話(でんわ)をかけてくる。
He rings at all hours without a thought.
</pre>

The Japanese is nearly parallel and the attitudes are opposite. The first is a
service being advertised; the second is a complaint. `もかまわず` always implies
that the thing being ignored had a claim on the person's attention, so it cannot be
used for a rule you are announcing about yourself.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 田中(たなか)さん<b>を問(と)わず</b>、全員(ぜんいん)来(き)た。 → ✅ 田中(たなか)さん<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、全員(ぜんいん)来(き)た。
  A named individual is a member, not a criterion. Waiving a criterion is
  <code>問(と)う</code>; removing a member is <code>除(のぞ)く</code>.

- ❌ 参加(さんか)する<b>を問(と)わず</b> → ✅ 参加(さんか)するかどうか<b>を問(と)わず</b>（or 参加(さんか)の有無(うむ)を問(と)わず）
  A bare verb cannot sit in front of it. Nominalise with `かどうか`, or use the noun
  compound with 有無(うむ).

- ❌ 国籍(こくせき)<b>に問(と)わず</b> → ✅ 国籍(こくせき)<b>を問(と)わず</b>
  <code>問(と)う</code> is transitive, so its object takes `を`. `に` belongs to
  `にかかわらず`, whose verb is intransitive. Getting the two particles crossed is
  the single commonest slip with this pair.

- ❌ 明日(あした)は天気(てんき)<b>を問(と)わず</b>寒(さむ)くなるでしょう。 → ✅ 明日(あした)は天気(てんき)<b>にかかわらず</b>寒(さむ)くなるでしょう。
  Nobody is screening the weather. With no implied asker,
  <code>を問(と)わず</code> sounds like a form filled in by mistake.

Professional versus amateur is a two-way category, and the sentence is competition

no particle between the two halves.

</details>

# ～もかまわず

**Meaning** · without caring about; paying no heed to - and the speaker thinks they should have

<code>かまう</code> means "to mind, to concern oneself with", so `もかまわず`
describes someone pressing ahead **without minding** something that had a fair claim
on their attention. That last clause is the whole point. This is not a neutral
"regardless of": there is always a judgement in it, usually disapproval, sometimes
admiration for the person's single-mindedness.

## Formation

The thing being ignored is marked with `も`. A clause has to be nominalised with
`の` first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + もかまわず | 人目(ひとめ) → 人目(ひとめ)もかまわず |
| Verb | plain form + のもかまわず | 濡(ぬ)れる → 濡(ぬ)れるのもかまわず |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + のもかまわず | 終(お)わっていない → 終(お)わっていないのもかまわず |
| い-adjective | ～い + のもかまわず | 遅(おそ)い → 遅(おそ)いのもかまわず |
| な-adjective | ～な + のもかまわず | 迷惑(めいわく)な → 迷惑(めいわく)なのもかまわず |
| With に | ～もかまわずに | 人目(ひとめ)もかまわずに |
| Fixed adverb (no も) | なりふりかまわず | - |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the kanji is 構(かま)わず, from 構(かま)う. It is written in kana at
least as often as in kanji. <code>なりふりかまわず</code> - "regardless of how it
looks", from 形(なり)振(ふ)り, one's appearance and bearing - is a set adverb
and drops the `も`.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
彼女(かのじょ)は人目(ひとめ)<b>もかまわず</b>泣(な)き出(だ)した。
She burst into tears with no thought for who was watching.
</pre>

<pre>
雨(あめ)に濡(ぬ)れるの<b>もかまわず</b>、子(こ)どもたちは外(そと)を走(はし)り回(まわ)っていた。
The children were tearing around outside, not caring that they were getting soaked.
</pre>

<pre>
服(ふく)が汚(よご)れるの<b>もかまわず</b>、彼(かれ)は地面(じめん)に座(すわ)り込(こ)んだ。
He sat down on the ground, never mind what it did to his clothes.
</pre>

<pre>
周(まわ)りの迷惑(めいわく)<b>もかまわず</b>大声(おおごえ)で電話(でんわ)している人(ひと)がいる。
There's someone on the phone at full volume, oblivious to everyone around him.
</pre>

<pre>
なりふり<b>かまわず</b>働(はたら)いてきたおかげで、店(みせ)をここまで大(おお)きくできた。
It's because I worked without a scrap of dignity that I got the shop this far.
</pre>

<pre>
時間(じかん)が遅(おそ)いの<b>もかまわず</b>電話(でんわ)をかけてきて、三十分(さんじゅっぷん)も愚痴(ぐち)を言(い)い続(つづ)けた。
He rang me with no regard for the hour and complained for a solid half hour.
</pre>

<pre>
医者(いしゃ)に止(と)められているの<b>もかまわず</b>、祖父(そふ)はまだ畑(はたけ)に出(で)ている。
Doctor's orders or not, my grandfather is still out working the field.
</pre>

<pre>
列(れつ)の順番(じゅんばん)<b>もかまわず</b>割(わ)り込(こ)んでくる客(きゃく)に、店員(てんいん)は困(こま)った顔(かお)をしていた。
The assistant looked helpless in the face of customers pushing in with no regard for the queue.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`もかまわず` is descriptive, not something you say about yourself in the present. It
narrates a third party: you are watching someone behave, and you are commenting. In
speech it is a shade literary but perfectly usable; in writing it is very common,
and novels use it constantly to sketch a character in four syllables.

The judgement it carries flexes with the noun. With 人目(ひとめ),
迷惑(めいわく), 順番(じゅんばん) the flavour is critical - the person is being
inconsiderate. With 服(ふく)が汚(よご)れるの, 雨(あめ)に濡(ぬ)れるの,
なりふり it turns admiring or at least sympathetic: they cared so much about the
goal that they stopped caring about themselves. Nothing in the grammar decides
which; the noun does.

About yourself the natural tense is past, in a slightly rueful register:
<code>あのときは人目(ひとめ)もかまわず泣(な)いてしまった</code>. Saying it about
your own present behaviour would be announcing that you are being rude, which is
either a joke or a threat.

<code>かまう</code> is worth keeping separate from <code>かまわない</code> as a
permission word. <code>かまいません</code> - "I don't mind, go ahead" - is one of the
most useful polite phrases in the language, and it comes from exactly this verb.
The difference is who is doing the not-minding: in <code>かまいません</code> it is
you, generously; in `もかまわず` it is somebody else, thoughtlessly.

## Contrast

### ～もかまわず vs ～にもかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `もかまわず` is about the person's **attitude**;
`にもかかわらず` is a **neutral logical** concession.

</div>

<pre>
寒(さむ)いの<b>もかまわず</b>、彼(かれ)は上着(うわぎ)を脱(ぬ)いだ。
Never mind the cold, he took his jacket off.
</pre>

<pre>
寒(さむ)かった<b>にもかかわらず</b>、会場(かいじょう)は満員(まんいん)だった。
Despite the cold, the venue was full.
</pre>

The first puts us inside his head - he registered the cold and dismissed it. The
second makes no claim about anyone's mind; it simply notes that two facts sit oddly
together. That is why `にもかかわらず` works with an inanimate subject and
`もかまわず` does not: a venue cannot ignore anything.

### ～もかまわず vs ～てもかまわない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same verb, opposite direction. `てもかまわない` **grants
permission**; `もかまわず` **reports someone taking it without asking**.

</div>

<pre>
ここに座(すわ)っ<b>てもかまいません</b>か。
Would you mind if I sat here?
</pre>

<pre>
席(せき)が空(あ)いていないの<b>もかまわず</b>、彼(かれ)は通路(つうろ)に座(すわ)り込(こ)んだ。
Never mind that there were no free seats, he plonked himself down in the aisle.
</pre>

Learners met <code>てもかまわない</code> at N3 and often assume `もかまわず` is just
its adverbial form. It is not, and the giveaway is the missing `て`:
<code>てもかまわない</code> takes a て-form and is a statement about what is
permitted, while `もかまわず` takes a noun and describes behaviour.

### ～もかまわず vs ～を問(と)わず・～にかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `もかまわず` ignores something **that mattered**;
<code>を問(と)わず</code> and `にかかわらず` declare that it **never mattered**.

</div>

<pre>
天候(てんこう)<b>にかかわらず</b>、工事(こうじ)は予定(よてい)どおり進(すす)めます。
The work will proceed on schedule whatever the weather.
</pre>

<pre>
台風(たいふう)が近(ちか)づいているの<b>もかまわず</b>、工事(こうじ)を続(つづ)けさせた。
He had them carry on working, never mind that a typhoon was coming in.
</pre>

The first is a policy, and it is reassuring. The second is a report of recklessness,
and it is close to an accusation. Choose by asking whether the thing named had a
legitimate claim on someone's attention: if yes, and it was overridden,
`もかまわず`; if it was simply not one of the criteria, the other two.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 人目(ひとめ)<b>をかまわず</b>泣(な)いた。 → ✅ 人目(ひとめ)<b>もかまわず</b>泣(な)いた。
  The particle is `も`, not `を`. The `も` is doing real work - "not even this" -
  and swapping it for `を` flattens the sentence. The one fixed exception is
  <code>なりふりかまわず</code>, which has no particle at all.

- ❌ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)る<b>もかまわず</b>出(で)かけた。 → ✅ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っている<b>のもかまわず</b>出(で)かけた。
  A clause needs `の` to become a noun before `も` can attach.

- ❌ 私(わたし)は人目(ひとめ)<b>もかまわず</b>話(はな)します。 → ✅ 私(わたし)は人目(ひとめ)を気(き)にせずに話(はな)します。
  In the present tense about yourself this announces your own bad manners. For a
  neutral statement about your own habits, use
  <code>を気(き)にせずに</code>.

- ❌ 静(しず)かにしなければならないの<b>もかまわず</b>図書館(としょかん)は閉(し)まった。 → ✅ 静(しず)かにしなければならないの<b>もかまわず</b>、彼(かれ)らは図書館(としょかん)で騒(さわ)いでいた。
  `もかまわず` needs an animate subject doing the ignoring. A library closing has no
  attitude.

## Practice

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

周(まわ)りの目(め)<b>もかまわず</b>、駅(えき)のホームで大声(おおごえ)で歌(うた)い始(はじ)めた。

Other people's stares are precisely the kind of thing that should have restrained
him, and he overrode them - the core condition for `もかまわず`. A bare noun takes

</details>

**2.** Choose: 年齢(ねんれい)（もかまわず・を問(と)わず）、この教室(きょうしつ)はどなたでも受(う)けられます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

年齢(ねんれい)<b>を問(と)わず</b>、どなたでも受(う)けられます。

This is a class advertising itself, so the sentence is a policy: age is not one of
the criteria. `もかまわず` would accuse somebody of ignoring an age limit that
existed, which is not what a course brochure wants to say.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "She kept working, never mind that she had a fever."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

彼女(かのじょ)は熱(ねつ)があるの<b>もかまわず</b>働(はたら)き続(つづ)けた。

<code>熱(ねつ)がある</code> is a clause, so it needs `の` before `も`. The past
tense and the third-person subject are both typical: this is a sentence about
watching someone push themselves too hard.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 時間(じかん)が遅(おそ)いもかまわず、彼(かれ)は練習(れんしゅう)を続(つづ)けた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 時間(じかん)が遅(おそ)い<b>のもかまわず</b>、彼(かれ)は練習(れんしゅう)を続(つづ)けた。
"He kept practising, never mind how late it was."

<code>時間(じかん)が遅(おそ)い</code> is a full clause, and `も` cannot attach to
a clause. The nominaliser `の` turns it into something a particle can follow. Note
also the reading: 遅(おそ)い is "late" as an adjective, while
遅(おく)れる is the verb "to be delayed" - same kanji, different stem.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 服(ふく)が汚(よご)れるの<b>もかまわず</b>、彼(かれ)は猫(ねこ)を助(たす)けに川(かわ)に入(はい)った。
(b) 服(ふく)が汚(よご)れるの<b>もかまわず</b>、彼(かれ)はソファに寝(ね)そべった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The grammar is identical and the verdicts are opposite. In (a) the thing he stopped
caring about was his own clothes, and he stopped caring in order to save a cat - so
the sentence reads as praise for his single-mindedness.

In (b) nothing is at stake but his own laziness, so the same phrase reads as
criticism: he could not be bothered. `もかまわず` supplies only the fact that
something was overridden; whether that is admirable or slovenly comes from what he
was doing instead. This is why the pattern is so useful in fiction and so easy to
misfire in a compliment.

</details>

# ～はともかく

**Meaning** · A aside; whether or not A - what I actually want to talk about is B

`はともかく` **demotes** something. The speaker names a point, declines to settle it,
and moves the conversation to the part that matters. What follows is always the real
message; the thing in front of `ともかく` is being parked, and very often it is being
parked because it is the weak spot.

## Formation

`ともかく` follows a noun or a nominalised question. `は` is part of the pattern and
is not usually dropped.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + はともかく | 値段(ねだん) → 値段(ねだん)はともかく |
| Noun (fuller form) | Noun + はともかくとして | 見(み)た目(め) → 見(み)た目(め)はともかくとして |
| Yes/no question | plain form + かどうか + はともかく | 勝(か)てる → 勝(か)てるかどうかはともかく |
| Embedded question | question word … + か + はともかく | いつ来(く)る → いつ来(く)るかはともかく |
| Two things compared | A はともかく B は… | 土曜(どよう)はともかく日曜(にちよう)は… |
| Adverbial, on its own | ともかく（＝ anyway） | ともかく行(い)ってみよう |

<div class="note">

**Note** - bare <code>ともかく</code> with no `は` is a separate adverb meaning
"anyway, at any rate", and it is extremely common in speech:
<code>ともかく座(すわ)ってください</code>. Same word, different job - the `は`
version needs something in front of it to demote.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
味(あじ)<b>はともかく</b>、値段(ねだん)は安(やす)い。
The taste is another matter, but it's cheap.
</pre>

<pre>
勝(か)てるかどうか<b>はともかく</b>、出(で)てみる価値(かち)はある。
Whether we can win is another question - it's worth entering.
</pre>

<pre>
見(み)た目(め)<b>はともかくとして</b>、この机(つくえ)は丈夫(じょうぶ)だ。
Looks aside, this desk is solid.
</pre>

<pre>
理由(りゆう)<b>はともかく</b>、遅刻(ちこく)したことは事実(じじつ)です。
Whatever your reasons, the fact is you were late.
</pre>

<pre>
私(わたし)のこと<b>はともかく</b>、まずお母(かあ)さんに連絡(れんらく)したほうがいいよ。
Never mind about me - you should call your mother first.
</pre>

<pre>
内容(ないよう)が正(ただ)しいかどうか<b>はともかく</b>、締(し)め切(き)りまでに出(だ)さないと話(はなし)にならない。
Right or wrong, if it isn't in by the deadline it doesn't count at all.
</pre>

<pre>
土曜日(どようび)<b>はともかく</b>、日曜日(にちようび)は人(ひと)が多(おお)すぎて並(なら)ぶ気(き)になれない。
Saturday I could live with, but Sunday it's so crowded I can't face queueing.
</pre>

<pre>
新人(しんじん)のうち<b>はともかく</b>、３年(ねん)も経(た)てば自分(じぶん)で判断(はんだん)できるようになってほしい。
While you're new it's one thing, but after three years I'd like you to be able to judge for yourself.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`はともかく` is conversational but not slangy. It is at home in a meeting, in an
email, and over a drink; the fuller <code>はともかくとして</code> is a step more
composed and fits writing and formal speech. Neither is impolite.

What makes it worth learning is the implication it drags along. When you park
something with `ともかく`, the listener infers that it did not survive inspection.
<code>味(あじ)はともかく、安(やす)い</code> is understood as "the taste is
nothing to write home about, but it's cheap." That is why
<code>値段(ねだん)はともかく、おいしい</code> is a compliment with a bill attached.
You cannot use it to park something you think is excellent - if the first item is
genuinely fine, the pattern misfires.

The politeness use follows from this. <code>私(わたし)のことはともかく</code>
deflects attention from yourself so that the other person's problem can take the
floor, and it is a warm thing to say. <code>あなたのことはともかく</code>, aimed at
your listener, is close to "never mind you" and lands badly. Direction matters more
than register here.

One more habit: `はともかく` very often introduces a whole clause with `かどうか`,
because the thing being parked is a question that has not been decided. That shape -
<code>できるかどうかはともかく</code>, <code>本当(ほんとう)かどうかはともかく</code>
- is worth memorising whole, since it is how the pattern most often appears in
adult speech.

## Contrast

### ～はともかく vs ～は別(べつ)として

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>は別(べつ)として</code> is **neutral bracketing**;
`はともかく` quietly says the parked item **is the weak one**.

</div>

<pre>
デザイン<b>はともかく</b>、性能(せいのう)はいい。
The design is another matter, but the performance is good.
</pre>

<pre>
デザイン<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、性能(せいのう)を比(くら)べてみましょう。
Setting design aside, let's compare the performance.
</pre>

The first is a verdict on the design and it is not a kind one. The second says
nothing at all about the design; it just removes it from the current comparison so
that a fair one can be made. If you want to be genuinely even-handed - in a report,
in a review, when talking about a person - <code>は別(べつ)として</code> is the
safer instrument.

There is also a difference in what they take. <code>は別(べつ)として</code>
happily heads a long analytical clause; `はともかく` prefers a short noun or a
`かどうか` question and starts to feel clumsy when the parked item runs on.

### ～はともかく vs ～はさておき

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `はさておき` manages the **conversation**; `はともかく`
weighs the **content**.

</div>

<pre>
冗談(じょうだん)<b>はさておき</b>、そろそろ本題(ほんだい)に入(はい)りましょう。
Joking aside, let's get down to business.
</pre>

<pre>
冗談(じょうだん)<b>はともかく</b>、今(いま)の話(はなし)には一理(いちり)ある。
Setting the joking aside, there's something in what you just said.
</pre>

`さておき` picks a topic up and puts it down elsewhere: it is a steering move, and
you can feel the speaker taking charge of the agenda. `ともかく` does not steer -
it evaluates, ranking one thing below another. That is why
<code>それはさておき</code> works as a stand-alone hinge in a speech and
<code>それはともかく</code>, while possible, sounds more like a shrug.

### ～はともかく vs ～ならともかく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `は` parks something **real**; `なら` raises a **hypothetical
case that would have been acceptable**.

</div>

This one costs marks in the exam and meaning in real life.

<pre>
子(こ)ども<b>はともかく</b>、大人(おとな)のチケットは買(か)っておこう。
Never mind the children's tickets - let's buy the adults' in advance.
</pre>

<pre>
子(こ)ども<b>ならともかく</b>、大人(おとな)がそんなことを言(い)うなんて。
If it were a child, fine - but for an adult to say that…
</pre>

In the first, children exist in the situation and are being set to one side. In the
second, no child is involved at all: the speaker invents the child as the case in
which the behaviour would have been forgivable, in order to condemn the case that
actually happened. `ならともかく` is therefore almost always followed by criticism,
while `はともかく` is neutral about what comes next.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 勝(か)てる<b>はともかく</b>、練習(れんしゅう)は続(つづ)けよう。 → ✅ 勝(か)てるかどうか<b>はともかく</b>、練習(れんしゅう)は続(つづ)けよう。
  A bare verb cannot be parked. Turn the undecided point into a question with
  `かどうか` first.

- ❌ この絵(え)は色(いろ)<b>はともかく</b>、本当(ほんとう)にすばらしい。 → ✅ この絵(え)は色(いろ)も形(かたち)も本当(ほんとう)にすばらしい。
  You cannot park something you are about to praise. `ともかく` implies the colour
  is the picture's weak point, which contradicts the rest of the sentence.

- ❌ あなたのこと<b>はともかく</b>、話(はなし)を続(つづ)けます。 → ✅ その件(けん)<b>はともかく</b>、話(はなし)を続(つづ)けます。
  Aimed at a person in front of you, this reads as "never mind about you". Park the
  topic, not the listener.

- ❌ 大人(おとな)<b>はともかく</b>、子(こ)どもがそんなことを言(い)うのはひどい。 → ✅ 子(こ)ども<b>ならともかく</b>、大人(おとな)がそんなことを言(い)うのはひどい。
  If the point is that one case would have been excusable, you need the
  hypothetical `なら`, and the excusable case goes first.

## Practice

**1.** 間(ま)に合(あ)うかどうか____、まず駅(えき)まで走(はし)ろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

間(ま)に合(あ)うかどうか<b>はともかく</b>、まず駅(えき)まで走(はし)ろう。

The undecided question is parked and the urgent action takes the floor. `かどうか`
is what makes the verb clause available to `は`, since `ともかく` needs something
noun-shaped in front of it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 経験(けいけん)（はともかく・ならともかく）、やる気(き)だけは誰(だれ)にも負(ま)けません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

経験(けいけん)<b>はともかく</b>、やる気(き)だけは誰(だれ)にも負(ま)けません。

The speaker's experience is a real quantity being set to one side - and, tellingly,
it is the weak point, which is exactly the flavour `はともかく` carries.
`ならともかく` would require a hypothetical situation and a complaint after it,
neither of which fits a job interview.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I don't know whether it will sell, but the idea itself is interesting."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

売(う)れるかどうか<b>はともかく</b>、アイデアそのものは面白(おもしろ)い。

"Whether it will sell" is the `かどうか` shape, and the sentence's real payload -
the idea is interesting - comes after. Note that the parked item is again the
doubtful one; that is not a coincidence but the pattern's built-in slant.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 彼(かれ)の説明(せつめい)はともかくとして、とてもわかりやすかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 彼(かれ)の説明(せつめい)はとてもわかりやすかった。
or ✅ 話(はな)し方(かた)<b>はともかくとして</b>、説明(せつめい)の中身(なかみ)はわかりやすかった。

The original parks his explanation and then praises the very thing it parked. Once
something is put aside with `ともかく`, the compliment has to land on something
else - so either drop the pattern, or park a different item.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 値段(ねだん)<b>はともかく</b>、この部屋(へや)は気(き)に入(い)った。
(b) 値段(ねだん)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、この部屋(へや)は気(き)に入(い)った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) tells the estate agent, without saying so, that the rent is a problem. `ともかく`
ranks what it parks, and the listener hears "it's expensive, but".

(b) is more measured: price is a separate question that we will come to, and my
feeling about the room is being reported independently of it. It leaves open the
possibility that the price is perfectly reasonable.

In practice (a) is the more common thing to say, because most of the time the item
you park really is the one you have doubts about. Reach for (b) when you do not want
that inference drawn - in a written assessment, or when talking about someone's
work in front of them.

</details>

# ～は別として

**Meaning** · leaving A out of consideration; A is a separate question - now, about B

<code>別(べつ)</code> is "separate", and <code>は別(べつ)として</code> does
exactly what it says: it takes one item out of the current discussion and treats it
as a different matter, so that the rest can be judged cleanly. It is the neutral
member of this family. Unlike `ともかく` it passes no verdict on what it removes -
the item may be excellent, terrible or simply undecided.

## Formation

The parked item is a noun or a nominalised question, marked with `は`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + は別(べつ)として | 費用(ひよう) → 費用(ひよう)は別(べつ)として |
| Noun (spoken variant) | Noun + は別(べつ)にして | 冗談(じょうだん) → 冗談(じょうだん)は別(べつ)にして |
| Yes/no question | plain form + かどうか + は別(べつ)として | できる → できるかどうかは別(べつ)として |
| Embedded question | question word … か + は別(べつ)として | だれが悪(わる)いか → だれが悪(わる)いかは別(べつ)として |
| Marked with を | Noun + を別(べつ)として | 一部(いちぶ)の例(れい) → 一部(いちぶ)の例(れい)を別(べつ)として |
| As a predicate | Noun + は別(べつ)だ | 今回(こんかい) → 今回(こんかい)は別(べつ)だ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>は別(べつ)にして</code> is the same pattern in a slightly more
spoken jacket; the two are interchangeable in almost every sentence.
<code>は別(べつ)として</code> is the safer default in writing.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
理由(りゆう)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、結果(けっか)だけを見(み)よう。
Reasons aside, let's look only at the outcome.
</pre>

<pre>
好(す)き嫌(きら)い<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、まず一口(ひとくち)食(た)べてみてください。
Whether you like it or not is a separate question - just try one bite first.
</pre>

<pre>
可能(かのう)かどうか<b>は別(べつ)にして</b>、アイデアを全部(ぜんぶ)書(か)き出(だ)してみよう。
Never mind for now whether it's feasible - let's get every idea down on paper.
</pre>

<pre>
費用(ひよう)の問題(もんだい)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、この計画(けいかく)自体(じたい)には賛成(さんせい)です。
Leaving the question of cost to one side, I'm in favour of the plan itself.
</pre>

<pre>
発音(はつおん)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、漢字(かんじ)の読(よ)み方(かた)だけは自信(じしん)がある。
My pronunciation is a separate matter, but I'm confident about kanji readings at least.
</pre>

<pre>
例外(れいがい)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、まず基本(きほん)のルールから覚(おぼ)えましょう。
Exceptions are a different story - start by learning the basic rules.
</pre>

<pre>
彼(かれ)が正(ただ)しかったかどうか<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、あの場(ば)で声(こえ)を上(あ)げたのは勇気(ゆうき)のいることだった。
Whether or not he was right is another question; speaking up in that room took courage.
</pre>

<pre>
勝(か)ち負(ま)け<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、最後(さいご)まで全力(ぜんりょく)で戦(たたか)った選手(せんしゅ)たちに拍手(はくしゅ)を送(おく)りたい。
Win or lose, I want to applaud the players who gave everything to the final whistle.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and safe almost everywhere. It appears in casual speech, in
meetings, and in written argument, and nothing about it is stiff or soft. If you
learn only one member of this family for productive use, this is the one, because
it is the hardest to offend anyone with.

Its real value is analytical. <code>は別(べつ)として</code> is how you isolate a
variable so the discussion can proceed: you are not dismissing the item, you are
saying *not now*. That implicit promise to return to it is why the pattern is so
common in planning and evaluation - <code>予算(よさん)は別(べつ)として</code>
means the budget will be dealt with, just not in this breath. If you genuinely never
intend to consider the thing, `にかかわらず` says so honestly.

Because it is neutral, it is also the polite choice for talking about people. When
you must praise one aspect of someone's work and not another,
<code>文章(ぶんしょう)の長(なが)さは別(べつ)として</code> lets you do it
without the audible sigh that `ともかく` carries.

Two idioms are worth having. <code>それは別(べつ)として</code> is a compact way to
change tack mid-conversation, and <code>話(はなし)は別(べつ)だ</code> - "that's
a different matter" - is what you say when someone's new information changes your
position: <code>本人(ほんにん)が希望(きぼう)しているなら話(はなし)は別(べつ)だ</code>.

## Contrast

### ～は別(べつ)として vs ～はともかく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both park an item. `ともかく` **judges** it on the way past;
<code>別(べつ)として</code> **does not**.

</div>

<pre>
文字(もじ)の大(おお)きさ<b>はともかく</b>、この資料(しりょう)はよくできている。
Font size aside, this document is well put together.
</pre>

<pre>
文字(もじ)の大(おお)きさ<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、この資料(しりょう)はよくできている。
Leaving the font size as a separate question, this document is well put together.
</pre>

Read the first to the person who made the document and they will go home and change
the font: `ともかく` marks the parked item as the flaw. Read the second and they
will hear only that the layout is a different discussion. The English translations
are nearly identical; the social effect is not.

### ～は別(べつ)として vs ～を除(のぞ)いて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>別(べつ)として</code> removes a **consideration**;
<code>を除(のぞ)いて</code> removes a **member of a set**.

</div>

<pre>
土日(どにち)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、毎日(まいにち)営業(えいぎょう)しています。
We're open every day except Saturday and Sunday.
</pre>

<pre>
土日(どにち)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、平日(へいじつ)の営業(えいぎょう)時間(じかん)を決(き)めましょう。
Weekends are a separate question - let's settle the weekday hours.
</pre>

The test is whether the sentence after it makes a claim about a *set*. If it does -
"every day", "everyone", "all the members" - and the parked item would have been
inside that set, you need <code>除(のぞ)く</code>. If instead the sentence is
choosing what to talk about, <code>別(べつ)として</code>. A useful symptom: an
answer that could be written as a percentage or a headcount belongs to
<code>除(のぞ)く</code>.

### ～は別(べつ)として vs ～にかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>別(べつ)として</code> **postpones**; `にかかわらず`
**dismisses for good**.

</div>

<pre>
経験(けいけん)<b>にかかわらず</b>、給料(きゅうりょう)は同(おな)じです。
Pay is the same regardless of experience.
</pre>

<pre>
経験(けいけん)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、まず人柄(ひとがら)を見(み)たい。
Experience is a separate matter - first I want to see what they're like as a person.
</pre>

The first says experience will never affect the salary; the question is closed. The
second says experience will be looked at, later, and something else comes first.
Learners who translate both as "regardless of experience" lose the distinction that
matters most in a negotiation: whether the item is off the table or merely
postponed.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 田中(たなか)さん<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、全員(ぜんいん)来(き)ました。 → ✅ 田中(たなか)さん<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、全員(ぜんいん)来(き)ました。
  全員(ぜんいん) is a claim about a set, and Tanaka would have been in it. That is
  subtraction, not bracketing.

- ❌ できる<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、やってみます。 → ✅ できるかどうか<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、やってみます。
  Only a noun or a nominalised question can carry the `は`. A bare verb needs
  `かどうか`.

- ❌ 値段(ねだん)<b>が別(べつ)として</b> → ✅ 値段(ねだん)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>
  The particle is `は`, which is what performs the setting-aside. `が` would make
  the price the subject of 別(べつ)だ and the sentence would stop making sense.

- ❌ この案(あん)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、一番(いちばん)いいと思(おも)います。 → ✅ この案(あん)は、細(こま)かい点(てん)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、一番(いちばん)いいと思(おも)います。
  You cannot set aside the very thing you go on to evaluate. Park a *part* of it,
  then judge the whole.

## Practice

**1.** 安(やす)いかどうか____、この機能(きのう)は他社(たしゃ)の製品(せいひん)にはない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

安(やす)いかどうか<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、この機能(きのう)は他社(たしゃ)の製品(せいひん)にはない。

Price is isolated as its own axis so that the feature can be judged on its own
merits, with no verdict passed on the price either way. An い-adjective clause needs
`かどうか` before `は` can attach to it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 一部(いちぶ)の商品(しょうひん)（は別(べつ)として・を除(のぞ)いて）、すべて半額(はんがく)になります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

一部(いちぶ)の商品(しょうひん)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、すべて半額(はんがく)になります。
"Everything is half price except for certain items."

すべて makes this a claim about a set, and the excluded items are members of it - so
the operation is subtraction. <code>は別(べつ)として</code> would suggest those
items are a topic for a later conversation, which is not what a sale sign means.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Setting aside whether it's true, it's an interesting story."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

本当(ほんとう)かどうか<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、面白(おもしろ)い話(はなし)だ。

<code>本当(ほんとう)かどうか</code> is the standard nominalised question, and
<code>別(べつ)として</code> keeps the speaker from committing to the truth of it
either way. <code>はともかく</code> would also be idiomatic here, but it would hint
that the speaker doubts the story.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 好(す)き嫌(きら)いは別(べつ)として、野菜(やさい)が大(だい)好(す)きです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 好(す)き嫌(きら)いは別(べつ)として、野菜(やさい)は体(からだ)にいい。
or ✅ 野菜(やさい)が大(だい)好(す)きです。

The original brackets the question of personal taste and then answers it in the same
breath. Once preference has been set aside, the main clause has to be about
something else - here, nutrition - or the pattern has to go.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 予算(よさん)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、まず理想(りそう)の案(あん)を考(かんが)えよう。
(b) 予算(よさん)<b>にかかわらず</b>、理想(りそう)の案(あん)を考(かんが)えよう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) shelves the budget temporarily so that thinking is not cramped by it. Everyone
in the room understands that the numbers will come back, probably next week, and
that the ideal plan will then be trimmed.

(b) is a much bigger claim: the budget is not to influence the design at all,
whatever it turns out to be. Said by a manager it sounds like permission to spend;
said by anyone else it sounds naive. The difference between *postponed* and
*irrelevant* is the whole distance between these two sentences.

</details>

# ～を除いて

**Meaning** · except; with the exception of - everything in this set but that one

<code>除(のぞ)く</code> is "to remove, to take out", so
<code>を除(のぞ)いて</code> subtracts. It presupposes a general claim -
*everyone*, *every day*, *all of them* - and cuts one named member out of it. That
presupposition is what separates it from everything else in this group: nothing is
being postponed and nothing is being called irrelevant, it is simply the one case
where the rule does not hold.

## Formation

<code>除(のぞ)く</code> is a transitive う-verb (五段(ごだん) / Group 1), so the thing
removed takes `を` and the phrase conjugates normally.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + を除(のぞ)いて | 日曜日(にちようび) → 日曜日(にちようび)を除(のぞ)いて |
| Noun (written / formal) | Noun + を除(のぞ)き | 一部(いちぶ) → 一部(いちぶ)を除(のぞ)き |
| Noun (with topic は) | Noun + を除(のぞ)いては | 彼(かれ) → 彼(かれ)を除(のぞ)いては |
| Noun (conditional flavour) | Noun + を除(のぞ)けば | ラスト → ラストを除(のぞ)けば |
| Modifying a noun | Noun + を除(のぞ)く／除(のぞ)いた + Noun | 税(ぜい) → 税(ぜい)を除(のぞ)いた金額(きんがく) |
| Situation (nominalised) | clause + 場合(ばあい)・とき + を除(のぞ)いて | 緊急(きんきゅう)の場合(ばあい)を除(のぞ)いて |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the base is <code>除(のぞ)く</code>, so the furigana sits on the single
kanji with く outside. Do not confuse it with <code>覗(のぞ)く</code>, "to peek",
which is a different character with the same reading.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
月曜日(げつようび)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、毎日(まいにち)練習(れんしゅう)があります。
There's practice every day except Monday.
</pre>

<pre>
一人(ひとり)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、全員(ぜんいん)が賛成(さんせい)した。
Everyone but one person was in favour.
</pre>

<pre>
一部(いちぶ)の地域(ちいき)<b>を除(のぞ)き</b>、明日(あす)は晴(は)れるでしょう。
It should be fine tomorrow, apart from in some areas.
</pre>

<pre>
年末年始(ねんまつねんし)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、受付(うけつけ)は午前(ごぜん)９時(じ)から午後(ごご)５時(じ)までです。
Except over the New Year period, reception is open from nine to five.
</pre>

<pre>
緊急(きんきゅう)の場合(ばあい)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、この扉(とびら)は使用(しよう)できません。
This door is not to be used except in an emergency.
</pre>

<pre>
あの映画(えいが)は、ラストの十分(じゅっぷん)<b>を除(のぞ)けば</b>本当(ほんとう)によくできていたと思(おも)う。
That film was genuinely well made, apart from the last ten minutes.
</pre>

<pre>
彼(かれ)<b>を除(のぞ)いては</b>、この仕事(しごと)を任(まか)せられる人(ひと)がいない。
There's nobody I could hand this job to apart from him.
</pre>

<pre>
税金(ぜいきん)<b>を除(のぞ)いた</b>金額(きんがく)が表示(ひょうじ)されていますので、ご注意(ちゅうい)ください。
Please note that the prices shown do not include tax.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>を除(のぞ)いて</code> is a shade formal. In everyday speech Japanese reaches
for <code>以外(いがい)</code> far more often -
<code>日曜(にちよう)以外(いがい)は毎日(まいにち)やってる</code> - and keeps
<code>除(のぞ)く</code> for writing, announcements, contracts and anywhere a
precise carve-out is being stated. It is not stiff enough to sound wrong in
conversation, but it is the register of the timetable rather than the chat about
the timetable.

The four endings shade differently. <code>を除(のぞ)き</code> is the written
listing form and appears in notices. <code>を除(のぞ)いては</code> puts a topic
marker on the exception, which foregrounds it, and it pairs naturally with a
negative main clause: <code>彼(かれ)を除(のぞ)いては、だれも来(こ)なかった</code>.
<code>を除(のぞ)けば</code> adds a hypothetical tint - "if you leave that out" -
and is the natural form for a review or an assessment, because it invites the
listener to imagine the thing without its flaw. Plain
<code>を除(のぞ)いて</code> is the neutral default.

<code>除(のぞ)く</code> is also a live verb outside this pattern -
<code>不安(ふあん)を取(と)り除(のぞ)く</code>, "to remove an anxiety" - and it
survives in one piece of signage worth recognising on sight: the parenthesis
<code>（土日(どにち)を除(のぞ)く）</code> printed under a set of opening hours,
using the bare dictionary form rather than the て-form.

## Contrast

### ～を除(のぞ)いて vs ～以外(いがい)（は）

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same job, different part of speech.
<code>以外(いがい)</code> is a **noun** and takes particles;
<code>を除(のぞ)いて</code> is a **verb phrase** and modifies the clause.

</div>

<pre>
私(わたし)<b>以外(いがい)</b>はみんな知(し)っていた。
Everyone except me knew.
</pre>

<pre>
私(わたし)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、みんな知(し)っていた。
Everyone knew, apart from me.
</pre>

The meanings match. What differs is flexibility and register.
<code>以外(いがい)</code> attaches straight to the noun with no particle in
between and then takes whatever particle the sentence needs -
<code>以外(いがい)は</code>, <code>以外(いがい)に</code>,
<code>以外(いがい)の人(ひと)</code> - which is why it dominates in speech.
<code>を除(のぞ)いて</code> cannot become a noun modifier without changing form to
<code>を除(のぞ)く</code> or <code>を除(のぞ)いた</code>. When in doubt in
conversation, use <code>以外(いがい)</code>; in a notice, use
<code>除(のぞ)く</code>.

### ～を除(のぞ)いて vs ～だけ・～しか～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>除(のぞ)く</code> describes the **majority** and names
the gap. `だけ` and `しか～ない` describe the **remainder**.

</div>

<pre>
彼(かれ)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、みんな反対(はんたい)だった。
Apart from him, everyone was against it.
</pre>

<pre>
賛成(さんせい)したのは彼(かれ)<b>だけ</b>だった。
He was the only one in favour.
</pre>

Identical facts, opposite camera angles. The first keeps the group in frame and
mentions the hole in it; the second points at the one person left. Choose by asking
which side of the line your sentence is about. A related consequence: because
<code>除(のぞ)く</code> needs the general claim to exist, it is odd in a sentence
where there is no group -
❌<code>彼(かれ)を除(のぞ)いて二人(ふたり)来(き)た</code> gives the listener
nothing to subtract from.

### ～を除(のぞ)いて vs ～は別(べつ)として

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>除(のぞ)く</code> takes a member **out of a set**;
<code>別(べつ)として</code> takes a topic **off the agenda**.

</div>

<pre>
新入生(しんにゅうせい)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、全員(ぜんいん)が試験(しけん)を受(う)けます。
All the students sit the exam except the first-years.
</pre>

<pre>
新入生(しんにゅうせい)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、試験(しけん)の日程(にってい)を決(き)めましょう。
Leaving the first-years aside, let's fix the exam dates.
</pre>

In the first, the first-years are definitively not sitting the exam - a fact about
the world. In the second nothing has been decided about them at all; they are merely
not what this meeting is handling. Learners who reach for
<code>を除(のぞ)いて</code> when they mean "let's not discuss that now" end up
announcing a decision they never made, which in a work context is an expensive
mistake.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)る<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b> → ✅ 雨(あめ)の日(ひ)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>（or 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)る場合(ばあい)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>）
  <code>除(のぞ)く</code> is transitive and needs a noun as its object. To exclude
  a situation, package it as <code>場合(ばあい)</code>,
  <code>とき</code> or <code>日(ひ)</code> first.

- ❌ 私(わたし)<b>は除(のぞ)いて</b>、みんな行(い)った。 → ✅ 私(わたし)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、みんな行(い)った。
  The particle on the excluded item is `を`. `は` only appears *after* the verb, in
  <code>を除(のぞ)いては</code>.

- ❌ 税(ぜい)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>金額(きんがく)をご確認(かくにん)ください。 → ✅ 税(ぜい)<b>を除(のぞ)いた</b>金額(きんがく)をご確認(かくにん)ください。
  To modify a noun you need the plain form
  <code>除(のぞ)く</code> or the past
  <code>除(のぞ)いた</code>. The て-form modifies the verb, not the noun.

- ❌ 経験(けいけん)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>採用(さいよう)します。 → ✅ 経験(けいけん)<b>を問(と)わず</b>採用(さいよう)します。
  Experience is a criterion, not a member of the group being hired. Criteria are
  waived with <code>問(と)う</code>; people and days are removed with
  <code>除(のぞ)く</code>.

## Practice

**1.** 一部(いちぶ)の商品(しょうひん)____、店内(てんない)すべて２割引(わりびき)です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

一部(いちぶ)の商品(しょうひん)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、店内(てんない)すべて２割引(わりびき)です。

すべて sets up a claim about the whole shop, and a named group of items is being cut
out of it - textbook subtraction. <code>を除(のぞ)き</code> would be equally
correct and slightly more formal, which suits a printed sign.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 会議(かいぎ)には部長(ぶちょう)（を除(のぞ)いて・は別(べつ)として）全員(ぜんいん)出席(しゅっせき)しました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

部長(ぶちょう)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>全員(ぜんいん)出席(しゅっせき)しました。

全員(ぜんいん) makes this a statement about a set, and the department head is a
member who was not there. <code>は別(べつ)として</code> would be about what the
speaker is choosing to discuss, which does not fit a factual report of who attended.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Apart from the price, there was nothing I disliked about this flat."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

値段(ねだん)<b>を除(のぞ)けば</b>、この部屋(へや)に気(き)に入(い)らないところはなかった。

<code>を除(のぞ)けば</code> is the reviewer's form: it invites the listener to
imagine the flat without that one flaw. <code>値段(ねだん)以外(いがい)に</code>
would be the plainer spoken equivalent.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 一部(いちぶ)の地域(ちいき)以外(いがい)を除(のぞ)いて、全国(ぜんこく)どこへでも配達(はいたつ)できます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 一部(いちぶ)の地域(ちいき)を除(のぞ)いて、全国(ぜんこく)どこへでも配達(はいたつ)できます。
or ✅ 一部(いちぶ)の地域(ちいき)以外(いがい)は、全国(ぜんこく)どこへでも配達(はいたつ)できます。

<code>以外(いがい)</code> and <code>を除(のぞ)いて</code> do the same job, so
stacking them subtracts twice - taken literally, the original says delivery is
available only to the areas that were meant to be excluded. Use the noun
<code>以外(いがい)</code> with `は`, or the verb phrase on its own, but not both.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 彼(かれ)<b>を除(のぞ)いて</b>、みんな知(し)っていた。
(b) 知(し)らなかったのは彼(かれ)<b>だけ</b>だった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both report the same state of affairs, and either could translate the other. The
difference is where the listener's attention lands.

(a) is a statement about the group: everybody was in the know, with one hole in the
picture. It is the neutral way to report the situation, and it does not by itself
comment on him.

(b) puts the spotlight on him and, because `だけ` isolates, it invites a judgement -
he was the odd one out, and depending on tone that is either pitying or pointed. If
you are telling someone gently that they were left out, (a) is the kinder sentence.

</details>

# ～ならまだしも・～はまだしも

**Meaning** · if it were only A, that would be bearable - but it isn't, it's B

<code>まだしも</code> is <code>まだ</code> ("still, even so") stiffened with
<code>しも</code>, and it means *relatively acceptable*. The pattern sets up a
tolerable case, grants it, and then produces the real one, which is over the line.
Every sentence built on it is a complaint. The half after `まだしも` carries the
grievance, and the half before it exists only to measure how far the grievance goes.

## Formation

`なら` builds a **hypothetical** case that did not happen; `は` takes a **real**
element of the situation and ranks it as the lesser problem.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (hypothetical) | Noun + ならまだしも | 子(こ)ども → 子(こ)どもならまだしも |
| Noun (actual, graded) | Noun + はまだしも | 値段(ねだん) → 値段(ねだん)はまだしも |
| Verb | plain form + （の）ならまだしも | 遅(おく)れる → 遅(おく)れるのならまだしも |
| Verb (past) | ～たのならまだしも | 知(し)らなかった → 知(し)らなかったのならまだしも |
| い-adjective | ～い + （の）ならまだしも | 近(ちか)い → 近(ちか)いならまだしも |
| な-adjective | ～なら / ～であるならまだしも | 下手(へた) → 下手(へた)ならまだしも |
| Limiting shape | ～だけならまだしも | 遅(おく)れるだけならまだしも |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the second clause is almost never neutral. It ends in
<code>困(こま)る</code>, <code>ひどい</code>, <code>許(ゆる)せない</code>,
<code>どうかと思(おも)う</code>, or trails off into <code>なんて</code>. If your
second half is good news, this is the wrong pattern.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
一度(いちど)<b>ならまだしも</b>、三度(さんど)目(め)はさすがに困(こま)る。
Once I could live with, but a third time is really too much.
</pre>

<pre>
近(ちか)く<b>ならまだしも</b>、そんなに遠(とお)い店(みせ)には行(い)けない。
If it were nearby, fine - but I can't go all the way to a shop that far off.
</pre>

<pre>
値段(ねだん)<b>はまだしも</b>、接客(せっきゃく)のひどさはどうにかしてほしい。
The price I can put up with; it's the appalling service someone needs to fix.
</pre>

<pre>
雨(あめ)<b>はまだしも</b>、風(かぜ)がこれだけ強(つよ)いと傘(かさ)は役(やく)に立(た)たない。
The rain I could handle, but with wind like this an umbrella is useless.
</pre>

<pre>
荷物(にもつ)が遅(おく)れるだけ<b>ならまだしも</b>、中身(なかみ)が壊(こわ)れていたのだから黙(だま)っていられない。
A late delivery would be one thing, but the contents were broken - I'm not letting that go.
</pre>

<pre>
自分(じぶん)のミス<b>ならまだしも</b>、人(ひと)のミスで謝(あやま)らされるのは納得(なっとく)がいかない。
If it were my own mistake, fine - being made to apologise for someone else's isn't right.
</pre>

<pre>
忙(いそが)しくて連絡(れんらく)できなかったの<b>ならまだしも</b>、ずっと家(いえ)にいたと聞(き)いて、さすがに腹(はら)が立(た)った。
Had he been too busy to call, all right - but hearing he'd been home the whole time, I lost my temper.
</pre>

<pre>
新入社員(しんにゅうしゃいん)<b>ならまだしも</b>、十年(じゅうねん)働(はたら)いている人(ひと)がこの書類(しょるい)の書(か)き方(かた)を知(し)らないのは問題(もんだい)だ。
In a new recruit it would be understandable, but someone with ten years in the job not knowing how to fill this in is a problem.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is grumbling, and it is adult grumbling. The form is a shade bookish -
`しも` is an old emphatic particle that survives only in fossils like this one - so
it lands as considered rather than hot-tempered. A person using
<code>ならまだしも</code> sounds like they have thought about where the line is and
concluded that you crossed it. That is often more cutting than shouting.

It works in both plain and polite endings, and `ます`/`です` do not soften the
complaint much: <code>一度(いちど)ならまだしも、三度(さんど)目(め)は困(こま)ります</code>
is polite in form and firm in effect. Speakers of any age and gender use it; if
anything it is commoner from people with some authority in the situation, because
its logic is the logic of setting standards.

What you cannot do with it is be nice. The pattern presupposes that the second case
falls short, so it has no positive use at all. It also cannot be aimed at yourself
as an excuse - <code>私(わたし)が新人(しんじん)ならまだしも</code> would be
building a case against yourself, which is occasionally the point in an apology but
never neutral.

In speech it very often trails off rather than finishing:
<code>子(こ)どもならまだしも…</code>, said with a sigh, is a complete utterance,
and the listener supplies the rest. That unfinished form is one of the most natural
ways the pattern appears, and worth being able to hear.

## Contrast

### ～ならまだしも vs ～ならともかく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ならともかく` says A would be **a different matter**;
`ならまだしも` says A would be **more tolerable than what actually happened**.

</div>

The two are close, and in many sentences either passes. The difference is that
`まだしも` explicitly grades: it puts A and B on one scale and marks the line
between them.

<pre>
学生(がくせい)<b>ならともかく</b>、社会人(しゃかいじん)がそれでは困(こま)る。
For a student that would be one thing, but from a working adult it won't do.
</pre>

<pre>
学生(がくせい)<b>ならまだしも</b>、社会人(しゃかいじん)がそれでは困(こま)る。
It would just about pass in a student - from a working adult it won't do.
</pre>

`ともかく` says the two cases are governed by different rules, and it can be
delivered fairly coolly. `まだしも` says they are governed by the same rule and one
of them is nearer the edge of it, which is why it carries more audible exasperation.
Where `ならともかく` can occasionally head a neutral sentence, `ならまだしも`
essentially cannot.

### ～ならまだしも vs ～はまだしも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なら` proposes a case that **did not happen**; `は` grades
something that **did**.

</div>

<pre>
雨(あめ)<b>ならまだしも</b>、雪(ゆき)では車(くるま)を出(だ)せない。
Rain would be all right, but I can't take the car out in snow.
</pre>

<pre>
雨(あめ)<b>はまだしも</b>、風(かぜ)まで出(で)てきては中止(ちゅうし)するしかない。
The rain we could cope with, but now the wind has got up we'll have to call it off.
</pre>

In the first it is snowing; there is no rain anywhere in the scene, and rain is
invented as the case that would have been manageable. In the second it really is
raining, and the rain is being ranked as the smaller of two present problems. Choose
by asking whether the item in front of the pattern is actually in the room.

Because `は` grades real things, it tends to appear where two properties of a single
object are being compared - price and service, size and weight, delay and damage.
`なら` typically swaps one whole scenario for another.

### ～ならまだしも vs ～方(ほう)がまだましだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ならまだしも` **concedes** A inside a complaint about B.
<code>方(ほう)がまだましだ</code> **chooses** A over B.

</div>

<code>まだしも</code> and <code>まだまし</code> share a root and a feeling - both mean
"the less bad one" - but they are used at different moments.

<pre>
待(ま)たされるの<b>ならまだしも</b>、予約(よやく)そのものが消(き)えていた。
Being kept waiting would be one thing - the booking itself had vanished.
</pre>

<pre>
これだけ待(ま)たされるなら、立(た)ち食(ぐ)いの店(みせ)の方(ほう)がまだましだ。
If I'm going to be kept waiting this long, I'd honestly rather go somewhere I can eat standing up.
</pre>

The first reports what went wrong. The second makes a choice between two options,
neither of them good. If you can insert "I'd rather" into the English,
<code>方(ほう)がまだましだ</code> is your pattern; if the English is "would have
been one thing, but", it is `まだしも`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 大人(おとな)<b>ならまだしも</b>、子(こ)どもがそんなことを言(い)うのはひどい。 → ✅ 子(こ)ども<b>ならまだしも</b>、大人(おとな)がそんなことを言(い)うのはひどい。
  The forgivable case goes **first**. Putting the worse case in front reverses the
  argument and leaves the listener confused about who you are annoyed with.

- ❌ 遅(おく)れるの<b>ならまだしも</b>、来(こ)なかったのはよかった。 → ✅ 遅(おく)れるの<b>ならまだしも</b>、来(こ)なかったのは困(こま)る。
  The second clause must be the complaint. `まだしも` cannot introduce good news;
  the whole structure exists to say that a line was crossed.

- ❌ 学生(がくせい)<b>だならまだしも</b> → ✅ 学生(がくせい)<b>ならまだしも</b>
  `なら` follows a bare noun with no `だ`. This is the same rule as for ordinary
  conditional `なら` and it is easy to forget under the weight of the longer pattern.

- ❌ この店(みせ)は値段(ねだん)<b>ならまだしも</b>、味(あじ)がひどい。 → ✅ この店(みせ)は値段(ねだん)<b>はまだしも</b>、味(あじ)がひどい。
  The price of this restaurant is a real fact about it, not a hypothetical, so it
  takes `は`. `なら` would mean "if it were a matter of price", which is not what you
  are saying.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both blame the department head, and in most conversations they are interchangeable.

the point where ignorance can be excused. The speaker sounds exasperated, and the
implication is that even a new recruit is only *just* forgivable.

(b) says the two cases are simply governed by different expectations - a newcomer is
not judged by this standard at all. It is the cooler sentence and could be said in a
meeting without much heat.

If you are building an argument, (b). If you are complaining, (a).

</details>

# ～はさておき

**Meaning** · setting that aside; never mind that for now - here is what we should be talking about

<code>さておく</code> is <code>さて</code> plus <code>置(お)く</code>: *to put it
over there*. `はさておき` is therefore less a statement about the world than a move
in a conversation. The speaker picks up a topic, sets it down out of the way, and
takes the floor for the thing they think should come next. Where `ともかく` weighs
and <code>別(べつ)として</code> brackets, `さておき` **steers**.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + はさておき | 冗談(じょうだん) → 冗談(じょうだん)はさておき |
| Noun (て-form variant) | Noun + はさておいて | 条件(じょうけん) → 条件(じょうけん)はさておいて |
| Yes/no question | plain form + かどうか + はさておき | できる → できるかどうかはさておき |
| Embedded question | question word … か + はさておき | どちらが正(ただ)しいか → どちらが正(ただ)しいかはさておき |
| Fixed opener | それはさておき | - |
| Casual equivalent | ～は置(お)いといて | その話(はなし)は置(お)いといて |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>何(なに)はさておき</code> is **not** this pattern with
何(なに) in the slot. It is a fixed adverb meaning "before anything else":
<code>何(なに)はさておき、まず手(て)を洗(あら)おう</code>. It promotes rather
than postpones, and mixing the two up reverses your sentence.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
冗談(じょうだん)<b>はさておき</b>、そろそろ本題(ほんだい)に入(はい)りましょう。
Joking aside, let's get to the main business.
</pre>

<pre>
その話(はなし)<b>はさておき</b>、まず予算(よさん)を決(き)めませんか。
Let's park that one - shall we settle the budget first?
</pre>

<pre>
結果(けっか)<b>はさておき</b>、よく最後(さいご)まで走(はし)りきったと思(おも)う。
Never mind the result - I think it's impressive she ran the whole way.
</pre>

<pre>
実現(じつげん)できるかどうか<b>はさておき</b>、まずアイデアを出(だ)してみましょう。
Leave the question of feasibility for now; let's get some ideas on the table.
</pre>

<pre>
私(わたし)の意見(いけん)<b>はさておき</b>、皆(みな)さんはどう思(おも)いますか。
Putting my own view to one side, what does everyone else think?
</pre>

<pre>
細(こま)かい条件(じょうけん)<b>はさておいて</b>、この方向(ほうこう)で進(すす)めていいかどうかだけ確認(かくにん)させてください。
Setting the detailed terms aside, let me just confirm whether we can proceed in this direction.
</pre>

<pre>
どちらが正(ただ)しいか<b>はさておき</b>、まずお互(たが)いの言(い)い分(ぶん)を最後(さいご)まで聞(き)きましょう。
Who is right can wait - let's first hear each side out in full.
</pre>

<pre>
費用(ひよう)のこと<b>はさておき</b>、この計画(けいかく)が町(まち)にとって必要(ひつよう)かどうかを先(さき)に議論(ぎろん)すべきだ。
Cost aside, we ought first to debate whether the town actually needs this scheme.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`はさておき` is a chairing move. Saying it claims, briefly, the right to decide what
the group talks about next, and that is why it turns up so often from the person
running the meeting, the writer organising an essay, or the speaker managing a
presentation. It is polite in form - nobody will take offence at the words - but it
is not modest, and a junior person using it to shelve a senior colleague's point can
land more bluntly than intended. Softer alternatives exist for that case:
<code>後(あと)ほど伺(うかが)うことにして</code>, or simply asking.

Between friends the same move is made with
<code>それは置(お)いといて</code> - literally the same verb, contracted and
relaxed. <code>まあそれはいいとして</code> does the job too. `さておき` in a casual
chat sounds like you are hosting a radio programme, which is occasionally funny and
usually a shade formal.

Two set uses are worth learning whole.
<code>冗談(じょうだん)はさておき</code> is the standard hinge after a light opening
remark, and Japanese speeches use it constantly to pivot from warm-up to substance. <code>それはさておき</code> stands alone as a paragraph opener in writing
and does the work of English "anyway".

Unlike `ともかく`, `さておき` passes no judgement on what it parks. You can set aside
something excellent - <code>結果(けっか)はさておき</code> above is about a runner
who did well - because the sentence is about the agenda, not about quality.

## Contrast

### ～はさておき vs ～はともかく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `さておき` moves the **conversation** on; `ともかく` ranks the
**content** and quietly marks the parked item as the weaker one.

</div>

<pre>
順位(じゅんい)<b>はさておき</b>、まず完走(かんそう)できたことを喜(よろこ)ぼう。
Never mind the placing - let's be glad she finished at all.
</pre>

<pre>
順位(じゅんい)<b>はともかく</b>、走(はし)り方(かた)はきれいだった。
The placing was what it was, but her running form was lovely.
</pre>

The first is about what to discuss now, and it implies nothing about the result. The
second is a verdict: the placing was disappointing. If you are complimenting
somebody and do not want a backhanded reading, `さておき` is the safer of the two.

There is also a difference in what follows. `さておき` is nearly always followed by a
proposal - <code>ましょう</code>, <code>ませんか</code>, an imperative, a question.
`ともかく` is followed by an assessment.

### ～はさておき vs ～は別(べつ)として

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>別(べつ)として</code> is **analytical**: it isolates a
variable. `さておき` is **conversational**: it manages the floor.

</div>

<pre>
時間(じかん)の問題(もんだい)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、この方法(ほうほう)が一番(いちばん)確実(かくじつ)だ。
Leaving the question of time aside, this method is the most reliable.
</pre>

<pre>
時間(じかん)の問題(もんだい)<b>はさておき</b>、まずどの方法(ほうほう)にするか決(き)めましょう。
Never mind the timing for now - let's decide on a method first.
</pre>

Both are neutral about the parked item, and in many sentences either would do. The
first is a piece of reasoning and could sit in a written report; the second is
addressed to people in a room and expects them to do something. A reliable tell: if
the sentence continues with <code>ましょう</code> or a question, you are steering,
and `さておき` fits best.

### ～はさておき vs ところで・それはそうと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `さておき` **drops a named topic and returns to the agenda**.
`ところで` **abandons the agenda for something new**.

</div>

<pre>
冗談(じょうだん)<b>はさておき</b>、来週(らいしゅう)の予定(よてい)を決(き)めましょう。
Joking aside, let's fix next week's schedule.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>ところで</b>、来週(らいしゅう)の予定(よてい)はもう決(き)まりましたか。
By the way, has next week's schedule been settled?
</pre>

Grammatically the difference is that `さておき` needs something in front of it to
name what is being put down, while `ところで` and
<code>それはそうと</code> stand alone. Socially the difference is direction of
travel. `さておき` signals a return to what we were supposed to be doing, so it reads
as responsible. `ところで` signals a swerve to a new subject, so it reads as a change
of mood - and in a formal meeting it can sound like you have stopped listening.
<code>それはそうと</code> is the warmest of the three and belongs in conversation
rather than on a page.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 結果(けっか)<b>をさておき</b> → ✅ 結果(けっか)<b>はさておき</b>
  The particle is `は`. It is the topic marker doing the setting-aside, and `を`
  makes the phrase ungrammatical rather than merely odd.

- ❌ 勝(か)てる<b>はさておき</b>、練習(れんしゅう)しよう。 → ✅ 勝(か)てるかどうか<b>はさておき</b>、練習(れんしゅう)しよう。
  A verb clause has to be nominalised with `かどうか` (or a question word plus `か`)
  before `は` can attach.

- ❌ 何(なに)<b>はさておき</b>、この問題(もんだい)は後(あと)で考(かんが)えよう。 → ✅ 何(なに)<b>はさておき</b>、まずこの問題(もんだい)から片(かた)づけよう。
  <code>何(なに)はさておき</code> means "before anything else". Using it to
  postpone something says the exact opposite of what you intend.

- ❌ 課長(かちょう)のお話(はなし)<b>はさておき</b>、次(つぎ)に進(すす)みます。 → ✅ 課長(かちょう)のお話(はなし)については後(あと)ほど伺(うかが)うことにして、次(つぎ)に進(すす)みます。
  Grammatical, but it shelves a superior's contribution with a wave of the hand.
  The pattern is a floor-taking move, so mind who you use it on.

## Practice

**1.** その件(けん)____、今日(きょう)は来年(らいねん)の計画(けいかく)について話(はな)しましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

その件(けん)<b>はさておき</b>、今日(きょう)は来年(らいねん)の計画(けいかく)について話(はな)しましょう。

A named topic is put down and the meeting is steered back to its agenda, and the
sentence ends in a proposal - the clearest sign that the speaker is steering rather
than assessing. A bare noun takes the pattern directly.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 味(あじ)（はさておき・はともかく）、この店(みせ)の雰囲気(ふんいき)は最高(さいこう)だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are grammatical, and they say different things.

<b>はともかく</b> is what a speaker would normally choose here, and it tells the
listener the food is not much good. <b>はさておき</b> leaves the food entirely
unjudged and simply announces that atmosphere is the topic - but because the
sentence goes on to an assessment rather than a proposal, it reads a little oddly,
as if the speaker were chairing a meeting about a restaurant.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Whose fault it was can wait - let's fix the problem first."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

だれの責任(せきにん)か<b>はさておき</b>、まず問題(もんだい)を解決(かいけつ)しましょう。

An embedded question with a question word takes plain `か`, not `かどうか`. The
`ましょう` ending is what makes `さておき` the natural choice over
<code>は別(べつ)として</code> here.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 何(なに)はさておき、その件(けん)は来週(らいしゅう)話(はな)し合(あ)いましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ その件(けん)<b>はさておき</b>、来週(らいしゅう)までに決(き)めるべきことを先(さき)に話(はな)し合(あ)いましょう。
or ✅ 何(なに)はさておき、その件(けん)から話(はな)し合(あ)いましょう。

<code>何(なに)はさておき</code> means "before anything else", so the original
sentence promises to deal with the matter immediately and then postpones it by a
week. Either park the specific topic, or keep 何(なに)はさておき and put the topic
first.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 細(こま)かい話(はなし)<b>はさておき</b>、全体(ぜんたい)の方針(ほうしん)を決(き)めましょう。
(b) 細(こま)かい話(はなし)<b>は別(べつ)として</b>、全体(ぜんたい)の方針(ほうしん)を決(き)めましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

They are close, and no listener would object to either. (a) is the more physical
gesture: the speaker is pushing the details off the table so the group can get on.
It carries a faint sense of impatience with the detail, and it is the sentence a
chair says when the meeting is running long.

(b) is cooler. It treats the details as a genuinely separate item that will be
handled in its own right, and it promises nothing about when. In a written agenda
(b) reads better; spoken in a room, (a) is what moves people along.

</details>

# ～ならともかく

**Meaning** · if it were A, that would be another matter - but it isn't

`ならともかく` invents a case in which the speaker would have had no objection, grants
it freely, and then points at the case that actually obtains. The generosity is
tactical: by showing exactly where the line would have been, the speaker makes it
unmistakable that the real situation falls on the wrong side of it. Nothing before
`ともかく` is true of the world - it is a foil.

## Formation

The `なら` is the ordinary conditional `なら`, so it attaches the way `なら` always
does.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + ならともかく | 子(こ)ども → 子(こ)どもならともかく |
| Noun (spoken) | Noun + だったらともかく | 学生(がくせい) → 学生(がくせい)だったらともかく |
| Verb | plain form + （の）ならともかく | 知(し)らない → 知(し)らないのならともかく |
| Verb (past) | ～たのならともかく | 頼(たの)まれた → 頼(たの)まれたのならともかく |
| い-adjective | ～い + ならともかく | 安(やす)い → 安(やす)いならともかく |
| な-adjective | ～なら / ～であるならともかく | 必要(ひつよう) → 必要(ひつよう)ならともかく |
| Fuller form | ～ならともかくとして | 急(きゅう)な用事(ようじ)ならともかくとして |

<div class="note">

**Note** - what follows is usually a complaint, a refusal or a piece of blunt
advice. `ともかく` is a little more tolerant than `まだしも` of a merely factual
second clause, but a cheerful one still sounds wrong: the pattern exists to mark a
boundary, and boundaries are drawn against something.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
安(やす)い<b>ならともかく</b>、この値段(ねだん)では買(か)えない。
If it were cheap that would be different, but not at this price.
</pre>

<pre>
子(こ)ども<b>ならともかく</b>、大人(おとな)がそれでは恥(は)ずかしい。
From a child it would be one thing; from an adult it's embarrassing.
</pre>

<pre>
一人(ひとり)<b>ならともかく</b>、家族(かぞく)四人(よにん)で泊(と)まるとなると予算(よさん)が足(た)りない。
On my own it would be fine, but for a family of four the budget won't stretch.
</pre>

<pre>
知(し)らなかったの<b>ならともかく</b>、知(し)っていて黙(だま)っていたのなら話(はなし)は別(べつ)だ。
Not knowing would be one thing - knowing and saying nothing is another matter entirely.
</pre>

<pre>
近所(きんじょ)<b>ならともかく</b>、電車(でんしゃ)を二回(にかい)乗(の)り換(か)えてまで通(かよ)う気(き)にはなれない。
If it were local, fine - but I can't face commuting there with two changes.
</pre>

<pre>
急(きゅう)な用事(ようじ)<b>ならともかく</b>、ただ寝坊(ねぼう)しただけで会議(かいぎ)を欠席(けっせき)するのはどうかと思(おも)う。
An emergency would be understandable; missing a meeting because you overslept is not.
</pre>

<pre>
本人(ほんにん)が希望(きぼう)しているの<b>ならともかく</b>、周(まわ)りが勝手(かって)に決(き)めてしまうのはよくない。
If he wanted it himself that would be different, but the people round him deciding for him isn't right.
</pre>

<pre>
一度(いちど)使(つか)うだけ<b>ならともかく</b>、毎日(まいにち)持(も)ち歩(ある)くつもりなら、もう少(すこ)し丈夫(じょうぶ)なものを選(えら)んだほうがいい。
For a single use it would be fine, but if you mean to carry it every day you should pick something sturdier.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The tone is reasonable-sounding disagreement. Because the speaker begins by
conceding, `ならともかく` reads as fair-minded even when the verdict is harsh, and
that makes it a useful move in an argument you intend to win. It is at home in
speech and in opinion writing, works with plain or polite endings, and is not marked
for age or gender.

It is a shade softer than `ならまだしも`. `まだしも` grades - it says the real case is
*worse* on the same scale - while `ともかく` says the two cases are simply not
governed by the same expectation. That extra step of judgement is what makes
`まだしも` sound more exasperated. Where you want to disagree without sounding
annoyed, `ともかく` is the better instrument.

Two things it cannot do. It cannot change the subject: it looks like a
setting-aside pattern but the `なら` binds it to a conditional, so it will not stand
in for `はさておき` at the top of a paragraph. And it cannot be used about a case
that is actually true - <code>学生(がくせい)ならともかく</code> said to a student
is incoherent, because the pattern's whole force comes from the case being
hypothetical.

The fuller <code>ならともかくとして</code> is available and slightly more measured,
though most speakers use the short form. <code>だったらともかく</code> is the casual
variant and is common in speech.

## Contrast

### ～ならともかく vs ～はともかく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `は` sets aside something **that is really there**. `なら`
invents a case that **is not**.

</div>

This is the pair the exam tests, and the pair that changes your meaning in real
conversation.

<pre>
値段(ねだん)<b>はともかく</b>、デザインは気(き)に入(い)っている。
Price aside, I do like the design.
</pre>

<pre>
値段(ねだん)が半分(はんぶん)<b>ならともかく</b>、この金額(きんがく)では手(て)が出(で)ない。
At half the price it would be different, but at this figure I can't touch it.
</pre>

In the first, the price is a real feature of a real object and is being parked. In
the second, no such price exists - "half price" is a fiction the speaker builds in
order to reject the price that does exist. A quick test: if you could point at the
thing you mentioned, use `は`; if you had to imagine it, use `なら`.

### ～ならともかく vs ～ならまだしも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ならともかく` says the case would have been **governed by a
different rule**. `ならまだしも` says it would have been **less bad on the same
scale**.

</div>

<pre>
初日(しょにち)<b>ならともかく</b>、三日(みっか)目(め)でこの状態(じょうたい)は困(こま)る。
On day one it would be another matter; on day three this state of affairs won't do.
</pre>

<pre>
初日(しょにち)<b>ならまだしも</b>、三日(みっか)目(め)でこの状態(じょうたい)は困(こま)る。
On day one it would just about pass; on day three this won't do.
</pre>

Both are natural and most listeners would not stop to distinguish them. The
difference is audible in the second version: `まだしも` implies that even day one was
borderline, and the speaker sounds closer to losing patience. `ともかく` grants day
one wholeheartedly and is the cooler sentence. Note the reading
三日(みっか), a native numeral - さんにち is not how the third day is counted.

### ～ならともかく vs ～にしても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `にしても` **grants the excuse and rejects it anyway**.
`ならともかく` grants that the excuse **would have worked, and denies it applies**.

</div>

<pre>
急(いそ)いでいた<b>にしても</b>、一言(ひとこと)連絡(れんらく)くらいはできたはずだ。
Even if you were in a hurry, you could at least have sent a word.
</pre>

<pre>
急(いそ)いでいたの<b>ならともかく</b>、ただ忘(わす)れていただけなら許(ゆる)せない。
Being in a hurry would be one thing; simply forgetting is not forgivable.
</pre>

The first accepts that you were rushing and holds you responsible regardless. The
second says rushing would have been an acceptable reason - and then takes it away,
because that is not what happened. The practical consequence: with `にしても` the
excuse is true, with `ならともかく` it is false. Learners who swap them accidentally
concede a fact they meant to dispute.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 子(こ)ども<b>はともかく</b>、大人(おとな)がそんなことをするなんて。 → ✅ 子(こ)ども<b>ならともかく</b>、大人(おとな)がそんなことをするなんて。
  There is no child in the situation; the child is imagined as the case that would
  have been excusable. A hypothetical needs `なら`.

- ❌ 学生(がくせい)<b>だならともかく</b> → ✅ 学生(がくせい)<b>ならともかく</b>
  `なら` follows a bare noun. The `だ` that feels natural in
  <code>学生(がくせい)だ</code> has no place before a conditional.

- ❌ 近(ちか)い<b>ならともかく</b>、近(ちか)いから歩(ある)いて行(い)こう。 → ✅ 遠(とお)い<b>ならともかく</b>、近(ちか)いのだから歩(ある)いて行(い)こう。
  The slot in front of `ならともかく` holds the case that is **not** true. Putting the
  actual situation there makes the sentence argue against itself.

- ❌ その話(はなし)<b>ならともかく</b>、次(つぎ)の議題(ぎだい)に移(うつ)りましょう。 → ✅ その話(はなし)<b>はさておき</b>、次(つぎ)の議題(ぎだい)に移(うつ)りましょう。
  `ならともかく` looks like a setting-aside phrase but is a conditional. To move a
  meeting on you need `はさておき` or <code>は別(べつ)として</code>.

## Practice

**1.** 新人(しんじん)____、５年(ねん)もやっている人(ひと)がこのミスをするのは信(しん)じられない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

新人(しんじん)<b>ならともかく</b>、５年(ねん)もやっている人(ひと)がこのミスをするのは信(しん)じられない。

The newcomer is imagined, not present - the person who made the mistake has five
years' experience. That hypothetical foil is exactly what `なら` marks, and the
complaint that follows is the pattern's usual second half.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 味(あじ)（ならともかく・はともかく）、この店(みせ)は雰囲気(ふんいき)がいい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

味(あじ)<b>はともかく</b>、この店(みせ)は雰囲気(ふんいき)がいい。

The food is a real property of a real restaurant, so it is parked with `は`.
`ならともかく` would require the taste to be hypothetical - "if it were a matter of
taste" - and would then demand a complaint rather than a compliment after it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If you'd asked me in advance it would be one thing, but I can't do it now."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

前(まえ)もって頼(たの)まれたの<b>ならともかく</b>、今(いま)からでは無理(むり)だ。

A past verb clause takes `の` before `なら`, which packages the whole clause as the
imagined condition. Note that the request was *not* made in advance - that is the
entire point of the sentence.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 雨(あめ)が降(ふ)っているならともかく、今日(きょう)は雨(あめ)だから中止(ちゅうし)にしよう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 晴(は)れている<b>ならともかく</b>、今日(きょう)は雨(あめ)だから中止(ちゅうし)にしよう。
"If it were fine that would be different, but it's raining today, so let's cancel."

The clause in front of `ならともかく` must be the case that does not hold. The
original puts the actual weather in the hypothetical slot and then repeats it as
fact, which leaves the sentence with nothing to contrast.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 忙(いそが)しかった<b>にしても</b>、返事(へんじ)くらいはしてほしかった。
(b) 忙(いそが)しかったの<b>ならともかく</b>、ただ面倒(めんどう)だっただけなら失礼(しつれい)だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) accepts that the person really was busy. The complaint stands anyway: even
granting the excuse, a reply was owed. The speaker is disappointed but not
questioning the facts.

(b) does not accept it. Being busy is offered as the reason that would have been
acceptable, and then set against what the speaker believes actually happened - the
person could not be bothered. This is an accusation, and it invites the listener to
deny it.

The English "even if" covers both, which is why learners reach for the wrong one.
Ask whether you believe the excuse: if yes, `にしても`; if no, `ならともかく`.

</details>
