# ～にあたり / ～にあたって

**Meaning** · on the occasion of; now that we stand at the point of - said at the threshold of something significant that is about to begin

<code>にあたり</code> is built on <code>当(あ)たる</code>, "to face something head on". It
places the sentence at the moment a **significant undertaking starts** - a posting,
a merger, an opening ceremony, a course of study - and reports what is done, felt or
asked for at that threshold. It lives in speeches, announcements, formal letters and
the "before you begin" page of an official form. Used over a trip to the shops it
sounds as though you have stepped up to a lectern.

## Formation

The noun or clause in front names the undertaking. `にあたり` is the 連用形(れんようけい)
(the stem-joining form) and `にあたって` the て-form of the
same expression; they mean the same thing.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event noun | Noun + にあたり | 開会(かいかい) → 開会(かいかい)にあたり |
| Event noun (slightly softer) | Noun + にあたって | 入学(にゅうがく) → 入学(にゅうがく)にあたって |
| Verb (dictionary form) | dictionary + にあたり／にあたって | 出発(しゅっぱつ)する → 出発(しゅっぱつ)するにあたって |
| Marked as the topic | ～にあたっては | 申(もう)し込(こ)む → 申(もう)し込(こ)むにあたっては |
| Before a noun | ～にあたっての + Noun | 着任(ちゃくにん) → 着任(ちゃくにん)にあたっての挨拶(あいさつ) |
| Spoken formal address | ～にあたりまして | 開店(かいてん) → 開店(かいてん)にあたりまして |
| ❌ Past form | ❌卒業(そつぎょう)したにあたって | ✅卒業(そつぎょう)するにあたって |
| ❌ Adjective | ❌忙(いそが)しいにあたって | ✅忙(いそが)しい時(とき)に |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - three tests. Is the occasion **big** enough to make a speech
about? Is it **starting**, not finished? Is the surrounding sentence formal? If any
answer is no, you want <code>時(とき)に</code> or <code>際(さい)に</code> instead.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
開会(かいかい)<b>にあたり</b>、会長(かいちょう)が挨拶(あいさつ)をした。
The chairman gave an address to open the meeting.
</pre>

<pre>
留学(りゅうがく)する<b>にあたって</b>、必要(ひつよう)な書類(しょるい)をそろえた。
I got the necessary documents together for going to study abroad.
</pre>

<pre>
ご入居(にゅうきょ)<b>にあたり</b>、契約書(けいやくしょ)にご署名(しょめい)をお願(ねが)いいたします。
We ask you to sign the contract when you take up residence.
</pre>

<pre>
工事(こうじ)を始(はじ)める<b>にあたって</b>、近所(きんじょ)の方(かた)に説明(せつめい)して回(まわ)った。
Before starting the building work, we went round explaining it to the neighbours.
</pre>

<pre>
新(あたら)しいシステムの導入(どうにゅう)<b>にあたっては</b>、全社員(ぜんしゃいん)に研修(けんしゅう)を行(おこな)う予定(よてい)です。
For the introduction of the new system, we plan to train every member of staff.
</pre>

<pre>
会社(かいしゃ)を設立(せつりつ)する<b>にあたり</b>、銀行(ぎんこう)から融資(ゆうし)を受(う)けることになった。
In setting up the company, we ended up taking a loan from the bank.
</pre>

<pre>
本日(ほんじつ)、新(あたら)しい支店(してん)を開(ひら)く<b>にあたりまして</b>、皆様(みなさま)に一言(ひとこと)お礼(れい)を申(もう)し上(あ)げます。
Today, on the occasion of opening our new branch, allow me to say a word of thanks to you all.
</pre>

<pre>
市長(しちょう)に就任(しゅうにん)する<b>にあたって</b>、彼女(かのじょ)はまず市民(しみん)の声(こえ)を聞(き)く場(ば)をつくると約束(やくそく)した。
On taking office as mayor, she promised that her first act would be to create a forum for residents to be heard.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is written and ceremonial Japanese. You will meet it in the opening line of a
speech, in the preamble of a notice, in a covering letter, and on the first page of
an official form. You will not meet it between friends, and a learner who deploys it
over a small personal plan sounds unintentionally grand.

Of the two shapes, `にあたり` is the stiffer: it is the form of choice for printed
notices and prepared remarks. `にあたって` is a shade lighter and is what a manager
would say aloud in a meeting. `にあたりまして` is the polite-spoken version and
belongs almost entirely to podium Japanese. There is no casual contraction; the
pattern simply does not go there.

The main clause is nearly always one of four things: a **greeting**
(ご挨拶(あいさつ)申(もう)し上(あ)げます), a **request** (ご注意(ちゅうい)ください),
a **preparation** (準備(じゅんび)した、調(しら)べた), or a **resolution**
(決(き)めた、約束(やくそく)した). Adverbs such as まず, 改(あらた)めて and ぜひ
sit naturally in front of it.

<div class="note">

**Note** - do not confuse this with the ordinary verb <code>当(あ)たる</code> doing
its other jobs: <code>一(いち)ドルは約(やく)百五十円(ひゃくごじゅうえん)に当(あ)たる</code>
("amounts to") and <code>対応(たいおう)に当(あ)たる</code> ("to be assigned to
handle"). Those are Noun + に + verb, not the connective.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～にあたり vs ～際(さい)に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>際(さい)に</code> is the formal word for **"when"** and
will take any occasion, including a repeated one or a past one. `にあたり` needs a
**one-off undertaking** and looks forward from its starting line.

</div>

<pre>
ご利用(りよう)の<b>際(さい)は</b>、受付(うけつけ)で番号札(ばんごうふだ)をお取(と)りください。
When you use the service, please take a numbered ticket at reception.
</pre>

<pre>
事業(じぎょう)を引(ひ)き継(つ)ぐ<b>にあたり</b>、従業員(じゅうぎょういん)全員(ぜんいん)と面談(めんだん)した。
On taking over the business, I met every employee individually.
</pre>

The first happens every time a customer walks in, and `にあたり` would be absurd
there. The second happens once, and the sentence stands at its beginning. Note also
that <code>際(さい)に</code> attaches happily to a た-form for a past occasion -
<code>面接(めんせつ)を受(う)けた際(さい)に</code> - while `にあたり` stays
non-past even when the whole sentence is in the past.

### ～にあたり vs ～に際(さい)して

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are formal occasion markers and they overlap. The tendency:
`にあたって` is the **threshold of something you are launching**, and carries
preparation and resolve; <code>に際(さい)して</code> simply marks the **time of a
specific event**, and is comfortable with unwelcome ones.

</div>

<pre>
退職(たいしょく)<b>に際(さい)し</b>、長年(ながねん)のご厚意(こうい)にお礼(れい)を申(もう)し上(あ)げます。
On my retirement, I would like to thank you for your kindness over many years.
</pre>

<pre>
新(あたら)しい部署(ぶしょ)を立(た)ち上(あ)げる<b>にあたって</b>、三年分(さんねんぶん)の計画(けいかく)を書(か)いた。
In launching the new department, I wrote out a three-year plan.
</pre>

Retirement, cancellation and emergencies take <code>に際(さい)して</code> readily;
`にあたって` there would suggest you were relishing the occasion. Treat this as a
leaning rather than a wall - <code>開店(かいてん)に際(さい)して</code> is
perfectly normal too.

### ～にあたり vs ～に先(さき)立(だ)ち

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</code> is strictly **before**: the
main clause happens in the run-up. `にあたり` sits **at** the occasion and covers the
doing of it as well.

</div>

<pre>
開幕(かいまく)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、記者会見(きしゃかいけん)が開(ひら)かれた。
Ahead of the opening, a press conference was held.
</pre>

<pre>
開幕(かいまく)<b>にあたり</b>、監督(かんとく)が今季(こんき)の目標(もくひょう)を語(かた)った。
At the opening of the season, the manager spoke about his goals for the year.
</pre>

The press conference is genuinely earlier than the event; the manager remarks as
part of the occasion itself. Where the main clause is a **preparation**, both are
possible; where it is a **greeting delivered on the day**, only `にあたり` fits.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ コンビニへ行(い)く<b>にあたって</b>、傘(かさ)を持(も)った。 → ✅ コンビニへ行(い)く<b>時(とき)に</b>、傘(かさ)を持(も)った。
  The occasion has to be worth a speech. A trip to the shop takes
  <code>時(とき)に</code>.

- ❌ 卒業(そつぎょう)した<b>にあたって</b>、恩師(おんし)に手紙(てがみ)を書(か)いた。 → ✅ 卒業(そつぎょう)する<b>にあたって</b>、恩師(おんし)に手紙(てがみ)を書(か)いた。
  The verb stays in the dictionary form. Tense is carried by the final verb, so a
  past sentence still reads <code>卒業(そつぎょう)するにあたって</code>.

- ❌ 新(あたら)しい生活(せいかつ)が不安(ふあん)な<b>にあたって</b>… → ✅ 新(あたら)しい生活(せいかつ)を始(はじ)める<b>にあたって</b>…
  Adjectives and states do not attach. `にあたり` wants an **event**: something that
  begins at a point in time.

- ❌ 開会(かいかい)<b>に当(あ)たる</b>、会長(かいちょう)が挨拶(あいさつ)した。 → ✅ 開会(かいかい)<b>に当(あ)たり</b>、会長(かいちょう)が挨拶(あいさつ)した。
  The connective is the 連用形(れんようけい) <code>あたり</code> or the て-form <code>あたって</code>.
  The dictionary form <code>あたる</code> is the plain verb and cannot join two clauses
  like this.

## Practice

**1.** 新製品(しんせいひん)を発売(はつばい)する____、大(おお)きな広告(こうこく)を出(だ)すことにした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

発売(はつばい)する<b>にあたり</b>（or にあたって）

A product launch is a one-off undertaking, and the advertising is the preparation
made at its threshold. The verb stays non-past even though the decision is reported
in the past.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 工場(こうじょう)を見学(けんがく)する（にあたって・際(さい)に）は、ヘルメットを着用(ちゃくよう)してください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

見学(けんがく)する<b>際(さい)</b>は

Factory tours happen over and over, and the rule applies to every visitor on every
visit. That is the routine, repeatable occasion <code>際(さい)に</code> was made
for. `にあたって` would frame one particular tour as a landmark event.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "On taking up my new post, I would like to say a few words."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

着任(ちゃくにん)する<b>にあたり</b>、一言(ひとこと)ご挨拶(あいさつ)申(もう)し上(あ)げます。

This is the archetypal use: a formal greeting delivered at the threshold of a new
role. <code>着任(ちゃくにん)にあたり</code> with the bare noun is equally good, and
is what a printed notice would say.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 試合(しあい)が終(お)わるにあたって、選手(せんしゅ)たちは握手(あくしゅ)をした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 試合(しあい)が終(お)わった<b>時(とき)</b>、選手(せんしゅ)たちは握手(あくしゅ)をした。

`にあたり` marks the **start** of an undertaking, not its end, so pairing it with
<code>終(お)わる</code> pulls against the pattern. An ending takes
<code>時(とき)</code>, と, or - in formal writing -
<code>終了(しゅうりょう)に際(さい)し</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 入学(にゅうがく)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、健康診断(けんこうしんだん)が行(おこな)われる。
(b) 入学(にゅうがく)<b>にあたり</b>、校長(こうちょう)が新入生(しんにゅうせい)に話(はなし)をする。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) puts the medical check clearly **before** enrolment - it is a hurdle in the
run-up, finished by the time term starts.

(b) puts the talk **at** enrolment itself. It is part of the occasion rather than a
preliminary to it, which is why `にあたり` and not
<code>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</code> is the natural choice.

</details>

# ～に際して / ～に際し

**Meaning** · on the occasion of; at the time of - pins an action to the moment of a specific, usually one-time event

<code>に際(さい)して</code> takes the noun <code>際(さい)</code>, "juncture,
occasion", and turns it into a connective. It says: **this event is the point in time
at which the following happens** - the paperwork you hand over, the instruction you
are given, the words you say. It is the language of contracts, hospital discharge
sheets, station announcements and prepared speeches, and it is comfortable with
occasions that are difficult as well as happy: retirement, cancellation, a disaster.

## Formation

The event may be a noun or a dictionary-form clause. Note that no `の` intervenes -
that `の` belongs to <code>際(さい)に</code>, not to this pattern.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event noun | Noun + に際(さい)して | 出発(しゅっぱつ) → 出発(しゅっぱつ)に際(さい)して |
| Event noun (stiffer, written) | Noun + に際(さい)し | 契約(けいやく) → 契約(けいやく)に際(さい)し |
| Verb (dictionary form) | dictionary + に際(さい)して | 申(もう)し込(こ)む → 申(もう)し込(こ)むに際(さい)して |
| Marked as the topic | ～に際(さい)しては | 解約(かいやく) → 解約(かいやく)に際(さい)しては |
| Before a noun | ～に際(さい)しての + Noun | 入居(にゅうきょ) → 入居(にゅうきょ)に際(さい)しての注意(ちゅうい) |
| Spoken formal address | ～に際(さい)しまして | 開会(かいかい) → 開会(かいかい)に際(さい)しまして |
| ❌ With の | ❌利用(りよう)のに際(さい)して | ✅利用(りよう)に際(さい)して |
| ❌ Past form | ❌退職(たいしょく)したに際(さい)して | ✅退職(たいしょく)するに際(さい)して |
| ❌ Adjective or state | ❌若(わか)いに際(さい)して | ✅若(わか)い時(とき)に |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the noun on its own survives in a handful of set phrases, above all
<code>非常(ひじょう)の際(さい)</code>, "in an emergency", which is printed beside
half the emergency buttons in Japan.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
出発(しゅっぱつ)<b>に際(さい)して</b>、注意(ちゅうい)事項(じこう)をお伝(つた)えします。
Before you set off, let me go over the points to be aware of.
</pre>

<pre>
契約(けいやく)<b>に際(さい)し</b>、印鑑(いんかん)が必要(ひつよう)になります。
A seal will be required when the contract is signed.
</pre>

<pre>
お申(もう)し込(こ)み<b>に際(さい)しては</b>、住所(じゅうしょ)を正確(せいかく)にご記入(きにゅう)ください。
When applying, please fill in your address accurately.
</pre>

<pre>
転校(てんこう)<b>に際(さい)して</b>、担任(たんにん)の先生(せんせい)が手紙(てがみ)をくれた。
When I changed schools, my class teacher gave me a letter.
</pre>

<pre>
災害(さいがい)発生(はっせい)<b>に際(さい)しては</b>、まず身(み)の安全(あんぜん)を確保(かくほ)してください。
In the event of a disaster, secure your own safety first.
</pre>

<pre>
閉店(へいてん)<b>に際(さい)しまして</b>、長年(ながねん)のご愛顧(あいこ)に心(こころ)よりお礼(れい)申(もう)し上(あ)げます。
As we close our doors, we thank you most sincerely for your custom over so many years.
</pre>

<pre>
入社(にゅうしゃ)<b>に際(さい)し</b>ご提出(ていしゅつ)いただいた書類(しょるい)は、当社(とうしゃ)で厳重(げんじゅう)に保管(ほかん)いたします。
The documents you submit when joining the company are kept under strict safeguard by us.
</pre>

<pre>
退院(たいいん)<b>に際(さい)して</b>、担当(たんとう)の医師(いし)から食事(しょくじ)についてくわしい説明(せつめい)があった。
On being discharged, I was given a detailed explanation about diet by the doctor in charge.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Firmly written and ceremonial. It belongs to announcements, terms and conditions,
formal letters and speeches; between friends it is unusable, and a sentence about
meeting a friend for coffee wants <code>時(とき)に</code>. The three shapes rank
by stiffness: <code>に際(さい)し</code> is the most formal and the most printed,
<code>に際(さい)して</code> is the everyday formal default, and
<code>に際(さい)しまして</code> is what you say out loud at a microphone.

What the pattern really asks for is a **juncture** - a moment at which something is
transacted or transitioned. That is why the main clause is so often a procedure
(ご記入(きにゅう)ください), an instruction
(確認(かくにん)してください), or a formal set of words
(お礼(れい)申(もう)し上(あ)げます).

Unlike `にあたって`, it does not need the occasion to be one you are launching with
enthusiasm. Cancellation, resignation, closure and emergencies all take
<code>に際(さい)して</code> naturally. That said, this is a tendency in what people
choose to write, not a grammatical rule: happy openings take it too.

The learner should also keep <code>際(さい)</code> the noun in view. N3 taught
<code>際(さい)に</code>, and the difference in surface shape is one particle:
<code>利用(りよう)の際(さい)に</code> against
<code>利用(りよう)に際(さい)して</code>. Mixing them produces
❌<code>利用(りよう)のに際(さい)して</code>, which is one of the commonest slips
at this level.

## Contrast

### ～に際(さい)して vs ～際(さい)に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>際(さい)に</code> is the general formal "when" and takes
**routine, repeatable** occasions happily, including past ones. <code>に際(さい)して</code>
frames the event as a **one-off juncture** and stays non-past.

</div>

<pre>
新幹線(しんかんせん)をご利用(りよう)の<b>際(さい)は</b>、大(おお)きな荷物(にもつ)を座席(ざせき)の後(うし)ろにお置(お)きください。
When travelling on the Shinkansen, please put large luggage behind your seat.
</pre>

<pre>
会員(かいいん)を退会(たいかい)する<b>に際(さい)して</b>、アンケートにご協力(きょうりょく)をお願(ねが)いしております。
When cancelling your membership, we ask for your help with a short survey.
</pre>

The first is addressed to every passenger on every trip. The second happens once per
member. Grammar follows the meaning: <code>際(さい)に</code> can look back at a
finished occasion with a た-form -
<code>退会(たいかい)した際(さい)に</code> - while
<code>に際(さい)して</code> cannot.

### ～に際(さい)して vs ～にあたって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `にあたって` stands at the **threshold of an undertaking** and
carries preparation and resolve. <code>に際(さい)して</code> merely marks **the
time of the event**, and does not mind if the event is unwelcome.

</div>

<pre>
解散(かいさん)<b>に際(さい)して</b>、メンバーは最後(さいご)の演奏(えんそう)を録音(ろくおん)した。
On disbanding, the members recorded one last performance.
</pre>

<pre>
新(あたら)しいクラブを作(つく)る<b>にあたって</b>、まず部員(ぶいん)を集(あつ)めた。
In setting up the new club, we started by gathering members.
</pre>

Swap them and both sentences sit oddly: disbanding is not an undertaking you launch,
and founding a club is exactly that. In the wide middle ground - an opening, a launch,
a signing - either is fine and the choice is one of flavour.

### ～に際(さい)して vs ～において

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>において</code> gives the **setting**: where, in what field,
under what circumstances. <code>に際(さい)して</code> gives the **moment**: at the
juncture of what event.

</div>

<pre>
本社(ほんしゃ)の会議室(かいぎしつ)<b>において</b>、来週(らいしゅう)説明会(せつめいかい)を行(おこな)います。
The briefing will be held next week in the meeting room at head office.
</pre>

<pre>
説明会(せつめいかい)<b>に際(さい)して</b>、資料(しりょう)を事前(じぜん)にお配(くば)りします。
For the briefing, we will hand out the materials in advance.
</pre>

<code>において</code> answers "where?", <code>に際(さい)して</code> answers "on
what occasion?". Because a venue is not an event, ❌<code>会議室(かいぎしつ)に際(さい)して</code>
is impossible, and conversely a bare procedural moment cannot be a setting.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ご利用(りよう)の<b>に際(さい)して</b>は… → ✅ ご利用(りよう)<b>に際(さい)して</b>は… （or ご利用(りよう)の<b>際(さい)は</b>…）
  The `の` belongs to the noun <code>際(さい)</code>. This pattern takes the noun
  directly, with nothing in between.

- ❌ 子(こ)ども<b>に際(さい)して</b>、よくこの川(かわ)で泳(およ)いだ。 → ✅ 子(こ)どもの<b>時(とき)</b>、よくこの川(かわ)で泳(およ)いだ。
  Childhood is a stretch of life, not an occasion. The pattern needs an **event**
  with a point in time attached to it.

- ❌ 留学(りゅうがく)した<b>に際(さい)して</b>、ビザを取(と)った。 → ✅ 留学(りゅうがく)する<b>に際(さい)して</b>、ビザを取(と)った。
  The verb stays in the dictionary form; the past tense is carried by the final verb
  of the sentence.

- ❌ 友(とも)だちと会(あ)う<b>に際(さい)して</b>、駅(えき)で待(ま)ち合(あ)わせた。 → ✅ 友(とも)だちと会(あ)う<b>時(とき)</b>、駅(えき)で待(ま)ち合(あ)わせた。
  Grammatically well formed, socially absurd. Meeting a friend is not a ceremonial
  juncture, and the formal register lands as a joke.

## Practice

**1.** ご入学(にゅうがく)____、制服(せいふく)の採寸(さいすん)を行(おこな)います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ご入学(にゅうがく)<b>に際(さい)し</b>（or に際(さい)して）

Enrolment is a one-time formal juncture and the uniform fitting is the procedure
transacted at that point. A school notice would use the stiffer
<code>に際(さい)し</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この機械(きかい)をご使用(しよう)（の際(さい)は・に際(さい)しては）、必(かなら)ず手袋(てぶくろ)をしてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ご使用(しよう)<b>の際(さい)は</b>

Both are grammatical, but using the machine is something the reader does repeatedly,
and a safety notice is aimed at every use. That is the ordinary formal "when", so
the noun <code>際(さい)</code> with `の` is the natural fit.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "When you cancel, please contact us at least three days in advance."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

キャンセル<b>に際(さい)しては</b>、三日前(みっかまえ)までにご連絡(れんらく)ください。

Cancellation is exactly the kind of unwelcome one-off juncture this pattern handles
without flinching. The `は` marks it as the topic and sets up the instruction that
follows.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 工場(こうじょう)のに際(さい)して、見学(けんがく)の方(かた)は受付(うけつけ)にお越(こ)しください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 工場(こうじょう)見学(けんがく)<b>に際(さい)して</b>は、受付(うけつけ)にお越(こ)しください。

Two things went wrong: the stray `の`, which belongs to
<code>際(さい)に</code> and not here, and the missing event -
<code>工場(こうじょう)</code> is a place, so it has to be turned into an occasion,
<code>工場(こうじょう)見学(けんがく)</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 移転(いてん)<b>に際(さい)して</b>、住所(じゅうしょ)変更(へんこう)の手続(てつづ)きが必要(ひつよう)です。
(b) 移転(いてん)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)って</b>、住所(じゅうしょ)変更(へんこう)の手続(てつづ)きが必要(ひつよう)です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply attaches the paperwork to the move as its occasion. It could be done
before, during or just after; the sentence does not say.

(b) puts the paperwork firmly **ahead** of the move and implies a deadline: finish
this first, then relocate. If the timing matters,
<code>に先(さき)立(だ)って</code> is the sentence that says so.

</details>

# ～に先立ち / ～に先立って

**Meaning** · prior to; ahead of - the main clause happens in the run-up to a scheduled event, as part of getting there

<code>先(さき)立(だ)つ</code> means "to go in front". The pattern names a
**planned event**, then reports something that happens **before it and because of
it**: the rehearsal before the ceremony, the briefing before the works, the anthem
before the match. It is announcement and news-report Japanese - the sentence shape
<code>～に先(さき)立(だ)ち、～が行(おこな)われた</code> turns up in a bulletin
several times a night.

## Formation

The event goes in front, and it is normally a noun. <code>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</code> is the stem form and
<code>に先(さき)立(だ)って</code> the て-form; <code>に先(さき)立(だ)つ</code> is used when a noun follows.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event noun | Noun + に先(さき)立(だ)ち | 開会(かいかい) → 開会(かいかい)に先(さき)立(だ)ち |
| Event noun (slightly lighter) | Noun + に先(さき)立(だ)って | 試合(しあい) → 試合(しあい)に先(さき)立(だ)って |
| Verb (dictionary form) | dictionary + に先(さき)立(だ)ち | 出発(しゅっぱつ)する → 出発(しゅっぱつ)するに先(さき)立(だ)ち |
| Before a noun | ～に先(さき)立(だ)つ + Noun | 発売(はつばい) → 発売(はつばい)に先(さき)立(だ)つ試写会(ししゃかい) |
| Marked as the topic | ～に先(さき)立(だ)っては | 審査(しんさ) → 審査(しんさ)に先(さき)立(だ)っては |
| ❌ Past form | ❌閉(し)めたに先(さき)立(だ)って | ✅閉(し)めるに先(さき)立(だ)って |
| ❌ As a main verb here | ❌会議(かいぎ)に先(さき)立(だ)った、資料(しりょう)を配(くば)った | ✅会議(かいぎ)に先(さき)立(だ)ち、資料(しりょう)を配(くば)った |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - two conditions. The event in front must be **scheduled**, and
the main clause must be **preparatory to it**. If either fails, you want
<code>前(まえ)に</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
試合(しあい)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、国歌(こっか)が演奏(えんそう)された。
Ahead of the match, the national anthem was played.
</pre>

<pre>
会議(かいぎ)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)って</b>、資料(しりょう)が配(くば)られた。
The materials were handed out ahead of the meeting.
</pre>

<pre>
面接(めんせつ)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、筆記(ひっき)試験(しけん)が行(おこな)われます。
A written test will be held prior to the interview.
</pre>

<pre>
工事(こうじ)の開始(かいし)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、住民(じゅうみん)への説明会(せつめいかい)が開(ひら)かれた。
Before the works began, a briefing was held for local residents.
</pre>

<pre>
発売(はつばい)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)つ</b>試写会(ししゃかい)に、多(おお)くのファンが集(あつ)まった。
Crowds of fans turned out for the preview screening held ahead of the release.
</pre>

<pre>
引(ひ)っ越(こ)し<b>に先(さき)立(だ)って</b>、電気(でんき)と水道(すいどう)の手続(てつづ)きを済(す)ませておいた。
Ahead of the move, I got the electricity and water sorted out.
</pre>

<pre>
新(あたら)しい制度(せいど)の導入(どうにゅう)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)って</b>、市(し)は一年間(いちねんかん)の試験的(しけんてき)な運用(うんよう)を行(おこな)った。
Ahead of introducing the new system, the city ran it on a trial basis for a year.
</pre>

<pre>
首相(しゅしょう)は訪問(ほうもん)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)って</b>、記者団(きしゃだん)に今回(こんかい)の狙(ねら)いを説明(せつめい)した。
Ahead of the visit, the prime minister explained its aims to the press.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Written, official and above all journalistic. Newsreaders, programmes, timetables
and company notices are its home; nobody says it about brushing their teeth. If you
want to be understood as neutral and human, <code>前(まえ)に</code> is the word.

<code>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</code> is the stiffer of the two shapes and dominates printed schedules and
formal writing. <code>に先(さき)立(だ)って</code> is a little lighter and is what a chairperson might say
aloud. Neither has a casual form.

The pattern implies more than sequence: the earlier action **belongs to** the later
event. That is why the two clauses are almost always about the same undertaking - a
rehearsal and its concert, a survey and the construction it clears the way for.
Two unrelated things that merely happened in that order need
<code>前(まえ)に</code>, which asks nothing of them.

One structural point that catches learners: the *event* takes `に`, and the *earlier
thing* is the subject of the main clause. English inverts this
("the anthem preceded the match"), so it is worth reading a few examples until the
Japanese order stops feeling backwards.

## Contrast

### ～に先(さき)立(だ)ち vs ～前(まえ)に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>前(まえ)に</code> is plain "before" and works for
anything at all. <code>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</code> is formal and needs the two
events to be **parts of one arrangement**.

</div>

<pre>
寝(ね)る<b>前(まえ)に</b>歯(は)を磨(みが)きなさい。
Brush your teeth before you go to bed.
</pre>

<pre>
出港(しゅっこう)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、船内(せんない)の安全(あんぜん)点検(てんけん)が行(おこな)われる。
Before departure, a safety inspection is carried out on board.
</pre>

Teeth and bedtime are not a joint arrangement, and the register is domestic;
<code>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</code> there would be comic. The inspection, by
contrast, exists **for** the sailing and appears on the same schedule.

### ～に先(さき)立(だ)ち vs ～にあたって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</code> is **earlier than** the
event; `にあたって` is **at** it. Ask whether the event has begun yet.

</div>

<pre>
改装(かいそう)工事(こうじ)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、店内(てんない)の商品(しょうひん)をすべて運(はこ)び出(だ)した。
Ahead of the refurbishment, we moved every item out of the shop.
</pre>

<pre>
改装(かいそう)工事(こうじ)を始(はじ)める<b>にあたって</b>、職人(しょくにん)に注文(ちゅうもん)を細(こま)かく伝(つた)えた。
In embarking on the refurbishment, I gave the tradesmen detailed instructions.
</pre>

Both sentences describe preparation, and in cases like this the two really do
overlap. They separate at the edges: a greeting on the day can only be `にあたって`,
and something completed weeks earlier can only be
<code>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</code>.

### ～に先(さき)立(だ)ち vs ～を前(まえ)に(して)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を前(まえ)に(して)</code> puts you **facing** something
that is coming, and often reports a feeling. <code>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</code>
reports an **arrangement** completed in advance.

</div>

<pre>
大会(たいかい)<b>を前(まえ)に</b>、選手(せんしゅ)たちは緊張(きんちょう)を隠(かく)せない様子(ようす)だった。
With the tournament looming, the players could not hide their nerves.
</pre>

<pre>
大会(たいかい)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、抽選(ちゅうせん)で対戦(たいせん)相手(あいて)が決(き)められた。
Ahead of the tournament, the draw decided who would face whom.
</pre>

Nerves are not a preparation, so the first sentence resists
<code>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</code>. The draw is a scheduled preliminary, so it
resists <code>を前(まえ)に</code>. Where the main clause is genuinely
preparatory, both are heard.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 買(か)い物(もの)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)って</b>、財布(さいふ)を確認(かくにん)した。 → ✅ 買(か)い物(もの)の<b>前(まえ)に</b>、財布(さいふ)を確認(かくにん)した。
  Everyday errands are not scheduled public events. The formal pattern makes a trip
  to the shops sound like a state visit.

- ❌ 会議(かいぎ)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)った</b>、資料(しりょう)を配(くば)った。 → ✅ 会議(かいぎ)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、資料(しりょう)を配(くば)った。
  The connective is <code>先(さき)立(だ)ち</code> or
  <code>先(さき)立(だ)って</code>. The form <code>先(さき)立(だ)つ</code>
  appears only directly in front of a noun.

- ❌ 閉店(へいてん)した<b>に先(さき)立(だ)って</b>、セールを行(おこな)った。 → ✅ 閉店(へいてん)する<b>に先(さき)立(だ)って</b>、セールを行(おこな)った。
  A verb in front stays in the dictionary form, however firmly the event is in the
  past.

- ❌ 開会(かいかい)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、会(かい)が開(ひら)かれた。 → ✅ 開会(かいかい)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、リハーサルが行(おこな)われた。
  The main clause must be a **different**, earlier action. Restating the same event
  produces a sentence that eats its own tail.

## Practice

**1.** 新(あたら)しい路線(ろせん)の開通(かいつう)____、試運転(しうんてん)が繰(く)り返(かえ)された。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

開通(かいつう)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>（or に先(さき)立(だ)って）

The test runs exist for the sake of the opening and are on the same schedule, which
is exactly the relationship this pattern encodes. A news report would use the stem
form.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 家(いえ)を出(で)る（前(まえ)に・に先(さき)立(だ)って）、ガスを消(け)したか確(たし)かめた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

家(いえ)を出(で)る<b>前(まえ)に</b>

Leaving the house is not a scheduled event with a programme attached, and the
register is ordinary domestic speech. Plain <code>前(まえ)に</code> is the only
natural choice here.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Ahead of the opening of the exhibition, the press were shown round."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

展覧会(てんらんかい)の開幕(かいまく)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、報道(ほうどう)関係者(かんけいしゃ)向(む)けの内覧会(ないらんかい)が行(おこな)われた。

The press view is a scheduled preliminary belonging to the exhibition itself, and
the whole sentence is in report register - the natural home of this pattern.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 卒業式(そつぎょうしき)に先(さき)立(だ)つ、練習(れんしゅう)が三回(さんかい)行(おこな)われた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 卒業式(そつぎょうしき)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、練習(れんしゅう)が三回(さんかい)行(おこな)われた。

<code>先(さき)立(だ)つ</code> is the noun-modifying form and cannot end a
clause. To join two clauses you need the stem
<code>先(さき)立(だ)ち</code> or the て-form
<code>先(さき)立(だ)って</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 開店(かいてん)<b>に先(さき)立(だ)ち</b>、従業員(じゅうぎょういん)に研修(けんしゅう)を行(おこな)った。
(b) 開店(かいてん)<b>に際(さい)して</b>、従業員(じゅうぎょういん)に研修(けんしゅう)を行(おこな)った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) places the training clearly **before** opening day and presents it as one of the
preparations that had to be finished first.

(b) attaches the training loosely to the opening as its occasion. It may have run
before the doors opened or in the first week; the sentence only says the opening was
what prompted it.

</details>

# ～に伴って / ～に伴い

**Meaning** · as; along with; in consequence of - one thing happens or changes, and a second thing comes with it

<code>伴(ともな)う</code> is "to accompany". The pattern names a change or an event,
and says that something else **comes along in its train**: prices move and the
timetable is revised, a typhoon approaches and services stop, a company grows and
the paperwork grows with it. It is the connective of notices, announcements and
news reports, and it is deliberately neutral - it reports the pairing without
blaming anyone for it.

## Formation

The front half is a noun naming a change or an event, or a verb of change in the
dictionary form.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun of change | Noun + に伴(ともな)って | 高齢化(こうれいか) → 高齢化(こうれいか)に伴(ともな)って |
| Noun (stiffer, written) | Noun + に伴(ともな)い | 改正(かいせい) → 改正(かいせい)に伴(ともな)い |
| Event noun | Noun + に伴(ともな)い | 接近(せっきん) → 台風(たいふう)の接近(せっきん)に伴(ともな)い |
| Verb (dictionary form) | dictionary + に伴(ともな)って | 増(ふ)える → 増(ふ)えるに伴(ともな)って |
| Before a noun | ～に伴(ともな)う + Noun | 工事(こうじ) → 工事(こうじ)に伴(ともな)う通行(つうこう)止(ど)め |
| ❌ Past form | ❌増(ふ)えたに伴(ともな)い | ✅増(ふ)えるに伴(ともな)い |
| ❌ Adjective | ❌寒(さむ)いに伴(ともな)って | ✅寒(さむ)くなるに伴(ともな)って |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the verb also appears as <code>～を伴(ともな)って</code>, which is a
different structure meaning "bringing X along":
<code>通訳(つうやく)を伴(ともな)って出席(しゅっせき)した</code>, "attended with an
interpreter". The connective always takes `に`.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
気温(きおん)の上昇(じょうしょう)<b>に伴(ともな)って</b>、電力(でんりょく)の使用量(しようりょう)が増(ふ)えた。
As the temperature rose, electricity consumption went up with it.
</pre>

<pre>
人口(じんこう)が増(ふ)える<b>に伴(ともな)い</b>、ごみの量(りょう)も増(ふ)えた。
As the population grew, so did the volume of rubbish.
</pre>

<pre>
台風(たいふう)の接近(せっきん)<b>に伴(ともな)い</b>、明日(あす)の試合(しあい)は中止(ちゅうし)となりました。
Owing to the approaching typhoon, tomorrow's match has been cancelled.
</pre>

<pre>
法律(ほうりつ)の改正(かいせい)<b>に伴(ともな)って</b>、申請(しんせい)の方法(ほうほう)が変(か)わります。
With the change in the law, the application procedure is changing.
</pre>

<pre>
会社(かいしゃ)の規模(きぼ)が大(おお)きくなる<b>に伴(ともな)い</b>、管理(かんり)の仕事(しごと)ばかりが増(ふ)えていった。
As the company got bigger, the management work was all that grew.
</pre>

<pre>
道路(どうろ)工事(こうじ)<b>に伴(ともな)う</b>通行(つうこう)止(ど)めは、来月(らいげつ)末(まつ)まで続(つづ)く見(み)込(こ)みです。
The road closure that goes with the works is expected to last until the end of next month.
</pre>

<pre>
在宅勤務(ざいたくきんむ)の普及(ふきゅう)<b>に伴(ともな)い</b>、都心(としん)のオフィスの需要(じゅよう)が減(へ)りつつある。
As working from home has spread, demand for offices in the city centre has been falling.
</pre>

<pre>
事務所(じむしょ)移転(いてん)<b>に伴(ともな)い</b>、電話(でんわ)番号(ばんごう)が下記(かき)のとおり変更(へんこう)となります。
In connection with our office relocation, our telephone number will change as below.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Formal and written. Its natural habitats are the notice on the door, the customer
email, the local-government leaflet and the news bulletin. In relaxed conversation
people say だから or ので and leave it at that.

`に伴い` is the stiffer stem form and is what printed announcements use; `に伴って`
is a shade lighter and is heard in careful speech. <code>に伴(ともな)う</code>
does the same work in front of a noun, and the phrase
<code>～に伴(ともな)う影響(えいきょう)</code> ("the knock-on effects of ～") is
close to a fixed expression in official writing.

The tone is worth noticing. Because the pattern presents the second event as
something that simply **came with** the first, it is a courteous way to announce an
unwelcome consequence: a price rise, a cancellation, a change of procedure. Nobody
is said to have decided anything. That is precisely why companies reach for it.

Two things it does not do. It does not carry emotion - a sentence about how you
felt as the day approached wants something else. And it does not mean "in the
company of a person"; that job belongs to
<code>～を伴(ともな)って</code> or, in ordinary speech,
<code>～と一緒(いっしょ)に</code>.

## Contrast

### ～に伴(ともな)って vs ～につれて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `につれて` needs **two gradual changes moving together**.
<code>に伴(ともな)って</code> accepts that too, but also takes a **single event**
with consequences hanging off it.

</div>

<pre>
年(とし)を取(と)る<b>につれて</b>、朝(あさ)早(はや)く目(め)が覚(さ)めるようになった。
As I have got older, I have started waking up early.
</pre>

<pre>
大雨(おおあめ)<b>に伴(ともな)い</b>、川(かわ)の水位(すいい)が急(きゅう)に上(あ)がった。
With the heavy rain, the river level rose sharply.
</pre>

Ageing is a slow slide, so `につれて` fits perfectly. Heavy rain arriving is an
event, not a slide, and ❌<code>大雨(おおあめ)につれて</code> is wrong for that
reason. Where both halves really are gradual, the two are interchangeable and
<code>に伴(ともな)い</code> simply sounds more official.

### ～に伴(ともな)って vs ～とともに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `とともに` says the two things are **simultaneous** and treats
them as equals. <code>に伴(ともな)って</code> makes one the **main event** and the
other its accompaniment.

</div>

<pre>
試験(しけん)の終了(しゅうりょう)<b>とともに</b>、教室(きょうしつ)が一気(いっき)に騒(さわ)がしくなった。
The moment the exam ended, the classroom erupted into noise.
</pre>

<pre>
新(あたら)しい駅(えき)の開業(かいぎょう)<b>に伴(ともな)って</b>、バスの路線(ろせん)が見直(みなお)された。
With the opening of the new station, the bus routes were reviewed.
</pre>

`とともに` also does a job this pattern cannot: it links people and things -
<code>家族(かぞく)とともに暮(く)らす</code>. Put a person in front of
<code>に伴(ともな)って</code> and the sentence breaks.

### ～に伴(ともな)って vs ～によって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `によって` names a **cause** and points at it. <code>に伴(ともな)って</code>
reports an **accompanying** change, and deliberately does not point.

</div>

<pre>
地震(じしん)<b>によって</b>水道管(すいどうかん)が壊(こわ)れた。
The earthquake broke the water mains.
</pre>

<pre>
原料費(げんりょうひ)の上昇(じょうしょう)<b>に伴(ともな)い</b>、価格(かかく)を改定(かいてい)させていただきます。
In line with the rise in raw-material costs, we will be revising our prices.
</pre>

The first sentence assigns responsibility to the earthquake. The second could have
said <code>によって</code> too, but the writer chose not to: the price rise is
presented as something that travels alongside the cost increase, which is both
gentler and vaguer. That choice is what makes this pattern so common in
announcements customers will not enjoy.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 妹(いもうと)<b>に伴(ともな)って</b>買(か)い物(もの)に行(い)った。 → ✅ 妹(いもうと)と<b>一緒(いっしょ)に</b>買(か)い物(もの)に行(い)った。
  The connective takes a change or an event, never a person. "Accompanied by" is
  <code>を伴(ともな)って</code>, and in speech
  <code>と一緒(いっしょ)に</code>.

- ❌ 給料(きゅうりょう)が上(あ)がった<b>に伴(ともな)い</b>、生活(せいかつ)が楽(らく)になった。 → ✅ 給料(きゅうりょう)が上(あ)がる<b>に伴(ともな)い</b>、生活(せいかつ)が楽(らく)になった。
  The verb in front stays non-past. Tense lives on the final verb only.

- ❌ 寒(さむ)い<b>に伴(ともな)って</b>、客(きゃく)が減(へ)った。 → ✅ 寒(さむ)くなる<b>に伴(ともな)って</b>、客(きゃく)が減(へ)った。
  Adjectives cannot attach. Turn the state into a change -
  <code>寒(さむ)くなる</code> - and the pattern works.

- ❌ 値上(ねあ)げ<b>に伴(ともな)った</b>、メニューを新(あたら)しくした。 → ✅ 値上(ねあ)げ<b>に伴(ともな)い</b>、メニューを新(あたら)しくした。
  To join two clauses, use the stem <code>伴(ともな)い</code> or the て-form
  <code>伴(ともな)って</code>. <code>伴(ともな)う</code> and
  <code>伴(ともな)った</code> can only sit in front of a noun.

<code>につれて</code> would also pass here, since both halves are gradual.

# ～をきっかけに / ～がきっかけで

**Meaning** · with X as the trigger; X was what started it - names the small event a lasting change can be traced back to

`きっかけ` is the noun "trigger, opening, the thing that got it going". The pattern
looks **back** from a change that has already taken hold and points at the moment it
began: an illness, a chance remark, a film seen in a lecture. The trigger may be
tiny or accidental; what matters is that life went differently afterwards.

## Formation

`きっかけ` is a noun, so a verb clause has to be nominalised with `の` or `こと`
first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event noun | Noun + をきっかけに | 入院(にゅういん) → 入院(にゅういん)をきっかけに |
| Event noun (emphatic) | Noun + をきっかけにして | 失敗(しっぱい) → 失敗(しっぱい)をきっかけにして |
| Event noun (formal, written) | Noun + をきっかけとして | 受賞(じゅしょう) → 受賞(じゅしょう)をきっかけとして |
| Verb clause | plain past + のを／ことをきっかけに | 引(ひ)っ越(こ)した → 引(ひ)っ越(こ)したのをきっかけに |
| As the subject (conversational) | Noun + がきっかけで | 一言(ひとこと) → 一言(ひとこと)がきっかけで |
| As the subject (narrative) | Noun + がきっかけとなって | 記事(きじ) → 記事(きじ)がきっかけとなって |
| ❌ Bare verb | ❌読(よ)むをきっかけに | ✅読(よ)んだのをきっかけに |
| ❌ Wrong particle | ❌一言(ひとこと)がきっかけに | ✅一言(ひとこと)がきっかけで |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the main clause must be a **change that stuck**: started, gave
up, became, came to. If the second half is just physical damage or an immediate
result, you want で or によって instead.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
けが<b>をきっかけに</b>、彼(かれ)は選手(せんしゅ)をやめた。
An injury was what made him give up playing.
</pre>

<pre>
ドラマ<b>をきっかけに</b>、日本語(にほんご)の勉強(べんきょう)を始(はじ)めました。
A TV drama was what got me started on Japanese.
</pre>

<pre>
友人(ゆうじん)の一言(ひとこと)<b>がきっかけで</b>、転職(てんしょく)を決(き)めた。
Something a friend said was what decided me on changing jobs.
</pre>

<pre>
入院(にゅういん)したこと<b>をきっかけに</b>、食生活(しょくせいかつ)を見直(みなお)した。
Being in hospital was what made me rethink the way I eat.
</pre>

<pre>
近所(きんじょ)の祭(まつ)り<b>をきっかけに</b>、隣(となり)の人(ひと)と話(はな)すようになった。
The local festival was how I came to talk to my neighbour.
</pre>

<pre>
大学(だいがく)の授業(じゅぎょう)で一本(いっぽん)の映画(えいが)を見(み)たの<b>をきっかけに</b>、彼女(かのじょ)は脚本(きゃくほん)を書(か)き始(はじ)めた。
Seeing one film in a university class was what set her writing scripts.
</pre>

<pre>
小(ちい)さな地震(じしん)<b>がきっかけとなって</b>、町(まち)の防災(ぼうさい)意識(いしき)が一気(いっき)に高(たか)まった。
A small earthquake was the spark that sharpened the town's awareness of disaster risk.
</pre>

<pre>
祖母(そぼ)からの一通(いっつう)の手紙(てがみ)<b>をきっかけとして</b>、長年(ながねん)続(つづ)いた兄弟(きょうだい)のけんかがようやく終(お)わった。
A single letter from my grandmother was what finally ended a quarrel between the brothers that had gone on for years.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is the member of the family you can actually say out loud. It is neutral,
common in interviews and self-introductions - 「何(なに)がきっかけでこの仕事(しごと)を選(えら)んだんですか」
is a standard question - and it appears in essays and magazine profiles just as
happily. Of the shapes, `がきっかけで` is the most conversational, `をきっかけに`
the everyday default, and `をきっかけとして` the one for written prose.

The trigger is allowed to be **small, private or accidental**, and often the point
of the sentence is precisely that something trivial changed everything. That
tolerance is what separates it from <code>を契機(けいき)に</code>, which reaches
for events of some weight.

The pattern is retrospective in feel: you are standing after the change and pointing
back. It is not wrong to look forward from a trigger that has just occurred -
<code>これをきっかけに頑張(がんば)ります</code> is natural - but the trigger has to
exist already. A ceremony still in the diary is the business of `にあたって`, not of
this pattern.

One restriction worth holding on to: the second half should be a **development**,
something taken up, given up or turned around. Direct physical results do not
qualify. A storm can break a window, but it does not "trigger" the breakage.

## Contrast

### ～をきっかけに vs ～を契機(けいき)に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same structure at two altitudes. `をきっかけに` takes
anything, including the trivial and the personal.
<code>を契機(けいき)に</code> is formal and prefers events with **weight**.

</div>

<pre>
弟(おとうと)にすすめられたこと<b>をきっかけに</b>、走(はし)り始(はじ)めた。
My younger brother suggesting it was what got me running.
</pre>

<pre>
創業(そうぎょう)五十周年(ごじゅっしゅうねん)<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、社名(しゃめい)を改(あらた)めた。
Marking fifty years since its founding, the company changed its name.
</pre>

Put the brother into the second frame and it turns comic; put a fiftieth anniversary
into the first and a company announcement suddenly sounds like a diary entry. The
patterns are otherwise interchangeable, and the choice is one of register.

### ～をきっかけに vs ～て以来(いらい)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `て以来` measures the **period since** an event and says the
state has held ever since. `をきっかけに` names the event as the **cause** of a
change, and says nothing about duration.

</div>

<pre>
東京(とうきょう)に来(き)<b>て以来(いらい)</b>、ずっと同(おな)じアパートに住(す)んでいる。
Ever since I came to Tokyo, I have lived in the same flat.
</pre>

<pre>
東京(とうきょう)に来(き)たの<b>をきっかけに</b>、自転車(じてんしゃ)で通勤(つうきん)するようになった。
Moving to Tokyo was what got me commuting by bicycle.
</pre>

The first sentence is about a stretch of time; the second is about a change of
habit. Note the different attachment as well: `て以来` follows the て-form directly,
while `きっかけ`, being a noun, needs <code>のを</code> in front of it.

### ～をきっかけに vs ～によって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `によって` names a **cause** that directly produced the result.
`をきっかけに` names an **opening** after which someone did something new.

</div>

<pre>
大雨(おおあめ)<b>によって</b>橋(はし)が流(なが)された。
The bridge was washed away by the heavy rain.
</pre>

<pre>
大雨(おおあめ)の被害(ひがい)<b>をきっかけに</b>、市(し)は避難(ひなん)計画(けいかく)を作(つく)り直(なお)した。
The damage from the heavy rain prompted the city to redraw its evacuation plan.
</pre>

Rain destroys a bridge by itself; ❌<code>大雨(おおあめ)をきっかけに橋(はし)が流(なが)された</code>
is wrong because nobody decided anything. Redrawing a plan, by contrast, is a
choice people made afterwards - and that is what a trigger triggers.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 雪(ゆき)<b>をきっかけに</b>、電車(でんしゃ)が止(と)まった。 → ✅ 雪(ゆき)<b>のため</b>、電車(でんしゃ)が止(と)まった。
  Trains stopping is a direct effect of snow, not a new development someone
  embarked on. Plain cause takes で, のため or によって.

- ❌ 結婚(けっこん)する<b>をきっかけに</b>、料理(りょうり)を習(なら)い始(はじ)めた。 → ✅ 結婚(けっこん)<b>をきっかけに</b>、料理(りょうり)を習(なら)い始(はじ)めた。
  `きっかけ` is a noun and takes `を`. Either use the bare event noun, or nominalise
  the clause: <code>結婚(けっこん)したのをきっかけに</code>.

- ❌ 先生(せんせい)の言葉(ことば)<b>がきっかけに</b>、考(かんが)えが変(か)わった。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)の言葉(ことば)<b>がきっかけで</b>、考(かんが)えが変(か)わった。
  With `が` the noun is the subject of a small clause, so the linking form is で -
  or <code>がきっかけとなって</code>. Mixing the two frames is the commonest slip
  here.

- ❌ 事故(じこ)<b>をきっかけに</b>、足(あし)をけがした。 → ✅ 事故(じこ)<b>で</b>足(あし)をけがした。
  The injury *is* the accident, not something that followed from it. A trigger and
  its outcome have to be two separate events.

## Practice

Retirement is the event that opened the way, and moving to the countryside is the

would sound like a newspaper profile.

</details>

Tiles falling is what the typhoon did, directly and physically. There is no later

</details>

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A photograph is exactly the kind of small accidental trigger this pattern was made

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 病気(びょうき)<b>をきっかけに</b>、たばこをやめた。

(b) measures the time since falling ill and reports the state that has held
throughout it. Nothing is said about deciding; the sentence simply covers the
period from then until now.

</details>

# ～を契機に / ～を機に

**Meaning** · taking X as the turning point; in the wake of X - formal, and used of events with some weight behind them

<code>契機(けいき)</code> is a heavy Sino-Japanese noun meaning "the moment a thing
turns". The pattern points at an event and says that things went a different way
from there: a merger, a disaster, an anniversary, a court ruling. Structurally it is
identical to `をきっかけに`; the difference is altitude. This one belongs to
newspapers, company statements and history writing.

## Formation

<code>契機(けいき)</code> and <code>機(き)</code> are nouns, so a verb clause is
nominalised with `の` first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event noun | Noun + を契機(けいき)に | 上場(じょうじょう) → 上場(じょうじょう)を契機(けいき)に |
| Event noun (emphatic) | Noun + を契機(けいき)にして | 合併(がっぺい) → 合併(がっぺい)を契機(けいき)にして |
| Event noun (written) | Noun + を契機(けいき)として | 進出(しんしゅつ) → 進出(しんしゅつ)を契機(けいき)として |
| Shorter, lighter variant | Noun + を機(き)に | 退職(たいしょく) → 退職(たいしょく)を機(き)に |
| Verb clause | plain past + のを契機(けいき)に | 公開(こうかい)された → 公開(こうかい)されたのを契機(けいき)に |
| As the subject | Noun + が契機(けいき)となって | 報道(ほうどう) → 報道(ほうどう)が契機(けいき)となって |
| ❌ Bare verb | ❌辞(や)めるを契機(けいき)に | ✅辞(や)めたのを契機(けいき)に |
| ❌ Wrong particle | ❌報道(ほうどう)が契機(けいき)に | ✅報道(ほうどう)が契機(けいき)となって |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>を機(き)に</code> is the same idea in four fewer syllables. It is
lighter on the page and turns up constantly in press releases and in personal
announcements of the <code>定年(ていねん)を機(き)に</code> kind.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
地震(じしん)<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、防災(ぼうさい)計画(けいかく)が見直(みなお)された。
The earthquake was the turning point at which the disaster plan was reviewed.
</pre>

<pre>
退職(たいしょく)<b>を機(き)に</b>、生(う)まれた町(まち)へ帰(かえ)ることにした。
On retiring, I decided to move back to the town where I was born.
</pre>

<pre>
大会(たいかい)での優勝(ゆうしょう)<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、クラブの入会者(にゅうかいしゃ)が急(きゅう)に増(ふ)えた。
Winning the tournament was the point from which club membership shot up.
</pre>

<pre>
出産(しゅっさん)<b>を機(き)に</b>、働(はたら)き方(かた)を変(か)えた人(ひと)は少(すく)なくない。
Plenty of people change the way they work when a child is born.
</pre>

<pre>
海外(かいがい)進出(しんしゅつ)<b>を契機(けいき)として</b>、社内(しゃない)の公用語(こうようご)が英語(えいご)に改(あらた)められた。
With the move overseas as the turning point, the company language was changed to English.
</pre>

<pre>
工場(こうじょう)の事故(じこ)<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、安全(あんぜん)基準(きじゅん)が全面的(ぜんめんてき)に見直(みなお)された。
The factory accident was what led to a wholesale review of the safety standards.
</pre>

<pre>
創立(そうりつ)百周年(ひゃくしゅうねん)<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、大学(だいがく)は図書館(としょかん)を建(た)て直(なお)す計画(けいかく)を発表(はっぴょう)した。
Marking a hundred years since its founding, the university announced plans to rebuild the library.
</pre>

<pre>
一冊(いっさつ)の報告書(ほうこくしょ)が公開(こうかい)されたの<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、長(なが)く放置(ほうち)されてきた問題(もんだい)に光(ひかり)が当(あ)たり始(はじ)めた。
The release of a single report was the turning point after which a long-neglected problem began to get attention.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Written and formal. <code>を契機(けいき)に</code> is at home in editorials,
annual reports, museum captions and academic prose; said in casual conversation it
sounds as though you are quoting a document. <code>を機(き)に</code> is a step
lighter and does appear in careful speech and in the announcements people make about
their own lives, but it is still a long way from `をきっかけに`.

The syllabus gloss "as a good opportunity" is worth qualifying. The pattern does not
require a welcome event: <code>震災(しんさい)を契機(けいき)に</code> and
<code>事故(じこ)を契機(けいき)に</code> are entirely normal, and much of its use
is about turns for the worse forcing a change. What is true is that when the event
*is* favourable, the sentence reads as seizing it -
<code>受賞(じゅしょう)を機(き)に</code> has exactly that flavour.

What the pattern will not take is the trivial. A friend's offhand joke, a drama you
happened to watch, a chance meeting in a lift: these are `きっかけ` material, and
<code>契機(けいき)</code> over them is comically grand. The event should be one an
outsider would also count as significant.

Note finally that the turning point is presented as **historical**. The sentence
looks back and traces a line, which is why the main clause is so often in the past
and so often passive: 見直(みなお)された, 改(あらた)められた,
進(すす)んだ.

## Contrast

### ～を契機(けいき)に vs ～をきっかけに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one structure, two registers. `をきっかけに` is neutral and
takes small private triggers; <code>を契機(けいき)に</code> is formal and wants
an event of public weight.

</div>

<pre>
同僚(どうりょう)にすすめられたの<b>をきっかけに</b>、朝(あさ)走(はし)るようになった。
A colleague suggesting it was what got me running in the mornings.
</pre>

<pre>
法改正(ほうかいせい)<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、各社(かくしゃ)が表示(ひょうじ)の方法(ほうほう)を改(あらた)めた。
The change in the law was the turning point at which firms revised their labelling.
</pre>

Swap the two and both sentences misfire: a colleague's suggestion is not a national
turning point, and a legal reform reported with `きっかけ` sounds like gossip. In
the middle - a marriage, a move, a company anniversary - either is possible, and the
register of the surrounding text decides.

### ～を契機(けいき)に vs ～を機(き)に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different weight.
<code>を機(き)に</code> is the shorter, lighter one and often reports a choice
somebody made; <code>を契機(けいき)に</code> is heavier and comfortable describing
an impersonal historical turn.

</div>

<pre>
結婚(けっこん)<b>を機(き)に</b>、通勤(つうきん)に便利(べんり)な町(まち)へ移(うつ)った。
When we married, we moved somewhere handier for commuting.
</pre>

<pre>
鉄道(てつどう)の開通(かいつう)<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、この地域(ちいき)は大(おお)きく発展(はってん)した。
The opening of the railway was the turning point from which this area developed.
</pre>

The first is a decision within one family; the second is a change nobody in
particular decided. Either form would be understood in either sentence, so treat
this as a leaning rather than a rule - but the leaning is consistent enough to be
worth following.

### ～を契機(けいき)に vs ～にあたって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を契機(けいき)に</code> looks **back** at an event and
traces what changed. `にあたって` stands **at** an event and reports what is done
there and then.

</div>

<pre>
移転(いてん)<b>にあたって</b>、取引先(とりひきさき)に案内状(あんないじょう)をお送(おく)りしました。
On relocating, we sent a notice to our business partners.
</pre>

<pre>
移転(いてん)<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、紙(かみ)の書類(しょるい)をほとんど廃止(はいし)した。
The relocation was the point at which we did away with paper documents almost entirely.
</pre>

The notice is part of relocating; scrapping the paperwork is a change the move set
off. Ask whether the main clause is **business belonging to the occasion** or
**a lasting consequence of it**.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 友(とも)だちの冗談(じょうだん)<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、みんなが笑(わら)った。 → ✅ 友(とも)だちの冗談(じょうだん)<b>で</b>、みんなが笑(わら)った。
  A joke is not a turning point. Small triggers take `をきっかけに`, and immediate
  reactions take plain で.

- ❌ 会社(かいしゃ)を辞(や)める<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、資格(しかく)を取(と)った。 → ✅ 会社(かいしゃ)を辞(や)めたの<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、資格(しかく)を取(と)った。
  <code>契機(けいき)</code> is a noun. A clause has to be nominalised with
  <code>のを</code>, and the past form is what you want, since the event came first.

- ❌ 報道(ほうどう)<b>が契機(けいき)に</b>、調査(ちょうさ)が始(はじ)まった。 → ✅ 報道(ほうどう)<b>が契機(けいき)となって</b>、調査(ちょうさ)が始(はじ)まった。
  With `を` the pattern ends in `に`; with `が` it needs
  <code>となって</code>. The two frames cannot be half-mixed.

- ❌ 大雪(おおゆき)<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、道路(どうろ)が閉鎖(へいさ)された。 → ✅ 大雪(おおゆき)<b>のため</b>、道路(どうろ)が閉鎖(へいさ)された。
  Closing a road is the immediate effect of the snow, not a new direction taken
  afterwards. A turning point needs something to have turned.

## Practice

**1.** 社長(しゃちょう)の入院(にゅういん)____、会社(かいしゃ)は健康(けんこう)管理(かんり)の制度(せいど)を整(ととの)えた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

社長(しゃちょう)の入院(にゅういん)<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>（or を機(き)に）

A hospital admission that leads an organisation to rebuild its health policy is a
genuine turning point, and the sentence is written in report register. The lighter
<code>をきっかけに</code> would also be understood, just less official.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 映画(えいが)を見(み)たの（をきっかけに・を契機(けいき)に）、山登(やまのぼ)りを始(はじ)めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

映画(えいが)を見(み)たの<b>をきっかけに</b>

Watching a film is a private, small-scale trigger and the sentence is a personal
anecdote. <code>契機(けいき)</code> over it would sound as though the film had
altered the course of the nation.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The pandemic was the turning point at which many companies introduced working from home."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

感染症(かんせんしょう)の流行(りゅうこう)<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、多(おお)くの企業(きぎょう)が在宅勤務(ざいたくきんむ)を導入(どうにゅう)した。

A society-wide event and a society-wide response: this is exactly the altitude the
pattern is written for, and the past-tense main clause traces the line backwards
from now.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 転勤(てんきん)を契機(けいき)で、車(くるま)を買(か)い替(か)えた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 転勤(てんきん)<b>を契機(けいき)に</b>、車(くるま)を買(か)い替(か)えた。

The frame is <code>を…に</code>, not <code>を…で</code>. If you want `で`, drop the
pattern altogether and say <code>転勤(てんきん)で車(くるま)を買(か)い替(か)えた</code>,
which states a plain reason instead of a turning point.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 受賞(じゅしょう)<b>を機(き)に</b>、作家(さっか)は新(あたら)しい連載(れんさい)を始(はじ)めた。
(b) 受賞(じゅしょう)<b>にあたって</b>、作家(さっか)は感謝(かんしゃ)の言葉(ことば)を述(の)べた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) treats the prize as an opening the writer took: the serial is a consequence, and
the sentence looks back from after the change.

(b) stands at the ceremony itself. The words of thanks are part of the occasion, not
something the prize set in motion, and only `にあたって` will carry that.

</details>

# のもとで / のもとに

**Meaning** · under - under someone's supervision, or under a stated condition or principle

`のもとで` says that whatever follows happens **within somebody's reach or under some
governing condition**: studying under a professor, growing crops under strict
control, working to a fixed budget. The "under" is figurative, never physical. Write
it in kana or with the kanji <code>下(もと)</code>, which is read もと here and not
した.

## Formation

The word in front is a noun - a person, an authority, or a named condition. A clause
may modify that noun in the usual way.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Person, authority | Noun + のもとで | 教授(きょうじゅ) → 教授(きょうじゅ)のもとで |
| Condition, environment | Noun + のもとで | 管理(かんり) → 厳(きび)しい管理(かんり)のもとで |
| Principle, agreement | Noun + のもとに | 条件(じょうけん) → その条件(じょうけん)のもとに |
| Modified by a clause | Clause + Noun + のもとで | 信頼(しんらい)できる → 信頼(しんらい)できる上司(じょうし)のもとで |
| Kanji spelling | Noun + の下(もと)で | 法(ほう) → 法(ほう)の下(もと)に |
| Moving to someone's side | Noun + のもとに + verb of arrival | 親(おや) → 親(おや)のもとに帰(かえ)る |
| ❌ Physical "under" | ❌机(つくえ)のもとに | ✅机(つくえ)の下(した)に |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>のもとで</code> is where an activity takes place under some
influence; <code>のもとに</code> leans towards a principle or an agreement the action
is carried out on. In many sentences either is heard, and the difference is slight.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
田中(たなか)先生(せんせい)<b>のもとで</b>、三年間(さんねんかん)学(まな)びました。
I studied under Professor Tanaka for three years.
</pre>

<pre>
厳(きび)しい管理(かんり)<b>のもとで</b>、この野菜(やさい)は育(そだ)てられている。
These vegetables are grown under strict controls.
</pre>

<pre>
新(あたら)しい監督(かんとく)<b>のもとで</b>、チームは生(う)まれ変(か)わった。
Under the new manager, the team has been reborn.
</pre>

<pre>
法(ほう)<b>の下(もと)に</b>、すべての人(ひと)は平等(びょうどう)である。
All people are equal under the law.
</pre>

<pre>
祖父母(そふぼ)<b>のもとで</b>育(そだ)った私(わたし)は、畑(はたけ)仕事(しごと)が少(すこ)しも苦(く)にならない。
Having grown up with my grandparents, I do not find work in the fields the least bit hard.
</pre>

<pre>
名前(なまえ)は公表(こうひょう)しないという約束(やくそく)<b>のもとに</b>、調査(ちょうさ)が行(おこな)われた。
The survey was carried out on the undertaking that no names would be made public.
</pre>

<pre>
限(かぎ)られた予算(よさん)<b>のもとで</b>、職員(しょくいん)たちは工夫(くふう)を重(かさ)ねてきた。
Working to a limited budget, the staff have found one workaround after another.
</pre>

<pre>
世界的(せかいてき)に知(し)られた指揮者(しきしゃ)<b>のもとで</b>演奏(えんそう)できたことは、一生(いっしょう)の思(おも)い出(で)になりました。
Getting to play under a conductor of world renown became a memory for life.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Written and careful. It fills company profiles, research summaries, programme notes
and the biographical paragraphs of newspapers. It is not exotic in speech -
<code>先生(せんせい)のもとで学(まな)ぶ</code> is said out loud without
embarrassment - but it carries a slightly literary weight, and friends chatting
would more likely say <code>先生(せんせい)に習(なら)った</code>.

The two endings are worth separating even though they overlap.
<code>のもとで</code> gives the **place under an influence** where something goes on,
so it takes people, institutions and environments. <code>のもとに</code> leans
towards a **principle, promise or premise** the action rests on, which is why the
fixed expressions - <code>法(ほう)の下(もと)に</code>,
<code>～という条件(じょうけん)のもとに</code>,
<code>一(ひと)つの目標(もくひょう)のもとに</code> - almost all take `に`. With a
condition noun both are heard; with a supervisor, `で` is the safer default.

<code>のもとに</code> has a second, concrete life as a **destination**: the place a
person is, thought of as their side.
<code>息子(むすこ)のもとに荷物(にもつ)を送(おく)った</code> means the parcel went
to where the son is. This use is common in narrative and in news, and it is not the
"under" meaning at all.

<div class="note">

**Note** - the kanji is <code>下(もと)</code>, and the reading is the whole point
of the trap: <code>法(ほう)の下(もと)に</code> is ほうのもとに, never
ほうのしたに. When 下(した) means physically underneath, it is した and takes a real
object: <code>ベッドの下(した)</code>.

</div>

## Contrast

### のもとで vs のもとに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `で` marks the **setting an activity goes on in**; `に` marks the
**principle it is done on**. Where a condition is named, both occur.

</div>

<pre>
優(やさ)しい上司(じょうし)<b>のもとで</b>、安心(あんしん)して働(はたら)くことができた。
Under a kind boss, I was able to work without anxiety.
</pre>

<pre>
全員(ぜんいん)が参加(さんか)するという前提(ぜんてい)<b>のもとに</b>、計画(けいかく)を立(た)てた。
We drew up the plan on the premise that everyone would take part.
</pre>

A boss is somebody you work **beneath**; a premise is something you build **on**.
Note also that only the `に` form can mean "to where someone is" -
<code>恩師(おんし)のもとに手紙(てがみ)を送(おく)った</code>.

### のもとで vs において

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `において` gives a **neutral setting**: a place, a field, an era.
`のもとで` adds that the setting **governs you** - somebody is in charge, or a
condition constrains you.

</div>

<pre>
この研究(けんきゅう)は京都(きょうと)の大学(だいがく)<b>において</b>行(おこな)われた。
This research was carried out at a university in Kyoto.
</pre>

<pre>
この研究(けんきゅう)は厳(きび)しい倫理(りんり)規定(きてい)<b>のもとで</b>行(おこな)われた。
This research was carried out under strict ethical rules.
</pre>

Both sentences say where the work happened, in a sense. Only the second says
something had authority over it. Swap them and you get either a rule presented as a
venue or a university presented as a constraint, and both misread.

### のもとで vs の中(なか)で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>の中(なか)で</code> is **inside** something - a space, a
crowd, a situation you happen to be in. `のもとで` is **beneath** something that has
authority over you.

</div>

<pre>
<b>雨(あめ)の中(なか)で</b>一時間(いちじかん)待(ま)たされた。
I was kept waiting for an hour in the rain.
</pre>

<pre>
新(あたら)しい校長(こうちょう)<b>のもとで</b>、校則(こうそく)が大(おお)きく変(か)わった。
Under the new head teacher, the school rules changed considerably.
</pre>

Rain does not govern you, it surrounds you, so ❌<code>雨(あめ)のもとで</code> is
wrong. A head teacher is exactly the sort of authority `のもとで` names. With a
difficult situation - <code>厳(きび)しい状況(じょうきょう)</code> - both appear,
and the choice turns on whether the situation is felt as an enclosure or as a set of
constraints.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 机(つくえ)<b>のもとに</b>猫(ねこ)が寝(ね)ている。 → ✅ 机(つくえ)の<b>下(した)に</b>猫(ねこ)が寝(ね)ている。
  Physical "underneath" is <code>下(した)</code>. `のもとで` is only ever
  figurative: supervision, conditions, principles.

- ❌ 先生(せんせい)<b>にもとで</b>研究(けんきゅう)している。 → ✅ 先生(せんせい)<b>のもとで</b>研究(けんきゅう)している。
  The connecting particle is `の`. <code>もと</code> is a noun and needs a noun
  modifier in front of it.

- ❌ 強(つよ)い風(かぜ)<b>のもとで</b>作業(さぎょう)を続(つづ)けた。 → ✅ 強(つよ)い風(かぜ)の<b>中(なか)で</b>作業(さぎょう)を続(つづ)けた。
  Weather is something you are in, not something you are under. Governing conditions
  - rules, budgets, supervision - are what this pattern takes.

- ❌ 法(ほう)の下(した)に、すべての人(ひと)は平等(びょうどう)だ。 → ✅ 法(ほう)の<b>下(もと)</b>に、すべての人(ひと)は平等(びょうどう)だ。
  Same kanji, different reading. In this expression <code>下(もと)</code> is もと;
  reading it した turns a constitutional principle into a description of the floor.

## Practice

**1.** 有名(ゆうめい)な職人(しょくにん)____、彼(かれ)は十年間(じゅうねんかん)修業(しゅぎょう)した。

有名(ゆうめい)な職人(しょくにん)<b>のもとで</b>

A named master craftsman is exactly the kind of authority someone trains beneath,
and the training is an activity going on in that setting, so the `で` form is the
natural one.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 厳(きび)しい寒(さむ)さ（のもとで・の中(なか)で）、工事(こうじ)が続(つづ)けられた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

厳(きび)しい寒(さむ)さ<b>の中(なか)で</b>

Cold is a condition you are surrounded by rather than an authority you answer to.
Had the sentence named a rule or a budget instead, `のもとで` would be right.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The experiment was conducted under conditions where the temperature never changed."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

温度(おんど)が変(か)わらない条件(じょうけん)<b>のもとで</b>、実験(じっけん)が行(おこな)われた。

A stated condition governing the work is the classic use, and a clause may modify
<code>条件(じょうけん)</code> freely. <code>のもとに</code> would also be accepted
here, since a condition sits on the border between the two forms.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この会社(かいしゃ)は社長(しゃちょう)にもとで大(おお)きくなった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この会社(かいしゃ)は社長(しゃちょう)<b>のもとで</b>大(おお)きくなった。

<code>もと</code> is a noun, so the particle joining it to

verbs like <code>習(なら)う</code>, which really do take `に`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 息子(むすこ)<b>のもとで</b>暮(く)らしている。
(b) 息子(むすこ)<b>のもとに</b>荷物(にもつ)を送(おく)った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the "under" meaning: the speaker lives in the son's household, within his
care or authority.

(b) is not "under" at all. Here <code>のもとに</code> names a destination - the place
where the son is - and the sentence simply says the parcel was sent to him.

</details>

# にて

**Meaning** · in; at; by means of - the formal, written counterpart of で

`にて` is `で` in a stiff collar. It marks the **place** something is held, the
**medium** through which it is done, and, in a couple of set phrases, the point at
which something ends. It is the particle of invitations, notices, ceremonies and
business correspondence; historically it is the ancestor of `で`, which is why the
two overlap so exactly. Nothing forces you to use it - every `にて` could be `で` -
but choosing it marks the whole sentence as formal.

## Formation

`にて` attaches to nouns only, and it never changes shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Place | Noun + にて | 市民(しみん)ホール → 市民(しみん)ホールにて |
| Means, medium | Noun + にて | メール → メールにて |
| Method of application | Noun + にて | 郵送(ゆうそう) → 郵送(ゆうそう)にて |
| Fixed closing phrase | これ + にて | - → これにて終了(しゅうりょう) |
| Ceremonial farewell | これ + にて | - → これにて失礼(しつれい)いたします |
| ❌ Verb or adjective | ❌忙(いそが)しいにて | ✅忙(いそが)しいので |
| ❌ Range of comparison | ❌日本(にほん)にて一番(いちばん)高(たか)い山(やま) | ✅日本(にほん)で一番(いちばん)高(たか)い山(やま) |

<div class="note">

**Note** - `にて` is a **particle**, so it takes no conjugation and no `の`. Where
you would say <code>東京(とうきょう)で</code>, you may say
<code>東京(とうきょう)にて</code>; where you would say
<code>東京(とうきょう)の…</code>, `にて` has no place.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
受付(うけつけ)は一階(いっかい)<b>にて</b>行(おこな)っております。
Reception is on the first floor.
</pre>

<pre>
詳(くわ)しくはメール<b>にて</b>ご連絡(れんらく)いたします。
We will be in touch by email with the details.
</pre>

<pre>
会場(かいじょう)は市民(しみん)ホール<b>にて</b>、開演(かいえん)は六時(ろくじ)でございます。
The venue is the civic hall, and the performance begins at six.
</pre>

<pre>
お申(もう)し込(こ)みは窓口(まどぐち)または郵送(ゆうそう)<b>にて</b>受(う)け付(つ)けます。
Applications are accepted at the counter or by post.
</pre>

<pre>
本日(ほんじつ)の営業(えいぎょう)はこれ<b>にて</b>終了(しゅうりょう)とさせていただきます。
That concludes our business for today.
</pre>

<pre>
結果(けっか)は後日(ごじつ)、書面(しょめん)<b>にて</b>お知(し)らせいたします。
You will be informed of the result in writing at a later date.
</pre>

<pre>
来週(らいしゅう)の説明会(せつめいかい)は本社(ほんしゃ)三階(さんがい)の大会議室(だいかいぎしつ)<b>にて</b>開催(かいさい)いたします。
Next week's briefing will be held in the large meeting room on the third floor of head office.
</pre>

<pre>
ご不明(ふめい)な点(てん)がございましたら、お電話(でんわ)<b>にて</b>お問(と)い合(あ)わせくださいますようお願(ねが)い申(もう)し上(あ)げます。
Should anything be unclear, we would ask you to enquire by telephone.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`にて` is a register marker and almost nothing else. It belongs to printed notices,
wedding invitations, itineraries, station and shop announcements, and the polite
half of business email. Staff use it out loud to customers; a shop assistant saying
<code>一階(いっかい)にて承(うけたまわ)ります</code> is unremarkable. Between
friends it is not so much wrong as theatrical, and a learner who says
<code>カフェにて待(ま)ってるね</code> will get a laugh.

Because it is a formality signal, it needs the rest of the sentence to match. `にて`
with a plain-form ending clashes badly: if you have reached for `にて`, the verb
should be です・ます at least, and more often the humble forms いたします,
申(もう)し上(あ)げます, させていただきます.

The one shape everyone meets first is <code>これにて</code>, which closes things:
<code>これにて終了(しゅうりょう)</code> at the end of a programme,
<code>これにて失礼(しつれい)いたします</code> when taking your leave. These are
frozen expressions, and they are worth learning as units.

A last historical note. The very formal use of `にて` for a cause - the sort of thing
you meet in old documents and stiff notices,
<code>都合(つごう)にて欠席(けっせき)</code> - is genuinely archaic and reads as
period flavour today. Recognise it; do not build sentences on it.

## Contrast

### にて vs で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, two registers. `で` is always available and always
safe. `にて` is `で` dressed for an occasion, and it obliges everything around it to
dress up too.

</div>

<pre>
明日(あした)の打(う)ち合(あ)わせ、駅前(えきまえ)のカフェ<b>で</b>いい?
Shall we make it the cafe by the station for tomorrow's chat?
</pre>

<pre>
明日(あす)の打(う)ち合(あ)わせは駅前(えきまえ)の支店(してん)<b>にて</b>行(おこな)います。
Tomorrow's meeting will be held at the branch office by the station.
</pre>

The first is speech between colleagues; the second is a line in a calendar
invitation. Note that `で` covers jobs `にて` does not touch: the range of a
superlative (<code>クラスで一番(いちばん)</code>), a cause
(<code>風邪(かぜ)で休(やす)む</code>) and a material
(<code>木(き)で作(つく)る</code>) all stay with `で` in modern Japanese.

### にて vs において

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are formal. `において` handles **abstract domains** as well
as places - fields, eras, situations. `にて` stays **concrete**: a venue, a medium, a
counter.

</div>

<pre>
医療(いりょう)の分野(ぶんや)<b>において</b>、AIの利用(りよう)が広(ひろ)がっている。
In the field of medicine, the use of AI is spreading.
</pre>

<pre>
受付(うけつけ)カウンター<b>にて</b>整理券(せいりけん)をお配(くば)りします。
Numbered tickets will be handed out at the reception counter.
</pre>

Put `にて` in the first sentence and a field of study is turned into a room; put
`において` in the second and a counter is inflated into a domain. Where the noun
really is a venue, both work, and `において` is the more official of the two - it is
what a formal programme or a legal document prefers. `にて` also does something
`において` cannot: mark a **medium**. ❌<code>メールにおいてご連絡(れんらく)します</code>
is not idiomatic.

### にて vs をもって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for a means, <code>をもって</code> is heavier and more
legalistic than `にて`. For a **closing date**, <code>をもって</code> is the standard
expression and `にて` is not used.

</div>

<pre>
合否(ごうひ)は書面(しょめん)<b>にて</b>お知(し)らせします。
You will be notified of the outcome in writing.
</pre>

<pre>
当店(とうてん)は三月(さんがつ)末日(まつじつ)<b>をもって</b>閉店(へいてん)いたします。
This shop will close its doors at the end of March.
</pre>

Both sentences are formal announcements, but only <code>をもって</code> can draw the
line under a period. The one place `にて` does mark an ending is the frozen
<code>これにて</code>, which refers to the present moment rather than to a date.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ 友(とも)だちと公園(こうえん)<b>にて</b>遊(あそ)んだよ。 → ✅ 友(とも)だちと公園(こうえん)<b>で</b>遊(あそ)んだよ。
  A casual sentence with a plain ending cannot carry `にて`. The particle and the
  verb ending have to be at the same level of formality.

- ❌ 日本(にほん)<b>にて</b>一番(いちばん)人気(にんき)のある漫画(まんが)です。 → ✅ 日本(にほん)<b>で</b>一番(いちばん)人気(にんき)のある漫画(まんが)です。
  Marking the range of a comparison is a job `にて` never took over. That `で` stays
  `で` however formal the sentence.

- ❌ 事故(じこ)<b>にて</b>電車(でんしゃ)が遅(おく)れております。 → ✅ 事故(じこ)の<b>影響(えいきょう)で</b>電車(でんしゃ)が遅(おく)れております。
  Cause with `にて` survives only as archaic flavour. Modern announcements use `で`
  or <code>のため</code>.

- ❌ 会場(かいじょう)<b>にてで</b>お待(ま)ちください。 → ✅ 会場(かいじょう)<b>にて</b>お待(ま)ちください。
  `にて` already contains the `で`. Stacking the two is a slip that comes from
  thinking of `にて` as に + で rather than as a single particle.

## Practice

**1.** 詳(くわ)しい日程(にってい)は、後日(ごじつ)メール____ご案内(あんない)いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

メール<b>にて</b>

Email here is the medium through which the information is sent, which is one of the
two core jobs of this particle. The humble
<code>ご案内(あんない)いたします</code> sets exactly the register `にて` expects.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 環境(かんきょう)問題(もんだい)の分野(ぶんや)（にて・において）、日本(にほん)は多(おお)くの技術(ぎじゅつ)を持(も)っている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

分野(ぶんや)<b>において</b>

A field of activity is an abstract domain, not a venue you can stand in. `にて`
handles concrete places and media only, so `において` is the only option here.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The ceremony will be held in the main hall on the second floor."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

式(しき)は二階(にかい)の大(おお)ホール<b>にて</b>行(おこな)われます。

A named venue in an announcement is the archetypal use, and the passive
<code>行(おこな)われます</code> keeps the sentence in the impersonal register that
notices are written in.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: 会議(かいぎ)は三階(さんがい)にてやるから、早(はや)めに来(き)てね。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 会議(かいぎ)は三階(さんがい)<b>で</b>やるから、早(はや)めに来(き)てね。

The ending やるから…来(き)てね is relaxed speech to a friend, and `にて` cannot
live in that sentence. Either lower the particle to `で`, as here, or raise the whole
sentence to <code>三階(さんがい)にて行(おこな)いますので…</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 本日(ほんじつ)の受付(うけつけ)はこれ<b>にて</b>終了(しゅうりょう)いたします。
(b) 本日(ほんじつ)の受付(うけつけ)は五時(ごじ)<b>をもって</b>終了(しゅうりょう)いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) closes the desk **now**, at the moment of speaking.
<code>これにて</code> is a frozen phrase pointing at the present instant, and it is
what staff actually say as they put the sign up.

(b) names a **time** as the cut-off, and would be announced or printed in advance. It
is the more official of the two, and it is the form a written notice uses.

</details>
