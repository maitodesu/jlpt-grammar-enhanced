# かどうか

**Meaning** · whether or not - packs a yes/no question inside a bigger sentence

`かどうか` takes a question that could be answered *yes* or *no*, ties it into a
single lump, and hands that lump to a verb like <code>{f|分|わ}かる</code>,
<code>{f|知|し}らない</code> or <code>{f|調|しら}べる</code>. It is literally
"か（yes?）どう（how?）か" - is it so, or how is it? The English that maps onto it is
**whether**, and mapping it onto **if** instead is where nearly all the trouble starts.

## Formation

`か` attaches to plain forms. Nouns and な-adjectives **lose** their `だ` - the
opposite of what happens before the quotative `と`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + かどうか | {f|届|とど}く → {f|届|とど}くかどうか |
| Verb (past) | た-form + かどうか | {f|送|おく}った → {f|送|おく}ったかどうか |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + かどうか | {f|要|い}らない → {f|要|い}らないかどうか |
| い-adjective | plain form + かどうか | {f|新|あたら}しい → {f|新|あたら}しいかどうか |
| な-adjective | drop だ + かどうか | {f|安全|あんぜん}だ → {f|安全|あんぜん}かどうか |
| Noun | drop だ + かどうか | {f|本物|ほんもの}だ → {f|本物|ほんもの}かどうか |
| Both options spelled out | ～か～ないか | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}くか{f|行|い}かないか |

Typical main verbs after it: <code>{f|分|わ}かる</code>, <code>{f|知|し}らない</code>,
<code>{f|聞|き}く</code>, <code>{f|調|しら}べる</code>, <code>{f|確|たし}かめる</code>,
<code>{f|迷|まよ}う</code>, <code>{f|覚|おぼ}えていない</code>, <code>{f|決|き}める</code>.

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - nothing polite and no `だ` in front of `か`.
❌<code>{f|行|い}きますかどうか</code>, ❌<code>{f|安全|あんぜん}だかどうか</code> →
✅<code>{f|行|い}くかどうか</code>, ✅<code>{f|安全|あんぜん}かどうか</code>.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the particle that would follow the lump (`を`, `が`) is normally left out:
{f|届|とど}いたかどうか{f|確|たし}かめます. `は` does appear, and adds contrast:
{f|届|とど}いたかどうかは、まだ{f|分|わ}かりません。

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
これでいい<b>かどうか</b>、{f|見|み}てください。
Please have a look and tell me whether this is all right.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>かどうか</b>{f|分|わ}かりません。
I don't know whether it will snow tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|水|みず}が{f|安全|あんぜん}<b>かどうか</b>、{f|先|さき}に{f|調|しら}べたほうがいいよ。
You should check first whether this water is safe.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|受|う}かった<b>かどうか</b>は、{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|金曜日|きんようび}に{f|分|わ}かります。
Whether I passed or not will be clear next Friday.
</pre>

<pre>
パーティーに{f|出|で}る<b>かどうか</b>、まだ{f|迷|まよ}っているんです。
I still can't make up my mind whether to go to the party.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}が{f|本当|ほんとう}のことを{f|言|い}っている<b>かどうか</b>、{f|私|わたし}には{f|判断|はんだん}できません。
I'm in no position to judge whether he's telling the truth.
</pre>

<pre>
サイズが{f|合|あ}う<b>かどうか</b>、{f|試着|しちゃく}してから{f|決|き}めようと{f|思|おも}います。
I'll try it on before deciding, to see whether the size works.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みが{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りに{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}った<b>かどうか</b>、{f|事務所|じむしょ}に{f|確認|かくにん}してみます。
I'll check with the office whether my application made the deadline.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`かどうか` is register-neutral. It appears unchanged in a chat with friends, in a
lecture and in a contract; as always, the politeness of the sentence lives on the
final verb. It is also one of the few N4 patterns with no casual contraction of its
own - although in relaxed speech people often drop `どうか` entirely and leave a bare
`か`: {f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}うか{f|分|わ}かんない。 That is shorter, not ruder.

The one thing `かどうか` cannot do is finish a sentence. It is a lump that some other
verb has to pick up, so <code>{f|来|く}るかどうか。</code> on its own is a fragment. If
you want to end there, you need the wondering forms instead - 
<code>{f|来|く}るかな</code>, <code>{f|来|く}るでしょうか</code>.

Two fixed expressions are worth having ready. <code>かどうかは{f|別|べつ}として</code>
("setting aside whether…") concedes a point before moving on, and
<code>かどうか{f|分|わ}かりませんが</code> is the standard hedge for offering something
you are not sure will help: お{f|役|やく}に{f|立|た}つかどうか{f|分|わ}かりませんが、こちらの{f|資料|しりょう}をどうぞ。

## Contrast

### かどうか vs か

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if the embedded question contains a **question word**
（{f|何|なに}, どこ, だれ, いつ, どう, どちら）, use bare `か`. `かどうか` is only for
questions whose answer is *yes* or *no*.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}{f|何|なに}を{f|注文|ちゅうもん}した<b>か</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えていません。
I don't remember what I ordered yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}{f|注文|ちゅうもん}した<b>かどうか</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えていません。
I don't remember whether I ordered yesterday.
</pre>

The first asks about the *contents* of the order, the second about whether the
ordering happened at all. Sticking `どうか` onto the first - 
❌<code>{f|何|なに}を{f|注文|ちゅうもん}したかどうか</code> - is ungrammatical, because
{f|何|なに} has already supplied the question and `どうか` has nothing left to ask.

Bare `か` is also available for yes/no questions, especially in speech:
{f|空|あ}いているか{f|見|み}てきます。 `かどうか` simply spells the alternative out and
is the clearer choice in writing.

### かどうか vs ～たら／～ば

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - English "if" is two unrelated words. "If" you could replace with
**whether** → `かどうか`. "If" meaning **on condition that** → `たら`／`ば`／`と`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|木村|きむら}さんが{f|参加|さんか}する<b>かどうか</b>、{f|聞|き}いてみます。
I'll ask whether Kimura is taking part.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|木村|きむら}さんが{f|参加|さんか}し<b>たら</b>、{f|席|せき}を{f|一|ひと}つ{f|増|ふ}やします。
If Kimura takes part, I'll add one more seat.
</pre>

These are not stylistic variants - they say different things. The first is about the
speaker's ignorance; the second is about a consequence. "I don't know if he's coming"
is ❌<code>{f|来|き}たら{f|分|わ}かりません</code> and
✅<code>{f|来|く}るかどうか{f|分|わ}かりません</code>. This is the most common single error
with the pattern, and it comes straight out of English.

### かどうか vs ～か～ないか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, more deliberate delivery. And when the two options
are not *yes* and *no* but two **different things**, only the spelled-out version
works.

</div>

<pre>
{f|出席|しゅっせき}する<b>かどうか</b>、{f|金曜日|きんようび}までにお{f|返事|へんじ}ください。
Please reply by Friday as to whether you'll attend.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電車|でんしゃ}で{f|行|い}く<b>かバスで{f|行|い}くか</b>、まだ{f|決|き}めていません。
I haven't decided yet whether to go by train or by bus.
</pre>

The second could not be rewritten with `かどうか`: there is no *no* to negate, just
two alternatives. Keep `かどうか` for the yes/no case and lay both options out when
they are genuinely two options.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|部屋|へや}が<b>{f|静|しず}かだかどうか</b>{f|確|たし}かめます。 → ✅ この{f|部屋|へや}が<b>{f|静|しず}かかどうか</b>{f|確|たし}かめます。
  Nouns and な-adjectives drop `だ` before `か`. It is `だ` before `と`, no `だ` before
  `か` - the two quotative-ish particles behave in opposite ways.

- ❌ {f|田中|たなか}さんが<b>{f|来|き}ますかどうか</b>{f|知|し}りません。 → ✅ {f|田中|たなか}さんが<b>{f|来|く}るかどうか</b>{f|知|し}りません。
  The embedded clause is plain. `ます` belongs to the end of the sentence, not to the
  middle of it.

- ❌ {f|荷物|にもつ}が<b>{f|届|とど}いたら</b>{f|分|わ}かりません。 → ✅ {f|荷物|にもつ}が<b>{f|届|とど}いたかどうか</b>{f|分|わ}かりません。
  "I don't know if the parcel arrived" is *whether*, not a condition. `たら` here would
  mean "once it arrives, I won't understand", which is nonsense.

- ❌ <b>どこで{f|買|か}えるかどうか</b>{f|教|おし}えてください。 → ✅ <b>どこで{f|買|か}えるか</b>{f|教|おし}えてください。
  With a question word present, `どうか` has nothing to do. Drop it.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|情報|じょうほう}が{f|正確|せいかく}____{f|確|たし}かめる{f|必要|ひつよう}があります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|情報|じょうほう}が<b>{f|正確|せいかく}かどうか</b>{f|確|たし}かめる{f|必要|ひつよう}があります。

<code>{f|正確|せいかく}</code> is a な-adjective, so its `だ` disappears before `か`.
Writing ❌<code>{f|正確|せいかく}だかどうか</code> is the single most frequent form error
here.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|誰|だれ}が{f|担当|たんとう}する（か・かどうか）{f|聞|き}いておきます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|誰|だれ}が{f|担当|たんとう}する<b>か</b>{f|聞|き}いておきます。

The clause already contains the question word {f|誰|だれ}, so the question has been
asked and `どうか` would be asking a second, different one. `かどうか` is reserved for
yes/no.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I don't know whether this key fits that door."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|鍵|かぎ}があのドアに<b>{f|合|あ}うかどうか</b>{f|分|わ}かりません。

"Whether" is the giveaway for `かどうか`. Note that a conditional
（❌<code>{f|合|あ}ったら{f|分|わ}かりません</code>）would say something quite different and
quite strange.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|息子|むすこ}が{f|朝|あさ}ごはんを{f|食|た}べましたかどうか{f|覚|おぼ}えていません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|息子|むすこ}が{f|朝|あさ}ごはんを<b>{f|食|た}べたかどうか</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えていません。

An embedded question takes the plain form, so
<code>{f|食|た}べました</code> becomes <code>{f|食|た}べた</code>. The sentence stays polite
thanks to <code>{f|覚|おぼ}えていません</code> at the end.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|注文|ちゅうもん}した{f|品物|しなもの}が{f|届|とど}いたかどうか{f|確|たし}かめます。
(b) {f|注文|ちゅうもん}した{f|品物|しなもの}が{f|届|とど}いたら{f|確|たし}かめます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) makes the arrival itself the unknown: you do not yet know whether the goods came,
and checking is how you will find out.

(b) treats the arrival as a condition that has not been met yet: once the goods do
arrive, you will check them (presumably for damage). English "if" covers both, which
is exactly why the two get swapped.

</details>
