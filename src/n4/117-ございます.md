# ございます

**Meaning** · the very polite form of ある - "there is", "we have"

`ございます` does the same work as `あります`, one register higher. It is
{f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご}: it lifts the *tone of your speech*, not the subject of
the sentence. That is why you can use it about the listener's questions and about
your own shop's stock without contradicting yourself - nobody is being raised or
lowered, only the language.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ある | ございます | {f|時間|じかん}がある → {f|時間|じかん}がございます |
| ない | ございません | {f|席|せき}がない → {f|席|せき}がございません |
| Past | ございました／ございませんでした | {f|連絡|れんらく}がございました |
| Conditional | ございましたら | ご{f|希望|きぼう}がございましたら |
| い-adjective + ございます | sound change on the く-form | ありがたい → ありがとうございます |
| Noun + です | でございます | {f|受付|うけつけ}でございます |

The い-adjective pattern is worth seeing once, because three of the commonest
greetings in the language come out of it:

| Adjective | く-form | Sound change | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| ありがたい | ありがたく | ～あく → ～おう | ありがとうございます |
| おめでたい | おめでたく | ～あく → ～おう | おめでとうございます |
| {f|早|はや}い | {f|早|はや}く | ～あく → ～おう | お{f|早|はよ}うございます |
| うれしい | うれしく | ～しく → ～しゅう | うれしゅうございます |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the plain form ござる has dropped out of modern speech; you will meet it
only in samurai films. Treat ございます as a word without a dictionary form, and
note that it shares the irregular ます-form of いらっしゃる and なさる:
❌ござります does not exist.

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is service language above all. Shops, hotels, restaurants, station
announcements, formal speeches and business correspondence run on `ございます`; a
conversation between friends does not. Saying
❌{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}にビールがございます to a flatmate is not politer, it is
theatrical.

Because it is {f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご} and not
{f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご}, `ございます` never honours anybody by itself. That has
one practical consequence worth knowing: strictly, asking about something the
listener possesses calls for the honorific おありです - 
ご{f|質問|しつもん}はおありでしょうか. In real service Japanese,
ご{f|質問|しつもん}はございませんか is what you will hear almost every time, and
it is not treated as an error. Use whichever you like; recognise both.

Three details:

- **ありがとうございます／ありがとうございました.** The present form thanks for
  something still going on or just now done; the past form closes it off. A shop
  says ありがとうございました as you leave with the bag.
- **{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございません** is the standard business apology. A
  minority of usage guides object to it on the grounds that
  {f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ない is a single adjective that should not be split, and
  prefer {f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}なく{f|存|ぞん}じます. In practice the objection
  has lost; you will hear ございません everywhere, and using it will not mark you.
- **The い-adjective form is dated.** よろしゅうございます, うれしゅうございます
  and {f|大|おお}きゅうございます are grammatical, but they belong to older, very
  formal or self-consciously refined speech. The frozen greetings survive; the
  productive pattern has largely gone.

## Contrast

### ございます vs あります

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different altitude. `あります` is polite;
`ございます` is polite and formal, and it announces that you are in a service or
ceremonial situation.

</div>

<pre>
{f|地下|ちか}に{f|駐車場|ちゅうしゃじょう}が<b>あります</b>。
There's a car park in the basement.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|地下|ちか}に{f|駐車場|ちゅうしゃじょう}が<b>ございます</b>。
There is a car park in the basement.
</pre>

Nothing about the car park has changed. The second sentence tells you the speaker
is behind a counter. Reach for `あります` in ordinary conversation, in class and
with colleagues, and switch to `ございます` when you are serving somebody or
speaking formally to a group.

### ございます vs いらっしゃいます

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ございます` is for things and facts; `いらっしゃいます` is for
people you are honouring.

</div>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}に{f|案内図|あんないず}が<b>ございます</b>。
There is a floor plan at reception.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}に{f|係|かかり}の{f|方|かた}が<b>いらっしゃいます</b>。
There is a member of staff at reception.
</pre>

The mistake goes one way: learners reach for `ございます` about a person because it
feels like the most polite word available. It is not more polite about a person - 
it is wrong, because it treats them as an object. Note that your **own** side does
take a non-honorific verb, but the word is おります, not ございます:
{f|担当|たんとう}の{f|者|もの}が{f|受付|うけつけ}におります.

### ございます vs でございます

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ございます` replaces `あります`; `でございます` replaces
`です`. Existence versus identity.

</div>

<pre>
{f|三階|さんがい}に{f|喫茶店|きっさてん}が<b>ございます</b>。
There is a coffee shop on the third floor.
</pre>

<pre>
こちらが{f|喫茶店|きっさてん}<b>でございます</b>。
This is the coffee shop.
</pre>

The `で` is the giveaway. If the English is "there is a…" or "we have a…", you
want the bare form; if it is "X is Y", you want `でございます`. Mixing them
produces ❌こちらが{f|喫茶店|きっさてん}ございます, which is simply ungrammatical.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ご{f|用件|ようけん}は{f|何|なに}か<b>ござりますか</b>。 → ✅ ご{f|用件|ようけん}は{f|何|なに}か<b>ございますか</b>。
  The `り` becomes `い` before ます - the same irregularity as いらっしゃいます,
  おっしゃいます, なさいます and くださいます.

- ❌ お{f|客|きゃく}{f|様|さま}は{f|二階|にかい}に<b>ございます</b>。 → ✅ お{f|客|きゃく}{f|様|さま}は{f|二階|にかい}に<b>いらっしゃいます</b>。
  A person is not a thing. `ございます` about a customer sounds as though they are
  stock on a shelf.

- ❌ ねえ、{f|消|け}しゴム<b>ございます</b>? → ✅ ねえ、{f|消|け}しゴム<b>ある</b>?
  A register error rather than a grammar one, and the kind that makes people
  laugh. Service Japanese aimed at a classmate is comic.

- ❌ <b>ありがとうでございます</b>。 → ✅ <b>ありがとうございます</b>。
  `ございます` follows the adjective form directly. The `で` belongs only after a
  noun.

## Practice

**1.** {f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません、{f|本日|ほんじつ}のチケットは{f|売|う}り{f|切|き}れて{f|席|せき}が____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません、{f|本日|ほんじつ}のチケットは{f|売|う}り{f|切|き}れて{f|席|せき}が<b>ございません</b>。

The negative of `ございます` is `ございません` - there is no ❌ございないです. The
whole sentence is service register, so ありません would sit a step lower than the
rest of it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|田中|たなか}{f|様|さま}は{f|会議室|かいぎしつ}に（ございます・いらっしゃいます）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|田中|たなか}{f|様|さま}は{f|会議室|かいぎしつ}に<b>いらっしゃいます</b>。

`ございます` is the polite form of `ある`, which is used for things. A person you
are honouring takes the honorific of `いる`. If {f|田中|たなか} were your own
colleague and you were speaking to a client, the answer would change again - to
おります.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, as a hotel receptionist: "There is a convenience store next door."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|隣|となり}にコンビニが<b>ございます</b>。

The subject is a shop, so the verb of existence is `ある`, raised to service
register as `ございます`. Note that no honorific prefix is needed on コンビニ - 
loanwords do not take one.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: こちらが{f|新|あたら}しいパンフレットございます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ こちらが{f|新|あたら}しいパンフレット<b>でございます</b>。

This sentence identifies one thing as another, so it needs the polite copula, not
the polite verb of existence. Without the `で` there is nothing linking the noun
to the sentence at all.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ご{f|意見|いけん}はありませんか。
(b) ご{f|意見|いけん}はございませんか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are polite and both are correct. (a) is ordinary polite Japanese - a chair
asking a familiar committee, or a teacher asking a class. (b) lifts the whole
utterance into formal register, and suits a public meeting, a shareholders'
briefing or a customer.

Neither honours the listener grammatically, since `ございます` is
{f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご}. If you want to raise the listener as well, the
honorific of `ある` is おありです: ご{f|意見|いけん}はおありでしょうか. That is the
strictly correct form, though (b) is far more often heard.

</details>
