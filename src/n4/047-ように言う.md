# ～ように言う

**Meaning** · tell someone to do something - reporting an instruction without
quoting it

<code>～ように{f|言|い}う</code> passes on the **content** of an order or request. The original
words are gone: you cannot tell from the report whether the speaker barked
{f|帰|かえ}れ or murmured
お{f|帰|かえ}りいただけますか. That flattening is the whole point of the pattern, and
it is why learners meet it most often in the passive - 
{f|言|い}われた, "I was told to".

## Formation

`ように` takes the **plain form** of the verb, positive or negative. Nothing
changes about the verb itself, which is why the four verb classes all behave the
same way here.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | dictionary form + ように + {f|言|い}う | {f|待|ま}つ → {f|待|ま}つように{f|言|い}う |
| Verb (る-verb) | dictionary form + ように + {f|言|い}う | {f|片付|かたづ}ける → {f|片付|かたづ}けるように{f|言|い}う |
| Verb (する) | dictionary form + ように + {f|言|い}う | {f|準備|じゅんび}する → {f|準備|じゅんび}するように{f|言|い}う |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | dictionary form + ように + {f|言|い}う | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|く}るように{f|言|い}う |
| Negative order | ない-form + ように + {f|言|い}う | {f|遅|おく}れる → {f|遅|おく}れないように{f|言|い}う |
| な-adjective / noun | ～にする + ように | {f|静|しず}か → {f|静|しず}かにするように{f|言|い}う |
| Asking a favour | ～てくれる／～てもらう + ように | {f|手伝|てつだ}う → {f|手伝|てつだ}ってくれるように{f|頼|たの}む |

The verb at the end is not restricted to <code>{f|言|い}う</code>:

| Verb | Sense | Example |
|---|---|---|
| {f|言|い}う | tell | {f|来|く}るように{f|言|い}う |
| {f|伝|つた}える | pass on a message | {f|来|く}るように{f|伝|つた}える |
| {f|頼|たの}む | ask a favour | {f|来|く}るように{f|頼|たの}む |
| お{f|願|ねが}いする | request politely | {f|来|く}るようにお{f|願|ねが}いする |
| {f|注意|ちゅうい}する | warn | {f|来|こ}ないように{f|注意|ちゅうい}する |
| {f|命|めい}じる | order (formal) | {f|来|く}るように{f|命|めい}じる |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the person receiving the instruction is marked with `に`, the
same as the listener of any speech act:
{f|弟|おとうと}<b>に</b>{f|片付|かたづ}けるように{f|言|い}った. In the passive that
person becomes the subject and the speaker moves into `に`:
{f|母|はは}<b>に</b>{f|言|い}われた.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}は{f|学生|がくせい}に{f|静|しず}かに<b>するように{f|言|い}った</b>。
The teacher told the students to be quiet.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|母|はは}に{f|早|はや}く<b>{f|帰|かえ}るように{f|言|い}われた</b>。
My mother told me to come home early.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|医者|いしゃ}に、お{f|酒|さけ}を<b>{f|飲|の}まないように{f|言|い}われました</b>。
The doctor told me not to drink.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}で、ここに{f|名前|なまえ}を<b>{f|書|か}くように{f|言|い}われた</b>。
At reception they told me to write my name here.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}に、{f|金曜日|きんようび}までに{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}を<b>{f|出|だ}すように{f|言|い}われている</b>。
The manager has told me to hand in the report by Friday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}に{f|部屋|へや}を<b>{f|片付|かたづ}けるように</b>{f|何度|なんど}も<b>{f|言|い}った</b>のに、まだそのままだ。
I've told my little brother to tidy his room over and over, and it's still exactly as it was.
</pre>

I asked Tanaka to let everyone know that the meeting time had changed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもたちには、{f|知|し}らない{f|人|ひと}について<b>{f|行|い}かないようにと{f|注意|ちゅうい}して</b>あります。
We've warned the children not to go off with strangers.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral. It appears in family conversation and in business
email alike, and any politeness sits on the final verb - 
{f|言|い}いました, {f|伝|つた}えていただけますか, お{f|願|ねが}いしました.

**The lost politeness.** Because `ように` reports content rather than words, the
report is silent about the tone of the original. If that matters, you say so
outside the pattern: {f|強|つよ}い{f|口調|くちょう}で{f|言|い}われた,
{f|丁寧|ていねい}に{f|頼|たの}まれた. Alternatively, quote directly with `と`.

<b>ようにと.</b> Adding `と` - {f|来|く}るように<b>と</b>{f|言|い}われた - is very common
and slightly more formal or written. It changes nothing semantically; think of it
as the quotative `と` reasserting itself.

**The passive is the everyday form.** In real speech you will hear
<code>～ように{f|言|い}われた</code> far more than the active. Reporting what you were told to
do is simply a more frequent thing to say than reporting what you told someone
else.

**Business softening.** Upward, <code>{f|言|い}う</code> is too blunt. Use
<code>ようにお{f|願|ねが}いします</code> or <code>ようにお{f|伝|つた}えください</code> - 
{f|山田|やまだ}さんに{f|折|お}り{f|返|かえ}し{f|電話|でんわ}をくださるように
お{f|伝|つた}えください is the sort of sentence this pattern was made for.

<b>Dangling ように.</b> A written instruction sometimes stops after `ように`, with the
verb left off entirely: {f|受付|うけつけ}にお{f|越|こ}しくださいますように. This is
formal and a bit old-fashioned, but you will see it on notices.

## Contrast

### ～ように{f|言|い}う vs ～と{f|言|い}う

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `と` quotes the **words**; `ように` reports the **instruction**.
If you can put quotation marks round it, use `と`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|父|ちち}は「{f|早|はや}く{f|寝|ね}ろ」<b>と{f|言|い}った</b>。
My father said, "Get to bed."
</pre>

<pre>
{f|父|ちち}は{f|早|はや}く{f|寝|ね}る<b>ように{f|言|い}った</b>。
My father told me to go to bed early.
</pre>

The first preserves the imperative and with it the temper. The second could have
been said in any tone at all. Note what happens to the verb: inside a `と` quote
you keep whatever form was actually uttered ({f|寝|ね}ろ, {f|寝|ね}なさい,
{f|寝|ね}てください); with `ように` the verb goes into the plain dictionary form no
matter what.

`と` can also report a request in indirect speech - 
{f|早|はや}く{f|寝|ね}るように{f|言|い}った and
「{f|早|はや}く{f|寝|ね}なさい」と{f|言|い}った are both natural - but
❌「{f|早|はや}く{f|寝|ね}なさい」ように{f|言|い}った mixes the two and is simply
ungrammatical.

### ～ように{f|頼|たの}む vs ～てくださいと{f|頼|たの}む

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same message, different packaging.
`～てくださいと` reproduces the polite words that were used;
`～ように` summarises what was asked for.

</div>

<pre>
{f|友|とも}だちに{f|空港|くうこう}まで{f|送|おく}っ<b>てくれるように{f|頼|たの}んだ</b>。
I asked my friend to drive me to the airport.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|友|とも}だちに「{f|空港|くうこう}まで{f|送|おく}ってください」<b>と{f|頼|たの}んだ</b>。
I asked my friend, "Please take me to the airport."
</pre>

When the request is a favour rather than an order, the `ように` version usually
keeps a benefactive verb - `～てくれるように`, `～てもらえるように` - because
without it the sentence sounds like an instruction rather than a request. Compare
bare {f|送|おく}るように{f|頼|たの}んだ, which reads oddly high-handed for
something a friend is doing for you.

### ～ように{f|言|い}う vs ～ようにする・～ようになる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same `ように`, three unrelated jobs. <code>{f|言|い}う</code> reports an
instruction, `する` describes an effort you make, `なる` describes a change that
happened.

</div>

<pre>
{f|医者|いしゃ}に{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|歩|ある}く<b>ように{f|言|い}われた</b>。
The doctor told me to walk every day.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|歩|ある}く<b>ようにしている</b>。
I make a point of walking every day.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|歩|ある}く<b>ようになった</b>。
I've got into the habit of walking every day.
</pre>

Learners meet all three within a few weeks of each other and fold them together.
Keep the final verb in view: it, not `ように`, is carrying the meaning.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|母|はは}は「{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}りなさい」ように{f|言|い}った。 → ✅ {f|母|はは}は{f|早|はや}く<b>{f|帰|かえ}るように{f|言|い}った</b>。
  `ように` never follows a quotation. Either quote with `と` or summarise with
  `ように` - not both.

- ❌ {f|来|き}てように{f|言|い}う → ✅ <b>{f|来|く}るように{f|言|い}う</b>
  `ように` takes the plain form, not the て-form.

- ❌ {f|遅|おく}れないで<b>ように</b>{f|言|い}う → ✅ {f|遅|おく}れ<b>ないように{f|言|い}う</b>
  The negative slot wants the plain ない-form. `～ないで` belongs with
  <code>～ないでください</code>, a different pattern.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}は{f|私|わたし}<b>を</b>{f|発表|はっぴょう}するように{f|言|い}った。 → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}は{f|私|わたし}<b>に</b>…
  The person told is the addressee of a speech act, so `に`.

- ❌ {f|静|しず}か<b>ように</b>{f|言|い}った → ✅ {f|静|しず}か<b>にするように</b>{f|言|い}った
  `ように` needs a verb in front of it. A な-adjective has to be turned into one
  with `～にする`.

## Practice

**1.** {f|受付|うけつけ}の{f|人|ひと}に、ここで{f|待|ま}____ように{f|言|い}われた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ここで{f|待|ま}<b>つ</b>ように{f|言|い}われた。

`ように` takes the plain dictionary form, so <code>{f|待|ま}つ</code> goes in
unchanged. The receptionist is the source of the instruction and takes `に` in the
passive.

</details>

**2.** {f|田中|たなか}さんは「{f|明日|あした}は{f|早|はや}く{f|来|き}てください」（と・ように）{f|言|い}いました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

「{f|明日|あした}は{f|早|はや}く{f|来|き}てください」<b>と</b>{f|言|い}いました。

Once the words are inside quotation marks, only the quotative `と` can follow. The
`ように` version would drop the quotes and the politeness:
{f|明日|あした}は{f|早|はや}く{f|来|く}るように{f|言|い}いました.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The doctor told me not to eat anything before the
examination."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|医者|いしゃ}に、{f|検査|けんさ}の{f|前|まえ}は{f|何|なに}も<b>{f|食|た}べないように{f|言|い}われた</b>。

A negative instruction goes in as the plain ない-form,
<code>{f|食|た}べない</code>, directly before `ように`. The passive
{f|言|い}われた is the natural way to report an instruction you received.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部長|ぶちょう}に、この{f|書類|しょるい}をコピーしてように{f|頼|たの}まれました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}に、この{f|書類|しょるい}を<b>コピーするように</b>{f|頼|たの}まれました。

The て-form cannot sit in front of `ように`; the slot takes the plain form, here
<code>コピーする</code>. If you wanted to keep the て-form you would have to quote:
「コピーしてください」と{f|頼|たの}まれました.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|先生|せんせい}は{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|日記|にっき}を{f|書|か}くように{f|言|い}った。
(b) {f|先生|せんせい}は「{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|日記|にっき}を{f|書|か}きなさい」と{f|言|い}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports only the substance of the instruction. It tells you what the students
now have to do and says nothing about how the teacher put it - she may have asked
politely or issued an order.

(b) reproduces the teacher's actual words, and those words carry information: the
`なさい` marks her as speaking downward with authority, calmly rather than angrily.
Direct quotation is how Japanese keeps that texture, since `ように` deliberately
throws it away.

</details>
