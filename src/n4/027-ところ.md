# ～ところ

**Meaning** · about to do; on the point of doing

With a verb in the **dictionary form**, `ところ` names the stage just *before* the
action: you have not started, and you are about to. The same construction in the
past - <code>～ところだった</code> - becomes one of the most useful sentences in
the language: "I very nearly did."

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | **dictionary form** + ところだ | {f|出|で}る → {f|出|で}るところだ |
| Polite | dictionary form + ところです | {f|帰|かえ}るところです |
| Past - "nearly" | dictionary form + ところだった | {f|遅|おく}れるところだった |
| Past polite | dictionary form + ところでした | {f|忘|わす}れるところでした |
| More imminent still | ようとしている + ところ | {f|出|で}かけようとしているところ |
| Interrupted | ところ + に／へ | {f|寝|ね}るところに{f|電話|でんわ}が{f|来|き}た |
| Typical companions | これから、{f|今|いま}から、ちょうど、もう{f|少|すこ}しで | ちょうど{f|始|はじ}めるところ |
| Casual contraction | とこ | {f|今|いま}{f|出|で}るとこ |
| ❌ ます-form | ❌{f|行|い}きますところ | ✅{f|行|い}くところ |
| ❌ Distant future | ❌{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|行|い}くところです | ✅{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|行|い}く{f|予定|よてい}です |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the dictionary form in front of `ところ` means the action has
**not begun**. Not "I am doing it", not "I have done it" - the foot is still in
the air.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - in this sense `ところ` normally ends the sentence with `だ`／`です`, or
carries on with `に`／`へ` to introduce an interruption. It is not a free
subordinate clause: to say "the phone rang as I was about to go to bed" you need
<code>{f|寝|ね}るところに</code>, not a bare <code>{f|寝|ね}るところ</code> followed by
a comma.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|今|いま}から{f|出|で}かける<b>ところ</b>です。
I'm just heading out now.
</pre>

<pre>
これから{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|始|はじ}まる<b>ところ</b>です。
The meeting is about to start.
</pre>

<pre>
あぶない! もう{f|少|すこ}しで{f|転|ころ}ぶ<b>ところ</b>だった。
Careful! I nearly went over.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|風呂|ふろ}に{f|入|はい}る<b>ところ</b>だったので、{f|電話|でんわ}に{f|出|で}られませんでした。
I was about to get in the bath, so I couldn't pick up.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|寝|ね}る<b>ところ</b>に{f|友|とも}だちから{f|電話|でんわ}がかかってきた。
Just as I was going to bed, a friend rang.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|危|あや}うく{f|終電|しゅうでん}を{f|逃|のが}す<b>ところ</b>でした。
I very nearly missed the last train.
</pre>

<pre>
ちょうど{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}る<b>ところ</b>なので、あと{f|十分|じゅっぷん}くらいで{f|着|つ}くと{f|思|おも}います。
I'm walking out the door right now, so I should be there in about ten minutes.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|聞|き}かずに{f|帰|かえ}る<b>ところ</b>でしたが、{f|同僚|どうりょう}が{f|教|おし}えてくれて{f|助|たす}かりました。
I was about to go home without hearing the explanation, but a colleague filled me in - a lucky escape.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

In its present-tense use, <code>ところです</code> is a status report about the very
near future, and it is the natural answer to
<code>{f|今|いま}{f|何|なに}してる?</code> or <code>もう{f|出|で}た?</code>. In casual
speech it contracts to `とこ` and drops the copula:
<code>{f|今|いま}{f|出|で}るとこ</code>. Both registers are extremely common; what
you will not hear is this pattern in a formal written notice, where a schedule
would be stated with times instead.

The past form is where the real value is.
<code>～ところだった</code> means *it nearly happened and it didn't*, and it almost
always carries relief. It collects a fixed set of adverbs - 
<code>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</code>, <code>{f|危|あや}うく</code>,
<code>あぶなく</code> - and it is how Japanese says all of these:

- <code>{f|忘|わす}れるところでした</code> - "I almost forgot."
- <code>{f|遅刻|ちこく}するところだった</code> - "I nearly ended up late."
- <code>{f|買|か}ってしまうところだった</code> - "I almost bought it."

Note that the verb stays in the **dictionary form** even though the sentence is
about the past. The past lives in `だった`／`でした` at the end; the verb has to stay
un-happened, because the whole point is that it never happened.

One thing this pattern does **not** do is announce plans. It has a horizon of a
few minutes. For anything further out, the words are
<code>つもり</code> and <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code>.

## Contrast

### {f|出|で}るところ vs {f|出|で}たところ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the form of the verb walks you across the event. Dictionary
form: **not started**. た-form: **just finished**. The `ところ` is identical in
both.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}、{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}る<b>ところ</b>です。
I'm just about to leave the house.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}、{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}た<b>ところ</b>です。
I've just left the house.
</pre>

Said into a phone, these two put you in completely different places: still
indoors, or already on the street. The middle member of the set - 
<code>{f|出|で}ているところ</code> - is built on `ている` and covers the stretch in
between.

This is why the form in front of `ところ` deserves more care than any other choice
on this page. The word never changes; the verb carries all the information.

### ～ところだ vs ～つもり・～{f|予定|よてい}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ところ` reaches a few **minutes** into the future.
<code>つもり</code> is an **intention** and <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> is a
**schedule**, and both stretch as far as you like.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|京都|きょうと}へ{f|行|い}く<b>つもり</b>です。
I'm planning to go to Kyoto next month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}{f|駅|えき}へ{f|行|い}く<b>ところ</b>です。
I'm on my way to the station this minute.
</pre>

❌<code>{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|京都|きょうと}へ{f|行|い}くところです</code> is wrong, and
audibly so: `ところ` claims you are on the very brink of the action, and you cannot
be on the brink of something a month away.

The reverse substitution is not an error so much as a loss.
<code>{f|今|いま}{f|行|い}くつもりです</code> reports your intention and leaves open
whether you have moved; <code>{f|今|いま}{f|行|い}くところです</code> says you are
already on your feet.

### ～ところだった vs ～そうになった

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both mean "almost". <code>ところだった</code> is a whole
**situation** you narrowly avoided; <code>そうになった</code> is a **physical
event** that nearly happened to you.

</div>

<pre>
もう{f|少|すこ}しで{f|遅刻|ちこく}する<b>ところ</b>でした。
I very nearly ended up late.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|階段|かいだん}で{f|転|ころ}び<b>そうになり</b>ました。
I almost fell on the stairs.
</pre>

The mechanics differ too: <code>そうになる</code> attaches to the <b>ます-stem</b>
(<code>{f|転|ころ}び</code>, <code>{f|落|お}ち</code>, <code>{f|泣|な}き</code>),
while `ところ` takes the dictionary form. And their coverage differs.
<code>そうになる</code> wants something on the verge of physically occurring - 
falling, dropping, crying, crashing. <code>ところだった</code> is happy with
outcomes you avoided by *deciding differently*:
<code>{f|危|あや}うく{f|高|たか}いほうを{f|買|か}うところだった</code>, "I almost bought
the expensive one." <code>そうになる</code> cannot say that.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|今|いま}{f|出|で}かけ<b>ますところ</b>です。 → ✅ {f|今|いま}{f|出|で}かけ<b>る</b>ところです。
  `ところ` is a noun, and a noun takes the plain form in front of it. The
  politeness is already there in `です`.

- ❌ {f|来年|らいねん}、アメリカに{f|留学|りゅうがく}する<b>ところ</b>です。 → ✅ {f|来年|らいねん}、アメリカに{f|留学|りゅうがく}する<b>{f|予定|よてい}</b>です。
  A year is far outside the range of this pattern. Distant plans take
  <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> or `つもり`.

- ❌ もう{f|少|すこ}しで{f|財布|さいふ}を{f|忘|わす}れ<b>たところ</b>でした。 → ✅ もう{f|少|すこ}しで{f|財布|さいふ}を{f|忘|わす}れ<b>るところ</b>でした。
  The verb stays in the dictionary form: the forgetting never happened. Only
  <code>でした</code> moves into the past.

- ❌ {f|今|いま}{f|食|た}べ<b>ているところ</b>だったので、{f|後|あと}で{f|行|い}きます。（meaning "I was about to eat"） → ✅ {f|今|いま}{f|食|た}べ<b>るところ</b>だったので…
  `ている` puts you in the middle of the meal. If the food is untouched, the
  dictionary form is the one you want.

## Practice

**1.** あぶない! もう{f|少|すこ}しで{f|車|くるま}にぶつかる____だった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

もう{f|少|すこ}しで{f|車|くるま}にぶつかる<b>ところ</b>だった。

<code>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</code> is the giveaway: it introduces something that came
close to happening and then did not, and the pattern for that is
<code>～るところだった</code>. Note that the verb stays in the dictionary form
because the collision never took place.

</details>

**2.** ちょうど{f|今|いま}、{f|会社|かいしゃ}を（{f|出|で}る・{f|出|で}た）ところです。これから{f|向|む}かいます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ちょうど{f|今|いま}、{f|会社|かいしゃ}を<b>{f|出|で}る</b>ところです。

The second sentence - "I'll head over now" - tells you the journey has not started
yet, so you are still on the office side of the door. That is the dictionary form.
<code>{f|出|で}たところ</code> would put you outside already.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I was just about to phone you."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ちょうど{f|電話|でんわ}する<b>ところ</b>でした。

The call had not been made, so the verb is in the dictionary form, and the past
sits in <code>でした</code>. This is a very common thing to say when someone
contacts you first, and it is friendlier than
<code>{f|電話|でんわ}しようと{f|思|おも}っていました</code> because it claims you were
already in motion.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|東京|とうきょう}に{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}すところです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|東京|とうきょう}に{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}す<b>{f|予定|よてい}</b>です。（or {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}す<b>つもり</b>です）

`ところ` means you are on the brink of the action, within minutes. A move next
month is a plan, so it needs <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> for a fixed arrangement or
`つもり` for an intention.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|財布|さいふ}を{f|忘|わす}れるところでした。
(b) {f|財布|さいふ}を{f|忘|わす}れてしまいました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a near miss with a happy ending: the wallet is in your pocket. You noticed
in time, and the sentence is coloured by relief - this is what you say as you turn
back from the door with it in your hand.

(b) is the disaster itself. The wallet is on the kitchen table and you are on the
train. `てしまう` reports a completed action you regret, where
<code>ところでした</code> reports one that never happened at all.

</details>
