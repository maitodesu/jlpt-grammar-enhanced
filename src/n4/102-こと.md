# こと - nominaliser

**Meaning** · turns a verb, an adjective or a whole clause into a noun, so the sentence can talk *about* it

Japanese has no infinitive and no "-ing" noun. When you need an action to behave
like a thing - to be liked, to be important, to be decided at a meeting - you put
`こと` on the end of the clause. What comes out is an ordinary noun and takes `が`,
`を`, `は` or `です` like any other.

## Formation

`こと` attaches to the **plain form**. Only な-adjectives and nouns change shape
first, and they change in the way they always do before a noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain) | dictionary form + こと | {f|集|あつ}める → {f|集|あつ}めること |
| Verb (past) | た-form + こと | {f|決|き}まった → {f|決|き}まったこと |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + こと | {f|行|い}かない → {f|行|い}かないこと |
| い-adjective | plain form + こと | {f|忙|いそが}しい → {f|忙|いそが}しいこと |
| な-adjective | ～**な** + こと | {f|大切|たいせつ}だ → {f|大切|たいせつ}なこと |
| Noun | Noun + **である** + こと | {f|学生|がくせい}だ → {f|学生|がくせい}であること |
| A whole clause | clause (plain) + こと | {f|兄|あに}が{f|結婚|けっこん}する → {f|兄|あに}が{f|結婚|けっこん}すること |
| As the predicate | ～ことだ／～ことです | {f|趣味|しゅみ}は{f|走|はし}ることです |
| Fixed frames | ことができる／たことがある／ことにする | {f|泳|およ}ぐことができる |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - never ❌<code>{f|学生|がくせい}だこと</code>. A noun needs
<code>である</code> in front of `こと`, and in everyday Japanese most speakers avoid
the problem altogether by using <code>{f|学生|がくせい}だということ</code>, which is
the clause-packaging pattern treated separately at this level.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - `こと` is also an ordinary noun meaning "thing, matter", and it keeps that
job here: <code>{f|日本|にほん}のことを{f|知|し}りたい</code> ("I want to know about
Japan"), <code>{f|田中|たなか}さんのこと</code> ("the Tanaka business", or simply
Tanaka as a topic). The nominaliser is the same word doing the same thing to a
clause instead of a noun.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|絵|え}をかく<b>こと</b>が{f|好|す}きです。
I like drawing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|約束|やくそく}の{f|時間|じかん}を{f|守|まも}る<b>こと</b>は{f|大切|たいせつ}です。
Keeping to the time you agreed is important.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}の{f|趣味|しゅみ}は{f|古|ふる}い{f|切手|きって}を{f|集|あつ}める<b>こと</b>です。
My hobby is collecting old stamps.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|外国|がいこく}で{f|働|はたら}く<b>こと</b>を{f|考|かんが}えています。
I'm thinking about working abroad.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|兄|あに}が{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|結婚|けっこん}する<b>こと</b>は、まだ{f|誰|だれ}にも{f|話|はな}していません。
I haven't told anyone yet that my brother is getting married next month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日本語|にほんご}で{f|電話|でんわ}をかける<b>こと</b>が、まだ{f|少|すこ}し{f|怖|こわ}いです。
Making phone calls in Japanese still frightens me a little.
</pre>

<pre>

It was settled at yesterday's meeting that the manager is going away on business next week.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The nominaliser itself carries no politeness. What it carries is a slight lean
towards the **written and the abstract**, which is the one thing that separates it
from its rival `の`. `こと` names a fact, a rule, a plan, a proposition - something
you can think about, decide, promise or announce. `の` names an event you are
watching, hearing or living through. Where both are grammatical, `の` is the more
spoken of the two, and a page of conversation will contain many more of them.

`こと` never contracts. `の` shrinks to `ん` before `です`, and the packaging form
`ということ` shrinks to `ってこと` in casual speech, but plain `こと` stays as it is
in every register.

<code>{f|趣味|しゅみ}は…ことです</code> deserves a line of its own because it is the
first thing you will need it for. Self-introductions, job interviews and profile
forms are full of it, and there `の` is not an option at all - the reason is in the
Contrast section.

One more `こと` exists and it looks identical on the page. A verb plus a bare `こと`
and a full stop, with **nothing after it**, is not a noun phrase at all: it is a
written instruction, the `こと` of classroom notices and exam papers, and it is
treated as its own point at N3. The test is mechanical: if a particle or `です`
follows the `こと`, you are looking at the nominaliser on this page. If the sentence
stops dead, you are looking at a rule.

## Contrast

### こと vs の

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `の` for what you see, hear and take part in; `こと` for facts,
plans and propositions. Where either would work, `の` is the spoken choice - but
three situations take the decision out of your hands.

</div>

**Verbs of direct perception require `の`.** {f|見|み}る, {f|聞|き}く,
{f|聞|き}こえる, {f|見|み}える, {f|感|かん}じる are watching an event happen, and an
event is not a fact.

<pre>
{f|外|そと}で{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}した<b>の</b>に{f|気|き}がつかなかった。
I didn't notice that it had started raining outside.
</pre>

**A nominalised clause standing in front of `だ`／`です` requires `こと`.**
<code>{f|趣味|しゅみ}は{f|走|はし}ることです</code> is right;
❌<code>{f|走|はし}るのです</code> is not available, because that string already means
something else - the explanatory `んです`.

**A set of fixed frames requires `こと`.** <code>ことができる</code>,
<code>たことがある</code>, <code>ことにする</code>, <code>ことになる</code> are frozen
expressions and admit no substitution: ❌<code>{f|泳|およ}ぐのができる</code>.

Everywhere else both are grammatical. There is one pair worth memorising because
the two versions genuinely mean different things:

<pre>
{f|電話|でんわ}する<b>の</b>を{f|忘|わす}れた。
I forgot to ring.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電話|でんわ}した<b>こと</b>を{f|忘|わす}れた。
I forgot that I had rung.
</pre>

The first is an action that never happened; the second is a fact that slipped your
mind. That is the difference between the two words in miniature.

### ことができる vs the potential form

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same meaning at two lengths. The potential form
({f|話|はな}せる) is the default in speech; <code>ことができる</code> is longer,
flatter and more official, and it is what notices and manuals use.

</div>

<pre>
ここで{f|切符|きっぷ}を{f|買|か}う<b>ことができます</b>。
Tickets may be purchased here.
</pre>

<pre>
ここで{f|切符|きっぷ}が<b>{f|買|か}えます</b>。
You can buy tickets here.
</pre>

Both are correct and mean the same thing. The `こと` in <code>ことができる</code> is
frozen in place - it cannot become `の`, and it cannot be dropped - which is a good
reminder that `こと` is not always a free choice.

### Nominalising こと vs the instruction こと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - look at what comes **after** the `こと`. A particle or `です`
means it is a noun. A full stop and nothing else means it is a rule.

</div>

<pre>
{f|使|つか}った{f|皿|さら}を{f|洗|あら}う<b>こと</b>が、{f|今週|こんしゅう}の{f|係|かかり}の{f|仕事|しごと}です。
Washing the dishes we've used is this week's duty.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|使|つか}った{f|皿|さら}は{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|洗|あら}う<b>こと</b>。
Wash your own dishes.
</pre>

The first sentence talks about the washing-up as a thing; the second orders it. The
second has no politeness level and no addressee - it is the voice of a notice on a
kitchen wall - and that impersonal instruction `こと` is a separate grammar point
you meet at N3. Nothing may follow it, so the moment you see `が`, `を`, `は` or
`です` after the `こと`, you are back on this page.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|学生|がくせい}<b>だこと</b>を{f|知|し}らなかった。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|学生|がくせい}<b>だということ</b>を{f|知|し}らなかった。
  A plain noun cannot sit in front of `こと` with `だ` on it. Everyday Japanese uses
  `だということ`; formal writing uses <code>であること</code>.

- ❌ {f|部屋|へや}が{f|静|しず}か<b>こと</b>が{f|一番|いちばん}{f|大事|だいじ}です。 → ✅ {f|部屋|へや}が{f|静|しず}か<b>なこと</b>が{f|一番|いちばん}{f|大事|だいじ}です。
  な-adjectives keep their `な` in front of `こと`, exactly as they do in front of
  any other noun.

- ❌ {f|隣|となり}の{f|人|ひと}がピアノを{f|弾|ひ}いている<b>こと</b>が{f|聞|き}こえます。 → ✅ …{f|弾|ひ}いている<b>の</b>が{f|聞|き}こえます。
  You are hearing an event, not a fact. Verbs of direct perception take `の` and
  nothing else.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}の{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|子|こ}どもに{f|英語|えいご}を{f|教|おし}える<b>のです</b>。 → ✅ …{f|教|おし}える<b>ことです</b>。
  In front of `です`, only `こと` works. With `の` the sentence turns into the
  explanatory `んです` and stops saying what you meant.

- ❌ {f|朝|あさ}{f|早|はや}く{f|起|お}きること{f|苦手|にがて}です。 → ✅ {f|朝|あさ}{f|早|はや}く{f|起|お}きること<b>が</b>{f|苦手|にがて}です。
  Once the clause has become a noun it needs a particle like any other noun. The
  nominaliser does not replace `が` or `を`; it makes room for one.

## Practice

**1.** {f|私|わたし}の{f|夢|ゆめ}は、いつか{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|店|みせ}を{f|持|も}つ____です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|店|みせ}を{f|持|も}つ<b>こと</b>です。

The nominalised clause is standing directly in front of `です` as the predicate, and
that slot only accepts `こと`. <code>{f|持|も}つのです</code> would be read as the
explanatory `んです` instead.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|子|こ}どもたちが{f|公園|こうえん}で{f|走|はし}り{f|回|まわ}っている（の・こと）が{f|窓|まど}から{f|見|み}えます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

…{f|走|はし}り{f|回|まわ}っている<b>の</b>が{f|窓|まど}から{f|見|み}えます。

<code>{f|見|み}える</code> is direct perception: the running is happening in front of
your eyes, so the nominaliser has to be `の`. `こと` would turn it into a fact you
had been told about, which is not something a window can show you.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I promised my mother that I would come home by nine."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|九時|くじ}までに{f|家|いえ}に{f|帰|かえ}る<b>こと</b>を{f|母|はは}と{f|約束|やくそく}しました。

A promise is made about a proposition, not about an event you are watching, so
`こと` is the natural choice. <code>{f|帰|かえ}ると{f|母|はは}に{f|約束|やくそく}した</code>
with the quotative `と` is also perfectly good Japanese.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|散歩|さんぽ}するのが{f|私|わたし}の{f|習慣|しゅうかん}で、{f|健康|けんこう}にいいのは{f|続|つづ}けるのです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|散歩|さんぽ}するのが{f|私|わたし}の{f|習慣|しゅうかん}で、{f|健康|けんこう}にいいのは{f|続|つづ}ける<b>ことです</b>。

The first `の` is fine - it is followed by `が`, and either nominaliser would do
there. The second one is in front of `です`, where only `こと` is possible.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference:
(a) {f|鍵|かぎ}をかける<b>の</b>を{f|忘|わす}れました。
(b) {f|鍵|かぎ}をかけた<b>こと</b>を{f|忘|わす}れました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the locking never happened. `の` names the act itself, and
<code>{f|忘|わす}れる</code> reports that you failed to carry it out - the door is
unlocked.

(b) says the locking did happen, and the memory of it is what went missing. `こと`
names the *fact* that you locked up, which is why the past form
<code>かけた</code> is possible here and impossible in (a). The door is locked; you
are just about to go back and check it.

</details>
