# など

**Meaning** · such as; things like - it declares a list unfinished

`など` is a full stop that refuses to be one. You name one or two items, add
`など`, and the listener understands that more of the same kind exist but are not
worth spelling out. Its second use grows straight out of the first: if you present
something as a mere example, you are also saying it is nothing special - which is
how `など` becomes modest about yourself and dismissive about everything else.

## Formation

`など` follows the **last** item of the list. The case particle for the whole
phrase then follows `など`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + など | ペン → ペンなど |
| A や-list | AやB + など | パンや{f|卵|たまご}など |
| A single example | Noun + など | お{f|茶|ちゃ}など |
| Before a case particle | ～など + が／を／に／で | {f|野菜|やさい}などを{f|買|か}う |
| Quoted words | ～などと{f|言|い}う／{f|思|おも}う | {f|無理|むり}だなどと{f|言|い}う |
| Dismissive / humble | Noun + など + deprecating predicate | お{f|礼|れい}など{f|要|い}りません |
| Casual equivalent | なんか | ケーキなんか |
| Emphatic equivalent | なんて | {f|私|わたし}なんて |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `や` *joins* items; `など` *closes* the list. They are not
alternatives, and the two together - AやBなど - is the most common shape of all.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - `など` attaches to nouns. To leave a list of **actions** open, use
`～とか～とか` or the `～たり～たり` frame instead; ❌{f|走|はし}るなど{f|泳|およ}ぐ
is not a sentence. In writing you will meet ～などする
（{f|運動|うんどう}するなどして）, but that is a formal construction, not everyday
speech.

</div>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

In its plain listing sense `など` is neutral, and if it leans anywhere it leans
**written and careful**. It is the form you want in a report, an announcement or a
polite explanation to a customer.

Its two informal twins are worth keeping straight:

- **なんか** is the everyday spoken form. Same meaning, no formality.
  {f|漫画|まんが}なんか{f|読|よ}まないよ. Very frequent, completely ordinary in
  conversation, out of place in writing.
- **なんて** adds feeling - surprise, contempt, or dismissal.
  {f|日本語|にほんご}なんて{f|簡単|かんたん}だ is boastful; {f|私|わたし}なんて
  is self-deprecating. It is not a neutral option.

The **softening** use is one you will hear constantly and rarely see taught:
offering something as a mere example makes the offer less pushy.
コーヒーなどいかがですか is warmer and less presumptuous than
コーヒーはいかがですか, because it pretends not to have decided anything for you.

The dismissive use cuts both ways, and the direction depends on **who** the noun
refers to. Pointed at yourself it is humble and perfectly polite:
{f|私|わたし}などが{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げることではありませんが. Pointed at
someone else it belittles them, so
❌{f|田中|たなか}さんなどに{f|聞|き}いてみます, said in front of Tanaka, is an
insult you did not intend.

## Contrast

### など vs や

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `や` sits **between** two nouns and links them. `など` sits
**after** the last noun and stops the list without finishing it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|机|つくえ}の{f|上|うえ}にノート<b>や</b>はさみがあります。
There's a notebook, scissors and so on on the desk.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|机|つくえ}の{f|上|うえ}にノートやはさみ<b>など</b>があります。
There's a notebook, scissors and that sort of thing on the desk.
</pre>

`や` already implies the list is partial, so `など` is not strictly required - it
just makes the implication explicit and a little more polite. What you cannot do
is swap their positions: ❌ノートなどはさみ joins nothing.

### など vs なんか・なんて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one meaning, three registers: `など` written and neutral,
`なんか` casual and spoken, `なんて` emotional.

</div>

<pre>
{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}には{f|人数|にんずう}や{f|費用|ひよう}<b>など</b>を{f|書|か}きます。
In the report we write the number of people, the cost and so on.
</pre>

<pre>
そんなこと、{f|私|わたし}<b>なんか</b>に{f|聞|き}かないでよ。
Don't ask me about that, of all people.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|行|い}く<b>なんて</b>、あぶないよ。
Going alone - that's dangerous.
</pre>

Note the third one: `なんて` will attach to a whole verb phrase, which `など`
resists in speech. `なんて` also tends to run straight into the predicate without
a case particle behind it.

### など vs とか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `とか` is repeated after **each** item and is casual; `など`
appears **once** at the end and is the neutral or written choice.

</div>

<pre>
{f|週末|しゅうまつ}は{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}<b>とか</b>{f|散歩|さんぽ}<b>とか</b>しています。
At weekends I go shopping, take walks, that sort of thing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|週末|しゅうまつ}は{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}や{f|散歩|さんぽ}<b>など</b>をしています。
At weekends I do things like shopping and going for walks.
</pre>

Both say the same thing about the world. The difference is who is speaking: the
first is a friend over coffee, the second could go in a self-introduction. `とか`
also attaches happily to verbs and whole clauses, where `など` has to be helped
along by a noun.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ パン<b>など</b>{f|卵|たまご}を{f|買|か}いました。 → ✅ パン<b>や</b>{f|卵|たまご}<b>など</b>を{f|買|か}いました。
  `など` is not a conjunction. It cannot stand between two nouns; it can only
  close the list from behind.

- ❌ {f|野菜|やさい}<b>を</b>など{f|買|か}います。 → ✅ {f|野菜|やさい}<b>など</b>を{f|買|か}います。
  The case particle goes **outside** `など`, because `など` belongs to the noun
  phrase and `を` belongs to the sentence.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>など</b>にご{f|相談|そうだん}します。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}にご{f|相談|そうだん}します。
  Attaching `など` to a person you are showing respect to reduces them to a random
  example. Keep the dismissive `など` for yourself.

- ❌ この{f|資料|しりょう}には{f|住所|じゅうしょ}や{f|電話番号|でんわばんごう}<b>なんか</b>が{f|書|か}いてあります。 → ✅ ～{f|電話番号|でんわばんごう}<b>など</b>が{f|書|か}いてあります。
  Not ungrammatical, but `なんか` is spoken register and jars against a written
  document. Match the word to the medium.

## Practice

**1.** {f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}に{f|牛乳|ぎゅうにゅう}____チーズ____{f|入|はい}っています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}に{f|牛乳|ぎゅうにゅう}<b>や</b>チーズ<b>など</b>が{f|入|はい}っています。

`や` links the two nouns; `など` closes the list off as partial. This double
frame - AやBなど - is the default shape for an open-ended list of things, and
`が` then attaches after `など`.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural option for a company email:
{f|会場|かいじょう}や{f|時間|じかん}（など・なんか）は{f|後|あと}でお{f|知|し}らせします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会場|かいじょう}や{f|時間|じかん}<b>など</b>は{f|後|あと}でお{f|知|し}らせします。

`なんか` carries the same meaning but belongs to casual speech, and it would sit
badly next to the humble お{f|知|し}らせします. In writing, `など` is the only
comfortable choice.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Would you like some tea or something?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お{f|茶|ちゃ}<b>など</b>いかがですか。

`など` is doing softening work here rather than listing work: by offering tea as
just one example of what might be available, you avoid pinning the other person
down. お{f|茶|ちゃ}でもいかがですか is the more casual version of the same move.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|山田|やまだ}さんなどが{f|手伝|てつだ}ってくださいました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|山田|やまだ}さんが{f|手伝|てつだ}ってくださいました。

The sentence uses くださる, an honorific, and then undercuts it with `など`, which
treats {f|山田|やまだ}さん as one interchangeable example among many. Delete the
`など`. If you genuinely mean "Yamada and others", say
{f|山田|やまだ}さんたち.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|私|わたし}は{f|料理|りょうり}が{f|下手|へた}です。
(b) {f|私|わたし}など{f|料理|りょうり}が{f|下手|へた}で、お{f|見|み}せできません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain statement of fact about your cooking, with no attitude attached.

(b) uses `など` to put yourself forward as a nobody - "someone like me". It is a
humbling move, common when declining praise or an invitation to demonstrate
something, and it makes the sentence noticeably more modest and more formal. Aimed
at anyone other than yourself, the same word would be rude.

</details>
