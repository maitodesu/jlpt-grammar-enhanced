# ～出す

**Meaning** · suddenly start doing; burst out doing

<code>{f|出|だ}す</code> on the stem of another verb says the action **broke out**.
Where <code>～{f|始|はじ}める</code> merely marks a beginning,
<code>～{f|出|だ}す</code> adds abruptness: nobody saw it coming, nobody arranged
it, and often nobody could have stopped it.

## Formation

Attach <code>{f|出|だ}す</code> to the <b>ます-stem</b>. The compound is a う-verb, so
it conjugates like <code>{f|話|はな}す</code>.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + {f|出|だ}す | {f|降|ふ}ります → {f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}す |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + {f|出|だ}す | {f|逃|に}げます → {f|逃|に}げ{f|出|だ}す |
| Verb (する) | し + {f|出|だ}す | {f|心配|しんぱい}する → {f|心配|しんぱい}し{f|出|だ}す |
| Conjugation | う-verb | {f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}した、{f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}して、{f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}します |
| Object particle | unchanged from the first verb | {f|文句|もんく}<b>を</b>{f|言|い}い{f|出|だ}す |
| Often paired with | {f|急|きゅう}に、いきなり、{f|突然|とつぜん} | {f|急|きゅう}に{f|笑|わら}い{f|出|だ}す |
| ❌ Request | ❌{f|走|はし}り{f|出|だ}してください | ✅{f|走|はし}り{f|始|はじ}めてください |
| ❌ Invitation / volitional | ❌{f|歌|うた}い{f|出|だ}そう | ✅{f|歌|うた}い{f|始|はじ}めよう |
| ❌ Instantaneous verb | ❌{f|消|き}え{f|出|だ}す | ✅{f|急|きゅう}に{f|消|き}える |
| ❌ | ❌ dictionary form + {f|出|だ}す | ❌{f|読|よ}む{f|出|だ}す |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|出|だ}す</code> removes the plan. Because of that, it
cannot appear in any form that assumes control: no ください, no ましょう, no
volitional, and no announcement of what you intend to do next week.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - like <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code>, this suffix needs a verb with some
duration. Something that is over in an instant has no onset to mark:
❌<code>{f|電気|でんき}が{f|消|き}え{f|出|だ}した</code>. Say
<code>{f|急|きゅう}に{f|消|き}えた</code> instead, and keep
<code>{f|出|だ}す</code> for things that go on once they have started - rain,
crying, running, arguing.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|赤|あか}ちゃんが{f|急|きゅう}に<b>{f|泣|な}き{f|出|だ}し</b>た。
The baby suddenly burst into tears.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|外|そと}を{f|見|み}たら、{f|雪|ゆき}が<b>{f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}し</b>ていた。
I looked outside and it had started snowing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}の{f|話|はなし}がおもしろくて、クラスのみんなが<b>{f|笑|わら}い{f|出|だ}し</b>た。
The teacher's story was so funny the whole class burst out laughing.
</pre>

<pre>
バスが<b>{f|動|うご}き{f|出|だ}し</b>てから、かばんを{f|忘|わす}れたことに{f|気|き}がついた。
It was only after the bus pulled away that I realised I'd left my bag behind.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼女|かのじょ}が{f|何|なに}も{f|言|い}わずに<b>{f|走|はし}り{f|出|だ}し</b>たので、みんな{f|驚|おどろ}きました。
She broke into a run without a word, and everyone was taken aback.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|静|しず}かだった{f|教室|きょうしつ}が、ベルの{f|音|おと}で{f|一度|いちど}に<b>{f|騒|さわ}ぎ{f|出|だ}し</b>た。
The classroom had been silent, and at the bell it erupted all at once.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|夜中|よなか}に{f|隣|となり}の{f|犬|いぬ}が<b>{f|鳴|な}き{f|出|だ}し</b>て、{f|朝|あさ}まで{f|眠|ねむ}れませんでした。
The dog next door started barking in the night and I couldn't sleep till morning.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わりそうになったころ、{f|田中|たなか}さんが{f|急|きゅう}に{f|反対|はんたい}だと<b>{f|言|い}い{f|出|だ}し</b>て、また{f|一時間|いちじかん}かかりました。
Just as the meeting was winding down, Tanaka suddenly came out against it, and it took another hour.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The suffix is register-neutral - it is as at home in a novel as in a complaint to
a friend - but it is never *emotionally* neutral. Something about the event was
unexpected, and the speaker is registering that. This is why
<code>{f|出|だ}す</code> collects the same adverbs over and over:
<code>{f|急|きゅう}に</code>, <code>いきなり</code>, <code>{f|突然|とつぜん}</code>.

The loss of control has one consequence learners rarely anticipate: used about
your own deliberate action, <code>{f|出|だ}す</code> sounds as if you surprised
yourself. <code>{f|気|き}がついたら{f|書|か}き{f|出|だ}していた</code> ("I looked up
and I was already writing") is a good sentence; ❌
<code>{f|明日|あした}から{f|書|か}き{f|出|だ}します</code> is not, because you cannot
schedule your own outburst.

<code>{f|言|い}い{f|出|だ}す</code> deserves separate mention. It means "to come
out with", "to be the one who raises it", and it carries a whiff of blame:
<code>{f|言|い}い{f|出|だ}したのは{f|君|きみ}だよ</code> - "you're the one who brought
it up." That is the suffix doing exactly its usual job, applied to speech.

Finally, be aware that a handful of very common verbs ending in
<code>{f|出|だ}す</code> are fully lexicalised and have nothing to do with
suddenness: <code>{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}す</code> (remember),
<code>{f|取|と}り{f|出|だ}す</code> (take out),
<code>{f|引|ひ}き{f|出|だ}す</code> (draw out),
<code>{f|貸|か}し{f|出|だ}す</code> (lend out),
<code>{f|見|み}つけ{f|出|だ}す</code> (track down). You learn these as words, not as
this pattern.

## Contrast

### ～{f|出|だ}す vs ～{f|始|はじ}める

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same slot, different attitude.
<code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> is available in every form of the verb;
<code>{f|出|だ}す</code> is available only where nobody is in charge.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}から{f|朝|あさ}のジョギングを<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ます。
I'm taking up jogging in the mornings from next week.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|信号|しんごう}が{f|青|あお}になって、{f|車|くるま}が{f|一斉|いっせい}に<b>{f|走|はし}り{f|出|だ}し</b>た。
The lights went green and the cars all pulled away together.
</pre>

The first announces a plan; <code>{f|出|だ}す</code> cannot go there. The second
describes something that simply happened, and
<code>{f|走|はし}り{f|始|はじ}めた</code> would drain the moment of its snap.

When the same verb takes either suffix, the difference is a threshold.
<code>{f|泣|な}き{f|始|はじ}めた</code> reports that crying commenced;
<code>{f|泣|な}き{f|出|だ}した</code> shows you the face crumpling.

### ～{f|出|だ}す vs {f|急|きゅう}に・いきなり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|急|きゅう}に</code> is an **adverb** that can modify
any event. <code>{f|出|だ}す</code> is a **suffix** that can only mark a beginning.
They stack, and neither replaces the other.

</div>

<pre>
{f|急|きゅう}に{f|電気|でんき}が{f|消|き}えた。
The lights suddenly went out.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|急|きゅう}に{f|雨|あめ}が<b>{f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}し</b>た。
It suddenly started to rain.
</pre>

Going out is instantaneous, so there is no onset for
<code>{f|出|だ}す</code> to mark and only the adverb is possible. Raining lasts, so
both work - and the second sentence uses both at once, which is completely normal
and not redundant: the adverb says it was sudden, the suffix says this was the
*start* of something that then went on.

### {f|出|だ}す "suddenly begin" vs {f|出|だ}す "outwards"

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if the first verb is about **handling or moving something**,
<code>{f|出|だ}す</code> is probably the literal "out". If it is about an
**activity that can start**, it is the aspect marker.

</div>

<pre>
ポケットからかぎを<b>{f|取|と}り{f|出|だ}し</b>た。
I took the key out of my pocket.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|犬|いぬ}が{f|庭|にわ}から<b>{f|逃|に}げ{f|出|だ}し</b>た。
The dog got out of the garden and ran off.
</pre>

Nothing in the first sentence is sudden - the key came *out*, and that is all the
suffix is contributing. The second sits on the border and carries a little of
both: escaping is outward motion, and it is also something that happened without
warning.

The one to watch is <code>{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}す</code>. It means *remember*, not
"suddenly begin to think" - the memory comes out, it does not start. Treat it as a
single word.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ みなさん、そろそろ{f|歌|うた}い<b>{f|出|だ}し</b>てください。 → ✅ みなさん、そろそろ{f|歌|うた}い<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>てください。
  A request assumes the listener is in control, which is precisely what
  <code>{f|出|だ}す</code> denies.

- ❌ {f|来年|らいねん}からピアノを{f|習|なら}い<b>{f|出|だ}し</b>ます。 → ✅ {f|来年|らいねん}からピアノを{f|習|なら}い<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ます。
  You cannot schedule a surprise. Plans announced in advance take
  <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code>.

- ❌ {f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}む<b>{f|出|だ}す</b> → ✅ {f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}み<b>{f|出|だ}す</b>
  The suffix goes on the ます-stem, exactly as
  <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> does.

- ❌ {f|急|きゅう}に{f|電気|でんき}が{f|消|き}え<b>{f|出|だ}し</b>た。 → ✅ {f|急|きゅう}に{f|電気|でんき}が{f|消|き}えた。
  <code>{f|消|き}える</code> happens in an instant, so it has no beginning for the
  suffix to mark. The adverb alone carries the suddenness.

## Practice

**1.** {f|話|はなし}を{f|聞|き}いたとき、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|急|きゅう}に{f|笑|わら}い____た。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}は{f|急|きゅう}に{f|笑|わら}い<b>{f|出|だ}し</b>た。

Laughing is something that starts and then continues, and
<code>{f|急|きゅう}に</code> tells you it was unplanned - both conditions for
<code>{f|出|だ}す</code>. <code>{f|笑|わら}い{f|始|はじ}めた</code> is possible but
flatter; it reports a beginning without the burst.

</details>

**2.** {f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|新|あたら}しい{f|習|なら}い{f|事|ごと}を（{f|出|だ}します・{f|始|はじ}めます）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|新|あたら}しい{f|習|なら}い{f|事|ごと}を<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ます。

This is a plan, stated in advance, about your own action.
<code>{f|出|だ}す</code> is blocked in exactly that situation, because it means the
action broke out of its own accord.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "As soon as the film started, the person next to me began talking."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|映画|えいが}が{f|始|はじ}まると、となりの{f|人|ひと}が<b>{f|話|はな}し{f|出|だ}し</b>ました。

The talking was neither planned by the speaker nor welcome, and it went on - the
classic conditions for <code>{f|出|だ}す</code>. Note that
<code>{f|始|はじ}まる</code> earlier in the sentence is the ordinary intransitive
verb, not this suffix.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|準備|じゅんび}ができましたので、そろそろ{f|食|た}べ{f|出|だ}しましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|準備|じゅんび}ができましたので、そろそろ{f|食|た}べ<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ましょう。

`ましょう` is an invitation, and an invitation cannot be built on a suffix that
means "it happened without anyone deciding". Swap in
<code>{f|始|はじ}める</code>, which is free in every form.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|子|こ}どもが{f|泣|な}き{f|始|はじ}めた。
(b) {f|子|こ}どもが{f|泣|な}き{f|出|だ}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) records the fact: at some point the crying began. It fits a calm account - a
nurse writing notes, or a parent explaining what happened at what time.

(b) puts you in the room. It says the crying broke out, probably without warning
and probably to everyone's dismay. Both are correct Japanese about the same event;
(b) is the one with a reaction built into it, which is also why it cannot be used
to announce a plan.

</details>
