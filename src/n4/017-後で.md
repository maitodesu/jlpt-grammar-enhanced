# ～後で

**Meaning** · after something; afterwards, later on

N5 taught this word in kana, as <code>～たあとで</code>. Here it is in kanji, plus the
two jobs the kana page left alone: `後で` standing on its own as the adverb
*later*, and the four different readings the character takes once it appears in
writing.

## Formation

The clause in front of `後で` is in the <b>た-form</b>, whatever the tense of the
sentence as a whole. A noun joins with `の`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | た-form + {f|後|あと}で | {f|食|た}べる → {f|食|た}べた{f|後|あと}で |
| Noun | Noun + <b>の</b> + {f|後|あと}で | {f|授業|じゅぎょう} → {f|授業|じゅぎょう}<b>の</b>{f|後|あと}で |
| On its own (adverb) | {f|後|あと}で + clause | {f|後|あと}で{f|連絡|れんらく}します |
| Demonstrative | その{f|後|あと}で／この{f|後|あと} | その{f|後|あと}で{f|決|き}めましょう |
| Without で | た-form + {f|後|あと} | {f|見|み}た{f|後|あと}、{f|考|かんが}えます |
| With に instead | た-form + {f|後|あと}に | {f|私|わたし}の{f|後|あと}に{f|発表|はっぴょう}します |
| Amount of time | Quantity + {f|後|ご}（に） | {f|三日|みっか}{f|後|ご}に、{f|一時間|いちじかん}{f|後|ご}に |
| Sino-Japanese compound | Noun + {f|後|ご} | {f|卒業|そつぎょう}{f|後|ご}、{f|使用|しよう}{f|後|ご} |
| Formal register | {f|後|のち}ほど | {f|後|のち}ほどご{f|連絡|れんらく}いたします |
| Politeness | on the **final** verb only | {f|着|つ}いた{f|後|あと}で{f|電話|でんわ}し<b>ます</b> |
| ❌ | ❌ dictionary form + {f|後|あと}で | ❌{f|食|た}べる{f|後|あと}で |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one character, four readings, and they are not
interchangeable. <code>{f|後|あと}</code> is *after* in time,
<code>{f|後|うし}ろ</code> is *behind* in space, <code>{f|後|ご}</code> is the suffix on
Sino-Japanese words and on amounts of time, and <code>{f|後|のち}</code> is the formal,
written one. Reading <code>{f|三日|みっか}{f|後|ご}</code> as あと marks you out instantly.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - `で` is optional and drops freely, and the whole phrase can take `は` or
`も`: <code>{f|食|た}べた{f|後|あと}は{f|眠|ねむ}くなる</code>. What you cannot do is put a
verb in the dictionary form in front of it - that slot belongs to
<code>{f|前|まえ}に</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>メールしますね。
I'll email you later.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|薬|くすり}は{f|食事|しょくじ}の<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>{f|飲|の}んでください。
Please take the medicine after meals.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|仕事|しごと}の<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>、ジムで{f|泳|およ}ぐつもりです。
I'm planning to swim at the gym after work.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|宿題|しゅくだい}を{f|終|お}わらせた<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>なら、ゲームをしてもいいよ。
You can play games once you've finished your homework.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試合|しあい}の<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>、チームのみんなでラーメンを{f|食|た}べに{f|行|い}った。
After the match the whole team went out for ramen.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}した<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>、{f|住所|じゅうしょ}が{f|変|か}わったことを{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|伝|つた}えました。
After I moved, I let the company know my address had changed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|聞|き}いた<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>{f|質問|しつもん}がある{f|方|かた}は、{f|手|て}を{f|挙|あ}げてください。
If you have a question after hearing the explanation, please raise your hand.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}<b>{f|後|ご}</b>に{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}しますので、それまでに{f|準備|じゅんび}をお{f|願|ねが}いします。
We leave in thirty minutes, so please be ready by then.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

As a clause connector `後で` is plain and neutral. As a **standalone adverb** it
carries something extra, and that is worth knowing: `後で` is deliberately vague.
It means "at some point after now, probably today", and it names no time at all.

That vagueness is friendly among equals - <code>じゃあ、また{f|後|あと}でね</code> is
how you end a conversation you expect to resume - and unhelpful upward. Told
<code>{f|後|あと}でやります</code>, a manager has learned nothing; <code>{f|三時|さんじ}までに{f|出|だ}します</code>
is what they wanted. If you need the vagueness *and* the politeness, the business
word is <code>{f|後|のち}ほど</code>:
<code>{f|後|のち}ほど{f|担当|たんとう}よりご{f|連絡|れんらく}いたします</code>. It is one register
step above `後で` and never sounds casual.

Spelling is a genuine choice here. In the grammatical use, careful writing often
prefers kana - <code>{f|食|た}べたあとで</code> - because the kanji pulls the reader
towards the "behind" meaning. Inside a compound the kanji is compulsory and the
reading switches: <code>{f|卒業|そつぎょう}{f|後|ご}</code>, <code>{f|開店|かいてん}{f|後|ご}</code>,
<code>{f|二年|にねん}{f|後|ご}</code>.

One last spoken habit: <code>{f|後|あと}にする</code> means "leave it for later" - 
<code>その{f|話|はなし}は{f|後|あと}にしよう</code>. It is not the same construction as
`後で`, but it lives in the same corner of the language.

## Contrast

### ～た{f|後|あと}で vs ～てから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `てから` says the second thing does not happen **until** the
first is done. `後で` only says it happens later. When the order is a requirement,
use `てから`; when it is just the order things came in, `後で` is enough.

</div>

<pre>
お{f|金|かね}を{f|入|い}れ<b>てから</b>、ボタンを{f|押|お}してください。
Put the money in first, then press the button.
</pre>

Here `てから` is doing real work: pressing the button before paying achieves
nothing. Swap in <code>{f|入|い}れた{f|後|あと}で</code> and the instruction goes soft - it
now merely reports a sequence.

<pre>
{f|映画|えいが}の<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>、{f|喫茶店|きっさてん}で{f|少|すこ}し{f|話|はな}した。
After the film we chatted for a bit in a coffee shop.
</pre>

Nothing about the film enabled the chat. This is plain sequence, so `後で` fits and
`てから` would sound oddly purposeful.

`てから` has one job `後で` cannot take on at all: measuring elapsed time from a
starting point. <code>{f|日本|にほん}に{f|来|き}てから{f|三年|さんねん}になります</code> - 
"it's been three years since I came to Japan." ❌<code>{f|来|き}た{f|後|あと}で{f|三年|さんねん}になります</code>
is not Japanese. Going the other way, `後で` attaches straight to a noun with `の`,
which `てから` cannot do: <code>{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|後|あと}で</code> has no `てから` equivalent
short of adding a verb.

### {f|後|あと}で vs {f|後|あと}に vs ～{f|後|ご}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `で` is the everyday "afterwards". `に` locates a **point**:
use it when you are placing something in an order, and you must use the
<code>{f|後|ご}</code> reading with a measured quantity of time.

</div>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さんの<b>{f|後|あと}に</b>{f|発表|はっぴょう}します。
I'm presenting after Tanaka.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|五分|ごふん}<b>{f|後|ご}</b>にまたかけ{f|直|なお}します。
I'll call back in five minutes.
</pre>

A quantity of time never takes `後で`: ❌<code>{f|五分|ごふん}{f|後|あと}で</code> is simply
wrong, both in the particle and in the reading. In the ordinary
"X happened, then Y" use, `後で` and `後に` overlap heavily and `後で` is the safer
spoken default; `後に` leans written, and it is what you want when the sentence is
about a slot in a sequence rather than about a time.

There is also <code>{f|後|のち}に</code>, read のち, which means "later in life" or
"subsequently" and belongs to biography and history:
<code>{f|彼|かれ}は{f|後|のち}に{f|市長|しちょう}になった</code>. `後で` cannot carry that sense.

### {f|後|あと}で vs {f|今度|こんど}・そのうち

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `後で` is later **today**. <code>{f|今度|こんど}</code> is **next
time**, on some other occasion. `そのうち` is **eventually**, with no occasion in
mind at all.

</div>

English collapses all three into "later", and picking the wrong one changes what
you have promised.

<pre>
その{f|書類|しょるい}は<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>{f|見|み}ておきます。
I'll look over that document later on. (today, before I go home)
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|今度|こんど}</b>いっしょに{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}きましょう。
Let's go for a drink sometime.
</pre>

That second sentence is an invitation, not an appointment, and everyone hears it
that way. If you say <code>{f|今度|こんど}{f|電話|でんわ}します</code> to someone waiting
for a call in the next hour, they will assume you mean next week. `そのうち` is
vaguer still - <code>そのうち{f|片付|かたづ}けます</code> is close to admitting it may
never happen.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|食|た}べる<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>{f|歯|は}をみがきます。 → ✅ {f|食|た}べ<b>た</b>{f|後|あと}で{f|歯|は}をみがきます。
  `後で` takes the た-form. The dictionary form in that slot belongs to
  <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code>.

- ❌ {f|授業|じゅぎょう}<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>{f|質問|しつもん}しました。 → ✅ {f|授業|じゅぎょう}<b>の</b>{f|後|あと}で{f|質問|しつもん}しました。
  `後` is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs `の`. (The compound
  <code>{f|授業|じゅぎょう}{f|後|ご}</code> exists and needs no `の` - but then it is read
  ご and belongs to written style.)

- ❌ {f|二時間|にじかん}<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>{f|戻|もど}ります。 → ✅ {f|二時間|にじかん}<b>{f|後|ご}に</b>{f|戻|もど}ります。
  With a measured amount of time the reading is ご and the particle is `に`.

- ❌ すぐ{f|終|お}わるので、<b>{f|今度|こんど}</b>{f|電話|でんわ}します。 → ✅ すぐ{f|終|お}わるので、<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>{f|電話|でんわ}します。
  <code>{f|今度|こんど}</code> pushes the call to another occasion. For "later today",
  it has to be `後で`.

## Practice

**1.** シャワーを{f|浴|あ}び____{f|後|あと}で、ビールを{f|飲|の}みます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

シャワーを{f|浴|あ}び<b>た</b>{f|後|あと}で、ビールを{f|飲|の}みます。

The verb in front of `後で` is always in the た-form, even though the sentence
describes a habit and ends in the present `ます`. The た marks the shower as
complete relative to the beer, not relative to now.

</details>

**2.** {f|十分|じゅっぷん}（{f|後|あと}で・{f|後|ご}に）{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|始|はじ}まります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|十分|じゅっぷん}<b>{f|後|ご}に</b>{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|始|はじ}まります。

A measured quantity of time takes the ご reading and the particle `に`.
❌<code>{f|十分|じゅっぷん}{f|後|あと}で</code> is wrong on both counts, and native
listeners notice the reading before they notice the particle.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'll explain the details later - let's eat first."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|詳|くわ}しいことは<b>{f|後|あと}で</b>{f|説明|せつめい}します。まず{f|食|た}べましょう。

`後で` on its own is the everyday adverb "later", and it is exactly right for
pushing something to the end of the same afternoon. <code>{f|今度|こんど}</code> would
mean the explanation comes on a different day.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べる{f|後|あと}で、{f|公園|こうえん}を{f|散歩|さんぽ}しませんか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べ<b>た</b>{f|後|あと}で、{f|公園|こうえん}を{f|散歩|さんぽ}しませんか。

Only the た-form goes in front of `後で`. The invitation is still about the future,
but the た is measuring the lunch against the walk, not against the present
moment.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|後|あと}でご{f|連絡|れんらく}します。
(b) {f|後|のち}ほどご{f|連絡|れんらく}いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both promise a message with no time attached. (a) is ordinary polite Japanese and
fits a colleague, a friend, or a shop you deal with often. (b) is the same promise
in business register: `後ほど` is the formal word for "later", and `いたします`
lowers the speaker. You would hear (b) from a call centre or a receptionist, and
it would sound stiff between friends.

</details>
