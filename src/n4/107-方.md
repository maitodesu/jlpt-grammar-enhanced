# ～方 - way of doing

**Meaning** · the way of doing something - how to do it, or the manner in which someone does it

Read `かた`. Attach it to a verb's ます-stem and you get a noun:
<code>{f|作|つく}り{f|方|かた}</code> "how to make it",
<code>{f|話|はな}し{f|方|かた}</code> "the way someone speaks". This is the single
most productive noun-building suffix at N4, and it turns any verb you already know
into something you can ask about, teach, learn and criticise.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + {f|方|かた} | {f|使|つか}う → {f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた} |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + {f|方|かた} | {f|開|あ}ける → {f|開|あ}け{f|方|かた} |
| Verb (する) | the noun is {f|仕方|しかた} | する → {f|仕方|しかた} |
| Noun + する | Noun + の + {f|仕方|しかた} | {f|予約|よやく}する → {f|予約|よやく}の{f|仕方|しかた} |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | {f|来|き} + {f|方|かた} | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|き}{f|方|かた}（rare） |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the object particle `を` becomes `の`.
{f|漢字|かんじ}<b>を</b>{f|書|か}く → {f|漢字|かんじ}<b>の</b>{f|書|か}き{f|方|かた}.
Nothing in the phrase is a verb any more, so nothing can take an object.

</div>

A destination `に`／`へ` usually survives, with `の` added after it:
{f|空港|くうこう}へ{f|行|い}く → {f|空港|くうこう}への{f|行|い}き{f|方|かた}. Some
fixed pairs simply drop the particle instead - バスに{f|乗|の}る is normally
バスの{f|乗|の}り{f|方|かた} - so learn the common ones as phrases.

The finished word is an ordinary noun and behaves like one:
{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}<b>が</b>{f|分|わ}からない、{f|作|つく}り{f|方|かた}<b>を</b>{f|習|なら}う、{f|話|はな}し{f|方|かた}<b>で</b>{f|分|わ}かる。

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>方</code> is only one of the things a ます-stem can do. The bare stem is
itself a noun-maker, and a good deal of everyday vocabulary is built that way:
{f|動|うご}く → {f|動|うご}き, {f|始|はじ}まる → {f|始|はじ}まり,
{f|帰|かえ}る → {f|帰|かえ}り, {f|考|かんが}える → {f|考|かんが}え,
{f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}み. Not every verb yields a usable one, so treat these
as words to collect rather than forms to generate - but when you meet a noun that
looks like a ます-stem, it usually is one.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>
この{f|漢字|かんじ}の<b>{f|読|よ}み{f|方|かた}</b>が{f|分|わ}かりません。
I don't know how to read this kanji.
</pre>

<pre>

Curry is easier to make than you'd think.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼女|かのじょ}の<b>{f|話|はな}し{f|方|かた}</b>はとてもていねいです。
The way she speaks is very polite.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|空港|くうこう}への<b>{f|行|い}き{f|方|かた}</b>をスマホで{f|調|しら}べた。

</pre>

<pre>
おはしの<b>{f|持|も}ち{f|方|かた}</b>は{f|子|こ}どものときに{f|習|なら}いました。
I learned how to hold chopsticks when I was a child.
</pre>

<pre>
パソコンの{f|調子|ちょうし}が{f|悪|わる}いので、{f|修理|しゅうり}の<b>{f|仕方|しかた}</b>をネットで{f|探|さが}しています。
My computer is playing up, so I'm hunting online for how to repair it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|同|おな}じことを{f|伝|つた}えても、<b>{f|言|い}い{f|方|かた}</b>によって{f|相手|あいて}の{f|気持|きも}ちは{f|変|か}わります。
Even when you convey the same thing, how you put it changes how the other person feels.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>～方</code> splits into two closely related senses, and it is worth knowing which one
you are producing.

- **Method** - <code>{f|作|つく}り{f|方|かた}</code>,
  <code>{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}</code>, <code>{f|行|い}き{f|方|かた}</code>,
  <code>{f|直|なお}し{f|方|かた}</code>. These answer "how do I…?" and are what you
  ask a shop assistant or a recipe.
- **Manner** - <code>{f|話|はな}し{f|方|かた}</code>,
  <code>{f|歩|ある}き{f|方|かた}</code>, <code>{f|食|た}べ{f|方|かた}</code>,
  <code>{f|笑|わら}い{f|方|かた}</code>. These describe someone's personal style and
  almost always come with an evaluation attached:
  {f|食|た}べ{f|方|かた}がきれいですね。

Because the manner sense evaluates a person, it can carry criticism.
{f|言|い}い{f|方|かた}が{f|悪|わる}かった - "I put that badly" - is a standard
apology, and あの{f|人|ひと}の{f|話|はな}し{f|方|かた}はちょっと… is a soft way of
saying you dislike how someone talks. Nothing about the suffix itself is rude; the judgement
comes from the adjective you pair it with.

The suffix is register-neutral and equally normal in speech and writing.
<code>{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}を{f|教|おし}えていただけますか</code> is a polite
request; <code>{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}{f|分|わ}かる?</code> is what you say to a
friend. Only the surrounding grammar changes.

One high-frequency idiom: <code>{f|仕方|しかた}がない</code> - "it can't be
helped", "there's nothing for it". In casual speech this contracts to
<code>{f|仕方|しかた}ない</code> or, very commonly, しょうがない. Learn it as a set
phrase; the <code>方</code> inside it is the same word, but the meaning of the whole is
fixed.

## Contrast

### ～{f|方|かた} vs {f|方法|ほうほう}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～方</code> is built out of a specific verb and is everyday.
<code>{f|方法|ほうほう}</code> is a free-standing noun meaning "method", and it is
more abstract and more formal.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|問題|もんだい}の<b>{f|解|と}き{f|方|かた}</b>を{f|教|おし}えてください。
Please show me how to solve this problem.
</pre>

Compare この{f|問題|もんだい}を{f|解決|かいけつ}する{f|方法|ほうほう}を{f|考|かんが}えましょう
 - "let's think of a way to resolve this issue." The <code>方</code> version is about the
concrete steps of one action; <code>{f|方法|ほうほう}</code> takes a whole clause in
front of it and suits reports, meetings and instructions. In a manual you will
often see both: {f|支払|しはら}い{f|方法|ほうほう} for the section heading and
{f|払|はら}い{f|方|かた} in the shop.

### ～{f|方|かた} vs どうやって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - どうやって **asks** the question. <code>～方</code> packages the answer as
a noun you can then do things to.

</div>

<pre>
このお{f|茶|ちゃ}の<b>いれ{f|方|かた}</b>は{f|少|すこ}し{f|難|むずか}しいです。
The way you brew this tea is a little tricky.
</pre>

You could ask このお{f|茶|ちゃ}はどうやっていれますか and get the same information,
but only the noun can be the subject of {f|難|むずか}しい, the object of
{f|習|なら}う, or the thing you {f|忘|わす}れた. That is the whole point of
nominalising: どうやって is a question word and cannot sit in a noun slot.

### {f|方|かた} vs {f|方|ほう}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - after a ます-stem, <code>方</code> is always **かた**. The `ほう` reading
belongs to the comparison word <code>～の{f|方|ほう}が</code>, which follows a noun
or a plain-form clause, not a stem.

</div>

<pre>
バスの<b>{f|乗|の}り{f|方|かた}</b>が{f|分|わ}からなくて、{f|駅員|えきいん}さんに{f|聞|き}きました。
I didn't know how to ride the bus, so I asked a station attendant.
</pre>

Set that against {f|電車|でんしゃ}の{f|方|ほう}が{f|速|はや}いです, "the train is
faster", where <code>方</code> means "side" and is read ほう. There is a third word here too:
<code>方</code> read かた as a free noun is the polite word for a person - 
あの{f|方|かた}はどなたですか. Same character, three jobs; the stem test sorts out
the suffix instantly.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|読|よ}む<b>{f|方|かた}</b> → ✅ {f|読|よ}<b>み{f|方|かた}</b>
  <code>方</code> attaches to the ます-stem, like `やすい` and `すぎる`. The dictionary form
  before <code>方</code> produces the *ほう* comparison word instead, which means something
  else entirely.

- ❌ {f|漢字|かんじ}<b>を</b>{f|書|か}き{f|方|かた} → ✅ {f|漢字|かんじ}<b>の</b>{f|書|か}き{f|方|かた}
  The compound is a noun, so its former object is attached with `の`.

- ❌ {f|運転|うんてん}<b>し{f|方|かた}</b> → ✅ {f|運転|うんてん}<b>の{f|仕方|しかた}</b>
  A noun + する verb uses the lexicalised noun {f|仕方|しかた}, linked with `の`.

- ❌ この{f|言葉|ことば}の{f|読|よ}み<b>{f|方|ほう}</b>を{f|教|おし}えてください。 → ✅ …{f|読|よ}み<b>{f|方|かた}</b>…
  A written mistake becomes a spoken one the moment you read it aloud. On a
  ます-stem the reading is かた, always.

## Practice

**1.** すみません、この{f|料理|りょうり}の{f|作|つく}____を{f|教|おし}えてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|作|つく}<b>り{f|方|かた}</b>を{f|教|おし}えてください

The stem of {f|作|つく}る is {f|作|つく}り, from {f|作|つく}ります, and <code>方</code> goes
straight onto it. Note the `の` linking it to the dish, where the plain verb would
have taken `を`.

</details>

**2.** ネットで（{f|予約|よやく}の{f|仕方|しかた}・{f|予約|よやく}し{f|方|かた}）が{f|分|わ}かりません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ネットで<b>{f|予約|よやく}の{f|仕方|しかた}</b>が{f|分|わ}かりません。

<code>{f|予約|よやく}する</code> is a noun + する verb, and that type takes the
lexicalised noun {f|仕方|しかた}, joined on with `の`. The stem of する does not
appear bare in front of <code>方</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Could you tell me how to get to the station?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|駅|えき}への<b>{f|行|い}き{f|方|かた}</b>を{f|教|おし}えていただけますか。

<code>{f|駅|えき}へ{f|行|い}く</code> keeps its destination particle, and `の` is
added so the phrase can modify the noun. <code>{f|教|おし}えていただけますか</code>
is the polite way to ask a stranger.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|日本語|にほんご}を{f|話|はな}す{f|方|かた}を{f|習|なら}いたいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|日本語|にほんご}<b>の{f|話|はな}し{f|方|かた}</b>を{f|習|なら}いたいです。

Two corrections at once: the dictionary form {f|話|はな}す becomes the stem
{f|話|はな}し, and the object particle `を` on {f|日本語|にほんご} becomes `の`,
because there is no longer a verb to take an object.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|字|じ}の{f|書|か}き{f|方|かた}はきれいです。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|字|じ}を{f|書|か}くのが{f|上手|じょうず}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) puts the manner itself under the microscope: his stroke order, his hand, the
shape of his writing. <code>方</code> names a style, so the compliment is about *how* the
action is carried out.

(b) uses the N5 nominaliser `の` on the whole action and rates his ability at it.
It says he is good at writing, without committing to anything about what the
result looks like. Use <code>方</code> when the way of doing it is the point, and `の` when
the doing is.

</details>
