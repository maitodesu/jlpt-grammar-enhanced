# お○○する

**Meaning** · the general-purpose humble frame - describes *your own* action while lowering you

`お○○する` is the mirror image of `お○○になる`. Same prefix, same ます-stem, but
`する` on the end instead of `になる`, and the whole thing points the other way: it
lowers the speaker, and by lowering the speaker it raises the person the action is
done for. This is {f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご}, and it is the half of
{f|敬語|けいご} that learners most often leave out.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | お + ます-stem + する | {f|持|も}ちます → お{f|持|も}ちする |
| Verb (る-verb) | お + ます-stem + する | {f|届|とど}けます → お{f|届|とど}けする |
| する-verb, Chinese-derived root | ご + noun + する | {f|説明|せつめい}する → ご{f|説明|せつめい}する |
| する-verb, native root | お + noun + する | {f|手伝|てつだ}いする → お{f|手伝|てつだ}いする |
| More humble | お／ご + stem + いたす | お{f|持|も}ちいたします |
| Most formal (written) | お／ご + stem + {f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げる | ご{f|案内|あんない}{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます |
| Ongoing / standing state | お／ご + stem + しております | お{f|待|ま}ちしております |
| Verb with a set humble word | frame **not** used | {f|見|み}る → {f|拝見|はいけん}する |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the frame needs a **recipient**: somebody the action is done
to, for, or in front of, whom you respect. お{f|持|も}ちします works because you
carry it *for them*. ❌お{f|寝|ね}します and ❌お{f|散歩|さんぽ}します do not,
because nobody is on the receiving end.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
お{f|荷物|にもつ}を<b>お{f|持|も}ちします</b>。
Let me take your bag.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|傘|かさ}を<b>お{f|貸|か}しします</b>。
I'll lend you this umbrella.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}を<b>ご{f|説明|せつめい}します</b>。
I'll explain how it works.
</pre>

<pre>
ロビーで<b>お{f|待|ま}ちしております</b>。
I'll be waiting in the lobby.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|書類|しょるい}は{f|明日|あした}までに<b>お{f|届|とど}けします</b>。
I'll get the documents to you by tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|少|すこ}し<b>お{f|尋|たず}ねしたい</b>ことがあるのですが、よろしいでしょうか。
There's something I'd like to ask you, if I may.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}から{f|会場|かいじょう}まで、{f|係|かかり}の{f|者|もの}が<b>ご{f|案内|あんない}します</b>。
A member of staff will show you from reception through to the hall.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しいサービスにつきましては、あらためて{f|資料|しりょう}を<b>お{f|送|おく}りします</b>ので、ご{f|確認|かくにん}いただければ{f|幸|さいわ}いです。
I'll send you the material on the new service separately, so I'd be grateful if you would take a look.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Everything here belongs to work, service and formal dealings with strangers. In a
shop, on a client call, in a business email it is the normal way to describe what
you are about to do; between friends it is not, and お{f|貸|か}ししようか to a
classmate sounds like you are role-playing a hotel clerk.

The ladder inside the frame is worth knowing:

- **お{f|送|おく}りします** - standard humble. Fine for almost everything.
- **お{f|送|おく}りいたします** - `いたす` is itself the humble `する`, so this is
  one notch lower and one notch more formal. Common in business speech.
- **お{f|送|おく}り{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます** - ceremonial and written. Notices,
  formal letters, wedding invitations.

Treat that as a gradient rather than three fixed rungs: most speakers move between
the first two without thinking about it, and the third is rare enough that using
it in conversation would draw attention.

`しております` is the humble `～ている`, and it is everywhere in service Japanese
because so much of that language describes standing states rather than single
acts - 
お{f|待|ま}ちしております,
ご{f|連絡|れんらく}をお{f|待|ま}ちしております,
お{f|問|と}い{f|合|あ}わせをお{f|受|う}けしております.

Two limits to keep straight. First, the frame never describes somebody else's
action: ❌{f|先生|せんせい}がお{f|話|はな}しします turns your teacher into your
servant. Second, an action with no human on the other end will not take it, which
is why "I'll go home early today" cannot be ❌{f|早|はや}くお{f|帰|かえ}りします - 
say {f|失礼|しつれい}します or
{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}らせていただきます instead.

## Contrast

### お○○する vs お○○になる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical up to the last word. `する` = my action, lowered.
`になる` = their action, raised.

</div>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|新|あたら}しい{f|住所|じゅうしょ}を<b>お{f|知|し}らせします</b>。
I'll let sensei know my new address.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}が{f|新|あたら}しい{f|住所|じゅうしょ}を<b>お{f|知|し}らせになりました</b>。
Sensei let us know his new address.
</pre>

Get the pair backwards and you say the opposite of what you mean. It is worth
practising them as a drill: pick a verb, build both, and say out loud who is doing
which.

### お○○する vs ～させていただく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `お○○する` says "I'll do it for you"; `～させていただく` says
"I'll take the liberty, with your permission".

</div>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|予定|よてい}を<b>ご{f|連絡|れんらく}します</b>。
I'll let you know next week's schedule.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本日|ほんじつ}は{f|早|はや}めに<b>{f|閉|し}めさせていただきます</b>。
We'll be closing early today.
</pre>

`させていただく` frames the action as something you are permitted to do, so it fits
things that affect the listener without being *for* them - closing early, taking
leave, declining. It has spread so far in recent business Japanese that it is
regularly criticised as overused; where the action really is a service you are
performing, `お○○する` is the cleaner choice.

### お○○する vs plain ます

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ます` is {f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご} only: polite speech, no
humility. `お○○する` adds the humility.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}{f|資料|しりょう}を<b>{f|送|おく}ります</b>。
I'll send the material tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}{f|資料|しりょう}を<b>お{f|送|おく}りします</b>。
I'll send the material to you tomorrow.
</pre>

Both are polite; neither is rude. The first is what you write to a colleague, the
second to a customer. This is the distinction to keep hold of when people say
{f|敬語|けいご} is "polite Japanese": です／ます politeness and
{f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご} humility are separate dials, and you can turn one
without the other.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}が{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|内容|ないよう}を<b>ご{f|説明|せつめい}します</b>。（to a colleague）→ ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}が{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|内容|ないよう}を<b>ご{f|説明|せつめい}になります</b>。
  The frame lowers whoever is doing the verb. Applied to your department head in
  front of colleagues, it demotes him.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}が<b>お{f|説明|せつめい}します</b>。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}が<b>ご{f|説明|せつめい}します</b>。
  {f|説明|せつめい} is a Chinese-derived root, so the prefix is `ご`. Compare
  ご{f|連絡|れんらく}します, ご{f|報告|ほうこく}します, ご{f|案内|あんない}します.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}の{f|論文|ろんぶん}を<b>お{f|見|み}しました</b>。 → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}の{f|論文|ろんぶん}を<b>{f|拝見|はいけん}しました</b>。
  One-mora stems are shut out of the frame, and {f|見|み}る has its own humble
  verb.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|用事|ようじ}があるので、{f|早|はや}く<b>お{f|帰|かえ}りします</b>。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|用事|ようじ}があるので、<b>お{f|先|さき}に{f|失礼|しつれい}します</b>。
  Going home is not done *for* anybody, so there is no recipient for the humility
  to reach. Worse, お{f|帰|かえ}り is strongly associated with the honorific, so
  the sentence sounds as if you are honouring yourself.

## Practice

**1.** {f|重|おも}そうですね。{f|私|わたし}が____{f|持|も}ち____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|重|おも}そうですね。{f|私|わたし}が<b>お{f|持|も}ちします</b>。

`お` + ます-stem + `する`. The action is done for the listener, which is exactly
the condition the frame needs. お{f|持|も}ちいたします is a shade more formal and
equally correct.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|係|かかり}の{f|者|もの}が{f|後|あと}ほど（ご{f|連絡|れんらく}します・ご{f|連絡|れんらく}になります）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|係|かかり}の{f|者|もの}が{f|後|あと}ほど<b>ご{f|連絡|れんらく}します</b>。

<code>{f|係|かかり}の{f|者|もの}</code> is on your own side, so the sentence lowers.
ご{f|連絡|れんらく}になります would honour your own colleague in front of the
person you are speaking to.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, as a hotel receptionist: "I'll show you to your room."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お{f|部屋|へや}まで<b>ご{f|案内|あんない}いたします</b>。

{f|案内|あんない} takes `ご`. `いたします` rather than `します` because hotel
speech sits at the formal end of the range; ご{f|案内|あんない}します is also
correct and slightly lighter.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|社長|しゃちょう}はこれから{f|新|あたら}しい{f|計画|けいかく}についてお{f|話|はな}しします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|社長|しゃちょう}はこれから{f|新|あたら}しい{f|計画|けいかく}についてお{f|話|はな}し<b>になります</b>。

The subject is the president, so the frame must raise rather than lower. Note
that if you were saying this to an outside audience about your own company's
president, you would drop the honorific again and say
{f|社長|しゃちょう}の{f|佐藤|さとう}がご{f|説明|せつめい}いたします.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|窓口|まどぐち}でお{f|預|あず}かりします。
(b) {f|窓口|まどぐち}で{f|預|あず}かります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both mean the counter will hold the item, and both are polite in form. (a) uses
the humble frame, so the speaker is lowered and the customer raised; it is the
sentence a bank clerk or a cloakroom attendant says, and the courtesy is aimed at
a specific person standing in front of them.

(b) is only {f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご}. It reports the arrangement neutrally, the
way a colleague explains a procedure to another colleague, or a sign states a
rule. Nothing in it honours the listener.

</details>
