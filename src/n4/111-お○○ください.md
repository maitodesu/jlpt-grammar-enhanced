# お○○ください

**Meaning** · "please do ○○" - an honorific request, the form used to customers, guests and audiences

`お○○ください` asks the listener to do something while raising them. It is the
{f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご} counterpart of `～てください`: same job, but the listener
is honoured rather than simply addressed politely. Every announcement you hear on
a Japanese train, in a lift and in a shop is built out of this frame.

## Formation

`ください` is the imperative of `くださる`, the honorific of `くれる`. Put it after
`お` + the ます-stem.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | お + ます-stem + ください | {f|待|ま}ちます → お{f|待|ま}ちください |
| Verb (る-verb) | お + ます-stem + ください | {f|調|しら}べます → お{f|調|しら}べください |
| する-verb, Chinese-derived root | ご + noun + ください | {f|確認|かくにん}する → ご{f|確認|かくにん}ください |
| する-verb, native root | お + noun + ください | {f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みする → お{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みください |
| Set honorific verb | fixed form | {f|見|み}る → ご{f|覧|らん}ください |
| Extra-polite service form | + ませ | お{f|待|ま}ちくださいませ |
| Negative ("please don't") | frame **not** used | ご{f|遠慮|えんりょ}ください／～ないでください |

<div class="note">

**Note** - one-mora stems are shut out here for the same reason as in
`お○○になる`: ❌お{f|見|み}ください、❌お{f|来|き}ください do not exist. Use
ご{f|覧|らん}ください and お{f|越|こ}しください.

</div>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

This frame is service and announcement language, and it carries a faint flavour of
*instruction*. A station guard, a shop assistant, a nurse calling a patient and a
website's help page all use it. Between friends it is bizarre - you do not tell a
classmate ❌ちょっとお{f|待|ま}ちください unless you are joking.

The instructional flavour matters when you want a personal favour. `ください` is
still an imperative underneath, so a bare お{f|書|か}きください to your boss can
land like a directive from below. For asking a superior for something, shift to a
question form:

- お{f|書|か}きいただけますか - "could you write it?"
- お{f|書|か}きいただけませんでしょうか - the softest, and standard in email.
- {f|書|か}いていただけると{f|助|たす}かります - "it would help me if you would."

`ませ` on the end（お{f|待|ま}ちくださいませ、いらっしゃいませ）belongs to shop and
hotel speech specifically. It is warm and formal at once, and it is heard far more
from staff than from anyone else.

Two words about お{f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がりください and お{f|求|もと}めください.
Strictly, {f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がる is already honorific, so adding `お` makes a
double layer; usage guides nonetheless list these as established by custom, and
you will hear them constantly. Treat them as fixed phrases rather than as models
for building new ones.

One more tendency worth knowing: many speakers avoid お{f|座|すわ}りください in
favour of おかけください, because お{f|座|すわ}り on its own is what you say to a
dog. Plenty of hospitals and offices say お{f|座|すわ}りください anyway, so it is a
preference rather than a rule - but おかけください is never the wrong choice.

## Contrast

### お○○ください vs ～てください

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～てください` is polite; `お○○ください` is polite **and**
honorific. The first addresses the listener courteously, the second raises them.

</div>

<pre>
ここに{f|名前|なまえ}を<b>{f|書|か}いてください</b>。
Please write your name here.
</pre>

<pre>
こちらにお{f|名前|なまえ}を<b>お{f|書|か}きください</b>。
Please write your name here.
</pre>

The first is what a teacher says to a class, or a friend to a friend. The second
is what a bank clerk says to a customer - notice how `ここ` becomes `こちら` and
{f|名前|なまえ} takes its prefix. {f|敬語|けいご} rarely arrives alone; the whole
sentence shifts register together.

### お○○ください vs お○○いただけますか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ください` tells; `いただけますか` asks. When the favour is for
*your* benefit, ask.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}までに<b>ご{f|返事|へんじ}ください</b>。
Please reply by tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}までに<b>ご{f|返事|へんじ}いただけますでしょうか</b>。
Might I ask you to reply by tomorrow?
</pre>

Both are polite. The first states what needs to happen and suits a notice or a
form; the second leaves the decision with the listener and suits a request to a
client or a superior. `いただく` is {f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご}, so the second
sentence lowers you at the same time as it raises them - which is why it feels so
much softer.

### お○○ください vs ご{f|遠慮|えんりょ}ください

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the frame has no negative. To ask someone *not* to do
something, use a different verb: ご{f|遠慮|えんりょ}ください or
お{f|控|ひか}えください.

</div>

<pre>
{f|車内|しゃない}でのお{f|電話|でんわ}は<b>ご{f|遠慮|えんりょ}ください</b>。
Please refrain from making phone calls on board.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|写真|しゃしん}の{f|撮影|さつえい}は<b>お{f|控|ひか}えください</b>。
Please refrain from taking photographs.
</pre>

There is no ❌お{f|話|はな}しにならないでください in ordinary use. Japanese public
notices prefer to name a polite verb of refraining rather than to negate the
request - it reads as an appeal to the listener's discretion instead of a ban.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}{f|会社|かいしゃ}に<b>お{f|来|き}ください</b>。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}{f|会社|かいしゃ}に<b>お{f|越|こ}しください</b>。
  <code>{f|来|く}る</code> has no place in the frame. Use お{f|越|こ}しください,
  おいでください or いらしてください.

- ❌ こちらのボタンを<b>ご{f|確認|かくにん}してください</b>。 → ✅ こちらのボタンを<b>ご{f|確認|かくにん}ください</b>。
  `ご○○する` is the *humble* frame, so <code>ご{f|確認|かくにん}してください</code> tells the
  listener to humble themselves. Drop the `して`.

- ❌ メニューを<b>お{f|見|み}ください</b>。 → ✅ メニューを<b>ご{f|覧|らん}ください</b>。
  One-mora stem, dedicated honorific verb. The same goes for
  お{f|食|た}べください → お{f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がりください.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、この{f|書類|しょるい}に<b>おサインください</b>。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、この{f|書類|しょるい}に<b>ご{f|署名|しょめい}いただけますでしょうか</b>。
  Two problems: loanwords do not take the prefix, and a direct `ください` to your
  own boss for your own paperwork is too flat. Ask with `いただけますか`.

## Practice

**1.** {f|危|あぶ}ないですので、{f|白線|はくせん}の{f|内側|うちがわ}まで____{f|下|さ}がり____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|危|あぶ}ないですので、{f|白線|はくせん}の{f|内側|うちがわ}まで<b>お{f|下|さ}がりください</b>。

`お` + ます-stem + `ください`. <code>{f|下|さ}がります</code> gives the stem
<code>{f|下|さ}がり</code>. This is the standard platform announcement, and it is
honorific because it is addressed to passengers.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|資料|しりょう}を（ご{f|覧|らん}ください・{f|拝見|はいけん}ください）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|資料|しりょう}を<b>ご{f|覧|らん}ください</b>。

<code>{f|拝見|はいけん}する</code> is {f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご} - it lowers the
person doing the looking. Telling the listener to {f|拝見|はいけん} orders them to
be humble towards you, which is the opposite of what a request should do.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Please use the lift on the left."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|左|ひだり}のエレベーターを<b>ご{f|利用|りよう}ください</b>。

{f|利用|りよう} is a Chinese-derived する-verb root, so it takes `ご` and the
`する` disappears entirely: ご{f|利用|りよう}ください, not
❌ご{f|利用|りよう}してください.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|館内|かんない}ではおタバコをお{f|吸|す}いにならないでください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|館内|かんない}での{f|喫煙|きつえん}は<b>ご{f|遠慮|えんりょ}ください</b>。

Two faults. Loanwords do not take the polite prefix, and the honorific frame has
no natural negative - Japanese notices name a verb of refraining instead.
{f|館内|かんない}ではタバコをお{f|吸|す}いにならないでください is understandable but
sounds clumsy next to the fixed phrase.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|少|すこ}々お{f|待|ま}ちください。
(b) {f|少|すこ}々お{f|待|ま}ちいただけますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the standard service line - a receptionist, a phone operator, a waiter. It
is honorific and perfectly courteous, but it *tells* the listener what will
happen, which is fine when the waiting is part of the process.

(b) turns the same content into a question, so the listener is free to say no. It
is what you use when the delay is your fault, or when you are asking a favour of
someone senior. Between colleagues (a) would sound oddly official; from a shop
assistant (b) can sound over-apologetic. Match the form to who owns the delay.

</details>
