# 必要

**Meaning** · necessary; needed - the word itself, not the pattern

Read {f|必要|ひつよう}. It is a noun that behaves as a な-adjective: something
<code>が{f|必要|ひつよう}だ</code>「is needed」, and a <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}な</code> thing is a required
one. The verb pattern <code>～{f|必要|ひつよう}がある</code> is built out of this word, but the
word does plenty of work on its own, and getting its particles right is what stops
learners writing English with Japanese vocabulary in it.

## Formation

The thing that is needed takes `が`, not `を` - <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> is not a verb and
has no object.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (predicate) | Noun + が + {f|必要|ひつよう}だ／です | ビザ → ビザが{f|必要|ひつよう}です |
| Before a noun | {f|必要|ひつよう} + な + Noun | {f|道具|どうぐ} → {f|必要|ひつよう}な{f|道具|どうぐ} |
| Purpose | Noun + に + {f|必要|ひつよう}な + Noun | {f|生活|せいかつ} → {f|生活|せいかつ}に{f|必要|ひつよう}なもの |
| Negative (casual) | {f|必要|ひつよう}ない／{f|必要|ひつよう}じゃない | それは{f|必要|ひつよう}ない |
| Negative (polite) | {f|必要|ひつよう}ありません／{f|必要|ひつよう}ではありません | それは{f|必要|ひつよう}ありません |
| Past | {f|必要|ひつよう}だった／{f|必要|ひつよう}なかった | {f|時間|じかん}が{f|必要|ひつよう}だった |
| Becoming | {f|必要|ひつよう}になる | お{f|金|かね}が{f|必要|ひつよう}になった |
| Verb instead of noun | dictionary form + {f|必要|ひつよう}がある | {f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}む{f|必要|ひつよう}がある |
| Formal verb | Noun + を + {f|必要|ひつよう}とする | {f|助|たす}けを{f|必要|ひつよう}とする{f|人|ひと} |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> is a な-adjective, so it never takes `くない`
or `かった`. The negative is <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}じゃない</code>／<code>{f|必要|ひつよう}ない</code>, and
the past is <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}だった</code>.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the written antonym is <code>{f|不要|ふよう}</code>, which you will meet on forms and
labels rather than in speech: <code>{f|記入|きにゅう}{f|不要|ふよう}</code>「no entry
required」, <code>{f|返却|へんきゃく}{f|不要|ふよう}</code>「no need to return this」.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
パスポートが<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}です</b>。
You'll need your passport.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}な</b>ものだけ{f|持|も}って{f|来|き}てください。
Please bring only what you need.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|説明|せつめい}は<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}ない</b>よ。
That explanation isn't necessary.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みに<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}な</b>{f|書類|しょるい}は{f|三|みっ}つです。
There are three documents required for the application.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|急|きゅう}に{f|現金|げんきん}が<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}になった</b>。
I suddenly found myself needing cash.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|仕事|しごと}に{f|経験|けいけん}は<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}ではありません</b>。
No experience is required for this job.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}{f|暮|ぐ}らしを{f|始|はじ}めるとき、{f|思|おも}っていたより{f|多|おお}くのものが<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}だった</b>。
When I started living on my own, I needed more things than I had expected.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|災害|さいがい}のときに{f|本当|ほんとう}に<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}な</b>のは、{f|水|みず}と{f|情報|じょうほう}だと{f|言|い}われています。
What you really need in a disaster, they say, is water and information.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> is a Sino-Japanese word, and like most of them it leans formal
and slightly abstract. It is completely ordinary in polite speech, business email,
notices and writing. In casual conversation, though, Japanese usually reaches for
the plain verb `いる` instead: a friend asks <code>{f|袋|ふくろ}いる？</code>, not
<code>{f|袋|ふくろ}が{f|必要|ひつよう}ですか</code>. At a convenience store counter you will
hear both, because staff speech is formal.

The negatives are worth separating. <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}ない</code> is short, everyday and
can sound abrupt - <code>そんなの{f|必要|ひつよう}ない</code> is close to "we don't need
that rubbish." <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}ありません</code> is the neutral polite version and the one
to use with strangers. <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}ではありません</code> is stiffer again and belongs
in writing. As a refusal at a shop counter, the standard phrase is not any of these
but <code>{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}です</code> or <code>{f|結構|けっこう}です</code>.

<code>{f|必要|ひつよう}とする</code> is a genuine verb and a register marker: it appears in
journalism, official documents and formal description - 
<code>{f|支援|しえん}を{f|必要|ひつよう}としている{f|地域|ちいき}</code>「areas that require
assistance」. Using it in conversation would sound like reading a press release.

One more thing to notice: <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> describes what a *purpose* demands. It
carries no warmth. That is why it is the right word for documents, tools, time and
money, and the wrong word for people - see the contrast below.

## Contrast

### {f|必要|ひつよう} vs いる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different word class and different register.
`いる` is a verb and casual; <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> is a な-adjective and formal.

</div>

<pre>
レシート、<b>いる</b>？
D'you want the receipt?
</pre>

<pre>
レシートは<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}ですか</b>。
Would you like a receipt?
</pre>

Both take their object with `が` (or with no particle at all in speech), which is
the one thing they share structurally. `いる` - written {f|要|い}る, though usually
left in kana - is a う-verb: `いります`, `いらない`, `いった`. Beware that its past
and て-forms collide with <code>{f|入|はい}る</code>, <code>{f|行|い}く</code> and the existence verb
`いる`, which is why it is far more common in the non-past.

`いる` is also limited to things. For an *action* you need
<code>～{f|必要|ひつよう}がある</code> or one of the obligation forms: there is no
❌<code>{f|行|い}くがいる</code>.

### {f|必要|ひつよう} vs {f|大切|たいせつ}・{f|大事|だいじ}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> = required for a purpose.
<code>{f|大切|たいせつ}</code>／<code>{f|大事|だいじ}</code> = important, precious, valued. Do not use
<code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> about people.

</div>

<pre>
{f|家族|かぞく}はとても<b>{f|大切|たいせつ}です</b>。
My family means a great deal to me.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|仕事|しごと}には{f|集中力|しゅうちゅうりょく}が<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}です</b>。
This job requires concentration.
</pre>

Swap them and both sentences go wrong in interesting ways.
<code>{f|家族|かぞく}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です</code> sounds like you are shopping for one - 
the word treats its object as a resource. And
<code>{f|集中力|しゅうちゅうりょく}が{f|大切|たいせつ}です</code> is fine but says something
milder: concentration *matters*, rather than the job being impossible without it.

<code>{f|大事|だいじ}</code> is the more conversational of the two importance words;
<code>{f|大切|たいせつ}</code> is a shade warmer and more careful. Both are な-adjectives, like
<code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> itself.

### {f|必要|ひつよう}だ vs {f|必要|ひつよう}がある

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a **noun** is needed → <code>が{f|必要|ひつよう}だ</code>. An **action** is
needed → <code>～する{f|必要|ひつよう}がある</code>.

</div>

<pre>
{f|予約|よやく}が<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}です</b>。
A reservation is required.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|予約|よやく}する<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}があります</b>。
You need to make a reservation.
</pre>

The information is the same and the framing is not. The first names a thing the
situation demands, and would suit a sign on a door. The second describes something
the listener has to do, and suits an explanation.

A noun can be pushed into the `ある` frame with `の`
(<code>{f|予約|よやく}の{f|必要|ひつよう}があります</code>), which sounds noticeably more
bureaucratic. A verb, on the other hand, cannot use the `だ` frame at all - there is
no ❌<code>{f|予約|よやく}するが{f|必要|ひつよう}です</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|必要|ひつよう}<b>の</b>{f|書類|しょるい} → ✅ {f|必要|ひつよう}<b>な</b>{f|書類|しょるい}
  <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> is a な-adjective when it modifies a noun. `の` looks tempting
  because the word is also a noun, but it is not how the phrase is built.

- ❌ パスポート<b>を</b>{f|必要|ひつよう}です。 → ✅ パスポート<b>が</b>{f|必要|ひつよう}です。
  There is no verb here, so there is no object. The needed thing is the subject and
  takes `が`.

- ❌ この{f|靴|くつ}は{f|必要|ひつよう}<b>くない</b>。 → ✅ この{f|靴|くつ}は{f|必要|ひつよう}<b>じゃない</b>。
  Only い-adjectives negate with `くない`. <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> takes `じゃない`,
  `ではない`, or bare `ない`.

- ❌ {f|病院|びょういん}に{f|行|い}く<b>が</b>{f|必要|ひつよう}です。 → ✅ {f|病院|びょういん}に{f|行|い}く<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}があります</b>。
  A verb cannot be marked with `が`. To say an action is needed, switch to the
  <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}がある</code> frame.

- ❌（to a friend, about a plastic bag）{f|袋|ふくろ}が{f|必要|ひつよう}ですか。 → ✅ {f|袋|ふくろ}いる？
  Grammatical and oddly formal. Among friends the everyday word for needing a thing
  is `いる`.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|手続|てつづ}きに____な{f|物|もの}を{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|持|も}って{f|来|き}ました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|手続|てつづ}きに<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}</b>な{f|物|もの}を{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|持|も}って{f|来|き}ました。

Modifying a noun, <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> behaves as a な-adjective, so the connector is
`な`. The `に` marks the purpose the things are needed *for*.

</details>

**2.** ペン、（いる・{f|必要|ひつよう}でいらっしゃいますか）？ - said to a close friend.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ペン、<b>いる</b>？

Between friends the plain verb is what people use; the honorific version is not
merely over-polite, it is comic. <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> itself is not rude here, but it
still sounds like a form rather than a question about a pen.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, as a line on a website: "A credit card is not required."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

クレジットカードは<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}ありません</b>。

<code>{f|必要|ひつよう}ありません</code> is the standard polite negative and the usual wording on
notices. <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}ない</code> would be too blunt for a public-facing page, and
<code>{f|必要|ひつよう}ではございません</code> would be stiffer than most sites go.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|明日|あした}は{f|早|はや}く{f|起|お}きるが{f|必要|ひつよう}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|早|はや}く{f|起|お}きる<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}があります</b>。

`が` can only mark a noun. When what is needed is an action, the dictionary form
attaches straight to <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> and the sentence closes with `ある`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|会社|かいしゃ}には{f|君|きみ}が<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}だ</b>。
(b) この{f|会社|かいしゃ}にとって{f|君|きみ}は<b>{f|大切|たいせつ}な{f|人|ひと}だ</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the company cannot function without you. <code>{f|必要|ひつよう}</code> frames a person
as something the organisation requires, so the compliment is real but cool - it is
about what you produce. In a film this is the line a boss says to stop someone
resigning.

(b) says you are valued. <code>{f|大切|たいせつ}</code> is about worth rather than utility, and
it is the word you would use about a person if you wanted the sentence to sound
warm. Both are natural Japanese; only one of them is affectionate.

</details>
