# ～ということ - packaging a clause as a noun

**Meaning** · takes a whole sentence and hands it on as a single noun - "the fact that ～", "that ～"

`こと` on its own nominalises a verb phrase. `ということ` nominalises a **finished
statement**: the quotative `という` wraps the clause up with its own ending intact,
`だ` and all, and `こと` then turns the parcel into a noun the rest of the sentence
can take as its subject or object.

## Formation

`という` attaches to a **plain-form clause** exactly as it does after
{f|言|い}う and {f|思|おも}う. Nouns and な-adjectives keep their `だ`, which is the
whole reason the pattern exists.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + ということ | {f|来|こ}ない → {f|来|こ}ないということ |
| Verb (past) | た-form + ということ | {f|決|き}まった → {f|決|き}まったということ |
| い-adjective | plain form + ということ | {f|暑|あつ}い → {f|暑|あつ}いということ |
| な-adjective | ～**だ** + ということ | {f|無理|むり}だ → {f|無理|むり}だということ |
| Noun | Noun + **だ** + ということ | {f|中止|ちゅうし}だ → {f|中止|ちゅうし}だということ |
| Embedded question | ～か + ということ | どこで{f|会|あ}うか → どこで{f|会|あ}うかということ |
| Taking a particle | ～ということ + が／を／は | ～ということを{f|知|し}る |
| Casual speech | ってこと | {f|無理|むり}だ → {f|無理|むり}だってこと |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the `だ` is what tells the two nominalisers apart. Plain `こと`
cannot follow `だ` (❌<code>{f|中止|ちゅうし}だこと</code>), so a clause ending in a
noun or a な-adjective needs `ということ`.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the same `という` sits inside a family of expressions built the same way:
<code>という{f|話|はなし}</code>, <code>という{f|意味|いみ}</code>,
<code>という{f|理由|りゆう}</code>. `こと` is simply the most general noun you can put
there, which is why this one turns up far more often than the others.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|山田|やまだ}さんが{f|来|こ}ない<b>ということ</b>を{f|知|し}っていましたか。
Did you know that Yamada isn't coming?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}だ<b>ということ</b>を{f|聞|き}きました。
I heard that the meeting has been called off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日本|にほん}の{f|夏|なつ}が{f|暑|あつ}い<b>ということ</b>は、{f|前|まえ}から{f|知|し}っていました。
I already knew that summers in Japan are hot.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|失敗|しっぱい}しても{f|続|つづ}ける<b>ということ</b>が{f|大事|だいじ}だと{f|思|おも}います。
I think what matters is carrying on even when you fail.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}が{f|本当|ほんとう}のことを{f|話|はな}している<b>ということ</b>が、だんだん{f|分|わ}かってきました。
It gradually became clear that he was telling the truth.
</pre>

<pre>
どこで{f|待|ま}ち{f|合|あ}わせるか<b>ということ</b>を、まだ{f|決|き}めていません。
We still haven't settled the question of where to meet.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|父|ちち}が{f|若|わか}いころ{f|東京|とうきょう}に{f|住|す}んでいた<b>ということ</b>は、この{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|見|み}るまで{f|知|し}りませんでした。
I had no idea my father lived in Tokyo when he was young until I saw this photograph.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}に、{f|来週|らいしゅう}までにお{f|返事|へんじ}がほしい<b>ということ</b>を{f|伝|つた}えておいてください。
Please pass on to the manager that we would like an answer by next week.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ということ` is register-neutral but noticeably **heavy**. It is four syllables of
scaffolding, and Japanese drops it whenever the sentence can stand without it:
<code>{f|来|こ}ないと{f|聞|き}きました</code> says the same thing as
<code>{f|来|こ}ないということを{f|聞|き}きました</code> in half the space. Reach for the
full form when the clause is long, when it is doing something a bare `と` cannot do
(taking `が`, taking `は`, sitting in front of `です`), or when you want the content
held up for inspection rather than slipped past.

In casual speech it contracts to `ってこと`, and this is extremely common:
<code>{f|明日|あした}{f|休|やす}みだってこと、{f|聞|き}いてる?</code> The contraction is
conversational only - it would look wrong in a report - but it is what you will
actually hear, and learners who only know the long form miss it entirely.

Because the pattern begins with a quotative, it leans towards content that came
from **somewhere**: something said, written, decided, noticed or worked out. That
is not a rule - <code>{f|暑|あつ}いということ</code> above is nobody's utterance - but
it explains why the pattern feels natural after {f|聞|き}く, {f|知|し}る,
{f|伝|つた}える, {f|意味|いみ}する and awkward after a verb of direct perception.

One warning about the shape. Put `だ`／`です` on the end of this string and let the
sentence stop there, and you have a different pattern altogether: the hearsay
`ということだ`, "I hear that ～", which is a separate point at N3. On this page the
`ということ` is always followed by something - a particle, or a predicate that takes
it as an argument. The next section sets the two side by side.

## Contrast

### ということ vs こと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `こと` nominalises an **action**. `ということ` nominalises a
**statement**. If the clause ends in a noun or a な-adjective, only `ということ` is
available, because plain `こと` cannot follow `だ`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|走|はし}る<b>こと</b>は{f|体|からだ}にいいです。
Running every day is good for you.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}が{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|走|はし}っている<b>ということ</b>は、{f|誰|だれ}も{f|知|し}りませんでした。
Nobody knew that he ran every day.
</pre>

The first names an activity; the second names a piece of information about
somebody. Where the clause is a complete statement with a subject in it, the longer
form is the natural one. Note the difference this makes with a noun in front:
<code>{f|事故|じこ}のことを{f|聞|き}きました</code> is "I heard about the accident" - a
topic - while <code>{f|事故|じこ}だったということを{f|聞|き}きました</code> is "I heard
that it was an accident", a proposition.

### ということ vs ということだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - look at what comes **after** it. A particle means it is a noun
and the sentence is still going. `だ`／`です` closing the sentence means it is a
report: "I hear that ～".

</div>

<pre>
{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|終|お}わる<b>ということ</b>を、{f|係|かかり}の{f|人|ひと}に{f|確認|かくにん}しました。
I checked with the person in charge that the work finishes next month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|終|お}わる<b>ということです</b>。
The work finishes next month, I gather.
</pre>

Same eleven characters, two different grammars. In the first the `ということ` is the
object of {f|確認|かくにん}する - it is a noun, and it could equally take `が` or `は`.
In the second nothing follows, `です` closes the sentence, and the whole frame has
become a sentence-final marker that puts the information at arm's length; that
hearsay use is treated at N3. The one place the test needs care is a cleft
frame - <code>{f|問題|もんだい}なのは…ということです</code> - where the sentence does
end on です but the `のは` in front makes it plainly the nominaliser again.

### ということ vs the bare quotative と

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `と` feeds a clause straight into {f|言|い}う, {f|思|おも}う,
{f|聞|き}く. `ということ` makes it a noun first, so it can take a particle or be the
subject.

</div>

<pre>
{f|林|はやし}さんは{f|行|い}かない<b>と</b>{f|言|い}っていました。
Hayashi said he wasn't going.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|林|はやし}さんが{f|行|い}かない<b>ということ</b>は、みんなに{f|伝|つた}えました。
I've told everyone that Hayashi isn't going.
</pre>

With {f|言|い}う and {f|思|おも}う, `と` is shorter and better, and a learner who
writes `ということ` every time sounds laboured. But `と` cannot be marked with `は` or
`が`, and it cannot be the subject of an adjective, so the moment the clause needs
to move around the sentence, the full nominaliser is the only option.

## Common mistakes

- ❌（meaning "I heard that it was cancelled"）{f|中止|ちゅうし}のことを{f|聞|き}きました。 → ✅ {f|中止|ちゅうし}だ<b>ということ</b>を{f|聞|き}きました。
  <code>{f|中止|ちゅうし}のこと</code> is a topic - "about the cancellation". To report
  the statement itself you need the clause packaged with `という`.

- ❌ この{f|町|まち}は{f|静|しず}か<b>ということ</b>を{f|知|し}りませんでした。 → ✅ この{f|町|まち}は{f|静|しず}か<b>だということ</b>を{f|知|し}りませんでした。
  A な-adjective keeps its `だ` in front of `という`. This is the opposite of what
  happens before plain `こと`, where the same word takes `な`.

- ❌ {f|高|たか}い<b>だということ</b>が{f|問題|もんだい}です。 → ✅ {f|高|たか}い<b>ということ</b>が{f|問題|もんだい}です。
  い-adjectives never take `だ`. Only nouns and な-adjectives do.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|来|こ}ない<b>ということだ</b>を{f|知|し}らなかった。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|来|こ}ない<b>ということ</b>を{f|知|し}らなかった。
  The `だ` belongs to the sentence-final hearsay pattern. Inside a sentence, where a
  particle follows, the noun ends at `こと`.

- ❌ {f|子|こ}どもが{f|泣|な}いている<b>ということ</b>を{f|見|み}ました。 → ✅ {f|子|こ}どもが{f|泣|な}いている<b>の</b>を{f|見|み}ました。
  You cannot watch a proposition. Verbs of direct perception take `の`, never `こと`
  and never `ということ`.

## Practice

**1.** {f|田中|たなか}さんが{f|来年|らいねん}アメリカに{f|行|い}く____を、{f|昨日|きのう}{f|初|はじ}めて{f|聞|き}きました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

…アメリカに{f|行|い}く<b>ということ</b>を、{f|昨日|きのう}{f|初|はじ}めて{f|聞|き}きました。

The clause is a complete statement with its own subject, and it has to become a
noun to take `を`. <code>{f|行|い}くと{f|聞|き}きました</code> with the bare quotative
is shorter and equally correct.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|明日|あした}の{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}（だということ・なこと）が{f|今朝|けさ}{f|決|き}まりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|中止|ちゅうし}<b>だということ</b>が{f|今朝|けさ}{f|決|き}まりました。

<code>{f|中止|ちゅうし}</code> is a noun, so the clause ends in `だ`, and plain `こと`
cannot follow `だ`. ❌<code>{f|中止|ちゅうし}なこと</code> would treat it as a
な-adjective, which it is not.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I didn't know that this shop is closed on Mondays."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|店|みせ}が{f|月曜日|げつようび}は{f|休|やす}みだ<b>ということ</b>を{f|知|し}りませんでした。

<code>{f|休|やす}み</code> is a noun, so the clause keeps `だ` and needs the `という`
before `こと`. In speech you would very likely hear the contracted
<code>{f|休|やす}みだってこと</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼女|かのじょ}が{f|忙|いそが}しいだということは、{f|顔|かお}を{f|見|み}れば{f|分|わ}かります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}が{f|忙|いそが}しい<b>ということ</b>は、{f|顔|かお}を{f|見|み}れば{f|分|わ}かります。

<code>{f|忙|いそが}しい</code> is an い-adjective and takes no `だ`. The `だ` row in the
Formation table applies to nouns and な-adjectives only.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference:
(a) {f|台風|たいふう}で{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まった<b>ということ</b>を、{f|駅|えき}で{f|聞|き}きました。
(b) {f|台風|たいふう}で{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まった<b>ということです</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the pattern on this page. The `ということ` is a noun, it is marked with `を`,
and the sentence carries on to say where you heard it. You are reporting your own
act of finding out.

(b) has nothing after it: `です` closes the sentence, and the frame has become the
hearsay ending taught at N3. You are not describing an event of hearing - you are
passing the information on while keeping it at arm's length, roughly "apparently
the trains have stopped."

</details>
