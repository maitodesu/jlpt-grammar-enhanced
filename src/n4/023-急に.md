# 急に

**Meaning** · suddenly, all at once - and, of an arrangement, at short notice

<code>{f|急|きゅう}に</code> marks a change that arrived with no warning. Its second
job is quieter and just as common: applied to plans and appointments it means
*unexpectedly, at the last minute*, which is why it opens so many apologies.

## Usage

<code>{f|急|きゅう}に</code> is the adverbial form of the な-adjective
<code>{f|急|きゅう}だ</code>. That matters in practice, because in front of a noun it
has to change shape: <code>{f|急|きゅう}な{f|用事|ようじ}</code> ("something urgent
that has come up"), <code>{f|急|きゅう}な{f|話|はなし}</code> ("short notice"),
<code>{f|急|きゅう}な{f|坂|さか}</code> ("a steep hill" - the same word, applied to a
slope). Only the `に` form modifies a verb.

Its position in the sentence is free; most often it sits immediately before the
verb, or at the head of the clause when it colours the whole event. It clusters
around three kinds of predicate:

- **change** - <code>{f|急|きゅう}に{f|暗|くら}くなる</code>,
  <code>{f|急|きゅう}に{f|静|しず}かになる</code>,
  <code>{f|急|きゅう}に{f|変|か}わる</code>
- **starting and stopping** - <code>{f|急|きゅう}に{f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}す</code>,
  <code>{f|急|きゅう}に{f|止|と}まる</code>, <code>{f|急|きゅう}に{f|消|き}える</code>
- **arrangements** - <code>{f|急|きゅう}に{f|決|き}まる</code>,
  <code>{f|急|きゅう}に{f|休|やす}む</code>,
  <code>{f|急|きゅう}に{f|呼|よ}ばれる</code>

It does not take degree adverbs. ❌<code>とても{f|急|きゅう}に</code> is not said - 
suddenness is not a quantity you can have more of.

The word to keep separate is <code>{f|急|いそ}いで</code>, the て-form of the verb
<code>{f|急|いそ}ぐ</code>. Same kanji, different reading, opposite kind of meaning:
<code>{f|急|きゅう}に</code> is about the event surprising you,
<code>{f|急|いそ}いで</code> is about you hurrying.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|空|そら}が<b>{f|急|きゅう}に</b>{f|暗|くら}くなった。
The sky went dark all of a sudden.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電車|でんしゃ}が<b>{f|急|きゅう}に</b>{f|止|と}まって、びっくりしました。
The train stopped suddenly and gave me a fright.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|急|きゅう}に</b>{f|用事|ようじ}ができたので、{f|今日|きょう}の{f|約束|やくそく}はキャンセルさせてください。
Something's come up out of the blue, so please let me cancel today's plans.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先月|せんげつ}まで{f|元気|げんき}だった{f|祖父|そふ}が<b>{f|急|きゅう}に</b>{f|入院|にゅういん}することになって、{f|家族|かぞく}みんなで{f|病院|びょういん}に{f|通|かよ}っています。
My grandfather was fine until last month and has suddenly ended up in hospital, so the whole family has been going in to see him.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>{f|急|きゅう}に</code> is register-neutral and extremely frequent. It is
equally at home in a weather report and in a text to a friend, and it carries no
politeness of its own.

Its most useful social function is as the opening of an apology. When you have to
change something at the last minute, Japanese says so first and explains
afterwards: <code>{f|急|きゅう}にすみません</code> among friends,
<code>{f|急|きゅう}で{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありませんが</code> at work,
<code>{f|急|きゅう}なお{f|願|ねが}いで{f|恐縮|きょうしゅく}ですが</code> in a formal
email. In all three the word is doing the same thing: acknowledging that the other
person has had no time to prepare. Leaving it out makes the same request sound
inconsiderate.

Watch the shape when a noun follows. <code>{f|急|きゅう}だ</code> is a な-adjective,
so it is <code>{f|急|きゅう}な{f|仕事|しごと}</code>,
<code>{f|急|きゅう}な{f|変更|へんこう}</code>,
<code>{f|急|きゅう}な{f|出張|しゅっちょう}</code>. The related noun
<code>{f|急|いそ}ぎ</code> exists too and behaves differently:
<code>{f|急|いそ}ぎの{f|用事|ようじ}</code> joins with `の`, and it means "a job that
must be done fast" rather than "a job that came up unexpectedly".

## Contrast

### {f|急|きゅう}に vs {f|突然|とつぜん}・いきなり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - all three mean "suddenly".
<code>{f|急|きゅう}に</code> is the everyday default;
<code>{f|突然|とつぜん}</code> is stronger and more written;
`いきなり` complains that there was no lead-up at all.

</div>

<pre>
<b>{f|急|きゅう}に</b>{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|入|はい}りました。
A meeting's been put in my diary at short notice.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|突然|とつぜん}</b>、{f|部屋|へや}の{f|電気|でんき}が{f|消|き}えた。
All at once the lights in the room went out.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>いきなり</b>{f|大声|おおごえ}で{f|話|はな}しかけられて、こわかった。
Someone started shouting at me out of nowhere, and it was frightening.
</pre>

<code>{f|突然|とつぜん}</code> is dramatic. It suits accidents, disappearances and
news, and it reads as slightly literary in ordinary chat. `いきなり` is the most
colloquial and focuses on the missing preliminaries - no greeting, no warning, no
run-up - which is why it so often carries irritation.

<code>{f|急|きゅう}に</code> is the only one of the three that comfortably covers
scheduling. <code>{f|急|きゅう}に{f|休|やす}みます</code> is the normal way to say you
are taking unplanned leave; ❌<code>{f|突然|とつぜん}{f|休|やす}みます</code> would
sound as though you had vanished.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|急|きゅう}に{f|来|き}てください。（meaning "come quickly"） → ✅ {f|急|いそ}いで{f|来|き}てください。／すぐに{f|来|き}てください。
  <code>{f|急|きゅう}に</code> describes an event that surprises people. Telling
  someone to hurry needs <code>{f|急|いそ}いで</code> or `すぐに`.

- ❌ {f|急|きゅう}に{f|用事|ようじ}があるので、{f|失礼|しつれい}します。 → ✅ {f|急|きゅう}な{f|用事|ようじ}があるので、{f|失礼|しつれい}します。
  In front of a noun the な-adjective needs its `な`. The `に` form can only modify
  a verb.

- ❌ バスがとても{f|急|きゅう}に{f|止|と}まった。 → ✅ バスが{f|急|きゅう}に{f|止|と}まった。
  Suddenness has no degrees, so `とても` has nothing to intensify. If you want the
  event to sound more violent, change the verb, not the adverb.

## Practice

**1.** {f|山|やま}の{f|天気|てんき}は____{f|変|か}わるので、{f|上着|うわぎ}を{f|持|も}っていったほうがいいですよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|山|やま}の{f|天気|てんき}は<b>{f|急|きゅう}に</b>{f|変|か}わるので…

<code>{f|変|か}わる</code> is a verb of change, which is where this adverb lives, and
the point of the sentence is that the change gives you no warning. The `に` form is
required because a verb follows.

</details>

**2.** Translate into Japanese: "My plans changed at the last minute, so I couldn't go."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|急|きゅう}に</b>{f|予定|よてい}が{f|変|か}わって、{f|行|い}けなくなりました。

This is the scheduling sense: the change came without notice. `いきなり` would
sound like a complaint about somebody's manners, and
<code>{f|突然|とつぜん}</code> would be dramatic for a diary clash.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|急|きゅう}に{f|出|で}かけました。
(b) {f|急|いそ}いで{f|出|で}かけました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Same kanji, two different words. (a) is
<code>{f|急|きゅう}に</code> - the leaving was unplanned. Something came up and you
went out when nobody expected you to, and the sentence says nothing about how fast
you moved.

(b) is <code>{f|急|いそ}いで</code>, the て-form of
<code>{f|急|いそ}ぐ</code> - you left **in a hurry**. The trip may have been on your
calendar for weeks; what the sentence reports is that you rushed.

</details>
