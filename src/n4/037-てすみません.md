# ～てすみません

**Meaning** · I'm sorry for doing (or not doing) that - an apology attached to the specific act

Bare `すみません` apologises for nothing in particular. `～てすみません` names the
offence: the て-form supplies the cause, `すみません` supplies the apology, and the
two are joined by the ordinary "reason" use of the て-form you already know from
{f|遅|おく}れて{f|困|こま}りました. What makes it worth its own page is that
Japanese reaches for this construction in places where English would say *thank
you*.

## Formation

The て-form of whatever caused the trouble, then the apology word.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + すみません | {f|遅|おく}れる → {f|遅|おく}れてすみません |
| Verb (negative) | なくて + すみません | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}けなくてすみません |
| Verb (past act) | て-form + すみませんでした | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}んですみませんでした |
| い-adjective | くて + すみません | {f|遅|おそ}い → {f|遅|おそ}くてすみません |
| な-adjective | で + すみません | {f|下手|へた} → {f|下手|へた}ですみません |
| Noun | で + すみません | この{f|字|じ} → {f|汚|きたな}い{f|字|じ}ですみません |
| Casual | て + ごめん／ごめんね | {f|待|ま}たせる → {f|待|ま}たせてごめん |
| Formal | て + {f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません | {f|遅|おく}れる → {f|遅|おく}れて{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `すみません` for a thing that is still going on, and
`すみませんでした` for a thing that is finished. Arriving late, you say
{f|遅|おく}れてすみません as you come in; the next day you say
{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|遅|おく}れてすみませんでした.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - `すみません` is the standard written and careful-speech form.
`すいません` is the same word as it is actually pronounced in casual speech.
Say it, do not write it.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
<b>{f|遅|おく}れてすみません</b>。
Sorry I'm late.
</pre>

<pre>
うるさく<b>してすみません</b>。
Sorry for the noise.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|急|きゅう}に<b>{f|休|やす}んですみませんでした</b>。
I'm sorry for taking the day off at short notice yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電話|でんわ}に<b>{f|出|で}られなくてすみません</b>でした。{f|会議|かいぎ}{f|中|ちゅう}だったんです。
Sorry I couldn't pick up - I was in a meeting.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|何度|なんど}も<b>{f|聞|き}いてすみません</b>が、もう{f|一度|いちど}{f|住所|じゅうしょ}を{f|教|おし}えてください。
Sorry to keep asking, but could you give me the address once more?
</pre>

<pre>
わざわざ{f|来|き}て<b>いただいてすみません</b>。{f|近|ちか}くまで{f|送|おく}りますよ。
It's very good of you to have come all this way. Let me give you a lift part of the way back.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|手伝|てつだ}っていただいたのに、お{f|礼|れい}も<b>{f|言|い}わなくてすみませんでした</b>。
I'm sorry I never even thanked you after you helped me out.
</pre>

<pre>
つまらないことで{f|時間|じかん}を<b>{f|取|と}らせてしまってすみません</b>でした。
I'm sorry to have taken up your time with something so trivial.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**The apology ladder.** Four rungs, and they are not interchangeable:

- <code>～てごめん／ごめんね</code> - casual, friends and family. Warm.
- <code>～てすみません</code> - the everyday polite apology. Works almost everywhere.
- <code>～て{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません</code> - formal; business, customers,
  real mistakes.
- <code>～て{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございません</code> - the most formal; service
  industry and written apologies.

`すみません` is the safe default. `ごめんなさい` sits between rungs one and two: it
is politer than `ごめん` but has a slightly childlike, personal quality, and it is
odd in a business setting. Note also that `ごめん` cannot take the past-tense trick
 - there is no ❌ごめんでした.

**Japanese apologises where English thanks.** This is the point that catches
learners hardest. When someone has gone out of their way for you, Japanese often
acknowledges the *trouble you caused them* rather than the *good they did you*:

- {f|重|おも}い{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|持|も}っていただいてすみません。
- こんなに{f|遅|おそ}くまで{f|付|つ}き{f|合|あ}わせてすみません。

Both would be "thank you" in English. `ありがとうございます` is also correct in
these situations and is increasingly common among younger speakers; the
`すみません` version sounds more considerate and slightly more formal. Using both
together - すみません、ありがとうございます - is entirely normal.

**Negatives.** For an apology about something you *failed* to do, the form is
`～なくてすみません`, built on the plain negative plus て:
{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|遅|おそ}くなってすみません,
{f|手伝|てつだ}えなくてすみません. `～ないですみません` is not used.

**Common set phrases** worth learning whole, because their internal grammar is
opaque until you have seen it:

- お{f|待|ま}たせしてすみません - sorry to have kept you waiting (causative + humble)
- ご{f|迷惑|めいわく}をおかけしてすみません - sorry for the trouble I've caused
- {f|無理|むり}を{f|言|い}ってすみません - sorry to ask something unreasonable

Register: `すみません` itself is neutral, used by all speakers. `すまない` is its
plain form and is male-marked and gruff. In Kansai speech `すんません` and
`かんにん` turn up; both are regional and casual.

## Contrast

### ～てすみません vs ～てありがとう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ありがとう` looks at what the other person gave you;
`すみません` looks at what it cost them. Both can describe the same kindness.

</div>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}まで{f|送|おく}って<b>くれてありがとう</b>。
Thanks for driving me to the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}まで{f|送|おく}って<b>いただいてすみません</b>。
I'm sorry to have made you drive me to the station.
</pre>

Both are natural and both are grateful. The first is warmer and more direct - the
normal choice between friends. The second acknowledges the imposition, which reads
as more considerate and fits an unequal relationship or a genuinely large favour.
Note the pairing that native speakers keep: `くれて` with thanks, because thanks
point at the giver's act, and `いただいて` with an apology, because the apology
points at your receiving.

### ～てすみません vs ～て{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません</code> is for real
damage. If nobody was actually harmed, `すみません` is enough, and the heavier form
sounds melodramatic.

</div>

<pre>
ペンをお{f|借|か}りします。<b>{f|使|つか}わせてもらってすみません</b>。
Let me borrow your pen - sorry to use your things.
</pre>

<pre>
こちらの{f|確認|かくにん}{f|不足|ぶそく}で<b>ご{f|迷惑|めいわく}をおかけして{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません</b>でした。
I sincerely apologise for the trouble caused by our failure to check properly.
</pre>

In business the two also split by role: `すみません` is what you say about your own
small slips, while <code>{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません</code> is what you say on behalf of your company. A
customer-facing apology almost never uses `すみません`.

### ～てすみません vs ～てしまってすみません

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - inserting `しまって` says the act was unintended or
regrettable. It makes the apology sound more sincere, and it is very common.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大切|たいせつ}な{f|本|ほん}を{f|汚|よご}して<b>すみません</b>。
Sorry for getting your important book dirty.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大切|たいせつ}な{f|本|ほん}を{f|汚|よご}して<b>しまってすみません</b>。
I'm so sorry - I've gone and got your important book dirty.
</pre>

`～てしまう` adds the sense of "and now it's done, and I wish it weren't". Because
apologies are exactly the situation where that feeling belongs, the combination is
almost formulaic: {f|遅|おく}れてしまってすみません,
{f|忘|わす}れてしまって{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|来|こ}ない<b>ですみません</b>。 → ✅ {f|来|こ}られ<b>なくてすみません</b>。
  A negative reason takes `なくて`, not `ないで`. `ないで` marks manner, not cause.

- ❌ {f|遅|おく}れる<b>すみません</b>。 → ✅ {f|遅|おく}れ<b>てすみません</b>。
  The apology attaches to the て-form. The dictionary form cannot sit in front of
  `すみません`.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は<b>ごめんでした</b>。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は<b>ごめんね</b>。／{f|昨日|きのう}は<b>すみませんでした</b>。
  `ごめん` has no past form. If you need the past, move up the ladder to
  `すみませんでした`.

- ❌ お{f|客|きゃく}さま、{f|大変|たいへん}お{f|待|ま}たせして<b>すみません</b>。 → ✅ お{f|客|きゃく}さま、{f|大変|たいへん}お{f|待|ま}たせして<b>{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございません</b>。
  To a customer, `すみません` is too light. Service register uses
  <code>{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございません</code>.

- ❌ {f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|持|も}っていただいて<b>すみましたか</b>。 → ✅ {f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|持|も}っていただいて<b>すみません</b>。
  `すみません` is a fixed apology expression here. Do not try to conjugate it like
  the verb <code>{f|済|す}む</code> it came from.

## Practice

**1.** {f|返事|へんじ}が{f|遅|おそ}く____すみませんでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|遅|おそ}く<b>なって</b>すみませんでした。

The cause is expressed with the て-form, and here the cause is a change of state:
<code>{f|遅|おそ}くなる</code> → <code>{f|遅|おそ}くなって</code>. The past
`でした` fits because the delay is over.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|傘|かさ}を{f|貸|か}して（くれてありがとう・いただいてすみません）。You are talking to a close friend.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|傘|かさ}を{f|貸|か}して<b>くれてありがとう</b>。

Between friends, thanks are direct and the giving verb is `くれる`. The
`いただいてすみません` version is not wrong, but its combination of humble verb and
apology creates a formal distance that would sound strange with someone close.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'm sorry I couldn't come to the party yesterday."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨日|きのう}はパーティーに<b>{f|行|い}けなくてすみませんでした</b>。

Two things to get right: the negative reason takes `なくて`, and the event is
finished, so the apology is `すみませんでした`. Japanese normally says
<code>{f|行|い}けなくて</code> rather than <code>{f|来|こ}られなくて</code> when
talking about a place you did not go to.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|遅|おく}れないですみません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|遅|おく}れて<b>すみません</b>。

Two problems. The negative was inserted by mistake - you are apologising *for*
being late, not for not being late - and even for a genuine negative the reason
form is `なくて`, never `ないで`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|手伝|てつだ}ってくれてありがとう。
(b) {f|手伝|てつだ}っていただいてすみません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both respond to the same help, and both are appropriate responses to it.

(a) is gratitude, pure and simple. It points at what the other person did for you,
and its plain form makes it friendly and equal.

(b) apologises instead: it points at the trouble your problem caused them. Combined
with the humble `いただく` it marks the helper as senior or as having gone
substantially out of their way. Japanese uses this move constantly where English
would only ever say thank you, and getting comfortable with it is one of the
clearer steps from correct Japanese to natural Japanese.

</details>
