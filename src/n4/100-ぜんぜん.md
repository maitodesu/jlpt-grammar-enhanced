# ぜんぜん

**Meaning** · (not) at all; not in the slightest

`ぜんぜん` is the zero end of the scale. Where `あまり` says "not much",
`ぜんぜん` says "none, nothing, no part of it". It normally pairs with a negative,
and in casual modern speech it has picked up a second, affirmative life that
textbooks tend not to mention and learners hear on their first day in Japan.

## Usage

`ぜんぜん` is an adverb. It sits in front of the predicate it is emptying out, and
anything may stand between it and the end of the sentence. In kanji it is
{f|全然|ぜんぜん}; both spellings are ordinary.

There are three patterns to recognise:

- **With a negative** - the core use.
  {f|全然|ぜんぜん}{f|分|わ}かりません, {f|全然|ぜんぜん}{f|寒|さむ}くなかった,
  {f|全然|ぜんぜん}{f|好|す}きじゃない.
- **With a positive verb or adjective that already means "not"** - 
  {f|全然|ぜんぜん}{f|違|ちが}います ("completely wrong"),
  {f|全然|ぜんぜん}だめです ("no good at all"). These are positive in form and
  negative in sense, and they are fully standard.
- **With an ordinary positive, in casual speech** - {f|全然|ぜんぜん}{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ},
  {f|全然|ぜんぜん}いいよ, meaning "totally fine, no problem at all". Extremely
  common in conversation; see the register note below before you write it down.

It also works as a one-word answer: 「{f|疲|つか}れた?」「ぜんぜん。」 - "Tired?"
"Not a bit."

## Examples

<pre>
その{f|話|はなし}は<b>ぜんぜん</b>{f|知|し}りませんでした。
I had absolutely no idea about that.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|問題|もんだい}の{f|意味|いみ}が<b>ぜんぜん</b>{f|分|わ}かりません。
I don't understand what this question means at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|説明|せつめい}と<b>ぜんぜん</b>{f|違|ちが}うじゃないですか。
That's completely different from what you told us yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
「{f|待|ま}たせてごめん。」「ううん、<b>ぜんぜん</b>{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}だよ。」
"Sorry to keep you waiting." "No, no, it's totally fine."
</pre>

## Nuance & register

In its negative use `ぜんぜん` is register-neutral and works in speech and writing
alike. What it is not is **soft**. It is an absolute, and pointed at another
person's work it can be blunt: {f|全然|ぜんぜん}おもしろくなかったです about a
friend's film recommendation is a flat verdict. When you want to be diplomatic,
`あまり` is the word.

The affirmative use - {f|全然|ぜんぜん}{f|平気|へいき}, {f|全然|ぜんぜん}いける - 
needs a clear-eyed description, because you will meet it constantly and be told it
is wrong. It is casual spoken Japanese, especially common among younger speakers,
and in conversation nobody blinks. Style guides and many older speakers treat it as
incorrect, and it does not belong in an essay, a business email or a JLPT answer.
Interestingly the affirmative pattern is not new: it appears in Meiji-era
literature, and the "negative only" rule solidified later - so what is often called
a modern corruption is closer to a revival. Use it in speech if you like; keep it
off paper.

Two more points of feel:

- The intensity ladder runs {f|全然|ぜんぜん} → あまり → ちょっと. Only the first
  is a true zero.
- Because it is absolute, `ぜんぜん` cannot be qualified. There is no "almost not
  at all"; if the amount is small but real, you have moved to `あまり`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ その{f|映画|えいが}はぜんぜん{f|楽|たの}しかったです。（meaning "not fun at all"） → ✅ その{f|映画|えいが}はぜんぜん{f|楽|たの}しく<b>なかった</b>です。
  With an ordinary adjective the predicate has to go negative. Leaving it positive
  produces the casual "totally fun", which is the opposite of what you meant.

- ❌ ぜんぜん{f|少|すこ}し{f|分|わ}かります。 → ✅ {f|少|すこ}し{f|分|わ}かります。／あまり{f|分|わ}かりません。
  `ぜんぜん` is zero, so it cannot be attached to a small quantity. Choose one or
  the other.

- ❌ この{f|作品|さくひん}は{f|全然|ぜんぜん}すばらしいと{f|思|おも}います。（in an essay） → ✅ この{f|作品|さくひん}は{f|非常|ひじょう}にすばらしいと{f|思|おも}います。
  The affirmative `ぜんぜん` is spoken register. In writing it reads as an error
  even to readers who use it themselves every day.

- ❌ {f|時間|じかん}が{f|全然|ぜんぜん}ありましたから、ゆっくりできました。 → ✅ {f|時間|じかん}がたくさんありましたから、ゆっくりできました。
  {f|全然|ぜんぜん}ある is not the affirmative pattern; the casual use attaches to
  evaluations like {f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ} and いい, not to plain statements of
  quantity.

## Practice

**1.** {f|三年|さんねん}{f|習|なら}いましたが、まだ____{f|話|はな}せ____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|三年|さんねん}{f|習|なら}いましたが、まだ<b>ぜんぜん</b>{f|話|はな}せ<b>ません</b>。

`ぜんぜん` demands the negative ending, and the contrast with
{f|三年|さんねん}{f|習|なら}いました is what makes it the right adverb here - 
three years of study and *nothing* to show for it. あまり{f|話|はな}せません would
mean you can manage a little, which is a different complaint.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: {f|昨日|きのう}のテストはぜんぜん{f|難|むずか}しいでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|昨日|きのう}のテストはぜんぜん{f|難|むずか}しく<b>ありませんでした</b>。

Two things. い-adjectives never take でした - the past goes on the adjective
itself. And `ぜんぜん` needs the negative, so the correct past negative is
{f|難|むずか}しくありませんでした（or {f|難|むずか}しくなかったです）.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|料理|りょうり}は<b>あまり</b>{f|辛|から}くないです。
(b) この{f|料理|りょうり}は<b>ぜんぜん</b>{f|辛|から}くないです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says there is a little heat in it, but not much. Someone who cannot take spice
at all should still be careful.

(b) says there is no heat whatsoever - it is safe for anyone. `ぜんぜん` is an
absolute zero, `あまり` a small but real amount, and choosing the wrong one is a
genuinely useful thing to get right at a dinner table.

</details>
