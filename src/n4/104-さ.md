# ～さ

**Meaning** · turns an adjective into a noun - the height, the weight, the difficulty of it

At N5 you learned to nominalise a whole clause with `の`. `さ` does something
narrower and more mechanical: it takes a single adjective and gives you the noun
for the **dimension it measures**. <code>{f|高|たか}い</code> is "high";
<code>{f|高|たか}さ</code> is "height" - a scale that short things have too.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective | drop い + さ | {f|長|なが}い → {f|長|なが}さ |
| な-adjective | stem (no な, no だ) + さ | {f|便利|べんり}だ → {f|便利|べんり}さ |
| いい／よい | よ + さ (irregular) | いい → よさ |
| ない | な + さ | ない → なさ |
| ～やすい／～にくい | drop い + さ | {f|読|よ}みやすい → {f|読|よ}みやすさ |
| ～たい | たい → たさ | {f|見|み}たい → {f|見|み}たさ |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - whatever you would put in front of `くない` (for い-adjectives)
or in front of `です` (for な-adjectives) is what `さ` attaches to. If you can
still see an `い` or a `な`, you have cut too little.

</div>

A `さ`-noun is an ordinary noun from that moment on. It takes `が`, `を`, `の`,
`に` like any other: {f|重|おも}さ**を**{f|測|はか}る、{f|寒|さむ}さ**に**{f|驚|おどろ}く、
{f|広|ひろ}さ**が**{f|足|た}りない。

<div class="note">

**Note** - `さ` is productive: given an adjective, you can generally build its
`さ`-noun and be understood. The exceptions are な-adjectives that are already
nouns in their own right - {f|健康|けんこう}, {f|自由|じゆう}, {f|安心|あんしん} - 
where the bare word normally does the job and the `さ`-form sounds laboured.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|山|やま}の<b>{f|高|たか}さ</b>は{f|三千|さんぜん}メートルです。
This mountain is three thousand metres high.
</pre>

<pre>
テーブルの<b>{f|長|なが}さ</b>を{f|測|はか}ってください。
Please measure the length of the table.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|荷物|にもつ}の<b>{f|重|おも}さ</b>はどのくらいですか。
How much does this parcel weigh?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今年|ことし}の{f|夏|なつ}の<b>{f|暑|あつ}さ</b>は{f|特別|とくべつ}でした。
The heat this summer was something else.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}の<b>やさしさ</b>に{f|感謝|かんしゃ}しています。
I'm grateful for my teacher's kindness.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|町|まち}の<b>よさ</b>は、{f|人|ひと}が{f|親切|しんせつ}なところです。
What's good about this town is that the people are kind.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日本語|にほんご}を{f|勉強|べんきょう}してみて、{f|漢字|かんじ}の<b>{f|難|むずか}しさ</b>がやっと{f|分|わ}かりました。
Now that I've tried studying Japanese, I finally understand how hard kanji are.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}は<b>{f|広|ひろ}さ</b>が{f|十分|じゅうぶん}ですが、{f|窓|まど}が{f|少|すく}なくて{f|暗|くら}いです。
This room is big enough, but it has few windows and it's dark.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The single most useful thing to understand about `さ` is that it is **neutral**.
<code>{f|高|たか}さ</code> does not mean "tallness" in the sense of being tall; it
names the scale, and a low thing has one too. That is why
この{f|机|つくえ}の{f|高|たか}さは{f|七十|ななじゅう}センチです is perfectly
ordinary Japanese about an unremarkable desk. The same goes for
<code>{f|長|なが}さ</code>, <code>{f|重|おも}さ</code>, <code>{f|広|ひろ}さ</code>,
<code>{f|深|ふか}さ</code>, <code>{f|速|はや}さ</code> - these are the words you use
to ask for a measurement.

With adjectives that judge rather than measure - 
<code>{f|難|むずか}しさ</code>, <code>{f|楽|たの}しさ</code>, <code>{f|大切|たいせつ}さ</code>,
やさしさ - the noun names *how much* or *the quality of being so*, and it tends to
turn up after verbs of realising and feeling:
{f|大切|たいせつ}さに{f|気|き}づく、{f|楽|たの}しさを{f|知|し}る。

`さ` is register-neutral, and it is if anything commoner in writing than in
speech: product specifications, recipes, weather reports and essays all lean on
it. Nothing about it sounds stiff in conversation either.

<div class="note">

**Note** - do not confuse this suffix with the casual sentence-final particle
`さ`, as in そんなの{f|関係|かんけい}ないさ. That one attaches to a finished
sentence, is markedly casual, and leans male in most speech. Two unrelated
words that happen to share a syllable.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～さ vs ～み

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `さ` is productive and measures. `み` is **not productive**: it
belongs to a fixed set of established words, and it names something felt or
concrete rather than a quantity.

</div>

This is the pair learners most want to treat as a free choice, and it is not one.
You may build a `さ`-noun from any adjective; you may only *use* the `み`-nouns
that already exist.

<pre>
この{f|石|いし}の<b>{f|重|おも}さ</b>は{f|五|ご}キロです。
This stone weighs five kilos.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}の{f|言葉|ことば}には<b>{f|重|おも}み</b>があります。
The manager's words carry weight.
</pre>

The established pairs are worth learning as vocabulary:
<code>{f|深|ふか}さ</code> is a depth in metres while <code>{f|深|ふか}み</code> is a
deep spot or a richness of character; <code>{f|甘|あま}さ</code> is a degree of
sweetness while <code>{f|甘|あま}み</code> is the taste itself;
<code>{f|楽|たの}しさ</code> is how enjoyable something is while
<code>{f|楽|たの}しみ</code> has become a plain noun meaning something you look
forward to. Some adjectives have only the `み` form in common use - the noun for
hurting is <code>{f|痛|いた}み</code>, not <code>{f|痛|いた}さ</code>, unless you
specifically mean the degree of the pain.

Anything outside that set is not available: ❌{f|新|あたら}しみ,
❌{f|難|むずか}しみ, ❌{f|広|ひろ}み. When in doubt, use `さ`.

### ～さ vs こと／の

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `さ` nominalises **one adjective** and names its degree.
`こと` and `の` nominalise **a whole clause** and name the fact.

</div>

<pre>
{f|川|かわ}の<b>{f|深|ふか}さ</b>に{f|驚|おどろ}きました。
I was taken aback by how deep the river was.
</pre>

Compare {f|川|かわ}が{f|深|ふか}いことに{f|驚|おどろ}きました, "I was surprised at
the fact that the river was deep." The `さ` version takes the depth for granted
and points at its degree; the `こと` version presents the depth as news. The `さ`
one is also the only option once you attach a number:
{f|深|ふか}さは{f|二|に}メートルです, never ❌{f|深|ふか}いことは{f|二|に}メートルです.

Because `さ` only takes adjectives, a verb has no `さ`-form at all: for
"swimming", you still need {f|泳|およ}ぐこと or {f|泳|およ}ぐの.

### ～さ vs leaving the adjective alone

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - use `さ` only when the sentence needs a **noun** in that slot.
If the adjective can simply be the predicate, leave it as an adjective.

</div>

Learners who have just met `さ` tend to sprinkle it everywhere, and the result is
a sentence that says the same thing twice:

<pre>
この{f|山|やま}は<b>{f|高|たか}い</b>です。
This mountain is high.
</pre>

❌この{f|山|やま}の{f|高|たか}さは{f|高|たか}いです is not a stronger version of that
sentence; it is a broken one, roughly "the height of this mountain is high." Use
`さ` when a noun is genuinely required - as the object of
<code>{f|測|はか}る</code>, after `に`, before `です` with a number, or as the topic
of a comparison: この{f|山|やま}の{f|高|たか}さは{f|富士山|ふじさん}と{f|同|おな}じくらいです。

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|高|たか}<b>いさ</b> → ✅ {f|高|たか}<b>さ</b>
  The final `い` of an い-adjective is replaced, not kept.

- ❌ {f|静|しず}か<b>なさ</b>／{f|静|しず}か<b>ださ</b> → ✅ {f|静|しず}か<b>さ</b>
  な-adjectives contribute their bare stem. Neither `な` nor `だ` survives.

- ❌ この{f|町|まち}の<b>いさ</b> → ✅ この{f|町|まち}の<b>よさ</b>
  `いい` is irregular everywhere, and `さ` is no exception - it builds on the
  older stem `よ`, exactly as `よくない` and `よかった` do.

- ❌ {f|旅行|りょこう}の{f|楽|たの}し<b>み</b>を{f|友|とも}だちに{f|話|はな}しました。（meaning "I told them how fun the trip was"）→ ✅ {f|旅行|りょこう}の{f|楽|たの}し<b>さ</b>を{f|友|とも}だちに{f|話|はな}しました。
  <code>{f|楽|たの}しみ</code> is a separate word meaning something you are looking
  forward to, so the sentence with `み` says you talked about your anticipation.

## Practice

**1.** このプールの{f|深|ふか}____は{f|二|に}メートルです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|深|ふか}<b>さ</b>は{f|二|に}メートルです

A number of metres needs a noun in front of `です`, and that noun is the
measurement word. Drop the `い` of {f|深|ふか}い and add `さ`.

</details>

**2.** {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|旅行|りょこう}が（{f|楽|たの}しさ・{f|楽|たの}しみ）です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|旅行|りょこう}が<b>{f|楽|たの}しみ</b>です。

This is the fixed lexical `み` word, meaning something you look forward to, and it
is the only natural choice here. <code>{f|楽|たの}しさ</code> would name how
enjoyable the trip was, which you cannot yet know about next week.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I was surprised at the size of this city."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|町|まち}の<b>{f|大|おお}きさ</b>に{f|驚|おどろ}きました。

<code>{f|驚|おどろ}く</code> takes `に`, and `に` needs a noun in front of it, so the
adjective has to be nominalised. <code>{f|大|おお}きい</code> drops its `い` and
takes `さ`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|荷物|にもつ}の{f|重|おも}いさを{f|教|おし}えてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|荷物|にもつ}の<b>{f|重|おも}さ</b>を{f|教|おし}えてください。

The `い` of {f|重|おも}い is replaced by `さ`, not followed by it. A useful check:
the finished noun should end in exactly one `さ` and contain no `い` from the
adjective ending.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|石|いし}の{f|重|おも}さ
(b) この{f|石|いし}の{f|重|おも}み

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the measurable weight, the thing a scale reports in kilos. It is neutral
and works for a pebble as well as a boulder.

(b) is the felt heaviness - the sense of substance or gravity the stone gives you
when you pick it up, and by extension the impressiveness of something. It is one
of the established `み` words, and it is a judgement rather than a measurement.
You cannot generate new `み` nouns this way; where no fixed `み` word exists, `さ`
is what you use.

</details>
