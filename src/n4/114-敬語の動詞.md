# 敬語の動詞

**Meaning** · the closed set of verbs that have their own honorific and humble replacements

A dozen or so of the commonest Japanese verbs do not use the
お○○になる／お○○する frames at all. They have **separate words** instead - one
that raises the person you are talking about, and one that lowers you. Learn them
as pairs and most of {f|敬語|けいご} is done.

## Formation

These verbs are not built; they are swapped in. The plain verb disappears and a
different word takes its place.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb with a set honorific | plain verb → {f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご} word | {f|食|た}べる → {f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がる |
| Verb with a set humble | plain verb → {f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご} word | {f|見|み}る → {f|拝見|はいけん}する |
| Noun + する (honorific) | ご／お + noun + になる・なさる | {f|説明|せつめい}する → ご{f|説明|せつめい}になる |
| Noun + する (humble) | ご／お + noun + する・いたす | {f|説明|せつめい}する → ご{f|説明|せつめい}いたす |
| ～ている (honorific) | ～ていらっしゃる | {f|待|ま}っている → {f|待|ま}っていらっしゃる |
| ～ている (humble) | ～ておる | {f|待|ま}っている → {f|待|ま}っております |

The set itself, laid out as the mirror it is. The middle column is for the other
person; the right-hand column is for you and for anyone on your side.

| Plain verb | {f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご} - raises them | {f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご} - lowers you |
|---|---|---|
| いる | いらっしゃる／おいでになる | おる |
| {f|行|い}く | いらっしゃる／お{f|越|こ}しになる | {f|参|まい}る／{f|伺|うかが}う |
| {f|来|く}る | いらっしゃる／お{f|見|み}えになる | {f|参|まい}る／{f|伺|うかが}う |
| する | なさる | いたす |
| {f|言|い}う | おっしゃる | {f|申|もう}す／{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げる |
| {f|食|た}べる・{f|飲|の}む | {f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がる | いただく |
| {f|見|み}る | ご{f|覧|らん}になる | {f|拝見|はいけん}する |
| {f|聞|き}く・{f|尋|たず}ねる | お{f|聞|き}きになる | {f|伺|うかが}う／{f|承|うけたまわ}る |
| {f|知|し}っている | ご{f|存|ぞん}じだ | {f|存|ぞん}じている／{f|存|ぞん}じ{f|上|あ}げる |
| もらう | - | いただく／{f|頂戴|ちょうだい}する |
| あげる | - | さし{f|上|あ}げる |
| くれる | くださる | - |
| {f|会|あ}う | お{f|会|あ}いになる | お{f|目|め}にかかる |
| {f|着|き}る | お{f|召|め}しになる | - |
| {f|寝|ね}る | お{f|休|やす}みになる | - |
| {f|借|か}りる | - | {f|拝借|はいしゃく}する |

Four of the honorific verbs conjugate irregularly before ます. This is the detail
that most often gives a learner away:

| Dictionary | ます-form | Never |
|---|---|---|
| いらっしゃる | いらっしゃいます | ❌いらっしゃります |
| おっしゃる | おっしゃいます | ❌おっしゃります |
| なさる | なさいます | ❌なさります |
| くださる | くださいます | ❌くださります |

## Nuance & register

Hold the three-way system in mind and none of this is guesswork.
{f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご}（です・ます）makes your speech polite to the listener.
{f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご} raises the subject of the verb.
{f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご} lowers the subject of the verb, which raises whoever
the action is aimed at. Choosing a form is therefore a question about *who is
doing the verb*, not about how polite you feel.

Two refinements worth having early:

**The humble column has two halves.** {f|伺|うかが}う,
{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げる, {f|拝見|はいけん}する and お{f|目|め}にかかる need a
specific person on the receiving end who deserves the respect. {f|参|まい}る,
{f|申|もう}す and おる do not - they simply lower and formalise, which is why
{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|参|まい}ります works on a platform announcement even though a
train respects nobody. That second group is sometimes labelled
{f|丁重|ていちょう}{f|語|ご}, and it is the group you use for your own side in
front of an outsider.

**In-group before rank.** English speakers expect politeness to track seniority.
Japanese tracks the boundary of your group first. Talking to a client, your own
{f|部長|ぶちょう} is lowered exactly like you: not
❌{f|部長|ぶちょう}がおっしゃっていました but
{f|部長|ぶちょう}の{f|中村|なかむら}が{f|申|もう}しておりました - no `さん`, humble
verb, and the title placed before the surname rather than after it. Inside the
office, with only colleagues listening, おっしゃいました is right again. Nothing
about the boss changed; the audience did.

Some smaller points:

- `いただく` does double duty - humble "eat/drink" and humble "receive". The
  context separates them.
- {f|存|ぞん}じ{f|上|あ}げる is used about **people**
  （お{f|名前|なまえ}は{f|存|ぞん}じ{f|上|あ}げております）, and
  {f|存|ぞん}じる about **facts**.
- {f|承|うけたまわ}る is heavy formal business language - order-taking, phone
  messages - and is rare in speech otherwise.
- お{f|伺|うかが}いします is strictly a double layer, but it is so established that
  usage guides let it stand. Do not extend the pattern to new verbs.

And the honest register note: this is the language of work and of service. It is
not a politer version of everyday Japanese that you can switch on at will. Using
it with friends puts a formal wall between you, and Japanese speakers read that
wall as coldness or as mockery.

## Contrast

### {f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がる vs いただく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - {f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がる is *them* eating; いただく is *you*
eating. Both mean "eat", and they are never interchangeable.

</div>

<pre>
{f|冷|さ}めないうちに<b>{f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がって</b>ください。
Do please eat before it gets cold.
</pre>

<pre>
おいしいお{f|菓子|かし}を<b>いただきました</b>。
I had some lovely cakes.
</pre>

The commonest single {f|敬語|けいご} error learners make is turning the honorific
on themselves, and this is where it happens most:
❌{f|私|わたし}はもう{f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がりました. You have just told the room you
consider yourself the honoured guest. The word for your own eating is
いただきました - the same word as in the いただきます said before a meal.

### おっしゃる vs {f|申|もう}す・{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - おっしゃる is their speaking; {f|申|もう}す is yours.
{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げる adds a person you are speaking *to* and deserves.

</div>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}は{f|何|なに}か<b>おっしゃいました</b>か。
Did sensei say anything?
</pre>

<pre>
はじめまして、{f|鈴木|すずき}と<b>{f|申|もう}します</b>。
How do you do - my name is Suzuki.
</pre>

Introducing yourself with ❌{f|鈴木|すずき}とおっしゃいます would mean "I am called
Suzuki, the honourable gentleman". The reverse error is just as common: asking a
customer ❌{f|何|なん}と{f|申|もう}しますか lowers the customer.

### {f|伺|うかが}う vs {f|参|まい}る

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are humble "go/come". <code>{f|伺|うかが}う</code> requires a
person at the destination whom you respect; <code>{f|参|まい}る</code> does not.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}、{f|先生|せんせい}のお{f|宅|たく}へ<b>{f|伺|うかが}います</b>。
I'll visit your home next week, sensei.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三番|さんばん}{f|線|せん}に{f|電車|でんしゃ}が<b>{f|参|まい}ります</b>。
A train is arriving at platform three.
</pre>

Announcements, weather reports and formal self-description use {f|参|まい}る
freely; you cannot substitute {f|伺|うかが}う into any of them, because there is
nobody to honour. Note too that {f|伺|うかが}う has a second job - humble "ask" - 
so ちょっと{f|伺|うかが}いますが is "may I ask you something?"

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|資料|しりょう}を<b>ご{f|覧|らん}になりました</b>。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|資料|しりょう}を<b>{f|拝見|はいけん}しました</b>。
  {f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご} about yourself. Looking at somebody's documents is
  your own action, so it takes the humble word.

- ❌ （to a customer）{f|社長|しゃちょう}さんが<b>そうおっしゃいました</b>。 → ✅ {f|社長|しゃちょう}の{f|佐藤|さとう}が<b>そう{f|申|もう}しておりました</b>。
  The in-group rule. Your own president is lowered in front of an outsider,
  referred to by bare surname, with the title in front.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}のお{f|話|はなし}を<b>お{f|聞|き}きになりました</b>。 → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}のお{f|話|はなし}を<b>{f|伺|うかが}いました</b>。
  Same error with a different verb. When *you* are the listener, the humble verb
  is {f|伺|うかが}う.

- ❌ その{f|方|かた}のお{f|名前|なまえ}は<b>{f|存|ぞん}じております</b>。 → ✅ その{f|方|かた}のお{f|名前|なまえ}は<b>{f|存|ぞん}じ{f|上|あ}げております</b>。
  Knowing *a person* takes {f|存|ぞん}じ{f|上|あ}げる;
  {f|存|ぞん}じる on its own is for facts and things.

- ❌ ねえ、もう<b>{f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がった</b>? → ✅ ねえ、もう{f|食|た}べた?
  Correct grammar, wrong world. Full {f|敬語|けいご} between friends reads as a
  joke at the listener's expense.

## Practice

**1.** どうぞ、{f|温|あたた}かいうちに____ください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

どうぞ、{f|温|あたた}かいうちに<b>{f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がって</b>ください。

The person eating is your guest, so the verb must raise them. There is no such
form as ❌お{f|食|た}べください, and お{f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がりください is the
fixed extra-polite variant of the same thing.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|明日|あした}、{f|部長|ぶちょう}のお{f|宅|たく}へ（いらっしゃいます・{f|伺|うかが}います）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|明日|あした}、{f|部長|ぶちょう}のお{f|宅|たく}へ<b>{f|伺|うかが}います</b>。

The speaker is the one travelling, so the verb lowers the speaker.
{f|伺|うかが}う rather than {f|参|まい}る because the destination belongs to a
person being honoured.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, as a shop assistant to a customer: "Have you seen this month's catalogue?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|今月|こんげつ}のカタログはもう<b>ご{f|覧|らん}になりました</b>か。

The customer is doing the looking, so {f|見|み}る becomes the honorific
ご{f|覧|らん}になる. {f|拝見|はいけん}なさいましたか is wrong twice over - a humble
verb dressed in an honorific ending.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|山田|やまだ}{f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|今|いま}{f|席|せき}にいらっしゃいませんが、{f|戻|もど}りましたらご{f|連絡|れんらく}なさいます。（said to a client on the phone）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}の{f|山田|やまだ}は{f|今|いま}{f|席|せき}を{f|外|はず}して<b>おります</b>が、{f|戻|もど}りましたらご{f|連絡|れんらく}<b>いたします</b>。

`いらっしゃる` and `なさる` both honour your own department head in front of a
customer. Lower him instead: humble `おる` and `いたす`, bare surname, and the
title moved in front of the name.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|明日|あした}{f|大阪|おおさか}へ{f|参|まい}ります。
(b) {f|明日|あした}{f|大阪|おおさか}へ{f|伺|うかが}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both lower the speaker, so both are humble. (a) simply makes the statement formal:
it works whether you are visiting a client, going to a conference or moving house,
because {f|参|まい}る does not point at anybody.

(b) says there is a particular person in Osaka whom you are going to see and whom
you respect - a client, a teacher, a customer. Said about a purely private trip
it sounds off, because the respect in the verb has nowhere to land.

</details>
