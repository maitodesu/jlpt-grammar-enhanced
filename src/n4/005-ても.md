# ～ても

**Meaning** · even if; even though - the second half happens anyway

`～ても` sets up a condition and then knocks it down. Whatever is in the `ても` clause
would normally be expected to change the outcome, and the sentence says it does not.
Depending on context it covers "even if it happens" (a hypothesis) and "even when it
happens" (a repeated fact).

## Formation

The mechanics are simple: build the て-form, add `も`. The only thing to watch is that
adjectives and nouns have their own て-forms.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + も | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}っても |
| Verb (negative) | なくて + も | {f|行|い}かない → {f|行|い}かなくても |
| い-adjective | くて + も | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}くても |
| い-adjective (negative) | くなくて + も | {f|高|たか}くない → {f|高|たか}くなくても |
| な-adjective | で + も | {f|不便|ふべん} → {f|不便|ふべん}でも |
| Noun | で + も | {f|雨|あめ} → {f|雨|あめ}でも |
| Noun / な-adjective (negative) | じゃなくて + も | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}じゃなくても |
| Question word | question word + ても | どんなに → どんなに{f|待|ま}っても |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - no tense ever appears before `ても`. Past, future and habit are all
decided by the final verb: ❌<code>{f|降|ふ}ったても</code>, ✅<code>{f|降|ふ}っても</code>
for "even if it rains" *and* for "even though it rained".

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - you already know this pattern from N5 without knowing it. `～てもいい`
("you may") is literally "even if you do, it is fine", and `～なくてもいい` ("you
don't have to") is "even if you don't, it is fine". Same construction, with an
adjective supplied for the second half.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|寒|さむ}く<b>ても</b>、{f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|走|はし}っています。
Even when it's cold, I run every morning.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っ<b>ても</b>、{f|山|やま}に{f|登|のぼ}ります。
Even if it rains tomorrow, we're climbing the mountain.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|急|いそ}が<b>なくても</b>{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}いますよ。
You'll make it even without rushing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日曜日|にちようび}<b>でも</b>{f|開|あ}いている{f|店|みせ}を{f|探|さが}しています。
I'm looking for a shop that's open even on Sundays.
</pre>

<pre>
いくら{f|説明|せつめい}し<b>ても</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|分|わ}かってくれません。
No matter how much I explain, he just won't understand.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|何|なに}を{f|食|た}べ<b>ても</b>おいしく{f|感|かん}じないので、{f|少|すこ}し{f|休|やす}むことにしました。
Nothing tastes good to me at the moment, so I've decided to take a short break.
</pre>

<pre>
どんなに{f|練習|れんしゅう}し<b>ても</b>、{f|兄|あに}には{f|一度|いちど}も{f|勝|か}てませんでした。
However much I practised, I never once beat my older brother.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|留学|りゅうがく}の{f|費用|ひよう}が{f|高|たか}く<b>ても</b>、{f|一度|いちど}は{f|海外|かいがい}で{f|生活|せいかつ}してみたいと{f|思|おも}っています。
Even if studying abroad is expensive, I'd like to live overseas at least once.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ても` is register-neutral; the politeness of the sentence is set entirely by its final
verb. It is as much at home in a contract as in a conversation.

The casual spoken contraction is `～たって`, and it is everywhere:
<code>{f|行|い}ったって</code>, <code>{f|見|み}たって</code>, <code>{f|高|たか}くたって</code>,
<code>{f|子|こ}どもだって</code>. Verbs build it on the plain past - take
<code>{f|行|い}った</code> and add `って`; い-adjectives use `くたって`
(<code>{f|高|たか}くたって</code>), and nouns and な-adjectives use `だって`. It is
friendly, slightly emphatic, and out of place in writing or with a superior.
「そんなこと、{f|今|いま}{f|言|い}ったって{f|遅|おそ}いよ」 - "It's too late to say that
now."

`ても` pairs up with a small family of adverbs that make the concession explicit, and
using one makes you sound noticeably more fluent:

- **たとえ～ても** - "even supposing", for hypotheticals: たとえ{f|失敗|しっぱい}しても。
- **どんなに～ても／いくら～ても** - "no matter how much".
- **{f|何|なに}を／だれが／どこへ～ても** - "no matter what/who/where".

One caution about the noun form. <code>{f|雨|あめ}でも</code> can be this pattern ("even
in the rain"), but `でも` is *also* an independent word meaning "…or something"
（お{f|茶|ちゃ}でも{f|飲|の}みませんか）and the sentence-initial "but"
（でも、{f|高|たか}いですよ）. Three unrelated things that look identical; the context
always sorts them out, but expect the double-take.

## Contrast

### ～ても vs ～たら・～ば

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たら` and `ば` say the result **depends** on the condition; `ても`
says the result comes anyway. Same slot in the sentence, opposite claims.

</div>

<pre>
{f|安|やす}くなっ<b>たら</b>、{f|買|か}います。
If it gets cheaper, I'll buy it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|安|やす}くなっ<b>ても</b>、{f|買|か}いません。
Even if it gets cheaper, I won't buy it.
</pre>

This is the pair to burn into memory, because English "if" hides the difference. A
useful test: if you could insert "in that case" before the main clause, you want
`たら`; if you could insert "all the same", you want `ても`.

### ～ても vs ～のに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `のに` reports something that **actually happened** and adds
frustration or surprise. `ても` is neutral, and it covers hypotheses and repeated
situations that `のに` cannot.

</div>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っ<b>ても</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|行|おこな}われます。
The match will go ahead even if it rains.
</pre>

Swapping in `のに` there is impossible - nothing has rained yet, and `のに` cannot talk
about a hypothesis. Where both are available, the difference is attitude:

<pre>
{f|三回|さんかい}メールを{f|送|おく}っ<b>ても</b>、{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|来|き}ません。
I've sent three emails and I still get no reply.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三回|さんかい}メールを{f|送|おく}った<b>のに</b>、{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|来|き}ません。
I sent three emails and there's *still* no reply.
</pre>

The `ても` version states the situation. The `のに` version complains about it - you can
hear the speaker's irritation, and it would be undiplomatic in front of the person
responsible.

### ～ても vs ～けど・～が

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `けど` joins **two facts**; `ても` names a condition that fails to
have its usual effect. Only `ても` can be hypothetical.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}いです<b>が</b>、{f|日|ひ}あたりがいいです。
This room is small, but it gets good light.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}が{f|狭|せま}く<b>ても</b>、{f|日|ひ}あたりがよければ{f|満足|まんぞく}です。
As long as it gets good light, I don't mind a small room.
</pre>

The `が` sentence describes one particular room you have both seen. The `ても` sentence
describes a policy that would hold for any room at all. Note also that `ても` attaches
to a bare stem while `けど・が` follow a complete clause with its own tense.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|値段|ねだん}が{f|高|たか}い**ても**、{f|買|か}います。 → ✅ {f|値段|ねだん}が{f|高|たか}く**ても**、{f|買|か}います。
  An い-adjective has to become `くて` first. The `い` never survives in front of `も`.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}**ても**{f|出|で}かけます。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}**でも**{f|出|で}かけます。
  Nouns and な-adjectives take `で`, not `て` - the same shape they use for the
  て-form everywhere else.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}く**ても**いいですか。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}っ**ても**いいですか。
  `ても` is built on the て-form, never the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|何|なに}**も**{f|言|い}っても、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|怒|おこ}りません。 → ✅ {f|何|なに}**を**{f|言|い}っても、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|怒|おこ}りません。
  The "no matter what" meaning comes from `ても` itself, so the question word keeps its
  ordinary particle. <code>{f|何|なに}も</code> plus a negative is a different pattern
  entirely.

## Practice

**1.** {f|安|やす}く____、いらないものは{f|買|か}いません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|安|やす}く<b>ても</b>、いらないものは{f|買|か}いません。

Cheapness would normally be a reason to buy, and the sentence overrules it - that is
exactly the `ても` move. <code>{f|安|やす}い</code> becomes <code>{f|安|やす}くて</code> before
`も`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|明日|あした}{f|晴|は}れ（ても・たら）、{f|海|うみ}に{f|行|い}きましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|明日|あした}{f|晴|は}れ<b>たら</b>、{f|海|うみ}に{f|行|い}きましょう。

The trip to the sea *depends* on the weather, so this is a plain condition.
<code>{f|晴|は}れても</code> would mean you are going regardless - and then mentioning the
sunshine would make no sense.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Even if you're busy, please eat breakfast."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|忙|いそが}しく<b>ても</b>、{f|朝|あさ}ごはんは{f|食|た}べてください。

<code>{f|忙|いそが}しい</code> is an い-adjective, so it goes to <code>{f|忙|いそが}しくて</code>
before `も`. The `は` on <code>{f|朝|あさ}ごはん</code> is optional but natural - it sets
breakfast against everything else you might skip.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|難|むずか}しいても、{f|自分|じぶん}でやってみます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|難|むずか}し<b>くても</b>、{f|自分|じぶん}でやってみます。

い-adjectives form `ても` from their くて-form. A quick check: if you can say
<code>{f|難|むずか}しくて</code>, you can say <code>{f|難|むずか}しくても</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}ん**でも**、{f|熱|ねつ}が{f|下|さ}がりません。
(b) {f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}んだ**のに**、{f|熱|ねつ}が{f|下|さ}がりませんでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) describes an ongoing situation flatly: taking the medicine does not bring the
fever down, and it may have happened several times. It is what you would say to a
doctor when reporting symptoms.

(b) points at one occasion that is over and adds an emotional charge - you took the
medicine, so the fever *should* have gone down, and you are frustrated or puzzled that
it did not. `のに` always carries that "but it didn't work out" feeling; `ても` does
not.

</details>
