# ～やすい

**Meaning** · easy to do - or, with some verbs, liable to happen

`～やすい` attaches to a verb and produces an い-adjective. What that adjective
describes is the **thing**, not the person: the pen is easy to write with, the
white shirt is quick to get dirty. You already met this machinery at N5 with
`～すぎる` - same ます-stem, same い-adjective coming out the other end.

## Formation

Cut the verb back to its ます-stem and add `やすい`. The result conjugates exactly
like <code>{f|高|たか}い</code>.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + やすい | {f|使|つか}う → {f|使|つか}いやすい |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + やすい | {f|覚|おぼ}える → {f|覚|おぼ}えやすい |
| Verb (する) | し + やすい | {f|説明|せつめい}する → {f|説明|せつめい}しやすい |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | {f|来|き} + やすい | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|き}やすい |

<div class="note">

**Note** - う-verb / る-verb are the labels used throughout this book; you will
also meet them as {f|五段|ごだん}/Group 1 and {f|一段|いちだん}/Group 2 elsewhere.

</div>

Once `やすい` is on, everything an い-adjective does is available:

| い-adjective form | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Polite | + です | {f|使|つか}いやすいです |
| Negative | やすい → やすくない | {f|使|つか}いやすくない |
| Past | やすい → やすかった | {f|使|つか}いやすかった |
| て-form | やすい → やすくて | {f|使|つか}いやすくて |
| Before a noun | やすい + Noun | {f|使|つか}いやすいアプリ |
| Adverbial | やすい → やすく | {f|使|つか}いやすくなる |
| Noun form | やすい → やすさ | {f|使|つか}いやすさ |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the thing that is easy takes `は` or `が`, never `を`. Adding
`やすい` turns the verb into an adjective, and adjectives do not take objects:
この{f|本|ほん}<b>は</b>{f|読|よ}みやすい, not ❌この{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}みやすい.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
このペンは<b>{f|書|か}きやすい</b>。
This pen is easy to write with.
</pre>

<pre>
ひらがなは<b>{f|覚|おぼ}えやすい</b>です。
Hiragana is easy to learn.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|白|しろ}いシャツは<b>{f|汚|よご}れやすい</b>。
White shirts get dirty easily.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}の{f|説明|せつめい}は<b>{f|分|わ}かりやすかった</b>です。
The teacher's explanation was easy to follow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|疲|つか}れているときは<b>{f|間違|まちが}えやすい</b>から、{f|気|き}をつけてください。
You slip up easily when you're tired, so please be careful.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}は{f|字|じ}が{f|小|ちい}さくて、あまり<b>{f|読|よ}みやすくない</b>。
The print in this manual is small, so it isn't very easy to read.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|靴|くつ}は{f|軽|かる}くて<b>{f|歩|ある}きやすい</b>ので、{f|旅行|りょこう}のときはいつもこれをはいています。
These shoes are light and easy to walk in, so I always wear them when I travel.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|春|はる}は{f|天気|てんき}が<b>{f|変|か}わりやすい</b>ですから、かさを{f|持|も}って{f|出|で}かけたほうがいいですよ。
The weather turns quickly in spring, so you'd do well to take an umbrella out with you.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The two meanings are not two words. Which one a listener hears is decided by the
verb underneath.

- A verb someone **chooses** to do - <code>{f|書|か}く</code>, <code>{f|使|つか}う</code>,
  <code>{f|歩|ある}く</code>, <code>{f|飲|の}む</code> - gives **ease**. The thing has a
  property that makes the action comfortable.
- A verb that **just happens** - <code>{f|汚|よご}れる</code>, <code>{f|割|わ}れる</code>,
  <code>{f|忘|わす}れる</code>, <code>{f|太|ふと}る</code>, <code>{f|変|か}わる</code> - gives
  **propensity**. Nobody is finding anything easy; the event simply occurs
  readily.

The propensity reading is often, but not always, a complaint.
<code>{f|壊|こわ}れやすい</code> is a defect; <code>{f|覚|おぼ}えやすい</code> is a
compliment. Do not assume `やすい` is the positive one and `にくい` the negative
one - each takes its colour from the verb.

`～やすい` is register-neutral. It is equally at home in casual speech
（このアプリ、{f|使|つか}いやすいね）, in polite conversation, and in written product
copy, where <code>{f|持|も}ちやすい</code>・<code>{f|洗|あら}いやすい</code>・<code>{f|落|お}ちやすい</code>
are everyday advertising vocabulary.

Two derived forms are worth having now. <code>やすくなる</code> means "become easier"
or "start happening more readily":
{f|漢字|かんじ}を{f|覚|おぼ}えてから、{f|新聞|しんぶん}が{f|読|よ}みやすくなりました。
And <code>やすさ</code> is its noun - the `さ` nominaliser applied to the whole
compound: この{f|車|くるま}は{f|運転|うんてん}のしやすさで{f|人気|にんき}があります。

## Contrast

### ～やすい vs ～にくい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - they are opposites in meaning but not perfect mirrors in use.
Negating a `やすい` word by swapping in `にくい` is safe when the verb is a
happening; it can shift the sense when the verb is a choice.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|皿|さら}は<b>{f|割|わ}れやすい</b>から、{f|気|き}をつけて。
These plates break easily, so be careful.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|皿|さら}は{f|丈夫|じょうぶ}で<b>{f|割|わ}れにくい</b>です。
These plates are sturdy and don't break easily.
</pre>

That pair is a clean mirror. Now compare <code>{f|来|き}やすい</code>, an everyday word
meaning "easy to get to", with <code>{f|来|き}にくい</code>, which a listener will
usually take socially - awkwardness about turning up - rather than as a statement
about distance. The lesson is to learn the useful compounds as words, not to
generate every possible one mechanically.

### ～やすい vs {f|簡単|かんたん}だ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|簡単|かんたん}だ</code> judges the **task**;
`～やすい` credits a **thing** with making the task go smoothly.

</div>

<pre>
{f|図|ず}があるので、この{f|問題|もんだい}は<b>{f|考|かんが}えやすい</b>です。
There's a diagram, so this question is easy to think through.
</pre>

Compare この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|簡単|かんたん}です, which says the question is not
difficult. The `やすい` sentence says nothing about its difficulty - it says the
diagram makes the thinking go smoothly. That gap matters when you write a review:
このアプリは{f|簡単|かんたん}です sounds like the app barely does anything, while
<code>{f|使|つか}いやすい</code> praises how it was built.

### ～やすい vs the potential form

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the potential form asks *can you at all?*; `～やすい` asks
*how smoothly?*

</div>

<pre>
この{f|字|じ}は{f|小|ちい}さいですが、<b>{f|読|よ}みやすい</b>です。
The characters are small, but they're easy to read.
</pre>

<code>{f|読|よ}めます</code> is a yes-or-no ability. `やすい` is a comfort rating, and
a sentence can carry both at once:
{f|眼鏡|めがね}をかければ{f|読|よ}めますが、{f|読|よ}みやすくはありません。
"I can read it with glasses on, but it isn't comfortable reading."

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|使|つか}う<b>やすい</b> → ✅ {f|使|つか}<b>いやすい</b>
  `やすい` attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.
  {f|使|つか}います → {f|使|つか}い + やすい.

- ❌ {f|読|よ}みやすい<b>でした</b> → ✅ {f|読|よ}みやす<b>かったです</b>
  The compound is an い-adjective, so the past tense is built on its `い`, and
  `です` simply rides on top without changing.

- ❌ この{f|本|ほん}<b>を</b>{f|読|よ}みやすいです。 → ✅ この{f|本|ほん}<b>は</b>{f|読|よ}みやすいです。
  Once `やすい` is attached there is no verb left to take an object, so the former
  object becomes the topic or the subject.

- ❌ うちは<b>{f|来|こ}やすい</b>です。 → ✅ うちは<b>{f|来|き}やすい</b>です。
  <code>{f|来|く}る</code> contributes its ます-stem here, the same {f|来|き} you see
  in {f|来|き}ます - not the ない-form stem {f|来|こ}.

## Practice

**1.** このスプーンは{f|小|ちい}さいので、{f|子|こ}どもでも{f|使|つか}____です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|使|つか}<b>いやすい</b>です

The stem is {f|使|つか}い, taken from {f|使|つか}います, and `やすい` goes straight
onto it. The spoon has a property that makes using it comfortable, which is the
ease reading.

</details>

**2.** この{f|紙|かみ}はうすいので、（{f|破|やぶ}れやすい・{f|破|やぶ}れにくい）です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|紙|かみ}はうすいので、<b>{f|破|やぶ}れやすい</b>です。

Thin paper tears readily, so you want the propensity reading. Note that
<code>{f|破|やぶ}れる</code> is intransitive, so nobody is finding anything easy here
 - the tearing happens by itself.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "This town is quiet and easy to live in."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|町|まち}は{f|静|しず}かで<b>{f|住|す}みやすい</b>です。

<code>{f|住|す}む</code> gives the stem {f|住|す}み, and a な-adjective links onward
with `で`. <code>{f|住|す}みやすい</code> is the ordinary way to praise a place as
liveable.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|辞書|じしょ}を{f|引|ひ}きやすいじゃないです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|辞書|じしょ}<b>は{f|引|ひ}きやすくない</b>です。

There are two faults. The object particle `を` cannot survive, because the
compound is an adjective and adjectives take no object; and `じゃない` is the
negative for nouns and な-adjectives, while `やすい` needs `やすくない`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|皿|さら}は{f|割|わ}りやすい。
(b) この{f|皿|さら}は{f|割|わ}れやすい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The difference lives in the verb, not in `やすい`. (a) uses transitive
<code>{f|割|わ}る</code>, so somebody is deliberately breaking the plate and finding
it easy - the sort of remark you would make while smashing old crockery on
purpose.

(b) uses intransitive <code>{f|割|わ}れる</code>, so nothing is deliberate: the plate
is fragile and breaks of its own accord. This is the sentence you actually want
when warning someone to handle it carefully.

</details>
