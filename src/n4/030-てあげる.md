# ～てあげる

**Meaning** · do something *for* someone - the action itself is the gift, and it travels away from the speaker

At N5 you learned that `あげる` hands an **object** to someone outside your circle.
`～てあげる` hands over an **action** instead. The direction rule is identical; what
is new is that naming an act as a favour puts it on the record, and that can be
generous or it can be smug, depending on who you say it to.

## Formation

Attach `あげる` to the て-form. `あげる` then conjugates as an ordinary る-verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + あげる | {f|作|つく}る → {f|作|つく}ってあげる |
| Verb (polite) | て-form + あげます | {f|貸|か}す → {f|貸|か}してあげました |
| Verb (offering) | て-form + あげよう／あげましょうか | {f|運|はこ}ぶ → {f|運|はこ}んであげましょうか |
| To someone above you (humble) | て-form + さしあげる | {f|案内|あんない}する → {f|案内|あんない}してさしあげる |
| To someone below you / an animal | て-form + やる | {f|買|か}う → {f|買|か}ってやる |

The particle on the person who benefits is **not** decided by `あげる`. It is
decided by the verb underneath, exactly as it would be without `あげる` attached:

| Underlying verb frame | Particle | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb already takes {f|人|ひと}に | に | {f|友|とも}だち<b>に</b>{f|道|みち}を{f|教|おし}えてあげる |
| Verb already takes {f|人|ひと}を | を | {f|子|こ}ども<b>を</b>{f|迎|むか}えに{f|行|い}ってあげる |
| Verb acts on the person's possession | の | {f|母|はは}<b>の</b>{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|持|も}ってあげる |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the benefit must land **outside** the speaker. There is no
❌<code>{f|私|わたし}に{f|教|おし}えてあげる</code>. If the favour comes toward you or your family,
the verb you need is <code>{f|教|おし}えてくれる</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|妹|いもうと}に{f|本|ほん}を<b>{f|読|よ}んであげた</b>。
I read my little sister a book.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|荷物|にもつ}が{f|重|おも}そうだったので、<b>{f|持|も}ってあげました</b>。
The bags looked heavy, so I carried them for her.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|母|かあ}さんに<b>{f|電話|でんわ}してあげて</b>。{f|心配|しんぱい}してるよ。
Give your mum a call. She's worried.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|道|みち}が{f|分|わ}からないと{f|言|い}うので、{f|駅|えき}まで<b>{f|案内|あんない}してあげた</b>。
He said he didn't know the way, so I walked him to the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}の{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}を<b>{f|直|なお}してあげたら</b>、とても{f|喜|よろこ}んだ。
When I fixed my son's bike for him, he was delighted.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|留学生|りゅうがくせい}に{f|漢字|かんじ}の{f|読|よ}み{f|方|かた}を<b>{f|教|おし}えてあげる</b>{f|約束|やくそく}をしました。
I promised to teach the exchange student how to read kanji.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ってきたから、{f|隣|となり}のおばあさんの{f|洗濯物|せんたくもの}を<b>{f|取|と}り{f|込|こ}んであげました</b>。
It started raining, so I brought in the laundry for the old lady next door.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|考|かんが}えさせたほうがいいので、{f|答|こた}えは<b>{f|教|おし}えてあげません</b>でした。
It's better to make him think for himself, so I didn't give him the answer.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**`～てあげる` announces the favour.** That is its entire function, and it is why
the pattern needs handling. <code>{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|持|も}ちましょうか</code> is a
plain offer to carry a bag. <code>{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|持|も}ってあげましょうか</code> adds
"…as a kindness to you", and the person hearing it now has a debt on the table
they did not ask for. Between friends this is warm. Aimed at a stranger, a
customer or anyone senior, it lands as condescending.

The safe habit: **do not say `～てあげる` to the person receiving the favour.**
Say it *about* the favour afterwards, or to a third party.

- To the person: {f|手伝|てつだ}いましょうか。／お{f|手伝|てつだ}いしましょうか。
- About it later: {f|昨日|きのう}、{f|田中|たなか}さんの{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}しを{f|手伝|てつだ}ってあげた。

`さしあげる` does **not** rescue the situation. It is grammatically the humble
form, but announcing to a superior that you have bestowed something on them is
worse, not better - the humility is in the word and the presumption is in the
construction. Real Japanese reaches for the honorific-humble frame instead:
お{f|持|も}ちします, ご{f|案内|あんない}します, お{f|貸|か}しします. Keep
`さしあげる` for narrating in the third person
（{f|部長|ぶちょう}にお{f|土産|みやげ}を{f|買|か}ってさしあげた）and even there it
is slightly stiff.

`～てやる` is the downward member of the family - for animals, plants, small
children, one's own younger siblings - and it is blunter and more male-marked.

There is a second, sharper `～てあげる`: said with a flat tone about something the
listener does not want, it turns sarcastic or threatening.
{f|本当|ほんとう}のことを{f|言|い}ってあげようか - "shall I *do you the favour* of
telling you the truth?" The mechanism is the same: you are insisting that this is
a kindness when both of you know it is not.

In casual speech the contraction is `～たげる`:
{f|買|か}ってあげる → {f|買|か}ったげる, {f|見|み}ておいてあげる →
{f|見|み}といたげる. Common in the Kansai area and in relaxed speech generally;
never write it.

## Contrast

### ～てあげる vs ～てくれる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same event, opposite direction. `～てあげる` when the favour
leaves your side; `～てくれる` when it arrives at your side.

</div>

The subject of both is the person **doing** the action. What changes is where the
benefit ends up relative to the うち／そと line you learned at N5.

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}が{f|山田|やまだ}さんの{f|仕事|しごと}を<b>{f|手伝|てつだ}ってあげた</b>。
I helped Yamada with his work.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|山田|やまだ}さんが{f|私|わたし}の{f|仕事|しごと}を<b>{f|手伝|てつだ}ってくれた</b>。
Yamada helped me with my work.
</pre>

The line is your family, not just your body. If a teacher explains something to
your daughter, that favour has come toward your side, so it is
{f|娘|むすめ}に{f|説明|せつめい}してくださいました - never ❌してあげました, which
would push your own daughter into the そと group.

### ～てあげる vs the plain verb

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the plain verb reports *what happened*. `～てあげる` reports
*who it was for*. If the beneficiary is not the point, leave it off.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}、{f|弟|おとうと}の{f|部屋|へや}を<b>{f|片|かた}づけました</b>。
I tidied my brother's room yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}、{f|弟|おとうと}の{f|部屋|へや}を<b>{f|片|かた}づけてあげました</b>。
I tidied my brother's room for him yesterday.
</pre>

Both are correct. The second one is the version you say when you want credit, or
when you are complaining that you had to. This is not a subtlety learners can
skip: overusing `～てあげる` is one of the clearest markers of a foreign speaker,
because English "for him" is neutral and Japanese `～てあげる` is not.

### ～てあげる vs ～させてあげる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～てあげる` means you *did* it for them. `～させてあげる`
means you *let them* do it, as a kindness.

</div>

English "let him" and "did it for him" are far apart; in Japanese they are one
suffix apart, and learners reach for the wrong one.

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもに{f|絵|え}を<b>{f|描|か}いてあげた</b>。
I drew a picture for my child.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもに{f|自由|じゆう}に{f|絵|え}を<b>{f|描|か}かせてあげた</b>。
I let my child draw whatever they liked.
</pre>

The causative `～させてあげる` is extremely common with permission
（{f|休|やす}ませてあげる, {f|遊|あそ}ばせてあげる）and carries the same warmth - 
and the same risk of sounding lofty about your own generosity.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|田中|たなか}さんが{f|私|わたし}に{f|地図|ちず}を{f|書|か}いて<b>あげました</b>。 → ✅ {f|田中|たなか}さんが{f|私|わたし}に{f|地図|ちず}を{f|書|か}いて<b>くれました</b>。
  A favour arriving at the speaker can never be `～てあげる`. This is the N5
  あげる／くれる rule, unchanged.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、この{f|資料|しりょう}をコピーして<b>さしあげましょうか</b>。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、この{f|資料|しりょう}をコピー<b>しましょうか</b>。／お{f|持|も}ちしましょうか。
  Offering a superior a *labelled* favour is presumptuous even in the humble form.
  Drop the giving verb and use the plain offer or the お～します frame.

- ❌ {f|友|とも}だち<b>に</b>{f|手伝|てつだ}ってあげました。 → ✅ {f|友|とも}だち<b>を</b>{f|手伝|てつだ}ってあげました。
  `あげる` does not assign the particle. <code>{f|手伝|てつだ}う</code> takes {f|人|ひと}を, so the
  person keeps `を`.

- ❌ {f|傘|かさ}を{f|貸|か}し<b>あげます</b>。 → ✅ {f|傘|かさ}を{f|貸|か}し<b>てあげます</b>。
  `あげる` attaches to the て-form, not the ます-stem.

- ❌ {f|母|はは}が{f|妹|いもうと}にセーターを{f|編|あ}んで<b>あげました</b>。（telling an outsider）→ ✅ {f|母|はは}が{f|妹|いもうと}にセーターを{f|編|あ}んで<b>くれました</b>。
  Both are inside your family, so from your chair the favour landed on your side.
  `あげる` here would sound as though neither of them were related to you.

## Practice

**1.** {f|荷物|にもつ}が{f|多|おお}かったので、{f|後輩|こうはい}のかばんを{f|持|も}っ____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|持|も}っ<b>てあげました</b>。

The favour goes from the speaker outward to a junior colleague, so `～てあげる` is
the right direction. Note the `の` on <code>{f|後輩|こうはい}</code>: the verb acts on the
bag, and the owner of the bag is marked with `の`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|課長|かちょう}が{f|新|あたら}しいやり{f|方|かた}を{f|教|おし}えて（あげました・くださいました）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|課長|かちょう}が{f|新|あたら}しいやり{f|方|かた}を<b>{f|教|おし}えてくださいました</b>。

The section chief is the one acting and the benefit reaches the speaker, so the
verb must be the toward-me one, in its honorific form. `あげました` would reverse
the direction and put the speaker above the boss.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'll look after the dog for you while you're away."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|留守|るす}の{f|間|あいだ}、{f|犬|いぬ}の{f|世話|せわ}を<b>してあげるよ</b>。

Said to a close friend this is warm and normal. To an acquaintance you would drop
the giving verb - {f|犬|いぬ}の{f|世話|せわ}をしておきますよ - because announcing
the favour to its recipient is what makes `～てあげる` sound self-satisfied.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|先生|せんせい}、{f|重|おも}そうですね。{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|持|も}ってあげましょうか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|先生|せんせい}、{f|重|おも}そうですね。お{f|持|も}ち<b>しましょうか</b>。

The grammar is fine and the effect is rude: you are informing your teacher that
you are about to be generous to them. Swapping in `さしあげましょうか` does not
help. The natural offer upward is the humble お + ます-stem + します frame.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けました。
(b) {f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けてあげました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a neutral report of an action: the window got opened, and nothing is said
about who wanted it open.

(b) frames the same action as a service performed for somebody - you opened it
*for* them, because they were hot or could not reach. It implies a specific
beneficiary who is not you, and depending on tone it can carry either kindness or
a hint of "and you're welcome". If the beneficiary is the person you are speaking
to, (a) is almost always the better choice.

</details>
