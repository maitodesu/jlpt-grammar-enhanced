# ～たところ

**Meaning** · have just done it - standing at the moment immediately after

With the verb in the <b>た-form</b>, `ところ` names the stage right after the action
finished. The emphasis is on *right*: this is a claim about the clock, not about
how fresh the event feels. Minutes ago, not last month.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | <b>た-form</b> + ところだ | {f|着|つ}く → {f|着|つ}いたところだ |
| Verb (voiced た-form) | だ-form + ところだ | {f|読|よ}む → {f|読|よ}んだところだ |
| Polite | た-form + ところです | {f|終|お}わったところです |
| Past reference | た-form + ところだった | {f|出|で}たところだった |
| Followed by an event | たところ + に／へ／で | {f|出|で}たところに{f|会|あ}った |
| Typical companions | {f|今|いま}、たった{f|今|いま}、ちょうど | たった{f|今|いま}{f|帰|かえ}ったところ |
| Casual contraction | たとこ | {f|今|いま}{f|着|つ}いたとこ |
| ❌ Modifying a noun | ❌{f|買|か}ったところの{f|車|くるま} | ✅{f|買|か}ったばかりの{f|車|くるま} |
| ❌ Distant past | ❌{f|去年|きょねん}{f|結婚|けっこん}したところです | ✅{f|去年|きょねん}{f|結婚|けっこん}したばかりです |
| ❌ Negative | ❌{f|食|た}べなかったところ | ✅まだ{f|食|た}べていません |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たところ` pins you to the moment immediately after the action.
If you can put <code>たった{f|今|いま}</code> in front of the sentence, it fits. If
you would have to put <code>{f|先月|せんげつ}</code> there, it does not.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - `～たところ` has a second, unrelated life as a **connective**, and you
will meet it in reading before you produce it:
<code>{f|店|みせ}に{f|電話|でんわ}したところ、もう{f|閉|し}まっていました</code> - "when I
rang the shop, it turned out to be closed." That version has no `だ`, sits at a
comma, and belongs to formal writing. The last contrast section below separates
the two.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
たった{f|今|いま}うちに<b>{f|帰|かえ}ったところ</b>です。
I've literally just got home.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}<b>{f|起|お}きたところ</b>なので、まだ{f|顔|かお}も{f|洗|あら}っていません。
I've only just woken up - I haven't even washed my face yet.
</pre>

<pre>
ちょうど{f|会議|かいぎ}が<b>{f|終|お}わったところ</b>です。
The meeting has just this minute finished.
</pre>

<pre>
メールは{f|今|いま}<b>{f|送|おく}ったところ</b>ですので、{f|確認|かくにん}してみてください。
I've just sent the email, so please check for it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}を<b>{f|出|で}たところ</b>で、{f|高校|こうこう}の{f|友|とも}だちにばったり{f|会|あ}った。
I'd just come out of the station when I ran straight into an old school friend.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|湯|ゆ}が<b>わいたところ</b>だから、お{f|茶|ちゃ}を{f|入|い}れましょうか。
The water's just boiled - shall I make some tea?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}しの{f|荷物|にもつ}を<b>{f|運|はこ}び{f|終|お}わったところ</b>に、{f|大家|おおや}さんが{f|来|き}ました。
The landlord turned up just as we'd finished carrying in the boxes.
</pre>

<pre>
さっき{f|銀行|ぎんこう}から{f|戻|もど}って、{f|今|いま}やっと{f|書類|しょるい}を{f|全部|ぜんぶ}<b>{f|出|だ}したところ</b>です。
I got back from the bank a while ago, and I've only just now handed in all the paperwork.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>たところです</code> is the polite way to report that something is done *this
instant*, and it is a workplace staple. Asked whether you have sent the file,
<code>{f|今|いま}{f|送|おく}ったところです</code> is better than
<code>{f|送|おく}りました</code> because it tells the other person to look now. In
casual speech it shortens to `たとこ`:
<code>ごめん、{f|今|いま}{f|起|お}きたとこ</code>.

The construction is **objective**. It is measured against the clock, and it will
not stretch. That is its whole personality, and it is exactly what separates it
from `たばかり`, which is measured against how the speaker feels. Because of that,
`たところ` has no emotional colour of its own: it neither excuses nor boasts, it
just reports where on the timeline you are standing.

Two structural limits follow from the same fact. `たところ` cannot modify a noun - 
there is no ❌<code>{f|作|つく}ったところの{f|料理|りょうり}</code> - because it
describes a moment rather than a property of the thing. And it has no negative
form: "I haven't eaten yet" is <code>まだ{f|食|た}べていません</code>, never
❌<code>{f|食|た}べなかったところです</code>.

For narration in the past, use <code>たところだった</code>:
<code>{f|部屋|へや}に{f|入|はい}ったとき、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|電話|でんわ}を{f|切|き}ったところだった</code>.

## Contrast

### ～たところ vs ～たばかり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たところ` measures with a **clock**; `たばかり` measures with a
**feeling**. Both translate as "just", and only one of them will stretch to last
year.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}{f|駅|えき}に<b>{f|着|つ}いたところ</b>です。
I've just arrived at the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}{f|日本|にほん}に<b>{f|来|き}たばかり</b>です。
I only came to Japan last year.
</pre>

The second sentence is impossible with `ところ`:
❌<code>{f|去年|きょねん}{f|来|き}たところです</code>. A year is not a moment. `ばかり`
survives it because the speaker is not reporting the clock - they are saying the
arrival is still recent *as far as they are concerned*, which is why it works as
an excuse for not knowing their way around.

The second difference is structural and easy to test. `ばかり` modifies nouns
freely; `ところ` does not:

<pre>
{f|買|か}った<b>ばかりの</b>パソコンが{f|壊|こわ}れてしまった。
The computer I'd only just bought has broken.
</pre>

❌<code>{f|買|か}ったところのパソコン</code> is not Japanese. If a noun follows,
`ばかり` is your only option.

### ～たところ vs ～たあとで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たところ` is a **statement about now** and ends the sentence.
<code>～たあとで</code> is a **connector** and always has another clause after it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|仕事|しごと}が<b>{f|終|お}わったところ</b>です。
I've just finished work.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|仕事|しごと}が{f|終|お}わった<b>あとで</b>、{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}きます。
After work I'm going out for a drink.
</pre>

The first sentence is complete; the second would be unfinished without its second
half. And they carry different information: `あとで` says nothing at all about how
recent the event is, while `たところ` says nothing about what happens next.

When you do want to hang a following clause on `たところ`, it needs a particle - 
`に`, `へ` or `で` - and the meaning becomes "just as I had done X, Y happened":
<code>{f|席|せき}に{f|座|すわ}ったところに{f|先生|せんせい}が{f|入|はい}ってきた</code>.

### ～たところだ vs ～たところ、…

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with `だ`／`です` it means **"just finished"**. Standing at a
comma with no copula, it is a formal connective meaning **"when I did X, it turned
out that Y"**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|課長|かちょう}に<b>{f|聞|き}いたところ</b>です。
I've just this minute asked the section chief.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|課長|かちょう}に<b>{f|聞|き}いたところ</b>、{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になったそうです。
I asked the section chief, and apparently the meeting has been cancelled.
</pre>

The connective version has three tells: no `だ`, a comma, and a second clause that
reports something **discovered** as a result. It belongs to written and formal
Japanese - reports, business email, news - and in speech people say
<code>{f|聞|き}いてみたら</code> instead. You need to recognise it so that a
sentence like the second one does not read to you as "I have just asked."

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|去年|きょねん}この{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|入|はい}ったところです。 → ✅ {f|去年|きょねん}この{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|入|はい}った<b>ばかり</b>です。
  A year ago is not "just now" on any clock. Subjective recency is `ばかり`'s job.

- ❌ これは{f|先週|せんしゅう}{f|買|か}った<b>ところの</b>{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}です。 → ✅ これは{f|先週|せんしゅう}{f|買|か}った<b>ばかりの</b>{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}です。
  `ところ` cannot modify a noun. When a noun follows, only `ばかり` works.

- ❌ {f|今|いま}{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べる<b>ところ</b>です。（meaning "I've just eaten"） → ✅ {f|今|いま}{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べ<b>た</b>ところです。
  The dictionary form means you have not started. One character decides whether
  the food is gone.

- ❌ まだ{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|来|こ}なかったところです。 → ✅ まだ{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|来|き}ていません。
  There is no negative version of this pattern. "Not yet" is
  <code>まだ～ていません</code>.

## Practice

**1.** 「もう{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べましたか。」「ええ、{f|今|いま}{f|食|た}べ____ところです。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ええ、{f|今|いま}{f|食|た}べ<b>た</b>ところです。

The た-form places you immediately after the meal, which is what
<code>{f|今|いま}</code> and the question both call for.
<code>{f|食|た}べるところです</code> would mean the food is still untouched, and
would answer the question with a flat no.

</details>

**2.** {f|三年|さんねん}{f|前|まえ}に{f|大学|だいがく}を{f|卒業|そつぎょう}した（ところ・ばかり）です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|三年|さんねん}{f|前|まえ}に{f|大学|だいがく}を{f|卒業|そつぎょう}した<b>ばかり</b>です。

Three years cannot be squeezed into `ところ`, which is measured on the clock.
`ばかり` reports how recent it feels to the speaker - here, that they still count
as a new graduate.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The train has just left, so the next one is in ten minutes."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|今|いま}<b>{f|出|で}たところ</b>なので、{f|次|つぎ}は{f|十分|じゅっぷん}{f|後|ご}です。

The departure happened seconds ago and the sentence is about the clock, so
`たところ` is exactly right. <code>{f|出|で}たばかり</code> would also be understood
but would sound like a complaint about your luck rather than a timetable fact.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: これは{f|先月|せんげつ}{f|直|なお}したところのテレビです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ これは{f|先月|せんげつ}{f|直|なお}した<b>ばかりの</b>テレビです。

Two problems, one fix. `ところ` cannot sit in front of a noun, and last month is
too far back for it anyway. `ばかり` handles both: it takes `の` before a noun and
it stretches to cover anything the speaker still thinks of as recent.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|日本|にほん}に{f|来|き}たところです。
(b) {f|日本|にほん}に{f|来|き}たばかりです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) means you have this moment arrived - you are at the airport, or you have just
walked out of it, and the suitcase is still in your hand. It is a statement about
the clock and it will not stretch past a few minutes.

(b) means you are new here. It might be a week, it might be six months; what it
reports is that the arrival is still recent from where you stand. This is the one
you use to explain why your Japanese is shaky or why you got lost, and (a) would
be strange in that role.

</details>
