# ～たらどうですか

**Meaning** · why don't you…? - putting an action to the listener as a suggestion

Literally "if you did X, how would that be?" - the `たら` is the ordinary
conditional and `どうですか` is the N5 "how about it?". Put together they make a
suggestion aimed squarely at the other person, and that aim is what makes the
pattern touchier than it looks.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | た-form + らどうですか | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}んだらどうですか |
| Verb (negative) | なかった + らどうですか | {f|行|い}かない → {f|行|い}かなかったらどうですか |
| Casual | た-form + ら? | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}んだら? |
| Softer | た-form + らどうでしょうか | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}んだらどうでしょうか |
| Politer | た-form + らいかがですか | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}んだらいかがですか |
| Politest (upward) | て-form + はいかがでしょうか | {f|休|やす}む → お{f|休|やす}みになってはいかがでしょうか |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the suggestion lives in `どう`, not in `たら`. Change `どう`
and you change the sentence completely:
`～たらどうですか` = "why don't you…?", but
`～たらどうしますか` = "what will you do if…?" - a real question about a real
condition.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - inserting `～てみる` softens the whole thing, because it proposes a
*trial* rather than a course of action:
{f|聞|き}いてみたらどうですか lands much more gently than
{f|聞|き}いたらどうですか. This is the single easiest way to take the edge off.

</div>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is **not** a neutral pattern, and the polite `ですか` does not make it safe.
`～たらどうですか` carries an unstated "you are not doing the obvious thing",
which is fine from a friend and grating from anyone else. Repeated, it is
nagging; delivered flatly, it is criticism.

The register scale, roughly:

- **{f|休|やす}んだら?** - casual, friendly with rising intonation. With
  *falling* intonation it is openly annoyed: {f|自分|じぶん}でやったら? = "then
  do it yourself."
- **{f|休|やす}んだらどうですか** - polite in form; use downward or sideways, not
  upward.
- **{f|休|やす}んだらどうでしょうか** - hedged, noticeably softer.
- **{f|休|やす}んだらいかがですか** - polite vocabulary, safe with customers and
  acquaintances.
- **お{f|休|やす}みになってはいかがでしょうか** - the form to use with a superior.
  Notice it abandons `たら` entirely for `～ては`.

It is a spoken pattern. In writing you would use
`～てはいかがでしょうか` or `～ことをおすすめします`.

Two traps worth keeping apart, both hinging on what follows `たら`:

- **～たらどうですか** = a suggestion.
- **～たらどうしますか** = "what will you do if…?" - 
  {f|終電|しゅうでん}を{f|逃|のが}したらどうしますか。
- **～たらどうなりますか** = "what happens if…?" - about consequences, not
  people.

## Contrast

### ～たらどうですか vs ～ほうがいい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～たらどうですか` **asks**; `～ほうがいい` **tells**.

</div>

`～ほうがいい` (N5) weighs one option against the alternative and comes down on
one side, often with an unspoken warning attached. `～たらどうですか` floats an
action and hands the decision back to the listener - formally, at least. It ends
in a question, and an answer is expected.

<pre>
{f|病院|びょういん}に<b>{f|行|い}ったらどうですか</b>。
Why don't you go to the doctor?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|病院|びょういん}に<b>{f|行|い}ったほうがいいですよ</b>。
You'd better go to the doctor.
</pre>

The second is stronger and implies a consequence if you don't. The first is
softer on the surface - but because it points at something the listener has
visibly failed to do, it can end up sounding more pointed. Neither is safe to aim
at a superior.

### ～たらどうですか vs ～ませんか／～ましょう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～ませんか` and `～ましょう` include the speaker.
`～たらどうですか` is for the listener **alone**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|昼|ひる}ご{f|飯|はん}を<b>{f|食|た}べませんか</b>。
Shall we have lunch together?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昼|ひる}ご{f|飯|はん}くらい<b>{f|食|た}べたらどうですか</b>。
Why don't you at least eat some lunch?
</pre>

The second explicitly excludes the speaker: you are telling someone else to eat.
Using `～たらどうですか` when you mean to come along is one of the more
embarrassing N4 errors, because it turns an invitation into a criticism of the
listener's habits.

### ～たらどうですか vs ～たらいいですよ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～たらいいですよ` **answers** a request for advice.
`～たらどうですか` **offers** advice nobody asked for.

</div>

<pre>
「どこで{f|買|か}えますか。」「{f|駅前|えきまえ}の{f|店|みせ}で<b>{f|買|か}ったらいいですよ</b>。」
"Where can I buy one?" "You could buy one at the shop by the station."
</pre>

`～たらいい` states the good option as information - no question, no pressure. It
is the natural reply to どうしたらいいですか. Swapping in
{f|買|か}ったらどうですか would be odd there, because the listener has already
asked; suggesting it back at them implies they should have thought of it
themselves.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|休|やす}む**たらどうですか** → ✅ {f|休|やす}**んだら**どうですか
  `たら` is built on the past form: {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}んだ →
  {f|休|やす}んだら.

- ❌ {f|社長|しゃちょう}、{f|少|すこ}し{f|休|やす}んだらどうですか。 → ✅ {f|社長|しゃちょう}、{f|少|すこ}しお{f|休|やす}みに**なってはいかがでしょうか**。
  Grammatical, and rude upward. Telling a superior what they ought to be doing
  needs the `～てはいかがでしょうか` frame and an honorific verb.

- ❌ {f|一緒|いっしょ}にコンサートに{f|行|い}ったらどうですか。（meaning "shall we go together?"）→ ✅ {f|一緒|いっしょ}にコンサートに{f|行|い}き**ませんか**。
  `～たらどうですか` never includes the speaker.

- ❌ Reading {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|き}たらどうしますか as a suggestion → it
  means "what will you do if a typhoon comes?" `どうですか` suggests;
  `どうしますか` asks.

## Practice

**1.** {f|眠|ねむ}そうですね。コーヒーでも{f|飲|の}____どうですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

コーヒーでも{f|飲|の}<b>んだら</b>どうですか。

The pattern is built on the plain past: {f|飲|の}む → {f|飲|の}んだ →
{f|飲|の}んだら. The `でも` softens it further by making the coffee an example
rather than a demand.

</details>

**2.** {f|明日|あした}{f|一緒|いっしょ}にテニスを（しませんか・したらどうですか）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|明日|あした}{f|一緒|いっしょ}にテニスを<b>しませんか</b>。

{f|一緒|いっしょ}に puts the speaker in the picture, and `～たらどうですか` is
strictly about what the *listener* should do. With `したらどうですか` the
sentence would mean "why don't you play tennis with someone tomorrow" - and would
sound faintly critical of how they spend their time.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Why don't you try asking the station staff?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|駅|えき}の{f|人|ひと}に<b>{f|聞|き}いてみたらどうですか</b>。

`～てみる` inside the pattern turns it into "why not give it a try", which is the
friendliest version available. Without it,
{f|聞|き}いたらどうですか sounds more like "you should have asked already".

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|先生|せんせい}、この{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}んだらどうですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|先生|せんせい}、この{f|本|ほん}をお{f|読|よ}みに<b>なってはいかがでしょうか</b>。

The grammar of the original is fine; the problem is direction. Suggesting an
action to a teacher, boss or customer needs `～てはいかがでしょうか` with an
honorific verb. A slightly lighter option is
{f|読|よ}まれてはいかがでしょうか.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|医者|いしゃ}に{f|行|い}ったらどうですか。
(b) {f|医者|いしゃ}に{f|行|い}ったほうがいいですよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is shaped as a question and formally leaves the choice open - but it also
points out that the listener has not gone yet, so depending on tone it can come
across as impatient: "why don't you just go?"

(b) is a straight recommendation with the speaker's judgement behind it, and the
`よ` adds a note of concern. It is stronger, but paradoxically often kinder,
because it reads as looking out for the listener rather than commenting on their
inaction.

Neither is appropriate upward; to a superior you would say
{f|一度|いちど}{f|病院|びょういん}へ{f|行|い}かれてはいかがでしょうか.

</details>
