# ～終わる

**Meaning** · finish doing something; get to the end of it

<code>{f|終|お}わる</code> on a verb stem closes the bracket that
<code>～{f|始|はじ}める</code> opened. It says the action ran its course and reached
its natural end - the book was read to the last page, the meal was eaten, the
letter was written and signed.

## Formation

Attach <code>{f|終|お}わる</code> to the <b>ます-stem</b>. The compound is a う-verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + {f|終|お}わる | {f|読|よ}みます → {f|読|よ}み{f|終|お}わる |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + {f|終|お}わる | {f|食|た}べます → {f|食|た}べ{f|終|お}わる |
| Verb (する) | し + {f|終|お}わる | {f|掃除|そうじ}する → {f|掃除|そうじ}し{f|終|お}わる |
| Conjugation | う-verb | {f|書|か}き{f|終|お}わった、{f|書|か}き{f|終|お}わって |
| Object particle | unchanged from the first verb | {f|本|ほん}<b>を</b>{f|読|よ}み{f|終|お}わる |
| Deliberate / written twin | ます-stem + {f|終|お}える | {f|読|よ}み{f|終|お}える |
| Independent verb | Noun + <b>が</b> + {f|終|お}わる | {f|授業|じゅぎょう}が{f|終|お}わる |
| Independent transitive | Noun + <b>を</b> + {f|終|お}える／{f|終|お}わらせる | {f|仕事|しごと}を{f|終|お}わらせる |
| Very common with | ～{f|終|お}わったら、～{f|終|お}わってから | {f|食|た}べ{f|終|お}わったら |
| ❌ Instantaneous verb | ❌{f|着|つ}き{f|終|お}わる | ✅{f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}いた |
| ❌ Uncontrolled event | ❌{f|降|ふ}り{f|終|お}わる | ✅{f|雨|あめ}がやむ |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|終|お}わる</code> wants a verb with a beginning, a
middle and a finish line. Reading a book qualifies. Arriving does not: it has no
middle. Raining does not either - it has no finish line, only a stop, and Japanese
uses <code>やむ</code> for that.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the compound looks intransitive but keeps the `を` of the first verb:
<code>{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}み{f|終|お}わりました</code>. Nothing about the object
changes when you bolt <code>{f|終|お}わる</code> on, even though the independent
verb <code>{f|終|お}わる</code> would never take `を` on its own.

</div>

<pre>

When you've finished eating, wash your plate.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|本|ほん}はもう<b>{f|読|よ}み{f|終|お}わり</b>ました。
I've finished this book.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|作文|さくぶん}を<b>{f|書|か}き{f|終|お}わっ</b>た{f|人|ひと}から{f|出|だ}してください。
Hand your composition in as soon as you've finished writing it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|洗濯|せんたく}を<b>し{f|終|お}わっ</b>たら、ベランダに{f|干|ほ}しに{f|行|い}きます。

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

I got through every question on the exam and then forgot to write my name.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|荷物|にもつ}を<b>{f|片付|かたづ}け{f|終|お}わっ</b>たら、{f|近|ちか}くの{f|喫茶店|きっさてん}でコーヒーでも{f|飲|の}みませんか。
When we've finished putting everything away, shall we get a coffee somewhere nearby?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The suffix is register-neutral and enormously frequent, above all in the form
<code>～{f|終|お}わったら</code>, which is how Japanese sequences the tasks of an
ordinary day.

Its close relative <code>～{f|終|お}える</code> means the same thing with a
different feel. <code>{f|終|お}える</code> is genuinely transitive and sounds more
deliberate and slightly more written: <code>{f|読|よ}み{f|終|お}える</code> suggests
you set out to get through the thing and did.
<code>{f|読|よ}み{f|終|お}わる</code> just notes that the reading came to an end.
In conversation <code>{f|終|お}わる</code> is far more common; in a report or an
essay <code>{f|終|お}える</code> often reads better.

Around the independent verbs the picture is worth keeping straight.
<code>{f|終|お}わる</code> is basically intransitive - 
<code>{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わる</code> - and its everyday transitive partners are
<code>{f|終|お}わらせる</code> ("get it finished") and
<code>{f|終|お}える</code> ("bring it to a close"). There is one place where
<code>{f|終|お}わる</code> does take `を`, and you will hear it constantly: the fixed
announcement <code>これで{f|授業|じゅぎょう}を{f|終|お}わります</code>,
<code>{f|本日|ほんじつ}はこれで{f|終|お}わります</code>. Careful writers prefer
<code>{f|終|お}わりにします</code> or <code>{f|終了|しゅうりょう}します</code>, but the
`を` version is standard spoken Japanese in that formula.

## Contrast

### ～{f|終|お}わる vs ～{f|終|お}える

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different temperature.
<code>{f|終|お}わる</code> is the neutral everyday form;
<code>{f|終|お}える</code> is transitive, deliberate, and at home in writing.

</div>

<pre>
レポートを{f|書|か}き<b>{f|終|お}わり</b>ました。
I've finished writing the report.
</pre>

<pre>
レポートを{f|書|か}き<b>{f|終|お}え</b>ました。
I have completed the report.
</pre>

The second sounds like a person reporting an achievement, and it would be the
better choice in an email to a client. Between friends it is faintly formal.

The one thing <code>{f|終|お}える</code> cannot do is describe something ending by
itself: ❌<code>{f|授業|じゅぎょう}が{f|終|お}える</code> is impossible, because
<code>{f|終|お}える</code> always needs someone bringing the thing to an end.

### ～{f|終|お}わる vs ～てしまう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|終|お}わる</code> is about **reaching the end** of a
process. `てしまう` is about **completion with an attitude** - either "the whole
lot" or "and I wish I hadn't".

</div>

<pre>
ケーキを<b>{f|食|た}べ{f|終|お}わっ</b>てから、コーヒーを{f|入|い}れた。
I made coffee after I'd finished the cake.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}でケーキを{f|食|た}べ<b>てしまっ</b>た。
I went and ate the whole cake on my own.
</pre>

The first is neutral sequencing. The second reports a completed act with a
feeling attached - pride, or more often regret. `てしまう` also attaches happily to
verbs with no duration at all (<code>{f|忘|わす}れてしまった</code>,
<code>{f|落|お}としてしまった</code>), which is exactly where
<code>{f|終|お}わる</code> is impossible.

### ～{f|終|お}わる vs {f|終|お}わる・{f|終|お}わらせる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - as a **suffix** it rides on another verb's stem. As an
**independent verb** it needs a noun and `が`; to say that *you* finished
something, the verb is <code>{f|終|お}わらせる</code> or
<code>{f|終|お}える</code>.

</div>

<pre>
{f|授業|じゅぎょう}は{f|三時|さんじ}に<b>{f|終|お}わり</b>ます。
Class finishes at three.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}{f|中|じゅう}にこの{f|仕事|しごと}を<b>{f|終|お}わらせ</b>ます。
I'll get this job finished before the day is out.
</pre>

The first has no agent - three o'clock arrives and the class is over. The second
has you in it, pushing. That is why a manager says
<code>{f|終|お}わらせてください</code> and never
❌<code>{f|終|お}わってください</code> when they mean "get it done";
<code>{f|終|お}わってください</code> would be asking the task to end of its own
accord.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}む<b>{f|終|お}わる</b> → ✅ {f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}み<b>{f|終|お}わる</b>
  The suffix goes on the ます-stem, like
  <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> and <code>{f|続|つづ}ける</code>.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}り<b>{f|終|お}わっ</b>た。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}がやんだ。
  Rain does not complete anything; it stops. Uncontrolled processes with no
  finish line take <code>やむ</code>, <code>{f|止|と}まる</code> or
  <code>{f|終|お}わる</code> as an independent verb.

- ❌ {f|掃除|そうじ}<b>{f|終|お}わり</b>ました。 → ✅ {f|掃除|そうじ}<b>し{f|終|お}わり</b>ました。／{f|掃除|そうじ}<b>が{f|終|お}わり</b>ました。
  A する-noun needs either `し` to build the compound, or `が` in front of the
  independent verb.

- ❌ {f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}き<b>{f|終|お}わっ</b>た。 → ✅ {f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}いた。
  Arriving takes no time, so there is no process to finish. The plain past says
  everything.

## Practice

**1.** レポートを{f|書|か}き____たら、{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|見|み}せてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

レポートを{f|書|か}き<b>{f|終|お}わっ</b>たら、{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|見|み}せてください。

Writing a report is a process with a clear end, so the suffix fits, and
<code>～{f|終|お}わったら</code> is the standard way to sequence "once you've
finished X, do Y". <code>{f|書|か}いたら</code> alone would not insist that the
report is complete.

</details>

**2.** {f|授業|じゅぎょう}は{f|三時|さんじ}に（{f|終|お}わります・{f|終|お}えます）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|授業|じゅぎょう}は{f|三時|さんじ}に<b>{f|終|お}わり</b>ます。

Nobody is bringing the class to an end here - the sentence just states when it is
over, and that is the intransitive verb.
<code>{f|終|お}える</code> would need an agent and a `を`:
<code>{f|先生|せんせい}が{f|授業|じゅぎょう}を{f|終|お}えました</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Once I've finished reading this book, I'll lend it to you."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|本|ほん}を<b>{f|読|よ}み{f|終|お}わっ</b>たら、{f|貸|か}してあげます。

The `を` belongs to <code>{f|読|よ}む</code> and stays where it is when the suffix
is added. <code>{f|読|よ}み{f|終|お}える</code> would also be correct and would
sound a shade more formal.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}り{f|終|お}わってから、{f|散歩|さんぽ}に{f|行|い}きました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|雨|あめ}が<b>やんで</b>から、{f|散歩|さんぽ}に{f|行|い}きました。

Rain has no completion point, only a moment when it stops, so
<code>{f|終|お}わる</code> cannot describe it. <code>やむ</code> is the verb for
weather and noise coming to an end.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) お{f|菓子|かし}を{f|食|た}べ{f|終|お}わった。
(b) お{f|菓子|かし}を{f|食|た}べてしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the eating ran to its end. It is neutral and factual, and it usually sets
up whatever comes next - you have finished, so now you can do something else.

(b) adds an attitude. Depending on the context it means "I ate the lot" or "oh no,
I've eaten them" - the sweets were somebody else's, or you were supposed to be
saving them. The grammar is not reporting a finish line so much as a completed act
you have feelings about.

</details>
