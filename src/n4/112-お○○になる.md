# お○○になる

**Meaning** · the general-purpose honorific frame - describes *someone else's* action while raising them

Japanese politeness runs on three separate systems. {f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご} - 
です／ます - makes your *speech* polite to whoever is listening.
{f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご} raises the **person you are talking about**.
{f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご} lowers **you**. `お○○になる` is the workhorse of the
second group: take almost any verb, and it becomes a respectful description of
what your customer, your teacher or your boss did.

## Formation

Take the ます-stem, put `お` in front and `になる` behind. `になる` then conjugates
as an ordinary う-verb ({f|五段|ごだん}, Group 1).

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | お + ます-stem + になる | {f|書|か}きます → お{f|書|か}きになる |
| Verb (る-verb) | お + ます-stem + になる | {f|調|しら}べます → お{f|調|しら}べになる |
| する-verb, Chinese-derived root | ご + noun + になる | {f|利用|りよう}する → ご{f|利用|りよう}になる |
| する-verb, native root | お + noun + になる | {f|問|と}い{f|合|あ}わせする → お{f|問|と}い{f|合|あ}わせになる |
| Polite / past | お + stem + になります／になりました | お{f|使|つか}いになりました |
| Verb with its own honorific | frame **not** used | {f|食|た}べる → {f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がる |
| One-mora ます-stem | frame **not** used | {f|見|み}る → ご{f|覧|らん}になる、{f|寝|ね}る → お{f|休|やす}みになる |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if the ます-stem is only one mora long
（{f|見|み}、{f|着|き}、{f|寝|ね}、{f|来|き}、し）, the frame will not carry it.
Those verbs all have a dedicated honorific word instead:
ご{f|覧|らん}になる、お{f|召|め}しになる、お{f|休|やす}みになる、いらっしゃる、なさる.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}は{f|何時|なんじ}に<b>お{f|帰|かえ}りになります</b>か。
What time will you be leaving, sensei?
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|本|ほん}をもう<b>お{f|読|よ}みになりました</b>か。
Have you read this book already?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|昼|ひる}すぎに<b>お{f|出|で}かけになりました</b>。
The department head went out in the early afternoon.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|客|きゃく}{f|様|さま}が<b>お{f|選|えら}びになった</b>のは、こちらの{f|色|いろ}でよろしいでしょうか。
Is this the colour you chose, madam?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|課長|かちょう}が<b>お{f|決|き}めになった</b>{f|方法|ほうほう}で{f|進|すす}めます。
We'll go ahead with the method the section chief settled on.
</pre>

<pre>
エレベーターを<b>ご{f|利用|りよう}になる</b>{f|方|かた}は、{f|右|みぎ}の{f|通路|つうろ}へお{f|回|まわ}りください。
Those using the lift, please go round to the right-hand corridor.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|皆|みな}{f|様|さま}が<b>お{f|集|あつ}まりになる</b>{f|時間|じかん}は{f|午後|ごご}{f|二時|にじ}でよろしいでしょうか。
Would two o'clock suit as the time for everyone to gather?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|計画|けいかく}について、{f|社長|しゃちょう}はどう<b>お{f|考|かんが}えになっている</b>のでしょうか。
I wonder what the president makes of the new plan.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is business and service language. In a shop, a hospital, a hotel, a
{f|会議|かいぎ}{f|室|しつ} or a classroom it is completely ordinary; among friends
it is not. Saying お{f|食|た}べになりますか to a classmate is not "extra polite",
it is strange - it puts a counter between the two of you. Overusing
{f|敬語|けいご} socially is a real mistake, not a safe default.

Three things follow from the fact that `お○○になる` is
{f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご}:

- **Never about yourself.** ❌{f|私|わたし}がお{f|作|つく}りになりました is the
  single commonest {f|敬語|けいご} error learners make. You cannot raise your own
  head. Your own actions take {f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご} - お{f|作|つく}りしました
 - or plain ます.
- **Never doubled.** ❌お{f|読|よ}みになられる stacks the frame on top of the
  `られる` honorific. That is {f|二重|にじゅう}{f|敬語|けいご}, and it sounds
  overcooked rather than extra respectful. One layer is enough.
- **Not for your own side.** Speaking to a customer about a colleague, even your
  own boss, you drop the honorific:
  {f|田中|たなか}はただいま{f|席|せき}をはずしております - never
  お{f|席|せき}をはずしになっています.

On the politeness ladder, `お○○になる` sits above `れる／られる` and below the set
honorific verbs. Treat that as a tendency rather than a measurement: in most
offices お{f|書|か}きになる and {f|書|か}かれる are simply two acceptable choices,
and which one a person reaches for depends on the workplace and on how far above
them the other person stands.

## Contrast

### お○○になる vs ～れる／～られる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are honorific. `られる` is the light, everyday office
form; `お○○になる` is heavier, more deferential, and unambiguous.

</div>

<pre>
{f|社長|しゃちょう}はもう<b>{f|帰|かえ}られました</b>。
The president has already left for the day.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|社長|しゃちょう}はもう<b>お{f|帰|かえ}りになりました</b>。
The president has already left for the day.
</pre>

The second is the more respectful of the two, and it is what you would say to an
outside visitor; the first is what one colleague says to another. The practical
advantage of `お○○になる` is that it cannot be misread. <code>{f|帰|かえ}られる</code> is also
the passive and the potential, so {f|先生|せんせい}に{f|来|こ}られました can be
read two ways, while {f|先生|せんせい}がお{f|越|こ}しになりました cannot.

### お○○になる vs お○○する

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same stem, opposite direction. `になる` raises the other
person; `する` lowers you.

</div>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}が{f|荷物|にもつ}を<b>お{f|持|も}ちになります</b>。
The teacher is going to carry the bags himself.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}の{f|荷物|にもつ}を<b>お{f|持|も}ちします</b>。
I'll carry your bags for you, sensei.
</pre>

One says the teacher does the carrying; the other offers to do it for him.
Swapping them reverses who is being honoured, which is why the pair is worth
drilling until it is automatic.

### お○○になる vs the set honorific verbs

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if a verb owns a dedicated honorific word, the frame is
closed to it. Learn that short list; use the frame for everything else.

</div>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}はもうお{f|昼|ひる}を<b>{f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がりました</b>か。
Has sensei had lunch yet?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|資料|しりょう}はもう<b>ご{f|覧|らん}になりました</b>か。
Have you had a look at the documents yet?
</pre>

<code>{f|食|た}べる</code>, <code>{f|見|み}る</code>, <code>{f|言|い}う</code>,
`する`, <code>{f|行|い}く</code>, <code>{f|来|く}る</code>, `いる` and
<code>{f|寝|ね}る</code> all belong to that closed set. Note that
<code>ご{f|覧|らん}になる</code> ends in `になる` but is not built from a ます-stem - 
<code>ご{f|覧|らん}</code> is a noun, and the word has to be memorised whole.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}が{f|資料|しりょう}を<b>お{f|作|つく}りになりました</b>。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}が{f|資料|しりょう}を<b>お{f|作|つく}りしました</b>。
  Honorific language points away from the speaker. Describing your own work with
  <code>お○○になる</code> is the error a Japanese listener notices fastest.

- ❌ {f|地図|ちず}を<b>お{f|見|み}になりました</b>か。 → ✅ {f|地図|ちず}を<b>ご{f|覧|らん}になりました</b>か。
  <code>{f|見|み}る</code> has a one-mora stem and its own honorific verb. The
  frame does not build on it.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}が<b>お{f|話|はな}しになられました</b>。 → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}が<b>お{f|話|はな}しになりました</b>。
  Two honorific layers on one verb is {f|二重|にじゅう}{f|敬語|けいご}. Pick one.

- ❌ {f|課長|かちょう}が<b>お{f|説明|せつめい}になりました</b>。 → ✅ {f|課長|かちょう}が<b>ご{f|説明|せつめい}になりました</b>。
  A Chinese-derived する-verb root takes `ご`: ご{f|利用|りよう}、
  ご{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}、ご{f|記入|きにゅう}.

- ❌ ねえ、この{f|漫画|まんが}<b>お{f|読|よ}みになった</b>? → ✅ ねえ、この{f|漫画|まんが}{f|読|よ}んだ?
  Grammatical, wrong for the situation. Full {f|敬語|けいご} aimed at a friend
  reads as sarcasm or as sudden distance.

## Practice

**1.** {f|先生|せんせい}は{f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|新聞|しんぶん}を____{f|読|よ}____になります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|先生|せんせい}は{f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|新聞|しんぶん}を<b>お{f|読|よ}みになります</b>。

The frame is `お` + ます-stem + `になる`. The stem of
<code>{f|読|よ}みます</code> is <code>{f|読|よ}み</code>, so `お` goes in front of it
and `になります` behind.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|係|かかり}の{f|者|もの}が（ご{f|案内|あんない}になります・ご{f|案内|あんない}します）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|係|かかり}の{f|者|もの}が<b>ご{f|案内|あんない}します</b>。

<code>{f|係|かかり}の{f|者|もの}</code> is a member of your own side, so the
sentence has to lower rather than raise. `ご○○になる` would honour your own staff
in front of a customer, which is exactly backwards.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "What time will the president be arriving?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|社長|しゃちょう}は{f|何時|なんじ}に<b>お{f|着|つ}きになります</b>か。

<code>{f|着|つ}きます</code> gives the stem <code>{f|着|つ}き</code>, hence
お{f|着|つ}きになります. ご{f|到着|とうちゃく}になりますか works too, since
{f|到着|とうちゃく} is a Chinese-derived root and takes `ご`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|明日|あした}、{f|私|わたし}は{f|十時|じゅうじ}にお{f|越|こ}しになります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|明日|あした}、{f|私|わたし}は{f|十時|じゅうじ}に{f|参|まい}ります。

Two faults in one clause. The verb describes you, so it needs
{f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご}, not {f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご}. And
<code>{f|来|く}る</code> is outside the frame anyway: the honorific for someone
else coming is お{f|越|こ}しになる or いらっしゃる, while the humble word for your
own coming is {f|参|まい}る.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|田中|たなか}{f|様|さま}はいつ{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}されますか。
(b) {f|田中|たなか}{f|様|さま}はいつご{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}になりますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are honorific and both are correct. (a) uses the `される` form, which is
light and quick - the register of a colleague, or of a routine business email.

(b) is the heavier frame, and it is what a hotel clerk or a receptionist would
say to a guest. It sounds more careful and more deliberate. The gap is one of
degree, not of grammar: choosing (a) with a customer would not offend anyone, it
would only sound a little brisk.

</details>
