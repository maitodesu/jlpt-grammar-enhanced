# ～なら

**Meaning** · if that's the case - taking up something just said, and replying to it

`～なら` is the conditional that **answers**. It picks up a premise - nearly always
one the other person has just handed you - and builds the speaker's judgement on
top of it: advice, an offer, an opinion, a decision. Nothing about `なら` reports
what will happen; it reports what the speaker thinks about it.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + なら (**no** だ) | {f|明日|あした} → {f|明日|あした}なら |
| な-adjective | stem + なら (**no** だ) | {f|静|しず}か → {f|静|しず}かなら |
| い-adjective | plain form + なら | {f|忙|いそが}しい → {f|忙|いそが}しいなら |
| Verb | plain non-past + なら | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}くなら |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + なら | {f|要|い}らない → {f|要|い}らないなら |
| Verb (past) | plain past + なら | {f|終|お}わった → {f|終|お}わったなら |
| With の | plain form + のなら／んなら | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}くのなら／{f|行|い}くんなら |
| Formal | + であれば／でしたら | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}であれば |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だ` never appears in front of `なら`.
❌{f|静|しず}かだなら, ❌{f|雨|あめ}だなら. Compare `と`, which **requires** it
({f|雨|あめ}だと). Getting these two the wrong way round is the commonest form
error at N4.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - `だったら`, `でしたら` and `それなら` are the everyday cousins of this
pattern. `だったら` is the casual "in that case"; `でしたら` is the polite version
you hear across a counter: {f|明日|あした}でしたら、お{f|席|せき}がございます。

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
ビール<b>なら</b>{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}にありますよ。
Beer? There's some in the fridge.
</pre>

<pre>

Saturday's no good, but Sunday I'm free.
</pre>

<pre>

If your head hurts, you should go home early.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|寿司|すし}<b>なら</b>、{f|駅前|えきまえ}の{f|店|みせ}がおいしいですよ。
For sushi, the place in front of the station is good.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|君|きみ}が{f|行|い}かない<b>なら</b>、{f|私|わたし}も{f|行|い}かない。
If you're not going, I'm not going either.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|宿題|しゅくだい}が{f|終|お}わった<b>なら</b>、{f|遊|あそ}びに{f|行|い}ってもいいよ。
If you've finished your homework, you can go out and play.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

If you're buying a used car, it's safer to have a specialist look at it first.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`なら` is conversational glue, and its natural habitat is the second turn of a
dialogue. Someone says something; you take their words and hand back a judgement.
That is why so many `なら` sentences begin by repeating a noun the other person
just used - ビール**なら**, {f|京都|きょうと}**なら**, その{f|本|ほん}**なら**.
In that use it behaves almost like a topic marker, and it is the only conditional
that can attach to a bare noun this way.

The main clause is always the speaker: `～たほうがいい`, `～てあげる`,
`～てください`, `～つもりだ`, an opinion, an offer. It is never a natural
consequence - that is `と`'s territory.

`のなら`（casual `んなら`）adds a shade of "given that you're telling me this",
which can read as sympathetic or as slightly pointed depending on the tone:
そんなに{f|嫌|きら}いなのなら、{f|食|た}べなくていいよ。 Used that way, `なら`
carries a real edge - you are throwing the listener's own premise back at them.

In writing, `なら` is replaced by `であれば` or by
<code>{f|場合|ばあい}は</code>; the stiff `ならば` survives in formal prose
({f|必要|ひつよう}ならば{f|再度|さいど}ご{f|連絡|れんらく}ください). In speech,
`ならば` sounds bookish.

## Contrast

### ～なら vs ～たら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with `たら`, the main clause comes **after** the condition in
time. With `なら`, it can come **before**. And `たら` reports events, while `なら`
responds to premises.

</div>

<pre>
{f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}く<b>なら</b>、カメラを{f|貸|か}してあげるよ。
If you're going on a trip, I'll lend you my camera.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}っ<b>たら</b>、{f|写真|しゃしん}をたくさん{f|撮|と}りたい。
When I go on the trip, I want to take lots of photos.
</pre>

The camera is lent **before** the trip - the trip is only a plan the listener has
mentioned. The photos are taken **during** it. `たら` can never do what the first
sentence does; that inversion is `なら`'s alone.

The second half of the difference is where the condition comes from. `たら`
supposes something itself; `なら` accepts something already on the table. So
`なら` is at home in replies, and slightly odd as the opening line of a
conversation.

### ～なら vs ～と

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `と` is a mechanism with no speaker in it. `なら` is nothing
*but* the speaker, reacting to a premise.

</div>

<pre>
このボタンを{f|押|お}す<b>と</b>、お{f|湯|ゆ}が{f|出|で}ます。
Press this button and hot water comes out.
</pre>

❌このボタンを{f|押|お}すなら、お{f|湯|ゆ}が{f|出|で}ます is wrong: the water
coming out is a fact about the machine, not the speaker's opinion about a
supposition. Conversely:

<pre>
このボタンを{f|押|お}す<b>なら</b>、{f|先|さき}に{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}を{f|読|よ}んでください。
If you're going to press this button, read the manual first.
</pre>

Now there is a speaker with an instruction, and the instruction applies *before*
the button is pressed - both hallmarks of `なら`, both impossible for `と`.

`なら` also cannot express a certain future "when":
❌{f|春|はる}になるなら, ❌{f|夜|よる}になるなら. Something guaranteed to occur
is not a premise anyone needs to entertain; use `と` or `たら`.

### ～なら vs ～ば

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なら(ば)` is literally the ば-form of the copula, which is
why they blur. Separate them by what the condition is *about*: `ば` conditions on
an event **occurring**, `なら` on a supposition **being true**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}に<b>{f|聞|き}けば</b>、すぐ{f|分|わ}かりますよ。
Ask the manager and you'll know right away.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|聞|き}く<b>なら</b>、{f|午前中|ごぜんちゅう}がいいですよ。
If you're going to ask the manager, the morning is a good time.
</pre>

In the first, the asking produces the result. In the second, the asking is a plan
the listener has announced, and the advice is about *how* to carry it out. Note
again that the advice applies before the asking happens.

The other split is mechanical. `ば` needs a predicate to attach to; `なら` fastens
straight onto a bare noun and lifts it out as a topic - 
その{f|辞書|じしょ}**なら**{f|机|つくえ}の{f|上|うえ}にあります - and no ば-form
can do that.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|静|しず}か**だなら** → ✅ {f|静|しず}か**なら**
  `だ` disappears before `なら`. The same for nouns: ❌{f|雨|あめ}だなら → ✅
  {f|雨|あめ}なら／{f|雨|あめ}だったら.

- ❌ {f|家|いえ}に{f|帰|かえ}る**なら**、シャワーを{f|浴|あ}びます。 → ✅ {f|家|いえ}に{f|帰|かえ}っ**たら**、シャワーを{f|浴|あ}びます。
  A plain sequence of your own actions is not a premise you are responding to,
  and the shower comes after you get home. That is a `たら` sentence.

- ❌ {f|春|はる}になる**なら**、{f|暖|あたた}かくなります。 → ✅ {f|春|はる}になる**と**、{f|暖|あたた}かくなります。
  Spring is not a supposition, and warming up is not the speaker's judgement.
  Natural consequences belong to `と`.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る**なら**、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}です。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っ**たら**／{f|降|ふ}れ**ば**…
  If *you* are the one supposing the rain, use `たら` or `ば`. `降るなら` becomes
  correct the moment somebody else says it is going to rain and you reply to
  them.

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}**の**なら{f|割引|わりびき}があります。 → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}なら{f|割引|わりびき}があります。
  A noun takes `なら` directly. The `の` belongs with
  <code>{f|場合|ばあい}</code>, not with `なら`.

## Practice

**1.** 「{f|傘|かさ}を{f|忘|わす}れました。」「{f|傘|かさ}____、{f|私|わたし}のを{f|貸|か}しますよ。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

「{f|傘|かさ}<b>なら</b>、{f|私|わたし}のを{f|貸|か}しますよ。」

The second speaker picks up the exact noun the first one used and replies to it
with an offer. That is `なら` at its most typical - attached to a bare noun, no
`だ`, and a main clause that is purely the speaker's own decision.

</details>

**2.** {f|温泉|おんせん}に（{f|行|い}くなら・{f|行|い}ったら）、{f|平日|へいじつ}のほうがすいていますよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|温泉|おんせん}に<b>{f|行|い}くなら</b>、{f|平日|へいじつ}のほうがすいていますよ。

The advice is about *which day to choose*, so it has to reach the listener before
the trip. `たら` would put the advice after arrival, which makes no sense - you
cannot pick a weekday once you are already there.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If you're not going to use it, could you lend it to me?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|使|つか}わない<b>なら</b>、{f|貸|か}してくれませんか。

You are responding to something the other person has implied or said, and the
main clause is a request - both squarely `なら`. `たら` would be understood, but
it would frame not-using as an event that happens first, which is not the point.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|大学|だいがく}を{f|卒業|そつぎょう}するなら、{f|国|くに}に{f|帰|かえ}ります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|大学|だいがく}を{f|卒業|そつぎょう}し<b>たら</b>、{f|国|くに}に{f|帰|かえ}ります。

Graduating is a scheduled certainty and the return happens afterwards, so this is
"when", not "if it's the case that" - `たら`. `卒業するなら` would only work as a
reply to someone else raising the possibility, with advice attached:
{f|卒業|そつぎょう}するなら、{f|早|はや}めに{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|探|さが}したほうがいい。

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}すなら、{f|手伝|てつだ}うよ。
(b) {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}したら、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|住所|じゅうしょ}を{f|教|おし}えてね。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) responds to news: you have just said you are moving, and the speaker offers
help - help that will be given **during** the move, not after it. That
before-or-during timing is only possible with `なら`.

(b) waits for the move to be over. `たら` locks the main clause into second
place, so the new address can only be requested once there is one.

Both sentences are about the same move. `なら` reacts to the plan; `たら` waits
for the event.

</details>
