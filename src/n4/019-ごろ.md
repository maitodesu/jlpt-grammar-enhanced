# ～ごろ

**Meaning** · around, about - attached to a **point** in time and nothing else

`ごろ` blurs a moment on the clock or the calendar: <code>{f|三時|さんじ}ごろ</code>,
<code>{f|来週|らいしゅう}ごろ</code>. It is not a general-purpose "approximately". The
moment you are approximating an *amount* - three hours, five people, a thousand
yen - `ごろ` is the wrong word and `ぐらい` is the right one.

## Formation

`ごろ` is a suffix. It goes straight onto the time word, with nothing in between.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Clock time | Time + ごろ | {f|三時|さんじ} → {f|三時|さんじ}ごろ |
| Date, month, year | Noun + ごろ | {f|五月|ごがつ} → {f|五月|ごがつ}ごろ |
| Part of the day | Noun + ごろ | {f|夕方|ゆうがた} → {f|夕方|ゆうがた}ごろ |
| Question word | {f|何時|なんじ}／いつ + ごろ | {f|何時|なんじ}ごろ、いつごろ |
| With に | ごろ（に） - the に is optional | {f|七時|しちじ}ごろ（に）{f|起|お}きます |
| With other particles | ごろから、ごろまで、ごろには | {f|九時|くじ}ごろから |
| As an independent noun | ころ | {f|子|こ}どものころ、あのころ |
| In kanji | {f|頃|ころ}／{f|頃|ごろ} | {f|若|わか}い{f|頃|ころ} |
| ❌ Length of time | ❌{f|三時間|さんじかん}ごろ | ✅{f|三時間|さんじかん}ぐらい |
| ❌ Number or amount | ❌{f|五人|ごにん}ごろ | ✅{f|五人|ごにん}ぐらい |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ask which question the phrase answers. If it answers
<code>いつ</code> ("when?"), use `ごろ`. If it answers <code>どのくらい</code> ("how
much? how long?"), use `ぐらい`. <code>{f|三時|さんじ}</code> is a when;
<code>{f|三時間|さんじかん}</code> is a how-long, and one character is the whole
difference.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the `に` sits *after* `ごろ`, never before it:
<code>{f|六時|ろくじ}ごろに</code>, ❌<code>{f|六時|ろくじ}にごろ</code>. And once `ごろ` is
there, `に` is optional - it is dropped more often than not, because an
approximate time is already loose.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|七時|しちじ}<b>ごろ</b>{f|起|お}きます。
I get up around seven every morning.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昼|ひる}<b>ごろ</b>から{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るそうですよ。
Apparently it's going to rain from around midday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|何時|なんじ}<b>ごろ</b>そちらに{f|着|つ}きますか。
Roughly what time will you get there?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|桜|さくら}は{f|四月|しがつ}の{f|初|はじ}め<b>ごろ</b>に{f|咲|さ}きます。
The cherry blossom comes out around the beginning of April.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもの<b>ころ</b>、この{f|川|かわ}でよく{f|泳|およ}いだものだ。
When I was small I used to swim in this river all the time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|今|いま}<b>ごろ</b>は、まだ{f|大阪|おおさか}に{f|住|す}んでいました。
This time last year I was still living in Osaka.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|夕方|ゆうがた}<b>ごろ</b>から{f|風|かぜ}が{f|強|つよ}くなるので、{f|洗濯物|せんたくもの}は{f|早|はや}めに{f|取|と}り{f|込|こ}んでください。
The wind picks up from around evening, so bring the washing in early.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|中|なか}<b>ごろ</b>に{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}す{f|予定|よてい}なので、それまでに{f|荷物|にもつ}をまとめないといけません。
I'm due to move around the middle of next month, so I have to get my things packed before then.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ごろ` is register-neutral and extremely common in speech. It does, however, do
something socially useful: it declines to pin anyone down. Saying
<code>{f|三時|さんじ}ごろ{f|伺|うかが}います</code> promises an afternoon visit without
promising the minute, which is why it turns up in arrangements between friends and
in service Japanese alike. The flip side is that a business appointment that
genuinely starts at three is announced as <code>{f|三時|さんじ}から</code> - putting
`ごろ` on a fixed start time reads as vagueness where precision was expected.

The `ご`/`こ` alternation is not two words. It is one word that voices when it is
stuck straight onto a time expression: <code>{f|今|いま}ごろ</code>,
<code>{f|近|ちか}ごろ</code>, <code>{f|日|ひ}ごろ</code>, <code>{f|六時|ろくじ}ごろ</code>. Left
standing on its own - after `の`, after an adjective, after この／その／あの - it
stays unvoiced: <code>{f|学生|がくせい}のころ</code>, <code>{f|若|わか}いころ</code>,
<code>あのころ</code>. Both are written <code>{f|頃|ころ}</code> when written in kanji,
though modern text usually leaves them in kana.

<code>{f|今|いま}ごろ</code> deserves a line of its own, because it has an idiomatic
second life. Literally "around now", it is also the word for *too late*:
<code>{f|今|いま}ごろ{f|来|き}ても、もう{f|終|お}わったよ</code> - "turning up now, are
you? It's over." Tone carries the difference, and the sting is real.

Finally, `ごろ` has quietly built a small family of vocabulary meaning "the right
time for": <code>{f|見|み}ごろ</code> (best time to see it),
<code>{f|食|た}べごろ</code> (perfectly ripe), <code>{f|手|て}ごろ</code> (a reasonable
price or size). <code>{f|桜|さくら}は{f|今|いま}が{f|見|み}ごろです</code> is what the
sign in the park says.

## Contrast

### ごろ vs ぐらい・くらい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ごろ` approximates a **point**; `ぐらい` approximates an
**amount**. Clock times, dates and eras take `ごろ`. Durations, counts, prices,
distances and sizes take `ぐらい`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|三時|さんじ}<b>ごろ</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|出|で}ます。
I'll leave the office at about three.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三時間|さんじかん}<b>ぐらい</b>かかりました。
It took about three hours.
</pre>

The pair differ by one character, and that character decides everything: `三時` is
a place on the clock, `三時間` is a length. Everything countable behaves like the
second one - <code>{f|五人|ごにん}ぐらい</code>, <code>{f|千円|せんえん}ぐらい</code>,
<code>{f|十|じゅう}キロぐらい</code> - and none of them will take `ごろ`.

<div class="note">

**Note** - you will hear <code>{f|三時|さんじ}ぐらいに</code> in casual speech, and
speakers do not treat it as an error any more. `ごろ` is still the standard choice
for a clock time and the one an exam expects. What is unambiguously clumsy is
stacking them: <code>{f|三時|さんじ}ごろぐらい</code> says the same thing twice.

</div>

### ごろ vs {f|約|やく}・だいたい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ごろ` is a **suffix** and goes after the time.
<code>{f|約|やく}</code> is a **prefix** on a number and belongs to written and
formal Japanese. `だいたい` is an ordinary **adverb** and floats freely.

</div>

<pre>
{f|電車|でんしゃ}は<b>{f|約|やく}</b>{f|十分|じゅっぷん}{f|遅|おく}れています。
The train is running about ten minutes late.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}は<b>だいたい</b>{f|五時|ごじ}<b>ごろ</b>{f|終|お}わります。
The meeting will finish around five-ish.
</pre>

<code>{f|約|やく}</code> attaches to a quantity, so it patterns with `ぐらい`, not
with `ごろ`; you see it in timetables, reports and announcements, and it sounds
stiff in conversation. `だいたい` is happy in front of almost anything and can sit
in the same sentence as `ごろ`, as the second example shows - the two are not in
competition. In written notices you will also meet
<code>{f|前後|ぜんご}</code>: <code>{f|三時|さんじ}{f|前後|ぜんご}に{f|到着|とうちゃく}の{f|予定|よてい}</code>,
"scheduled to arrive around three", which is `ごろ` in a suit.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|二時間|にじかん}<b>ごろ</b>{f|待|ま}ちました。 → ✅ {f|二時間|にじかん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|待|ま}ちました。
  <code>{f|二時間|にじかん}</code> is a length of time, not a point on the clock.
  Lengths take `ぐらい`.

- ❌ {f|六時|ろくじ}<b>にごろ</b>{f|帰|かえ}ります。 → ✅ {f|六時|ろくじ}<b>ごろに</b>{f|帰|かえ}ります。
  `ごろ` bonds to the time word first; any particle comes after it. The `に` is
  optional, but it can only stand in that order.

- ❌ {f|子|こ}どもの<b>ごろ</b>は{f|野球|やきゅう}が{f|好|す}きでした。 → ✅ {f|子|こ}どもの<b>ころ</b>は{f|野球|やきゅう}が{f|好|す}きでした。
  After `の` the word is standing on its own as a noun, so it does not voice.

- ❌ {f|十時|じゅうじ}<b>ごろぐらいに</b>{f|寝|ね}ます。 → ✅ {f|十時|じゅうじ}<b>ごろ</b>{f|寝|ね}ます。
  Both words mean "about". Using them together is not wrong so much as sloppy - 
  pick one.

## Practice

**1.** {f|映画|えいが}は{f|八時|はちじ}____{f|終|お}わると{f|思|おも}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|映画|えいが}は{f|八時|はちじ}<b>ごろ</b>{f|終|お}わると{f|思|おも}います。

Eight o'clock is a point on the clock, and the sentence is guessing at *when*, so
`ごろ` is the suffix that fits. Nothing here is being measured, so `ぐらい` has no
job to do.

</details>

**2.** {f|駅|えき}まで{f|歩|ある}いて{f|十五分|じゅうごふん}（ごろ・ぐらい）かかります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|駅|えき}まで{f|歩|ある}いて{f|十五分|じゅうごふん}<b>ぐらい</b>かかります。

<code>{f|十五分|じゅうごふん}</code> here is a duration - how long the walk takes - and
durations take `ぐらい`. It would only be `ごろ` if it named a time on the clock,
as in <code>{f|十時|じゅうじ}{f|十五分|じゅうごふん}ごろ</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I think I'll get home around eleven tonight."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|今夜|こんや}は{f|十一時|じゅういちじ}<b>ごろ</b>{f|家|いえ}に{f|着|つ}くと{f|思|おも}います。

Eleven is a clock time, so `ごろ` blurs it. The `に` after `ごろ` would not be
wrong, but with an approximate time it is usually left out.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|夏休|なつやす}みは{f|一|いっ}か{f|月|げつ}ごろあります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|夏休|なつやす}みは{f|一|いっ}か{f|月|げつ}<b>ぐらい</b>あります。

<code>{f|一|いっ}か{f|月|げつ}</code> measures how long the holiday lasts, so it needs
`ぐらい`. Compare <code>{f|八月|はちがつ}ごろ</code> - "around August" - where the same
kind of word names a position in the year instead.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|三時|さんじ}に{f|伺|うかが}います。
(b) {f|三時|さんじ}ごろ{f|伺|うかが}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) commits to three o'clock. It is what you say when the appointment is fixed and
someone is expecting you at that minute - and it means being late is a broken
promise.

(b) commits to the middle of the afternoon and leaves yourself ten or fifteen
minutes of room. It is friendlier and lower-risk, and it is the normal choice for
a casual visit. Said about a formal appointment, though, it can sound as if you
have not really committed.

</details>
