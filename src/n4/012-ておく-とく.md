# ～ておく / ～とく

**Meaning** · do it now so that later is taken care of - or leave it as it stands

`～ておく` frames an action as **setup**. The speaker is not merely doing the
thing; they are doing it *now* so that some later moment goes smoothly. Out of
that comes a second, quieter use: if something is already in a useful state,
`～ておく` means deliberately leaving it there instead of undoing it.

## Formation

`おく` is a う-verb and conjugates normally after the て-form.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (て) | て-form + おく | {f|買|か}う → {f|買|か}っておく |
| Verb (で) | で-form + おく | {f|読|よ}む → {f|読|よ}んでおく |
| Verb (する) | しておく | {f|用意|ようい}する → {f|用意|ようい}しておく |
| Polite | ておきます | {f|買|か}っておきます |
| Past | ておいた／ておきました | {f|買|か}っておきました |
| Negative | ておかない／ておきません | {f|買|か}っておかない |
| Deliberately not doing it | ない-form + でおく | {f|買|か}わないでおく |
| Volitional | ておこう | {f|買|か}っておこう |
| Request | ておいて（ください） | {f|買|か}っておいてください |

The contractions matter more than the table above, because they are what you will
actually hear. `ておく` collapses to `とく`, and `でおく` to `どく`, all the way
through the conjugation:

| Full form | Contracted |
|---|---|
| ておく | {f|買|か}っておく → {f|買|か}っとく |
| ておいた | {f|買|か}っておいた → {f|買|か}っといた |
| ておいて | {f|買|か}っておいて → {f|買|か}っといて |
| ておこう | {f|買|か}っておこう → {f|買|か}っとこう |
| でおく | {f|飲|の}んでおく → {f|飲|の}んどく |
| でおいた | {f|飲|の}んでおいた → {f|飲|の}んどいた |
| しておく | {f|用意|ようい}しておく → {f|用意|ようい}しとく |

<div class="note">

**Note** - write the auxiliary in kana. The source verb {f|置|お}く means "put
something down and leave it there", and that is exactly the image: you put the
situation into a state and walk away from it.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
ビールを<b>{f|冷|ひ}やしておきます</b>。
I'll put the beer in to chill, ready for later.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}{f|前|まえ}にホテルを<b>{f|調|しら}べておいた</b>。
I looked up hotels before we set off.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|書類|しょるい}、コピー<b>しといて</b>くれる？
Could you get this photocopied?
</pre>

<pre>
そのままでいいですよ。エアコンは<b>つけておいて</b>ください。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}のうちに{f|宿題|しゅくだい}を<b>やっとこう</b>。

</pre>

</pre>

</pre>

<pre>

It tastes fine cold, so I made the lunchbox first thing this morning.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**`とく` is not slang; it is how people talk.** <code>やっとく</code>,
<code>{f|見|み}とく</code>, <code>{f|言|い}っとくけど</code>,
<code>{f|覚|おぼ}えとけ</code> - the contraction runs across every age and both
genders, and refusing to use it will make your casual speech sound stiff. It stays
out of writing, out of `ます` speech and out of exams, but it belongs in your ear
from day one.

**As a request, `ておいて` carries an edge.** It hands somebody a task and closes
the topic - 「じゃ、よろしく。{f|予約|よやく}しといて」 is what a manager says to a
junior. Aimed upward it is too brisk; use
<code>{f|予約|よやく}しておいていただけますか</code> or
<code>{f|予約|よやく}をお{f|願|ねが}いできますでしょうか</code> instead.

**There is a self-interested tinge to it.** `ておく` says the action clears your
own path, so it can sound slightly calculating when the beneficiary is somebody
else: 「{f|言|い}っておきますが…」 is a common preface meaning "just so we're
clear", and it is a mild warning shot, not a favour.

**The negative splits two ways.** <code>{f|買|か}っておかない</code> is "I'm not
going to buy it in advance"; <code>{f|買|か}わないでおく</code> is "I'm going to
deliberately not buy it (and leave things that way)". The second is the one people
mean when they choose to hold back - <code>{f|聞|き}かないでおく</code>, "I'll not
ask".

**In polite speech it is completely ordinary**, and it appears constantly in
service Japanese: <code>お{f|預|あず}かりしておきます</code>,
<code>{f|確認|かくにん}しておきます</code>. Nothing about `ておく` is casual by
itself - only the contraction is.

## Contrast

### The two jobs of ～ておく - get it done ahead, or leave it as it is

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if the て-verb *changes* something, `ておく` means do it now
for later. If the て-verb has *already produced* the state you want, `ておく`
means don't undo it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|前|まえ}に{f|席|せき}を<b>{f|決|き}めておく</b>。
We'll settle the seating before the meeting.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|暑|あつ}いので、{f|窓|まど}は<b>{f|開|あ}けておいて</b>ください。
It's hot, so please leave the window open.
</pre>

There is only one grammar here. The "leave it" reading appears when the state is
already in place and closing it again would take an action - so telling somebody
<code>{f|開|あ}けておいて</code> is telling them to *keep choosing* the open window. English needs
two different verbs, "set up" and "leave", where Japanese needs one.

The clearest case of the second reading is the fixed phrase
<code>そのままにしておく</code>:

<pre>
{f|散|ち}らかっているけど、{f|今日|きょう}は<b>そのままにしておこう</b>。
It's a mess, but let's just leave it for today.
</pre>

### ～ておく vs ～てある

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ておく` is what you do; `てある` is what somebody finds
afterwards. `ておく` is a live action verb, `てある` is a state.

</div>

<pre>
{f|飲|の}み{f|物|もの}を<b>{f|用意|ようい}しておきました</b>。
I got the drinks ready.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|飲|の}み{f|物|もの}は<b>{f|用意|ようい}してあります</b>。
The drinks are ready - somebody's seen to it.
</pre>

The first answers "what did you do?"; the second answers "is everything sorted?".
The grammatical fallout is the sharper half of the distinction. Because `ておく`
is an action, it takes everything an action takes - <code>{f|買|か}っておこう</code>,
<code>{f|買|か}っておいてください</code>, <code>{f|買|か}っておかなければ</code>.
`てある` takes none of them, so the moment you need a command, an invitation or an
intention, `ておく` is your only option.

Note also the particles. `ておく` keeps the original verb's frame, so the object
stays `を`: {f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けておく. `てある` normally promotes that object
to `が`: {f|窓|まど}が{f|開|あ}けてある.

### ～ておく vs ～てしまう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ておく` looks **forward**: it's done so the future is easier.
`てしまう` looks at the **finish line**: it's done, over, off the list.

</div>

<pre>
{f|夜|よる}のうちにレポートを<b>{f|書|か}いておく</b>。
I'll write the report tonight so it's ready.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|夜|よる}のうちにレポートを<b>{f|書|か}いてしまう</b>。
I'll get the report finished off tonight.
</pre>

In practice the same evening produces both sentences, but the reason differs.
`ておく` is chosen because something comes *next* - the report is needed tomorrow.
`てしまう` is chosen because you want the thing *behind* you, complete, with
nothing left over.

Two further separations. `てしまう` can carry regret, and `ておく` never can:
there is no way to say `ておく` about something you wish had not happened. And
`てしまう` insists on completion - you finish the whole report - whereas `ておく`
is satisfied with whatever preparation is enough:
{f|少|すこ}しだけ{f|書|か}いておく is fine, while
❌{f|少|すこ}しだけ{f|書|か}いてしまう fights itself.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|買|か}うておく → ✅ <b>{f|買|か}っておく</b>
  `おく` attaches to the て-form, not the dictionary form.

- ❌ まだホテルを{f|予約|よやく}しておきません。 → ✅ まだホテルを<b>{f|予約|よやく}していません</b>。
  "I haven't booked yet" is the plain <code>ていない</code>. The negative of
  `ておく` means you have *decided against* preparing, which is a different claim.

- ❌ {f|窓|まど}が{f|開|あ}けておく。 → ✅ {f|窓|まど}を<b>{f|開|あ}けておく</b>。
  `ておく` does not change the verb's particles. The object of a transitive verb
  stays on `を`; promotion to `が` belongs to <code>てある</code>.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}、これを{f|読|よ}んどいてください。 → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}、こちらを<b>{f|読|よ}んでおいて</b>いただけますか。
  Two problems at once: `どく` is a casual contraction, and `ておいてください`
  hands out tasks. Neither goes upward.

## Practice

**1.** {f|友|とも}だちが{f|来|く}る{f|前|まえ}に、{f|部屋|へや}を{f|片|かた}づけて____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|部屋|へや}を{f|片|かた}づけて<b>おきます</b>（casual: {f|片|かた}づけ<b>とく</b>）。

The tidying is being done *because* something is coming afterwards, which is
exactly the preparation reading of `ておく`. Plain
<code>{f|片|かた}づけます</code> would report the action with no forward-looking
purpose attached.

</details>

**2.** ケーキは{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}に（{f|入|い}れておきました・{f|入|い}れてあります）。{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|行動|こうどう}を{f|報告|ほうこく}する{f|場合|ばあい}は、どちらですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|入|い}れて<b>おきました</b>。

Reporting your own action calls for `ておく`, which is a normal action verb in the
past tense. <code>{f|入|い}れてあります</code> would describe the state of the
fridge without saying who did it - the right choice if you were answering "is the
cake somewhere safe?".

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'll charge my phone before I go out."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|出|で}かける{f|前|まえ}に{f|携帯|けいたい}を<b>{f|充電|じゅうでん}しておきます</b>。

The charging exists only to serve the trip that follows, which is what `ておく`
marks. In speech this would very often come out as
{f|充電|じゅうでん}<b>しとく</b>, and the meaning is identical.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|旅行|りょこう}のことはまだ{f|家族|かぞく}に{f|話|はな}しておきません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|旅行|りょこう}のことはまだ{f|家族|かぞく}に<b>{f|話|はな}していません</b>。／{f|旅行|りょこう}のことは{f|家族|かぞく}には<b>{f|話|はな}さないでおきます</b>。

If you simply have not got round to it, the plain negative
<code>ていません</code> is what you want. If you are choosing not to tell them and
leaving it that way on purpose, the form is
<code>ない-form + でおく</code> - and those are genuinely different situations.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|電気|でんき}をつけておきます。
(b) {f|電気|でんき}をつけます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b) simply reports switching the light on. It says nothing about why, and it could
be about the next second.

(a) says the light is being switched on *for what comes later* - so that the person
arriving home tonight is not fumbling in the dark, or so that the room is ready for
the guests. Depending on the situation it can also mean the light is already on and
you are leaving it that way rather than turning it off.

</details>
