# ～続ける

**Meaning** · keep doing; go on doing without stopping

<code>{f|続|つづ}ける</code> welded onto a verb stem is the mirror image of
<code>～{f|始|はじ}める</code>. Where that one marks the moment something starts,
this one claims the stretch after it: the action ran on, and it did not break off.
Very often the sentence is also saying that this took some doing.

## Formation

Attach <code>{f|続|つづ}ける</code> to the <b>ます-stem</b>. The compound is a る-verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + {f|続|つづ}ける | {f|話|はな}します → {f|話|はな}し{f|続|つづ}ける |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + {f|続|つづ}ける | {f|食|た}べます → {f|食|た}べ{f|続|つづ}ける |
| Verb (する) | し + {f|続|つづ}ける | {f|練習|れんしゅう}する → {f|練習|れんしゅう}し{f|続|つづ}ける |
| Conjugation | る-verb | {f|待|ま}ち{f|続|つづ}けた、{f|待|ま}ち{f|続|つづ}けて |
| "has been doing" | ～{f|続|つづ}けている | {f|十年|じゅうねん}{f|働|はたら}き{f|続|つづ}けている |
| Object particle | unchanged from the first verb | {f|本|ほん}<b>を</b>{f|読|よ}み{f|続|つづ}ける |
| Event noun | Noun + <b>を</b> + {f|続|つづ}ける | {f|話|はなし}を{f|続|つづ}ける |
| Intransitive twin | Noun + <b>が</b> + {f|続|つづ}く | {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|続|つづ}く |
| Suffix twin | ます-stem + {f|続|つづ}く | {f|降|ふ}り{f|続|つづ}く |
| As an adverb | {f|続|つづ}けて | {f|三日|みっか}{f|続|つづ}けて{f|休|やす}む |
| ❌ | ❌ dictionary form + {f|続|つづ}ける | ❌{f|読|よ}む{f|続|つづ}ける |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|続|つづ}ける</code> is transitive and needs someone
doing the continuing; <code>{f|続|つづ}く</code> is intransitive and describes
something that goes on by itself. The suffix pair splits the same way.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the suffix covers two shapes of continuation, and both are normal. One
is an **unbroken** action: <code>{f|二時間|にじかん}{f|話|はな}し{f|続|つづ}けた</code>.
The other is a habit **kept up** over a long period:
<code>{f|十年|じゅうねん}{f|通|かよ}い{f|続|つづ}けている</code>. What it cannot do is
attach to something over in an instant - ❌<code>{f|着|つ}き{f|続|つづ}ける</code> of
one arrival has nothing to stretch.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|一時間|いちじかん}も<b>{f|話|はな}し{f|続|つづ}け</b>た。
He talked for a solid hour.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}の{f|前|まえ}で{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}も<b>{f|待|ま}ち{f|続|つづ}け</b>ました。
I stood waiting outside the station for a full half hour.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|少|すこ}しずつ<b>{f|練習|れんしゅう}し{f|続|つづ}け</b>れば、きっと{f|上手|じょうず}になりますよ。
If you keep practising a little every day, you're bound to get good at it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|祖母|そぼ}は{f|六十年|ろくじゅうねん}{f|同|おな}じ{f|店|みせ}で<b>{f|働|はたら}き{f|続|つづ}け</b>ています。
My grandmother has been working in the same shop for sixty years.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|足|あし}が{f|痛|いた}くても、ゴールまで<b>{f|走|はし}り{f|続|つづ}け</b>た。
My legs hurt, but I kept running all the way to the finish.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|外|そと}が{f|暗|くら}くなっても、{f|兄|あに}は{f|机|つくえ}に{f|向|む}かって<b>{f|書|か}き{f|続|つづ}け</b>ていた。
Even after it got dark outside, my brother stayed at his desk writing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|野菜|やさい}の{f|値段|ねだん}が<b>{f|上|あ}がり{f|続|つづ}け</b>ているので、{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}が{f|大変|たいへん}です。
Vegetable prices just keep going up, so shopping is hard work.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|来|こ}なくても、{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|毎週|まいしゅう}{f|友|とも}だちに{f|手紙|てがみ}を<b>{f|送|おく}り{f|続|つづ}け</b>ました。
Even though no reply ever came, she went on sending her friend a letter every week.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The suffix is register-neutral, but it is rarely emotionally flat. Because it
insists that something *did not stop*, it tends to pick up either admiration or
exasperation from the surrounding sentence.
<code>{f|夢|ゆめ}を{f|追|お}い{f|続|つづ}ける</code> is a compliment;
<code>{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}い{f|続|つづ}ける</code> is not. That is context, not
grammar - but it is why the word is so common in speeches and so common in
complaints.

The form you will use most is <code>～{f|続|つづ}けている</code>, which is how
Japanese says "have been doing X for N years":
<code>{f|二十年|にじゅうねん}{f|住|す}み{f|続|つづ}けています</code>. The plain past
<code>～{f|続|つづ}けた</code> closes the stretch off, so it belongs to stories about
finished periods.

Two neighbours worth knowing. <code>{f|続|つづ}けて</code> works as a bare adverb
meaning "in a row" or "carry on":
<code>{f|三日|みっか}{f|続|つづ}けて{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った</code>,
<code>どうぞ{f|続|つづ}けてください</code>. And with a する-noun, spoken Japanese
often prefers the independent verb - <code>{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|続|つづ}ける</code>
rather than <code>{f|練習|れんしゅう}し{f|続|つづ}ける</code> - especially when the
activity has a name and a shape.

## Contrast

### ～{f|続|つづ}ける vs ～{f|続|つづ}く

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if a person is keeping it going, <code>{f|続|つづ}ける</code>.
If it simply goes on, <code>{f|続|つづ}く</code>. The particles follow: `を` with
the first, `が` with the second.

</div>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|三日|みっか}も<b>{f|降|ふ}り{f|続|つづ}い</b>ている。
It's been raining for three days straight.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|夜中|よなか}まで<b>ゲームをし{f|続|つづ}け</b>ていた。
My son carried on playing games until the middle of the night.
</pre>

With weather and other things nobody controls, both suffixes are heard, and
<code>{f|降|ふ}り{f|続|つづ}く</code> is the more usual - it is what a forecast says.
With a human agent only <code>{f|続|つづ}ける</code> is possible; there is no
❌<code>{f|話|はな}し{f|続|つづ}く</code>.

The independent verbs split the same way, and this is the pair to memorise:

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}を<b>{f|続|つづ}け</b>ましょう。
Let's carry on with the meeting.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|六時|ろくじ}まで<b>{f|続|つづ}き</b>ました。
The meeting went on until six.
</pre>

### ～{f|続|つづ}ける vs ～ている

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ている` says the action is **under way**;
<code>{f|続|つづ}ける</code> says it **has not stopped**. The first is a snapshot,
the second is a claim about the whole stretch.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}、レポートを{f|書|か}い<b>ています</b>。
I'm writing the report right now.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|朝|あさ}から{f|同|おな}じレポートを<b>{f|書|か}き{f|続|つづ}け</b>ています。
I've been writing the same report since this morning.
</pre>

Both are true of the same moment, and only the second complains. Note also what
`ている` can do that this suffix cannot: it covers habits
(<code>{f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|走|はし}っています</code>) and resulting states
(<code>{f|結婚|けっこん}しています</code>). ❌<code>{f|結婚|けっこん}し{f|続|つづ}けています</code>
is not a way to say you are married - it would mean you keep getting married.

### ～{f|続|つづ}ける vs ずっと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ずっと` is an **adverb** you drop into any sentence;
<code>{f|続|つづ}ける</code> is a **suffix** that changes the verb. They stack
happily, and `ずっと` reaches places the suffix cannot.

</div>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どものころから<b>ずっと</b>サッカーが{f|好|す}きです。
I've loved football ever since I was a child.
</pre>

<code>{f|好|す}き</code> is not a verb, so there is no stem for
<code>{f|続|つづ}ける</code> to attach to; `ずっと` has no such problem and works with
adjectives, nouns and states alike. It also does duty as "a long way back" - 
<code>ずっと{f|前|まえ}に{f|会|あ}いました</code> - which has nothing to do with
continuing at all.

Where both are available, `ずっと` reports the span while
<code>{f|続|つづ}ける</code> insists on the unbrokenness, and putting them together
is completely normal:
<code>ずっと{f|待|ま}ち{f|続|つづ}けていました</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}む<b>{f|続|つづ}ける</b> → ✅ {f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}み<b>{f|続|つづ}ける</b>
  The suffix takes the ます-stem, never the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|会議|かいぎ}を{f|続|つづ}きます。 → ✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}を<b>{f|続|つづ}け</b>ます。／{f|会議|かいぎ}が<b>{f|続|つづ}き</b>ます。
  <code>{f|続|つづ}く</code> is intransitive and cannot take a `を` object. Pick the
  verb that matches the particle.

- ❌ {f|夜中|よなか}まで{f|勉強|べんきょう}<b>{f|続|つづ}け</b>ました。 → ✅ {f|夜中|よなか}まで{f|勉強|べんきょう}<b>し{f|続|つづ}け</b>ました。
  A する-noun needs the `し` to make the compound - or use the independent verb
  with `を`: <code>{f|勉強|べんきょう}を{f|続|つづ}けました</code>.

- ❌ この{f|町|まち}に{f|十年|じゅうねん}{f|住|す}み<b>{f|続|つづ}け</b>ます。（meaning "I've lived here ten years"） → ✅ この{f|町|まち}に{f|十年|じゅうねん}{f|住|す}み<b>{f|続|つづ}け</b>ています。
  A stretch that reaches the present needs `ている`. The plain
  <code>{f|続|つづ}けます</code> is a promise about the future.

## Practice

**1.** {f|三時間|さんじかん}{f|以上|いじょう}{f|歩|ある}き____て、{f|足|あし}が{f|痛|いた}くなりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|三時間|さんじかん}{f|以上|いじょう}{f|歩|ある}き<b>{f|続|つづ}け</b>て、{f|足|あし}が{f|痛|いた}くなりました。

The walking went on without a break for over three hours, which is exactly what
this suffix claims. <code>{f|歩|ある}いて</code> alone would report the walking
without saying it never stopped, and the sore feet would lose their explanation.

</details>

**2.** すみません、{f|話|はなし}を（{f|続|つづ}けて・{f|続|つづ}いて）ください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|話|はなし}を<b>{f|続|つづ}け</b>てください。

You are asking a person to keep going, and the `を` marks what they are keeping
going - both point to the transitive verb.
<code>{f|続|つづ}く</code> would need <code>{f|話|はなし}が{f|続|つづ}く</code>, "the
conversation carries on by itself", which nobody can be asked to do.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "She has been teaching at the same school for twenty years."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|二十年|にじゅうねん}{f|同|おな}じ{f|学校|がっこう}で<b>{f|教|おし}え{f|続|つづ}け</b>ています。

For a stretch that runs up to the present, the suffix takes `ている`. The plain
past <code>{f|教|おし}え{f|続|つづ}けました</code> would close the period off and
imply she has stopped.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|去年|きょねん}から{f|運動|うんどう}{f|続|つづ}けていますが、なかなか{f|痩|や}せません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|去年|きょねん}から{f|運動|うんどう}<b>を{f|続|つづ}け</b>ています…（or {f|運動|うんどう}<b>し{f|続|つづ}け</b>ています）

A する-noun cannot sit bare against the suffix. Either insert `を` and use
<code>{f|続|つづ}ける</code> as an independent verb, or insert `し` and build the
compound. Both are natural; the `を` version is the more common in speech.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|一時間|いちじかん}{f|待|ま}っていました。
(b) {f|一時間|いちじかん}{f|待|ま}ち{f|続|つづ}けました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply reports that waiting was what you were doing for an hour. It is
neutral, and it would be a perfectly ordinary answer to "what were you doing?"

(b) puts weight on the unbrokenness: you stayed there, you did not give up, and
the hour felt like an hour. It invites sympathy or an apology, which is why it is
so often what you say to the person who was late.

</details>
