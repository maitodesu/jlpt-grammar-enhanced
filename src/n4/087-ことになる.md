# ～ことになる

**Meaning** · it has been decided that ～; it works out that ～ - an outcome, with
nobody named as having decided it

<code>ことになる</code> is the mirror image of <code>ことにする</code>. `なる` is the
verb of things happening by themselves, so the sentence reports a decision or an
arrangement as a **result**: something has been settled, and the sentence declines
to say by whom. English almost always has to supply an agent ("they decided",
"I've been transferred"); Japanese does not, and that gap is what makes this
pattern feel alien at first.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + ことになる | {f|通|かよ}う → {f|通|かよ}うことになる |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + ことになる | {f|通|かよ}わない → {f|通|かよ}わないことになる |
| Decision reached | ことになった／ことになりました | {f|通|かよ}うことになりました |
| Rule or arrangement in force | ことになっている | {f|通|かよ}うことになっています |
| Humble / business | ことになっております | お{f|届|とど}けすることになっております |
| "That amounts to" | plain clause + ということになる | {f|中止|ちゅうし}ということになる |
| Noun | Noun + ということになる | {f|休|やす}み → {f|休|やす}みということになる |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ことになる</code> takes a **verb** clause. A change of
state in an adjective or noun uses plain `なる`:
{f|寒|さむ}く**なる**, {f|先生|せんせい}に**なる** - never
❌<code>{f|寒|さむ}いことになる</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}たち、{f|来年|らいねん}<b>{f|結婚|けっこん}することになりました</b>。
We're getting married next year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}から{f|新|あたら}しい{f|先生|せんせい}が<b>{f|来|く}ることになりました</b>。
A new teacher will be joining us from next week.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|寮|りょう}では{f|十一時|じゅういちじ}までに<b>{f|帰|かえ}ることになっています</b>。
In this dormitory you're expected to be back by eleven.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}のため、{f|今日|きょう}の{f|試合|しあい}は<b>しないことになりました</b>。
Because of the typhoon, today's match has been called off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来年|らいねん}の{f|三月|さんがつ}で、この{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|十年|じゅうねん}<b>いることになります</b>。
Come March next year, I'll have been at this company ten years.
</pre>

<pre>
つまり、{f|最初|さいしょ}から{f|全部|ぜんぶ}やり{f|直|なお}す<b>ということになります</b>ね。
So in other words, we start the whole thing over from scratch.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}の{f|学校|がっこう}が{f|遠|とお}いので、{f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|駅|えき}まで<b>{f|送|おく}っていくことになりました</b>。
My son's school is a long way off, so it's ended up with me driving him to the station every morning.

Your order is scheduled for delivery next Wednesday.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Three things live inside this one form.

**1. A decision made elsewhere.** The everyday use.
{f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|支店|してん}に{f|移|うつ}ることになりました - the company
decided, and you are passing on the result. Because no agent is named, the
sentence works equally well when the decider is a committee, a rule, a
circumstance, or nobody at all.

**2. A rule in force.** <code>ことになっている</code> is how Japanese states
regulations, customs and standing arrangements:
{f|会社|かいしゃ}では{f|九時|くじ}までに{f|来|く}ることになっています,
ここでは{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|撮|と}らないことになっています. The `ている` is
essential - it says the decision is not news but the state of things. This is
also the polite way to refuse: rather than "you can't", you say "it isn't done",
and nobody has to be the one saying no.

**3. Logical consequence.** <code>ということになる</code> means *that amounts to*.
It is how you sum up: では、{f|来週|らいしゅう}は{f|休|やす}みということになりますね.
Here nothing has been decided at all; the sentence is doing arithmetic.

The social point is the interesting one. Japanese speakers very often report
their **own** decisions with <code>ことになりました</code>:
{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|退職|たいしょく}することになりました, said by someone who
resigned of their own accord. This is not deception. Putting the outcome forward
instead of your will is a way of not imposing, and it is close to standard for
announcements - marriages, job changes, moves - that affect the people listening.
The tendency is strong in workplaces and weaker among close friends, where
<code>ことにした</code> is perfectly normal. Read
<code>ことになりました</code> as "here is what has been settled", and do not assume
the speaker was passive.

Register is neutral; <code>ことになっております</code> is the humble business
version and belongs in customer-facing speech.

## Contrast

### ～ことになる vs ～ことにする

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ことにする</code> names the decider (you).
<code>ことになる</code> reports the outcome and leaves the decider out. That is the
whole distinction.

</div>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}を{f|変|か}えて<b>もらうことにしました</b>。
I decided to ask them to change my room.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}を{f|変|か}えて<b>もらうことになりました</b>。
It's been arranged that they'll change my room for me.
</pre>

Same event, two framings. The first foregrounds your initiative; the second
foregrounds the arrangement. Neither is more truthful - Japanese simply offers a
choice about how much of your own will to put on the table, and takes the modest
option more often than English does.

### ～ことになった vs ～ことになっている

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ことになった</code> is the **moment** something was
settled; <code>ことになっている</code> is the **state** of its being settled. News
versus rule.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|制服|せいふく}を<b>{f|着|き}ることになりました</b>。
From next month we'll be wearing uniforms. (this has just been decided)
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|学校|がっこう}では{f|制服|せいふく}を<b>{f|着|き}ることになっています</b>。
At this school you wear a uniform. (that's how it is)
</pre>

Use the past form to announce a change, and the `ている` form to explain the way
things are. Saying <code>ことになりました</code> about a rule that has been in
place for decades makes it sound like this morning's memo.

### ～ことになる vs ～ようになる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ことになる</code> is a **decision** landing;
<code>ようになる</code> is a **change** arriving - a new ability, or a habit that
grew.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来年|らいねん}、{f|海外|かいがい}で<b>{f|働|はたら}くことになりました</b>。
It's been settled that I'll work abroad next year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一年|いちねん}{f|習|なら}って、{f|英語|えいご}で<b>{f|話|はな}せるようになりました</b>。
After a year of lessons, I've got to the point where I can hold a conversation in English.
</pre>

The first happened at a stroke, because someone decided. The second happened
gradually, because you changed. If you can point to a meeting where it was agreed,
it is <code>ことになる</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|行|い}き**ます**ことになりました。 → ✅ {f|行|い}**く**ことになりました。
  `こと` is a noun being modified, so the verb before it is plain.

- ❌ この{f|会社|かいしゃ}では{f|制服|せいふく}を{f|着|き}ることに**なりました**。（describing a long-standing rule） → ✅ …{f|着|き}ることに**なっています**。
  A rule already in force needs the `ている` form. The past tense announces a
  change that has just been made.

- ❌ {f|今晩|こんばん}はカレーを{f|作|つく}ることになります。（your own choice） → ✅ {f|今晩|こんばん}はカレーを{f|作|つく}ることにします。
  If you are the one deciding and there is no arrangement to report,
  <code>ことにする</code> is the honest form. <code>ことになる</code> here sounds
  as though the curry were imposed on you.

- ❌ {f|来週|らいしゅう}から{f|忙|いそが}しいことになります。 → ✅ {f|来週|らいしゅう}から{f|忙|いそが}しくなります。
  <code>ことになる</code> attaches to verbs. Adjectives change with plain `なる`.

- ❌ {f|試験|しけん}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}ことになりました。 → ✅ {f|試験|しけん}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}**という**ことになりました。
  A noun needs `という` before `こと`. Without it there is nothing joining the two
  nouns.

## Practice

**1.** {f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|都合|つごう}で、{f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|名古屋|なごや}で{f|働|はたら}く____なりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|働|はたら}く<b>ことに</b>なりました。

The company's circumstances made the decision, so the outcome is reported with
<code>ことになる</code> and the agent is left unnamed. <code>ことにしました</code>
would claim the move was your idea.

</details>

**2.** {f|友|とも}だちに：「よく{f|考|かんが}えて、{f|留学|りゅうがく}する（ことにした・ことになった）んだ。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|留学|りゅうがく}する<b>ことにした</b>んだ。

<code>よく{f|考|かんが}えて</code> says the thinking was yours, so the decision is
yours too. Among friends there is no need to hide your own will - 
<code>ことになった</code> here would leave your friend wondering who decided for
you.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "At this hospital you're not allowed to use your phone."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|病院|びょういん}では{f|電話|でんわ}を<b>{f|使|つか}わないことになっています</b>。

A standing rule takes <code>ことになっている</code>, and the negative goes on the
main verb. This is softer than
<code>{f|使|つか}ってはいけません</code>: it presents the ban as an established
arrangement rather than as you forbidding something.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|金曜日|きんようび}は{f|休|やす}みことになりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|金曜日|きんようび}は{f|休|やす}み<b>という</b>ことになりました。

<code>{f|休|やす}み</code> is a noun, and a noun cannot modify `こと` directly.
`という` is the connector. Alternatively, rebuild it around a verb:
<code>{f|休|やす}むことになりました</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}すことにしました。
(b) {f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}すことになりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the move is your decision, taken by you, and it puts your will in the
foreground. It is what you say to a friend who asked what you had decided.

(b) reports the move as something that has been settled. It might mean your
company transferred you - or it might mean exactly the same situation as (a),
announced modestly. Japanese speakers frequently choose (b) for news that affects
other people, because it does not push the speaker's own choices at the listener.
Without further context, (b) does not actually tell you who decided; that is the
point of it.

</details>
