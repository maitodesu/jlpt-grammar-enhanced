# ～ながら

**Meaning** · while doing something else - two actions by one person at the same time

`～ながら` puts two activities on top of each other. The clause with `ながら` is the
**background**; the main clause at the end is what the sentence is really about. Both
actions must belong to the **same person**, and both must be things that last long
enough to overlap.

## Formation

`ながら` attaches to the ます-stem and never changes shape itself. All the tense and
politeness live in the final verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + ながら | {f|歩|ある}きます → {f|歩|ある}きながら |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + ながら | {f|食|た}べます → {f|食|た}べながら |
| Verb (する) | し + ながら | {f|運転|うんてん}する → {f|運転|うんてん}しながら |
| Verb (ている, concessive) | てい + ながら | {f|知|し}っている → {f|知|し}っていながら |
| い-adjective (concessive) | plain form + ながら（も） | {f|狭|せま}い → {f|狭|せま}いながらも |
| な-adjective (concessive) | stem + ながら（も） | {f|残念|ざんねん} → {f|残念|ざんねん}ながら |
| Noun (concessive) | Noun + ながら | {f|子|こ}ども → {f|子|こ}どもながら |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ながら` takes the same stem as `ます`, so
<code>{f|食|た}べます → {f|食|た}べながら</code>, never ❌<code>{f|食|た}べるながら</code>. The
verb before it also has to be one that *takes time*: you can walk while talking, but
not ❌<code>{f|着|つ}きながら</code> ("while arriving").

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the last four rows are a **different** `ながら`, meaning "although". It
attaches to `ている` forms, い-adjectives, な-adjective stems and bare nouns - shapes
the "at the same time" `ながら` never takes - so the form itself usually tells you which
one you are looking at. See the third Contrast section.

</div>

Regrettably, this time the plan has been called off.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ながら` is neutral and belongs equally to speech and writing. What learners get wrong
is not the register but the **weight**: whichever verb ends the sentence is the one
being reported. テレビを{f|見|み}<b>ながら</b>ごはんを{f|食|た}べる is a sentence about
eating; ごはんを{f|食|た}べ<b>ながら</b>テレビを{f|見|み}る is a sentence about watching
television. Both are correct, and they answer different questions.

The pattern also stretches over long spans of time, not just a single moment.
{f|働|はたら}きながら{f|勉強|べんきょう}する describes years of your life, and this use
is completely standard in a self-introduction or a job interview.

Modern Japanese has turned `ながら` into a noun prefix for doing something *while*
distracted: <code>ながら{f|運転|うんてん}</code> (using your phone at the wheel),
<code>ながら{f|食|た}べ</code>, <code>ながらスマホ</code>. These carry disapproval - they are the
words on the warning poster.

The "although" `ながら` is a different animal in register: it is **written and
formal**, and in conversation it survives mainly in fixed expressions - 
<code>{f|残念|ざんねん}ながら</code> (regrettably), <code>しかしながら</code> (however),
<code>いつもながら</code> (as always), <code>{f|失礼|しつれい}ながら</code> (if I may be so bold).
Adding `も` - <code>{f|狭|せま}いながらも</code> - makes the concession explicit and sounds
more literary still.

## Contrast

### ～ながら vs ～ている{f|間|あいだ}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ながら` needs **one** person doing both things. If the two clauses
have different subjects, you need <code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code>.

</div>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもが{f|昼寝|ひるね}をしている<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>、{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|片|かた}づけます。
I'll get my work done while the children are having their nap.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもの{f|話|はなし}を{f|聞|き}き<b>ながら</b>、{f|仕事|しごと}をしていました。
I was working while listening to my child talk.
</pre>

In the first, the sleeper and the worker are different people, so `ながら` is
impossible. In the second, one person is doing both. There is a second difference
worth noticing: <code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code> means "at some point during", so the main
action fits inside the other one, while `ながら` means the two run side by side for the
whole time.

### ～ながら vs the て-form

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - て puts one action *after* another, or gives the **means**;
`ながら` puts them on top of each other.

</div>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}まで{f|歩|ある}い<b>て</b>{f|行|い}きます。
I'll walk to the station. (walking is *how* I go)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|歩|ある}き<b>ながら</b>{f|考|かんが}えましょう。
Let's think it over as we walk.
</pre>

The て version answers "how did you get there?"; the `ながら` version answers "what
were you doing at the same time?" Swapping them changes the sentence rather than just
its style: <code>{f|歩|ある}いて{f|考|かんが}える</code> would suggest you walked and then
thought.

### Concessive ～ながら（も）vs ～のに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both mean "although". `のに` is spoken and carries surprise or
complaint; `ながらも` is written, restrained, and often faintly admiring.

</div>

<pre>
{f|小|ちい}さい{f|店|みせ}<b>ながらも</b>、{f|品|しな}ぞろえは{f|立派|りっぱ}だ。
Small though the shop is, the range it stocks is impressive.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|小|ちい}さい{f|店|みせ}な<b>のに</b>、{f|品|しな}ぞろえがすごいね。
The shop's tiny, but look at everything they've got!
</pre>

Two practical limits on the concessive `ながら`. It attaches only to a short list of
things - a ます-stem, a `ている` form, an い-adjective, a な-adjective stem or a plain
noun - so there is no ❌<code>{f|行|い}ったながら</code>. And with verbs it is almost
always a `ている` form, meaning "knowing full well that…":
<code>{f|知|し}っていながら</code>, <code>{f|分|わ}かっていながら</code>,
<code>{f|見|み}ていながら</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ごはんを{f|食|た}べる**ながら**テレビを{f|見|み}ます。 → ✅ ごはんを{f|食|た}べ**ながら**テレビを{f|見|み}ます。
  `ながら` attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|妹|いもうと}がピアノを{f|弾|ひ}き**ながら**、{f|私|わたし}は{f|勉強|べんきょう}しました。 → ✅ {f|妹|いもうと}がピアノを{f|弾|ひ}いている<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>、{f|私|わたし}は{f|勉強|べんきょう}しました。
  Two different people, so `ながら` cannot join them. The sentence as written claims
  your sister was studying.

- ❌ {f|電気|でんき}をつけ**ながら**{f|出|で}かけました。 → ✅ {f|電気|でんき}をつけた**まま**{f|出|で}かけました。
  <code>つける</code> is instantaneous - the light goes on and that's that. For "leaving
  something in a state", the word is `まま`.

- ❌ {f|歩|ある}き**ながらでした**、{f|話|はな}しました。 → ✅ {f|歩|ある}き**ながら**{f|話|はな}しました。
  `ながら` never takes tense or politeness. Both live on the final verb.

## Practice

**1.** {f|音楽|おんがく}を{f|聞|き}き____、{f|宿題|しゅくだい}をしています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|音楽|おんがく}を{f|聞|き}き<b>ながら</b>、{f|宿題|しゅくだい}をしています。

One person, two overlapping activities, and the homework is the main event - exactly
what `ながら` is for. The stem is <code>{f|聞|き}き</code>, from
<code>{f|聞|き}きます</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|赤|あか}ちゃんが{f|寝|ね}ている（ながら・{f|間|あいだ}に）、{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}ってきます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|赤|あか}ちゃんが{f|寝|ね}ている<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>、{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}ってきます。

The baby sleeps and you shop - two different subjects, so `ながら` is out.
<code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code> also gives the right meaning: the shopping happens somewhere
inside the nap, not for the whole of it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I studied Japanese while working at a hotel."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ホテルで{f|働|はたら}き<b>ながら</b>{f|日本語|にほんご}を{f|勉強|べんきょう}しました。

`ながら` happily covers a long stretch of life, not just a single moment. The past
tense goes on <code>{f|勉強|べんきょう}しました</code> only - <code>{f|働|はたら}きながら</code>
stays as it is.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けながら{f|寝|ね}てしまいました。（intended: "I fell asleep with the window open."）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けた<b>まま</b>{f|寝|ね}てしまいました。

<code>{f|開|あ}ける</code> takes a moment and is then over, so it cannot run alongside
sleeping. What you mean is that the window stayed open, and that is `まま`. As written,
the sentence describes you opening the window and sleeping simultaneously.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) テレビを{f|見|み}**ながら**ごはんを{f|食|た}べます。
(b) ごはんを{f|食|た}べ**ながら**テレビを{f|見|み}ます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both describe the same scene, but they report different things. (a) is a sentence
about a meal - television is the background noise, and it would answer "how do you
usually eat dinner?"

(b) is a sentence about watching television, with the meal as the accompaniment. You
would say it to explain how you spend your evenings, or to admit that dinner is not
getting your full attention.

</details>
