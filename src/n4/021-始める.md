# ～始める

**Meaning** · start doing something; begin to

<code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> welded onto the stem of another verb marks the moment an
action gets under way. It is the plain, unremarkable "start": no surprise, no
drama, and equally usable for something you planned, something you decided on the
spot, and a slow change in the weather.

## Formation

Attach <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> to the <b>ます-stem</b>. The result is a single
compound verb, and it conjugates as an ordinary る-verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + {f|始|はじ}める | {f|読|よ}みます → {f|読|よ}み{f|始|はじ}める |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + {f|始|はじ}める | {f|食|た}べます → {f|食|た}べ{f|始|はじ}める |
| Verb (する) | し + {f|始|はじ}める | {f|勉強|べんきょう}する → {f|勉強|べんきょう}し{f|始|はじ}める |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | き + {f|始|はじ}める | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|き}{f|始|はじ}める |
| い-adjective (change) | くなり + {f|始|はじ}める | {f|暑|あつ}い → {f|暑|あつ}くなり{f|始|はじ}める |
| な-adjective (change) | になり + {f|始|はじ}める | {f|元気|げんき}だ → {f|元気|げんき}になり{f|始|はじ}める |
| Conjugation | る-verb | {f|降|ふ}り{f|始|はじ}めた、{f|降|ふ}り{f|始|はじ}めている |
| Object particle | unchanged from the first verb | {f|本|ほん}<b>を</b>{f|読|よ}み{f|始|はじ}める |
| Event noun | Noun + <b>を</b> + {f|始|はじ}める | {f|会議|かいぎ}を{f|始|はじ}める |
| Intransitive twin | Noun + <b>が</b> + {f|始|はじ}まる | {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|始|はじ}まる |
| ❌ | ❌ dictionary form + {f|始|はじ}める | ❌{f|読|よ}む{f|始|はじ}める |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the compound keeps the particles of the **first** verb, not of
<code>{f|始|はじ}める</code>. <code>{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}む</code> becomes
<code>{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}み{f|始|はじ}める</code>; nothing moves and nothing is added.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> wants a verb that **lasts**. An action over in
an instant resists it: ❌<code>{f|着|つ}き{f|始|はじ}める</code> of one person arriving
is odd, because there is no process to begin. It becomes fine the moment the
subject is plural, though, since many arrivals spread out over time:
<code>お{f|客|きゃく}さんが{f|着|つ}き{f|始|はじ}めた</code> is perfectly natural.

</div>

<pre>

Oh, it's started raining.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}からギターを{f|習|なら}い<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ました。
I took up the guitar last year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|六時|ろくじ}になったので、{f|夕|ゆう}ごはんを{f|作|つく}り<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ます。
It's six, so I'm going to start making dinner.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|五月|ごがつ}に{f|入|はい}って、{f|朝|あさ}から{f|暑|あつ}くなり<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>た。
Now that it's May, it's started being hot from the morning on.
</pre>

<pre>

The children started filing into the classroom one by one.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

Compound verbs of this shape are register-neutral. <code>{f|書|か}き{f|始|はじ}める</code>
is as ordinary in a text message as in a report, and the politeness of the
sentence is decided at the very end - <code>{f|書|か}き{f|始|はじ}めた</code> versus
<code>{f|書|か}き{f|始|はじ}めました</code>.

What makes <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> the workhorse is that it has no attitude. It
does not say the start was sudden, welcome, overdue or surprising; it just puts a
mark on the timeline. That neutrality is also why it can appear in forms its rival
<code>～{f|出|だ}す</code> cannot: you can request one
(<code>{f|書|か}き{f|始|はじ}めてください</code>), propose one
(<code>{f|食|た}べ{f|始|はじ}めましょう</code>), or plan one
(<code>{f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|通|かよ}い{f|始|はじ}めます</code>).

With する-nouns there are two routes and they are not quite the same.
<code>{f|勉強|べんきょう}し{f|始|はじ}める</code> treats the studying as an activity
that has now begun; <code>{f|勉強|べんきょう}を{f|始|はじ}める</code> treats it as a
thing you have taken up. For events with a fixed shape - a meeting, a lesson, a
ceremony - only the second works: you say
<code>{f|会議|かいぎ}を{f|始|はじ}めます</code>, never ❌<code>{f|会議|かいぎ}し{f|始|はじ}めます</code>.

In speech you will also hear the whole idea carried by
<code>{f|始|はじ}めた</code> on its own - <code>もう{f|始|はじ}めた?</code> - where
context supplies the verb. That is ellipsis, not a different pattern.

## Contrast

### ～{f|始|はじ}める vs ～{f|出|だ}す

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> is a **neutral** start;
<code>{f|出|だ}す</code> is a **sudden, unplanned** one. If you chose to do it, or
you are asking someone else to, it has to be <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code>.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|水泳|すいえい}を{f|習|なら}い<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ます。
I'm starting swimming lessons next month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|後|うし}ろの{f|犬|いぬ}が{f|急|きゅう}に{f|走|はし}り<b>{f|出|だ}し</b>た。
The dog behind us suddenly took off running.
</pre>

The first is a decision announced in advance, and
❌<code>{f|習|なら}い{f|出|だ}します</code> would be strange - you cannot schedule a
surprise. The second is an event nobody controlled, which is exactly
<code>{f|出|だ}す</code>'s territory.

The clearest test is the form of the verb. Requests, invitations and volitionals
are closed to <code>{f|出|だ}す</code>:
❌<code>{f|書|か}き{f|出|だ}してください</code>, ❌<code>{f|歌|うた}い{f|出|だ}そう</code>.
Their <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> versions are unremarkable.

### ～{f|始|はじ}める vs {f|始|はじ}まる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> needs somebody doing the starting;
<code>{f|始|はじ}まる</code> is the thing starting by itself. The pair splits exactly
as <code>{f|開|あ}ける</code>／<code>{f|開|あ}く</code> does.

</div>

<pre>
{f|九時|くじ}にミーティングを<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ます。
We'll start the meeting at nine. (someone is starting it)
</pre>

<pre>
ミーティングは{f|九時|くじ}に<b>{f|始|はじ}まり</b>ます。
The meeting starts at nine. (that's the schedule)
</pre>

Note the particles: <code>を</code> with <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code>,
<code>が</code>／<code>は</code> with <code>{f|始|はじ}まる</code>. The compound suffix
built on the first of these is the only one that exists - there is **no**
❌<code>{f|読|よ}み{f|始|はじ}まる</code>. If you want to say a process got under way
on its own, either use <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> anyway
(<code>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}り{f|始|はじ}めた</code>) or reach for
<code>{f|出|だ}す</code>.

### ～{f|始|はじ}める vs ～てくる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> points at the **starting line**;
`～てくる` traces a change **moving towards now**. Both fit gradual change, and
they emphasise different halves of it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|葉|は}が{f|赤|あか}くなり<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ました。
The leaves have started turning red.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|葉|は}が{f|赤|あか}くなって<b>きました</b>。
The leaves have been turning red.
</pre>

The first says the process has a beginning and we have just passed it. The second
says the change has been accumulating and here is where it has got to. In everyday
speech about weather and seasons, `～てくる` is the more common of the two, and the
two happily combine: <code>{f|寒|さむ}くなり{f|始|はじ}めてきた</code> is heard, though
it is doing the job twice over.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}む<b>{f|始|はじ}める</b> → ✅ {f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}み<b>{f|始|はじ}める</b>
  The suffix attaches to the ます-stem, never to the dictionary form:
  {f|読|よ}みます → {f|読|よ}み + {f|始|はじ}める.

- ❌ {f|手紙|てがみ}を{f|書|か}き<b>{f|始|はじ}まり</b>ました。 → ✅ {f|手紙|てがみ}を{f|書|か}き<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ました。
  <code>{f|始|はじ}まる</code> is intransitive and cannot take the `を` object.
  The compound suffix is always <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code>.

- ❌ {f|来週|らいしゅう}から{f|運動|うんどう}<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ます。 → ✅ {f|来週|らいしゅう}から{f|運動|うんどう}<b>を</b>{f|始|はじ}めます。／{f|運動|うんどう}<b>し</b>{f|始|はじ}めます。
  A する-noun needs either `を` in front of the independent verb, or `し` to make
  the compound. It cannot sit bare against <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code>.

- ❌ {f|寒|さむ}い<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ました。 → ✅ {f|寒|さむ}く<b>なり</b>{f|始|はじ}めました。
  Adjectives have no ます-stem. Turn the adjective into the verb of change first - 
  <code>{f|寒|さむ}くなる</code> - and attach the suffix to that.

## Practice

**1.** {f|公園|こうえん}の{f|桜|さくら}が{f|咲|さ}き____ました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|公園|こうえん}の{f|桜|さくら}が{f|咲|さ}き<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ました。

<code>{f|咲|さ}きます</code> gives the stem <code>{f|咲|さ}き</code>, and
<code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> bolts straight onto it. Blossom opening over several days
is a gradual process, which is exactly what this suffix wants.

</details>

**2.** {f|来年|らいねん}から{f|新|あたら}しい{f|仕事|しごと}を（{f|始|はじ}めます・{f|始|はじ}まります）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|来年|らいねん}から{f|新|あたら}しい{f|仕事|しごと}を<b>{f|始|はじ}め</b>ます。

The `を` tells you the job is being acted on by somebody, and only the transitive
<code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> takes an object. With
<code>{f|始|はじ}まります</code> the sentence would need `が`:
<code>{f|新|あたら}しい{f|仕事|しごと}が{f|始|はじ}まります</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I started studying Japanese three years ago."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|三年|さんねん}{f|前|まえ}に{f|日本語|にほんご}を<b>{f|勉強|べんきょう}し{f|始|はじ}め</b>ました。

For a する-noun the stem is `し`, so the compound is
<code>{f|勉強|べんきょう}し{f|始|はじ}める</code>. The `を` still belongs to
<code>{f|日本語|にほんご}</code> and does not move.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: みんなそろいましたから、{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べる{f|始|はじ}めましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ みんなそろいましたから、{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}<b>べ{f|始|はじ}め</b>ましょう。

The dictionary form cannot carry the suffix. Take the ます-stem
<code>{f|食|た}べ</code> and attach <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code> to it; the invitation
ending `ましょう` then goes on the compound as a whole.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) みんなが{f|歩|ある}き{f|始|はじ}めた。
(b) みんなが{f|歩|ある}き{f|出|だ}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply records that the walking started. It fits a group that had been waiting
for a signal, or a hike that begins at the planned hour - nothing about it is
remarkable.

(b) adds abruptness. Something set them off, or they moved before you expected it,
and the sentence carries a flicker of surprise. That extra colour is why
<code>{f|出|だ}す</code> cannot be used to announce your own plans or to ask someone
to get going.

</details>
