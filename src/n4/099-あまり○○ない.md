# あまり～ない

**Meaning** · not very; not much; not particularly

`あまり` sits in front of a negative and softens it. It converts a flat denial into
a measured one: not "I don't like it" but "I'm not all that keen". Because
Japanese conversation avoids blunt refusals, this is one of the most-used adverbs
in the language - and one learners under-use, because English rarely needs it.

## Formation

`あまり` is an adverb, but it comes with a condition attached, which is why it is
treated as a pattern: the predicate at the end of its clause **must be negative**.
The two halves belong together the way <code>しか～ない</code> does.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective | あまり + ～くない | {f|高|たか}い → あまり{f|高|たか}くない |
| な-adjective | あまり + ～じゃない | {f|静|しず}かだ → あまり{f|静|しず}かじゃない |
| Noun predicate | あまり + ～じゃない | あまり{f|上手|じょうず}じゃない |
| Verb | あまり + ない-form | {f|見|み}る → あまり{f|見|み}ない |
| Polite | あまり + ～ません／～ないです | あまり{f|飲|の}みません |
| Past | あまり + ～なかった／～ませんでした | あまり{f|寒|さむ}くなかった |
| Casual | あんまり／あんま + negative | あんまりおいしくない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `あまり` goes in front of whatever it is measuring, and the
negative comes at the end of that same clause. Anything may sit between them:
あまり{f|日本|にほん}の{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}ません.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - `あまり` with a **positive** predicate is a different, more literary
construction meaning "excessively": あまりの{f|寒|さむ}さに{f|驚|おどろ}いた, "I
was startled by how bitterly cold it was". At N4 the safe rule is the one above - 
`あまり` takes a negative.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|料理|りょうり}は<b>あまり</b>{f|辛|から}く<b>ありません</b>。
This dish isn't very spicy.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}は<b>あまり</b>テレビを{f|見|み}<b>ません</b>。
I don't watch much television.
</pre>

<pre>
きのうの{f|夜|よる}は<b>あまり</b>{f|眠|ねむ}れ<b>ませんでした</b>。
I didn't sleep very well last night.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|映画|えいが}は<b>あまり</b>おもしろく<b>なかった</b>ですね。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|漢字|かんじ}は<b>あんまり</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えられ<b>ない</b>んです。
I'm not very good at getting kanji to stick, to be honest.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}から{f|遠|とお}いので、この{f|店|みせ}は<b>あまり</b>{f|込|こ}み<b>ません</b>。
It's a long way from the station, so this place doesn't get very crowded.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|給料|きゅうりょう}はいいのですが、{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|時間|じかん}が<b>あまり</b>{f|取|と}れ<b>ません</b>。

</pre>

<pre>

I'm not that fond of sweet things, so I'll just have a drink.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`あまり` itself is register-neutral. What varies is the contraction:

- **あまり** - the standard form. Safe everywhere, including writing.
- **あんまり** - very common in ordinary speech, slightly warmer and more
  emphatic. Fine in conversation, out of place in a report or an exam essay.
- **あんま** - clipped and quite casual. Recognise it; think twice before using it.

The reason to reach for `あまり` is almost always **politeness**. Japanese treats a
bare negative judgement as blunt, so the softened version is the default in
company:

- {f|嫌|きら}いです - "I dislike it." Strong, and can sound aggressive about
  someone's cooking.
- {f|好|す}きではありません - "I don't like it." Still flat.
- あまり{f|好|す}きではありません - "I'm not particularly keen." This is the one
  people actually say.

The same softening lets `あまり` function as a **polite refusal**. Asked whether
you can come on Saturday, {f|土曜日|どようび}はあまり… - trailing off, with no
negative at all - is understood as no. The listener supplies the rest. Learning to
hear this saves a lot of confusion.

Do not confuse the degree with zero. あまり{f|飲|の}みません means you drink a
little; {f|飲|の}みません means you do not drink; ぜんぜん{f|飲|の}みません means
never, not a drop.

## Contrast

### あまり～ない vs ぜんぜん～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `あまり` means a small amount. `ぜんぜん` means none at all.

</div>

<pre>
{f|肉|にく}は<b>あまり</b>{f|食|た}べ<b>ません</b>。
I don't eat much meat.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|肉|にく}は<b>ぜんぜん</b>{f|食|た}べ<b>ません</b>。
I don't eat meat at all.
</pre>

The first speaker will accept a slice of ham; the second will not. Because
`ぜんぜん` is absolute, it is also more forceful, and using it about someone's
work or cooking can sound harsh where `あまり` would be diplomatic. If you are
unsure how strong to be, `あまり` is the safer word.

### あまり～ない vs a plain negative

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the plain negative states a fact. `あまり` states the same fact
politely, and leaves the door slightly open.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|辺|へん}のことは{f|知|し}り<b>ません</b>。
I don't know this area.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|辺|へん}のことは<b>あまり</b>{f|知|し}り<b>ません</b>。
I don't know this area very well.
</pre>

The second is both more modest and more accurate - you know a little. In
conversation the softened version also invites the other person to keep talking,
where the flat one closes the topic. This is why Japanese speakers add `あまり` to
statements that are, strictly speaking, absolute.

### あまり～ない vs そんなに～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `あまり` measures against a general expectation. `そんなに`
measures against something **the other person just said or did**.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}は<b>あまり</b>{f|高|たか}く<b>ない</b>ですよ。
This place isn't very expensive.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}は<b>そんなに</b>{f|高|たか}く<b>ない</b>ですよ。
It isn't that expensive here - not as much as you're imagining.
</pre>

`そんなに` points backwards at a standard someone else raised: they winced at the
menu, or said they had heard the place was pricey. `あまり` needs no such
antecedent and works as an opening remark. In many contexts either is fine, and
`そんなに` simply sounds more conversational.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}は<b>あまり</b>{f|簡単|かんたん}です。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は<b>あまり</b>{f|簡単|かんたん}じゃありません。
  `あまり` cannot sit with a positive predicate. Without the negative the sentence
  has no meaning to land on.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}はあまり{f|暑|あつ}かったです。（meaning "it was very hot"） → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}はとても{f|暑|あつ}かったです。
  `あまり` is not a synonym for とても. It looks like an intensifier and behaves
  like the opposite of one.

- ❌ この{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}の{f|数字|すうじ}は<b>あんまり</b>{f|正|ただ}しくありません。 → ✅ ～は<b>あまり</b>{f|正|ただ}しくありません。
  Grammatically fine, but `あんまり` is spoken register and clashes with a written
  document.

- ❌ 「{f|納豆|なっとう}は{f|好|す}きですか。」「ぜんぜん{f|好|す}きじゃありません。」（meaning "not all that keen"） → ✅ 「あまり{f|好|す}きじゃありません。」
  `ぜんぜん` is a total rejection and can sting. If you only mean "not
  especially", `あまり` is the word - and the polite one.

## Practice

**1.** {f|今朝|けさ}は{f|忙|いそが}しくて、____{f|食|た}べ____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|今朝|けさ}は{f|忙|いそが}しくて、<b>あまり</b>{f|食|た}べ<b>ませんでした</b>。

`あまり` and the negative ending are one unit: you cannot supply one without the
other. The meaning is that you ate a little, not that you skipped breakfast - for
that you would need ぜんぜん{f|食|た}べませんでした.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural option:
「{f|新|あたら}しいカメラ、{f|使|つか}ってみましたか。」「いいえ、まだ（あまり・とても）{f|使|つか}っていません。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

「まだ<b>あまり</b>{f|使|つか}っていません。」

The reply ends in a negative, and only `あまり` can pair with one. `とても` needs a
positive predicate - とても{f|使|つか}いやすいです - so it has nothing to attach to
here.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, politely: "I'm not very good at singing."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|歌|うた}は<b>あまり</b>{f|上手|じょうず}じゃありません。

{f|上手|じょうず} is a な-adjective, so its negative is じゃありません, and
`あまり` sits in front of the whole thing. This is the standard modest reply when
someone praises your singing; {f|下手|へた}です would be true but unnecessarily
harsh about yourself.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|町|まち}は{f|静|しず}かあまりじゃないです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|町|まち}は<b>あまり</b>{f|静|しず}かじゃないです。

`あまり` is an adverb and has to come **before** the word it modifies, not after
it. Word order is what tells the listener how much of the sentence `あまり` is
measuring.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) その{f|話|はなし}は{f|知|し}りません。
(b) その{f|話|はなし}は<b>あまり</b>{f|知|し}りません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a clean denial: the topic is new to you, and the sentence closes the
subject.

(b) admits partial knowledge and hedges it. You have heard something, but not
enough to be useful - and by declining to sound certain, you invite the other
person to fill you in. In a workplace, (b) is also the safer answer, because it
does not commit you to knowing nothing.

</details>
