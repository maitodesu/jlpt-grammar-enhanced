# ～予定だ

**Meaning** · to be scheduled to; to be due to - the plan is on a calendar, not
just in your head

{f|予定|よてい} is an ordinary noun meaning *schedule* or *arrangement*, so
<code>{f|行|い}く{f|予定|よてい}です</code> says literally "the arrangement is that
I go". That is why it feels so different from `つもり`, which reports what you have
decided: <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> points at something outside you - a date, a
booking, a timetable other people can also see.

## Formation

The verb in front stays **plain**, exactly as it does before `つもり`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + {f|予定|よてい}だ | {f|着|つ}く → {f|着|つ}く{f|予定|よてい}だ |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + {f|予定|よてい}だ | {f|出|で}ない → {f|出|で}ない{f|予定|よてい}だ |
| Noun | Noun + の + {f|予定|よてい}だ | {f|出張|しゅっちょう} → {f|出張|しゅっちょう}の{f|予定|よてい}だ |
| Polite | {f|予定|よてい}だ → {f|予定|よてい}です | {f|着|つ}く{f|予定|よてい}です |
| Arrangement that changed | {f|予定|よてい}だった／でした | {f|着|つ}く{f|予定|よてい}でした |
| No such arrangement | ～{f|予定|よてい}はない／はありません | {f|行|い}く{f|予定|よてい}はありません |
| Honorific (asking others) | ご{f|予定|よてい} | ご{f|予定|よてい}はいかがですか |
| As a plain noun | {f|予定|よてい}がある／を{f|立|た}てる／が{f|変|か}わる | {f|週末|しゅうまつ}は{f|予定|よてい}があります |
| Fixed phrase | {f|予定|よてい}どおり | {f|予定|よてい}どおりに{f|進|すす}む |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ❌<code>{f|行|い}きます{f|予定|よてい}です</code> is the same
error as ❌<code>{f|行|い}きますつもりです</code>. A noun is being modified, and a
noun-modifying clause is always plain.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|三時|さんじ}に<b>{f|終|お}わる{f|予定|よてい}です</b>。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}、{f|大阪|おおさか}へ<b>{f|出張|しゅっちょう}する{f|予定|よてい}です</b>。
I'm due to travel to Osaka on business next week.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}の{f|午後|ごご}は{f|健康|けんこう}{f|診断|しんだん}<b>の{f|予定|よてい}です</b>。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|今月|こんげつ}は{f|忙|いそが}しいので、{f|旅行|りょこう}に<b>{f|行|い}く{f|予定|よてい}はありません</b>。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|飛行機|ひこうき}は{f|十時|じゅうじ}に<b>{f|着|つ}く{f|予定|よてい}でした</b>が、{f|一時間|いちじかん}{f|遅|おく}れました。
The plane was due in at ten, but it was an hour late.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> is **impersonal by design**, and that is its whole
value. It reports an arrangement without saying whose will produced it, so it can
describe a plane, a shop's opening hours, a company's project or your own Tuesday
with the same words. `つもり`, by contrast, can only ever describe a person's
intention.

That neutrality makes it the polite register for other people's plans. Asking a
superior <code>ご{f|予定|よてい}はいかがでしょうか</code> is safe; asking about
their `つもり` sounds like an interrogation. In business Japanese the noun turns up
constantly on its own - <code>{f|予定|よてい}を{f|立|た}てる</code> (draw up a
schedule), <code>{f|予定|よてい}を{f|変更|へんこう}する</code> (reschedule),
<code>{f|予定|よてい}どおり</code> (as planned) - and
<code>～{f|予定|よてい}となっております</code> is the standard humble phrasing for
telling a customer when something will happen.

Two limits to keep straight:

- **An arrangement is not a forecast.** Something has to have been *arranged* by
  someone. ❌<code>{f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る{f|予定|よてい}です</code>
  is wrong; weather takes <code>{f|予報|よほう}</code>, `でしょう` or `そうです`. A
  train arriving at 10:04 is fine, because a timetable is an arrangement.
- **<code>{f|予定|よてい}でした</code> implies it did not go that way.** Like
  <code>つもりだった</code>, the past tense sets up a "but". If the plan was kept,
  say <code>{f|予定|よてい}どおり{f|終|お}わりました</code>.

Because <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> keeps you out of the sentence, it can also be
used to duck. Answering
<code>{f|参加|さんか}する{f|予定|よてい}です</code> instead of
<code>{f|参加|さんか}します</code> quietly leaves room for the schedule to change
 - useful, and occasionally heard as non-committal.

## Contrast

### ～{f|予定|よてい}だ vs ～つもりだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> is what is booked;
`つもり` is what you have decided. If nothing exists outside your own head, it is
not a <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code>.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|京都|きょうと}で{f|友|とも}だちに<b>{f|会|あ}う{f|予定|よてい}です</b>。
I'm meeting a friend in Kyoto next month. (arranged, date agreed)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|京都|きょうと}で{f|友|とも}だちに<b>{f|会|あ}うつもりです</b>。
I mean to see a friend in Kyoto next month. (my plan; nothing fixed yet)
</pre>

The corollary is that <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> does not carry determination.
Pairing it with a word like <code>{f|絶対|ぜったい}に</code> or
<code>{f|今年|ことし}こそ</code> is odd, because those express resolve and a
schedule has none. Resolve is `つもり` territory.

### ～{f|予定|よてい}だ vs ～(よ)うと{f|思|おも}っている

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - three degrees of firmness:
<code>～(よ)うと{f|思|おも}っています</code> (a thought) → `つもりです` (a decision)
→ <code>{f|予定|よてい}です</code> (an entry in the diary).

</div>

<pre>
{f|夏休|なつやす}みは{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}へ<b>{f|行|い}こうと{f|思|おも}っています</b>。
I'm thinking of going to Hokkaido in the summer holidays.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|夏休|なつやす}みは{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}へ<b>{f|行|い}く{f|予定|よてい}です</b>。
I'm going to Hokkaido in the summer holidays. (tickets are booked)
</pre>

Answering a colleague's casual question with the second when nothing is booked
overstates your case; answering a travel agent with the first understates it.
Match the form to how real the plan is.

### ～{f|予定|よてい}だ vs ～ことになっている

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> is a **dated item**:
this happens, then. <code>ことになっている</code> is a **standing arrangement or
rule**: this is how it is done.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|月曜日|げつようび}に{f|試験|しけん}を<b>{f|受|う}ける{f|予定|よてい}です</b>。
I'm scheduled to sit the exam next Monday.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}では{f|靴|くつ}を<b>{f|脱|ぬ}ぐことになっています</b>。
You're supposed to take your shoes off in this room.
</pre>

Both hide the person who decided, which is why they feel related.
<code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> attaches to a point in time and can be missed;
<code>ことになっている</code> attaches to a practice and can be broken.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|来週|らいしゅう}、{f|帰|かえ}り**ます**{f|予定|よてい}です。 → ✅ {f|来週|らいしゅう}、{f|帰|かえ}**る**{f|予定|よてい}です。
  <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> is a noun, so the clause in front of it is plain.
  The politeness goes once, on `です`.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}る{f|予定|よてい}です。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}るそうです。
  Nobody schedules snow. <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> needs an arrangement behind
  it; a forecast is <code>{f|予報|よほう}</code>.

- ❌ {f|来月|らいげつ}は{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}し{f|予定|よてい}です。 → ✅ {f|来月|らいげつ}は{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}し**の**{f|予定|よてい}です。
  Two nouns are joined with `の`. This is the same rule that gives
  <code>{f|冗談|じょうだん}のつもり</code>.

- ❌ {f|今年|ことし}こそ、たばこをやめる{f|予定|よてい}です。 → ✅ {f|今年|ことし}こそ、たばこをやめるつもりです。
  <code>{f|今年|ことし}こそ</code> is a declaration of will, and a schedule has no
  will. Determination belongs with `つもり`.

- ❌ {f|先週|せんしゅう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|出|で}る{f|予定|よてい}でした。（and you did go） → ✅ {f|先週|せんしゅう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}には{f|予定|よてい}どおり{f|出|で}ました。
  The past <code>{f|予定|よてい}でした</code> almost always sets up a "but" - it
  says the plan was **not** kept. If it was, <code>{f|予定|よてい}どおり</code> is
  the phrase you want.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|金曜日|きんようび}までに{f|終|お}わ____{f|予定|よてい}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|終|お}わ<b>る</b>{f|予定|よてい}です。

<code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> is a noun and takes a plain, dictionary-form clause in
front of it. ❌<code>{f|終|お}わります{f|予定|よてい}</code> is the standard learner
slip.

</details>

**2.** {f|旅行|りょこう}{f|会社|がいしゃ}で：「ホテルはもう{f|取|と}りました。{f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|五日|いつか}に（{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}するつもりです・{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}する{f|予定|よてい}です）。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|五日|いつか}に<b>{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}する{f|予定|よてい}です</b>。

The hotel is booked and the date is fixed, so the plan exists outside your head - 
that is precisely what <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> reports. `つもり` would suggest
you had merely decided, which understates a booking.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The train was due to leave at 8:20, but it hasn't come yet."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|八時|はちじ}{f|二十分|にじゅっぷん}に<b>{f|出|で}る{f|予定|よてい}でした</b>が、まだ{f|来|き}ません。

A timetable is an arrangement, so a train can have a
<code>{f|予定|よてい}</code>. The past <code>{f|予定|よてい}でした</code> is doing
its usual work here: it signals that the schedule was not kept.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|木曜日|もくようび}は、{f|会議|かいぎ}{f|予定|よてい}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|木曜日|もくようび}は、{f|会議|かいぎ}<b>の</b>{f|予定|よてい}です。

<code>{f|会議|かいぎ}</code> is a noun, and one noun modifies another through `の`.
Only a **verb** clause attaches to <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> directly, and then
only in plain form.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|来年|らいねん}、{f|留学|りゅうがく}する{f|予定|よてい}です。
(b) {f|来年|らいねん}、{f|留学|りゅうがく}するつもりです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) presents study abroad as a settled arrangement: an acceptance letter, a
programme, dates. The speaker is reporting a fact about the calendar, and the
sentence says nothing about how they feel about it.

(b) presents it as the speaker's own decision. It can be said long before anything
is arranged, and it carries a personal commitment that (a) does not. If someone
argues with (b) they are arguing with you; if they argue with (a) they are arguing
with the schedule.

</details>
