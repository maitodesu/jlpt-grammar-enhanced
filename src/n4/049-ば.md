# ～ば

**Meaning** · if A, then B - A offered as the condition that would bring B about

`～ば` is the conditional of **reasoning**. It puts a premise on the table and
follows it to its consequence, which is why it dominates written Japanese,
instructions, arguments - and regrets about what you should have done.

## Formation

Every ば-form ends in **-eba**. For verbs, change the final `-u` of the dictionary
form to `-e` and add `ば`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | final -u → -e + ば | {f|書|か}く → {f|書|か}けば |
| Verb (う-verb) | final -u → -e + ば | {f|急|いそ}ぐ → {f|急|いそ}げば |
| Verb (る-verb) | る → れば | {f|見|み}る → {f|見|み}れば |
| Verb (する) | する → すれば | {f|練習|れんしゅう}する → {f|練習|れんしゅう}すれば |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | irregular | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|く}れば |
| Verb (negative) | ない → なければ | {f|行|い}かない → {f|行|い}かなければ |
| い-adjective | い → ければ | {f|忙|いそが}しい → {f|忙|いそが}しければ |
| いい | irregular | いい → よければ |
| い-adjective (negative) | くない → くなければ | {f|高|たか}くない → {f|高|たか}くなければ |
| な-adjective | stem + なら(ば) | {f|便利|べんり} → {f|便利|べんり}なら(ば) |
| Noun | Noun + なら(ば) | {f|雨|あめ} → {f|雨|あめ}なら(ば) |
| Noun / な-adj (formal) | + であれば | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}であれば |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the bottom two rows are why `なら` looks like part of this table. It
is: `なら(ば)` genuinely is the ば-form of the copula. But the standalone `なら`
you meet as its own pattern does a different job, and the two should be kept
apart in your head.

</div>

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if the ば-clause is an **action verb with the same subject**
as the main clause, you cannot end the sentence with a request, a command, an
invitation or a statement of intention. Switch to `たら`. The restriction lifts
when the ば-clause is a **state**: an adjective, `ある`／`いる`, a potential form,
or a negative.

</div>

That single rule explains why {f|分|わ}からなければ{f|聞|き}いてください is
perfectly idiomatic while ❌{f|東京|とうきょう}に{f|着|つ}けば{f|連絡|れんらく}してください
is not.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>{f|急|いそ}げば</b>、{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}いますよ。
If we hurry, we'll make it.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}が<b>あれば</b>、{f|手伝|てつだ}ってください。

</pre>

</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}を<b>{f|読|よ}まなければ</b>、{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}が{f|分|わ}かりません。

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が<b>{f|降|ふ}らなければ</b>、{f|川|かわ}でバーベキューをする{f|予定|よてい}です。
If it doesn't rain tomorrow, we're planning to have a barbecue by the river.
</pre>

<pre>

One look at this map and you'll see straight away where the car parks are.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ば` itself is neither polite nor casual, but it leans **written and considered**.
Notices, manuals, instructions, mathematical and legal prose, and any sentence
that is doing a piece of reasoning all reach for it. In relaxed conversation
`たら` is more frequent - except in the fixed patterns below, which are everywhere
in speech.

Patterns built on `ば` that you will meet constantly:

- **～ばいい** - "you just have to…", "it'd be good if…":
  この{f|紙|かみ}に{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|書|か}けばいいですか。
- **～ばよかった** - regret about the road not taken:
  もっと{f|早|はや}く{f|言|い}えばよかった。 There is no `～とよかった`.
- **～ば～ほど** - "the more…, the more…":
  {f|考|かんが}えれば{f|考|かんが}えるほど{f|分|わ}からなくなる。
- **～なければならない／なければいけない** - the written sibling of the N5
  `～なくてはいけない`. Same meaning, stiffer feel.

The casual contractions are the part that catches learners out in real
conversation:

- `なければ` → **なきゃ**: {f|帰|かえ}ら**なきゃ**。 - "I've got to go home."
  Very common, all ages, both genders.
- `なければならない` → **なきゃ** on its own, with the rest swallowed.
- `～れば` → **～りゃ**: {f|見|み}りゃ{f|分|わ}かる。 - blunt, older, and
  male-sounding. Recognise it; don't produce it.

## Contrast

### ～ば vs ～と

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `と` reports an **actual, invariable** link. `ば` floats a
**premise** and reasons from it, which is why only `ば` can end in a wish, an
offer or a request.

</div>

<pre>
{f|春|はる}になる<b>と</b>、この{f|池|いけ}の{f|氷|こおり}が{f|溶|と}けます。
Every spring the ice on this pond melts.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|春|はる}に<b>なれば</b>、この{f|池|いけ}の{f|氷|こおり}も{f|溶|と}けるでしょう。
Once spring comes, the ice on this pond should melt too.
</pre>

The `と` version is a fact about how the pond behaves. The `ば` version is the
speaker working forward from a supposition about *this* year - note that it needs
`でしょう`, because a prediction is a judgement, and `と` has no room for
judgements.

Two more `ば`-only territories: `もし` sits comfortably in front of it
(もし{f|間違|まちが}っていれば{f|直|なお}してください), and counterfactual regret
belongs to it alone (～ばよかった, ～ばよかったのに).

### ～ば vs ～たら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - where `ば` is blocked, `たら` steps in: before requests and
commands after an action verb, and for anything that actually happened once.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会社|かいしゃ}に<b>{f|着|つ}いたら</b>、すぐ{f|報告|ほうこく}してください。
Report in as soon as you get to the office.
</pre>

❌{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|着|つ}けば、すぐ{f|報告|ほうこく}してください is
rejected: {f|着|つ}く is an action the listener performs, and the main clause is a
command aimed at the same person. But make the condition a state and `ば`
returns:

<pre>
{f|分|わ}から<b>なければ</b>、いつでも{f|聞|き}いてください。
If you don't understand, ask me any time.
</pre>

The other gap is the past. `たら` can narrate a one-off sequence that really
happened - {f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}んだら{f|熱|ねつ}が{f|下|さ}がった - and `ば`
cannot. A `ば` sentence ending in the past tense is read as counterfactual
instead: {f|聞|き}けばよかった means "I should have asked", i.e. I didn't.

### ～ば vs ～なら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ば` conditions on the event **happening**; `なら` conditions
on a supposition **being true**. So `ば`'s main clause follows in time, while
`なら`'s can come first.

</div>

<pre>
{f|沖縄|おきなわ}に<b>{f|行|い}けば</b>、12{f|月|がつ}でも{f|泳|およ}げますよ。
If you go to Okinawa, you can swim even in December.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|沖縄|おきなわ}に{f|行|い}く<b>なら</b>、{f|飛行機|ひこうき}を{f|早|はや}めに{f|予約|よやく}したほうがいいですよ。
If you're going to Okinawa, you should book your flight early.
</pre>

In the first, the swimming happens once you are there. In the second, the booking
happens *before* you go - the trip is a premise the listener has just announced,
and the speaker responds to it. `ば` cannot reverse the order like that.

The second difference is mechanical: `なら` attaches straight to a bare noun and
picks it up as a topic - コーヒー**なら**{f|台所|だいどころ}にありますよ - a job
no ば-form can do.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|買|か}う**ば** → ✅ {f|買|か}**えば**
  The ば-form ends in **-eba**. For a う-verb the final vowel changes:
  ka-**u** → ka-**e**ba.

- ❌ {f|高|たか}**いければ** → ✅ {f|高|たか}**ければ**
  The final `い` of an い-adjective is *replaced* by `ければ`, not kept in front
  of it.

- ❌ **いければ**／**いいば** → ✅ **よければ**
  `いい` borrows every conjugated form from `よい`, here as elsewhere.

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}**ければ** → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}**なら(ば)**／{f|学生|がくせい}**であれば**
  `ければ` belongs to い-adjectives only. Nouns and な-adjectives take
  `なら(ば)`, or `であれば` in formal writing.

- ❌ {f|国|くに}に{f|帰|かえ}**れば**、{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|送|おく}ってください。 → ✅ {f|国|くに}に{f|帰|かえ}っ**たら**、{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|送|おく}ってください。
  Action verb plus a request aimed at the same person - the one configuration
  `ば` refuses.

## Practice

**1.** もっと{f|野菜|やさい}を{f|食|た}べ____、{f|健康|けんこう}になりますよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

もっと{f|野菜|やさい}を{f|食|た}べ<b>れば</b>、{f|健康|けんこう}になりますよ。

{f|食|た}べる is a る-verb, so the `る` is replaced by `れば`. The sentence is a
piece of reasoning - eat more vegetables and health follows - which is exactly
what `ば` is for.

</details>

**2.** {f|空港|くうこう}に（{f|着|つ}けば・{f|着|つ}いたら）、メールしてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|空港|くうこう}に<b>{f|着|つ}いたら</b>、メールしてください。

The condition is an action the listener performs, and the main clause is a
request to that same listener. `ば` is blocked in exactly that configuration.
Had the condition been a state - {f|時間|じかん}があれば - `ば` would have been
fine.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If it's cheap, I'll buy two."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|安|やす}<b>ければ</b>、2つ{f|買|か}います。

い-adjective: {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}ければ. The main clause is the speaker's
own decision, not a request to anyone, and the condition is a state - so `ば` is
comfortable here even though the sentence expresses an intention.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|本|ほん}が{f|高|たか}いければ、{f|図書館|としょかん}で{f|借|か}ります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|本|ほん}が{f|高|たか}<b>ければ</b>、{f|図書館|としょかん}で{f|借|か}ります。

い-adjectives lose their final `い` before `ければ`. Keeping it produces a form
that does not exist. The same trap catches {f|安|やす}ければ, {f|寒|さむ}ければ,
{f|忙|いそが}しければ.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}ければ、{f|涼|すず}しくなりますよ。
(b) {f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けると、{f|涼|すず}しくなります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b) is a statement about how this room works: open the window and it cools down,
every time. There is no speaker in it - it could be printed on a sign.

(a) presents opening the window as a **solution** the listener might adopt. The
speaker is reasoning from a premise and offering the result, which is why the
`よ` fits so naturally on the end. In practice you would say (a) to someone who
has just complained about the heat, and (b) to someone asking how the room
behaves.

</details>
