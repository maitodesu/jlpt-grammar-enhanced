# でございます

**Meaning** · the formal copula - "is", at the register of a hotel desk or a station announcement

`でございます` replaces `です`. Like `ございます`, it is
{f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご}: it raises the formality of the sentence without
honouring anybody in it. That is why you can say
{f|山田|やまだ}でございます about yourself, and why you must **not** say it about
the person you are talking to.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + でございます | {f|受付|うけつけ}です → {f|受付|うけつけ}でございます |
| な-adjective | な-adj + でございます | {f|結構|けっこう}です → {f|結構|けっこう}でございます |
| Negative | ではございません | ちがいます → ではございません |
| Past | でございました | {f|以上|いじょう}でございました |
| Tentative | でございましょう | そのとおりでございましょう |
| い-adjective | frame **not** used | よろしいです → よろしゅうございます |
| About a person you honour | でいらっしゃいます | {f|社長|しゃちょう}でいらっしゃいます |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `でございます` for things, places, prices, facts, and for
**yourself**. `でいらっしゃいます` for the listener and for anyone else you are
raising.

</div>

Thank you for coming today despite your busy schedules. I'm Sato, and I'll be chairing.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Announcements, counters, telephones and formal speeches. In a shop or a hotel it
is the default copula and nobody notices it; in a conversation with a friend it
would be as odd as answering the door in a uniform. Between those extremes, a
business meeting will use it for set pieces - introductions, figures, conclusions
 - and drop back to `です` for ordinary discussion.

The single most useful thing to keep straight is that `でございます` does not
honour. Saying ❌{f|田中|たなか}{f|様|さま}でございますか to a visitor is a real
error, not merely a stiff one: it equates the guest with a price or a room number.
The honorific copula is `でいらっしゃいます`, and it is used the same way for
descriptions: お{f|元気|げんき}でいらっしゃいますか,
{f|社長|しゃちょう}の{f|奥様|おくさま}でいらっしゃいます.

Some notes on the edges:

- **Negatives.** ではございません is the formal negative. In quick speech people
  reduce it to じゃございません, which is a mixed register and is best avoided in
  writing.
- **い-adjectives.** There is no ❌よろしいでございますか. The forms that exist are
  よろしゅうございますか, which sounds old-fashioned, and よろしいでしょうか, which
  is what you should actually say.
- **Placement.** `でございます` closes the sentence. Inside a sentence you use the
  て-form でございまして, which is heavy and almost entirely a written or
  speech-making form.
- **Do not stack it on {f|敬語|けいご} you have already used.**
  ご{f|覧|らん}になるお{f|品|しな}でございます is fine, but
  ❌ご{f|覧|らん}になられるお{f|品|しな}でございます piles two honorific layers
  under the formal copula.

## Contrast

### でございます vs です

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different height. `です` is polite; `でございます`
is polite and formal, and it signals a service or ceremonial setting.

</div>

<pre>
これは{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|資料|しりょう}<b>です</b>。
This is last year's material.
</pre>

<pre>
こちらが{f|昨年度|さくねんど}の{f|資料|しりょう}<b>でございます</b>。
This is last year's material.
</pre>

Notice how much moves with the copula. `これ` becomes `こちら`,
{f|去年|きょねん} becomes {f|昨年度|さくねんど}. Register is carried by the whole
sentence, and a lone `でございます` on top of casual vocabulary sounds like a joke.

### でございます vs でいらっしゃいます

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `でございます` about yourself and about things;
`でいらっしゃいます` about the person you are honouring. Getting this backwards is
the classic {f|敬語|けいご} error.

</div>

<pre>
{f|失礼|しつれい}ですが、ご{f|予約|よやく}の{f|方|かた}<b>でいらっしゃいます</b>か。
Excuse me - do you have a reservation with us?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}の{f|木村|きむら}<b>でございます</b>。
I'm Kimura from reception.
</pre>

The pair shows the whole three-way system in two lines. The first raises the
guest; the second simply formalises a statement about the speaker. Swap them and
you have honoured yourself while treating the guest as an object - which is why
❌{f|私|わたし}が{f|担当|たんとう}でいらっしゃいます is such a jarring sentence.

### でございます vs ～になります

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `になる` means "becomes". For "is", the formal copula is
`でございます`.

</div>

<pre>
こちらがお{f|飲|の}み{f|物|もの}のメニュー<b>でございます</b>。
Here is the drinks menu.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|合計|ごうけい}で{f|二千|にせん}{f|円|えん}<b>でございます</b>。
That's two thousand yen altogether.
</pre>

You will hear こちらメニューになります and
{f|二千|にせん}{f|円|えん}になります constantly in Japanese shops. It is common
enough to have its own nickname - マニュアル{f|敬語|けいご}, "manual keigo" - and
a large number of speakers consider it wrong, on the grounds that nothing is
turning into anything. `になります` is correct when there really is a change
（{f|四月|しがつ}から{f|新|あたら}しい{f|料金|りょうきん}になります）. For a plain
identification, `でございます` is the safe choice.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|田中|たなか}{f|様|さま}<b>でございます</b>か。 → ✅ {f|田中|たなか}{f|様|さま}<b>でいらっしゃいます</b>か。
  `でございます` does not raise anybody. Asked of a guest, it puts them in the same
  grammatical box as the room key.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|鈴木|すずき}<b>でいらっしゃいます</b>。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|鈴木|すずき}<b>でございます</b>。
  The mirror error, and the one learners make when they decide
  `でいらっしゃいます` is simply "the politest version".

- ❌ お{f|席|せき}はこちらで<b>よろしいでございますか</b>。 → ✅ お{f|席|せき}はこちらで<b>よろしいでしょうか</b>。
  An い-adjective cannot take `でございます`. Either use でしょうか, or the
  old-fashioned よろしゅうございますか.

- ❌ こちら、お{f|釣|つ}り<b>になります</b>。 → ✅ こちら、お{f|釣|つ}り<b>でございます</b>。
  The change is nothing to do with becoming. Very widely heard, and very widely
  disliked.

## Practice

**1.** お{f|電話|でんわ}ありがとうございます。{f|山|やま}の{f|上|うえ}ホテル、{f|予約|よやく}{f|係|がかり}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お{f|電話|でんわ}ありがとうございます。{f|山|やま}の{f|上|うえ}ホテル、{f|予約|よやく}{f|係|がかり}<b>でございます</b>。

You are identifying yourself and your own department, so the formal copula is
right. `でいらっしゃいます` here would honour yourself, which is the one thing
{f|敬語|けいご} never lets you do.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|失礼|しつれい}ですが、{f|部長|ぶちょう}の{f|奥様|おくさま}（でございます・でいらっしゃいます）か。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|失礼|しつれい}ですが、{f|部長|ぶちょう}の{f|奥様|おくさま}<b>でいらっしゃいます</b>か。

The sentence identifies the person in front of you, so it needs the honorific
copula. `でございます` would be merely formal, and asking a guest
❌{f|奥様|おくさま}でございますか sounds as though you are reading a label.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, as a shop assistant: "This one is this year's newest model."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

こちらが{f|今年|ことし}の{f|最新|さいしん}モデル<b>でございます</b>。

A product is a thing, so the formal copula is the right choice, and `こちら`
rather than `これ` keeps the whole sentence at the same height. ❌モデルになります
would say the item is turning into a model.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|田中|たなか}{f|様|さま}、こちらがご{f|注文|ちゅうもん}の{f|品|しな}になります。{f|合計|ごうけい}で{f|千五百|せんごひゃく}{f|円|えん}になります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|田中|たなか}{f|様|さま}、こちらがご{f|注文|ちゅうもん}の{f|品|しな}<b>でございます</b>。{f|合計|ごうけい}で{f|千五百|せんごひゃく}{f|円|えん}<b>でございます</b>。

Neither the goods nor the total is *becoming* anything, so `になります` has no
work to do. Both clauses are plain identifications and take the formal copula.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|一階|いっかい}です。
(b) {f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|一階|いっかい}でございます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are correct and neither honours anyone - `でございます` is
{f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご}, so the difference is formality, not respect.

(a) is ordinary polite Japanese: a colleague telling a visiting friend where to
go, or a sign in a small office. (b) places the speaker on the serving side of a
counter. Said by a hotel employee it is exactly right; said by one student to
another it sounds like a performance.

</details>
