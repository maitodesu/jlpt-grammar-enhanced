# は vs が

**Meaning** · which of the two to reach for once the "new information" test has done its work

At N5 the rule was: the news comes **after** `は` and **before** `が`. That is still
the first thing to check, and it settles most sentences. This page is about what the
rule does not cover - `は` used to set one thing against another, `が` used to say
"this one and no other", and what happens to both particles once a clause is buried
inside a larger sentence.

## Formation

Neither particle changes shape, and neither changes the noun. What you are choosing
between is a **job**, so the table sorts by job rather than by word class.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Known noun → topic | Noun + は | {f|祖母|そぼ} → {f|祖母|そぼ}は{f|元気|げんき}です |
| Two nouns set against each other → contrast | A は …、B は … | {f|平日|へいじつ}・{f|週末|しゅうまつ} → {f|平日|へいじつ}は{f|働|はたら}き、{f|週末|しゅうまつ}は{f|休|やす}む |
| Non-subject noun, contrasted | Particle + は | {f|駅|えき}まで → {f|駅|えき}までは{f|歩|ある}きます |
| Quantity word, contrasted | Quantity + は + negative | {f|全部|ぜんぶ} → {f|全部|ぜんぶ}は{f|覚|おぼ}えられません |
| New noun in a reported event | Noun + が | {f|事故|じこ} → {f|事故|じこ}がありました |
| The one and only → exhaustive が | Noun + が + predicate | {f|山口|やまぐち}さん → {f|山口|やまぐち}さんが{f|担当|たんとう}です |
| Subject inside a noun-modifying clause | Noun + が（or の） | {f|父|ちち} → {f|父|ちち}が{f|建|た}てた{f|家|いえ} |
| Subject inside a と・ば・たら・とき clause | Noun + が | {f|雪|ゆき} → {f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}ったら… |
| Contrast reaching into a けど・から clause | Noun + は | {f|値段|ねだん} → {f|値段|ねだん}は{f|高|たか}いけど… |

<div class="note">

**Note** - a sentence can carry only one **topic**, but it can carry several `は`.
The first is normally the topic; every later one is contrastive.
{f|私|わたし}は{f|刺身|さしみ}は{f|食|た}べません。 - "Me, I don't eat sashimi
(whatever else I might eat)."

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|漢字|かんじ}<b>は</b>{f|読|よ}めますが、{f|書|か}けません。
I can read kanji, but I can't write it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|荷物|にもつ}<b>は</b>{f|私|わたし}<b>が</b>{f|持|も}ちます。
I'll take the bags.
</pre>

<pre>
さっき{f|変|へん}な{f|音|おと}<b>が</b>したけど、{f|今|いま}<b>は</b>{f|静|しず}かだね。
There was an odd noise a minute ago, but it's quiet now.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|司会|しかい}は{f|森|もり}さん<b>が</b>、{f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|林|はやし}さん<b>が</b>{f|担当|たんとう}します。
Mori will run the meeting and Hayashi will look after reception.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|父|ちち}<b>が</b>{f|昔|むかし}{f|使|つか}っていたカメラ<b>は</b>、{f|今|いま}でもちゃんと{f|動|うご}きます。
The camera my father used years ago still works perfectly.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|野菜|やさい}の{f|値段|ねだん}<b>が</b>{f|上|あ}がったので、{f|今年|ことし}<b>は</b>{f|旅行|りょこう}をやめました。
Vegetable prices went up, so we've given up on a trip this year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|時間|じかん}が{f|変|か}わったこと、{f|部長|ぶちょう}に<b>は</b>{f|伝|つた}えましたが、{f|山田|やまだ}さんに<b>は</b>まだ{f|伝|つた}えていません。
I've told the manager the meeting time changed, but I haven't told Yamada yet.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}<b>は</b>{f|私|わたし}<b>が</b>しますので、{f|資料|しりょう}のコピー<b>は</b>{f|田中|たなか}さんにお{f|願|ねが}いできますか。
I'll do the explaining, so could I leave copying the handouts to you, Tanaka?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Both particles are register-neutral: nothing about choosing one makes you sound
formal or casual. What they do carry is **attitude**, and that is where politeness
gets involved.

`は` drags a comparison in behind it whether you want one or not, so an unnecessary
`は` can sound pointed. Volunteering with {f|私|わたし}<b>は</b>やります in a room
where a job is being handed out reads as "*I*, at any rate, will do it - never mind
the rest of you"; the cooperative version is {f|私|わたし}<b>が</b>やります, which
simply fills the empty slot of "who". The same edge is useful on purpose:
「{f|今日|きょう}はちょっと…」 turns an invitation down gently precisely because `は`
implies "another day, gladly."

`が` in its selecting sense is the particle of taking responsibility, and business
Japanese leans on it: {f|私|わたし}<b>が</b>{f|確認|かくにん}いたします,
こちら<b>が</b>{f|新|あたら}しい{f|資料|しりょう}でございます. Learners who default to
`は` in those slots sound oddly detached from what they are saying.

In casual speech both particles are routinely dropped - 
「あれ、{f|鍵|かぎ}、どこ?」 - and the contrast `は` would have carried is done by
stress instead. Note that a **contrastive** `は` survives that dropping far more often
than a topic `は`: 「{f|肉|にく}は{f|食|た}べない」 keeps its particle because the
particle is the whole point of the sentence.

Writing gives `は` one job that speech uses less: definitions.
<code>{f|敬語|けいご}とは、{f|相手|あいて}を{f|高|たか}める{f|言葉|ことば}である</code> - "*keigo*
is language that elevates the other party." That <code>とは</code> frame opens dictionary
entries and academic articles, and it is always `は`, never `が`.

## Contrast

### Topic は vs contrastive は

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if you can tack "…whatever about the others" onto the end and the
sentence still means what you meant, the `は` is contrastive. A topic `は` sits at the
head of the sentence; a contrastive `は` can sit anywhere, including on top of another
particle, and it takes the stress in speech.

</div>

The topic use simply announces what you are talking about:

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}<b>は</b>{f|大学|だいがく}で{f|建築|けんちく}を{f|勉強|べんきょう}しています。
My younger brother is studying architecture at university.
</pre>

The contrastive use is a different move. It lifts its noun out of a field of
alternatives and pointedly says nothing about the rest:

<pre>
{f|夜|よる}<b>は</b>コーヒーを{f|飲|の}みません。
I don't drink coffee at night. (daytime is another matter)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}まで<b>は</b>{f|走|はし}りましたが、{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}いませんでした。
I ran as far as the station, at least - but I didn't make it.
</pre>

The most useful case is a **quantity word plus a negative**. `は` there produces
partial negation, and leaving it out flips the meaning:

<pre>
{f|全部|ぜんぶ}<b>は</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えられませんでした。
I couldn't memorise all of it. (I did learn some)
</pre>

Without the `は`, {f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|覚|おぼ}えられませんでした is normally taken as "I
couldn't learn any of it." The same trick works on <code>{f|全員|ぜんいん}は</code>,
<code>いつもは</code> and <code>{f|毎日|まいにち}は</code>.

### Neutral-description が vs exhaustive が

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `が` either **reports** a whole event as news, or **selects** one
member out of a set. The selecting kind answers a "which one?" and cannot be swapped
for `は`.

</div>

Reporting: nothing is being singled out. You are presenting a situation you have just
noticed, or announcing one the listener has not heard.

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}の{f|前|まえ}に{f|新|あたら}しいパン{f|屋|や}<b>が</b>できました。
A new bakery has opened in front of the station.
</pre>

Selecting: everyone already knows the predicate applies to somebody, and `が` names
who. This is why identifying yourself takes `が`:

<pre>
{f|本日|ほんじつ}{f|案内|あんない}いたします、{f|木村|きむら}<b>が</b>{f|担当|たんとう}でございます。
I'm Kimura, and I'll be looking after you today.
</pre>

The selecting `が` is what makes **exhaustive listing** possible - handing out roles
one by one, with the understanding that between them the list is complete:

<pre>
{f|土曜日|どようび}は{f|私|わたし}<b>が</b>、{f|日曜日|にちようび}は{f|妻|つま}<b>が</b>{f|店|みせ}に{f|出|で}ます。
I'll be at the shop on Saturday and my wife on Sunday.
</pre>

One practical consequence: with an adjective and an already-known subject, `が` forces
the selecting reading and sounds strange as a plain description.
この{f|部屋|へや}<b>が</b>{f|明|あか}るいです means "*this* is the bright one"; for "this
room is bright" you want <code>この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|明|あか}るいです</code>.

### Where は cannot go, and what it means when it can

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `は` scopes over a whole sentence, so it cannot sit inside a
clause that is only a *part* of one. Inside noun-modifying clauses, and inside
と・ば・たら・なら・とき clauses, the subject takes `が` - or `の` in a noun-modifying
clause.

</div>

<pre>
{f|母|はは}<b>が</b>{f|送|おく}ってくれた{f|野菜|やさい}を、{f|近所|きんじょ}の{f|人|ひと}に{f|分|わ}けました。
I shared the vegetables my mother sent me out among the neighbours.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電車|でんしゃ}<b>が</b>{f|止|と}まったら、タクシーで{f|帰|かえ}ります。
If the trains stop, I'll go home by taxi.
</pre>

❌<code>{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|止|と}まったら</code> is not a stylistic slip - it breaks,
because the topic would be trying to scope over a sentence whose main clause is about
you, not about trains.

The exception is worth knowing, because it looks like a counterexample. Clauses joined
with けど・が・から・ので are not *embedded*; they are full clauses stuck together. `は`
can appear inside those - and when it does, it is always the contrastive `は`, never a
second topic:

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}<b>は</b>かかりますが、{f|必|かなら}ず{f|直|なお}します。
It will take time, granted - but I will definitely fix it.
</pre>

That is exactly the "…whatever about the rest" reading: time, yes; difficulty, cost
and outcome, not commented on. Test any `は` you meet inside a subordinate clause
against that, and it will almost always turn out to be contrast.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|外|そと}**は**{f|暗|くら}くなったら、{f|電気|でんき}をつけてください。 → ✅ {f|外|そと}**が**{f|暗|くら}くなったら、{f|電気|でんき}をつけてください。
  A たら clause is embedded inside the sentence, and a topic cannot live there. Its
  subject takes `が`.

- ❌ 「どちらが{f|田中|たなか}さんですか。」「{f|私|わたし}**は**{f|田中|たなか}です。」 → ✅ 「{f|私|わたし}**が**{f|田中|たなか}です。」
  <code>{f|私|わたし}は{f|田中|たなか}です</code> is a fine self-introduction, but as an answer
  to "which of you is Tanaka?" it dodges the question. You were asked to select, and
  selecting is `が`.

- ❌ この{f|部屋|へや}**が**{f|静|しず}かです。（meaning "this room is quiet"） → ✅ この{f|部屋|へや}**は**{f|静|しず}かです。
  With a known subject and an adjective, `が` is heard as "this is the quiet *one*".
  For a plain description, use `は`.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}**は**{f|雪|ゆき}**は**{f|降|ふ}りました。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}**は**{f|雪|ゆき}**が**{f|降|ふ}りました。
  The second `は` forces a contrast - "snow, at least, fell" - which is not what you
  meant. A reported event takes `が`.

## Practice

**1.** 「どうしたんですか。」「エアコン____{f|動|うご}かないんです。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

エアコン**が**{f|動|うご}かないんです。

You are reporting a problem the listener does not know about yet, so the air
conditioner is brand-new information - the reporting `が`. `は` would imply the two of
you were already discussing that particular machine.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|時間|じかん}（は・が）ないので、{f|今日|きょう}（は・が）ここまでにしましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|時間|じかん}**が**ないので、{f|今日|きょう}**は**ここまでにしましょう。

The first slot sits inside a `ので` clause and simply states a fact about time, so it
takes `が`. The second sets today against other days - we stop here today, and next
time we will go further - which is contrastive `は`.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I've read the first half, but I haven't read the second half yet."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|前半|ぜんはん}**は**{f|読|よ}みましたが、{f|後半|こうはん}**は**まだ{f|読|よ}んでいません。

Both halves are grammatical objects and would normally take `を`, but the sentence
exists in order to set them against each other, so `を` is replaced by contrastive
`は` on both sides.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|雨|あめ}は{f|降|ふ}ったら、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|雨|あめ}**が**{f|降|ふ}ったら、{f|試合|しあい}**は**{f|中止|ちゅうし}です。

The first `は` is inside a たら clause and has to become `が`. The second one is in the
main clause and is correct as it stands - the match is the topic of the sentence.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|山口|やまぐち}さん**は**{f|部長|ぶちょう}です。
(b) {f|山口|やまぐち}さん**が**{f|部長|ぶちょう}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) takes {f|山口|やまぐち}さん as already under discussion and tells you something
about him: his job is manager. It is the answer to "what does he do?"

(b) starts from the other end. Everyone knows there is a manager; the sentence
identifies which person it is - "{f|山口|やまぐち}さん is the one" - picking him out
of the group. It answers "which of them is the manager?", and it is what you say while
nodding across the room.

</details>
