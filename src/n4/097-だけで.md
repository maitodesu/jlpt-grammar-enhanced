# ～だけで

**Meaning** · just by doing ～; with only ～ - that small amount is all it takes

`だけ` draws the line; the `で` behind it says "and that line is where the cause
sits". So <code>～だけで</code> is not simply "only" - it links a minimal amount of
effort, time, money or evidence to a result that follows from it. Sometimes the
tone is admiring ("that's all it takes!") and sometimes dismissive ("that's all it
is").

## Formation

`だけ` attaches as it always does, and `で` follows it.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain) | Vる + だけで | {f|見|み}る → {f|見|み}るだけで |
| Verb (past) | Vた + だけで | {f|聞|き}いた → {f|聞|き}いただけで |
| Noun | Noun + だけで | {f|水|みず} → {f|水|みず}だけで |
| Number / counter | Counter + だけで | {f|一人|ひとり}だけで |
| い-adjective | plain form + だけで | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}いだけで |
| な-adjective | stem + な + だけで | {f|静|しず}かだ → {f|静|しず}かなだけで |
| Common endings | ～だけで + いい／{f|十分|じゅうぶん}／{f|分|わ}かる | {f|見|み}るだけでいいです |
| Negative judgement | ～だけで**は** + negative | それだけでは{f|足|た}りません |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if the sentence goes on to say what **results** from the small
amount, you want <code>だけで</code>. If it simply reports the amount and stops,
plain `だけ` is enough.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|顔|かお}を{f|見|み}る<b>だけで</b>{f|元気|げんき}になります。
Just seeing your face cheers me up.
</pre>

<pre>
ボタンを{f|押|お}す<b>だけで</b>お{f|湯|ゆ}が{f|出|で}ます。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|考|かんが}える<b>だけで</b>{f|怖|こわ}くなります。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}<b>だけで</b>{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|運|はこ}ぶのは{f|無理|むり}ですよ。
There's no way you can carry all of it with just one person.
</pre>

<pre>

Just walking for ten minutes every morning has made me feel a great deal better.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral in itself; it takes its politeness from the ending. Two very
different tones live inside the same three kana, and which one you hear depends on
what the result is:

- **"That's all it takes."** The result is good and the effort is tiny.
  Instruction manuals, advertising and recipes are built out of this:
  {f|混|ま}ぜるだけでできます. In speech it is warm and encouraging.
- **"That's all it is."** Here <code>だけで</code> follows an adjective and the
  second half of the sentence takes something away:
  {f|新|あたら}しいだけで、{f|使|つか}いにくい. This is faint praise turning into a
  complaint, and it is quite cutting.

The frame <code>～だけでいい</code> - "that's all you need to do" - is worth
memorising whole. It is how permission and reassurance are given in Japanese, and
it is much softer than an imperative: ここにサインするだけでいいです is far
friendlier than ここにサインしてください.

Adding `は` - <code>だけでは</code> - flips the sentence to "that alone is not
enough", and it almost always precedes a negative:
{f|練習|れんしゅう}だけでは{f|上手|じょうず}になりません. The `は` is the ordinary
contrastive `は`, and leaving it out sounds unfinished.

## Contrast

### ～だけで vs ～だけ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - plain `だけ` reports an amount. `だけで` makes that amount the
**cause** of what comes next.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|水|みず}<b>だけ</b>{f|飲|の}みました。
Yesterday I drank only water.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|水|みず}<b>だけで</b>{f|三日間|みっかかん}{f|生|い}きられますか。
Can you survive for three days on water alone?
</pre>

In the first, water is what got drunk. In the second, water is the *means* by
which surviving happens. Slipping a `で` into the first sentence - 
❌{f|水|みず}だけで{f|飲|の}みました - would say you drank something using only
water, which is nonsense.

### ～だけで vs ～だけでは

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だけで` says the small amount **is** enough. `だけでは` says
it **is not**.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|読|よ}む<b>だけで</b>{f|分|わ}かります。
You can understand it just by reading this explanation.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|読|よ}む<b>だけでは</b>{f|分|わ}かりません。
Just reading this explanation isn't enough to understand it.
</pre>

The `は` is contrastive: it sets "reading the explanation" against everything else
you would also have to do. Because of that, the predicate after `だけでは` is
nearly always negative - {f|足|た}りない, できない, いけない,
{f|分|わ}からない.

### ～だけで vs ～ば・～と

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a conditional says *if* X, then Y. `だけで` says X **and
nothing more** is required for Y.

</div>

<pre>
{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}を{f|読|よ}め<b>ば</b>{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}が{f|分|わ}かります。
If you read the manual you'll understand how to use it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}を{f|読|よ}む<b>だけで</b>{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}が{f|分|わ}かります。
Just reading the manual is enough to understand how to use it.
</pre>

The conditional states a route to the result; <code>だけで</code> also insists the
route is short. That extra claim is what makes it the natural choice for anything
selling ease - and what makes it sound sarcastic if the task is in fact difficult.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|見|み}て<b>だけで</b>{f|分|わ}かります。 → ✅ {f|見|み}る<b>だけで</b>{f|分|わ}かります。
  `だけ` attaches to the plain form or the past form, never to the て-form. Both
  {f|見|み}るだけで and {f|見|み}ただけで are fine; the past one presents the
  looking as already done.

- ❌ {f|静|しず}か<b>だ</b>だけで、{f|不便|ふべん}です。 → ✅ {f|静|しず}か<b>な</b>だけで、{f|不便|ふべん}です。
  な-adjectives take `な` before `だけ`, exactly as they do before a noun. `だ`
  only appears at the end of a sentence.

- ❌ これだけで{f|足|た}りません。 → ✅ これだけ<b>では</b>{f|足|た}りません。
  When the point is that the amount is **insufficient**, the contrastive `は` is
  required. Without it the sentence sounds like it was cut off halfway.

- ❌ サラダ<b>だけで</b>{f|食|た}べました。（meaning "I only ate salad"） → ✅ サラダ<b>だけ</b>{f|食|た}べました。
  The `で` adds "by means of", which the sentence does not want. Reserve
  <code>だけで</code> for when a result follows.

## Practice

**1.** このアプリは{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|撮|と}る____、{f|花|はな}の{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|教|おし}えてくれます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

このアプリは{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|撮|と}る<b>だけで</b>、{f|花|はな}の{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|教|おし}えてくれます。

Taking a photo is the whole of the effort required, and the app naming the flower
is the result. That "minimum input, full result" shape is exactly what
<code>だけで</code> exists for, which is why product descriptions are full of it.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural option:
{f|辞書|じしょ}を{f|買|か}う（だけで・だけでは）{f|上手|じょうず}になりません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|辞書|じしょ}を{f|買|か}う<b>だけでは</b>{f|上手|じょうず}になりません。

The predicate is negative - you will *not* improve - so the sentence is saying the
one action is insufficient, and that requires the contrastive `は`. With plain
`だけで` the sentence would be claiming the opposite and then contradicting itself.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "You can register with just an email address."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

メールアドレス<b>だけで</b>{f|登録|とうろく}できます。

A noun takes `だけ` directly, and the `で` then marks it as the means. Compare
メールアドレスだけを{f|入力|にゅうりょく}してください, "enter only your email
address", where nothing follows as a result and plain `だけ` is right.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}は{f|親切|しんせつ}だだけで、{f|仕事|しごと}はあまりできません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|親切|しんせつ}<b>な</b>だけで、{f|仕事|しごと}はあまりできません。

{f|親切|しんせつ} is a な-adjective, so it takes `な` in front of `だけ`. Notice
what the sentence does with the pattern: it grants one merit and then withdraws
everything else - the dismissive "that's all he is" flavour of
<code>だけで</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|話|はなし}を{f|聞|き}く<b>だけ</b>でした。
(b) {f|話|はなし}を{f|聞|き}く<b>だけで</b>{f|涙|なみだ}が{f|出|で}ました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a closed report: listening was the whole of what you did, and nothing is
said about any consequence. It often carries a hint of "I didn't get involved".

(b) makes the listening a trigger. The point is not the amount of listening but
how little of it was needed before you cried. The `で` is doing all of that work - 
it turns a boundary into a cause.

</details>
