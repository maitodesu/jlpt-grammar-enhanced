# かもしれない

**Meaning** · might; maybe - the possibility is open, and the speaker refuses to say more than that

Take the pieces apart and the word explains itself: `か` (is it so?) + `も` (even) +
<code>{f|知|し}れない</code> (cannot be known) - "it cannot even be known whether it is
so." `かもしれない` does **not** say something is likely. It says the door is open,
and leaves the door on the other side open too. That is the whole of its meaning, and
it is why it sits at the bottom of the confidence ladder.

## Formation

Attaches to plain forms. Nouns and な-adjectives drop `だ`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + かもしれない | {f|遅|おく}れる → {f|遅|おく}れるかもしれない |
| Verb (past) | た-form + かもしれない | {f|忘|わす}れた → {f|忘|わす}れたかもしれない |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + かもしれない | {f|届|とど}かない → {f|届|とど}かないかもしれない |
| い-adjective | plain form + かもしれない | {f|寒|さむ}い → {f|寒|さむ}いかもしれない |
| な-adjective | drop だ + かもしれない | {f|不便|ふべん}だ → {f|不便|ふべん}かもしれない |
| Noun | drop だ + かもしれない | {f|風邪|かぜ}だ → {f|風邪|かぜ}かもしれない |
| Polite | かもしれません | {f|遅|おく}れるかもしれません |
| Casual | かも／かもね／かもよ | {f|遅|おく}れるかも |
| Common partners | もしかしたら／ひょっとすると + …かもしれない | もしかしたら{f|遅|おく}れるかもしれない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - tense lives **inside**, politeness lives **outside**.
"It might have been wrong" is
<code>{f|間違|まちが}っていたかもしれません</code>, never
❌<code>{f|間違|まちが}っているかもしれませんでした</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|遅|おく}れる<b>かもしれない</b>。
The train might be late.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|田中|たなか}さんの{f|弟|おとうと}さん<b>かもしれません</b>。
That might be Tanaka's younger brother.
</pre>

<pre>
かぎを{f|家|いえ}に{f|忘|わす}れてきた<b>かもしれない</b>。
I may have left my keys at home.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|山|やま}の{f|上|うえ}は{f|思|おも}ったより{f|寒|さむ}い<b>かもしれない</b>から、{f|上着|うわぎ}を{f|持|も}っていこう。
It might be colder than we expect up the mountain, so let's take jackets.
</pre>

<pre>
もしかしたら、{f|私|わたし}の{f|勘違|かんちが}いだった<b>かもしれません</b>。
It's possible I got the wrong end of the stick.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|方法|ほうほう}は{f|少|すこ}し{f|面倒|めんどう}<b>かもしれない</b>けど、{f|確実|かくじつ}だよ。
This way might be a bit of a faff, but it's reliable.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}がないので、メールが{f|届|とど}いていない<b>かもしれません</b>ね。
There's been no reply, so perhaps the email never arrived.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|言|い}いにくいのですが、この{f|数字|すうじ}はどこかで{f|間違|まちが}っている<b>かもしれません</b>。
This is awkward to raise, but there may be an error somewhere in these figures.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Plain `かもしれない`, polite `かもしれません`. In speech the tail is very often chopped
off to `かも` - そうかも。／{f|行|い}けないかも。 - and `かも` on its own is a complete
reply, roughly "could be". `かもね` softens it further and `かもよ` warns. None of these
short forms belong in writing; `かもしれない` itself is perfectly at home in an essay.

Its social use is at least as important as its logical one. Because `かもしれません`
commits to nothing, it is the standard tool for **raising something delicate**: a risk
nobody has mentioned, an error in a superior's work, a disagreement you do not want to
turn into a confrontation. <code>{f|私|わたし}の{f|説明|せつめい}が{f|足|た}りなかったかもしれません</code>
takes the blame without quite admitting it, which is exactly why it is said so often.

The flip side is that overusing it sounds evasive. If your manager asks when your own
work will be finished, <code>{f|明日|あした}{f|終|お}わるかもしれません</code> reads as "I
haven't really thought about it". About your own actions and your own knowledge,
commit: <code>{f|終|お}わると{f|思|おも}います</code>.

`もしかしたら` and `ひょっとすると` at the head of the sentence flag the guess in
advance and push it further towards "just possibly". They pair with `かもしれない`
almost automatically, and a sentence that opens with `もしかしたら` and then ends
flatly in `です` sounds unfinished.

## Contrast

### かもしれない vs でしょう・だろう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `でしょう` is an **estimate**: the speaker thinks it probably *is*
so. `かもしれない` is an **admission**: the speaker will only say it *could* be so, and
that the opposite could be too.

</div>

<pre>
{f|午後|ごご}は{f|風|かぜ}が{f|強|つよ}くなる<b>でしょう</b>。
The wind will probably get stronger this afternoon.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|午後|ごご}は{f|風|かぜ}が{f|強|つよ}くなる<b>かもしれません</b>。
The wind might get stronger this afternoon.
</pre>

The forecaster says the first; the person deciding whether to hang the washing out says
the second. There is a neat test: you can chain `かもしれない` with its own negation - 
{f|強|つよ}くなるかもしれないし、ならないかもしれない - and it sounds entirely normal,
because both branches were open all along. Doing the same with `でしょう` sounds like
someone contradicting themselves.

### かもしれない vs はずだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the difference is **what justifies the claim**.
<code>はずだ</code> rests on a reason you could name if challenged. `かもしれない` rests
on nothing at all, and needs nothing.

</div>

<pre>
{f|課長|かちょう}はもう{f|着|つ}いている<b>はずです</b>。{f|七時|しちじ}の{f|電車|でんしゃ}に{f|乗|の}ると{f|言|い}っていましたから。
The section chief should already be there - he said he'd take the seven o'clock train.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|課長|かちょう}はもう{f|着|つ}いている<b>かもしれません</b>。
The section chief might already be there.
</pre>

Ask どうして？ after the first and you owe an answer; the timetable is the grounds. Ask
it after the second and "no particular reason, it's just possible" is a complete reply.
This is also why `はずです` can be **wrong** in a way `かもしれません` cannot: if he is
not there, your reasoning has failed, and Japanese has a form for exactly that
disappointment - <code>はずだったのに</code>.

### かもしれない vs ～と{f|思|おも}う

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>と{f|思|おも}う</code> puts an opinion on the record.
`かもしれない` declines to have one.

</div>

<pre>
こちらの{f|辞書|じしょ}のほうが{f|使|つか}いやすい<b>と{f|思|おも}います</b>。
I think this dictionary is easier to use.
</pre>

<pre>
こちらの{f|辞書|じしょ}のほうが{f|使|つか}いやすい<b>かもしれません</b>。
This dictionary might be easier to use.
</pre>

Both are polite; only the second leaves the listener free to disagree without
contradicting you, which is why it is the gentler way to suggest a change to someone
senior. The cost is authority: if you are the person who is supposed to know,
`かもしれません` shifts the decision back onto the listener.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|彼女|かのじょ}は<b>{f|元気|げんき}だかもしれません</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}は<b>{f|元気|げんき}かもしれません</b>。
  Nouns and な-adjectives lose `だ` in front of `か`. Compare
  <code>{f|元気|げんき}だと{f|思|おも}います</code>, where the `だ` stays.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は<b>{f|行|い}きますかもしれません</b>。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は<b>{f|行|い}くかもしれません</b>。
  The clause in front is plain. The politeness is already carried by
  `かもしれません`.

- ❌ さっきの{f|説明|せつめい}は<b>{f|間違|まちが}っているかもしれませんでした</b>。 → ✅ さっきの{f|説明|せつめい}は<b>{f|間違|まちが}っていたかもしれません</b>。
  Past tense goes on the embedded clause, not on `かもしれない`. There is no
  ❌`かもしれませんでした`.

- ❌ 「レポートはいつ{f|出|だ}せますか。」「{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|出|だ}せる<b>かもしれません</b>。」 → ✅ 「{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|出|だ}せる<b>と{f|思|おも}います</b>。」
  Not a grammar error, a register one. About your own work, `かもしれません` sounds
  like you have not looked at the calendar.

## Practice

**1.** {f|空|そら}が{f|暗|くら}くなってきましたね。{f|夕方|ゆうがた}から{f|雨|あめ}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|夕方|ゆうがた}から{f|雨|あめ}<b>かもしれません</b>。

<code>{f|雨|あめ}</code> is a noun, so it attaches bare - no `だ`. You could equally say
<code>{f|降|ふ}るかもしれません</code> with the verb; either way the `か` needs a plain
form or a bare noun in front of it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|新幹線|しんかんせん}は{f|時間|じかん}に{f|正確|せいかく}だから、もう{f|着|つ}いている（かもしれません・はずです）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

もう{f|着|つ}いている<b>はずです</b>。

The sentence supplies a reason - the bullet train runs on time - and a stated reason is
precisely what <code>はずです</code> requires. `かもしれません` would throw that reasoning
away and say only that it is possible.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I might have taken the wrong umbrella."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|傘|かさ}を<b>{f|間違|まちが}えたかもしれません</b>。

The past tense goes inside the clause, on
<code>{f|間違|まちが}える</code>. `かもしれません` itself never conjugates for tense.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|簡単|かんたん}だかもしれませんが、{f|時間|じかん}がかかります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は<b>{f|簡単|かんたん}かもしれません</b>が、{f|時間|じかん}がかかります。

<code>{f|簡単|かんたん}</code> is a な-adjective and must drop `だ` before `か`. Learners
who have just learned <code>{f|簡単|かんたん}だと{f|思|おも}う</code> carry the `だ` across
by habit.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|来|こ}ないでしょう。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|来|こ}ないかもしれません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a prediction the speaker is fairly confident in - "he probably won't come." If
you are organising the room, you would set out one chair fewer.

(b) only opens the possibility, and leaves his coming equally open. It is what you say
to warn people not to count on him, and it is also the more tactful of the two, since
it does not tell anyone what to expect.

</details>
