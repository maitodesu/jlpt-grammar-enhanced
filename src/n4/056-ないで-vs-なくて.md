# ～ないで vs ～なくて

**Meaning** · the two negative て-forms - *without doing* versus *because … not*

Japanese has one positive て-form and **two** negative ones. `～ないで` joins a
non-event to an action and says *how* that action happened. `～なくて` joins a
non-event to a consequence and says *why* it happened. They are not stylistic
variants, and swapping them usually produces a sentence that means nothing.

## Formation

Both start from the ない-form. The split is in the ending, and in what the word
being negated is.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb → *without doing* | ない-form + で | {f|言|い}う → {f|言|い}わないで |
| Verb → *because not* | ない-form − い + くて | {f|言|い}う → {f|言|い}わなくて |
| Verb (する) | しないで／しなくて | {f|練習|れんしゅう}する → {f|練習|れんしゅう}しないで／しなくて |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | {f|来|こ}ないで／{f|来|こ}なくて | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ないで／{f|来|こ}なくて |
| い-adjective | ～くない → ～くなくて (no `ないで`) | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}くなくて |
| な-adjective | じゃない → じゃなくて (no `ないで`) | {f|便利|べんり}だ → {f|便利|べんり}じゃなくて |
| Noun | じゃない → じゃなくて (no `ないで`) | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}じゃなくて |
| Written equivalent of `ないで` | ない-form − ない + ずに | {f|言|い}う → {f|言|い}わずに |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なくて` can negate **anything**; `ないで` only ever negates a
**verb**. So if the word before the blank is an adjective or a noun, the answer is
`なくて` before you have thought about meaning at all.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|上着|うわぎ}を<b>{f|着|き}ないで</b>{f|外|そと}に{f|出|で}た。
I went outside without putting a jacket on.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}が<b>{f|来|こ}なくて</b>、{f|心配|しんぱい}です。
No reply has come, and I'm worried.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|席|せき}を<b>{f|立|た}たないで</b>{f|待|ま}っていてください。
Please wait without leaving your seat.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|意味|いみ}が<b>わからなくて</b>、{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|聞|き}きました。
I didn't understand what it meant, so I asked the teacher.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|野菜|やさい}を<b>{f|食|た}べないで</b>{f|肉|にく}ばかり{f|食|た}べる。
My son leaves the vegetables and eats nothing but meat.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}が<b>{f|広|ひろ}くなくて</b>、ベッドが{f|入|はい}りませんでした。
The room wasn't big enough, so the bed wouldn't fit.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|原因|げんいん}は{f|雨|あめ}<b>じゃなくて</b>、{f|工事|こうじ}でした。
The cause wasn't the rain - it was the roadworks.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電車|でんしゃ}が<b>{f|動|うご}かなくて</b>、{f|面接|めんせつ}に{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}も{f|遅|おく}れてしまいました。
The trains weren't running, and I ended up half an hour late for my interview.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|誰|だれ}にも<b>{f|相談|そうだん}しないで</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|辞|や}めたので、{f|親|おや}にひどく{f|怒|おこ}られました。
I quit my job without talking to anyone about it, and my parents were furious.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Neither form is marked for politeness. Both sit in the middle of the sentence, and
the ending decides the register.

What `なくて` *is* marked for is **control**. Causal `なくて` is at its most natural
when the thing that failed to happen was outside the speaker's hands - the bus, the
weather, an email, your own memory or ability:
<code>{f|見|み}つからなくて</code>, <code>{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わなくて</code>,
<code>{f|来|こ}なくて</code>, <code>{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}せなくて</code>. When the non-action was a
choice you made, native speakers reach for `なかったので／なかったから` instead:
<code>{f|勉強|べんきょう}しなかったので、{f|試験|しけん}に{f|落|お}ちた</code> rather than
❌<code>{f|勉強|べんきょう}しなくて、{f|試験|しけん}に{f|落|お}ちた</code>.

The other half of that restriction is what comes **after** `なくて`. It wants a
state, a feeling or an involuntary result - {f|困|こま}った, {f|寂|さび}しい,
{f|安心|あんしん}した, よかった, {f|大変|たいへん}だった. It does not want a
deliberate next action, and it flatly cannot take a request, an invitation or an
intention. That single fact resolves most of the exam questions on this pair.

Two frozen expressions are worth memorising whole, because you will use them
constantly and both take `なくて`:
<code>{f|来|こ}られなくてすみません</code>「sorry I couldn't come」 and
<code>{f|大|おお}きい{f|事故|じこ}にならなくてよかった</code>「thank goodness it wasn't a
serious accident」.

Finally, `ないで` has a written twin, `ずに`, and `なくて` does not. In a formal
report <code>{f|確認|かくにん}せずに</code> replaces <code>{f|確認|かくにん}しないで</code>, while a
causal `なくて` in the same document would normally be rewritten as `ないため` - 
<code>{f|届|とど}かないため、{f|再送|さいそう}いたします</code>.

## Contrast

### Manner vs cause

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ask what the second clause *is*. An action performed → `ないで`.
A result suffered → `なくて`.

</div>

<pre>
めがねを<b>かけないで</b>{f|運転|うんてん}した。
I drove without wearing my glasses.
</pre>

<pre>
めがねが<b>なくて</b>{f|運転|うんてん}できなかった。
I didn't have my glasses, so I couldn't drive.
</pre>

In the first, the driving happened and the not-wearing describes the state I was in
while doing it - one event, described two ways. In the second there are two facts in
a chain: no glasses, therefore no driving. English blurs this because both come out
as "without" or "didn't … so"; Japanese does not.

A useful test: if you can insert 「そのまま」 or 「その{f|状態|じょうたい}で」 before the
main verb and the sentence still works, you want `ないで`.

### What can follow each

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - requests, commands, invitations and intentions can only follow
`ないで`. `なくて` is followed by things that happen *to* you.

</div>

<pre>
{f|砂糖|さとう}を<b>{f|入|い}れないで</b>ください。
Please don't put sugar in it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|砂糖|さとう}が<b>{f|入|はい}っていなくて</b>、{f|安心|あんしん}しました。
There was no sugar in it, which was a relief.
</pre>

There is no ❌<code>{f|入|い}れなくてください</code>, no ❌<code>{f|行|い}かなくて、{f|帰|かえ}ろう</code>,
no ❌<code>{f|待|ま}たなくて、{f|先|さき}に{f|始|はじ}めます</code>. If the sentence ends in
ください, ましょう, つもり, たい or an imperative, the negative in front of it is
`ないで`.

The mirror of that rule: an emotion word after the negative almost forces `なくて`.
{f|眠|ねむ}れなくて{f|辛|つら}かった, {f|会|あ}えなくて{f|残念|ざんねん}です,
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}らなくてよかった.

### Adjectives and nouns: only なくて exists

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ないで` is verb-only. Adjectives and nouns have exactly one
negative て-form, and it is `なくて` / `でなくて`.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|静|しず}か<b>じゃなくて</b>、{f|勉強|べんきょう}できません。
This room isn't quiet, so I can't study.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}は{f|高|たか}<b>くなくて</b>、{f|味|あじ}もよかったです。
It wasn't expensive, and it tasted good too.
</pre>

Note the second sentence: with adjectives, `なくて` is not always causal. It can
simply be the *and* of a list - "not A, and B" - exactly as the positive て-form
links two descriptions. The same neutral linking shows up in corrections, where
`じゃなくて` means "not A but B":
<code>{f|明日|あした}じゃなくて、あさってです</code>.

For nouns the formal written variant is `ではなくて`, and in the stiffest registers
`ではなく` with no `て` at all:
<code>{f|対象|たいしょう}は{f|学生|がくせい}ではなく、{f|社会人|しゃかいじん}です</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ここに{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|置|お}か<b>なくて</b>ください。 → ✅ ここに{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|置|お}か<b>ないで</b>ください。
  A request can only follow `ないで`. This is the single most common error with the
  pair, and it survives well past N4 if it is not fixed early.

- ❌ {f|部屋|へや}が{f|明|あか}る<b>くないで</b>、{f|本|ほん}が{f|読|よ}めない。 → ✅ {f|部屋|へや}が{f|明|あか}る<b>くなくて</b>、{f|本|ほん}が{f|読|よ}めない。
  <code>{f|明|あか}るい</code> is an adjective, and adjectives have no `ないで` form at all.

- ❌ {f|鍵|かぎ}が{f|見|み}つから<b>ないで</b>、{f|困|こま}りました。 → ✅ {f|鍵|かぎ}が{f|見|み}つから<b>なくて</b>、{f|困|こま}りました。
  Being stuck is a result, not an action performed while not finding the key. Causal
  chain → `なくて`.

- ❌ {f|練習|れんしゅう}し<b>なくて</b>、{f|試合|しあい}に{f|負|ま}けた。 → ✅ {f|練習|れんしゅう}し<b>なかったので</b>、{f|試合|しあい}に{f|負|ま}けた。
  The non-action was a choice. Causal `なくて` prefers things you could not help;
  for deliberate causes use `なかったので／なかったから`.

- ❌ {f|朝|あさ}ごはんを{f|食|た}べ<b>なくて</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ {f|朝|あさ}ごはんを{f|食|た}べ<b>ないで</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|行|い}きます。
  Going to work is a deliberate act, and skipping breakfast is the manner of doing
  it, not its cause.

## Practice

**1.** {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|暑|あつ}くて、エアコンを{f|消|け}さ____{f|寝|ね}てしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

エアコンを{f|消|け}さ<b>ないで</b>{f|寝|ね}てしまった。

Sleeping is something the speaker did, and leaving the air conditioner on describes
the state they were in while doing it. That is manner, so `ないで`.

</details>

**2.** バスが{f|来|こ}（ないで・なくて）、{f|一時間|いちじかん}も{f|待|ま}ちました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

バスが{f|来|こ}<b>なくて</b>、{f|一時間|いちじかん}も{f|待|ま}ちました。

The waiting is a consequence the speaker suffered, and the bus not coming was
outside their control - the textbook profile for causal `なくて`.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Please don't decide anything without asking me."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|私|わたし}に{f|聞|き}か<b>ないで</b>{f|決|き}めないでください。

Two negatives, both `ないで`, and for different reasons: the first is *without
asking* (manner), the second is the negative request. Neither slot can take
`なくて`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|店|みせ}は{f|安|やす}くないで、あまり{f|人気|にんき}がない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|店|みせ}は{f|安|やす}<b>くなくて</b>、あまり{f|人気|にんき}がない。

<code>{f|安|やす}い</code> is an い-adjective. Only verbs form `ないで`; every other word
class negates and joins with `なくて`, built from the `くない` form.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|連絡|れんらく}し<b>ないで</b>{f|帰|かえ}りました。
(b) {f|連絡|れんらく}が<b>なくて</b>{f|心配|しんぱい}しました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is about the speaker's own behaviour: they went home, and the going home was
done without getting in touch first. The focus is on the manner of one action, and
it carries a faint sense of something skipped that should have been done.

(b) is about what happened to the speaker as a result of someone else's silence.
There are two linked facts - no contact, therefore worry - and the second half is a
feeling rather than a deliberate act, which is exactly the environment causal
`なくて` wants.

</details>
