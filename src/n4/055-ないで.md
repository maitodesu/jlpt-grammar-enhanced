# ～ないで

**Meaning** · without doing something - and, in speech, a bare "don't"

`～ないで` is the <b>negative て-form of a verb</b>. Like the ordinary て-form it hooks
one clause onto the next, but it hooks on a *non-event*: the thing that did not
happen while the main action went ahead. You met it at N5 inside
`～ないでください`; that `で` was never decoration, and the form stands on its own.

## Formation

Build the ない-form, then add `で`. Nothing else changes.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ない-form + で | {f|急|いそ}ぐ → {f|急|いそ}がないで |
| Verb (る-verb) | ない-form + で | {f|閉|し}める → {f|閉|し}めないで |
| Verb (する) | しないで | {f|運動|うんどう}する → {f|運動|うんどう}しないで |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | {f|来|こ}ないで | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ないで |
| Written / formal equivalent | ない-form − ない + ずに | {f|急|いそ}ぐ → {f|急|いそ}がずに |
| With a following ending | ないで + ください／ほしい／くれる | {f|急|いそ}がないでください |
| い-adjective | not available - use くなくて | {f|高|たか}い → ❌{f|高|たか}くないで |
| な-adjective / Noun | not available - use でなくて | {f|静|しず}かだ → ❌{f|静|しず}かじゃないで |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ないで` is **verb-only**. If the negated word is an adjective
or a noun, the joining form is `なくて`, and no `で` version exists.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the same string does three jobs, and context separates them cleanly.
(1) *Without doing*: {f|地図|ちず}を{f|見|み}ないで{f|歩|ある}いた.
(2) *Instead of doing*: {f|勉強|べんきょう}しないでゲームをした.
(3) A **negative request**, with `ください` or with nothing at all:
{f|開|あ}けないでください／{f|開|あ}けないで.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
けさは<b>{f|食|た}べないで</b>{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}た。
I left the house this morning without eating.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|何|なに}も<b>{f|買|か}わないで</b>{f|帰|かえ}りました。
I came home without buying anything.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|靴|くつ}を<b>{f|脱|ぬ}がないで</b>{f|入|はい}ってください。
Please come in without taking your shoes off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|辞書|じしょ}を<b>{f|使|つか}わないで</b>{f|読|よ}んでみましょう。
Let's try reading it without using a dictionary.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日曜日|にちようび}はどこにも<b>{f|行|い}かないで</b>、{f|一日中|いちにちじゅう}ゲームをしていた。
On Sunday I didn't go anywhere - I played games all day.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|願|ねが}い、この{f|話|はなし}はまだ<b>{f|言|い}わないで</b>ね。
Please - don't tell anyone about this yet.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|疲|つか}れていたので、{f|晩|ばん}ごはんも<b>{f|作|つく}らないで</b>そのまま{f|寝|ね}てしまいました。
I was so tired yesterday that I fell asleep without even making dinner.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}を<b>{f|読|よ}まないで</b>{f|組|く}み{f|立|た}てたら、ねじが{f|二本|にほん}{f|余|あま}ってしまった。
I assembled it without reading the instructions and ended up with two screws left over.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The joining `ないで` is register-neutral. It appears in casual speech and in
ordinary polite writing alike, and the politeness of the sentence is decided at the
end, by the main verb: <code>{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}たないで{f|出|で}かけた</code> and
<code>{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}たないで{f|出|で}かけました</code> differ only there.

The **request** use is different, and it is the one to watch. Bare `ないで` with
nothing after it - {f|見|み}ないで, {f|行|い}かないで - is casual and emotionally
direct. It is what you say to a friend, a partner, a child or a pet, and it can
sound pleading rather than commanding. `ね` softens it further
(<code>{f|忘|わす}れないでね</code>); `よ` presses. Said to a stranger or a superior it is far
too blunt, and you want `ないでください` at minimum, or
`ないでいただけますか` if you are genuinely asking a favour.

Two neighbouring endings live on the same stem and are worth having ready:
`～ないでほしい`「I'd rather you didn't」, which reports a wish instead of issuing an
order, and `～ないでくれる？／～ないでくれない？`「could you not…?」, casual but softer
than the bare form because it is a question.

In writing - reports, instructions, signage, anything with a formal voice - the
joining `ないで` is routinely replaced by `ずに`. That swap is not optional in a
stiff register; <code>{f|確認|かくにん}しないで{f|送|おく}りました</code> in a written apology
reads as spoken language.

## Contrast

### ～ないで vs ～ずに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register. `ないで` is spoken and
neutral; `ずに` is written and formal. Only `ずに` has an irregular form: する
becomes <b>せずに</b>, never ❌しずに.

</div>

`ずに` is a survival from classical Japanese, and it attaches to the same negative
stem: {f|書|か}かない → {f|書|か}かずに, {f|食|た}べない → {f|食|た}べずに,
{f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ずに.

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|何|なに}も<b>{f|言|い}わずに</b>{f|部屋|へや}を{f|出|で}て{f|行|い}った。
He left the room without saying a word.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|無理|むり}を<b>せずに</b>ゆっくり{f|休|やす}んでください。
Please rest properly and don't push yourself.
</pre>

Two limits on `ずに`. It cannot carry a request: there is no ❌<code>{f|見|み}ずにください</code>
 - requests are `ないで` territory. And the *instead of* reading is weaker with `ずに`;
for that sense `ないで` is the natural choice in speech.

You will also meet bare `ず` with no `に`, joining clauses in written Japanese:
<code>{f|誰|だれ}にも{f|相談|そうだん}せず、{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|決|き}めた</code>. Treat it as the
same word wearing its most formal clothes.

### ～ないで vs ～なくて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ないで` tells you *how* the next thing happened;
`なくて` tells you *why* it happened.

</div>

<pre>
{f|朝|あさ}ごはんを<b>{f|食|た}べないで</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}へ{f|行|い}った。
I went to work without eating breakfast.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|朝|あさ}ごはんを<b>{f|食|た}べなくて</b>、おなかがすいた。
I didn't eat breakfast, so I got hungry.
</pre>

The first sentence is about the manner of going; the second gives the cause of the
hunger. The practical consequence is that `なくて` cannot introduce a request or a
command - ❌<code>{f|食|た}べなくてください</code> is not Japanese - and that a `ないで`
clause must be followed by something the same subject deliberately did. This
distinction has its own page at this level; it repays the time.

### ～ないで vs ～ないように

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ないで` is *without doing it*; `ないように` is *so that it does
not happen*. One reports, the other aims.

</div>

<pre>
{f|窓|まど}を<b>{f|閉|し}めないで</b>{f|出|で}かけた。
I went out without closing the window.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|風邪|かぜ}を<b>ひかないように</b>、{f|上着|うわぎ}を{f|持|も}って{f|行|い}きます。
I'll take a jacket so I don't catch a cold.
</pre>

The difference matters most in requests. <code>{f|遅|おく}れないでください</code> is a flat
"please don't be late"; <code>{f|遅|おく}れないようにしてください</code> is "please make sure
you're not late", which frames the same content as advice and lands far more gently.
`ように` also survives being said upward, where the bare negative request does not.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|見|み}ない<b>て</b> → ✅ {f|見|み}ない<b>で</b>
  The negative て-form is `ないで`. There is no form ❌`ないて` anywhere in the
  language.

- ❌ {f|静|しず}か<b>じゃないで</b>{f|集中|しゅうちゅう}できない。 → ✅ {f|静|しず}か<b>じゃなくて</b>{f|集中|しゅうちゅう}できない。
  `ないで` attaches only to verbs. Adjectives and nouns join with `なくて` /
  `でなくて`.

- ❌ {f|準備|じゅんび}を<b>しずに</b>{f|出|で}かけた。 → ✅ {f|準備|じゅんび}を<b>せずに</b>{f|出|で}かけた。
  する is irregular before `ずに`: the stem is `せ`, not `し`.

- ❌ {f|朝|あさ}ごはんを{f|食|た}べ<b>なくて</b>{f|出|で}かけた。 → ✅ {f|朝|あさ}ごはんを{f|食|た}べ<b>ないで</b>{f|出|で}かけた。
  Going out is not *caused* by not eating. When the negative describes the manner
  of the main action, it must be `ないで`.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、そこに{f|座|すわ}ら<b>ないで</b>。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、そちらには{f|座|すわ}ら<b>ないでください</b>。
  Bare `ないで` is intimate and blunt. Upward it needs `ください`, or better, a
  question form.

## Practice

**1.** {f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}た____{f|出|で}かけたので、ぬれてしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}た<b>ないで</b>{f|出|で}かけたので、ぬれてしまった。

Not taking an umbrella is the *manner* in which the going-out happened, so the
joining form is `ないで`. Writing <code>{f|持|も}たなくて</code> would claim that going out
was caused by the lack of an umbrella.

</details>

**2.** {f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|確認|かくにん}（しないで・しなくて）{f|送|おく}ってしまいました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|確認|かくにん}<b>しないで</b>{f|送|おく}ってしまいました。

The sending is the main event and the not-checking describes how it was done - 
carelessly, in one motion. `しなくて` would need a consequence after it, such as
<code>{f|確認|かくにん}しなくて、あとで{f|困|こま}った</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, in a formal written register: "He signed the contract without reading it."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}は{f|契約書|けいやくしょ}を<b>{f|読|よ}まずに</b>サインした。

`ずに` is the written counterpart of `ないで`, and a sentence about contracts is
exactly where it belongs. <code>{f|読|よ}まないで</code> is not wrong, but it sounds spoken.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|宿題|しゅくだい}をしずにねてしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|宿題|しゅくだい}を<b>せずに</b>{f|寝|ね}てしまった。

The `ず` ending takes an irregular stem from する - `せ`. Every other verb uses its
ordinary ない-stem, which is why this one is so easy to get wrong.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|電気|でんき}を{f|消|け}さ<b>ないで</b>。
(b) {f|電気|でんき}を{f|消|け}さ<b>ないでください</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is bare and intimate. With no ending after it, it reads as an immediate,
emotional "don't!" - the kind of thing said to a family member or a close friend,
and depending on the tone it can sound closer to pleading than to ordering.

(b) adds the standard polite request ending. It is neutral, usable with a
colleague or a stranger, and it sounds like an instruction rather than a reaction.
Between the two sit <code>{f|消|け}さないでね</code> (softer) and
<code>{f|消|け}さないでくれる？</code> (casual but framed as a question).

</details>
