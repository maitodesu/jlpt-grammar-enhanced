# ～にくい

**Meaning** · hard to do - the action meets resistance, physical or social

`～にくい` is built the same way as `～やすい`, from the ます-stem, and it comes out
as an い-adjective describing the thing. But it is not simply "not easy". `にくい`
says there is something in the way: the meat is tough, the handle is the wrong
shape, the request is embarrassing to make.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + にくい | {f|言|い}う → {f|言|い}いにくい |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + にくい | {f|食|た}べる → {f|食|た}べにくい |
| Verb (する) | し + にくい | {f|運転|うんてん}する → {f|運転|うんてん}しにくい |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | {f|来|き} + にくい | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|き}にくい |

The compound then behaves as a plain い-adjective:

| い-adjective form | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Polite | + です | {f|言|い}いにくいです |
| Negative | にくい → にくくない | {f|言|い}いにくくない |
| Past | にくい → にくかった | {f|言|い}いにくかった |
| て-form | にくい → にくくて | {f|言|い}いにくくて |
| Before a noun | にくい + Noun | {f|言|い}いにくい{f|話|はなし} |
| Adverbial | にくい → にくく | {f|言|い}いにくくなる |
| Noun form | にくい → にくさ | {f|言|い}いにくさ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - `にくい` is occasionally written {f|難|にく}い, but kana is the normal
spelling for the suffix. Keep it apart from the unrelated adjective
{f|憎|にく}い, "hateful", which is a full word in its own right and never attaches
to a stem.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|肉|にく}は{f|固|かた}くて<b>{f|食|た}べにくい</b>。
This meat is tough and hard to eat.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|漢字|かんじ}は<b>{f|書|か}きにくい</b>です。
That kanji is awkward to write.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}が{f|暗|くら}くて、{f|字|じ}が<b>{f|読|よ}みにくい</b>。
The room is dark and the writing is hard to read.
</pre>

<pre>
すみません、ちょっと<b>{f|言|い}いにくい</b>ことなんですが…
Sorry - this is a little awkward to bring up, but…
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|説明|せつめい}が{f|長|なが}くて、<b>{f|分|わ}かりにくかった</b>です。
The write-up for this question was long, and it was hard to follow.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|傘|かさ}は{f|風|かぜ}に{f|強|つよ}くて、<b>{f|壊|こわ}れにくい</b>そうです。
Apparently this umbrella stands up to wind and doesn't break easily.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しいスマホは{f|画面|がめん}が{f|大|おお}きすぎて、{f|片手|かたて}では<b>{f|使|つか}いにくい</b>です。
The new phone's screen is too big, so it's awkward to use one-handed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先輩|せんぱい}には{f|少|すこ}し<b>{f|頼|たの}みにくかった</b>ので、{f|同|おな}じクラスの{f|友|とも}だちに{f|手伝|てつだ}ってもらいました。
It felt a bit awkward to ask my senior, so I got a classmate to help me instead.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`にくい` covers three kinds of resistance, and it is worth naming them because
learners tend to notice only the first.

1. **Physical** - the shape, size or state of a thing fights the action:
   <code>{f|切|き}りにくい</code>, <code>{f|持|も}ちにくい</code>, <code>{f|歩|ある}きにくい</code>.
2. **Social or emotional** - nothing physical is stopping you; it is simply
   uncomfortable: <code>{f|言|い}いにくい</code>, <code>{f|聞|き}きにくい</code>,
   <code>{f|断|ことわ}りにくい</code>. This is one of the most useful things `にくい`
   does, and it has no neat English equivalent.
3. **Resistance to a spontaneous event** - with a verb that happens by itself,
   `にくい` means the thing holds out against it:
   <code>{f|汚|よご}れにくい</code>, <code>{f|冷|さ}めにくい</code>,
   <code>{f|太|ふと}りにくい</code>. This sense is a selling point, and packaging
   copy is full of it.

The social sense earns its keep as a softener. Opening a difficult request with
{f|言|い}いにくいんですが or お{f|願|ねが}いしにくいのですが tells the listener you
know you are imposing, and it is standard in polite adult speech at any level of
formality.

`にくい` itself is register-neutral: no politeness, no gender or age marking. In
casual speech people often reach for `づらい` instead (see below), but
`にくい` is never wrong and is the safer choice in writing.

Two derived forms carry over from `やすい`. <code>にくくなる</code> - "become harder":
{f|年|とし}を{f|取|と}ると、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|言葉|ことば}が{f|覚|おぼ}えにくくなります。
And the noun in `さ`: この{f|道具|どうぐ}は{f|使|つか}いにくさが{f|問題|もんだい}です。

## Contrast

### ～にくい vs ～づらい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `づらい` is about **the speaker's own discomfort** while doing
something on purpose. `にくい` is about the situation, and it can also describe a
thing resisting an event nobody is doing.

</div>

`づらい` is <code>{f|辛|つら}い</code>, "painful", in suffix form, and that origin
still shows: it reports how the action feels to the person performing it.

<pre>
{f|足|あし}が{f|痛|いた}くて、{f|階段|かいだん}が<b>{f|上|のぼ}りづらい</b>です。
My leg hurts, so the stairs are hard going.
</pre>

<pre>
このドアは{f|重|おも}くて<b>{f|開|あ}けにくい</b>です。
This door is heavy and hard to open.
</pre>

Both would be understood either way round, and for volitional actions
（{f|言|い}いにくい／{f|言|い}いづらい） the two are close to interchangeable, with
`づらい` a shade more subjective and more colloquial. The clear split is at sense
3 above: for a thing resisting a spontaneous event, `にくい` is the settled
choice. <code>{f|壊|こわ}れづらい</code> is increasingly heard in casual speech, but
many speakers still prefer <code>{f|壊|こわ}れにくい</code>, and it is what you will
see in print.

### ～にくい vs ～がたい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `がたい` means *almost impossible to bring oneself to*, it is
formal and written, and it attaches to a short fixed list of verbs.

</div>

<pre>
その{f|話|はなし}は<b>{f|信|しん}じがたい</b>。
That story is hard to credit.
</pre>

Compare <code>{f|信|しん}じにくい</code>, which is the everyday spoken version and
sounds milder. `がたい` lives on a handful of verbs of thought and feeling - 
{f|信|しん}じる, {f|想像|そうぞう}する, {f|理解|りかい}する, {f|忘|わす}れる - and
never on a physical action: ❌{f|切|き}りがたい is not Japanese, while
<code>{f|切|き}りにくい</code> is ordinary. Treat `がたい` as vocabulary to recognise
rather than a productive pattern.

### ～にくい vs the negative potential

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `にくい` says the action is possible but unpleasant. The
negative potential says it cannot be done at all.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|字|じ}は{f|小|ちい}さくて<b>{f|読|よ}みにくい</b>ですが、{f|読|よ}めます。
This print is small and hard on the eyes, but I can read it.
</pre>

Swap in {f|読|よ}めません and the sentence contradicts itself. This distinction is
worth practising because English uses "can't read this" loosely for both. If a
magnifying glass would solve it, you want `にくい`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|食|た}べる<b>にくい</b> → ✅ {f|食|た}<b>べにくい</b>
  Like `やすい` and `すぎる`, `にくい` attaches to the ます-stem, never the
  dictionary form.

- ❌ この{f|本|ほん}は{f|難|むずか}し<b>にくい</b>です。 → ✅ この{f|本|ほん}は<b>{f|読|よ}みにくい</b>です。
  `にくい` attaches to verbs only. There is no way to hang it on an adjective;
  if you want "hard", the adjective {f|難|むずか}しい already says it.

- ❌ {f|使|つか}いにくい<b>でした</b> → ✅ {f|使|つか}いにく<b>かったです</b>
  The whole compound is an い-adjective, so the past tense is formed on its `い`.

- ❌ この{f|靴|くつ}<b>を</b>{f|歩|ある}きにくいです。 → ✅ この{f|靴|くつ}<b>は</b>{f|歩|ある}きにくいです。
  There is no verb left to take an object once `にくい` is attached, so the thing
  being described becomes the topic.

## Practice

**1.** このコップは{f|口|くち}が{f|小|ちい}さいので、{f|洗|あら}____です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|洗|あら}<b>いにくい</b>です

The stem is {f|洗|あら}い, from {f|洗|あら}います. A narrow opening physically gets
in the way of washing, which is exactly the resistance `にくい` reports.

</details>

**2.** この{f|服|ふく}は{f|色|いろ}が{f|濃|こ}いので、{f|汚|よご}れが（{f|目立|めだ}ちやすい・{f|目立|めだ}ちにくい）です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|汚|よご}れが<b>{f|目立|めだ}ちにくい</b>です。

Dark colours hide marks, so the stains resist standing out. This is the third
sense: a thing holding out against something that happens by itself, which is the
one advertising copy uses constantly.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "That's a hard thing to ask my boss."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

それは{f|部長|ぶちょう}には<b>{f|聞|き}きにくい</b>ことです。

Nothing physical prevents the question; the obstacle is social, and that is
precisely what `にくい` conveys. <code>{f|聞|き}きづらい</code> would also work here
and sounds slightly more personal.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|机|つくえ}は{f|重|おも}くて{f|運|はこ}ぶにくいでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|机|つくえ}は{f|重|おも}くて<b>{f|運|はこ}びにくかった</b>です。

Two faults in one short sentence: `にくい` needs the ます-stem {f|運|はこ}び, not
the dictionary form, and the past of an い-adjective is `にくかった`, never
`にくいでした`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|質問|しつもん}は{f|答|こた}えにくいです。
(b) この{f|質問|しつもん}には{f|答|こた}えられません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says an answer is available but uncomfortable to give - you know the answer
and would rather not say it, or the question is delicate. It leaves the door open
and often precedes an answer anyway.

(b) shuts the door: you cannot answer, whether from ignorance or from a rule that
forbids it. Choosing (b) when you mean (a) sounds far more final than you
probably intend, which is why (a) is the diplomatic option.

</details>
