# ～とか～とか

**Meaning** · things like A and B - a casual, deliberately incomplete list

`とか` tags each item as *one example among others*. Two or three go on the
record, the rest stay implied, and the whole thing sounds relaxed rather than
official. Unlike the tidier `や`, it will happily list verbs, adjectives and whole
complaints, which is why spoken Japanese leans on it so heavily.

## Formation

`とか` follows **each** item, including - usually - the last one.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Nとか Nとか | すし → すしとかラーメンとか |
| Verb (plain form) | Vるとか Vるとか | {f|歩|ある}くとか{f|走|はし}るとか |
| Verb (past) | Vたとか Vたとか | {f|忘|わす}れたとか{f|落|お}としたとか |
| い-adjective | ～いとか | {f|暑|あつ}いとか{f|寒|さむ}いとか |
| な-adjective / noun | ～だとか | {f|静|しず}かだとか{f|便利|べんり}だとか |
| A single item | Nとか（one only） | {f|明日|あした}とか、どう? |
| Before a case particle | ～とか + が／を／に | パンとかを{f|買|か}う（often dropped） |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the final `とか` is optional but normal. Leaving it on is what
makes the list feel open; cutting it off after the last item makes the sentence
sound as though you had planned it.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - with な-adjectives and nouns the standard written form inserts `だ`:
{f|静|しず}かだとか. In fast speech you will hear the bare {f|静|しず}かとか as
well; treat `だとか` as the safe form.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|朝|あさ}はパン<b>とか</b>ヨーグルト<b>とか</b>を{f|食|た}べます。
For breakfast I have bread, yoghurt, that sort of thing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|音楽|おんがく}はジャズ<b>とか</b>クラシック<b>とか</b>が{f|好|す}きです。
Music-wise I like jazz, classical and so on.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}<b>とか</b>、{f|時間|じかん}ある?
Are you free tomorrow or something?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|休|やす}みの{f|日|ひ}は{f|部屋|へや}を{f|片付|かたづ}ける<b>とか</b>、{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}く<b>とか</b>しています。
On my days off I tidy the flat, go shopping, things like that.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|忙|いそが}しい<b>とか</b>{f|疲|つか}れた<b>とか</b>{f|言|い}って、{f|集|あつ}まりに{f|来|こ}ません。
He says he's busy, he's tired and so on, and never turns up to the get-togethers.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|沖縄|おきなわ}<b>とか</b>{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}<b>とか</b>、{f|遠|とお}いところへ{f|行|い}ってみたいなあ。
Okinawa, Hokkaido - I'd love to go somewhere far away like that.
</pre>

<pre>
アルバイトを{f|探|さが}すなら、カフェ<b>とか</b>{f|本屋|ほんや}<b>とか</b>がいいんじゃない?
If you're looking for part-time work, wouldn't a cafe or a bookshop or somewhere be good?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|面接|めんせつ}では{f|志望|しぼう}の{f|理由|りゆう}<b>とか</b>{f|将来|しょうらい}の{f|計画|けいかく}<b>とか</b>を{f|聞|き}かれました。
In the interview they asked me about my reasons for applying, my future plans, that kind of thing.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`とか` is **casual and spoken**. It is not slang and no one will think less of you
for it in conversation, but it does not belong in a report, a formal email or an
exam essay - the neutral equivalents there are `や` and `など`.

Piled on too thickly it makes a speaker sound vague and non-committal, which is
exactly why it is useful when you *want* to be non-committal:

- **Softening a proposal.** {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|火曜日|かようび}とか、どうですか
  floats a date instead of demanding one. This is one of the most common modern
  uses and it involves no listing at all.
- **Distancing yourself from what you are reporting.**
  {f|中止|ちゅうし}になるとか{f|聞|き}きました - "I heard something about it being
  cancelled." You pass on the information without vouching for it.
- **Quoting a complaint you find tiresome.** The
  <code>～とか～とか{f|言|い}って</code> frame in the fifth example above carries an
  audible eye-roll.

`とかいう` deserves its own line: {f|田中|たなか}とかいう{f|人|ひと}が{f|来|き}ました
means "some person called Tanaka, or something like that, came". It signals that
you barely registered the name - polite in a self-effacing way when the name is
yours to remember, faintly rude when it is a person the listener cares about.

Written forms of the same word exist（`だとか`, `～とか～とかいう`）, but if you are
choosing between registers, the rule is simple: speech takes `とか`, paper takes
`や` and `など`.

## Contrast

### とか vs や

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both leave the list open. `や` goes **between** nouns only and
is register-neutral; `とか` goes **after each** item, is casual, and takes verbs
and adjectives as well.

</div>

<pre>
{f|棚|たな}に{f|辞書|じしょ}<b>や</b>{f|地図|ちず}が{f|置|お}いてあります。
There are dictionaries, maps and so on on the shelf.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|棚|たな}に{f|辞書|じしょ}<b>とか</b>{f|地図|ちず}<b>とか</b>{f|置|お}いてあるよ。
There are dictionaries and maps and stuff on the shelf.
</pre>

The first could be a museum label; the second could not. And where `や` simply
stops working, `とか` carries on:

<pre>
{f|泳|およ}ぐ<b>とか</b>{f|走|はし}る<b>とか</b>、{f|体|からだ}を{f|動|うご}かすことが{f|好|す}きです。
I like moving my body - swimming, running, that sort of thing.
</pre>

❌{f|泳|およ}ぐや{f|走|はし}る is impossible: `や` joins nouns and nothing else.

### とか vs ～たり～たり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～たり～たり` lists **actions** and has to be closed with
`する`. `とか` just lists, and needs no closing verb.

</div>

<pre>
{f|夜|よる}は{f|音楽|おんがく}を{f|聞|き}い<b>たり</b>、{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}ん<b>だり</b>します。
In the evening I listen to music, read, and so on.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|夜|よる}は{f|音楽|おんがく}を{f|聞|き}く<b>とか</b>、{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}む<b>とか</b>かな。
Evenings are, like, listening to music or reading, I suppose.
</pre>

`～たり` is neutral in register and grammatically tighter - the `する` at the end
is not optional. `とか` is looser in both senses. Note also that `とか` can list
nouns, which `～たり` cannot.

### とか vs か

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `か` offers a genuine either/or. `とか` offers examples and
asks you to choose nothing.

</div>

<pre>
コーヒー<b>か</b>{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}を{f|持|も}ってきてください。
Please bring coffee or tea.
</pre>

<pre>
コーヒー<b>とか</b>{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}<b>とか</b>を{f|持|も}ってきてください。
Please bring coffee, tea, something along those lines.
</pre>

With `か` the listener picks one of exactly two. With `とか` the two named drinks
are only illustrations, and green tea would satisfy the request perfectly well.
This is also why a question offering a real choice cannot use `とか`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|泳|およ}ぐや{f|走|はし}るが{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ {f|泳|およ}ぐ<b>とか</b>{f|走|はし}る<b>とか</b>が{f|好|す}きです。
  `や` links nouns and only nouns. When the items are actions, `とか` is the
  listing particle that works.

- ❌ この{f|書類|しょるい}には{f|名前|なまえ}とか{f|住所|じゅうしょ}とかを{f|書|か}いてください。 → ✅ ～{f|名前|なまえ}や{f|住所|じゅうしょ}などを{f|書|か}いてください。
  Grammatically fine, socially wrong. A written instruction takes `や` and `など`;
  `とか` makes an official form sound like a chat.

- ❌ {f|肉|にく}とか{f|魚|さかな}とか、どちらが{f|好|す}きですか。 → ✅ {f|肉|にく}と{f|魚|さかな}と、どちらが{f|好|す}きですか。
  `どちら` asks for a choice between two fixed options, but `とか` says the two
  named items are merely examples. The two ideas cancel each other out.

- ❌ とかコーヒーとか{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}が{f|飲|の}みたい。 → ✅ コーヒー<b>とか</b>{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}<b>とか</b>{f|飲|の}みたい。
  `とか` is a postposition: it follows the item it tags. It never leads.

## Practice

**1.** {f|旅行|りょこう}のとき、カメラ____{f|地図|ちず}____{f|持|も}っていきます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|旅行|りょこう}のとき、カメラ<b>とか</b>{f|地図|ちず}<b>とか</b>{f|持|も}っていきます。

One `とか` after each item, and the `を` is comfortably dropped in speech. Written
neutrally, the same sentence would be
カメラや{f|地図|ちず}などを{f|持|も}っていきます.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural option for a job application form:
{f|趣味|しゅみ}は{f|読書|どくしょ}（とか・や）{f|映画|えいが}（とか・など）です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|趣味|しゅみ}は{f|読書|どくしょ}<b>や</b>{f|映画|えいが}<b>など</b>です。

A form is written register. `とか` would read as if you had wandered in mid
conversation. Keep `とか` for the interview itself, where speaking casually about
your hobbies is perfectly appropriate.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, casually: "Shall we do something on Saturday or whenever?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|土曜日|どようび}<b>とか</b>、{f|何|なに}かしない?

Here `とか` is not listing anything - a single `とか` after a date softens the
proposal into a suggestion the other person can decline without awkwardness. This
vagueness-as-politeness use is everywhere in casual Japanese.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会議|かいぎ}では{f|予算|よさん}とかスケジュールとかについて{f|報告|ほうこく}いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}では{f|予算|よさん}<b>や</b>スケジュール<b>など</b>について{f|報告|ほうこく}いたします。

いたします is humble, formal Japanese, and `とか` is casual speech; the two cannot
share a sentence. Swap in `や` and `など` and the register lines up from beginning
to end.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|昼|ひる}ごはんはうどんかそばにします。
(b) {f|昼|ひる}ごはんはうどんとかそばとかにします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) narrows lunch down to two candidates and commits to picking one of them. The
decision is nearly made.

(b) names udon and soba only as illustrations of the sort of thing you feel like - 
noodles, something light, whatever the shop has. Ramen would not contradict it.
`か` closes a set; `とか` opens one.

</details>
