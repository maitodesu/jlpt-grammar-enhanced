# ～の中で

**Meaning** · in; among - it fences off the set you are choosing or ranking within

<code>～の{f|中|なか}で</code> names the field. Everything that follows it - a
superlative, a second place, an exception, a question word - is understood to
apply *inside that fence and nowhere else*. Literally "in the middle of ～", but
the set does not have to be a physical container: friends, seasons, films you have
watched all work.

## Formation

The pattern is a noun ({f|中|なか}) plus the particle `で`, so anything that can
modify a noun can sit in front of it.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (a set) | Noun + の{f|中|なか}で | クラス → クラスの{f|中|なか}で |
| A spelled-out set | AとBとCの{f|中|なか}で | {f|米|こめ}とパンと{f|麺|めん}の{f|中|なか}で |
| Demonstrative | この／その／あの + {f|中|なか}で（no の） | この{f|中|なか}で |
| Noun-modifying clause | Clause + Noun + の{f|中|なか}で | {f|読|よ}んだ{f|本|ほん}の{f|中|なか}で |
| Topicalised | ～の{f|中|なか}では | スポーツの{f|中|なか}では |
| Place or time span | Place／span + で（**no** {f|中|なか}） | {f|日本|にほん}で／{f|一年|いちねん}で |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>の{f|中|なか}で</code> is only half a sentence. It has to be
followed by something that picks a member out: `いちばん`,
<code>{f|二番目|にばんめ}に</code>, `だけ`, <code>{f|特|とく}に</code>, or a question word
like `どれ` / `だれ` / `どこ`.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - a demonstrative already contains the "this set" idea, so it takes
{f|中|なか} directly: この{f|中|なか}で, never ❌このの{f|中|なか}で.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|四|よっ}つ<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、どれがいいですか。
Which of these four would you like?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|野菜|やさい}<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、トマトだけ{f|苦手|にがて}です。
Tomatoes are the one vegetable I can't handle.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日本|にほん}の{f|都市|とし}<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、{f|京都|きょうと}が{f|二番目|にばんめ}に{f|好|す}きです。
Of Japan's cities, Kyoto is my second favourite.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|友|とも}だち<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、{f|彼女|かのじょ}だけが{f|本当|ほんとう}のことを{f|知|し}っています。
She's the only one of my friends who knows the truth.
</pre>

<pre>
スポーツ<b>の{f|中|なか}では</b>、{f|特|とく}に{f|水泳|すいえい}が{f|得意|とくい}です。
Among sports, swimming is what I'm especially good at.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|世界|せかい}の{f|国|くに}<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、{f|一度|いちど}{f|行|い}ってみたいのはアイスランドです。
Of all the countries in the world, the one I'd like to visit once is Iceland.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今学期|こんがっき}{f|習|なら}った{f|文法|ぶんぽう}<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、いちばん{f|難|むずか}しかったのは{f|敬語|けいご}でした。
Of the grammar we studied this term, the hardest was the honorific language.
</pre>

<pre>

Out of everyone on the team, the one who gets in earliest is the department head.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and equally at home in speech and writing. {f|中|なか} is often
written in kanji and often in kana; neither spelling changes the reading or the
formality.

Two things do vary. First, with a **group of members** the {f|中|なか} is
frequently dropped in speech: クラスでいちばん{f|足|あし}が{f|速|はや}い says the
same thing as クラスの{f|中|なか}でいちばん{f|足|あし}が{f|速|はや}い, a little more
briskly. Keeping {f|中|なか} makes the boundary of the set explicit, which is why
it survives in careful speech and in writing.

Second, with a **place or a time span** the {f|中|なか} is not merely optional - 
it is out of place. {f|日本|にほん}で, {f|世界|せかい}で,
{f|一年|いちねん}で, クラスで are locations and stretches of time, not bags of
items, so there is no "inside the set" to point at.
{f|日本|にほん}の{f|中|なか}で is not ungrammatical, but it reads as though you
meant physically within Japan's borders, and most speakers would just say
{f|日本|にほん}で.

Adding `は` - <code>の{f|中|なか}では</code> - topicalises the field and quietly
implies a contrast: "*within this set* it's true; elsewhere, who knows."
{f|和食|わしょく}の{f|中|なか}ではおすしがいちばん{f|好|す}きです leaves room for
some non-Japanese dish to be the overall winner.

## Contrast

### ～の{f|中|なか}で vs bare で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a set of members takes <code>の{f|中|なか}で</code>; a place or a
period takes plain `で`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|家族|かぞく}の{f|中|なか}で{f|父|ちち}がいちばんよく{f|笑|わら}います。
My father laughs the most of anyone in the family.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|町|まち}で{f|一番|いちばん}{f|古|ふる}いお{f|寺|てら}はどこですか。
Which is the oldest temple in this town?
</pre>

A family is a collection of individuals you can line up and compare - the fence is
real, so {f|中|なか} earns its place. A town is where the comparing happens, not
what is being compared, and inserting {f|中|なか} there points at geography instead
of at a set.

### ～の{f|中|なか}で vs ～の{f|中|なか}から

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `で` compares within the set; `から` takes something out of it.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|中|なか}で、いちばん{f|軽|かる}いのはどれですか。
Which of these is the lightest?
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|中|なか}から、{f|好|す}きなものを{f|一|ひと}つ{f|選|えら}んでください。
Please choose one thing you like from these.
</pre>

Verbs of choosing and extracting - {f|選|えら}ぶ, {f|取|と}り{f|出|だ}す,
{f|決|き}める - pull the item out, so they want `から`. Adjectival
judgements leave everything where it is, so they want `で`. Using
<code>{f|中|なか}で{f|選|えら}ぶ</code> is not impossible, but it shifts the meaning to
"do your choosing inside this room".

### ～の{f|中|なか}で vs より

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - three or more members: <code>の{f|中|なか}で</code> plus a
superlative. Exactly two: `より`.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|三|さん}{f|種類|しゅるい}の{f|中|なか}で、これがいちばん{f|甘|あま}いです。
Of these three varieties, this one is the sweetest.
</pre>

<pre>
こちらのほうがあちら<b>より</b>{f|甘|あま}いです。
This one is sweeter than that one.
</pre>

The mistake to avoid is running a two-item set through the superlative frame.
{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}と{f|緑茶|りょくちゃ}の{f|中|なか}でどちらがいいですか is
overbuilt - with two candidates on the table, drop the fence and just ask
{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}と{f|緑茶|りょくちゃ}と、どちらがいいですか.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この<b>の</b>{f|中|なか}で → ✅ この{f|中|なか}で
  この, その, あの already attach to the following noun. Doubling the `の` is the
  single most frequent slip on this pattern.

- ❌ {f|果物|くだもの}の{f|中|なか}<b>に</b>みかんがいちばん{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ {f|果物|くだもの}の{f|中|なか}<b>で</b>みかんがいちばん{f|好|す}きです。
  `に` would say the tangerines are physically located inside the fruit. `で` is
  the particle that turns a set into an arena for comparison.

- ❌ {f|犬|いぬ}と{f|猫|ねこ}の{f|中|なか}で、{f|猫|ねこ}がいちばん{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ {f|犬|いぬ}と{f|猫|ねこ}と、{f|猫|ねこ}のほうが{f|好|す}きです。
  Two candidates cannot have a "number one". A superlative needs a field of at
  least three.

- ❌ {f|世界|せかい}の{f|中|なか}でこの{f|湖|みずうみ}がいちばん{f|深|ふか}いです。 → ✅ {f|世界|せかい}でこの{f|湖|みずうみ}がいちばん{f|深|ふか}いです。
  {f|世界|せかい}, {f|日本|にほん}, {f|町|まち} and the like are places. Attaching
  {f|中|なか} to them sounds like you are drawing a line on a map.

## Practice

**1.** {f|一年|いちねん}____{f|二月|にがつ}がいちばん{f|寒|さむ}いです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一年|いちねん}<b>で</b>{f|二月|にがつ}がいちばん{f|寒|さむ}いです。

A year is a stretch of time, not a bag of members, so it takes plain `で`.
{f|一年|いちねん}の{f|中|なか}で is heard, but it makes the year sound like a
container you are rummaging in; the bare `で` is what a native speaker says.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural option:
{f|三|さん}つのプラン（の{f|中|なか}で・の{f|中|なか}から）{f|一|ひと}つ{f|選|えら}んでください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|三|さん}つのプラン<b>の{f|中|なか}から</b>{f|一|ひと}つ{f|選|えら}んでください。

{f|選|えら}ぶ physically removes one item from the group, and the source of a
movement is `から`. Use <code>の{f|中|なか}で</code> when nothing is being taken out - 
when you are only judging which member wins.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Of the films I saw last year, this one was the most interesting."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|去年|きょねん}{f|見|み}た{f|映画|えいが}<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、これがいちばんおもしろかったです。

<code>{f|去年|きょねん}{f|見|み}た</code> is a clause modifying {f|映画|えいが}, and
the whole noun phrase then takes <code>の{f|中|なか}で</code>. Note that
{f|映画|えいが} is plural here without any marking - Japanese leaves number to
context, and the fence does the work of "of the films".

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: あのの{f|中|なか}にどれが{f|一番|いちばん}{f|便利|べんり}ですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ あの{f|中|なか}でどれが{f|一番|いちばん}{f|便利|べんり}ですか。

Two fixes. First, あの attaches straight to {f|中|なか} - there is no `の` in
between, because あの is already the modifier. Second, the particle on
{f|中|なか} must be `で`: the set is the arena in which the comparison is carried
out, and `に` would only say where something sits.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|日本料理|にほんりょうり}の{f|中|なか}で、てんぷらがいちばん{f|好|す}きです。
(b) {f|日本料理|にほんりょうり}の{f|中|なか}では、てんぷらがいちばん{f|好|す}きです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply states the winner inside a stated field: among Japanese dishes,
tempura tops the list.

(b) adds `は`, which topicalises the field and hints at a boundary - "*as far as
Japanese food goes*, tempura." It leaves open that something outside that set,
maybe a curry or a steak, would beat it overall. The `は` is doing its usual
contrastive work.

</details>
