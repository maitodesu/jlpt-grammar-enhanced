# ～そうだ - hearsay

**Meaning** · I hear that; they say that - passing on information you got from somewhere else

This `～そうだ` is a **relay**. Somebody told you, you read it, you saw it on the
news, and now you are handing the same content on unchanged. You are not judging it
and not adding to it.

<div class="rule">

**Warning** - there are **two** grammars spelled `そうだ`, and they are unrelated.
This one attaches to the **plain form** and means "I hear that". The other attaches
to the **stem** and means "looks like". <code>{f|高|たか}いそうだ</code> = "I hear it's
expensive". <code>{f|高|たか}そうだ</code> = "it looks expensive". One `い` is the entire
difference.

</div>

## Formation

Attach `そうだ` to the plain form of anything - and unlike `ようだ`, nouns and
な-adjectives **keep their だ**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + そうだ | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|く}るそうだ |
| Verb (past) | た-form + そうだ | {f|来|き}た → {f|来|き}たそうだ |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + そうだ | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ないそうだ |
| い-adjective | plain form + そうだ | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}いそうだ |
| い-adjective (past) | かった-form + そうだ | {f|高|たか}かった → {f|高|たか}かったそうだ |
| な-adjective | ～だ + そうだ | {f|便利|べんり}だ → {f|便利|べんり}だそうだ |
| Noun | Noun + だ + そうだ | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}だそうだ |
| Polite | そうだ → そうです | {f|来|く}るそうです |
| Naming the source | Source + によると／の{f|話|はなし}では | {f|新聞|しんぶん}によると… |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - hearsay `そうだ` **never conjugates**. There is no
❌<code>そうだった</code>, no ❌<code>そうな</code>, no ❌<code>そうに</code>, no
❌<code>そうじゃない</code>. Tense and negation go on the clause *in front* of it:
<code>{f|降|ふ}ったそうです</code>, not ❌<code>{f|降|ふ}るそうでした</code>. This is the
sharpest formal difference from the other `そうだ`, which conjugates freely.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|来|こ}ない<b>そうです</b>。
I hear Yamada isn't coming.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|店|みせ}のラーメンは{f|高|たか}い<b>そうだ</b>。
They say the ramen at that place is expensive.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|天気|てんき}{f|予報|よほう}によると、{f|明日|あした}は{f|雪|ゆき}だ<b>そうです</b>。
According to the forecast, tomorrow will be snowy.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|駅|えき}はとても{f|便利|べんり}だ<b>そうだ</b>。
The new station is supposed to be very convenient.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|母|はは}の{f|話|はなし}では、おばあさんは{f|若|わか}いころ{f|東京|とうきょう}に{f|住|す}んでいた<b>そうだ</b>。
According to my mother, my grandmother lived in Tokyo when she was young.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先輩|せんぱい}は{f|大学|だいがく}のとき{f|毎日|まいにち}アルバイトをしていた<b>そうです</b>。
Apparently my senior worked a part-time job every single day at university.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

Yamaguchi says the meeting time changes from next month.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`そうです` is neutral polite and turns up everywhere: news reports, workplace
updates, ordinary conversation. Plain `そうだ` is the written form; spoken between
friends it sounds bookish, and casual Japanese reaches instead for the quotative
`って` - 「{f|来|こ}ないって」 - or for `らしい`.

Two structural facts follow from the fact that this is a pure relay:

- **It cannot be questioned.** ❌<code>{f|来|く}るそうですか</code> is not how you ask.
  If you want to check what somebody heard, you say
  <code>{f|来|く}るそうですね</code> (seeking agreement) or
  <code>{f|来|く}るんですか</code>.
- **It cannot be hedged.** You do not write
  ❌<code>たぶん{f|来|く}るそうです</code>. The hedging belongs to whoever said it
  first, not to you.

Naming the source is optional but very common, and it is what makes the sentence
sound responsible rather than gossipy. `～によると` is the neutral formula;
<code>～の{f|話|はなし}では</code> is slightly softer and more personal;
<code>～の{f|話|はなし}によると</code> is the fullest and most formal of the three.

## Contrast

<div class="rule">

**The four evidentials, on one axis.** `らしい` leans on **outside** evidence - 
what you heard, read or were told. `ようだ` leans on **your own** evidence - what
you saw, felt and reasoned from. `みたい` is `ようだ` in casual clothes. Appearance
`そうだ` is narrower than any of them: the **immediate look** of a thing. Hearsay
`そうだ` is off the axis - it does not weigh evidence at all, it forwards a message.

</div>

### hearsay ～そうだ vs appearance ～そうだ

This is the distinction the whole page exists for.

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - **plain form** + `そうだ` = "I hear". **Stem** + `そうだ` =
"looks". Nouns can take the first and never the second.

</div>

<pre>
このかばんは{f|高|たか}い<b>そうだ</b>。
I hear this bag is expensive. (someone told me the price)
</pre>

<pre>
このかばんは<b>{f|高|たか}そうだ</b>。
This bag looks expensive. (I'm looking at it; I have no idea of the price)
</pre>

Three further tests separate them when the form alone is ambiguous:

1. **Conjugation.** Appearance `そうだ` becomes <code>{f|高|たか}そうな</code>,
   <code>{f|高|たか}そうに</code>, <code>{f|高|たか}そうだった</code>. Hearsay `そうだ`
   does none of that.
2. **Nouns.** <code>{f|学生|がくせい}だそうだ</code> ("I hear he's a student") is fine;
   ❌<code>{f|学生|がくせい}そうだ</code> does not exist, because appearance `そうだ`
   cannot attach to a noun at all.
3. **Source phrases.** `～によると` can only introduce the hearsay one.

### ～そうだ vs ～らしい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `そうだ` forwards a message with its source intact. `らしい` says
"this is going around" - the source is vaguer, and a little of the speaker's own
judgement leaks in.

</div>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}によると、その{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった<b>そうです</b>。
According to the department head, the plan has been cancelled.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|計画|けいかく}、{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった<b>らしい</b>ですよ。
That plan's apparently been cancelled.
</pre>

The first is a report you would stand behind because you can name who said it. The
second is what you say when you are not quite sure where you picked it up. There is
also a formal difference: `らしい` conjugates like an い-adjective and can modify a
noun - <code>{f|中止|ちゅうし}になったらしい{f|計画|けいかく}</code> - while `そうだ`
can only end a sentence.

### ～そうだ vs ～と{f|言|い}っていた

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>と{f|言|い}っていた</code> quotes a **person** saying a
**thing**. `そうだ` reports the **content** and leaves the speech act behind.

</div>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さんは、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}ると<b>{f|言|い}っていました</b>。
Tanaka said he'd be leaving early today.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|今日|きょう}{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}る<b>そうです</b>。
Tanaka is apparently leaving early today.
</pre>

Use the first when the fact that he *said it* matters - you are reporting a
conversation. Use the second when only the information matters. In a workplace, the
first is the safer choice if somebody might later ask who told you.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|医者|いしゃ}**そうです**。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|医者|いしゃ}**だそうです**。
  Nouns keep their `だ` before hearsay `そうだ`. Dropping it produces the other
  `そうだ`, which cannot take a noun anyway - so the sentence is simply broken.

- ❌ {f|元気|げんき}**なそうです** → ✅ {f|元気|げんき}**だそうです**
  な-adjectives also keep `だ`. The `な` form belongs in front of `よう`, not `そう`.

- ❌ きのうは{f|寒|さむ}い**そうでした**。 → ✅ きのうは{f|寒|さむ}かった**そうです**。
  `そうだ` itself never carries tense. Put the past on the adjective in front of it.

- ❌ この{f|料理|りょうり}は<b>{f|辛|から}そうです</b>。(meaning "I hear it's spicy") → ✅ この{f|料理|りょうり}は<b>{f|辛|から}いそうです</b>。
  Without the `い` you have said it *looks* spicy, which is a claim about the photo
  rather than the taste.

## Practice

**1.** {f|林|はやし}さんの{f|話|はなし}では、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|先生|せんせい}は{f|親切|しんせつ}____そうです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|新|あたら}しい{f|先生|せんせい}は{f|親切|しんせつ}**だ**そうです。

<code>{f|親切|しんせつ}</code> is a な-adjective, and な-adjectives keep `だ` in front of
hearsay `そうだ`. Writing ❌<code>{f|親切|しんせつ}なそうです</code> confuses this
pattern with `ようだ`.

</details>

**2.** {f|窓|まど}の{f|外|そと}を{f|見|み}ながら：「{f|空|そら}が{f|暗|くら}いね。（{f|降|ふ}るそうだ・{f|降|ふ}りそうだ）。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|空|そら}が{f|暗|くら}いね。<b>{f|降|ふ}りそうだ</b>。

The speaker is looking at the sky, so the evidence is a direct visual impression and
the appearance `そうだ` on the ます-stem is what fits. Hearsay `そうだ` would claim
somebody had told you it was going to rain, which the situation rules out.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "According to the news, that shop closed last month."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ニュースによると、あの{f|店|みせ}は{f|先月|せんげつ}しまった<b>そうです</b>。

The tense sits on the verb in front of `そうです` - <code>しまった</code> - because
`そうだ` never inflects. <code>によると</code> naming the source is the standard
partner of this pattern.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|田中|たなか}さんの{f|息子|むすこ}さんは{f|大学生|だいがくせい}そうだったそうです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|田中|たなか}さんの{f|息子|むすこ}さんは{f|大学生|だいがくせい}**だそうです**。

Two faults: a noun needs `だ` before `そうだ`, and `そうだ` cannot be put into the
past. If the point is that he *used to be* a student, move the tense to the clause:
<code>{f|大学生|だいがくせい}だったそうです</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|薬|くすり}は<b>よく{f|効|き}くそうだ</b>。
(b) この{f|薬|くすり}は<b>{f|効|き}きそうだ</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) forwards somebody else's claim: a pharmacist, a friend, a review said this
medicine works well, and the speaker is repeating it without having tried it. (b) is
the speaker's own impression from looking at the thing - the packaging, the size of
the tablet - with nobody else involved. The first is information; the second is a
hunch from the surface.

</details>
