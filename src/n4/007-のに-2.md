# ～のに - in order to

**Meaning** · for doing something - what a thing is good for, and what it costs you

This `のに` is not the "although" one at all. It is the nominaliser `の` plus the
particle `に`: the verb is turned into a noun ("the doing of it") and `に` points at it
as a target. The sentence then judges something against that target - this tool is
handy **for** it, that job took two hours **to** do.

## Formation

Only the **dictionary form** goes in front of it, and the sentence has to end in an
evaluation: a cost, a length of time, or a judgement of usefulness.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | dictionary form + のに | {f|切|き}る → {f|切|き}るのに |
| Verb (る-verb) | dictionary form + のに | {f|開|あ}ける → {f|開|あ}けるのに |
| Verb (する) | する + のに | {f|予約|よやく}する → {f|予約|よやく}するのに |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | {f|来|く}る + のに | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|く}るのに |
| With contrast marking | のに + は | {f|作|つく}る → {f|作|つく}るのには |

The predicates that finish these sentences come from a short list, and it is worth
learning them as a set:

| Second half | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| {f|使|つか}う | is used for | {f|紙|かみ}を{f|切|き}るのに{f|使|つか}う |
| {f|便利|べんり}だ・いい | is handy for | {f|調|しら}べるのに{f|便利|べんり}だ |
| {f|役|やく}に{f|立|た}つ | is useful for | {f|覚|おぼ}えるのに{f|役|やく}に{f|立|た}つ |
| {f|必要|ひつよう}だ・{f|要|い}る | is needed for | {f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}むのに{f|必要|ひつよう}だ |
| （{f|時間|じかん}・お{f|金|かね}が）かかる | takes / costs | {f|直|なお}すのに{f|時間|じかん}がかかる |
| {f|向|む}いている | is suited to | {f|歩|ある}くのに{f|向|む}いている |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - no past tense, no negative, no `な` ever appears before this
`のに`. If you are looking at ❌<code>{f|買|か}ったのに{f|使|つか}います</code> or
❌<code>{f|静|しず}かなのに{f|便利|べんり}です</code>, you have accidentally written the
"although" one.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
このはさみは{f|紙|かみ}を{f|切|き}る<b>のに</b>{f|使|つか}います。
These scissors are for cutting paper.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}まで{f|行|い}く<b>のに</b>{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}かかります。
It takes half an hour to get to the station.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|漢字|かんじ}を{f|調|しら}べる<b>のに</b>{f|便利|べんり}です。
This dictionary is handy for looking up kanji.
</pre>

<pre>
ビザを{f|取|と}る<b>のに</b>、{f|時間|じかん}もお{f|金|かね}もかかりました。
Getting the visa cost me both time and money.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|靴|くつ}は{f|山|やま}を{f|歩|ある}く<b>のに</b>{f|向|む}いています。
These shoes are made for walking in the mountains.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}を{f|片|かた}づける<b>のに</b>、{f|一日中|いちにちじゅう}かかってしまった。
Tidying the room ended up taking me the whole day.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|言葉|ことば}を{f|覚|おぼ}える<b>のに</b>、{f|私|わたし}はカードを{f|作|つく}って{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|見|み}るようにしています。
For learning new words, I make cards and try to look at them every day.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|書類|しょるい}を{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|確認|かくにん}する<b>のに</b>{f|二時間|にじかん}もかかったので、{f|残業|ざんぎょう}になりました。
Checking all these documents took a full two hours, so I ended up working late.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and unusually even-handed about where it appears: it is the ordinary
way to say what a thing is for, so it turns up in conversation, in instruction
manuals, in product descriptions and in recipes alike.

Two shapes to have ready. The `のに` phrase can be **fronted**, with a comma, when the
purpose is what the sentence is about: {f|大人数|おおにんずう}で{f|集|あつ}まるのに、
あの{f|店|みせ}はちょうどいいですよ。 And because `の` is a genuine nominaliser, ordinary
particles can stack on it - <code>のには</code> for contrast,
<code>のにも</code> for "for that as well": {f|作|つく}るのには{f|時間|じかん}が
かかりますが、{f|食|た}べるのは{f|一瞬|いっしゅん}です。

Watch what the sentence is actually judging, because that decides between `のに` and
`のが`. If the thing being described is a **tool, an amount of time or a cost**, you
want `のに`. If it is the **action itself** that is difficult, tiring or fun, you want
`のが`:

- この{f|机|つくえ}は{f|作業|さぎょう}する<b>のに</b>ちょうどいい。 - the desk is being judged.
- {f|作業|さぎょう}する<b>のが</b>{f|大変|たいへん}だった。 - the work is being judged.

Learners produce ❌<code>{f|覚|おぼ}えるのに{f|難|むずか}しい</code> constantly. Say
<code>{f|覚|おぼ}えるのが{f|難|むずか}しい</code>, or keep `のに` and change the ending to
<code>{f|時間|じかん}がかかる</code>.

## Contrast

### ～のに vs ～ために

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ために` is followed by what you **do**; `のに` is followed by an
**assessment**. If the second half is an action you deliberately take, it is `ために`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|健康|けんこう}のために、{f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|野菜|やさい}ジュースを{f|飲|の}んでいます。
I drink vegetable juice every morning for my health.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|健康|けんこう}を{f|保|たも}つ<b>のに</b>、{f|野菜|やさい}ジュースはとてもいいそうです。
Apparently vegetable juice is very good for keeping healthy.
</pre>

The first sentence reports a decision you made and carried out. The second rates the
juice against a purpose. That is why
❌<code>{f|日本語|にほんご}を{f|勉強|べんきょう}するのに{f|日本|にほん}へ{f|行|い}きます</code>
fails: going to Japan is a deliberate act, not a verdict - it needs `ために`.

There is also a subject rule that catches people out. `ために` normally requires the
same person to have the purpose and do the action; `のに` has no such restriction,
because nobody has to be doing anything at all - 
<code>この{f|道具|どうぐ}は{f|開|あ}けるのに{f|使|つか}われます</code> is fine.

### ～のに vs ～ように

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ように` follows a verb you **cannot simply decide to do**:
a potential form, a negative, or a change of state. `のに` follows a plain action.

</div>

<pre>
{f|後|うし}ろの{f|人|ひと}にも{f|見|み}える<b>ように</b>、{f|字|じ}を{f|大|おお}きく{f|書|か}きました。
I wrote the letters big so that the people at the back could see too.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大|おお}きい{f|字|じ}は{f|遠|とお}くから{f|読|よ}む<b>のに</b>いいですね。
Big letters are good for reading from a distance.
</pre>

`ように` states a desired outcome and is followed by the action taken to bring it
about; `のに` states a purpose and is followed by a judgement. A quick test:
<code>{f|忘|わす}れないように</code> ("so as not to forget") could never become
❌<code>{f|忘|わす}れないのに</code> in that sense, because `のに` takes no negatives.

### ～のに "in order to" vs ～のに "although"

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - look at the verb form. Dictionary form and an evaluation at the
end means "in order to". Anything else - past, negative, `な`, an い-adjective - means
"although".

</div>

<pre>
この{f|袋|ふくろ}は{f|本|ほん}を{f|運|はこ}ぶ<b>のに</b>{f|丈夫|じょうぶ}です。
This bag is sturdy enough for carrying books. (in order to)
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|袋|ふくろ}は{f|丈夫|じょうぶ}な<b>のに</b>、{f|軽|かる}いです。
This bag is sturdy and yet light. (although)
</pre>

The swap test settles the rest. Try `ために` in the gap: if the sentence survives, it is
the purpose one. Try `けど` instead: if *that* survives, it is the concessive one. No
sentence takes both, because the two `のに` want different verb forms in front of them
in the first place.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|机|つくえ}は{f|勉強|べんきょう}した**のに**{f|便利|べんり}です。 → ✅ この{f|机|つくえ}は{f|勉強|べんきょう}する**のに**{f|便利|べんり}です。
  Only the dictionary form goes in front of the purpose `のに`. The past tense turns it
  into the "although" one, and the sentence stops making sense.

- ❌ {f|大学|だいがく}に{f|入|はい}る**のに**、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|勉強|べんきょう}しています。 → ✅ {f|大学|だいがく}に{f|入|はい}る**ために**、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|勉強|べんきょう}しています。
  Studying every day is something you have decided to do, not an assessment of how
  hard university entrance is. Deliberate action takes `ために`.

- ❌ この{f|漢字|かんじ}は{f|覚|おぼ}える**のに**{f|難|むずか}しいです。 → ✅ この{f|漢字|かんじ}は{f|覚|おぼ}える**のが**{f|難|むずか}しいです。
  <code>{f|難|むずか}しい</code> describes the *act* of memorising, so it needs the
  nominaliser `の` as its subject. Keep `のに` only when the sentence is rating a tool,
  or counting time and money.

- ❌ {f|東京|とうきょう}まで{f|行|い}く**に**{f|三時間|さんじかん}かかります。 → ✅ {f|東京|とうきょう}まで{f|行|い}く**のに**{f|三時間|さんじかん}かかります。
  A verb cannot take `に` directly. The `の` is what turns it into something a particle
  can attach to.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|袋|ふくろ}は{f|野菜|やさい}を{f|入|い}れる____{f|使|つか}っています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|野菜|やさい}を{f|入|い}れる<b>のに</b>{f|使|つか}っています。

<code>{f|使|つか}う</code> is the classic partner of this pattern: the bag is being rated
against a job. The verb in front stays in the dictionary form no matter what tense the
sentence ends in.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|大学|だいがく}に{f|入|はい}る（のに・ために）、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|夜|よる}{f|遅|おそ}くまで{f|勉強|べんきょう}しています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|大学|だいがく}に{f|入|はい}る<b>ために</b>、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|夜|よる}{f|遅|おそ}くまで{f|勉強|べんきょう}しています。

The second half is an action you have chosen and are carrying out, which is `ために`
territory. `のに` would need an evaluation at the end - something like
<code>{f|時間|じかん}がかかります</code> or <code>お{f|金|かね}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "It took three hours to fix this computer."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

このパソコンを{f|直|なお}す<b>のに</b>{f|三時間|さんじかん}かかりました。

<code>かかる</code> is the other predicate this pattern lives with. Note the English
"to fix" becomes a dictionary form plus `のに`, and the past tense appears only on
<code>かかりました</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|地図|ちず}アプリは{f|道|みち}を{f|探|さが}したのに{f|便利|べんり}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|地図|ちず}アプリは{f|道|みち}を{f|探|さが}す<b>のに</b>{f|便利|べんり}です。

With the past tense in front, `のに` can only be read as "although", and "although I
looked for the road, it is convenient" is nonsense. The purpose `のに` takes the
dictionary form.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|書類|しょるい}を{f|作|つく}る**のに**{f|時間|じかん}がかかりました。
(b) {f|書類|しょるい}を{f|作|つく}る**のが**{f|大変|たいへん}でした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) measures the job against a cost: the paperwork was the purpose, and time was what
it consumed. The sentence is really about the hours.

(b) makes the act of writing the paperwork the subject and passes judgement on it - it
was hard going. Nothing is being measured; you are describing the experience.

The general rule behind the pair: `のに` for what something costs or is good for,
`のが` for what the activity itself is like.

</details>
