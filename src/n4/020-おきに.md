# ～おきに

**Meaning** · at intervals of - leaving a gap of X between one occurrence and the next

`おきに` describes something that repeats with a fixed **gap** in between. The word
to hold on to is *gap*, not *every*, because that single idea explains why
<code>{f|十分|じゅっぷん}おきに</code> means "every ten minutes" while
<code>{f|一日|いちにち}おきに</code> means "every **other** day". English translates them
differently; Japanese is saying the same thing both times.

## Formation

`おきに` is a suffix and needs a **number** in front of it. It attaches straight to
a quantity - of time, of distance, or of countable things.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Minutes, hours | Quantity + おきに | {f|十分|じゅっぷん} → {f|十分|じゅっぷん}おきに |
| Days, weeks, months, years | Quantity + おきに | {f|一日|いちにち} → {f|一日|いちにち}おきに |
| Distance | Quantity + おきに | {f|五|ご}メートル → {f|五|ご}メートルおきに |
| Countable things | Counter + おきに | {f|一|ひと}つ → {f|一|ひと}つおきに |
| People, seats, rows | Counter + おきに | {f|一人|ひとり}おきに、{f|一列|いちれつ}おきに |
| Before a noun | Quantity + おき + <b>の</b> | {f|一日|いちにち}おきの{f|練習|れんしゅう} |
| Vague quantity | {f|数|すう}～ + おきに | {f|数分|すうふん}おきに、{f|数|すう}メートルおきに |
| ❌ Bare unit | ❌{f|日|ひ}おきに | ✅{f|一日|いちにち}おきに |
| ❌ Before a noun with な | ❌{f|一日|いちにち}おきな | ✅{f|一日|いちにち}おきの |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `おきに` measures the **space between** two occurrences, never
the length of the cycle. <code>{f|一日|いちにち}おきに</code> leaves one whole day
empty: Monday, Wednesday, Friday.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - why do the English translations diverge? Because of what the unit is.
With days, weeks and years, and with seats and people, the unit is a **container**
the event sits inside, so a gap of one means one whole container left empty:
<code>{f|一|ひと}つおきに{f|座|すわ}る</code> is one seat used, one seat free. With
minutes, hours and metres, the event takes no measurable time or space of its own,
so a gap of ten minutes simply puts the events ten minutes apart. One definition,
two arithmetics.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`おきに` is neutral and faintly precise-sounding - it belongs to timetables,
instructions on a packet, and descriptions of routines. Nothing about it is formal
or casual, and it does not change shape in polite speech.

It does have one real-world problem, and you should know about it rather than be
caught by it: with days, weeks and years, **speakers themselves disagree** about
what `おきに` means. By the rule above, <code>{f|三日|みっか}おきに</code> leaves three
days empty and so happens once every four days - but a large minority of native
speakers use it to mean once every three days. Everyone agrees about
<code>{f|一日|いちにち}おき</code> ("every other day"); past that, confidence drops.

The practical consequence is that when it genuinely matters, Japanese avoids the
word. A pharmacist writes <code>{f|二日|ふつか}に{f|一回|いっかい}</code>, a timetable
writes <code>{f|十分|じゅっぷん}{f|間隔|かんかく}</code>, and a coach says
<code>{f|中|なか}{f|一日|いちにち}あけて</code>. If you are giving instructions someone
has to follow, say how many times per period and skip `おきに` entirely. If you are
merely describing a rhythm, `おきに` is exactly right.

Before a noun, use `の`: <code>{f|一日|いちにち}おきの{f|練習|れんしゅう}</code>,
<code>{f|十分|じゅっぷん}おきのアナウンス</code>. It is a noun-like word, not a
な-adjective.

## Contrast

### おきに vs ごとに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ごとに` names the **cycle**; `おきに` names the **gap**. So
<code>{f|三日|みっか}ごとに</code> is day 1, day 4, day 7 - and
<code>{f|三日|みっか}おきに</code> is day 1, day 5, day 9.

</div>

<pre>
{f|二日|ふつか}<b>ごとに</b>{f|水|みず}をやります。
I water them every two days.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一日|いちにち}<b>おきに</b>{f|水|みず}をやります。
I water them every other day.
</pre>

Those two sentences describe **the same schedule**, which is the neatest way to
feel the difference: a cycle of two days is a gap of one. Whenever you are unsure,
convert to `ごとに` in your head and count.

With small units of time the two land in the same place - 
<code>{f|十分|じゅっぷん}ごとに</code> and <code>{f|十分|じゅっぷん}おきに</code> both
describe buses ten minutes apart - which is why the distinction seems to vanish
and then reappear the moment you switch to days.

`ごとに` also does something `おきに` cannot: it attaches to ordinary nouns and to
verbs, meaning "each". <code>{f|人|ひと}ごとに{f|考|かんが}え{f|方|かた}が{f|違|ちが}う</code>
("everyone thinks differently"), <code>{f|会|あ}うごとに{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}くなる</code>
("he's taller every time I see him"). `おきに` needs a number and always will.

### おきに vs {f|毎|まい}～

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|毎|まい}</code> means **every single one, no gap**.
`おきに` exists precisely to describe the gaps that <code>{f|毎|まい}</code> denies.

</div>

<pre>
{f|毎週|まいしゅう}{f|日曜日|にちようび}にテニスをします。
I play tennis every Sunday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}<b>おきに</b>テニスをします。
I play tennis every other week.
</pre>

The mechanical difference is just as important: <code>{f|毎|まい}</code> is a prefix
on a bare unit - <code>{f|毎日|まいにち}</code>, <code>{f|毎週|まいしゅう}</code>,
<code>{f|毎月|まいつき}</code>, <code>{f|毎年|まいとし}</code>,
<code>{f|毎回|まいかい}</code> - and it will not take a number.
❌<code>{f|毎|まい}{f|三日|みっか}</code> does not exist; the Japanese for "every three
days" has to be <code>{f|三日|みっか}ごとに</code> or
<code>{f|二日|ふつか}おきに</code>.

### おきに vs ～に{f|一回|いっかい}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に{f|一回|いっかい}</code> counts occurrences inside a
period and is never ambiguous. When you need to be understood exactly, use it
instead of `おきに`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|一日|いちにち}<b>おきに</b>{f|走|はし}っています。
I go running every other day.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|二日|ふつか}<b>に{f|一回|いっかい}</b>{f|走|はし}っています。
I go running once every two days.
</pre>

Again the same schedule, said two ways. The second phrasing has no gap-versus-cycle
trap in it at all, which is why hospitals, schools and instruction manuals prefer
it. `おきに` is more compact and sounds more like a rhythm you live by; `に一回` is
what you write when someone will be counting.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|毎|まい}{f|三日|みっか}{f|掃除|そうじ}します。 → ✅ {f|三日|みっか}<b>ごとに</b>{f|掃除|そうじ}します。
  <code>{f|毎|まい}</code> cannot take a number. Use `ごとに` for a cycle, or
  <code>{f|二日|ふつか}おきに</code> for the same schedule expressed as a gap.

- ❌ {f|一日|いちにち}<b>おきに</b>{f|学校|がっこう}へ{f|行|い}っています。（meaning "every day"） → ✅ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|学校|がっこう}へ{f|行|い}っています。
  `おきに` always inserts a gap. If nothing is skipped, the word you want is
  <code>{f|毎日|まいにち}</code>.

- ❌ <b>おきに</b>{f|十分|じゅっぷん}{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|来|き}ます。 → ✅ {f|十分|じゅっぷん}<b>おきに</b>{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|来|き}ます。
  `おきに` is a suffix. The quantity always comes first.

- ❌ {f|一日|いちにち}おき<b>な</b>{f|練習|れんしゅう}はつらいです。 → ✅ {f|一日|いちにち}おき<b>の</b>{f|練習|れんしゅう}はつらいです。
  It behaves like a noun, so it links to the next noun with `の`, not `な`.

## Practice

**1.** {f|花|はな}に{f|水|みず}をやるのは____おきに、つまり{f|二日|ふつか}に{f|一回|いっかい}で{f|十分|じゅうぶん}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|花|はな}に{f|水|みず}をやるのは<b>{f|一日|いちにち}</b>おきに…

Once every two days means one whole day is skipped between waterings, and a gap of
one day is <code>{f|一日|いちにち}おきに</code>. The second half of the sentence is the
conversion rule in miniature: a gap of one equals a cycle of two.

</details>

**2.** バスは（{f|十分|じゅっぷん}おきに・{f|毎|まい}{f|十分|じゅっぷん}）{f|出|で}ています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

バスは<b>{f|十分|じゅっぷん}おきに</b>{f|出|で}ています。

<code>{f|毎|まい}</code> attaches only to a bare unit - <code>{f|毎日|まいにち}</code>,
<code>{f|毎朝|まいあさ}</code> - and never to a number, so
❌<code>{f|毎|まい}{f|十分|じゅっぷん}</code> is not a possible word.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Benches are placed every hundred metres along the river."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|川|かわ}に{f|沿|そ}って、{f|百|ひゃく}メートル<b>おきに</b>ベンチが{f|置|お}いてあります。

Distance is a measured quantity, so a gap of a hundred metres simply means the
benches are a hundred metres apart - no skipping involved. `おきに` attaches
straight to the quantity.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|毎|まい}{f|二週間|にしゅうかん}、{f|部長|ぶちょう}と{f|面談|めんだん}があります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}<b>おきに</b>、{f|部長|ぶちょう}と{f|面談|めんだん}があります。
（or {f|二週間|にしゅうかん}<b>ごとに</b>）

<code>{f|毎|まい}</code> refuses numbers. Expressed as a gap it is
<code>{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}おきに</code>; expressed as a cycle it is
<code>{f|二週間|にしゅうかん}ごとに</code>. Both describe the same fortnightly meeting.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|三日|みっか}おきに{f|通|かよ}っています。
(b) {f|三日|みっか}ごとに{f|通|かよ}っています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

By the rule, (a) leaves three days empty between visits, so you go once every four
days; (b) names a three-day cycle, so you go once every three days. They are not
the same schedule, and (a) is the sparser one.

In practice, be careful with (a). Speakers vary on how they count `おきに` with
days, and plenty of people would hear (a) as meaning (b). If the number has to be
right, say <code>{f|四日|よっか}に{f|一回|いっかい}</code> and remove the doubt.

</details>
