# ～以前

**Meaning** · before that point - and, at N1, before that even becomes the question: the matter fails at a more basic level than the one being discussed

{f|以前|いぜん} is the formal counterpart of {f|前|まえ}, and on its own it means simply
"earlier than". What makes it an N1 point is the second, argumentative use:
<code>A{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい}だ</code> refuses the topic A altogether, on the
grounds that something more elementary has already gone wrong.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (a time or event) | Noun + {f|以前|いぜん}（に） | {f|明治|めいじ} → {f|明治|めいじ}{f|以前|いぜん} |
| Verb | dictionary form + {f|以前|いぜん}に | {f|考|かんが}える → {f|考|かんが}える{f|以前|いぜん}に |
| Dismissing the topic | Noun + {f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい} | マナー → マナー{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい} |
| Dismissing, mid-sentence | Noun／Verb + {f|以前|いぜん}に | {f|好|す}き{f|嫌|きら}い → {f|好|す}き{f|嫌|きら}い{f|以前|いぜん}に |
| Standalone adverb | {f|以前|いぜん}、… | - → {f|以前|いぜん}、{f|京都|きょうと}に{f|住|す}んでいた |

<div class="note">

**Note** - {f|以前|いぜん} cannot count backwards from now.
"Three days ago" is {f|三日|みっか}{f|前|まえ}, never
❌{f|三日|みっか}{f|以前|いぜん}. With a number in front, {f|以前|いぜん} means "earlier
than that point", which is a different statement entirely.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
<b>{f|以前|いぜん}</b>、この{f|辺|あた}りに{f|大|おお}きな{f|市場|いちば}があった。
There used to be a big market around here.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明治|めいじ}<b>{f|以前|いぜん}</b>、この{f|村|むら}には{f|寺|てら}が{f|三|みっ}つあったという。
Before the Meiji period, they say, there were three temples in this village.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|入社|にゅうしゃ}<b>{f|以前|いぜん}に</b>{f|取|と}った{f|資格|しかく}も{f|評価|ひょうか}の{f|対象|たいしょう}になります。
Qualifications obtained before you joined the company also count towards your assessment.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|申込|もうしこみ}{f|締切|しめきり}<b>{f|以前|いぜん}に</b>{f|提出|ていしゅつ}された{f|書類|しょるい}のみ{f|受|う}け{f|付|つ}けます。
Only documents submitted on or before the application deadline will be accepted.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}を{f|考|かんが}える<b>{f|以前|いぜん}に</b>、この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|広|ひろ}さが{f|足|た}りない。
Never mind the price - the room simply isn't big enough.
</pre>

<pre>
デザインを{f|議論|ぎろん}する<b>{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい}</b>だ。そもそも{f|動|うご}かないのだから。
We're not even at the point of discussing the design. The thing doesn't run.
</pre>

<pre>

Forget manners for the moment - I'd like him to start by turning up on time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|好|す}き{f|嫌|きら}い<b>{f|以前|いぜん}に</b>、{f|彼|かれ}の{f|仕事|しごと}の{f|進|すす}め{f|方|かた}は{f|信用|しんよう}できない。
It isn't a question of whether I like him; I can't trust the way he works.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Plain {f|以前|いぜん} is **formal but entirely ordinary**. As a bare adverb at the head
of a sentence - <code>{f|以前|いぜん}、{f|大阪|おおさか}で{f|働|はたら}いていました</code>
- it means "previously, at some earlier time" and is safe in any polite conversation. It
is a shade more distant than <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> and much less specific than
<code>{f|去年|きょねん}</code>; the vagueness is the point, and it often signals that the
speaker does not intend to say exactly when.

In a document, {f|以前|いぜん} carries a boundary convention worth knowing. Like
{f|以上|いじょう} and {f|以下|いか}, it **includes** the point named:
<code>3{f|月|がつ}31{f|日|にち}{f|以前|いぜん}</code> covers the 31st itself, whereas
<code>3{f|月|がつ}31{f|日|にち}{f|前|まえ}</code> does not. Regulations, deadlines and
contracts rely on that difference. In everyday speech nobody counts on it, so if it
matters, say <code>31{f|日|にち}を{f|含|ふく}めて</code> and remove the doubt.

The argumentative use is a different animal. <code>～{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい}</code>
is **blunt**. It tells the listener that the thing they raised is premature and that
something more fundamental has already failed, and it is heard as a firm criticism -
often of a person, not just of a plan. Between colleagues it lands hard; aimed upward it
is close to insubordination. Said about a situation rather than a person
(<code>{f|安全|あんぜん}{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい}だ</code>) it is fair game and
very common in journalism and in meetings where something has gone badly wrong.

One homophone to keep straight. {f|依然|いぜん}, written with different characters, means
"still, unchanged", and appears almost always as
<code>{f|依然|いぜん}として</code>: <code>{f|原因|げんいん}は{f|依然|いぜん}として{f|不明|ふめい}だ</code>.
It is common in news writing, and because the two words sound identical, only the
characters or the surrounding grammar will tell you which you have.

## Contrast

### {f|以前|いぜん} vs {f|前|まえ}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|前|まえ}</code> is the everyday word and counts backwards
from a reference point. <code>{f|以前|いぜん}</code> is formal, includes the point named,
and cannot take a number of units.

</div>

<pre>
{f|寝|ね}る<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>{f|歯|は}を{f|磨|みが}きなさい。
Brush your teeth before you go to bed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|契約|けいやく}{f|締結|ていけつ}<b>{f|以前|いぜん}に</b>{f|発生|はっせい}した{f|費用|ひよう}は{f|対象外|たいしょうがい}となります。
Costs incurred prior to the conclusion of the contract fall outside the scope.
</pre>

The first sentence would sound absurd with {f|以前|いぜん} - a parent does not use
contract vocabulary at bedtime - and the second would lose its precision with
{f|前|まえ}. Where the sentence is about a routine, a habit or a short interval, use
{f|前|まえ}. Where it is about a formal cut-off, use {f|以前|いぜん}.

The number rule is the reliable one: {f|三日|みっか}{f|前|まえ} is "three days ago", and
❌{f|三日|みっか}{f|以前|いぜん} is not the same thing said politely - it is a different
and usually unintended claim.

### ～{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい} vs ～どころではない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both refuse the topic. <code>{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい}</code>
says it is **too advanced a question**; `どころではない` says circumstances leave **no
room** for it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りが{f|明日|あした}なので、{f|旅行|りょこう}の{f|話|はなし}をしている<b>どころではない</b>。
The deadline is tomorrow - this is no time to be talking about holidays.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|味|あじ}を{f|評価|ひょうか}する<b>{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい}</b>で、そもそも{f|注文|ちゅうもん}した{f|料理|りょうり}が{f|出|で}てこない。
We're a long way from judging the food; the dishes we ordered never arrived.
</pre>

The difference is where the obstacle sits. With `どころではない` the topic is a perfectly
good one and the *timing* is impossible - come back next week and the holiday
conversation can happen. With <code>{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい}</code> the topic is
still standing but something underneath it has collapsed, and no amount of waiting will
help until that is fixed.

### {f|以前|いぜん} vs {f|以来|いらい}・{f|以降|いこう}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - all three take a point in time and all three include it.
{f|以前|いぜん} faces **backwards**; {f|以降|いこう} faces **forwards**; {f|以来|いらい}
faces forwards **and reaches the present**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|震災|しんさい}<b>{f|以降|いこう}</b>、この{f|地区|ちく}の{f|建物|たてもの}は{f|基準|きじゅん}が{f|厳|きび}しくなった。
Since the earthquake, building standards in this district have been tightened.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|卒業|そつぎょう}<b>{f|以来|いらい}</b>、{f|彼|かれ}とは{f|一度|いちど}も{f|会|あ}っていない。
I haven't seen him once since we graduated.
</pre>

{f|以降|いこう} simply marks the start of a period and says nothing about whether it
continues; {f|以来|いらい} insists that it runs unbroken up to now, which is why it pairs
so naturally with a negative such as
<code>{f|会|あ}っていない</code>. {f|以前|いぜん} is the mirror of {f|以降|いこう}, not of
{f|以来|いらい} - there is no backward-facing form that reaches the present, because a
period ending at the reference point is simply over.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|三日|みっか}<b>{f|以前|いぜん}</b>に{f|電話|でんわ}しました。 → ✅ {f|三日|みっか}<b>{f|前|まえ}</b>に{f|電話|でんわ}しました。
  "Three days ago" is {f|前|まえ}. {f|以前|いぜん} cannot follow a duration; put a number
  in front of it and you have said "earlier than three days [from the reference point]",
  which is not what you meant.

- ❌ {f|食|た}べた<b>{f|以前|いぜん}に</b>{f|手|て}を{f|洗|あら}う。 → ✅ {f|食|た}べる<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>{f|手|て}を{f|洗|あら}う。
  The verb before {f|以前|いぜん} stays in the dictionary form, exactly as with
  {f|前|まえ}. And a routine like handwashing wants {f|前|まえ} anyway; {f|以前|いぜん}
  would make it sound like a clause in a food-hygiene regulation.

- ❌ <b>{f|依然|いぜん}</b>、この{f|会社|かいしゃ}で{f|働|はたら}いていました。 → ✅ <b>{f|以前|いぜん}</b>、この{f|会社|かいしゃ}で{f|働|はたら}いていました。
  Same sound, different word. {f|依然|いぜん} means "still, as before" and virtually always
  appears as <code>{f|依然|いぜん}として</code>.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、それは<b>{f|説明|せつめい}{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい}</b>です。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、その{f|前|まえ}に{f|確認|かくにん}したい{f|点|てん}がございます。
  Grammatically fine, and a serious social error. The phrase tells the listener their
  question was not worth asking; directed at a superior it is close to a rebuke.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|機械|きかい}は、{f|使|つか}いやすさを{f|議論|ぎろん}する____、{f|安全|あんぜん}{f|基準|きじゅん}を{f|満|み}たしていない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|議論|ぎろん}する<b>{f|以前|いぜん}に</b>（or <b>{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい}で</b>）

Usability is a reasonable thing to discuss, but the machine has failed a more basic
test, so the discussion is premature. The verb stays in the dictionary form
<code>{f|議論|ぎろん}する</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: レポートは{f|金曜日|きんようび}（{f|以前|いぜん}・{f|前|まえ}）に{f|提出|ていしゅつ}してください。ただし{f|金曜日|きんようび}{f|当日|とうじつ}も{f|受|う}け{f|付|つ}けます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|金曜日|きんようび}<b>{f|以前|いぜん}</b>に{f|提出|ていしゅつ}してください。

The second sentence says Friday itself is acceptable, and that is exactly the boundary
convention of {f|以前|いぜん}: it includes the day named.
<code>{f|金曜日|きんようび}{f|前|まえ}</code> would exclude Friday and contradict what
follows. In a real notice you would probably write
<code>{f|金曜日|きんようび}までに</code> and avoid the question.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Whether the design is good or bad isn't the issue - the site doesn't even load."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

デザインの{f|良|よ}し{f|悪|あ}し<b>{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい}</b>で、そもそもサイトが{f|表示|ひょうじ}されない。

<code>～{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい}</code> is the natural rendering of "isn't even
the issue", and <code>そもそも</code> is its usual companion - it introduces the more
basic failure. Keep in mind how blunt this is; it is a fair thing to say about a website
and a harsh thing to say about a person.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|始|はじ}まる{f|五分|ごふん}{f|以前|いぜん}に{f|部屋|へや}に{f|着|つ}きました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|始|はじ}まる{f|五分|ごふん}<b>{f|前|まえ}</b>に{f|部屋|へや}に{f|着|つ}きました。

A measured interval before an event is {f|前|まえ}. {f|以前|いぜん} cannot take a
duration in front of it. Note the reading {f|五分|ごふん} - the counter is ふん here,
where {f|一分|いっぷん}, {f|三分|さんぷん} and {f|十分|じゅっぷん} all take ぷん.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|今|いま}は{f|新|あたら}しい{f|企画|きかく}を{f|考|かんが}える<b>どころではない</b>。
(b) {f|新|あたら}しい{f|企画|きかく}を{f|考|かんが}える<b>{f|以前|いぜん}の{f|問題|もんだい}</b>だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the timing is wrong. Something urgent has taken over - a crisis, a deadline, a
staff shortage - and once it passes, thinking about new projects will be a perfectly
sensible thing to do. The complaint is about circumstances.

(b) says the question itself is out of order. Something more fundamental is broken -
the team, the budget, the last project - and no change in the schedule will make new
proposals appropriate until that is dealt with. (b) is by far the harder thing to hear,
because it does not blame the situation.

</details>
