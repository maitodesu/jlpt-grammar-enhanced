# ～ながらに / ～ながらの

**Meaning** · in that same unchanged state; just as it was - keeping the condition named while something else happens

This is not the everyday `ながら` of {f|歩|ある}きながら{f|話|はな}す. Here `ながら`
means "**staying in that state**": {f|涙|なみだ}ながらに is "with the tears still
running", {f|昔|むかし}ながらの is "the way it always was",
{f|生|う}まれながらに is "from birth and unchanged since". The pattern survives in a
small set of fixed expressions, and `に` or `の` chooses whether it modifies a verb
or a noun.

## Formation

The base is a **bare noun** or a **verb ます-stem**. `～ながらに` is adverbial and
comes before a verb; `～ながらの` is attributive and comes before a noun. That single
choice is what learners get wrong.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun, before a verb | Noun + ながらに | {f|涙|なみだ} → {f|涙|なみだ}ながらに{f|語|かた}る |
| Noun, before a noun | Noun + ながらの | {f|昔|むかし} → {f|昔|むかし}ながらの{f|味|あじ} |
| Verb (ます-stem), before a verb | stem + ながらに | {f|生|う}まれる → {f|生|う}まれながらに{f|持|も}つ |
| Verb (ます-stem), before a noun | stem + ながらの | {f|生|う}まれる → {f|生|う}まれながらの{f|才能|さいのう} |
| Emphatic | ～ながらにして | {f|居|い}る → {f|家|いえ}にいながらにして |
| Fixed adverbs | いつもながら / {f|我|われ}ながら | いつもながらの{f|手際|てぎわ} |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `に` before a **verb**, `の` before a **noun**. So
<code>{f|涙|なみだ}ながらに{f|訴|うった}える</code> but
<code>{f|涙|なみだ}ながらの{f|訴|うった}え</code>; ❌<code>{f|昔|むかし}ながらに
{f|製法|せいほう}</code> is impossible.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the productive list is tiny: {f|涙|なみだ}ながら, {f|昔|むかし}ながら,
{f|生|う}まれながら, いつもながら, {f|我|われ}ながら, and
<code>{f|居|い}ながらにして</code>. This is not a pattern to coin with. Note also
that `ながら` after a noun of *status* - {f|子|こ}どもながらに,
{f|素人|しろうと}ながら - is heard as the **concessive** "though only a child",
which is a different meaning; see the Contrast section.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|彼女|かのじょ}は<b>{f|涙|なみだ}ながらに</b>{f|当時|とうじ}のことを{f|語|かた}った。
In tears, she told us about that time.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}は<b>{f|昔|むかし}ながらの</b>{f|味|あじ}を{f|守|まも}っている。
This shop has kept the same taste it always had.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人|ひと}は<b>{f|生|う}まれながらに</b>{f|自由|じゆう}であり、{f|平等|びょうどう}である。
People are free and equal from birth.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|蔵|くら}では、{f|今|いま}も<b>{f|昔|むかし}ながらの</b>{f|製法|せいほう}で{f|醤油|しょうゆ}を{f|造|つく}っている。
At this brewery they still make soy sauce by the old methods.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>いつもながらの</b>{f|見事|みごと}な{f|手際|てぎわ}に、{f|今回|こんかい}も{f|感心|かんしん}させられた。
His usual deft handling of it impressed me all over again.
</pre>

<pre>

<pre>
{f|被害者|ひがいしゃ}の{f|母親|ははおや}は{f|記者|きしゃ}{f|会見|かいけん}で、<b>{f|涙|なみだ}ながらに</b>{f|再発|さいはつ}{f|防止|ぼうし}を{f|訴|うった}えた。

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}には<b>{f|生|う}まれながらの</b>{f|音楽|おんがく}の{f|才能|さいのう}があると、{f|指導|しどう}した{f|誰|だれ}もが{f|認|みと}めている。
Everyone who has taught him agrees that his musical gift was there from birth.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ながらに` and `ながらの` are **formulaic and literary**, and each of the surviving
expressions has a home register you should learn along with it.

<code>{f|涙|なみだ}ながらに</code> belongs to reporting. It is what a newspaper or a
television report says about someone speaking through tears at a press conference or
in court, and it carries a certain formality; between friends you would say
<code>{f|泣|な}きながら{f|話|はな}してた</code>.

<code>{f|昔|むかし}ながらの</code> is the language of **advertising and food
writing**: {f|昔|むかし}ながらの{f|中華|ちゅうか}そば,
{f|昔|むかし}ながらの{f|製法|せいほう}, {f|昔|むかし}ながらの{f|町並|まちな}み. It is
always warm - unchanged means authentic, not out of date - and you will see it on
shop signs and packaging constantly.

<code>{f|生|う}まれながら</code> is formal and slightly elevated, at home in writing
about rights, talent and temperament. <code>{f|居|い}ながらにして</code> is a set
phrase meaning "without having to go anywhere", now used mostly about what technology
lets you do from your desk. And <code>{f|我|われ}ながら</code> - "even I think so" -
is genuinely conversational: <code>{f|我|われ}ながらうまくできた</code>.

None of this is old-fashioned in a way that would sound strange; it is simply
frozen. Recognise the members, use the ones whose register you have a feel for, and
do not extend the pattern to new nouns.

## Contrast

### ～ながらに vs ～ながら (two things at once)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the ordinary `ながら` joins **two simultaneous actions** and
takes no `に`. The `ながら` of this page describes an **unchanging state** and takes
`に` or `の`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|音楽|おんがく}を<b>{f|聞|き}きながら</b>{f|宿題|しゅくだい}をしている。
He does his homework while listening to music.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼女|かのじょ}は<b>{f|生|う}まれながらに</b>{f|強|つよ}い{f|意志|いし}を{f|持|も}っていた。
She had a strong will in her from birth.
</pre>

Both attach to a ます-stem, which is why they get confused. The test is whether the
first element is an **action being performed at the same time**
({f|聞|き}きながら) or a **condition that simply holds**
({f|生|う}まれながら). Only the second takes `に`, and only the second can appear as
`の` before a noun.

### ～ながらに vs ～ながら(も) (although)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - after a word naming a **status or a quality**, `ながら` is read
as **"although"**. After {f|涙|なみだ}, {f|昔|むかし} or a stem like
{f|生|う}まれ, it is read as **"in that state"**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|狭|せま}い{f|部屋|へや}<b>ながらも</b>、{f|日|ひ}がよく{f|入|はい}って{f|気持|きも}ちがいい。
It's a cramped room, but the light is good and it feels pleasant.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|祖母|そぼ}は<b>{f|昔|むかし}ながらの</b>{f|梅干|うめぼ}しを{f|毎年|まいとし}{f|漬|つ}けている。
My grandmother pickles plums the old way every year.
</pre>

The concessive reading is signalled by `も` and by the base being an adjective or a
status noun; it usually stands at a clause boundary with a comma after it. Watch out
for the middle ground: <code>{f|子|こ}どもながらに{f|気|き}を{f|遣|つか}っていた</code>
means "young as I was, I was being careful", not "in an unchanged childlike state" -
the status noun pulls it towards "although".

### ～ながらの vs ～のまま

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>のまま</code> is **fully productive** and means "left as
it is". <code>ながらの</code> is a **frozen phrase** with the same sense, available
only for its handful of nouns.

</div>

<pre>
{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}して{f|三年|さんねん}{f|経|た}つが、{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|箱|はこ}に{f|入|はい}った<b>まま</b>だ。
Three years after moving, my things are still in their boxes.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}の{f|裏|うら}には<b>{f|昔|むかし}ながらの</b>{f|商店街|しょうてんがい}が{f|残|のこ}っている。
Behind the station there's an old-style shopping street still standing.
</pre>

<code>{f|昔|むかし}のまま</code> and <code>{f|昔|むかし}ながらの</code> are close, and
in many sentences either works. The difference is evaluative:
<code>のまま</code> is neutral and can be a complaint about something nobody has got
round to changing, while <code>{f|昔|むかし}ながらの</code> nearly always compliments
the thing for having been preserved. Note too that <code>のまま</code> can end a
sentence (<code>{f|昔|むかし}のままだ</code>) and `ながらの` cannot.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|涙|なみだ}ながら<b>の</b>{f|語|かた}った → ✅ {f|涙|なみだ}ながら<b>に</b>{f|語|かた}った
  `に` goes before a verb, `の` before a noun. The noun version would be
  <code>{f|涙|なみだ}ながらの{f|訴|うった}え</code>.

- ❌ {f|昔|むかし}ながら<b>に</b>{f|製法|せいほう} → ✅ {f|昔|むかし}ながら<b>の</b>{f|製法|せいほう}
  The same rule the other way round. {f|製法|せいほう} is a noun.

- ❌ コーヒーを{f|飲|の}み<b>ながらに</b>{f|新聞|しんぶん}を{f|読|よ}む。 → ✅ コーヒーを{f|飲|の}み<b>ながら</b>{f|新聞|しんぶん}を{f|読|よ}む。
  Two actions at the same time use the plain `ながら` with no `に`. The `に` belongs
  only to the fixed state expressions.

- ❌ この{f|町|まち}の{f|風景|ふうけい}は<b>{f|昔|むかし}ながらだ</b>。 → ✅ この{f|町|まち}の{f|風景|ふうけい}は<b>{f|昔|むかし}のままだ</b>。
  `ながらの` modifies a following noun; it does not work as a predicate. To end the
  sentence, use <code>のまま</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|裁判|さいばん}の{f|後|あと}、{f|遺族|いぞく}は____{f|心境|しんきょう}を{f|語|かた}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|涙|なみだ}ながらに</b>

The bereaved family spoke while in tears - the unchanged-state use - and because a
verb follows, the particle is `に`. This is also the exact register the phrase
belongs to: court and press reporting.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|旅館|りょかん}は（{f|昔|むかし}ながらに・{f|昔|むかし}ながらの）{f|木造|もくぞう}の{f|建物|たてもの}が{f|自慢|じまん}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|昔|むかし}ながらの</b>{f|木造|もくぞう}の{f|建物|たてもの}

{f|建物|たてもの} is a noun, so the attributive `の` is required. The phrase also does
its usual advertising work here - "unchanged" is the selling point.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Thanks to the internet, we can study at good universities abroad without leaving home."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

インターネットのおかげで、{f|家|いえ}に<b>いながらにして</b>{f|海外|かいがい}のいい{f|大学|だいがく}で{f|学|まな}べる。

<code>{f|居|い}ながらにして</code> is the set phrase for "without having to go
anywhere", and modern uses of it are almost all about what technology makes possible
from where you already are.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|母|はは}は{f|電話|でんわ}で{f|涙|なみだ}ながらの{f|事故|じこ}のことを{f|話|はな}してくれた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|母|はは}は{f|電話|でんわ}で{f|涙|なみだ}ながら<b>に</b>{f|事故|じこ}のことを{f|話|はな}してくれた。

The word being modified is the verb {f|話|はな}す, not {f|事故|じこ}, so the
adverbial `に` is correct. Written with `の`, the sentence claims there was such a
thing as a "tearful accident".

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|通|とお}りには<b>{f|昔|むかし}ながらの</b>{f|家|いえ}が{f|並|なら}んでいる。
(b) この{f|通|とお}りには<b>{f|昔|むかし}のままの</b>{f|家|いえ}が{f|並|なら}んでいる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both say the houses are unchanged, and either would be understood.

(a) is a compliment. <code>{f|昔|むかし}ながらの</code> frames the old style as
something valuable that has been kept, and it is the phrase a tourist leaflet would
use.

(b) is neutral and reports a fact. Depending on the sentence around it, it can even
carry a hint that nothing has been done to them - that they are simply as they were
left.

</details>
