# ～ほどのことはない

**Meaning** · it's not that big a deal; it doesn't rise to the level of doing that - the response would be out of proportion to the matter

ほど measures an extent, こと is the matter itself. Together they weigh one
against the other and find the matter **too small**: not worth the hospital trip,
not worth the meeting, not worth the fuss. Where までもない says the step would be
redundant, this pattern says the step would be **excessive**.

## Formation

The verb in front is in the **dictionary form**, and it names the response that
is out of proportion.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + ほどのことはない | {f|騒|さわ}ぐ → {f|騒|さわ}ぐほどのことはない |
| Verb (passive) | passive dictionary form + ほどのことはない | {f|褒|ほ}められる → {f|褒|ほ}められるほどのことはない |
| Noun | noun + というほどのことはない | けんか → けんかというほどのことはない |
| Variant | ～ほどのこともない | {f|心配|しんぱい}する → {f|心配|しんぱい}するほどのこともない |
| Past | ～ほどのことはなかった | {f|慌|あわ}てる → {f|慌|あわ}てるほどのことはなかった |
| Polite | ～ほどのことはありません | {f|報告|ほうこく}する → {f|報告|ほうこく}するほどのことはありません |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the は is fixed. ❌{f|騒|さわ}ぐほどのこと**が**ない is not the
pattern; if you hear が there, the speaker is saying something else - that no
such matter exists at all.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Register-neutral and genuinely usable.** Unlike most of the "no need"
expressions at this level, this one belongs to ordinary conversation as much as
to writing, and it is not stiff. The polite form
<code>ほどのことはありません</code> fits an office; the plain form fits a
kitchen table.

Its most graceful use is **about yourself**. Waved at your own injury, your own
trouble or your own kindness -
{f|感謝|かんしゃ}されるほどのことはしていません, "I did nothing worth
thanking me for" - it is modest and warm. Aimed at another person's distress it
becomes a judgement that they are overreacting, and it can wound. Same words,
opposite effect; the direction is everything.

Two variants are common and interchangeable in most contexts. The negative may
be <code>ではない</code> instead of <code>はない</code>, and the noun may take も:
{f|騒|さわ}ぐほどのことでもない. Adding も softens further, as も usually does.

With a noun in front, <code>というほどのことはない</code> denies the label rather
than the response: けんかというほどのことはない says what happened does not
deserve the name "fight" at all.

## Contrast

### ～ほどのことはない vs ～までもない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ほどのことはない</code> weighs the **matter** and finds
it small. <code>までもない</code> weighs the **step** and finds it redundant,
because the answer is already known.

</div>

<pre>
{f|微熱|びねつ}{f|程度|ていど}なので、{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|休|やす}む<b>ほどのことはない</b>。
It's barely a temperature - not enough to take the day off for.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}{f|同|おな}じ{f|質問|しつもん}に{f|答|こた}えたので、{f|説明|せつめい}する<b>までもない</b>。
I answered the same question yesterday, so there's nothing left to explain.
</pre>

Ask what the first clause is doing. If it establishes that the matter is
**minor**, use <code>ほどのことはない</code>. If it establishes that the outcome
is **already settled**, use <code>までもない</code>.

### ～ほどのことはない vs ～ことはない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ことはない` says the action is simply unnecessary.
<code>ほどのことはない</code> adds a scale: it would be **more than the case
deserves**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|急|いそ}ぐ<b>ことはない</b>よ。ゆっくりでいい。
There's no need to rush - take your time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|数分|すうふん}の{f|遅|おく}れなので、タクシーを{f|使|つか}う<b>ほどのことはない</b>。
We're only a few minutes behind; it hardly justifies a taxi.
</pre>

`ことはない` is often reassurance offered to a person.
<code>ほどのことはない</code> is a proportion being calculated, which is why a
scale word - {f|数分|すうふん}, {f|少|すこ}し, かすり{f|傷|きず} - so often sits
in the same sentence.

### ～ほどのことはない vs ～ほど…ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ほどのことはない</code> is one fixed expression about
proportion. `ほど…ない` is the comparison you learned earlier: **A is not as ～ as
B**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今年|ことし}の{f|冬|ふゆ}は{f|去年|きょねん}<b>ほど{f|寒|さむ}くない</b>。
This winter is not as cold as last year's.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|寒|さむ}いが、コートを{f|買|か}い{f|足|た}す<b>ほどのことはない</b>。
It's cold, but not so cold as to need another coat.
</pre>

In the first, ほど has a noun in front of it and the sentence compares two
winters. In the second, ほど follows a verb and is followed by のことはない, and
nothing is being compared - the sentence is deciding whether an action is
warranted.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|騒|さわ}ぐほどのこと<b>が</b>ない → ✅ {f|騒|さわ}ぐほどのこと<b>はない</b>
  The は is part of the expression. With が the sentence stops being about
  proportion.

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}は<b>{f|難|むずか}しいほどのことはない</b>。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は<b>{f|悩|なや}むほどのことはない</b>。
  The slot takes a verb naming the response, not an adjective describing the
  thing.

- ❌ {f|報告|ほうこく}する<b>ほどのことはないだ</b>。 → ✅ {f|報告|ほうこく}する<b>ほどのことはない</b>。
  ない is the predicate already; だ cannot follow it. The polite form is
  ありません.

- ❌ (to a grieving friend) そんなに{f|落|お}ち{f|込|こ}む<b>ほどのことはない</b>よ。 → ✅ {f|無理|むり}に{f|元気|げんき}を{f|出|だ}さなくていいよ。
  Correct grammar, badly aimed. Telling someone their grief is out of proportion
  is not comfort; keep this pattern for your own troubles.

## Practice

**1.** ボタンが{f|一|ひと}つ{f|取|と}れただけなので、{f|新|あたら}しいシャツを{f|買|か}う____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|買|か}う<b>ほどのことはない</b>

One missing button is being weighed against buying a whole shirt, and the
response is out of proportion - the core use of the pattern. {f|買|か}う stays in
the dictionary form.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|同|おな}じ{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|朝礼|ちょうれい}でしたばかりだから、もう{f|一度|いちど}{f|伝|つた}える（ほどのことはない・までもない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

もう{f|一度|いちど}{f|伝|つた}える<b>までもない</b>。

Everyone has already heard the explanation, so repeating it would add nothing -
that is redundancy, not disproportion. <code>ほどのことはない</code> would claim
the information is too trivial to pass on, which is not what the first clause
says.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "It's only a scratch - nothing worth going to the doctor about."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

かすり{f|傷|きず}だから、{f|医者|いしゃ}に{f|行|い}く<b>ほどのことはない</b>。

The English weighs a small injury against a disproportionate response, which is
exactly this pattern; ほどのことでもない would work equally well and sound a
shade softer.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|少|すこ}し{f|意見|いけん}が{f|合|あ}わなかっただけで、けんかほどのことはなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|少|すこ}し{f|意見|いけん}が{f|合|あ}わなかっただけで、けんか<b>というほどのことはなかった</b>。
"We just disagreed a little; it was nothing you could call a fight."

A noun needs という before ほどのことはない. Without it, けんか sits directly on
ほど and the sentence reads as a comparison with a fight rather than a denial of
the label.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|私|わたし}のしたことは、{f|感謝|かんしゃ}される<b>ほどのことはありません</b>。
(b) それくらいで{f|感謝|かんしゃ}される<b>ほどのことはない</b>でしょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is modesty about your own action, and it is one of the politest ways to
decline thanks: what I did was not on the scale that deserves this.

(b) turns the same measurement on someone else and finds **them** wanting - the
speaker is saying the other person is being thanked for too little. The pattern
is neutral; the choice of whose deed is being weighed decides whether it sounds
gracious or grudging.

</details>
