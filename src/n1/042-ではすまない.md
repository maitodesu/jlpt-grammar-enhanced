# ～ではすまない

**Meaning** · it will not end there; just that is not going to settle it - read <code>では{f|済|す}まない</code>

The verb is {f|済|す}む, "to be settled, to be got through". Its affirmative
<code>～で{f|済|す}む</code> is a small everyday pleasure -
{f|千円|せんえん}で{f|済|す}んだ, "it only came to a thousand yen". Negate it and you get a warning:
the thing named in front of では is not enough, and something heavier is on its way.
The heavier thing is usually left unsaid, which is most of the force.

## Formation

だけ is present or implied almost every time, because the whole job of the pattern
is to say "just that is not enough".

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + では{f|済|す}まない | {f|口頭|こうとう}の{f|注意|ちゅうい}では{f|済|す}まない |
| Noun + だけ (the usual shape) | ～だけでは{f|済|す}まない | {f|謝罪|しゃざい}だけでは{f|済|す}まない |
| Verb / い-adjective clause | plain form + のでは{f|済|す}まない | {f|知|し}らなかったのでは{f|済|す}まない |
| な-adjective | ～なのでは{f|済|す}まない | {f|不注意|ふちゅうい}なのでは{f|済|す}まない |
| Casual contraction | ～じゃ{f|済|す}まない | {f|冗談|じょうだん}じゃ{f|済|す}まない |
| Polite | ～では{f|済|す}みません | {f|注意|ちゅうい}では{f|済|す}みません |
| Heavier, passive | ～では{f|済|す}まされない | {f|知|し}らなかったでは{f|済|す}まされない |
| Set phrase | ただでは{f|済|す}まない | {f|今回|こんかい}はただでは{f|済|す}まない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the は is not optional. The affirmative is
<code>これで{f|済|す}む</code> with plain で; the negative takes the contrastive
は, <code>これでは{f|済|す}まない</code>. ❌これでは{f|済|す}む is not idiomatic.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|謝|あやま}るだけ<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>よ。
Just apologising isn't going to be the end of it.
</pre>

<pre>
これは{f|冗談|じょうだん}<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>。
This isn't something you can pass off as a joke.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|皿|さら}を{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|割|わ}ったのだから、「ごめん」<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>だろう。
You broke every one of the plates, so "sorry" is hardly going to cover it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|期限|きげん}を{f|過|す}ぎれば、{f|注意|ちゅうい}<b>では{f|済|す}みません</b>。{f|罰金|ばっきん}が{f|科|か}されます。
Past the deadline it doesn't stop at a warning - there is a fine.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|個人|こじん}{f|情報|じょうほう}が{f|流出|りゅうしゅつ}した{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|社内|しゃない}の{f|処分|しょぶん}だけ<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>。
Now that personal data has leaked, internal discipline is not going to be the end of it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|相手|あいて}は{f|本気|ほんき}で{f|怒|おこ}っている。{f|今回|こんかい}はただ<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>と{f|思|おも}ったほうがいい。
He is genuinely angry; you had better assume you won't get off lightly this time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|規則|きそく}を{f|知|し}らなかった<b>では{f|済|す}まされない</b>{f|立場|たちば}にある。
He is in a position where "I didn't know the rules" simply will not be accepted.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一度|いちど}{f|信用|しんよう}を{f|失|うしな}えば、{f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}を{f|替|か}えるだけ<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>。
Once trust has gone, swapping the account manager is not going to fix it.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The form is a **warning or a verdict**, and its natural settings are a telling-off,
a news commentary, a formal complaint and a legal argument. The consequence it
threatens is deliberately left vague or pushed into the following sentence; that
vagueness is why <code>ただでは{f|済|す}まない</code> reads as close to a threat.

<code>じゃ{f|済|す}まない</code> is the ordinary spoken contraction and is common
among adults. It is not childish, but it is not polite either - it is what you say
when you are annoyed. In writing, both {f|済|す}まない and the kana すまない are
standard; print tends to prefer the kanji.

Two things learners should keep straight. First, the apology すみません comes from
the same verb - "this is not settled on my side yet" - but
<code>では{f|済|す}まない</code> is never itself an apology. Saying
{f|遅刻|ちこく}では{f|済|す}みません to a client means "being late will not be the
end of this", which is not what you were reaching for. Second, the pattern says the
named thing is **insufficient**, not that it is **forbidden**; the action in front
of では is usually one that ought to happen, just not on its own.

## Contrast

### ～では{f|済|す}まない vs ～で{f|済|す}む

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>で{f|済|す}む</code> is relief: it turned out to be
**only** that much. <code>では{f|済|す}まない</code> is the warning that it will
**not** stop there.

</div>

<pre>
{f|修理|しゅうり}は{f|五千円|ごせんえん}<b>で{f|済|す}んだ</b>。
The repair only came to five thousand yen.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|傷|きず}だと、{f|五千円|ごせんえん}<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>だろう。
With damage like this it won't stop at five thousand.
</pre>

Learning the affirmative first makes the negative obvious. Notice again that the
affirmative uses plain で and the negative adds は.

### ～では{f|済|す}まない vs ～てはいけない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>てはいけない</code> says the action is **not allowed**.
<code>では{f|済|す}まない</code> says the action is fine, but **not enough**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|黙|だま}っていて<b>はいけない</b>。
You mustn't keep quiet about it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|黙|だま}って{f|頭|あたま}を{f|下|さ}げるだけ<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>。
Bowing your head in silence is not going to settle this.
</pre>

The first forbids; the second accepts the bow and demands more on top of it. If your
English is "you can't just…", the Japanese you want is
では{f|済|す}まない, not てはいけない.

### ～では{f|済|す}まない vs ～では{f|済|す}まされない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the passive raises it from "it will not end there" to **"the
world will not let it end there"**. Same event, more social pressure and a heavier
register.

</div>

<pre>
うっかりしていた<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>。
"I wasn't paying attention" is not going to be the end of it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|安全|あんぜん}を{f|預|あず}かる{f|立場|たちば}で、うっかりしていた<b>では{f|済|す}まされない</b>。
For someone responsible for safety, "I wasn't paying attention" cannot be allowed to stand.
</pre>

{f|済|す}まされない is the form editorials and courtrooms use, and it appears
especially after quoted excuses - {f|知|し}らなかった, {f|忘|わす}れていた,
{f|聞|き}いていなかった.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|謝|あやま}る<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b> → ✅ {f|謝|あやま}るだけ<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>（or {f|謝|あやま}ったの<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>）
  A plain verb cannot sit directly in front of では. Insert だけ, or nominalise the
  clause with の.

- ❌ この{f|金額|きんがく}<b>では{f|済|す}む</b> → ✅ この{f|金額|きんがく}<b>で{f|済|す}む</b>
  The は belongs to the negative. Affirmative {f|済|す}む takes bare で.

- ❌ {f|遅|おく}れて{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ない、{f|本当|ほんとう}に<b>では{f|済|す}みません</b> → ✅ {f|遅|おく}れて{f|本当|ほんとう}に<b>すみません</b>
  The apology is すみません on its own. <code>では{f|済|す}みません</code> is a
  warning aimed at someone else.

- ❌ {f|寄付|きふ}だけ<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>ので、{f|喜|よろこ}んで{f|参加|さんか}した → ✅ {f|寄付|きふ}だけ<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>ので、{f|会場|かいじょう}の{f|準備|じゅんび}まで{f|頼|たの}まれた
  The pattern always looks toward a consequence the speaker would rather avoid.
  Pairing it with a happy result contradicts the form.

## Practice

**1.** {f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|車|くるま}をぶつけたのだから、{f|口頭|こうとう}の{f|注意|ちゅうい}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|口頭|こうとう}の{f|注意|ちゅうい}<b>では{f|済|す}まないだろう</b>

A verbal warning is the light option, and the sentence says it will not be the end
of the matter - a written report or a deduction is coming. だろう is natural here
because the speaker is predicting the consequence rather than announcing it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|事故|じこ}の{f|原因|げんいん}を{f|知|し}っていながら{f|黙|だま}っていたのなら、「{f|忘|わす}れていた」（では{f|済|す}まされない・で{f|済|す}む）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

「{f|忘|わす}れていた」<b>では{f|済|す}まされない</b>。

The excuse is being rejected on behalf of everyone affected, not just by the
speaker, and the quoted excuse in front of it is the classic environment for the
passive form.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If the same fault happens a third time, a refund alone won't settle it."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|同|おな}じ{f|不具合|ふぐあい}が{f|三度|さんど}{f|起|お}きれば、{f|返金|へんきん}だけ<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>。

"Alone won't settle it" is だけでは{f|済|す}まない almost word for word. Note the
noun {f|返金|へんきん} before だけ - a verb would need の.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|規則|きそく}を{f|破|やぶ}ったのだから、{f|注意|ちゅうい}するでは{f|済|す}まない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|規則|きそく}を{f|破|やぶ}ったのだから、{f|注意|ちゅうい}<b>だけでは{f|済|す}まない</b>。

{f|注意|ちゅうい}する is a verb, and a plain verb cannot sit in front of では.
Dropping する turns it into the noun {f|注意|ちゅうい}, and だけ supplies the "just"
that the pattern needs.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|今回|こんかい}の{f|件|けん}は、{f|部長|ぶちょう}への{f|報告|ほうこく}<b>で{f|済|す}んだ</b>。
(b) {f|今回|こんかい}の{f|件|けん}は、{f|部長|ぶちょう}への{f|報告|ほうこく}<b>では{f|済|す}まない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is relief. Telling the section head turned out to be the whole of it - no
customer letter, no formal report, nothing further to do.

(b) is a warning. The report to the section head has been made or will be made, but
something bigger is coming: a written apology, a client visit, a disciplinary
process. The sentence deliberately leaves the listener to imagine what.

</details>
