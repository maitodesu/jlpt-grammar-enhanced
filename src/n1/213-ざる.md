# ～ざる

**Meaning** · not; un- - the classical negative <code>ず</code> in its noun-modifying shape, standing where modern Japanese would put `～ない`

<code>ざる</code> is the {f|連体形|れんたいけい} - the noun-modifying form - of the
Old Japanese negative auxiliary `ず`. It is not a living piece of
modern grammar. It survives in a small stock of frozen phrases -
<code>{f|知|し}られざる</code>, <code>{f|意図|いと}せざる</code>,
<code>{f|絶|た}えざる</code>, やむを{f|得|え}ざる - and in the deliberately weighty
register of documentary titles, editorials and formal notices. Recognise it and
read it as `ない`; write `ない` yourself.

## Formation

The base is the ない-stem (the classical {f|未然形|みぜんけい}), the same base `ない`
attaches to. Then `ざる`, then a noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ない-stem + ざる + noun | {f|望|のぞ}まない → {f|望|のぞ}まざる{f|結果|けっか} |
| Verb (る-verb) | ない-stem + ざる + noun | {f|絶|た}えない → {f|絶|た}えざる{f|努力|どりょく} |
| Verb (passive) | ～られない → ～られざる | {f|知|し}られない → {f|知|し}られざる{f|一面|いちめん} |
| Verb (する) | <b>せざる</b> | {f|意図|いと}しない → {f|意図|いと}せざる{f|結果|けっか} |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | <b>{f|来|こ}ざる</b> (very rare) | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ざる |
| Verb (ある) | あらざる (rare) | ある → あらざる |
| い-adjective | ～から + ざる | {f|少|すく}ない → {f|少|すく}なからざる{f|影響|えいきょう} |
| Adverbial form of the same auxiliary | ～ず | {f|少|すく}なからず、{f|思|おも}わず |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the auxiliary `ず` has **two** noun-modifying forms, and both are still
around. `ぬ` is the older one and is the commoner survivor:
<code>{f|予期|よき}せぬ{f|事態|じたい}</code>,
<code>{f|見知|みし}らぬ{f|人|ひと}</code>,
<code>{f|思|おも}わぬ{f|結果|けっか}</code>. `ざる` is the heavier, more bookish of
the pair. Note that `する` splits: せぬ and せざる - never ❌しざる, and never anything built on し.

</div>

<pre>

See no evil, hear no evil, speak no evil.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|知|し}られ<b>ざる</b>{f|一面|いちめん}が、そこにあった。
There was a side of it that nobody had ever known.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|絶|た}え<b>ざる</b>{f|努力|どりょく}が、この{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}を{f|支|ささ}えてきた。
Unceasing effort is what has sustained this technology.
</pre>

<pre>
やむを{f|得|え}<b>ざる</b>{f|事情|じじょう}により、{f|本日|ほんじつ}は{f|休業|きゅうぎょう}いたします。
Owing to circumstances beyond our control, we are closed today.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|規制|きせい}の{f|緩和|かんわ}は、{f|意図|いと}せ<b>ざる</b>{f|結果|けっか}を{f|招|まね}くおそれがある。
Deregulation carries the risk of producing unintended consequences.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工場|こうじょう}の{f|閉鎖|へいさ}は、{f|地域|ちいき}{f|経済|けいざい}に{f|少|すく}なから<b>ざる</b>{f|影響|えいきょう}を{f|与|あた}えた。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|遠|とお}から<b>ざる</b>{f|将来|しょうらい}、この{f|方式|ほうしき}が{f|標準|ひょうじゅん}になるだろう。
In the not-too-distant future this method will surely become the standard.
</pre>

<pre>

His name became public in a way he had never wanted, and he stopped appearing in public.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

**You will never say this.** <code>ざる</code> is classical Japanese embedded in
modern text. It appears in documentary and book titles
(<code>{f|知|し}られざる～</code>), in shop notices and official announcements
(やむを{f|得|え}ざる{f|事情|じじょう}), in editorials and academic writing
({f|意図|いと}せざる{f|結果|けっか}), and in proverbs. A learner who says
<code>{f|知|し}らざる{f|人|ひと}に{f|会|あ}った</code> to a friend has produced a
sentence that is not wrong so much as *costumed*. The word you want is `ない`:
{f|知|し}らない{f|人|ひと}.

Two things follow from `ざる` being a {f|連体形|れんたいけい}. First, it **must** be followed by a
noun. It is not a way of ending a sentence: ❌<code>{f|彼|かれ}は{f|知|し}らざる。</code>
is broken Japanese, modern or classical. Second, because it modifies a noun, what
you meet in practice is a **compound**: the phrase
<code>{f|知|し}られざる{f|歴史|れきし}</code> behaves like a single lexical item
("the untold history"), not like a clause you assembled.

The one exception to "never produce this" is the handful of set phrases that have
been absorbed into ordinary formal Japanese. やむを{f|得|え}ざる{f|事情|じじょう}
is normal on a shop shutter; {f|意図|いと}せざる{f|結果|けっか} is normal in a
report; <code>{f|少|すく}なからざる</code> and <code>{f|遠|とお}からざる</code>
appear in newspaper prose without anyone blinking. Learn those as vocabulary
rather than as a pattern you may extend.

The same auxiliary is behind the productive N2 pattern
<code>～ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> ("have no choice but to"), which *is* worth
producing in formal writing and speech. That expression is frozen; the free
noun-modifying `ざる` on this page is not.

## Contrast

### ～ざる vs ～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - they mean the same thing. `ない` is what you write and say;
<code>ざる</code> is what you decode when a title or a notice reaches for weight.

</div>

<pre>
まだ{f|知|し}られていない{f|事実|じじつ}が{f|出|で}てきた。
Facts that had not been known came to light.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|番組|ばんぐみ}の{f|題名|だいめい}は「{f|知|し}られ<b>ざる</b>{f|深海|しんかい}」だった。
The programme was called "The Unknown Deep".
</pre>

The first is how a person reports something. The second is how an editor names a
thing. If you are choosing between them for your own sentence, the answer is always
the first - unless you are writing a title, in which case `ざる` buys you brevity
and gravity in four syllables.

### ～ざる vs ～ぬ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are the noun-modifying negative. `ぬ` is shorter, older
and much more common in surviving vocabulary; `ざる` is heavier and more obviously
literary.

</div>

<pre>
{f|思|おも}わ<b>ぬ</b>ところで{f|昔|むかし}の{f|友人|ゆうじん}に{f|会|あ}った。
I ran into an old friend somewhere I never expected to.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|予期|よき}せ<b>ぬ</b>{f|事態|じたい}に、{f|現場|げんば}は{f|混乱|こんらん}した。
An unforeseen situation threw the site into confusion.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|許|ゆる}され<b>ざる</b>{f|行為|こうい}として、{f|厳|きび}しく{f|処分|しょぶん}された。
It was treated as an unforgivable act and punished severely.
</pre>

There is no rule for choosing; the phrases are fixed and you learn which one each
takes. <code>{f|思|おも}わぬ</code>, <code>{f|見知|みし}らぬ</code>,
<code>{f|予期|よき}せぬ</code> take `ぬ`. <code>{f|知|し}られざる</code>,
<code>{f|許|ゆる}されざる</code>, <code>{f|絶|た}えざる</code> take `ざる`. Swapping
them does not produce a grammatical error so much as a phrase nobody uses.

### ～ざる vs ～べからざる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ざる</code> alone is a plain negative: "not X-ed".
<code>べからざる</code> adds prohibition: "must not be X-ed, may not be X-ed".

</div>

<pre>
{f|許|ゆる}され<b>ざる</b>{f|行為|こうい}。
An act that was not forgiven / is unforgivable.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|許|ゆる}す<b>べからざる</b>{f|行為|こうい}。
An act that must not be tolerated.
</pre>

The difference is the same one that separates {f|許|ゆる}されない from
{f|許|ゆる}してはいけない. Note also the base each takes: <code>ざる</code> follows
the ない-stem ({f|許|ゆる}され<b>ざる</b>), while <code>べからざる</code> follows the
dictionary form ({f|許|ゆる}す<b>べからざる</b>). Getting those the wrong way round is
the standard slip.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|意図|いと}<b>しざる</b>{f|結果|けっか} → ✅ {f|意図|いと}<b>せざる</b>{f|結果|けっか}
  `する` takes the older negative stem `せ` in front of every classical ending.
  There is no ❌しざる and no ❌しぬ for する.

- ❌ {f|望|のぞ}む<b>ざる</b>{f|結果|けっか} → ✅ {f|望|のぞ}ま<b>ざる</b>{f|結果|けっか}
  The base is the ない-stem, not the dictionary form. Test it by producing the
  `ない` form first: {f|望|のぞ}まない → {f|望|のぞ}まざる.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}はその{f|事実|じじつ}を{f|知|し}ら<b>ざる</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}はその{f|事実|じじつ}を{f|知|し}ら<b>ない</b>。
  <code>ざる</code> modifies a noun and cannot close a sentence. If nothing follows
  it, you have written a fragment.

- ❌（to a friend）{f|知|し}ら<b>ざる</b>{f|人|ひと}に{f|道|みち}を{f|聞|き}いた。 → ✅ {f|知|し}ら<b>ない</b>{f|人|ひと}に{f|道|みち}を{f|聞|き}いた。
  Grammatically assembled, socially impossible. In conversation this is costume
  drama, and it is also not one of the frozen phrases, so it sounds invented as
  well as archaic.

- ❌ {f|少|すく}ない<b>ざる</b>{f|影響|えいきょう} → ✅ {f|少|すく}なから<b>ざる</b>{f|影響|えいきょう}
  い-adjectives reach `ざる` through the classical `～から` base, exactly as they
  reach `ず` in {f|少|すく}なからず.

## Practice

**1.** {f|店|みせ}のシャッターに{f|貼|は}られた{f|紙|かみ}: 「やむを{f|得|え}____{f|事情|じじょう}により、しばらく{f|休業|きゅうぎょう}いたします」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

やむを{f|得|え}<b>ざる</b>{f|事情|じじょう}

This is one of the frozen phrases: "for unavoidable reasons". The base is the
ない-stem of {f|得|え}る, which is え, so え + ざる. In ordinary speech you would say
{f|仕方|しかた}がない{f|事情|じじょう}, but nobody writes that on a shutter.

</details>

**2.** Where would you expect to meet this phrase, and what does it mean?
{f|知|し}られ<b>ざる</b>{f|素顔|すがお}

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

"The unknown face of X" - a television documentary title, a magazine feature, or
the subtitle of a biography. It is headline register: the whole point of
<code>ざる</code> here is that it is two syllables shorter and considerably more
solemn than {f|知|し}られていない. You would never say it about a colleague at
lunch.

</details>

**3.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|語|かた}られ<b>ざる</b>{f|部分|ぶぶん}がまだ{f|多|おお}い。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|語|かた}られていない{f|部分|ぶぶん}がまだ{f|多|おお}い。
"There is still a lot that has not been told."

Replace the classical {f|連体形|れんたいけい} with the ordinary negative. Because
<code>{f|語|かた}られざる</code> describes a state that continues, the natural
modern form is ていない rather than a bare ない.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|想像|そうぞう}しざる{f|影響|えいきょう}を{f|与|あた}えた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|予想|よそう}せ<b>ざる</b>{f|影響|えいきょう}を{f|与|あた}えた。
Better still in modern Japanese: この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|予想|よそう}できないほどの{f|影響|えいきょう}を{f|与|あた}えた。

Two things are wrong. ❌しざる is impossible - する-verbs take せ before classical
endings. And <code>{f|想像|そうぞう}せざる</code> is not a phrase anyone uses; the
fixed collocations are <code>{f|意図|いと}せざる</code> and
<code>{f|予期|よき}せぬ</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|絶|た}え<b>ざる</b>{f|努力|どりょく}によって{f|完成|かんせい}した。
(b) {f|努力|どりょく}を{f|続|つづ}けて{f|完成|かんせい}させた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a written formula. <code>{f|絶|た}えざる{f|努力|どりょく}</code> is a fixed
phrase of the kind that turns up in a commemorative address, a company history or a
museum caption. It presents the effort as an abstraction to be admired, and no
particular person is doing it.

(b) is what a person says. It names an agent, it can be spoken to anyone, and it
carries no ceremonial weight. If you are describing your own project to a colleague,
(b) is the only one of the two available to you.

</details>
