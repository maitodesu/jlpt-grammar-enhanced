# ～に限ったことではない

**Meaning** · it is not confined to this case - the thing being discussed happens elsewhere too, or has happened before

<code>{f|限|かぎ}る</code> is "to limit", and the past-attributive
<code>{f|限|かぎ}ったこと</code> is "a matter limited to". Negate it and you get a
move a writer makes constantly: someone has just pointed at one country, one company
or one occasion, and the sentence **widens the frame**. With a time word in front of
it - <code>{f|今|いま}に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</code> - it widens backwards
instead, meaning "this is nothing new".

## Formation

The base is a noun, and the pattern is a **predicate** that closes the clause.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない | {f|日本|にほん} → {f|日本|にほん}に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない |
| Time noun | {f|今|いま}・{f|今回|こんかい} + に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない | {f|今|いま} → {f|今|いま}に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない |
| Variant | Noun + に{f|限|かぎ}った{f|話|はなし}ではない | {f|若者|わかもの} → {f|若者|わかもの}に{f|限|かぎ}った{f|話|はなし}ではない |
| Standard frame | ～のは + Noun + に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない | {f|遅|おく}れるのは{f|今|いま}に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない |
| Polite | ～に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではありません | {f|当社|とうしゃ}に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではありません |
| Continuing | ～に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではなく、… | {f|今回|こんかい}に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではなく、… |

<div class="note">

**Note** - a verb cannot attach directly.
❌<code>{f|遅|おく}れるに{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</code> has to be rebuilt with a
`のは` clause: <code>{f|遅|おく}れるのは{f|今|いま}に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
これは{f|日本|にほん}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。
This is not something confined to Japan.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}が{f|遅刻|ちこく}するのは、{f|今|いま}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。
It's hardly the first time he has turned up late.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人手|ひとで}{f|不足|ぶそく}は、{f|飲食|いんしょく}{f|業界|ぎょうかい}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではありません</b>。
The labour shortage is not confined to the restaurant trade.
</pre>

<pre>
スマートフォンに{f|頼|たよ}りすぎるのは、{f|若者|わかもの}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}った{f|話|はなし}ではない</b>。
Leaning on a smartphone too heavily is not a young people's problem.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値上|ねあ}げに{f|苦|くる}しんでいるのは、{f|小|ちい}さな{f|店|みせ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。
It is not only small shops that are struggling with the price rises.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|地方|ちほう}の{f|路線|ろせん}が{f|廃止|はいし}されていくのは、この{f|県|けん}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。
Rural lines being closed is not something happening only in this prefecture.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|準備|じゅんび}が{f|前日|ぜんじつ}になるのは{f|今回|こんかい}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではなく</b>、そろそろ{f|進|すす}め{f|方|かた}を{f|見直|みなお}すべきだ。
Leaving the preparation to the day before is not a one-off, and it is about time we reviewed how we work.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|気候|きこう}{f|変動|へんどう}の{f|影響|えいきょう}が{f|農業|のうぎょう}に{f|及|およ}んでいるのは{f|一部|いちぶ}の{f|地域|ちいき}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>と{f|報告|ほうこく}されている。
It is reported that the effects of climate change on farming are not restricted to a few regions.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is **neutral to formal**, and it is genuinely common. Newspaper commentary,
opinion columns, meeting minutes and reports use it constantly, and so do adults in
conversation; there is nothing bookish about it, and
<code>ことではありません</code> makes it as polite as you need.

It has two distinct rhetorical jobs, and the noun in front tells you which.

With a **category noun** - a country, an industry, an age group - it is *widening*.
The speaker is pushing back against an argument that has been drawn too narrowly:
<code>{f|日本|にほん}に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</code> answers someone who has just
blamed something on Japan. This use is analytical and often corrective, so it can
carry a faint note of "let's be fair about this".

With a **time noun** - <code>{f|今|いま}</code>, <code>{f|今回|こんかい}</code>,
<code>{f|今日|きょう}</code> - it is *complaining*.
<code>{f|今|いま}に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</code> means the problem is chronic,
and the speaker has run out of patience with it. Said about a colleague it is a
criticism, and a fairly pointed one, because it converts a single lapse into a
pattern.

The variant <code>{f|限|かぎ}った{f|話|はなし}ではない</code> means the same thing and
leans slightly more spoken. Note also that `に限らず`, which the reader has from N2,
is the same verb in a different shape - and the two are not interchangeable, because
one ends a sentence and the other cannot.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない vs ～に{f|限|かぎ}らず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different position. `に限らず` is a **connector**
in the middle of a sentence; `に限ったことではない` is a **predicate** that ends one.

</div>

<pre>
{f|週末|しゅうまつ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>、{f|平日|へいじつ}も{f|混|こ}んでいる。
It's busy on weekdays as well, not just at weekends.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|混|こ}んでいるのは{f|週末|しゅうまつ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。
It isn't only at weekends that it's busy.
</pre>

`に限らず` has to be followed by the wider case it opens onto - that is its whole
function. `に限ったことではない` states the widening and stops, leaving the reader to
supply the rest. If you want to name what else is included, `に限らず` is the more
economical choice.

### ～に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない vs ～とは{f|限|かぎ}らない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `に限ったことではない` denies that a case is **the only one**.
`とは限らない` denies that a generalisation is **always true**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|高|たか}い{f|物|もの}がいいとは<b>{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。
Expensive things aren't necessarily good.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}で{f|判断|はんだん}してしまうのは、この{f|店|みせ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。
Judging by price is not something that happens only in this shop.
</pre>

The two share the verb and nothing else. `とは限らない` attacks a rule
("all X are Y"); `に限ったことではない` attacks a boundary ("only X does Y"). The
grammatical tell is what precedes: a clause plus `とは` for the first, a bare noun
plus `に` for the second.

### ～に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない vs ～だけではない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だけではない` is the everyday version.
`に限ったことではない` is longer, more deliberate, and sounds like a considered
correction rather than a passing remark.

</div>

<pre>
{f|困|こま}っているのは{f|私|わたし}<b>だけではない</b>。
I'm not the only one having trouble.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|困|こま}っているのは{f|私|わたし}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。
It is by no means only me who is in difficulty.
</pre>

Both are correct. The second is what you would write in a letter of complaint on
behalf of a group, and the first is what you would say to a friend. The extra length
of `に限ったことではない` is doing work: it signals that the speaker has thought
about the scope of the claim.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|遅|おく}れる<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。 → ✅ {f|遅|おく}れるのは{f|今|いま}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。
  The pattern takes a noun. A verb has to be packaged into a `のは` clause first, and
  the noun slot then holds the case being widened.

- ❌ {f|安|やす}い{f|物|もの}が{f|悪|わる}い<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。 → ✅ {f|安|やす}い{f|物|もの}が{f|悪|わる}い<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。
  The sentence denies a generalisation, not a boundary, so it needs `とは限らない`.
  The two patterns look similar and mean genuinely different things.

- ❌ {f|週末|しゅうまつ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではなく</b>。 → ✅ {f|週末|しゅうまつ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。（or …ことではなく、{f|平日|へいじつ}も{f|混|こ}む）
  The <code>なく</code> form is a connector and cannot end a sentence. Either finish
  with <code>ない</code> or carry on into the second half.

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|当社|とうしゃ}に<b>{f|限|かぎ}らないことではない</b>。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|当社|とうしゃ}に<b>{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。
  A blend of the two shapes, and a double negative into the bargain. The fixed form
  is <code>{f|限|かぎ}ったこと</code>, with the past-attributive.

## Practice

**1.** {f|残業|ざんぎょう}が{f|多|おお}いのは、この{f|部署|ぶしょ}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|部署|ぶしょ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。

The `のは` clause states the phenomenon and the noun names the case that has been
singled out; the pattern then denies that the case is the only one. Note that a
noun, not a verb, sits directly in front of `に`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|努力|どりょく}すれば{f|報|むく}われる（に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない・とは{f|限|かぎ}らない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|報|むく}われる<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。

The sentence is denying a general rule - that effort is always rewarded - so it needs
`とは限らない`. `に限ったことではない` would have to follow a noun and would be
saying that effort is rewarded in other places too, which is not the point being
made.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Staff leaving after two years is not something peculiar to our company."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|社員|しゃいん}が{f|二年|にねん}で{f|辞|や}めていくのは、{f|当社|とうしゃ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではありません</b>。

The English "peculiar to" is precisely what the pattern denies. The polite
<code>ありません</code> suits the corporate setting, and <code>{f|当社|とうしゃ}</code>
is the standard formal word for one's own company in writing.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部品|ぶひん}の{f|遅|おく}れが{f|出|で}たのは{f|今回|こんかい}に{f|限|かぎ}らないことではない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部品|ぶひん}の{f|遅|おく}れが{f|出|で}たのは{f|今回|こんかい}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。

Two shapes have been crossed. <code>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</code> and
<code>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</code> both exist; ❌<code>に{f|限|かぎ}らないことではない</code>
does not, and as written it cancels itself into a claim that the delay *was* unique.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|長引|ながび}くのは{f|今日|きょう}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。
(b) {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|長引|ながび}くのは{f|営業部|えいぎょうぶ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ったことではない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) widens backwards in time, and it is a complaint: meetings always overrun, and the
speaker is tired of it. A time word in the noun slot nearly always produces that
exasperated reading.

(b) widens sideways across the organisation, and it is a defence. Somebody has
criticised the sales department, and this sentence points out that other departments
do the same. Same grammar, and one sentence is an accusation while the other is an
answer to one.

</details>
