# ～といい～といい

**Meaning** · take A, take B - whichever part of it you look at, the same verdict holds

`といい` comes in pairs. The speaker picks two features out of one thing - a
person, a dish, a room, a season - and offers them as samples: look at this, look
at that. What closes the sentence is never a neutral fact but the speaker's
appraisal, admiring or fed up. Two items are quoted; the whole thing is being
judged.

## Formation

Nouns only, and the two nouns must be features of the same thing.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + といい + Noun + といい | {f|色|いろ}といい{f|形|かたち}といい |
| The thing being judged | ～は、A といい B といい、+ verdict | この{f|皿|さら}は、{f|色|いろ}といい{f|形|かたち}といい、{f|美|うつく}しい |
| An adjective idea | make it a noun first | {f|暑|あつ}い → {f|暑|あつ}さといい |
| Three items | A といい B といい C といい | {f|味|あじ}といい{f|量|りょう}といい{f|値段|ねだん}といい |
| Polite | … + です／ですね | {f|色|いろ}といい{f|形|かたち}といい、{f|見事|みごと}ですね |

<div class="note">

**Note** - whatever follows the pair has to be an evaluation. A plan, a request or
an instruction cannot close the pattern:
❌<code>{f|色|いろ}といい{f|形|かたち}といい、{f|買|か}ってください</code>. The
sentence has to end in a judgement, because the two items exist only to support
one.

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is considered language rather than quick language. It belongs to reviews,
appraisals, a complaint told properly, an essay, a report on how something turned
out - places where the speaker has stopped to weigh a thing up. It is not stiff,
though: it takes plain or polite endings equally and would not sound out of place
over dinner.

What it always carries is **attitude**. Roughly half of its uses are admiration
and half are exasperation, and the verdict at the end decides which. That is why a
flat, factual close does not work. <code>{f|色|いろ}といい{f|形|かたち}といい、{f|中国|ちゅうごく}{f|製|せい}だ</code>
leaves the listener waiting: two features have been produced as evidence, and no
judgement arrived.

One social point. Used about your own possessions, your own work or your own
family, it tends to sound like boasting, because the pattern builds to praise and
the praise is coming from you. 「うちの{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|成績|せいせき}といい{f|性格|せいかく}といい…」
is the shape of a parent showing off. That is a tendency and not a prohibition -
it is perfectly usable about your own failures, where the self-criticism lands
rather well.

Finally, two items is the normal number and it is enough. The pattern does not
mean "these two and no others"; it means "any part you pick". Piling up four or
five makes it sound like a list rather than a verdict.

## Contrast

### ～といい～といい vs ～も～も

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `も…も` **adds two facts together**. `といい…といい` offers
two features as **evidence for one judgement** about the thing they belong to.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|宿|やど}は{f|料理|りょうり}<b>も</b>{f|温泉|おんせん}<b>も</b>よかった。
The food was good at this inn, and so were the baths.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|宿|やど}は{f|料理|りょうり}<b>といい</b>{f|温泉|おんせん}<b>といい</b>、なかなかのものだ。
This inn - the food, the baths - is really quite something.
</pre>

The first is a report and could be the answer to "how was it?". The second is a
verdict, and the two items are only samples of it: the listener understands that
the service and the rooms would come out the same way. Where `も…も` can name two
completely unrelated things - {f|雨|あめ}も{f|風|かぜ}も{f|強|つよ}い - the pair
under `といい` has to belong to one whole.

### ～といい～といい vs ～といわず～といわず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `といい…といい` names two **features** and judges the thing.
`といわず…といわず` names two **points** and says there was no gap between them -
everywhere, or all the time.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|絵|え}は{f|色使|いろづか}い<b>といい</b>{f|構図|こうず}<b>といい</b>、プロ{f|並|な}みだ。
His painting is professional standard, in its use of colour and in its composition.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもたちは{f|顔|かお}<b>といわず</b>{f|服|ふく}<b>といわず</b>{f|泥|どろ}だらけだった。
The children were covered in mud, faces, clothes and everything in between.
</pre>

The second sentence is not appraising faces and clothes; it is saying the mud
respected no boundaries. Swap the patterns and both fail: composition and colour
are not areas that mud spreads across, and no one delivers a considered verdict on
a child's face versus a child's shirt.

### ～といい～といい vs ～にしても～にしても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `にしても…にしても` grants two **possibilities** and says what
is true either way. `といい…といい` takes two **actual features** and says what is
true of the thing that has them.

</div>

<pre>
{f|行|い}く<b>にしても</b>{f|行|い}かない<b>にしても</b>、{f|今日中|きょうじゅう}に{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|必要|ひつよう}だ。
Whether you go or not, an answer is needed today.
</pre>

<pre>
あのホテルは{f|立地|りっち}<b>といい</b>{f|設備|せつび}<b>といい</b>、{f|値段|ねだん}が{f|高|たか}いのも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ。
That hotel, with its location and its facilities, is expensive for good reason.
</pre>

`にしても` looks forward at branches that have not been settled, which is why it
takes verbs freely. `といい` looks at what is already there, which is why it takes
nothing but nouns.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|服|ふく}は{f|安|やす}い<b>といい</b>{f|丈夫|じょうぶ}だ<b>といい</b>、いい{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}だった。 → ✅ この{f|服|ふく}は{f|値段|ねだん}<b>といい</b>{f|生地|きじ}<b>といい</b>、いい{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}だった。
  Adjectives and verbs cannot sit in front of <code>といい</code>. Name the
  feature as a noun - {f|値段|ねだん}, {f|生地|きじ}, {f|安|やす}さ - and the
  sentence works.

- ❌ この{f|町|まち}は{f|気候|きこう}<b>といい</b>{f|物価|ぶっか}<b>といい</b>、ぜひ{f|一度|いちど}{f|来|き}てください。 → ✅ この{f|町|まち}は{f|気候|きこう}<b>といい</b>{f|物価|ぶっか}<b>といい</b>、{f|住|す}みやすいところです。
  The pattern has to land on a judgement. An invitation or an instruction leaves
  the two samples with nothing to support.

- ❌ {f|兄|あに}は{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}い<b>といい</b>、{f|姉|あね}は{f|頭|あたま}がいい<b>といい</b>… → ✅ {f|兄|あに}も{f|姉|あね}も{f|自慢|じまん}の{f|家族|かぞく}です。
  The two items have to be features of **one** thing. Two different people are two
  subjects, and that is what `も…も` is for.

- ❌ この{f|車|くるま}は{f|燃費|ねんぴ}<b>といい</b>、{f|気|き}に{f|入|い}っている。 → ✅ この{f|車|くるま}は{f|燃費|ねんぴ}<b>といい</b>{f|乗|の}り{f|心地|ごこち}<b>といい</b>、{f|気|き}に{f|入|い}っている。
  A single <code>といい</code> is not the pattern. It needs the pair to mean
  "wherever you look".

## Practice

**1.** この{f|時計|とけい}は、デザイン____{f|精度|せいど}____、{f|文句|もんく}なしの{f|一本|いっぽん}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

デザイン<b>といい</b>{f|精度|せいど}<b>といい</b>

Two features of one watch, quoted as samples, and the sentence closes on a verdict
- exactly the slot this pattern fills.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|頭痛|ずつう}（といい・も）{f|熱|ねつ}（といい・も）ひどくて、{f|一日中|いちにちじゅう}{f|寝|ね}ていた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|頭痛|ずつう}<b>も</b>{f|熱|ねつ}<b>も</b>ひどくて

A headache and a temperature are two separate symptoms being added together, not
two features of one object being appraised. `も…も` is the right tool;
<code>といい</code> would suggest you were reviewing your own illness.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "This restaurant - the service, the atmosphere - is not what it used to be."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|店|みせ}は、サービス<b>といい</b>{f|雰囲気|ふんいき}<b>といい</b>、{f|昔|むかし}とはすっかり{f|変|か}わってしまった。

Two aspects of one restaurant, leading to a judgement - and note that the judgement
here is a disappointed one. The pattern is just as at home in criticism as in
praise.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|作品|さくひん}は{f|色|いろ}といい{f|構図|こうず}といい、{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|美術館|びじゅつかん}に{f|展示|てんじ}されます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|作品|さくひん}は{f|色|いろ}<b>といい</b>{f|構図|こうず}<b>といい</b>、{f|展示|てんじ}する{f|価値|かち}が{f|十分|じゅうぶん}にある。

The original ends in a plain announcement of fact, and the two features are left
supporting nothing. Replace the ending with an evaluation and the sentence holds
together.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|演奏|えんそう}は{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}も{f|表現力|ひょうげんりょく}もすばらしかった。
(b) {f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|演奏|えんそう}は{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}といい{f|表現力|ひょうげんりょく}といい、すばらしかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) praises two things and stops there: the technique was excellent and so was the
expression. It reads as a report, and a listener may reasonably ask about the
programme or the tone.

(b) uses the same two things as samples. The claim is about the performance as a
whole - pick any aspect you like and it was excellent - and the speaker's
admiration is audible in the construction itself.

</details>
