# ～たる・～たるもの

**Meaning** · one who is a ~, with everything that position demands - names a station and then states the duty that comes with it

<code>たる</code> is the attributive form of <code>たり</code>, the classical copula
that modern Japanese replaced with <code>である</code>. So
<code>{f|教師|きょうし}たる{f|者|もの}</code> is literally "a person who is a
teacher" - but nobody uses it for mere identification. It survives because it carries
the weight of the old written language, and it is used to invoke an office and then
tell whoever holds it how to behave.

## Formation

<code>たる</code> attaches directly to a **noun naming a role, rank or office**, and
must be followed by a noun, because it is an attributive form.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun + noun | ～たる + {f|名詞|めいし} | {f|指導者|しどうしゃ} → {f|指導者|しどうしゃ}たる{f|責任|せきにん} |
| Noun, as the topic of a maxim | ～たる{f|者|もの}（は）、… | {f|医師|いし} → {f|医師|いし}たる{f|者|もの}、… |
| Followed by an obligation | ～たる{f|者|もの}、…べきだ／なければならない | {f|紳士|しんし}たる{f|者|もの}、{f|約束|やくそく}は{f|守|まも}るべきだ |
| Qualifying a standard | ～たるに{f|恥|は}じない／ふさわしい | {f|代表|だいひょう}たるにふさわしい{f|態度|たいど} |
| Fossilised adjectives | {f|堂々|どうどう}たる／{f|確固|かっこ}たる + {f|名詞|めいし} | {f|確固|かっこ}たる{f|信念|しんねん} |
| Modern equivalent | ～である{f|以上|いじょう}／～として | {f|医師|いし}である{f|以上|いじょう}、… |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|者|もの}</code> after it is read もの, and is very often written in
kana: <code>たるもの</code>. Do not confuse the pattern with
<code>～たりとも</code>, which comes from the same old copula but means "not even
one": <code>{f|一日|いちにち}たりとも{f|休|やす}めない</code>. The two are unrelated
in meaning.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the <code>{f|堂々|どうどう}たる</code> group is the one place this ending
is still fully alive. A closed set of two-kanji words - {f|確固|かっこ},
{f|堂々|どうどう}, {f|歴然|れきぜん}, {f|燦然|さんぜん}, {f|錚々|そうそう} - form
adjectives only in this shape, taking <code>たる</code> before a noun and
<code>と</code> before a verb: <code>{f|堂々|どうどう}と{f|歩|ある}く</code>. You cannot
coin new ones.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**This is a form to recognise, not to deploy.** It belongs to the written language of
another era and to the modern registers that borrow from it: an inaugural address, a
company creed, a school motto, a newspaper column scolding the government, the
narration of a historical drama, a character in period fiction, a manga villain
explaining his code. Said in an ordinary conversation it does not sound formal - it
sounds theatrical, and Japanese speakers often use it *because* it does, as a joke:
<code>{f|学生|がくせい}たるもの、{f|朝|あさ}は{f|起|お}きるべし</code> among friends
is a parody of a lecture, not a lecture.

The modern way to do this job is <code>～である{f|以上|いじょう}</code> or plain
<code>～として</code>:
<code>{f|医師|いし}である{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|患者|かんじゃ}に{f|説明|せつめい}する{f|義務|ぎむ}がある</code>.
That sentence says everything the <code>たるもの</code> version says and can be
spoken aloud in a hospital corridor.

Three things travel with the pattern. It **moralises**: what follows is nearly always
べきだ, なければならない, てはならない or an imperative. It **looks down**, or at
least across from a position of authority - it is the voice of a founder, a father, a
leader, an editorial. And it is **almost never used of oneself**:
<code>{f|私|わたし}はリーダーたる{f|者|もの}…</code> is self-important in a way that
is hard to recover from. Turned on a listener it is a rebuke, and a heavy one, since
it accuses them not of a mistake but of failing their station.

The one corner where a learner may safely produce it is the fossilised adjective
group - <code>{f|確固|かっこ}たる{f|証拠|しょうこ}</code>,
<code>{f|堂々|どうどう}と{f|話|はな}す</code> - which is ordinary formal vocabulary
and carries none of the moralising.

## Contrast

### ～たる{f|者|もの} vs ～である{f|以上|いじょう}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same argument, two centuries apart.
<code>～である{f|以上|いじょう}</code> is what a modern speaker says; use it, and
recognise <code>たる{f|者|もの}</code> when you meet it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|審判|しんぱん}<b>たる{f|者|もの}</b>、どちらにも{f|味方|みかた}してはならない。
He who is a referee must take neither side.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|審判|しんぱん}<b>である{f|以上|いじょう}</b>、どちらか{f|一方|いっぽう}に{f|味方|みかた}してはいけません。
Since you are the referee, you must not favour one side over the other.
</pre>

The content is identical. The first belongs on a wall or in a speech, the second in a
meeting, an email or a conversation with the referee. Note that the modern version
also lets you soften the ending - <code>ほうがいい</code>,
<code>べきではないでしょうか</code> - where <code>たる{f|者|もの}</code> practically
requires an absolute.

### ～たる vs ～として

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>として</code> states the **capacity someone acts in**, with
no moral weight. <code>たる</code> invokes the **dignity of the office** and expects
something of the holder.

</div>

<pre>
{f|通訳|つうやく}<b>として</b>{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|参加|さんか}しました。
I took part in the conference as an interpreter.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|通訳|つうやく}<b>たる{f|者|もの}</b>、{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|意見|いけん}を{f|混|ま}ぜてはならない。
An interpreter must not mix in opinions of his own.
</pre>

Only the second is a rule about how interpreters ought to behave, which is why
<code>として</code> would weaken it and <code>たる{f|者|もの}</code> in the first
sentence would be absurd - the speaker is reporting where they sat, not proclaiming a
creed. Grammatically, too, <code>として</code> attaches to a phrase and can precede a
verb; <code>たる</code> must be followed by a noun.

### ～たる vs ～なる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - two classical copulas, two closed sets.
<code>たる</code> goes with the {f|堂々|どうどう} group;
<code>なる</code> survives in {f|単|たん}なる, {f|大|おお}いなる,
{f|聖|せい}なる. Neither is productive, and you cannot choose between them - each
word takes one or the other.

</div>

<pre>
それは<b>{f|単|たん}なる</b>{f|偶然|ぐうぜん}にすぎない。
That is nothing more than a coincidence.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|確固|かっこ}たる</b>{f|証拠|しょうこ}をつかんでいた。
He had firm evidence in hand.
</pre>

Both endings mean roughly "which is", and both are frozen. The practical advice is
lexical, not grammatical: learn <code>{f|単|たん}なる</code>,
<code>{f|大|おお}いなる</code>, <code>{f|聖|せい}なる</code>,
<code>{f|確固|かっこ}たる</code>, <code>{f|堂々|どうどう}たる</code> as vocabulary
items, and never build a new one from a noun you like the look of.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>だたる{f|者|もの}</b> → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>たる{f|者|もの}</b>
  <code>たる</code> already contains the copula. Nothing goes between it and the
  noun - no `だ`, no <code>である</code>, no `の`.

- ❌ {f|優|やさ}しい<b>たる{f|者|もの}</b> → ✅ {f|優|やさ}しい{f|人|ひと}
  Only a noun that names a status or office can precede it. Adjectives and verbs
  cannot, and neither can an ordinary noun with no rank attached to it -
  ❌<code>{f|読者|どくしゃ}たる{f|者|もの}</code> is odd because readers have no
  duties.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}はこの{f|会|かい}の{f|代表|だいひょう}<b>たる</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}はこの{f|会|かい}の{f|代表|だいひょう}<b>である</b>。
  <code>たる</code> is an attributive form and cannot end a sentence. A noun must
  follow it.

- ❌ {f|一日|いちにち}<b>たる{f|者|もの}</b>{f|休|やす}めない。 → ✅ {f|一日|いちにち}<b>たりとも</b>{f|休|やす}めない。
  "Not even a single day" is <code>たりとも</code>. The similarity of shape catches
  learners often, and the two patterns share nothing but their ancestor.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>たる{f|者|もの}</b>、{f|部下|ぶか}を{f|守|まも}ります。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>として</b>、{f|部下|ぶか}は{f|私|わたし}が{f|守|まも}ります。
  Applied to yourself the pattern announces your own eminence. Use
  <code>として</code> when the person under discussion is you.

## Practice

**1.** 「{f|武士|ぶし}____{f|者|もの}、{f|恥|はじ}を{f|知|し}れ」というのは{f|時代劇|じだいげき}によくある{f|台詞|せりふ}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|武士|ぶし}<b>たる</b>{f|者|もの}

A samurai drama is exactly where this form lives now. Note the shape of the whole
line: a station, a comma, and then a bare imperative - the pattern's characteristic
rhythm.

</details>

**2.** Where would you expect to see this sentence, and what would a colleague say instead? {f|管理職|かんりしょく}<b>たる{f|者|もの}</b>、{f|部下|ぶか}の{f|失敗|しっぱい}の{f|責任|せきにん}を{f|負|お}うべきである。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In a printed company creed, a training handout, an executive's speech, or a
newspaper column about corporate failures. Not in the office itself.

A colleague would say
<code>{f|管理職|かんりしょく}なんだから、{f|部下|ぶか}の{f|失敗|しっぱい}の{f|責任|せきにん}は{f|取|と}らないと</code>,
or in a meeting
<code>{f|管理職|かんりしょく}である{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|責任|せきにん}を{f|負|お}う{f|必要|ひつよう}があります</code>.

</details>

**3.** Rewrite in modern Japanese: {f|研究者|けんきゅうしゃ}<b>たる{f|者|もの}</b>、{f|都合|つごう}の{f|悪|わる}い{f|結果|けっか}から{f|目|め}をそらしてはならない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|研究者|けんきゅうしゃ}<b>である{f|以上|いじょう}</b>、{f|都合|つごう}の{f|悪|わる}い{f|結果|けっか}から{f|目|め}をそらしてはいけません。

<code>たる{f|者|もの}</code> becomes <code>である{f|以上|いじょう}</code>, and the
classical <code>てはならない</code> becomes <code>てはいけません</code>. The
obligation survives intact; what disappears is the pulpit.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|一円|いちえん}たる{f|者|もの}{f|無駄|むだ}にはできない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|一円|いちえん}<b>たりとも</b>{f|無駄|むだ}にはできない。
"We cannot waste so much as a single yen."

<code>たる{f|者|もの}</code> needs a person holding an office; one yen holds no
office. The pattern wanted here is <code>たりとも</code>, "not even", which attaches
to a counted amount.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) プロの{f|選手|せんしゅ}<b>として</b>、{f|体調|たいちょう}{f|管理|かんり}に{f|気|き}をつけている。
(b) プロの{f|選手|せんしゅ}<b>たる{f|者|もの}</b>、{f|体調|たいちょう}{f|管理|かんり}に{f|気|き}をつけるべきだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the speaker describing their own practice in the capacity of a professional.
It is neutral, modern and unremarkable - a normal thing to say in an interview.

(b) lays down a rule for professionals in general, from somewhere above them, in the
language of a creed. Said by a coach at a ceremony it is stirring; said by a
team-mate in the changing room it is either an insult or a joke, and the listener
will decide which by your tone.

</details>
