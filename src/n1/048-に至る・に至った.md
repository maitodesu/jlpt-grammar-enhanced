# ～に至る・～に至った

**Meaning** · to reach the point of; to culminate in - read <code>に{f|至|いた}る</code>

{f|至|いた}る is a formal verb meaning "to arrive at, to reach". As a grammar point
it takes the end point of a long or difficult process - an agreement, a break-up, a
resignation, a war - and reports that things finally got that far. The road behind
matters as much as the destination: <code>に{f|至|いた}る</code> always implies
that something has been building. It is also the base form of a small family, since
に{f|至|いた}って, に{f|至|いた}っては and に{f|至|いた}っても are all built on it.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + に{f|至|いた}る | {f|合意|ごうい} → {f|合意|ごうい}に{f|至|いた}る |
| Noun (achieved) | Noun + に{f|至|いた}った | {f|離婚|りこん} → {f|離婚|りこん}に{f|至|いた}った |
| Verb (dictionary form) | Verb + に{f|至|いた}る | {f|辞任|じにん}する → {f|辞任|じにん}するに{f|至|いた}った |
| Continuing to the present | ～に{f|至|いた}っている | {f|今日|こんにち}に{f|至|いた}っている |
| Negative - "it did not go that far" | ～には{f|至|いた}らない／{f|至|いた}らなかった | {f|大|おお}きな{f|被害|ひがい}には{f|至|いた}らなかった |
| Modifying a noun | ～に{f|至|いた}った + Noun | {f|事故|じこ}に{f|至|いた}った{f|経緯|けいい} |
| Spatial range | ～から～に{f|至|いた}る | {f|北|きた}から{f|南|みなみ}に{f|至|いた}る |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the negative <code>には{f|至|いた}らなかった</code> is at least as
common in news copy as the affirmative. It is the standard way of saying that
something serious was narrowly avoided: けがには{f|至|いた}らなかった,
{f|中止|ちゅうし}には{f|至|いた}らなかった.

</div>

The sequence of events that led to the accident is set out in detail in the report.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Formal and written.** News reporting, company histories, court documents,
committee reports and academic prose are its home. In speech it appears in a
prepared statement or a briefing, not in conversation; a friend telling you about
their divorce says {f|別|わか}れることになった, not
{f|離婚|りこん}に{f|至|いた}った.

The verb is **narrative**. It wants a process in front of it, so it collocates with
～た{f|末|すえ}, ついに, ようやく, {f|次第|しだい}に, {f|何度|なんど}も and with
clauses that describe a build-up. Drop it onto a single quick event and the sentence
becomes pompous.

Unlike {f|始末|しまつ}だ or {f|羽目|はめ}になる, <code>に{f|至|いた}る</code> is
**not inherently negative**. {f|合意|ごうい}に{f|至|いた}る and
{f|完成|かんせい}に{f|至|いた}る are perfectly good. What it does insist on is
**weight**: the end point has to be consequential enough to be worth a long road.
In practice most real examples are grave, because grave things are what get written
up this way.

Three readings are worth pinning down. {f|今日|こんにち}に{f|至|いた}る is
**こんにち**, "the present day", not きょう. {f|至|いた}る{f|所|ところ},
"everywhere", is a separate lexical item. And the adverb
{f|至|いた}って, as in {f|至|いた}って{f|健康|けんこう}だ ("in perfect health"),
means "extremely" and has nothing to do with this pattern despite the shared kanji.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|至|いた}る vs ～になる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>になる</code> simply reports the change.
<code>に{f|至|いた}る</code> insists that a **long road** preceded it, and lifts
the register several notches.

</div>

<pre>
{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いの{f|結果|けっか}、{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|再開|さいかい}すること<b>になりました</b>。
Following discussion, it has been decided to resume next month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三年|さんねん}にわたる{f|交渉|こうしょう}の{f|末|すえ}、ようやく{f|再開|さいかい}に<b>{f|至|いた}った</b>。
After three years of negotiation, it finally came to a resumption.
</pre>

になる can report a change that took an afternoon. に{f|至|いた}る in the same
sentence would promise a history the sentence does not have, and the reader would
wonder what they had missed.

### ～に{f|至|いた}る vs ～に{f|及|およ}ぶ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|及|およ}ぶ</code> **spreads**: it reaches out over a
scale, a duration or a range. <code>{f|至|いた}る</code> **arrives**: it reaches an
end point.

</div>

<pre>
{f|被害|ひがい}は{f|十|じゅう}{f|億円|おくえん}に<b>{f|及|およ}んだ</b>。
The damage ran to a billion yen.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|調査|ちょうさ}の{f|末|すえ}、{f|工事|こうじ}の{f|中止|ちゅうし}に<b>{f|至|いた}った</b>。
The investigation ended in the works being called off.
</pre>

{f|及|およ}ぶ takes amounts, areas and lengths of time -
10{f|年|ねん}に{f|及|およ}ぶ{f|研究|けんきゅう}, {f|全国|ぜんこく}に{f|及|およ}ぶ{f|影響|えいきょう}.
It also has the separate use {f|犯行|はんこう}に{f|及|およ}ぶ, "to go so far as to
commit the act", which is where the two verbs come closest.

### ～に{f|至|いた}る vs ～に{f|達|たっ}する

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|達|たっ}する</code> is for **numbers, levels and
targets**. <code>{f|至|いた}る</code> is for **outcomes and states**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来場者|らいじょうしゃ}は{f|一万人|いちまんにん}に<b>{f|達|たっ}した</b>。
Attendance reached ten thousand.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|交渉|こうしょう}は{f|合意|ごうい}には<b>{f|至|いた}らなかった</b>。
The talks did not reach agreement.
</pre>

Swapping them is one of the clearest register errors an N1 candidate can make.
Ask what is on the other side of に: a figure takes {f|達|たっ}する, a situation
takes {f|至|いた}る.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}く{f|代|か}わりに「{f|駅|えき}に<b>{f|至|いた}りました</b>」 → ✅ {f|駅|えき}に<b>{f|着|つ}きました</b>
  For physically arriving somewhere, {f|至|いた}る survives only in route
  descriptions ({f|山頂|さんちょう}に{f|至|いた}る{f|道|みち}). Ordinary arrival is
  {f|着|つ}く.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}、パンを{f|買|か}うに<b>{f|至|いた}った</b> → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}、パンを<b>{f|買|か}った</b>
  No process, no weight. The verb makes a trivial event sound like the outcome of a
  parliamentary inquiry.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}に{f|至|いた}るまで → ✅ <b>{f|今日|こんにち}</b>に{f|至|いた}るまで
  A reading error, not a grammar one. In this expression {f|今日|こんにち} means
  "the present day", and きょう would be wrong.

- ❌ {f|売上|うりあげ}は{f|一億円|いちおくえん}に<b>{f|至|いた}った</b> → ✅ {f|売上|うりあげ}は{f|一億円|いちおくえん}に<b>{f|達|たっ}した</b>
  A figure is a level, not an outcome, so the verb is {f|達|たっ}する.

## Practice

**1.** {f|数|すう}か{f|月|げつ}にわたる{f|交渉|こうしょう}の{f|末|すえ}、ようやく{f|両社|りょうしゃ}は{f|合意|ごうい}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|両社|りょうしゃ}は{f|合意|ごうい}<b>に{f|至|いた}った</b>。

～た{f|末|すえ} and ようやく have already established the long road, which is
exactly the environment {f|至|いた}る needs. Note the noun {f|合意|ごうい} directly
before に - no する.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|参加者|さんかしゃ}の{f|数|かず}は{f|五百人|ごひゃくにん}に（{f|至|いた}った・{f|達|たっ}した）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|五百人|ごひゃくにん}に<b>{f|達|たっ}した</b>。

A head count is a figure, and figures take {f|達|たっ}する.
{f|至|いた}る would suggest that five hundred people was some sort of grave outcome
arrived at after a struggle.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The investigation dragged on for two years and finally led to the closure of the factory."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|調査|ちょうさ}は{f|二年|にねん}に{f|及|およ}び、ついに{f|工場|こうじょう}の{f|閉鎖|へいさ}<b>に{f|至|いた}った</b>。

Both verbs appear in their proper places: {f|及|およ}ぶ for the two-year span, and
{f|至|いた}る for the outcome the whole process ended in. ついに is the natural
adverb to mark the culmination.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|説明|せつめい}が{f|不十分|ふじゅうぶん}で、{f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}が{f|辞|や}めたに{f|至|いた}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|説明|せつめい}が{f|不十分|ふじゅうぶん}で、{f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}が<b>{f|辞|や}めるに{f|至|いた}った</b>。
（or {f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}の{f|辞任|じにん}<b>に{f|至|いた}った</b>。）

The verb in front of に{f|至|いた}る stays in the dictionary form; the past tense
sits on {f|至|いた}った. Replacing the clause with the action noun
{f|辞任|じにん} is the more usual choice in formal writing.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|工場|こうじょう}は{f|閉鎖|へいさ}すること<b>になった</b>。
(b) {f|工場|こうじょう}は{f|閉鎖|へいさ}する<b>に{f|至|いた}った</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports a decision in neutral, ordinary Japanese. It could be an internal email,
and it says nothing about how long or how painful the road to it was.

(b) frames the closure as the end point of a process the reader is expected to know
about - falling orders, failed negotiations, a scandal. It is written register, it
carries weight, and it invites the question "how did it come to that?", which the
surrounding text usually answers.

</details>
