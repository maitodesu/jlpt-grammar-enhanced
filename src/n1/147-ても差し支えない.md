# ～ても差し支えない

**Meaning** · it is fine to; there is no objection - permission given impersonally, as an absence of any obstacle

{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}える means "to get in the way, to cause a problem", so
{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない says **nothing stands in the way**. That is what
makes it different from てもいい: the speaker is not granting a favour, they are
reporting that no impediment exists. Hospitals, offices and forms talk this way.

## Formation

Attach も{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない to the て-form of whatever is being allowed.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + も{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない | {f|使|つか}う → {f|使|つか}っても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない |
| Verb (negative) | ～なくても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない | {f|出席|しゅっせき}する → {f|出席|しゅっせき}しなくても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない |
| い-adjective | ～くても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない | {f|遅|おそ}い → {f|遅|おそ}くても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない |
| な-adjective | ～でも{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない | {f|簡単|かんたん} → {f|簡単|かんたん}でも{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない |
| Noun | ～でも{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない | {f|鉛筆|えんぴつ} → {f|鉛筆|えんぴつ}でも{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない |
| Polite | ～も{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えありません | {f|撮|と}っても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えありません |
| Honorific | ～も{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えございません | {f|平服|へいふく}でも{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えございません |
| Conditional set phrase | {f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えなければ | - → {f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えなければ、お{f|名前|なまえ}を… |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the reading is **さしつかえない**. {f|支|つか}える here is the verb of
obstruction; it is not the {f|支|ささ}える of
<code>{f|支|ささ}える</code>, "to support", although the two share a kanji.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Formal, and institutional.** This is the Japanese of doctors, receptionists,
city offices, contracts, guidance notes and business email. Its politeness comes
from being impersonal: no one is being generous, there is simply no obstacle.
That distance is why it is safe to use upward, to a customer or a patient, where
plain てもいい can sound as if you were dispensing favours.

Between friends it is comic. ❌トイレ{f|借|か}りても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}え
ありませんか sounds like a form being read aloud; say
{f|借|か}りてもいい？

Three things worth taking away. The polite form is
{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えありません, and in service Japanese it climbs to
{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えございません. As a **question** -
{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えないでしょうか - it is a very courteous way to ask
permission. And the conditional
<code>{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えなければ</code>, "if it is not inconvenient for
you", is the phrase you will meet most often of all: it is the standard opener
for asking someone something slightly personal.

There is also the affirmative verb, alive and well:
{f|仕事|しごと}に{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}える, "it will interfere with work". Meet
that and you have the literal sense the pattern is built on.

## Contrast

### ～ても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない vs ～てもいい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `てもいい` is a **person** allowing something.
<code>ても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない</code> is an **institution** reporting that
nothing prevents it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|疲|つか}れたら{f|休|やす}ん<b>でもいい</b>よ。
You can take a break if you're tired.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|検査|けんさ}のあとは{f|通常|つうじょう}どおり{f|食事|しょくじ}をなさっ<b>ても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えありません</b>。
After the examination you may eat as normal.
</pre>

The first is warm and personal and can be said downward or between equals; said
to a customer it is presumptuous. The second is neutral in a way that suits
strangers, and it carries an implication `てもいい` does not: that the matter has
been **assessed** and found harmless.

### ～ても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない vs ～ても{f|構|かま}わない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|構|かま}わない</code> = *I* don't mind.
<code>{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない</code> = *it* causes no problem.

</div>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}がかかっ<b>ても{f|構|かま}いません</b>。{f|丁寧|ていねい}にお{f|願|ねが}いします。
I don't mind if it takes a while - please do it carefully.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|原本|げんぽん}でなくコピーを{f|提出|ていしゅつ}し<b>ても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えありません</b>。
Submitting a copy rather than the original is acceptable.
</pre>

<code>{f|構|かま}わない</code> reveals the speaker's own tolerance, so it is
personal and can sound faintly indifferent -
どちらでも{f|構|かま}わない, "either is fine by me".
<code>{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない</code> never says how anyone feels; it states a
rule.

### {f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えなければ vs よろしければ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - よろしければ = *if you would like to*.
<code>{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えなければ</code> = *if it causes you no trouble to
tell me* - the form for asking something you have no right to.

</div>

<pre>
<b>よろしければ</b>、こちらのパンフレットもお{f|持|も}ちください。
Do take one of these leaflets as well, if you'd like.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えなければ</b>、ご{f|年齢|ねんれい}をお{f|聞|き}かせいただけますか。
If you don't mind my asking, could you tell me your age?
</pre>

よろしければ offers; <code>{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えなければ</code> intrudes
carefully. Use the second before questions about age, income, family or health,
and the listener is given a graceful way to refuse.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|使|つか}う<b>も{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない</b> → ✅ {f|使|つか}っ<b>ても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない</b>
  The pattern needs the て-form plus も, exactly as てもいい does.

- ❌ {f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない<b>です</b>。 → ✅ {f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}え<b>ありません</b>。
  ないです is too casual for a phrase this formal, and it clashes with the
  register of everything around it.

- ❌ {f|差|さ}し{f|支|ささ}えありません → ✅ {f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えありません
  The verb is {f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}える. The reading ささ belongs to
  {f|支|ささ}える, "to support", which is a different word.

- ❌ (to a close friend) この{f|席|せき}に{f|座|すわ}っても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えありませんか。 → ✅ ここ{f|座|すわ}っ<b>てもいい</b>？
  Grammatically perfect, socially absurd. Between friends the institutional
  register turns the question into a joke.

## Practice

**1.** {f|申込書|もうしこみしょ}の{f|記入|きにゅう}は、{f|黒|くろ}のボールペン____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|黒|くろ}のボールペン<b>でも{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えありません</b>

A noun takes でも before {f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えない, and the sentence is a
piece of official guidance about a form - exactly where this pattern lives.

</details>

**2.** Choose: （{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えなければ・よろしければ）、ご{f|家族|かぞく}の{f|病歴|びょうれき}についてお{f|伺|うかが}いします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えなければ</b>、ご{f|家族|かぞく}の{f|病歴|びょうれき}について…

Family medical history is private, so the speaker needs the form that
acknowledges they are asking for something they are not owed. よろしければ frames
the question as an offer, which does not fit an intrusive request.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If it is not inconvenient, may I record today's meeting?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えなければ、{f|本日|ほんじつ}の{f|打|う}ち{f|合|あ}わせを{f|録音|ろくおん}し<b>ても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えないでしょうか</b>。

The negative question <code>{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えないでしょうか</code> is the
courteous way to ask permission in a business setting. Using
{f|録音|ろくおん}してもいいですか instead would be understood but noticeably
plainer.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会場|かいじょう}には{f|直接|ちょくせつ}{f|来|き}ても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|ささ}えないですよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会場|かいじょう}には{f|直接|ちょくせつ}{f|来|き}ても<b>{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えありません</b>。
"You are welcome to come straight to the venue."

The reading is つか, not ささ, and the polite ending should be
{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えありません. The よ at the end also pulls against the
formal register; drop it, or switch to {f|来|き}てもいいですよ.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|明日|あした}{f|休|やす}ん<b>でもいいですよ</b>。
(b) {f|明日|あした}{f|休|やす}ま<b>れても{f|差|さ}し{f|支|つか}えありません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a person - typically a manager - giving permission, and it sounds warm and
direct. Between colleagues it is the normal thing to say.

(b) reports that your absence would create no problem for the work. It is
politer, cooler, and it puts the decision on the organisation rather than on the
speaker's goodwill. Said with a smile it is considerate; said flatly, it can be
heard as "we can manage without you".

</details>
