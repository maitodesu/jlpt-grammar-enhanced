# ～だの～だの

**Meaning** · A and B and that sort of thing - a list quoted with a curl of the lip

`だの` is the **disparaging** member of the listing family. Like とか and やら it
names two representative items and leaves the rest implied, but it adds something
they do not: the speaker is unimpressed. What gets listed is usually other
people's complaints, other people's demands, or a pile of stuff the speaker would
rather not deal with.

## Formation

Each item carries its own `だの`, and the pattern is almost always followed by a
noun, by と{f|言|い}う, or by a predicate describing the mess.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + だの | {f|洗濯物|せんたくもの}だのゴミ{f|袋|ぶくろ}だの |
| Verb | plain form + だの | {f|行|い}くだの{f|行|い}かないだの |
| い-adjective | plain form + だの | {f|暑|あつ}いだの{f|寒|さむ}いだの |
| な-adjective | stem + だの | {f|嫌|いや}だの{f|面倒|めんどう}だの |
| Quoted grumbling | ～だの～だのと{f|言|い}う | {f|高|たか}いだの{f|遠|とお}いだのと{f|言|い}う |
| Closing the list | ～だの～だので／…だのなんだの | {f|会議|かいぎ}だの{f|出張|しゅっちょう}だので |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>だのなんだの</code> ("and what have you") is a fixed way of
finishing the list off with a shrug:
<code>{f|保険|ほけん}だのなんだので{f|給料|きゅうりょう}が{f|減|へ}る</code>. It is
very common and always dismissive.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`だの` is **spoken, plain and openly critical**. It is adult language rather than
rough language - you would hear it from a colleague over lunch or from a parent
complaining about a child - but there is nothing neutral about it. Putting
somebody's words under `だの` is a way of saying you did not think much of them.

That has an obvious social consequence: do not list a superior's requests, or a
customer's requirements, with `だの` in front of them. 「{f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|資料|しりょう}だの{f|日程|にってい}だの…」
said to the department head's face is rude in a way that とか is not. Behind their
back among equals it is exactly what people say.

It also sits at a certain age. Younger speakers reach for とか for nearly
everything, and `だの` can sound faintly like something an older relative says -
which is part of why it works so well in the mouth of a narrator or a grumbling
character in fiction. That is a tendency and no more; it is in ordinary use across
ages, especially in writing that wants a bit of bite.

In formal writing it is rare, and where it does appear it is because the writer
wants the reader to hear the sneer. A report that lists requirements with `だの`
has taken a side.

## Contrast

### ～だの～だの vs ～とか～とか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same job, different face. `とか` is **neutral** and belongs to
everyday speech; `だの` says the speaker finds the items **tiresome or silly**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|週末|しゅうまつ}は{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}<b>とか</b>{f|掃除|そうじ}<b>とか</b>で{f|終|お}わってしまう。
Weekends just go on shopping and cleaning and things like that.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|週末|しゅうまつ}は{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}<b>だの</b>{f|掃除|そうじ}<b>だの</b>で{f|終|お}わってしまう。
Weekends disappear into shopping, cleaning, all of that nonsense.
</pre>

The information is identical. The second speaker resents it. Because `とか` is
neutral it can list things you like - {f|旅行|りょこう}とか{f|映画|えいが}とか -
and `だの` in that slot would be strange, since you are not supposed to be
belittling your own pleasures.

### ～だの～だの vs ～やら～やら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `やら…やら` says the speaker was **overwhelmed**; `だの…だの`
says the speaker is **unimpressed**. Confusion versus contempt.

</div>

<pre>
{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}しの{f|日|ひ}は{f|荷物|にもつ}<b>やら</b>{f|来客|らいきゃく}<b>やら</b>で{f|目|め}が{f|回|まわ}った。
On moving day, what with the boxes and the visitors, my head was spinning.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|近所|きんじょ}の{f|人|ひと}は{f|音|おと}が{f|大|おお}きい<b>だの</b>{f|車|くるま}が{f|邪魔|じゃま}<b>だの</b>と{f|言|い}ってくる。
The neighbour comes round to say we're noisy, or the car is in the way.
</pre>

`やら` sympathises with the person in the middle of it, usually the speaker.
`だの` looks at someone else's words or someone else's stuff and finds them
wanting. One more formal difference: `やら` does not readily take quoted
complaints, while <code>だの…だのと{f|言|い}う</code> is `だの`'s home ground.

### ～だの～だの vs や

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `や` is the plain written "and, among others". `だの` is `や`
plus an opinion, and cannot be used where the list must stay factual.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}には{f|机|つくえ}<b>や</b>{f|椅子|いす}が{f|運|はこ}び{f|込|こ}まれた。
Desks, chairs and so on were carried into the hall.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|倉庫|そうこ}には{f|壊|こわ}れた{f|机|つくえ}<b>だの</b>{f|古|ふる}い{f|椅子|いす}<b>だの</b>が{f|放|ほう}り{f|込|こ}んであった。
The storeroom had broken desks and old chairs and the rest of it dumped in.
</pre>

A minutes-taker writes `や`. Someone describing a junk room writes `だの`. This is
why `だの` almost never appears in official notices, manuals or academic prose: it
editorialises, and those genres are not allowed to.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ お{f|客様|きゃくさま}から、{f|色|いろ}<b>だの</b>サイズ<b>だの</b>のご{f|要望|ようぼう}をいただきました。 → ✅ お{f|客様|きゃくさま}から、{f|色|いろ}<b>や</b>サイズについてご{f|要望|ようぼう}をいただきました。
  The polite frame and the sneer contradict each other. Keeping ご{f|要望|ようぼう}
  while dismissing the requests with `だの` is worse than plain rudeness - it
  sounds sarcastic.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|映画|えいが}<b>だの</b>{f|食事|しょくじ}<b>だの</b>、{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|楽|たの}しかった。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|映画|えいが}<b>とか</b>{f|食事|しょくじ}<b>とか</b>、{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|楽|たの}しかった。
  `だの` cannot carry enjoyment. Listing a good day with it makes the speaker
  sound as if they are complaining about their own fun.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|忙|いそが}しいだ<b>だの</b>{f|眠|ねむ}いだ<b>だの</b>と{f|言|い}う。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|忙|いそが}しい<b>だの</b>{f|眠|ねむ}い<b>だの</b>と{f|言|い}う。
  An い-adjective attaches in its plain form. The extra `だ` comes from thinking of
  `だの` as `だ` + `の`, which is not how it behaves after
  <code>{f|忙|いそが}しい</code>.

- ❌ {f|荷物|にもつ}<b>だの</b>で{f|部屋|へや}が{f|狭|せま}い。 → ✅ {f|荷物|にもつ}<b>だの</b>{f|本|ほん}<b>だの</b>で{f|部屋|へや}が{f|狭|せま}い。
  One `だの` is not the pattern. It comes in pairs, or ends with
  <code>だのなんだの</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|母|はは}は{f|部屋|へや}が{f|汚|きたな}い____{f|帰|かえ}りが{f|遅|おそ}い____と、{f|会|あ}うたびに{f|小言|こごと}を{f|言|い}う。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|汚|きたな}い<b>だの</b>{f|遅|おそ}い<b>だの</b>と

Two quoted complaints, and the speaker plainly finds them tedious. That combination
- other people's words, reported without respect - is `だの`'s core use.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|夏休|なつやす}みは{f|海|うみ}（だの・とか）{f|花火|はなび}（だの・とか）で{f|忙|いそが}しかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|海|うみ}<b>とか</b>{f|花火|はなび}<b>とか</b>

A summer of beaches and fireworks is being remembered fondly, and `とか` is the
neutral list. `だの` would imply the speaker had been dragged along and resented
every minute - possible as a joke, but not the plain reading.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He says the pay is low and the commute is long, but he never actually looks for another job."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|給料|きゅうりょう}が{f|安|やす}い<b>だの</b>{f|通勤|つうきん}が{f|長|なが}い<b>だの</b>と{f|言|い}うわりに、{f|転職|てんしょく}{f|活動|かつどう}は{f|一度|いちど}もしていない。

Two grumbles quoted by a speaker who does not accept them, closed with
<code>と{f|言|い}う</code>. English carries the scepticism in the second clause;
Japanese has already carried it in `だの`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|新|あたら}しい{f|企画|きかく}について、{f|社長|しゃちょう}は{f|予算|よさん}だの{f|人員|じんいん}だのを{f|検討|けんとう}してくださいました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|新|あたら}しい{f|企画|きかく}について、{f|社長|しゃちょう}は{f|予算|よさん}<b>や</b>{f|人員|じんいん}を{f|検討|けんとう}してくださいました。

The honorific くださいました shows respect while `だの` sneers at what was
considered. Use `や` when the list itself has to stay respectful or neutral.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|台所|だいどころ}は{f|鍋|なべ}やら{f|皿|さら}やらで{f|足|あし}の{f|踏|ふ}み{f|場|ば}もない。
(b) {f|台所|だいどころ}は{f|鍋|なべ}だの{f|皿|さら}だので{f|足|あし}の{f|踏|ふ}み{f|場|ば}もない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) puts the speaker inside the chaos: there was too much of it to sort out, and
the tone is closer to bewilderment than to blame. It fits a kitchen the speaker
themselves has just cooked in.

(b) is a judgement passed from outside. The pans and plates are being written off
as clutter, and someone - probably not the speaker - is being held responsible for
them.

</details>
