# ～にひきかえ

**Meaning** · in contrast to; unlike - two comparable things are set side by side, and the speaker makes it plain which one comes off better

The verb behind it is {f|引|ひ}き{f|換|か}える, "to exchange one thing for another".
`にひきかえ` swaps one item for its opposite number and invites you to look at the
gap. It is never neutral. Somebody is being praised and somebody is being found
wanting, and the sentence does not pretend otherwise - which is why it so often
comes out of the mouth of a parent, a boss or a disappointed customer.

## Formation

A noun goes in front. Anything else has to be turned into a noun phrase with `の`
first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + にひきかえ | {f|兄|あに} → {f|兄|あに}にひきかえ |
| Noun (fuller) | Noun + であるのにひきかえ | {f|好調|こうちょう} → {f|好調|こうちょう}であるのにひきかえ |
| Verb | plain form + のにひきかえ | {f|売|う}れている → {f|売|う}れているのにひきかえ |
| い-adjective | plain form + のにひきかえ | {f|明|あか}るい → {f|明|あか}るいのにひきかえ |
| な-adjective | ～な + のにひきかえ | {f|静|しず}かな → {f|静|しず}かなのにひきかえ |
| Variant | ～にひきかえて | {f|去年|きょねん}にひきかえて |
| Kanji spelling | ～に{f|引|ひ}き{f|換|か}え | {f|以前|いぜん}に{f|引|ひ}き{f|換|か}え |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the two things compared have to be **the same kind of thing**:
two people, two periods, two products, two departments. A sentence that compares the
weather with an exam has nothing to swap, and `にひきかえ` will sound broken.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - it is usually written in kana. Do not be thrown by
{f|代金|だいきん}{f|引換|ひきかえ} on a delivery slip - that is the same verb used
literally, "cash on delivery", and has nothing to do with this pattern.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`にひきかえ` is **formal-leaning and a shade literary**. It is at home in essays,
columns, reviews and business commentary, and it does occur in speech - but in the
careful register, and with a faint air of pronouncement. Everyday conversation uses
<code>とは{f|違|ちが}って</code> or simply でも.

The thing to internalise is that the pattern **takes sides**. Where
<code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> can report a contrast with no opinion in it,
`にひきかえ` puts the speaker's verdict on the page: this one is good and that one
is not. Because the comparison is usually unflattering to one party, saying it about
somebody in their hearing is a genuinely unkind thing to do -
「{f|お姉|ねえ}ちゃんにひきかえ、あなたは…」 is the classic parental line that children
remember for thirty years.

There is a milder use where nobody is hurt: comparing two summers, two flats, two
periods of one's own life. Even there the speaker is clearly preferring one over the
other. If you need a comparison with no preference in it, this is the wrong pattern.

## Contrast

### ～にひきかえ vs ～に{f|対|たい}して

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> reports a contrast and takes no
view; it is happy with figures. `にひきかえ` **evaluates**, and needs the speaker to
prefer one side.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昨年|さくねん}の{f|輸出|ゆしゅつ}が3%{f|減|げん}だったの<b>に{f|対|たい}し</b>、{f|今年|ことし}は5%{f|増|ぞう}となった。
Against a 3 per cent fall in exports last year, this year saw a 5 per cent rise.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|不振|ふしん}だった{f|昨年|さくねん}<b>にひきかえ</b>、{f|今年|ことし}の{f|営業|えいぎょう}{f|部|ぶ}はよくやっている。
After last year's poor showing, the sales team have done well this year.
</pre>

The first sentence is a table read out loud, and swapping in `にひきかえ` would make
a statistical report sound as though it were grading the two years. The second has
a judgement in it - {f|不振|ふしん}, よくやっている - and that is `にひきかえ`'s
natural habitat. <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> also handles contrasts between
things that are not really rivals; `にひきかえ` does not.

### ～にひきかえ vs ～とは{f|違|ちが}って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same comparison, different register and different manners.
<code>とは{f|違|ちが}って</code> is ordinary spoken Japanese and can be perfectly
kind; `にひきかえ` is written, and carries a verdict.

</div>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}<b>とは{f|違|ちが}って</b>、{f|今年|ことし}は{f|会場|かいじょう}が{f|広|ひろ}くて{f|助|たす}かる。
Unlike last year, the venue this time is nice and big - a relief.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|手|て}ぜまだった{f|去年|きょねん}<b>にひきかえ</b>、{f|今年|ことし}の{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|申|もう}し{f|分|ぶん}がない。
Set against last year's cramped hall, this year's venue leaves nothing to be desired.
</pre>

Both work here, and the difference is one of tone: the first is a remark to a
colleague, the second is a line in the write-up. Where they really part company is
in politeness. <code>あなたとは{f|違|ちが}って</code> is already blunt;
「あなたにひきかえ」 is worse, because it does not merely note a difference but ranks
the two of you.

### ～にひきかえ vs {f|一方|いっぽう}（で）

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}</code> puts two statements **side by
side as equals**. `にひきかえ` makes one of them the **background** against which the
other is measured.

</div>

<pre>
{f|都市部|としぶ}では{f|人口|じんこう}が{f|増|ふ}えている。<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}</b>、{f|地方|ちほう}では{f|減少|げんしょう}が{f|続|つづ}く。
Population is rising in the cities. In rural areas, meanwhile, it continues to fall.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人口|じんこう}が{f|増|ふ}え{f|続|つづ}ける{f|都市部|としぶ}<b>にひきかえ</b>、{f|地方|ちほう}の{f|過疎化|かそか}は{f|深刻|しんこく}だ。
Against the cities and their growing populations, rural depopulation is severe.
</pre>

<code>{f|一方|いっぽう}</code> is grammatically independent - two sentences, two
equal halves, no judgement - and it is the safe choice in a neutral report.
`にひきかえ` subordinates the first item to the second and colours it, which is what
{f|深刻|しんこく}だ in the example is already doing.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|兄|あに}はまじめだ<b>にひきかえ</b>、{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|遊|あそ}んでばかりだ。 → ✅ まじめな{f|兄|あに}<b>にひきかえ</b>、{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|遊|あそ}んでばかりだ。
  It attaches to a noun, not to a finished predicate. Either put the noun in front,
  or nominalise the clause: <code>まじめであるのにひきかえ</code>.

- ❌ {f|天気|てんき}<b>にひきかえ</b>、{f|試験|しけん}は{f|難|むずか}しかった。 → ✅ {f|天気|てんき}はよかったが、{f|試験|しけん}は{f|難|むずか}しかった。
  The weather and an exam are not two of the same thing, so there is nothing to
  exchange. The pattern needs a genuine pair.

- ❌ {f|昨年|さくねん}の{f|数値|すうち}<b>にひきかえ</b>、{f|今年|ことし}は3%{f|増|ふ}えた。 → ✅ {f|昨年|さくねん}の{f|数値|すうち}<b>に{f|対|たい}し</b>、{f|今年|ことし}は3%{f|増|ふ}えた。
  A bare percentage carries no verdict. `にひきかえ` would make the report sound as
  if it were disappointed in last year.

- ❌ {f|山田|やまだ}さん<b>にひきかえ</b>、あなたは{f|遅|おそ}いですね。（said to the person） → ✅ {f|山田|やまだ}さんはもう{f|終|お}わったそうですよ。
  Not ungrammatical - unkind. Ranking the listener below a named third party to
  their face is about as direct as Japanese criticism gets.

## Practice

**1.** {f|静|しず}かだった{f|以前|いぜん}のこの{f|町|まち}____、{f|今|いま}は{f|観光客|かんこうきゃく}であふれている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|静|しず}かだった{f|以前|いぜん}のこの{f|町|まち}<b>にひきかえ</b>、{f|今|いま}は{f|観光客|かんこうきゃく}であふれている。

Two states of one town, and the speaker plainly preferred the earlier one - a
comparable pair plus a verdict, which is what the pattern is for. The thing in front
is a noun phrase, so `の` is not needed.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|輸入|ゆにゅう}が{f|前年|ぜんねん}{f|並|な}みだったの（にひきかえ・に{f|対|たい}し）、{f|輸出|ゆしゅつ}は2%{f|伸|の}びた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|輸入|ゆにゅう}が{f|前年|ぜんねん}{f|並|な}みだったの<b>に{f|対|たい}し</b>、{f|輸出|ゆしゅつ}は2%{f|伸|の}びた。

The sentence is reporting numbers with no opinion attached, which is
<code>に{f|対|たい}し</code>. `にひきかえ` would smuggle in a judgement about imports
that the figures do not support.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Unlike her cheerful sister, she hardly says a word."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|明|あか}るい{f|姉|あね}<b>にひきかえ</b>、{f|彼女|かのじょ}はほとんど{f|口|くち}をきかない。

Two sisters are exactly the comparable pair the pattern wants, and the English
"unlike" is already doing the evaluating. The い-adjective modifies the noun
directly - {f|明|あか}るい{f|姉|あね} - rather than being nominalised.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|大会|たいかい}は{f|盛|さか}り{f|上|あ}がったにひきかえ、{f|今年|ことし}は{f|客|きゃく}が{f|少|すく}なかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|盛|さか}り{f|上|あ}がった{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|大会|たいかい}<b>にひきかえ</b>、{f|今年|ことし}は{f|客|きゃく}が{f|少|すく}なかった。
（or {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|大会|たいかい}が{f|盛|さか}り{f|上|あ}がった<b>のにひきかえ</b>…）

`にひきかえ` needs a noun in front of it. Either make the verb clause into a
modifier of {f|大会|たいかい}, or nominalise it with `の`. A plain past verb cannot
sit there on its own.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|去年|きょねん}<b>とは{f|違|ちが}って</b>、{f|今年|ことし}の{f|新人|しんじん}はよく{f|質問|しつもん}する。
(b) {f|去年|きょねん}<b>にひきかえ</b>、{f|今年|ことし}の{f|新人|しんじん}はよく{f|質問|しつもん}する。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) notes a difference. It is neutral spoken Japanese, and last year's intake are
not necessarily being criticised - they were simply quieter.

(b) grades them. Last year's group becomes the poor benchmark against which this
year's are being praised, and anyone from last year's intake who overheard it would
be entitled to feel got at. The register is also a step more written, the kind of
line that turns up in a manager's report rather than in the break room.

</details>
