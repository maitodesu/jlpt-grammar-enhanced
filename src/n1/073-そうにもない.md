# ～そうにもない

**Meaning** · shows no sign of happening; doesn't look at all likely - judged from what the speaker can see in front of them

This is the negative of the "looks like it" そうだ, the one that attaches to the
ます-stem. {f|止|や}みそうにもない is a verdict on the sky: not "it will not stop" but
"there is nothing in the look of it that suggests stopping". The も is emphasis - the
plain form is そうにない, and adding も takes it from *unlikely* to *no sign
whatsoever*.

## Formation

The base is the **ます-stem**. That is what makes it the appearance そうだ rather
than the hearsay one.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + そうにもない | {f|止|や}む → {f|止|や}みそうにもない |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + そうにもない | {f|晴|は}れる → {f|晴|は}れそうにもない |
| Verb (する) | し + そうにもない | {f|回復|かいふく}する → {f|回復|かいふく}しそうにもない |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | **き** + そうにもない | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|き}そうにもない |
| Potential verb | potential stem + そうにもない | {f|勝|か}てる → {f|勝|か}てそうにもない |
| Weaker variants | ～そうにない／～そうもない | {f|終|お}わる → {f|終|お}わりそうにない |
| Polite | ～そうにもありません | {f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}う → {f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}いそうにもありません |
| Before a noun | ～そうにない + Noun | {f|終|お}わる → {f|終|お}わりそうにない{f|作業|さぎょう} |
| い-adjective (different form) | ～く + なさそうだ | おいしい → おいしくなさそうだ |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - three forms, one meaning, rising emphasis:
<code>そうにない</code> → <code>そうもない</code> → <code>そうにもない</code>. All
three are correct and interchangeable in most sentences; the last is the one you
reach for when you want to sound resigned.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}は{f|当分|とうぶん}{f|止|や}み<b>そうにもない</b>。
This rain doesn't look like stopping any time soon.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|仕事|しごと}、{f|今日中|きょうじゅう}には{f|終|お}わり<b>そうにない</b>な。
There's no way this job is getting finished today by the look of it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|渋滞|じゅうたい}がひどくて、{f|待|ま}ち{f|合|あ}わせの{f|時間|じかん}には{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}い<b>そうにもありません</b>。
The traffic is dreadful and I don't see us making the meeting time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|熱|ねつ}が{f|下|さ}がらず、{f|明日|あした}も{f|出社|しゅっしゃ}でき<b>そうにない</b>。
My temperature isn't coming down; it doesn't look as though I'll make it in tomorrow either.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|様子|ようす}では、{f|彼|かれ}のほうから{f|謝|あやま}り<b>そうにもない</b>ね。
Judging by the look on his face, an apology from him isn't coming.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|相手|あいて}が{f|強|つよ}すぎて、この{f|調子|ちょうし}では{f|勝|か}て<b>そうにもない</b>。
They're too strong; at this rate we haven't a hope of winning.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|議論|ぎろん}は{f|平行線|へいこうせん}のままで、{f|今日|きょう}のうちに{f|結論|けつろん}が{f|出|で}<b>そうにありません</b>。
The discussion is going in circles and there is no sign of a conclusion today.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|交渉|こうしょう}は{f|続|つづ}いているものの、{f|来月|らいげつ}までに{f|話|はなし}がまとまり<b>そうにもない</b>。
Talks are continuing, but there is no prospect at all of an agreement by next month.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and extremely common. It fits a text message, a weather bulletin and
a business report equally, and the polite そうにもありません is standard in an email
explaining that you will be late.

What it always carries is **evidence in view**. The speaker is looking at the sky,
the queue, the pile of documents, the other person's expression. That is why it does
not work for your own intentions: ❌{f|明日|あした}は{f|行|い}きそうにない about a
decision you have made is odd, because you are not inferring your own plans from the
outside. It works fine for your own **uncontrollable** states -
{f|眠|ねむ}れそうにない, {f|治|なお}りそうにない, {f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}いそうにない -
because those are things happening to you.

The も is doing what も usually does. {f|終|お}わりそうにない is a flat prediction;
{f|終|お}わりそうにもない adds a shrug to it. That is a tendency rather than a rule,
and in fast speech people use whichever comes out, but the emphatic version pairs
naturally with resigned particles - ね, な, よ - and with adverbs like
{f|当分|とうぶん} and この{f|調子|ちょうし}では.

One structural point worth holding onto: the same そう has two completely separate
grammars. **ます-stem + そう** is appearance; **plain form + そう** is hearsay.
{f|降|ふ}りそうだ means "it looks like rain"; {f|降|ふ}るそうだ means "I hear it will
rain". The negatives diverge accordingly, and mixing them is the classic error.

## Contrast

### ～そうにもない vs ～ないそうだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>そうにもない</code> is what the speaker **sees**.
<code>ないそうだ</code> is what the speaker **heard**. Different base form, different
source of knowledge.

</div>

<pre>
{f|天気|てんき}{f|予報|よほう}によると、{f|明日|あした}は{f|降|ふ}ら<b>ないそうです</b>。
According to the forecast, it isn't going to rain tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|空|そら}を{f|見|み}る{f|限|かぎ}り、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|降|ふ}り<b>そうにもありません</b>。
Looking at the sky, there's no sign of rain today.
</pre>

The first cites a source and would be false if the speaker made it up; the second
reports an impression and can be wrong without anybody having lied. Check the base:
{f|降|ふ}ら**ない**そう is a plain form, so hearsay; {f|降|ふ}り**そう** is a
ます-stem, so appearance.

### ～そうにもない vs ～なさそうだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for **verbs**, the idiomatic negative of the appearance そうだ is
<code>そうにない</code>. <code>なさそうだ</code> is the form for **adjectives and
ある**.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|料理|りょうり}、あまりおいし<b>くなさそう</b>だね。
This dish doesn't look very appetising.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|今日中|きょうじゅう}に{f|戻|もど}り<b>そうにない</b>。
The department head doesn't look like getting back today.
</pre>

Verbs do also produce {f|戻|もど}らなさそう in casual speech, and you will hear it;
そうにない is the neutral, unmarked choice and the one to write. Going the other way
is not available - ❌おいしそうにない is not how Japanese negates an adjective's
appearance.

### ～そうにもない vs ～はずがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>そうにもない</code> reads the **signs**, and could turn out
wrong. <code>はずがない</code> reasons from **what must be true**, and claims the
thing is impossible.

</div>

<pre>
{f|鍵|かぎ}をかけたのだから、{f|誰|だれ}かが{f|入|はい}った<b>はずがない</b>。
I locked the door, so nobody can possibly have got in.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|人手|ひとで}では、{f|今週|こんしゅう}のうちに{f|終|お}わり<b>そうにもない</b>。
With this few people there's no sign of it being done this week.
</pre>

The first is a deduction and admits no exceptions; the second is an estimate and
could be overturned if three more people turned up on Thursday. Learners reach for
はずがない when they mean "it doesn't look like it", which overstates the case
considerably.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|終|お}わる<b>そうにない</b> → ✅ {f|終|お}わり<b>そうにない</b>
  The appearance そう takes the ます-stem. With the dictionary form you have built
  the hearsay そうだ instead, and then negated it in a way that does not work.

- ❌ {f|来|こ}<b>そうにない</b> → ✅ {f|来|き}<b>そうにない</b>
  {f|来|く}る is irregular. Its ます-stem is き, as in {f|来|き}ます.

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|難|むずか}し<b>そうにない</b>。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|難|むずか}し<b>くなさそうだ</b>。
  い-adjectives use the く form plus なさそう. そうにない is for verbs.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|行|い}き<b>そうにない</b>。（about your own plan） → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|休|やす}むつもりだ。
  You do not infer your own decisions from evidence. The pattern needs something you
  are observing rather than choosing.

## Practice

**1.** {f|前|まえ}の{f|人|ひと}の{f|列|れつ}が{f|全然|ぜんぜん}{f|進|すす}まない。{f|開演|かいえん}までに{f|入|はい}れ____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|入|はい}れ<b>そうにもない</b>

The queue in front of the speaker is the visible evidence, and the emphatic も
matches the frustration. Note the potential stem {f|入|はい}れ- before そう.

</details>

**2.** Choose: ニュースによると、{f|台風|たいふう}は{f|上陸|じょうりく}（しそうにない・しないそうだ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|台風|たいふう}は{f|上陸|じょうりく}<b>しないそうだ</b>。

The sentence names a source - ニュースによると - so this is hearsay, which takes the
plain form plus そうだ. しそうにない would mean the speaker had looked at the sky and
formed their own impression.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He's still asleep; it doesn't look as though he'll be up in time."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

まだ{f|寝|ね}ている。{f|時間|じかん}までに{f|起|お}き<b>そうにない</b>。

The speaker is looking at him, so this is the appearance そう on the ます-stem
{f|起|お}き-. Adding も（{f|起|お}きそうにもない）would sharpen it into "no chance
whatsoever".

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|工事|こうじ}は{f|来年|らいねん}まで{f|終|お}わるそうにもない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|工事|こうじ}は{f|来年|らいねん}まで<b>{f|終|お}わりそうにもない</b>。
"The building work shows no sign of finishing before next year."

The dictionary form {f|終|お}わる cannot precede the appearance そう. Change it to
the ます-stem {f|終|お}わり.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|雨|あめ}は{f|止|や}み<b>そうにない</b>。
(b) {f|雨|あめ}は{f|止|や}み<b>そうにもない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain reading of the weather: on the evidence, the rain will continue. It is
the neutral form and would be fine in a forecast.

(b) adds the も, and with it a shrug. The speaker has been watching for a while,
sees nothing at all promising, and has given up expecting it to clear. The difference
is emphasis and mood rather than fact, and it is a tendency rather than a rule - but
(b) is the one you would say while putting your umbrella back up.

</details>
