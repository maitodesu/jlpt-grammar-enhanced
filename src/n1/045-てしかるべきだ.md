# ～てしかるべきだ

**Meaning** · it is only right that ~; there ought properly to be ~ - read <code>てしかるべきだ</code>

しかるべき is the old {f|然|しか}るべき, "as it should be" - {f|然|しか}り, "so, thus",
plus the classical べし. <code>てしかるべきだ</code> therefore does not ask for
anything and does not order anything. It asserts that something is **no more than
proper**, and it is nearly always written because the proper thing has *not*
happened.

## Formation

The passive is by far the commonest verb shape, because the sentence is usually
about what ought to be done *to* something rather than by anybody in particular.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (て-form) | ～てしかるべきだ | {f|考慮|こうりょ}する → {f|考慮|こうりょ}してしかるべきだ |
| Verb (passive) - the usual shape | ～されてしかるべきだ | {f|評価|ひょうか}する → {f|評価|ひょうか}されてしかるべきだ |
| ある | あってしかるべきだ | {f|説明|せつめい}があってしかるべきだ |
| Adjective or noun, via である | ～であってしかるべきだ | {f|慎重|しんちょう}だ → {f|慎重|しんちょう}であってしかるべきだ |
| Written ending | ～てしかるべきである | {f|見直|みなお}されてしかるべきである |
| Rhetorical question | ～てしかるべきではないか | {f|配慮|はいりょ}があってしかるべきではないか |
| Before a noun (adjectival use) | しかるべき + Noun | しかるべき{f|処置|しょち}／しかるべき{f|立場|たちば}の{f|人|ひと} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the word has nothing to do with {f|叱|しか}る, "to scold". It is
{f|然|しか}る, an old verb meaning "to be so". The accidental resemblance sends a
lot of learners looking for a telling-off that is not there.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|被害者|ひがいしゃ}への{f|説明|せつめい}が<b>あってしかるべきだ</b>。
There ought, at the very least, to have been an explanation for the victims.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|決定|けってい}は{f|見直|みなお}され<b>てしかるべきである</b>。
This decision ought properly to be reconsidered.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|長年|ながねん}の{f|功績|こうせき}は、もっと{f|評価|ひょうか}され<b>てしかるべきだ</b>。
Years of service like his deserve rather more recognition than they have had.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|税金|ぜいきん}の{f|使|つか}い{f|道|みち}については、{f|住民|じゅうみん}に{f|十分|じゅうぶん}な{f|情報|じょうほう}が{f|示|しめ}され<b>てしかるべきだ</b>。
On the use of public money, residents ought to be given full information.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|安全|あんぜん}に{f|関|かか}わる{f|判断|はんだん}である{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|現場|げんば}の{f|意見|いけん}は{f|尊重|そんちょう}され<b>てしかるべきだ</b>ろう。
Since it is a judgement that bears on safety, the view from the site ought surely to be respected.
</pre>

<pre>
「{f|知|し}らなかった」で{f|片付|かたづ}けるのではなく、{f|経緯|けいい}が{f|公|おおやけ}に{f|説明|せつめい}され<b>てしかるべきだ</b>。
Rather than disposing of it with "we didn't know", the sequence of events ought to be set out publicly.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|遺族|いぞく}の{f|心情|しんじょう}に{f|対|たい}する{f|配慮|はいりょ}が、もう{f|少|すこ}し<b>あってしかるべきではないか</b>。
Should there not have been rather more consideration for the feelings of the bereaved?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|制度|せいど}が{f|時代|じだい}に{f|合|あ}わなくなったのであれば、{f|速|すみ}やかに{f|改|あらた}められ<b>てしかるべきである</b>。
If the system no longer fits the times, then it ought to be amended without delay.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise this; do not say it.** <code>てしかるべきだ</code> is written
Japanese and effectively nothing else. It belongs to newspaper editorials and
opinion columns, to formal letters of complaint, to committee minutes, to
administrative and legal documents, and to the more solemn kind of speech. Said
out loud to a colleague it sounds as though you are reading aloud from a leader
column - not rude, but distinctly odd. When you want to say the same thing, use
<code>～べきだ</code>, or <code>～て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code>, or
<code>～なければならない</code>.

Two features are worth carrying away from the page. First, it is an **assertion
about propriety, not a request**: the writer is not asking anyone to do anything,
they are declaring what the right state of affairs would be. Second, it almost
always carries an **implied complaint** - the propriety is being asserted precisely
because reality has fallen short. <code>{f|説明|せつめい}があってしかるべきだ</code>
means, in practice, "and there wasn't one".

The one part of the word a learner really will use is the **adjectival**
しかるべき, which is alive and well in formal speech and email:
<code>しかるべき{f|処置|しょち}を{f|取|と}る</code> ("take appropriate measures"),
<code>しかるべき{f|部署|ぶしょ}にお{f|伝|つた}えします</code> ("I'll pass this to
the relevant department"). That much is ordinary business Japanese. The sentence
ending is not.

## Contrast

### ～てしかるべきだ vs ～べきだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>べきだ</code> is what you **say**;
<code>てしかるべきだ</code> is what a leader column **writes**. The meaning is
close; the register is not, and the longer form adds "and it is only proper".

</div>

<pre>
{f|会社|かいしゃ}はきちんと{f|説明|せつめい}<b>するべきだ</b>。
The company ought to give a proper explanation.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|利用者|りようしゃ}に{f|対|たい}し、{f|十分|じゅうぶん}な{f|説明|せつめい}がなされ<b>てしかるべきである</b>。
A full explanation ought properly to be given to users.
</pre>

The first can be said at a meeting or written in an email. The second cannot be said
at all without sounding like a quotation. If you are choosing which to produce, the
answer is always べきだ.

### ～てしかるべきだ vs ～て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> is about what is
**naturally to be expected**; <code>てしかるべきだ</code> is about what is
**right**. Expectation versus propriety.

</div>

<pre>
あれだけ{f|待|ま}たされたのだから、{f|怒|おこ}っ<b>て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>。
After being kept waiting that long, of course he's angry.
</pre>

<pre>
これほどの{f|不手際|ふてぎわ}である{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|責任者|せきにんしゃ}は{f|処分|しょぶん}され<b>てしかるべきだ</b>。
Given a failure on this scale, those responsible ought properly to face sanction.
</pre>

{f|当然|とうぜん}だ predicts and excuses - anyone would react that way. しかるべきだ
judges - this is what justice requires. Note also that
{f|当然|とうぜん}だ is at home in speech, where しかるべきだ is not.

### ～てしかるべきだ vs ～なければならない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>なければならない</code> is **necessity**: it must
happen, or something breaks. <code>てしかるべきだ</code> is **propriety**: it ought
to happen, because that is what is right. Weaker in force, heavier in tone.

</div>

<pre>
{f|申請書|しんせいしょ}は{f|今週|こんしゅう}{f|中|ちゅう}に{f|出|だ}さ<b>なければなりません</b>。
The application has to be submitted this week.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|申請|しんせい}の{f|理由|りゆう}は、{f|本人|ほんにん}に{f|説明|せつめい}され<b>てしかるべきだ</b>。
The reasons for the decision on the application ought to be explained to the applicant.
</pre>

The first states a hard requirement with a consequence behind it. The second states
what would be decent, and quietly notes that it did not happen.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|考慮|こうりょ}する<b>しかるべきだ</b> → ✅ {f|考慮|こうりょ}し<b>てしかるべきだ</b>
  The base is the て-form. A dictionary-form verb in front of しかるべき is the
  commonest structural error.

- ❌ {f|静|しず}か<b>でしかるべきだ</b> → ✅ {f|静|しず}か<b>であってしかるべきだ</b>
  Adjectives and nouns connect through である, not through the plain で.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}までに{f|出|だ}し<b>てしかるべきだ</b>（said to a colleague at the next desk） → ✅ {f|明日|あした}までに{f|出|だ}す<b>べきだ</b>
  Not a grammar error, a register error. The long form makes an office remark sound
  like a published opinion.

- ❌ Reading しかるべき as a form of {f|叱|しか}る → the word is {f|然|しか}るべき.
  There is no scolding in it; the sense is "as things ought to be".

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to meet this sentence? Choose one:
{f|被害者|ひがいしゃ}への{f|補償|ほしょう}は、{f|速|すみ}やかに{f|行|おこな}われ<b>てしかるべきである</b>。
(a) {f|友人|ゆうじん}との{f|会話|かいわ}　(b) {f|新聞|しんぶん}の{f|社説|しゃせつ}　(c) {f|子供|こども}{f|向|む}けの{f|絵本|えほん}

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b) {f|新聞|しんぶん}の{f|社説|しゃせつ} - a newspaper editorial.

Everything about the sentence points there: the passive
{f|行|おこな}われ, the adverb {f|速|すみ}やかに, the ending である, and
てしかるべき itself. In conversation it would sound like a quotation, and in a
children's book it would be incomprehensible.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese, keeping the meaning:
{f|住民|じゅうみん}の{f|意見|いけん}はもっと{f|尊重|そんちょう}され<b>てしかるべきだ</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|住民|じゅうみん}の{f|意見|いけん}をもっと{f|尊重|そんちょう}<b>するべきだ</b>。
（or {f|住民|じゅうみん}の{f|意見|いけん}をもっと{f|大切|たいせつ}にしたほうがいい。）

べきだ carries the same "ought" with none of the editorial weight. Notice that the
rewrite is easier in the active voice: modern spoken Japanese does not lean on the
passive the way this pattern does.

</details>

**3.** {f|事故|じこ}から{f|一年|いちねん}が{f|過|す}ぎたが、{f|会社|かいしゃ}からは{f|一言|ひとこと}の{f|謝罪|しゃざい}もない。せめて{f|説明|せつめい}くらい____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

せめて{f|説明|せつめい}くらい<b>あってしかるべきだ</b>

<code>あってしかるべきだ</code> - "there ought at least to have been" - is the
single commonest collocation of the pattern, and せめて…くらい in front of it makes
the implied complaint explicit.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|専門家|せんもんか}の{f|意見|いけん}を{f|聞|き}くしかるべきだった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|専門家|せんもんか}の{f|意見|いけん}を<b>{f|聞|き}いてしかるべきだった</b>。
（more naturally: {f|専門家|せんもんか}の{f|意見|いけん}を{f|聞|き}くべきだった。）

しかるべき attaches to the て-form, so {f|聞|き}く has to become {f|聞|き}いて. In
practice, though, a sentence about what one person should have done is better served
by べきだった - the しかるべき version wants an institution as its subject.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|説明|せつめい}<b>するべきだ</b>。
(b) {f|会社|かいしゃ}からの{f|説明|せつめい}が<b>あってしかるべきだ</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a direct opinion in ordinary register. It names the company as the actor and
tells it what to do; you could say it in a meeting or write it in an email.

(b) is a written verdict. The passive-flavoured
{f|説明|せつめい}があって backgrounds the actor, てしかるべきだ frames the demand as
a matter of propriety rather than of preference, and the whole sentence carries the
unspoken complaint that no explanation was given. It belongs on an opinion page.

</details>
