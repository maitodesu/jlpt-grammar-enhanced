# ～並み

**Meaning** · on a par with ~; of ~ grade - measures something against a named standard and says it reaches that level, no more and no less

Read <code>{f|並|な}み</code>. It is a noun suffix, not a grammatical ending: you
attach it to a word that names a **recognised level** - プロ, {f|大人|おとな},
{f|例年|れいねん}, {f|人|ひと} - and the compound behaves like an ordinary noun.
The measurement is *equality*, which is why the same word can be a compliment to an
amateur and a slight to an expert.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun naming a standard | ～{f|並|な}み | プロ → プロ{f|並|な}み |
| Modifying a noun | ～{f|並|な}みの + {f|名詞|めいし} | プロ{f|並|な}みの{f|腕前|うでまえ} |
| Modifying a verb or adjective | ～{f|並|な}みに + … | {f|大人|おとな}{f|並|な}みに{f|働|はたら}く |
| As predicate | ～{f|並|な}みだ／です | {f|気温|きおん}は{f|例年|れいねん}{f|並|な}みだ |
| Benchmark periods | {f|例年|れいねん}／{f|平年|へいねん}／{f|前年|ぜんねん} + {f|並|な}み | {f|前年|ぜんねん}{f|並|な}みの{f|売上|うりあげ} |
| Set compounds | {f|人|ひと}{f|並|な}み, {f|世間|せけん}{f|並|な}み | {f|人|ひと}{f|並|な}みの{f|生活|せいかつ} |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the noun in front must name a **class or benchmark** that the
listener can already picture as a level: a profession, an age group, a typical year,
"most people", a named product. It is not a free suffix you can bolt onto any noun.
❌<code>{f|机|つくえ}{f|並|な}みの{f|重|おも}さ</code> fails because a desk is not a
recognised unit of heaviness; <code>{f|大人|おとな}{f|並|な}みの{f|重|おも}さ</code>
works because adults are.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - a second <code>{f|並|な}み</code> means "a row", and it is a different
word-formation: {f|町並|まちな}み (a row of houses), {f|山並|やまな}み (a mountain
range), {f|軒並|のきな}み (eaves in a row → *across the board, every one of them*).
And beware <code>{f|月|つき}{f|並|な}み</code>, which is frozen with the meaning
**trite, hackneyed** - <code>{f|月|つき}{f|並|な}みな{f|表現|ひょうげん}</code> is a
criticism, and has nothing to do with months.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}の{f|料理|りょうり}はプロ<b>{f|並|な}み</b>だ。
My little brother's cooking is on a par with a professional's.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今年|ことし}の{f|夏|なつ}の{f|暑|あつ}さは{f|例年|れいねん}<b>{f|並|な}み</b>だそうだ。
This summer's heat is apparently about the same as an average year's.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|中学生|ちゅうがくせい}のころから{f|大人|おとな}<b>{f|並|な}みに</b>{f|働|はたら}いていた。
From his middle-school days he was working as hard as a grown man.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|値段|ねだん}で{f|専門店|せんもんてん}<b>{f|並|な}みの</b>{f|味|あじ}が{f|楽|たの}しめる。
At this price you get flavour on a par with a specialist shop.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人|ひと}<b>{f|並|な}みの</b>{f|暮|く}らしができれば、それで{f|十分|じゅうぶん}だと{f|思|おも}っている。
If I can live the way most people do, I think that is enough.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}の{f|影響|えいきょう}で、{f|市内|しない}の{f|店|みせ}は<b>{f|軒並|のきな}み</b>{f|休業|きゅうぎょう}となった。
Because of the typhoon, the shops in town closed one and all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新型|しんがた}の{f|電池|でんち}は、{f|容量|ようりょう}こそ{f|従来|じゅうらい}{f|品|ひん}<b>{f|並|な}み</b>だが、{f|寿命|じゅみょう}が{f|二倍|にばい}になったという。
The new battery only matches the old one for capacity, they say, but it lasts twice as long.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|入社|にゅうしゃ}{f|三年|さんねん}{f|目|め}で、すでに{f|課長|かちょう}<b>{f|並|な}みの</b>{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|任|まか}されているらしい。
In his third year with the company he is apparently already handling work on a par with a manager's.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and much more common than its N1 label suggests. Weather forecasts
run on it ({f|平年|へいねん}{f|並|な}みの{f|気温|きおん}), so do business reports
({f|前年|ぜんねん}{f|並|な}みの{f|売上|うりあげ}) and advertising
({f|専門店|せんもんてん}{f|並|な}みの{f|品|しな}ぞろえ). Nothing about it is
literary.

The thing to feel is the **ceiling**. <code>{f|並|な}み</code> claims equality and
implicitly denies anything beyond it, so where the standard is above the subject it
flatters - an amateur cook who is プロ{f|並|な}み has been paid a real compliment -
and where the standard is at or below the subject it damns.
<code>この{f|店|みせ}の{f|寿司|すし}はスーパー{f|並|な}みだ</code> is a bad review, and
telling a professional their work is プロ{f|並|な}み would be heard as saying they
have only just cleared the bar.

Two set compounds are worth knowing as vocabulary.
<code>{f|人|ひと}{f|並|な}み</code> means "like everybody else, ordinary", and is
usually neutral or quietly positive:
<code>{f|人|ひと}{f|並|な}みに{f|幸|しあわ}せです</code> is a modest, contented thing
to say, and <code>{f|人|ひと}{f|並|な}み{f|以上|いじょう}</code> is the standard way
to say "above average". <code>{f|世間|せけん}{f|並|な}み</code> means "in line with
what society does", and often carries a hint that the speaker finds the standard a
little constraining.

There is also a bare {f|並|なみ}, an ordinary noun meaning "the standard grade" -
<code>{f|並|なみ}の{f|選手|せんしゅ}ではない</code>, "no ordinary player", and on old
menus {f|上|じょう}・{f|中|ちゅう}・{f|並|なみ} for the three grades of a dish. The derived verb
<code>{f|並外|なみはず}れる</code> ("to be out of the ordinary") comes from the same
noun and is common in praise:
<code>{f|並外|なみはず}れた{f|記憶力|きおくりょく}</code>.

## Contrast

### ～{f|並|な}み vs ～{f|級|きゅう}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|並|な}み</code> says the thing **measures up to** a
standard. <code>{f|級|きゅう}</code> says it **belongs to** a rank, and it scales
upward without a ceiling.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|走|はし}りはオリンピック<b>{f|級|きゅう}</b>だ。
His running is Olympic class.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|走|はし}りは{f|実業団|じつぎょうだん}の{f|選手|せんしゅ}<b>{f|並|な}み</b>だ。
His running is on a par with a corporate-team athlete's.
</pre>

<code>{f|級|きゅう}</code> assigns membership of a class and sounds like a
classification - <code>{f|世界|せかい}{f|級|きゅう}</code>,
<code>{f|一流|いちりゅう}{f|級|きゅう}</code> - so it fits records, storms and
talent. <code>{f|並|な}み</code> lines two things up side by side and states that
they match. That is why <code>{f|並|な}み</code> readily takes an everyday standard
(スーパー{f|並|な}み, {f|去年|きょねん}{f|並|な}み) where
<code>{f|級|きゅう}</code> would sound absurd.

### ～{f|並|な}み vs ～どおり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|並|な}み</code> matches a **level or amount**.
<code>どおり</code> matches a **plan, expectation or manner**. Compare
<code>{f|例年|れいねん}{f|並|な}み</code> with
<code>{f|例年|れいねん}どおり</code>.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今年|ことし}の{f|桜|さくら}は<b>{f|例年|れいねん}どおり</b>{f|四月|しがつ}{f|初|はじ}めに{f|咲|さ}いた。
The cherries bloomed at the start of April, as they do every year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今年|ことし}の{f|花見|はなみ}{f|客|きゃく}の{f|数|かず}は<b>{f|例年|れいねん}{f|並|な}み</b>だった。
The number of blossom-viewers was about the same as in a normal year.
</pre>

The first is about following the usual course; the second is about hitting the usual
figure. Where the sentence has a number, a temperature or a volume in it,
<code>{f|並|な}み</code> is what you want; where it has a schedule, a procedure or a
promise, <code>どおり</code> is. Note the reading:
<code>どおり</code> with rendaku after a noun, never ❌とおり in this compound.

### ～{f|並|な}み vs ～ほど・～くらい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ほど</code> and <code>くらい</code> compare inside a
**clause** and are flexible about what they compare. <code>{f|並|な}み</code> builds
a **compound noun** and needs the standard to be a recognised class.

</div>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さん<b>ほど</b>{f|速|はや}くは{f|泳|およ}げません。
I can't swim as fast as Tanaka.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|中学生|ちゅうがくせい}<b>{f|並|な}みに</b>しか{f|泳|およ}げません。
I can only swim about as well as a middle-schooler.
</pre>

Only <code>ほど</code> works with a named individual in an ordinary sentence, and only
<code>ほど</code> pairs with a negative to mean "not as ... as".
<code>{f|並|な}み</code> can take a person's name if that person is famous enough to
function as a yardstick - <code>あの{f|選手|せんしゅ}{f|並|な}みの{f|肩|かた}</code>
about a legendary throwing arm - but that is the exception, and it is doing
comparison-to-a-standard, not comparison-to-a-friend.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ プロ<b>{f|並|な}みだの</b>{f|腕前|うでまえ} → ✅ プロ<b>{f|並|な}みの</b>{f|腕前|うでまえ}
  The compound is a noun, so it modifies another noun with `の`, and modifies a verb
  or adjective with `に`.

- ❌ {f|速|はや}い<b>{f|並|な}み</b>の{f|車|くるま} → ✅ レーシングカー<b>{f|並|な}み</b>の{f|車|くるま}
  <code>{f|並|な}み</code> attaches to a noun that names a standard, never to an
  adjective or a verb.

- ❌ {f|月|つき}{f|並|な}みの{f|支払|しはら}い（meaning "the monthly payment"） → ✅ {f|毎月|まいつき}の{f|支払|しはら}い
  <code>{f|月|つき}{f|並|な}み</code> is a frozen word meaning *trite*. Asking about
  your <code>{f|月|つき}{f|並|な}みの{f|支払|しはら}い</code> asks about your
  unimaginative payment.

- ❌ {f|世界|せかい}{f|記録|きろく}{f|保持者|ほじしゃ}の{f|泳|およ}ぎは、さすがオリンピック{f|選手|せんしゅ}<b>{f|並|な}み</b>だ。 → ✅ …さすがオリンピック{f|選手|せんしゅ}を<b>{f|上回|うわまわ}る</b>{f|泳|およ}ぎだ。
  Praising someone with a standard they already exceed puts a ceiling on them. The
  compliment lands as a demotion.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|冬|ふゆ}の{f|降雪|こうせつ}{f|量|りょう}は{f|平年|へいねん}____で、{f|交通|こうつう}への{f|影響|えいきょう}は{f|少|すく}なかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|平年|へいねん}<b>{f|並|な}み</b>

A quantity measured against a benchmark year is the forecaster's standard phrase.
<code>{f|平年|へいねん}どおり</code> would be about timing or procedure rather than
about how much snow fell.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|開会式|かいかいしき}は（{f|例年|れいねん}{f|並|な}み・{f|例年|れいねん}どおり）{f|体育館|たいいくかん}で{f|行|おこな}われた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

（{f|例年|れいねん}どおり）{f|体育館|たいいくかん}で{f|行|おこな}われた。

Holding the ceremony in the gym is a matter of following the usual practice, so
<code>どおり</code>. <code>{f|例年|れいねん}{f|並|な}み</code> would need something
countable - the number of guests, the length of the ceremony - to be level with.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "For a first-year student, her presentation was on a par with a graduate student's."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一年生|いちねんせい}なのに、{f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|発表|はっぴょう}は{f|大学院生|だいがくいんせい}<b>{f|並|な}み</b>だった。

The standard - graduate students - is a recognised class, and the subject sits below
it, so the comparison reads as praise. <code>{f|大学院生|だいがくいんせい}{f|並|な}みの{f|発表|はっぴょう}だった</code>
works equally well.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|新|あたら}しいカメラは{f|画質|がしつ}がいい{f|並|な}みで、{f|値段|ねだん}も{f|安|やす}い。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|新|あたら}しいカメラは{f|一眼|いちがん}レフ<b>{f|並|な}みの</b>{f|画質|がしつ}で、{f|値段|ねだん}も{f|安|やす}い。
"The new camera has image quality on a par with an SLR, and it's cheap too."

<code>{f|並|な}み</code> cannot attach to the adjective
<code>いい</code>. It needs a noun that names the level being matched, and then `の`
to modify the following noun.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|英語|えいご}は{f|通訳|つうやく}<b>{f|並|な}み</b>だ。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|英語|えいご}は{f|通訳|つうやく}に<b>{f|負|ま}けない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) places his English level with that of interpreters: equal to them, and by
implication not beyond them. Said of a salesman it is high praise; said of a working
interpreter it would sting.

(b) frames the same comparison as a contest he does not lose, which allows for the
possibility that he is better. It is warmer and more admiring, and it carries none
of the ceiling that <code>{f|並|な}み</code> quietly installs.

</details>
