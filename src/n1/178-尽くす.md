# ～尽くす

**Meaning** · do it exhaustively - keep going until there is none of it left. Read {f|尽|つ}くす.

Attached to the stem of another verb, <code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code> says the action
covered **all of it**: every snack, every square metre, every avenue, every last
scrap of strength. The focus is not on how long the doer kept going but on the
object being used up. This is the dignified, written-leaning end of the
"to the limit" family - the shouty colloquial end is
<code>～まくる</code>, and the two are almost never interchangeable.

## Formation

The stem is the ます-stem. <code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code> is a う-verb and conjugates
as one.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + {f|尽|つ}くす | {f|使|つか}う → {f|使|つか}い{f|尽|つ}くす |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + {f|尽|つ}くす | {f|食|た}べる → {f|食|た}べ{f|尽|つ}くす |
| Past | ～{f|尽|つ}くした | {f|語|かた}り{f|尽|つ}くした |
| Passive | ～{f|尽|つ}くされる | {f|埋|う}め{f|尽|つ}くされる |
| Potential (usually negative) | ～{f|尽|つ}くせない | {f|言|い}い{f|尽|つ}くせない |
| Intransitive partner | {f|尽|つ}きる (not {f|尽|つ}くす) | {f|力|ちから}が{f|尽|つ}きる |
| As a verb on its own | {f|尽|つ}くす = devote oneself | {f|家族|かぞく}に{f|尽|つ}くす |
| Not possible | a verb with nothing to use up | ❌{f|眠|ねむ}り{f|尽|つ}くす |

<div class="note">

**Note** - this is **semi-productive**, not free. It wants a verb whose object or
territory can be covered completely, and the compounds that really occur are a
recognisable set: {f|食|た}べ{f|尽|つ}くす, {f|飲|の}み{f|尽|つ}くす,
{f|使|つか}い{f|尽|つ}くす, {f|出|だ}し{f|尽|つ}くす, やり{f|尽|つ}くす,
{f|語|かた}り{f|尽|つ}くす, {f|言|い}い{f|尽|つ}くす, {f|知|し}り{f|尽|つ}くす,
{f|調|しら}べ{f|尽|つ}くす, {f|探|さが}し{f|尽|つ}くす, {f|埋|う}め{f|尽|つ}くす,
{f|焼|や}き{f|尽|つ}くす, {f|売|う}り{f|尽|つ}くす, {f|味|あじ}わい{f|尽|つ}くす.
Invent a new one and it will usually sound odd.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - three set phrases use the bare verb rather than the suffix, and they are
worth learning as units: {f|手|て}を{f|尽|つ}くす ("try everything"),
{f|最善|さいぜん}を{f|尽|つ}くす ("do one's very best"),
{f|力|ちから}を{f|尽|つ}くす ("give it everything"). All three are common in formal
speech and in news writing.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
お{f|菓子|かし}は{f|子|こ}どもたちが{f|食|た}べ<b>{f|尽|つ}くした</b>。
The children ate every last one of the sweets.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今月|こんげつ}の{f|予算|よさん}を{f|使|つか}い<b>{f|尽|つ}くして</b>しまった。
We've gone through this month's budget down to the last yen.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|開演|かいえん}{f|前|まえ}から{f|観客|かんきゃく}で{f|埋|う}め<b>{f|尽|つ}くされて</b>いた。
The hall was packed solid with the audience before the curtain even went up.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}はこのあたりの{f|山道|やまみち}を{f|知|し}り<b>{f|尽|つ}くしている</b>。
He knows every path in these hills.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|二人|ふたり}は{f|夜|よる}が{f|明|あ}けるまで{f|語|かた}り<b>{f|尽|つ}くした</b>。
The two of them talked everything out until dawn.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一晩|ひとばん}の{f|火事|かじ}が{f|倉庫|そうこ}を{f|焼|や}き<b>{f|尽|つ}くした</b>。
A single night's fire burned the warehouse to the ground.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|感謝|かんしゃ}の{f|気持|きも}ちは、{f|言葉|ことば}では{f|言|い}い<b>{f|尽|つ}くせません</b>。
Words cannot express everything I feel by way of thanks.
</pre>

<pre>

The president explained that the decision came only after every possible measure had been tried.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code> sounds **weighty and slightly literary**. It is at home
in news copy, formal statements, essays and narration, and it is what a speaker
reaches for when they want the exhaustiveness to sound serious - a search that left
nothing unchecked, a fire that left nothing standing, a conversation that left
nothing unsaid. Said about small everyday things it is faintly grand:
<code>{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}のプリンを{f|食|た}べ{f|尽|つ}くした</code> is funny
precisely because the register is too big for the pudding.

That said, several individual compounds have settled into ordinary use and carry no
special weight. <code>{f|使|つか}い{f|尽|つ}くす</code> and
<code>{f|食|た}べ{f|尽|つ}くす</code> are unremarkable in conversation, and
<code>{f|売|う}り{f|尽|つ}くし</code> is written on shop banners across the
country. Treat the *suffix* as formal and the *frozen compounds* as neutral.

There is no casual contraction, and no gender or age marking. What there is instead
is a register partner: when you want to say someone did something wildly and
repeatedly, in casual speech, the word is <code>まくる</code>, not
<code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code>. Getting these two the wrong way round is the single
most noticeable error with either of them.

Finally, keep the intransitive apart. <code>{f|尽|つ}きる</code> is what happens to
the thing that runs out - {f|力|ちから}が{f|尽|つ}きる, {f|話|はなし}が{f|尽|つ}きない,
{f|燃|も}え{f|尽|つ}きる. <code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code> always needs someone doing the
using up.

## Contrast

### ～{f|尽|つ}くす vs ～{f|切|き}る

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～{f|切|き}る</code> means the action was **carried
through to the end**. <code>～{f|尽|つ}くす</code> means **nothing was left over**.
Often both are true, but they emphasise different halves.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大|おお}きいケーキを{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|食|た}べ<b>{f|切|き}った</b>。
I got through a whole big cake on my own.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|棚|たな}のお{f|菓子|かし}を{f|食|た}べ<b>{f|尽|つ}くした</b>。
They ate every last snack on the shelf.
</pre>

<code>{f|切|き}る</code> looks at one task and reports that it was completed - it
fits a portion, a distance, a book. <code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code> looks at a supply
and reports that it is gone. This is why
<code>{f|走|はし}り{f|切|き}る</code> ("run the whole course") is normal and
❌<code>{f|走|はし}り{f|尽|つ}くす</code> is not: a course is finished, not used up.

### ～{f|尽|つ}くす vs ～{f|抜|ぬ}く

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> is about the **doer's endurance**
through difficulty. <code>～{f|尽|つ}くす</code> is about the **object being
exhausted**, and says nothing about how hard it was.

</div>

<pre>
{f|苦|くる}しかったが、{f|最後|さいご}までやり<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>。
It was hard going, but I saw it through to the end.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試|ため}せる{f|方法|ほうほう}はやり<b>{f|尽|つ}くした</b>。
We have tried everything there was to try.
</pre>

Swap them and the sentences change subject. <code>やり{f|抜|ぬ}いた</code> praises
the person; <code>やり{f|尽|つ}くした</code> reports that the options are gone -
which is often said just before admitting defeat.

### ～{f|尽|つ}くす vs ～まくる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same family, opposite ends of the register scale.
<code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code> is written and dignified and counts what was used up;
<code>まくる</code> is loudly colloquial and counts how wildly the doer went at it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|連休|れんきゅう}は{f|寝|ね}<b>まくった</b>。
I slept like a log the whole long weekend.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|連休|れんきゅう}の{f|間|あいだ}に{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|読|よ}み<b>{f|尽|つ}くした</b>。
Over the long weekend I got through every one of the documents.
</pre>

Sleeping uses nothing up, so <code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code> cannot take it. Documents
can be finished off, so both would be grammatical with
{f|読|よ}む - but <code>{f|読|よ}みまくった</code> means "I read like mad" and
belongs in a message to a friend, while
<code>{f|読|よ}み{f|尽|つ}くした</code> means "there was nothing left unread" and
belongs in a report.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|食|た}べる<b>{f|尽|つ}くす</b> → ✅ {f|食|た}べ<b>{f|尽|つ}くす</b>
  The base is the ます-stem, not the dictionary form - the same join as
  {f|食|た}べ{f|始|はじ}める or {f|食|た}べ{f|終|お}わる.

- ❌ {f|力|ちから}が<b>{f|尽|つ}くした</b>。 → ✅ {f|力|ちから}が<b>{f|尽|つ}きた</b>。
  When the thing runs out by itself, the verb is the intransitive
  {f|尽|つ}きる. <code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code> needs an agent doing the exhausting.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}はカラオケで{f|歌|うた}い<b>{f|尽|つ}くした</b>よ。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}はカラオケで{f|歌|うた}い<b>まくった</b>よ。
  A casual sentence with よ on the end wants the casual word. Singing a lot at
  karaoke is まくる territory; {f|尽|つ}くす would suggest the songbook was
  exhausted.

- ❌ {f|昼|ひる}まで{f|眠|ねむ}り<b>{f|尽|つ}くした</b>。 → ✅ {f|昼|ひる}まで{f|寝|ね}<b>まくった</b>。
  Sleep has nothing to use up, so no exhaustive reading is available. The suffix is
  not free to attach to any verb you like.

## Practice

**1.** {f|警察|けいさつ}はあらゆる{f|可能性|かのうせい}を{f|調|しら}べ____が、{f|手|て}がかりは{f|見|み}つからなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|調|しら}べ<b>{f|尽|つ}くした</b>

"Every possibility" is a supply that can be worked through completely, which is
what <code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code> reports. It also matches the formal register of a
police investigation being described in writing.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|土曜日|どようび}はゲームをやり（{f|尽|つ}くした・まくった）。{f|楽|たの}しかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ゲームをやり<b>まくった</b>。

The sentence is chatty - short, plain, with a bare {f|楽|たの}しかった after it -
so the colloquial member of the pair fits. <code>やり{f|尽|つ}くした</code> would
mean the games themselves had been finished off, every level and every mode, and
would sound oddly formal next to {f|楽|たの}しかった.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The fire burned the whole forest, leaving nothing."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|火|ひ}は{f|森|もり}を{f|焼|や}き<b>{f|尽|つ}くした</b>。

"Leaving nothing" is carried by <code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code> itself, so there is no
need to translate it separately. {f|焼|や}き{f|尽|つ}くす is one of the most
established compounds in the set and is standard in news reporting.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|三時間|さんじかん}{f|話|はな}したので、もう{f|話題|わだい}を{f|尽|つ}くしてしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|三時間|さんじかん}{f|話|はな}したので、もう{f|話題|わだい}が<b>{f|尽|つ}きて</b>しまった。
"We talked for three hours, so we've run out of things to talk about."

Topics run out on their own; nobody is exhausting them deliberately. That calls for
the intransitive {f|尽|つ}きる with が, not {f|尽|つ}くす with を. The alternative
repair is {f|話題|わだい}を{f|話|はな}し{f|尽|つ}くしてしまった, with a real verb
stem in front.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|最後|さいご}まで{f|走|はし}り<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>。
(b) {f|持|も}てる{f|力|ちから}を{f|出|だ}し<b>{f|尽|つ}くした</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) praises the runner's persistence. The race was hard, and the point of the
sentence is that they did not stop - <code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> always implies
difficulty pushed through.

(b) reports the state of the runner's reserves afterwards: there is nothing left in
the tank. It says nothing about whether the effort was difficult, only that it was
total. The two are often true of the same race, and a sports report may well use
both in consecutive sentences.

</details>
