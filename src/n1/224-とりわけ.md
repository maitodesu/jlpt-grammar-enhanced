# とりわけ

**Meaning** · especially; above all - singling out the one member of a group that
has the quality most

`とりわけ` does not just say "this one is X". It says: the whole set is X already,
and this one is X even more than the rest. Without a set in view, the word has
nothing to do.

## Usage

`とりわけ` is an adverb, and it can sit in two places:

- **In front of the clause**, picking out a whole situation:
  とりわけ{f|今年|ことし}の{f|冬|ふゆ}は{f|厳|きび}しかった。
- **Directly in front of the element it selects**, including a bare noun phrase:
  {f|果物|くだもの}では、とりわけ{f|桃|もも}が{f|好|す}きだ。

The group it selects from is normally spelled out in the sentence before it, or in
the clause before the comma. Very often it teams up with
<code>{f|中|なか}でも</code> - <code>{f|中|なか}でもとりわけ</code> - which makes
the set-and-member logic explicit and is a good frame to memorise.

Two things it is not. It is not a noun, so there is no ❌とりわけの;
and it does not head the "not particularly" frame that
<code>{f|特|とく}に</code> owns. Asked whether you have any requests, you answer
<code>{f|特|とく}にありません</code>, never ❌とりわけありません.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|冬|ふゆ}の{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}は{f|寒|さむ}いが、<b>とりわけ</b>{f|二月|にがつ}が{f|厳|きび}しい。
Hokkaido is cold in winter, and February in particular is brutal.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|果物|くだもの}の{f|中|なか}でも、<b>とりわけ</b>{f|桃|もも}が{f|好|す}きです。
Of all the fruit there is, peaches are the ones I like best.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今年|ことし}の{f|新人|しんじん}はよく{f|働|はたら}くが、<b>とりわけ</b>{f|田中|たなか}さんの{f|伸|の}びが{f|著|いちじる}しい。
This year's new hires all work hard, but Tanaka's progress in particular is striking.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|物価|ぶっか}の{f|上昇|じょうしょう}はどの{f|家庭|かてい}にも{f|影響|えいきょう}するが、<b>とりわけ</b>{f|年金|ねんきん}で{f|暮|く}らす{f|人|ひと}たちには{f|重|おも}い{f|負担|ふたん}となっている。
Rising prices affect every household, but for people living on a pension they are a particularly heavy burden.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Bookish but not stiff. `とりわけ` belongs to newspapers, essays, presentations and
careful spoken Japanese; used in a chat with friends it lands roughly the way
"above all" does in English - understood, slightly formal, faintly literary. It is
not marked for age or gender.

The everyday word is <code>{f|特|とく}に</code>, and it will cover almost every
occasion on which a learner reaches for `とりわけ`. Where `とりわけ` earns its keep
is in writing, because it makes the comparison audible: a reader who meets it knows
immediately that a group has just been narrowed down. In a paragraph that already
listed the group, `とりわけ` is the cleaner choice.

There is a rarer written variant <code>とりわけて</code>, which means the same
thing and sounds older. You need only recognise it.

## Contrast

### とりわけ vs {f|特|とく}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|特|とく}に</code> is the all-purpose word and needs no
group. `とりわけ` is its written cousin and only works when a set is in play; it
also has no negative "not particularly" use.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}は<b>{f|特|とく}に</b>{f|予定|よてい}がありません。
I've got nothing particular on today.
</pre>

<pre>
どの{f|作品|さくひん}もよかったが、<b>とりわけ</b>{f|最後|さいご}の{f|一枚|いちまい}が{f|印象|いんしょう}に{f|残|のこ}った。
Every piece was good, but the last one especially stayed with me.
</pre>

Swap them in the first sentence and you get ❌とりわけ{f|予定|よてい}がありません,
which is broken: there is no set of plans being ranked. Swap them in the second and
nothing breaks - <code>{f|特|とく}に</code> works there too, just with less of the
"of all of them" flavour.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ とりわけ{f|問題|もんだい}はありません。 → ✅ <b>{f|特|とく}に</b>{f|問題|もんだい}はありません。
  The "nothing in particular" answer is fixed to <code>{f|特|とく}に</code>.
  `とりわけ` cannot take that job because there is no group to select from.

- ❌ とりわけの{f|好|す}きな{f|季節|きせつ}は{f|秋|あき}です。 → ✅ {f|季節|きせつ}の{f|中|なか}では、<b>とりわけ</b>{f|秋|あき}が{f|好|す}きです。
  No `の` after an adverb - and notice the repair also supplies the set, which is
  what the word was waiting for.

- ❌ （out of nowhere）とりわけ{f|今日|きょう}は{f|忙|いそが}しい。 → ✅ {f|今週|こんしゅう}はずっと{f|忙|いそが}しいが、<b>とりわけ</b>{f|今日|きょう}はひどい。
  Not ungrammatical, but it leaves the listener waiting for the comparison. State
  the group first; `とりわけ` is the second half of a two-part idea.

## Practice

**1.** {f|彼|かれ}の{f|小説|しょうせつ}はどれもおもしろいが、____{f|初期|しょき}の{f|作品|さくひん}がいい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}の{f|小説|しょうせつ}はどれもおもしろいが、<b>とりわけ</b>{f|初期|しょき}の{f|作品|さくひん}がいい。

The first clause sets up the group - all of his novels - and the second narrows it
to one part of that group, which is exactly the shape this adverb was built for.

</details>

**2.** Choose とりわけ or {f|特|とく}に, and say why:
「{f|何|なに}か{f|希望|きぼう}はありますか。」「いえ、____ございません。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|特|とく}に</b>ございません。

The negative "nothing in particular" reply is a fixed frame belonging to
<code>{f|特|とく}に</code>. Here nothing is being ranked or compared, so `とりわけ`
has no work to do and the sentence would simply sound wrong.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, using とりわけ:
"The whole team played well, but the goalkeeper was outstanding."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

チーム{f|全体|ぜんたい}がよく{f|戦|たたか}ったが、<b>とりわけ</b>キーパーの{f|活躍|かつやく}が{f|目立|めだ}った。

The English "but ... outstanding" hides the same two-part structure: a group that
was all good, then one member above the rest. Note that `とりわけ` goes in front of
the member being singled out, not in front of the verb.

</details>
