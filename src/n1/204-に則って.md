# ～に則って

**Meaning** · in accordance with; in conformity with - what was done followed the letter of a rule, a law, a ceremony's established form or a precedent

<code>{f|則|のっと}る</code> is a verb meaning "to take something as one's standard and
follow it". What stands in front of it is always a **codified norm**: a statute, a
set of regulations, the traditional order of a ceremony, a long-standing custom, an
earlier case treated as binding. The sentence then asserts that the procedure was
correct - not merely sensible, but *proper*. This is the language of legal texts,
company rules and formal ceremonies. Nobody says it about their afternoon.

## Formation

<code>{f|則|のっと}る</code> is a う-verb ({f|五段|ごだん}/godan/Group 1) and takes its
norm with `に`, never `を`. The pattern attaches to a **noun** only.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + に{f|則|のっと}って | {f|法律|ほうりつ} → {f|法律|ほうりつ}に{f|則|のっと}って |
| Noun (stiffer, documents) | Noun + に{f|則|のっと}り | {f|規約|きやく} → {f|規約|きやく}に{f|則|のっと}り |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + に{f|則|のっと}った + Noun | {f|伝統|でんとう} → {f|伝統|でんとう}に{f|則|のっと}った{f|儀式|ぎしき} |
| Same, non-past shape | Noun + に{f|則|のっと}る + Noun | {f|手|て}{f|続|つづ}き → {f|手|て}{f|続|つづ}きに{f|則|のっと}る{f|処理|しょり} |
| Negative | Noun + に{f|則|のっと}らない + Noun | {f|前例|ぜんれい} → {f|前例|ぜんれい}に{f|則|のっと}らない{f|判断|はんだん} |
| Written in kana (common) | Noun + にのっとって | ルール → ルールにのっとって |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the reading. <code>則</code> is ソク in {f|規則|きそく} and
{f|原則|げんそく}, but the verb
{f|則|のっと}る is **のっとる**, and the ～って ending is okurigana outside the kanji:
{f|則|のっと}って. Learners who guess そくって or きそくって from the compound reading
give themselves away instantly.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
ルールに<b>のっとって</b>{f|試合|しあい}が{f|行|おこな}われた。
The match was played by the rules.
</pre>

{f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|規則|きそく}<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>{f|処理|しょり}します。
We will handle it in accordance with company regulations.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|式|しき}は{f|昔|むかし}からの{f|作法|さほう}<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>{f|進|すす}められた。
The ceremony proceeded according to the old forms.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|憲法|けんぽう}<b>に{f|則|のっと}り</b>、{f|国会|こっかい}が{f|召集|しょうしゅう}された。
The Diet was convened in accordance with the constitution.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|制度|せいど}とはいえ、{f|運用|うんよう}はこれまでの{f|前例|ぜんれい}<b>に{f|則|のっと}った</b>{f|形|かたち}になるでしょう。

## Nuance & register

This belongs to contracts, official notices, ceremonies and formal speeches, and
almost nowhere else. You will meet it in a statute, a set of {f|定款|ていかん}, a
press release, a wedding or funeral programme, a sports federation's rulebook. In
ordinary conversation a Japanese speaker says
<code>ルール<b>どおりに</b></code> or <code>{f|決|き}まりに{f|従|したが}って</code>. Say
<code>{f|夕食|ゆうしょく}は{f|母|はは}の{f|方針|ほうしん}に{f|則|のっと}って{f|作|つく}った</code>
and you will get a laugh, because you have dressed a family kitchen in the clothes
of a statute.

The `に則り` shape is if anything the more frequent one in written notices; `に則って`
is what you hear read aloud at a ceremony. Neither has a casual form, and there is
no contraction.

<div class="note">

**Note** - written in kana, `のっとる` is identical to {f|乗|の}っ{f|取|と}る, "to
hijack, to take over". <code>{f|会社|かいしゃ}をのっとる</code> and
<code>{f|規則|きそく}にのっとる</code> are unrelated words that sound the same; the
particle (`を` versus `に`) is what separates them, and it is one reason writers of
formal documents keep the kanji <code>則</code>.

</div>

The pattern carries a faint note of self-justification. Announcing that you acted
<code>{f|規則|きそく}に{f|則|のっと}って</code> is a way of saying that whatever the
outcome, no one can fault the process - which is exactly why organisations reach for
it when something has gone wrong.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|則|のっと}って vs ～に{f|基|もと}づいて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に{f|基|もと}づいて</code> takes anything that can serve as
**grounds**, including data and facts. <code>～に{f|則|のっと}って</code> takes only a
**norm you conform your conduct to**, and it comments on the *manner* of doing.

</div>

<pre>
{f|調査|ちょうさ}{f|結果|けっか}<b>に{f|基|もと}づいて</b>{f|対策|たいさく}を{f|決|き}めた。
We decided on countermeasures on the basis of the survey findings.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|所定|しょてい}の{f|手|て}{f|順|じゅん}<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>{f|対策|たいさく}を{f|決|き}めた。
We decided on countermeasures by following the prescribed procedure.
</pre>

The first says where the content of the decision came from; the second says nothing
about the content and everything about the process. Findings, figures, evidence and
experience are all fine before <code>{f|基|もと}づいて</code> and all impossible
before <code>{f|則|のっと}って</code>, because you cannot *obey* a number.

### ～に{f|則|のっと}って vs ～に{f|従|したが}って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に{f|従|したが}って</code> is the everyday word for
following, and its object can be a person. <code>～に{f|則|のっと}って</code> is its
ceremonial counterpart, and its object must be an impersonal norm.

</div>

<pre>
{f|係|かかり}の{f|人|ひと}の{f|指示|しじ}<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>{f|外|そと}に{f|出|で}てください。
Please go outside, following the instructions of the staff.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|避難|ひなん}{f|訓練|くんれん}は{f|消防法|しょうぼうほう}<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>{f|実施|じっし}されます。
The evacuation drill is conducted in accordance with the fire services act.
</pre>

<code>{f|従|したが}う</code> works at every level of the language: a child follows a
parent's word, a driver follows a sign. <code>{f|則|のっと}る</code> works only where
the thing followed has been written down or handed down. Replace
<code>{f|係|かかり}の{f|人|ひと}の{f|指示|しじ}</code> with
<code>{f|則|のっと}って</code> and it sounds as though the staff member's off-hand
remark had the standing of a statute.

### ～に{f|則|のっと}って vs ～に{f|沿|そ}って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に{f|沿|そ}って</code> keeps to a line without wandering
off it, and the line can be soft - a plan, a theme, somebody's wishes.
<code>～に{f|則|のっと}って</code> demands that the line be a **rule**.

</div>

<pre>
ご{f|要望|ようぼう}<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|日程|にってい}を{f|組|く}み{f|直|なお}しました。
We have rearranged the schedule in line with your wishes.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|表彰|ひょうしょう}{f|式|しき}は{f|例年|れいねん}の{f|次第|しだい}<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>{f|行|おこな}います。
The award ceremony will be held according to the customary order of proceedings.
</pre>

A wish is not a rule, so ❌<code>ご{f|要望|ようぼう}に{f|則|のっと}って</code> is wrong.
Going the other way is merely weaker rather than wrong:
<code>{f|次第|しだい}に{f|沿|そ}って</code> is possible and means the ceremony broadly
tracked the programme, where <code>{f|則|のっと}って</code> insists it was done
properly.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>{f|進|すす}めます。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}の{f|指示|しじ}<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>{f|進|すす}めます。
  A person cannot be a norm. Either follow the person with
  <code>{f|従|したが}って</code>, or make the norm explicit - their instruction, their
  policy - and then <code>{f|則|のっと}る</code> still sounds too grand for one manager.

- ❌ データ<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>{f|結論|けつろん}を{f|出|だ}す → ✅ データ<b>に{f|基|もと}づいて</b>{f|結論|けつろん}を{f|出|だ}す
  Data are grounds, not rules. Nothing that merely *informs* a decision can stand
  before <code>{f|則|のっと}って</code>.

- ❌ {f|規則|きそく}<b>を{f|則|のっと}って</b> → ✅ {f|規則|きそく}<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>
  The verb is intransitive and marks its norm with `に`. Writing `を` here also
  collides with {f|乗|の}っ{f|取|と}る, which really does take `を`.

- ❌ {f|規則|きそく}に<b>{f|則|そく}って</b> → ✅ {f|規則|きそく}に<b>{f|則|のっと}って</b>
  The compound reading ソク does not carry over to the verb. Note too that the
  okurigana って sits outside the furigana group: the kanji alone is のっと.

## Practice

**1.** {f|開票|かいひょう}は{f|公職|こうしょく}{f|選挙|せんきょ}{f|法|ほう}____{f|行|おこな}われた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|公職|こうしょく}{f|選挙|せんきょ}{f|法|ほう}<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>{f|行|おこな}われた。

A statute is the model case for this pattern, and counting votes is exactly the kind
of procedure that has to be certified as properly done. <code>に{f|則|のっと}り</code>
would be equally correct and slightly stiffer.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|過去|かこ}{f|三年|さんねん}の{f|売|う}り{f|上|あ}げ（に{f|則|のっと}って・に{f|基|もと}づいて）{f|来年度|らいねんど}の{f|目標|もくひょう}を{f|立|た}てる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|過去|かこ}{f|三年|さんねん}の{f|売|う}り{f|上|あ}げ<b>に{f|基|もと}づいて</b>

Sales figures are grounds you reason from, not a rule you obey. Only
<code>{f|基|もと}づいて</code> accepts data. Swap in a target-setting *procedure* laid
down by head office and <code>{f|則|のっと}って</code> becomes possible again.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The wedding was held in accordance with Shinto tradition."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|結婚式|けっこんしき}は{f|神道|しんとう}の{f|伝統|でんとう}<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>{f|行|おこな}われた。

A religious tradition is a handed-down norm with a fixed order of acts, which is
precisely what this pattern wants. The noun phrase
<code>{f|伝統|でんとう}に{f|則|のっと}った{f|結婚式|けっこんしき}</code> works just as well.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|審査|しんさ}は{f|委員|いいん}{f|長|ちょう}の{f|気持|きも}ちに{f|則|のっと}って{f|進|すす}められた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|審査|しんさ}は{f|審査|しんさ}{f|基準|きじゅん}<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>{f|進|すす}められた。

A feeling is not a codified norm, and the original sentence accidentally says the
opposite of what such a report wants to say - that the process was subjective. If
the chair's view really was decisive, write
<code>{f|委員|いいん}{f|長|ちょう}の{f|意向|いこう}に{f|沿|そ}って</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会員|かいいん}{f|規約|きやく}<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>{f|退会|たいかい}の{f|手|て}{f|続|つづ}きをした。
(b) {f|会員|かいいん}{f|規約|きやく}<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b>{f|退会|たいかい}の{f|手|て}{f|続|つづ}きをした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the withdrawal was carried out the way the membership rules prescribe: the
rules dictated each step, and the sentence is a claim that nothing was skipped.

(b) says the rules were read and taken into account before acting, which leaves open
the possibility that the person did something the rules do not actually cover. In a
dispute, (a) is the sentence you want on your side.

</details>
