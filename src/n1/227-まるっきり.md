# まるっきり

**Meaning** · totally; not in the slightest - a blunt spoken intensifier that
almost always lands on a negative

`まるっきり` is the sound of someone throwing up their hands. It is emphatic,
colloquial, and it needs something negative to attach to: a `ない`, or a word whose
content is already "not that".

## Usage

The partner is the lesson.

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `まるっきり` must be followed by a negative predicate, or by a
word of negative content: <code>{f|違|ちが}う</code>, <code>{f|別|べつ}</code>,
<code>{f|反対|はんたい}</code>, <code>{f|逆|ぎゃく}</code>, ダメ.

</div>

- With a negative: <code>まるっきり{f|知|し}らない</code>,
  <code>まるっきり{f|通|つう}じなかった</code>
- With a negative-content word: <code>まるっきり{f|別|べつ}の{f|話|はなし}</code>,
  <code>まるっきり{f|反対|はんたい}だ</code>

There is no affirmative use. ❌<code>まるっきり{f|上手|じょうず}だ</code> and
❌<code>まるっきりおいしい</code> are simply wrong, in the same way that "not in the
slightest good" cannot be turned into "in the slightest good".

`まるきり` is the same word without the small つ, and you will hear both. You may
also meet the very casual <code>まるっきし</code>. None of the three is a writing
word.

## Examples

<pre>
その{f|話|はなし}は<b>まるっきり</b>{f|知|し}らなかった。
I had absolutely no idea about that.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|兄弟|きょうだい}なのに、{f|性格|せいかく}は<b>まるっきり</b>{f|違|ちが}う。
They're brothers, but their personalities are nothing alike.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|習|なら}ったはずなのに、<b>まるっきり</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えていないんだよね。
I'm supposed to have learned it, and yet I don't remember a thing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|前|まえ}の{f|職場|しょくば}とは<b>まるっきり</b>{f|別|べつ}の{f|世界|せかい}で、{f|最初|さいしょ}の{f|一年|いちねん}は{f|苦労|くろう}した。
It was a completely different world from my old workplace, and the first year was hard going.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Spoken, casual, and faintly of an older generation. `まるっきり` turns up in
grumbling, in storytelling, in dubbed dialogue and in the speech of people who are
not choosing their words carefully. It is not rude, but it is not neat either, and
it has no place in a report, an email to a client, or an exam essay.

The neutral replacements are <code>{f|全然|ぜんぜん}</code> in speech and
<code>まったく</code> in writing; both do the same work with none of the flavour.
If you want the flavour in writing, put it in a character's mouth.

Younger speakers use it less than older ones - a tendency, not a rule, and you will
still hear it from anyone recounting a total failure. What learners should take
from that is simply this: understanding it matters more than producing it, and if
you do produce it, do so in conversation and nowhere else.

## Contrast

### まるっきり vs まるで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - they overlap on "not at all", and only `まるで` can build a
simile. `まるっきり` is the more colloquial of the two and never means "as if".

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|説明|せつめい}は<b>まるっきり</b>{f|分|わ}からなかった。
I couldn't follow his explanation at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|説明|せつめい}は<b>まるで</b>{f|暗号|あんごう}のようだった。
His explanation was like something in code.
</pre>

Put `まるっきり` into the second sentence and it breaks: there is no
❌<code>まるっきり{f|暗号|あんごう}のようだ</code>. Put `まるで` into the first and
nothing breaks, but the sentence rises a step in register, toward writing.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|映画|えいが}はまるっきりおもしろい。 → ✅ この{f|映画|えいが}は<b>すごく</b>おもしろい。
  There is no positive use. To keep the word, negate the predicate:
  <code>まるっきりおもしろくない</code>.

- ❌ {f|調査|ちょうさ}の{f|結果|けっか}は、{f|両者|りょうしゃ}でまるっきり{f|一致|いっち}しなかった。 → ✅ {f|調査|ちょうさ}の{f|結果|けっか}は、{f|両者|りょうしゃ}で<b>まったく</b>{f|一致|いっち}しなかった。
  Grammatically fine, stylistically wrong. A written report takes
  <code>まったく</code>.

- ❌ {f|彼女|かのじょ}はまるっきり{f|子供|こども}のように{f|泣|な}いた。 → ✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}は<b>まるで</b>{f|子供|こども}のように{f|泣|な}いた。
  Similes belong to `まるで`. `まるっきり` has no comparison use at all.

## Practice

**1.** {f|習|なら}ったばかりの{f|言葉|ことば}なのに、テストでは____{f|出|で}てこなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|習|なら}ったばかりの{f|言葉|ことば}なのに、テストでは<b>まるっきり</b>{f|出|で}てこなかった。

The predicate is negative, the tone is exasperated and the sentence is spoken
Japanese - all three point to this adverb. In a written account you would put
<code>まったく</code> here instead.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: この{f|辞書|じしょ}はまるっきり{f|役|やく}に{f|立|た}つ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|辞書|じしょ}はまるっきり{f|役|やく}に<b>{f|立|た}たない</b>。

Reverse the predicate. As written, the sentence pairs a negative-only intensifier
with a positive statement, which no amount of context can rescue.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}のやり{f|方|かた}とは、まるっきり{f|逆|ぎゃく}です。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}のやり{f|方|かた}とは、まったく{f|逆|ぎゃく}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The information is identical: both say the two methods are opposites. The
difference is the speaker. Sentence (a) sounds like conversation, a little heated,
and would be at home in a complaint over coffee; sentence (b) is neutral and could
appear in a written comparison without anyone noticing the adverb at all.

</details>
