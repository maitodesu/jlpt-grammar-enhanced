# ～には及ばない

**Meaning** · there is no need to go to that trouble - and, with a noun, does not come up to it

{f|及|およ}ぶ means "to reach, to extend as far as". The negative therefore says
the case **does not reach** the point where the action is required: no need to
come, no need to thank me, no need to worry. The same verb gives a second,
comparative use - {f|兄|あに}には{f|及|およ}ばない, "I am not the equal of my
brother" - and the two share one form.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb ("no need to") | dictionary form + には{f|及|およ}ばない | {f|心配|しんぱい}する → {f|心配|しんぱい}するには{f|及|およ}ばない |
| Verb (humble request form) | ～いただくには{f|及|およ}ばない | {f|来|き}ていただく → {f|来|き}ていただくには{f|及|およ}びません |
| Noun ("no need for") | noun + には{f|及|およ}ばない | お{f|礼|れい} → お{f|礼|れい}には{f|及|およ}びません |
| Noun ("is no match for") | noun + には{f|及|およ}ばない | {f|兄|あに} → {f|兄|あに}には{f|及|およ}ばない |
| Noun, は dropped | noun + に{f|及|およ}ばない | {f|先輩|せんぱい} → {f|先輩|せんぱい}に{f|及|およ}ばない |
| Past | ～には{f|及|およ}ばなかった | {f|予想|よそう}には{f|及|およ}ばなかった |
| Polite | ～には{f|及|およ}びません | ご{f|返信|へんしん}には{f|及|およ}びません |

<div class="note">

**Note** - a third, frozen use of the same verb is
<code>～は{f|言|い}うに{f|及|およ}ばず</code>, "to say nothing of":
{f|漢字|かんじ}は{f|言|い}うに{f|及|およ}ばず、{f|敬語|けいご}も{f|難|むずか}しい.
Learn it as a phrase; it is not built from the pattern on this page.

</div>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

The "no need" use is **polite deflection**, and that is its whole character. It
releases the other person from an effort they were about to make - coming,
replying, paying, apologising, worrying - and it does so gracefully, which is why
it lives in service Japanese, business email and careful speech. It appears
overwhelmingly in the polite form
<code>には{f|及|およ}びません</code>, usually after a reason.

Said plainly to a friend it is too stiff:
<code>{f|来|こ}なくていいよ</code> is what you would actually say. Said to a
customer it is exactly right.

There is one trap of tone. ご{f|心配|しんぱい}には{f|及|およ}びません is
reassuring when the worry is about you and your own affairs. Used to someone who
is worried about damage **you** caused, it can sound as though you are brushing
their concern aside; there, an apology plus an explanation is safer.

The comparative use is different in register - plain written or spoken Japanese,
common in modest self-assessment ({f|私|わたし}などまだまだ{f|及|およ}びません)
and in sober comparisons of quality or figures. It is almost always negative:
Japanese does not use the affirmative {f|及|およ}ぶ to say "A equals B".

## Contrast

### ～には{f|及|およ}ばない vs ～なくてもいい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なくてもいい` gives **permission** to skip it.
<code>には{f|及|およ}ばない</code> **declines an offer** on the other person's
behalf, politely.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|早|はや}く{f|来|こ}<b>なくてもいい</b>よ。
You don't have to come in early tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|当日|とうじつ}は{f|受付|うけつけ}でお{f|名前|なまえ}をおっしゃっていただくだけで、{f|事前|じぜん}の{f|登録|とうろく}には<b>{f|及|およ}びません</b>。
On the day you need only give your name at reception; there is no need to register in advance.
</pre>

The first is a boss to a subordinate; the second is an organiser to a guest.
`なくてもいい` can be said downward or between equals, but it is a permission,
so saying it to a customer sounds presumptuous.
<code>には{f|及|およ}びません</code> has no such problem.

### ～には{f|及|およ}ばない vs ～にはかなわない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both mean "no match for".
<code>には{f|及|およ}ばない</code> measures a **gap**, calmly.
<code>にはかなわない</code> **gives up**, with feeling.

</div>

<pre>
{f|将棋|しょうぎ}では{f|祖父|そふ}に<b>かなわない</b>。
At shogi I just can't beat my grandfather.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|完成度|かんせいど}という{f|点|てん}では、{f|前作|ぜんさく}に<b>{f|及|およ}ばない</b>。
In terms of polish it does not match the previous work.
</pre>

<code>かなわない</code> is personal and often rueful, and it needs a contest -
a game, an argument, a person you cannot outdo.
<code>{f|及|およ}ばない</code> takes figures, quality and reputation as well, and
sounds like an assessment rather than a surrender.

### ～には{f|及|およ}ばない vs ～にはあたらない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>には{f|及|およ}ばない</code> tells you not to **take the
trouble**. <code>にはあたらない</code> tells you the **reaction is unwarranted**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|軽|かる}いので、{f|手伝|てつだ}っていただくには<b>{f|及|およ}びません</b>。
The bags are light, so there's no need for you to help.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事前|じぜん}に{f|説明|せつめい}があったのだから、{f|変更|へんこう}を{f|非難|ひなん}する<b>にはあたらない</b>。
We were told in advance, so the change hardly deserves criticism.
</pre>

Helping is a kindness offered; criticism is a judgement passed. Put
<code>にはあたらない</code> on the first and you appear to be ruling that the
offer was out of order.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|来|き}た<b>には{f|及|およ}ばない</b> → ✅ <b>{f|来|く}るには{f|及|およ}ばない</b>
  The verb stays in the dictionary form. Note the reading too:
  {f|来|く}る, not こる.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}の{f|実力|じつりょく}は{f|兄|あに}に<b>{f|及|およ}ぶ</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}の{f|実力|じつりょく}は{f|兄|あに}<b>と{f|同|おな}じくらいだ</b>。
  The comparative use exists only in the negative. Affirmative
  {f|及|およ}ぶ means "reaches, extends to" -
  {f|被害|ひがい}は{f|全域|ぜんいき}に{f|及|およ}ぶ - which is a different
  sentence entirely.

- ❌ お{f|礼|れい}には{f|及|およ}びません<b>です</b>。 → ✅ お{f|礼|れい}には<b>{f|及|およ}びません</b>。
  {f|及|およ}びません is already the polite negative; です cannot be stacked on
  top of it.

- ❌ ({f|自分|じぶん}の{f|仕事|しごと}について) {f|私|わたし}が{f|出席|しゅっせき}するには{f|及|およ}びません。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}が{f|出席|しゅっせき}する<b>{f|必要|ひつよう}はありません</b>。
  The pattern normally excuses **the other person** from an effort. Turned on
  your own duties it sounds oddly self-important, as though you were graciously
  letting yourself off.

## Practice

**1.** {f|資料|しりょう}はこちらで{f|印刷|いんさつ}しておきますので、お{f|持|も}ちいただく____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お{f|持|も}ちいただく<b>には{f|及|およ}びません</b>

The speaker is releasing the listener from bringing anything, which is the polite
deflection use, and the humble ～いただく in front of it fits the business
register perfectly.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|記録|きろく}には（{f|及|およ}ばなかった・あたらなかった）が、{f|自己|じこ}ベストは{f|更新|こうしん}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|記録|きろく}には<b>{f|及|およ}ばなかった</b>が…

This is the comparative use: the result fell short of a benchmark.
<code>にはあたらない</code> judges whether a reaction is warranted and cannot
measure a gap between two numbers.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Please don't trouble yourself to come to the station - I'll take a taxi."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

タクシーで{f|向|む}かいますので、{f|駅|えき}までお{f|迎|むか}えいただくには<b>{f|及|およ}びません</b>。

Declining someone's kindness while giving a reason is exactly the shape this
pattern wants, and the polite {f|及|およ}びません keeps the refusal warm rather
than blunt.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: ご{f|連絡|れんらく}ありがとうございます。{f|確認|かくにん}しましたので、{f|返信|へんしん}したには{f|及|およ}びません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ ご{f|連絡|れんらく}ありがとうございます。{f|確認|かくにん}しましたので、ご{f|返信|へんしん}には<b>{f|及|およ}びません</b>。
"Thank you for your message. I have checked it, so there is no need to reply."

The past form {f|返信|へんしん}した cannot precede には{f|及|およ}ばない; use
either the dictionary form {f|返信|へんしん}する or, as here, the bare noun with
the honorific ご, which is the standard wording in email.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ご{f|心配|しんぱい}には<b>{f|及|およ}びません</b>。
(b) {f|心配|しんぱい}する<b>にはあたりません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is addressed **to the person worrying**, and it is warm: thank you for your
concern, but please set it down. It is the standard line when someone asks after
your health or your family.

(b) is a judgement **about the situation**: given the facts, worry is not
justified. It looks past the listener to the case itself, so it fits a report or
an announcement, and in a personal exchange it can sound as if you were correcting
the other person for feeling anything at all.

</details>
