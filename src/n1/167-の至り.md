# ～の至り

**Meaning** · nothing short of; I could not be more ～ - a ceremonial way of reporting the speaker's own overwhelming feeling

{f|至|いた}り is the noun of {f|至|いた}る, "to reach", so
<code>～の{f|至|いた}り</code> is "the point that ～ arrives at". Unlike
<code>～の{f|極|きわ}み</code>, which can describe a situation, this one is almost
always about **the person speaking**: their honour, their gratitude, their
embarrassment. It survives in a handful of frozen greetings and in one everyday
idiom, {f|若気|わかげ}の{f|至|いた}り.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (emotion) | noun + の{f|至|いた}り | {f|光栄|こうえい} → {f|光栄|こうえい}の{f|至|いた}り |
| Ending a sentence | ～の{f|至|いた}りです／でございます | {f|恐縮|きょうしゅく}の{f|至|いた}りでございます |
| Humble report | ～の{f|至|いた}りに{f|存|ぞん}じます | {f|慶賀|けいが}の{f|至|いた}りに{f|存|ぞん}じます |
| Fixed idiom | {f|若気|わかげ}の{f|至|いた}り = youthful folly | あれは{f|若気|わかげ}の{f|至|いた}りだった |
| Never a mere state | ❌{f|混乱|こんらん}の{f|至|いた}り | ✅{f|混乱|こんらん}の{f|極|きわ}み |

<div class="note">

**Note - the collocations that are actually live.** This is a closed list, and a
very short one: {f|光栄|こうえい}の{f|至|いた}り (honoured),
{f|感激|かんげき}の{f|至|いた}り (deeply moved),
{f|恐縮|きょうしゅく}の{f|至|いた}り (mortified at the trouble caused),
{f|赤面|せきめん}の{f|至|いた}り and {f|汗顔|かんがん}の{f|至|いた}り (both
"I blush to say it"), {f|慶賀|けいが}の{f|至|いた}り (formal congratulation), and
the idiom {f|若気|わかげ}の{f|至|いた}り. Anything you assemble yourself will be
wrong: ❌{f|疲労|ひろう}の{f|至|いた}り, ❌{f|贅沢|ぜいたく}の{f|至|いた}り,
❌{f|驚|おどろ}きの{f|至|いた}り.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise this; produce the plain version instead.** Outside
{f|若気|わかげ}の{f|至|いた}り, every use of this pattern belongs to ceremonial
Japanese: an acceptance speech, the opening of a formal address, a letter of
congratulation or thanks, a message read aloud at a company anniversary. It sits
alongside でございます, {f|賜|たまわ}る and {f|存|ぞん}じます, and it sounds absurd
without them. If you are moved or honoured in real life, say
<code>{f|大変|たいへん}{f|光栄|こうえい}です</code>,
<code>{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|嬉|うれ}しく{f|思|おも}います</code> or
<code>お{f|恥|は}ずかしい{f|限|かぎ}りです</code>.

Two restrictions decide whether a sentence works. First, **the feeling must be the
speaker's own.** ❌{f|先生|せんせい}は{f|感激|かんげき}の{f|至|いた}りです describes
someone else's inner state and is not said; you would write
{f|先生|せんせい}は{f|大変|たいへん}{f|感激|かんげき}しておられました. Second,
**the noun must name an emotion, not a condition.** Fatigue, chaos and
extravagance are conditions, and they take
<code>の{f|極|きわ}み</code> instead.

<code>{f|若気|わかげ}の{f|至|いた}り</code> is the exception you can actually use.
It is a fixed noun phrase meaning "something rash I did because I was young", it
appears in ordinary adult conversation, and it usually comes with a rueful smile.
Note that it does not mean "the height of youth" - the {f|至|いた}り has become
part of a word, and the phrase names the *deed*, not the feeling.

Younger speakers today are more likely to have heard
{f|光栄|こうえい}の{f|至|いた}り in a costume drama or a stiff speech than to have
used it, and some of the other collocations -
{f|汗顔|かんがん}の{f|至|いた}り especially - already read as distinctly
old-fashioned even in formal writing.

## Contrast

### ～の{f|至|いた}り vs the modern equivalent

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - what you should say is
<code>{f|大変|たいへん}{f|光栄|こうえい}です</code>,
<code>{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|嬉|うれ}しいです</code>,
<code>{f|恐縮|きょうしゅく}です</code>. They cover every situation
<code>の{f|至|いた}り</code> covers, in language that does not need a lectern.

</div>

<pre>
このような{f|席|せき}にお{f|招|まね}きいただき、<b>{f|光栄|こうえい}の{f|至|いた}り</b>に{f|存|ぞん}じます。
To be invited to an occasion such as this is the greatest of honours.
</pre>

<pre>
こんな{f|会|かい}に{f|呼|よ}んでいただいて、<b>{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|光栄|こうえい}です</b>。
It's a real honour to have been invited to something like this.
</pre>

Same event, same gratitude. The first is read from a card at a formal dinner; the
second is what you say when the host comes over to your table.
{f|恐縮|きょうしゅく}です on its own is likewise the everyday descendant of
{f|恐縮|きょうしゅく}の{f|至|いた}りでございます.

### ～の{f|至|いた}り vs ～の{f|極|きわ}み

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>の{f|至|いた}り</code> = a feeling rising in **me**.
<code>の{f|極|きわ}み</code> = a state at its **maximum**, mine or anyone's or
nobody's.

</div>

<pre>
ご{f|指名|しめい}をいただき、<b>{f|感激|かんげき}の{f|至|いた}り</b>でございます。
To have been chosen leaves me profoundly moved.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|連日|れんじつ}の{f|残業|ざんぎょう}で、{f|職場|しょくば}は<b>{f|疲労|ひろう}の{f|極|きわ}み</b>にあった。
Day after day of overtime had left the office completely exhausted.
</pre>

The second cannot be rebuilt with <code>の{f|至|いた}り</code>: an office is not
feeling anything, it is in a condition. In the small overlap where both are
possible - {f|光栄|こうえい} takes either - the
{f|至|いた}り version is the humbler and the older.

### ～の{f|至|いた}り vs ～{f|限|かぎ}りだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both report the speaker's own feeling.
<code>{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code> takes an **adjective** and is merely formal;
<code>の{f|至|いた}り</code> takes a **noun** and is ceremonial. Of the two, only
<code>{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code> is worth producing.

</div>

<pre>
{f|久|ひさ}しぶりに{f|皆|みな}さんに{f|会|あ}えて、<b>{f|嬉|うれ}しい{f|限|かぎ}りです</b>。
I could not be happier to see everyone again after so long.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|身|み}に{f|余|あま}るお{f|言葉|ことば}をいただき、<b>{f|恐縮|きょうしゅく}の{f|至|いた}り</b>です。
Such undeserved praise leaves me quite abashed.
</pre>

You could say the first at a reunion, in a work email, or in a speech. The second
would be strange anywhere except in front of an audience or on paper - and even
there, {f|恐縮|きょうしゅく}です would do the job.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|疲労|ひろう}<b>の{f|至|いた}り</b>です → ✅ {f|疲労|ひろう}<b>の{f|極|きわ}み</b>です（or とても{f|疲|つか}れました）
  Exhaustion is a condition, not a welling-up feeling, so it takes
  <code>の{f|極|きわ}み</code>. This is the single commonest error with the pair.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}は<b>{f|感激|かんげき}の{f|至|いた}り</b>です。 → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}は{f|大変|たいへん}{f|感激|かんげき}しておられました。
  The pattern reports the speaker's own emotion. Used of a third person it reads
  as though you were speaking their lines for them.

- ❌ お{f|会|あ}いできて{f|光栄|こうえい}<b>の{f|至|いた}ります</b>。 → ✅ お{f|会|あ}いできて{f|光栄|こうえい}<b>の{f|至|いた}りです</b>。
  {f|至|いた}り is a noun here, not the verb {f|至|いた}る. It takes です,
  でございます or に{f|存|ぞん}じます.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は<b>{f|若気|わかげ}の{f|至|いた}り</b>をしてしまった。 → ✅ あれは<b>{f|若気|わかげ}の{f|至|いた}り</b>でした。
  The idiom is a label you put on a past deed, not something you do. It also needs
  distance in time: a person in their twenties saying it about yesterday sounds
  like a joke.

- ❌ （to a friend）ケーキありがとう。{f|光栄|こうえい}の{f|至|いた}りだよ。 → ✅ ケーキありがとう。すごく{f|嬉|うれ}しい。
  Register, not grammar. Ceremonial vocabulary aimed at a friend is heard as
  sarcasm.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to hear this sentence, and who is speaking? {f|本日|ほんじつ}このような{f|賞|しょう}をいただき、{f|光栄|こうえい}の{f|至|いた}りに{f|存|ぞん}じます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

At an award ceremony, spoken by the recipient into a microphone. Three signals
stack up: {f|本日|ほんじつ} rather than {f|今日|きょう}, the ceremonial
{f|光栄|こうえい}の{f|至|いた}り, and the humble に{f|存|ぞん}じます. Written down
it would suit a letter of thanks; it fits no ordinary conversation.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: このたびはお{f|招|まね}きいただき、{f|感激|かんげき}の{f|至|いた}りでございます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|今日|きょう}は{f|招|まね}いてくださって、<b>{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|嬉|うれ}しいです</b>。（more polite: お{f|招|まね}きいただき、{f|大変|たいへん}{f|嬉|うれ}しく{f|思|おも}います）

The ceremonial noun phrase and でございます both come out.
{f|嬉|うれ}しく{f|思|おも}います keeps the sentence polite enough for a work
setting without sounding like a speech.

</details>

**3.** Choose: {f|三日|みっか}{f|連続|れんぞく}の{f|徹夜|てつや}で、チームは（{f|疲労|ひろう}の{f|至|いた}り・{f|疲労|ひろう}の{f|極|きわ}み）だった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|疲労|ひろう}<b>の{f|極|きわ}み</b>だった。

The subject is a team and the noun names a physical condition, so
<code>の{f|至|いた}り</code> is doubly wrong: it wants an emotion, and it wants
that emotion to be the speaker's. Note that a plain
{f|疲|つか}れ{f|切|き}っていた would be the natural sentence outside a written
report.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|若|わか}いころ{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|辞|や}めて{f|世界|せかい}を{f|回|まわ}ったのは、{f|若気|わかげ}の{f|極|きわ}みだった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|若|わか}いころ{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|辞|や}めて{f|世界|せかい}を{f|回|まわ}ったのは、<b>{f|若気|わかげ}の{f|至|いた}り</b>だった。
"Quitting my job to travel the world when I was young was youthful recklessness."

{f|若気|わかげ} only ever pairs with {f|至|いた}り. The phrase is frozen, which is
also why it is the one member of this family a learner can safely use.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ご{f|指導|しどう}いただき、{f|恐縮|きょうしゅく}の{f|至|いた}りです。
(b) ご{f|指導|しどう}いただき、ありがとうございます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is ceremonial and self-lowering. {f|恐縮|きょうしゅく} says the speaker feels
they have imposed and are barely worthy of the trouble taken, and
<code>の{f|至|いた}り</code> pushes that to the ceiling. It fits a written note to
a senior figure, or remarks at a formal gathering.

(b) simply thanks the person. It is polite, warm and usable anywhere, including to
the same senior figure in the corridor. A learner should reach for (b) every time
and recognise (a) when it appears in a letter.

</details>
