# ～ぶり

**Meaning** · the way someone does something, as it looks to an onlooker; also, after an interval of ～

`ぶり` turns a verb or a noun into a **noun** naming the *manner* of the thing -
{f|働|はたら}きぶり is not the work itself but the style and impression of the
working. A second, very common use attaches it to a length of time and means "for
the first time in that long": {f|三年|さんねん}ぶり.

## Formation

The result is always a **noun**. It takes が, を, に, and modifies with `の`; it is
never a な-adjective.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | stem + ぶり | {f|働|はたら}く → {f|働|はたら}きぶり |
| Noun (an activity) | Noun + ぶり | {f|仕事|しごと} → {f|仕事|しごと}ぶり |
| Noun (an observed state) | Noun + ぶり | {f|混雑|こんざつ} → {f|混雑|こんざつ}ぶり |
| Whose manner it is | Person + の + ～ぶり | {f|彼|かれ}の{f|話|はな}しぶり |
| Emphatic, spoken | ます-stem + っぷり | {f|飲|の}む → {f|飲|の}みっぷり |
| Time span, before a verb | Time + ぶりに | {f|三年|さんねん} → {f|三年|さんねん}ぶりに{f|会|あ}う |
| Time span, before a noun | Time + ぶりの | {f|十年|じゅうねん} → {f|十年|じゅうねん}ぶりの{f|再会|さいかい} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the manner sense is not fully productive. The stock members are
<code>{f|仕事|しごと}ぶり</code>, <code>{f|働|はたら}きぶり</code>,
<code>{f|話|はな}しぶり</code>, <code>{f|暮|く}らしぶり</code>,
<code>{f|生活|せいかつ}ぶり</code>, plus a large journalistic family built on
nouns of state - <code>{f|混雑|こんざつ}ぶり</code>,
<code>{f|活躍|かつやく}ぶり</code>, <code>{f|成長|せいちょう}ぶり</code>,
<code>{f|徹底|てってい}ぶり</code>. If the base names something with no visible
manner to it, `ぶり` will not attach.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the time use needs an event that has **happened before and is happening
again**. <code>{f|三年|さんねん}ぶりに{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}した</code> works because
there was an earlier win. A first-ever or once-only event cannot take it.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`ぶり` is register-neutral and turns up everywhere from performance reviews to
travel documentaries. Its distinguishing feature is that it always implies an
**observer**: {f|仕事|しごと}ぶり is your work as it appears to someone watching it,
which is why the word lives in appraisal language
(<code>{f|仕事|しごと}ぶりが{f|評価|ひょうか}される</code>) and in reporting
(<code>{f|徹底|てってい}ぶりが{f|話題|わだい}になる</code>). You do not normally
describe your own manner with it, for the same reason you do not describe your own
charisma.

The suffix carries no evaluation of its own; the surrounding sentence supplies it.
{f|見事|みごと}な{f|働|はたら}きぶり is praise, ひどい{f|慌|あわ}てぶり is not.

<code>っぷり</code> is the same suffix with a colloquial, appreciative kick to it.
<code>{f|飲|の}みっぷり</code>, <code>{f|食|た}べっぷり</code>,
<code>{f|脱|ぬ}ぎっぷり</code>, <code>{f|男|おとこ}っぷり</code> - all of them praise
someone for doing the thing wholeheartedly and without fuss. It belongs to speech
and to lively writing, and it would look out of place in a report.

The time use is the one you will hear most. <code>{f|久|ひさ}しぶり</code> is its
frozen everyday form, and <code>お{f|久|ひさ}しぶりです</code> is the standard
greeting to someone you have not seen for a while. Note that with an actual number
the phrase is quietly newsworthy - it is the shape headlines use for a drought
broken, a record equalled, a champion returning.

## Contrast

### ～ぶり vs ～{f|方|かた}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～{f|方|かた}</code> is the **method**, something that can
be taught and copied. `ぶり` is the **impression** the doing gives, which cannot.

</div>

<pre>
{f|敬語|けいご}の{f|使|つか}い<b>{f|方|かた}</b>を{f|教|おし}えてもらった。
I was taught how to use honorific language.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}いた{f|話|はな}し<b>ぶり</b>に{f|安心|あんしん}した。
Her calm way of speaking put me at ease.
</pre>

Swap them and both fail. ❌<code>{f|話|はな}しぶりを{f|教|おし}える</code> - you
cannot teach someone the impression they make. ❌<code>{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}に
{f|感心|かんしん}する</code> is possible only if you mean the technique itself was
clever. The test: if you could write it in a manual, it is
<code>{f|方|かた}</code>.

### ～ぶり - manner vs interval

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one suffix, two jobs, and the **base** tells you which. A verb
stem or an activity noun gives **manner**. A length of time gives **interval**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新聞|しんぶん}は{f|市長|しちょう}の{f|仕事|しごと}<b>ぶり</b>を{f|厳|きび}しく{f|批判|ひはん}した。
The paper was sharply critical of the mayor's performance in the job.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|五年|ごねん}<b>ぶり</b>に{f|富士山|ふじさん}に{f|登|のぼ}った。
I climbed Mt Fuji for the first time in five years.
</pre>

There is no ambiguity in practice, because the two senses take different bases and
different particles. Manner: {f|仕事|しごと}ぶり**が**{f|評価|ひょうか}される,
{f|話|はな}しぶり**を**{f|見|み}る. Interval: {f|五年|ごねん}ぶり**に**
{f|登|のぼ}る, {f|五年|ごねん}ぶり**の**{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}. The interval use also
requires the event to have happened before and be happening again, which is why
❌<code>{f|卒業|そつぎょう}ぶり</code> fails - it names an event, not a span of time.

### ～ぶり vs ～ぶる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - two different words that happen to look alike. `ぶり` after a
verb stem or activity noun = **manner**, neutral. The `ぶり` that comes from the
verb `ぶる` = **posturing**, and it is an insult.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新人|しんじん}の{f|働|はたら}き<b>ぶり</b>を{f|部長|ぶちょう}が{f|褒|ほ}めていた。
The department head was praising how the new hire works.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新人|しんじん}の{f|知|し}ったか<b>ぶり</b>に{f|部長|ぶちょう}があきれていた。
The department head was exasperated by the new hire pretending to know everything.
</pre>

You can tell them apart by the base. <code>{f|働|はたら}き</code> is a verb stem, so
the noun means the manner of working. <code>{f|知|し}ったか</code> is not a stem at
all - the word is built on the verb <code>{f|知|し}ったかぶる</code>, and the whole
thing is a judgement about pretence. The same applies to
<code>{f|上品|じょうひん}ぶり</code>, which means affected refinement, not
"refined manner".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|働|はたら}<b>くぶり</b> → ✅ {f|働|はたら}<b>きぶり</b>
  A verb joins `ぶり` at the ます-stem, not the dictionary form. {f|話|はな}す →
  {f|話|はな}しぶり, {f|暮|く}らす → {f|暮|く}らしぶり.

- ❌ {f|久|ひさ}<b>しいぶり</b>ですね。 → ✅ お{f|久|ひさ}<b>しぶり</b>ですね。
  The frozen greeting keeps the old stem {f|久|ひさ}し with no `い`, and takes the
  polite お in most situations.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}{f|生|う}まれた{f|子|こ}{f|牛|うし}を{f|一年|いちねん}<b>ぶり</b>に{f|見|み}た。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}{f|生|う}まれた{f|子|こ}{f|牛|うし}を{f|一年|いちねん}{f|後|ご}に{f|見|み}た。
  `ぶり` needs a *repeat*. If there was no earlier occasion, the sentence has no gap
  to measure.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|仕事|しごと}ぶりな</b>{f|人|ひと}だ。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|仕事|しごと}ぶり</b>のいい{f|人|ひと}だ。
  `ぶり` gives a noun, not a な-adjective. Nouns modify with `の`, and the quality
  has to be stated separately.

## Practice

**1.** {f|台風|たいふう}の{f|被害|ひがい}を{f|受|う}けた{f|町|まち}の{f|復興|ふっこう}____が{f|全国|ぜんこく}に{f|伝|つた}えられた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|復興|ふっこう}<b>ぶり</b>

{f|復興|ふっこう} is a noun of state with something visible about it, which is the
journalistic use of `ぶり` - how the recovery looks to an outside observer. The
result is a noun and takes `が`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|包丁|ほうちょう}の（{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}・{f|使|つか}いぶり）を{f|母|はは}に{f|習|なら}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|包丁|ほうちょう}の<b>{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}</b>を{f|母|はは}に{f|習|なら}った。

You learn a *method*, and that is <code>{f|方|かた}</code>. `ぶり` names the
impression someone's handling gives, which is not something that can be handed over
in a lesson.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The magazine reported in detail on how the young mayor goes about the job."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|雑誌|ざっし}は{f|若|わか}い{f|市長|しちょう}の<b>{f|仕事|しごと}ぶり</b>を{f|詳|くわ}しく{f|伝|つた}えた。

"How he goes about the job", as seen from outside and open to judgement, is
<code>{f|仕事|しごと}ぶり</code>; <code>{f|働|はたら}きぶり</code> would also pass.
The result is a noun, so it takes `を` as the object of {f|伝|つた}える.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|面接官|めんせつかん}は{f|彼|かれ}の{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}く{f|話|はな}すぶりを{f|評価|ひょうか}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|面接官|めんせつかん}は{f|彼|かれ}の{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}いた{f|話|はな}し<b>ぶり</b>を{f|評価|ひょうか}した。

Two repairs. `ぶり` takes the ます-stem {f|話|はな}し, not the dictionary form; and
the adjectival clause in front of a noun needs
<code>{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}いた</code>, the state, rather than the plain present.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|食|た}べ<b>{f|方|かた}</b>を{f|見|み}ていた。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|食|た}べ<b>っぷり</b>を{f|見|み}ていた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is about technique or table manners: how he holds the chopsticks, whether he
makes a noise. It can easily be critical.

(b) is warm and admiring. <code>っぷり</code> praises someone for doing the thing
with gusto - he ate a great deal, cheerfully, and it was good to watch. The
colloquial doubling of the consonant is what carries that appreciation.

</details>
