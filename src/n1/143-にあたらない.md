# ～にあたらない

**Meaning** · there is no call for it; it does not warrant that - the reaction you were about to have is not justified by the facts

{f|当|あ}たる here is "to hit the mark, to correspond to". The negative says the
case **does not amount to** the thing named: not enough to be surprised at, not
enough to blame anyone for, not enough to count as a breach. The writer is
heading off a reaction, usually one the reader has already begun to have.

## Formation

Written には{f|当|あ}たらない or にはあたらない; the は is often dropped, and the
kana spelling is common in newspapers.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (reaction) | dictionary form + には{f|当|あ}たらない | {f|驚|おどろ}く → {f|驚|おどろ}くには{f|当|あ}たらない |
| Verb, は dropped | dictionary form + に{f|当|あ}たらない | {f|感心|かんしん}する → {f|感心|かんしん}するに{f|当|あ}たらない |
| Noun | noun + には{f|当|あ}たらない | {f|違反|いはん} → {f|違反|いはん}には{f|当|あ}たらない |
| Typical frame | ～からといって、～には{f|当|あ}たらない | {f|負|ま}けたからといって{f|落|お}ち{f|込|こ}むには{f|当|あ}たらない |
| Polite | ～には{f|当|あ}たりません | {f|心配|しんぱい}するには{f|当|あ}たりません |
| Literary | ～には{f|当|あ}たるまい | {f|責|せ}めるには{f|当|あ}たるまい |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the verbs that fit in front are **reactions**: {f|驚|おどろ}く,
{f|感心|かんしん}する, {f|非難|ひなん}する, {f|責|せ}める, {f|褒|ほ}める,
{f|同情|どうじょう}する, {f|悲観|ひかん}する, {f|心配|しんぱい}する,
{f|恥|は}じる. Actions that are merely unnecessary - coming, phoning, paying -
take <code>には{f|及|およ}ばない</code> instead. {f|心配|しんぱい}する sits in
both camps: {f|心配|しんぱい}するには{f|及|およ}びません deflects the listener's
concern, while {f|心配|しんぱい}するにはあたりません rules that the situation
does not justify it.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
その{f|程度|ていど}のことで{f|驚|おどろ}く<b>にはあたらない</b>。
Something on that scale is nothing to be surprised at.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一度|いちど}{f|失敗|しっぱい}したくらいで{f|悲観|ひかん}する<b>にはあたらない</b>。
One failure is no reason for despair.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|規則|きそく}を{f|確認|かくにん}したが、{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|対応|たいおう}は{f|違反|いはん}<b>にはあたらない</b>。
We checked the rules, and what was done this time does not constitute a breach.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事情|じじょう}を{f|聞|き}けば、{f|彼|かれ}{f|一人|ひとり}を{f|責|せ}める<b>にはあたらない</b>{f|気|き}がしてくる。
Once you hear the circumstances, you start to feel there is no call to blame him alone.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新記録|しんきろく}といっても{f|条件|じょうけん}が{f|違|ちが}うのだから、{f|大|おお}さわぎする<b>にはあたらない</b>。
It may be a record, but the conditions were different, so there is no cause for all the fuss.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|準備|じゅんび}に{f|三年|さんねん}かけたことを{f|思|おも}えば、この{f|結果|けっか}は{f|驚|おどろ}く<b>にはあたりません</b>。
Given that they spent three years preparing, the outcome is hardly astonishing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|就任|しゅうにん}{f|早々|そうそう}の{f|失言|しつげん}ではあるが、{f|辞任|じにん}を{f|求|もと}める<b>にはあたらない</b>という{f|声|こえ}も{f|根強|ねづよ}い。
It was a gaffe within days of taking office, but there is a persistent view that it does not warrant calling for a resignation.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|寄付|きふ}をしたのは{f|当然|とうぜん}の{f|務|つと}めを{f|果|は}たしたまでで、{f|特|とく}に{f|感心|かんしん}する<b>にはあたらない</b>と{f|本人|ほんにん}は{f|語|かた}っている。
He says himself that in donating he only did what was expected, and that it is nothing to admire him for.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Formal, and above all **argumentative**. This is a writer's move: the reader is
supposed to be surprised, or indignant, and the sentence tells them to stand
down, usually giving the reason in the same breath with からといって, のだから or
を{f|考|かんが}えれば. Editorials, commentary, analyst reports, official
statements and calm advice all run on it.

Because it overrules the reader's reaction, it can sound superior. Aimed at
someone's feelings in conversation - {f|泣|な}くにはあたりませんよ - it comes
across as lecturing; the warm version is
{f|泣|な}かなくてもいいですよ or {f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}ですよ.

The noun use is a separate habit and is worth learning as its own phrase.
<code>{f|違反|いはん}にはあたらない</code>,
<code>{f|差別|さべつ}にはあたらない</code>,
<code>{f|非難|ひなん}にはあたらない</code> mean "does not constitute" and are
standard in legal, regulatory and corporate language. In that use there is no
reaction being suppressed at all - only a category the case fails to fall into.

Spoken, the pattern is largely confined to prepared or professional talk:
meetings, interviews, courtrooms, the news. The everyday equivalent is
<code>{f|驚|おどろ}くことはない</code> or
<code>{f|驚|おどろ}かなくてもいい</code>.

## Contrast

### ～にはあたらない vs ～ことはない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same message, different height.
<code>ことはない</code> is spoken reassurance to a person;
<code>にはあたらない</code> is a written judgement about a case.

</div>

<pre>
そんなに{f|落|お}ち{f|込|こ}む<b>ことはない</b>よ。
There's no need to get so down about it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|統計|とうけい}の{f|範囲|はんい}{f|内|ない}の{f|変動|へんどう}であり、{f|懸念|けねん}する<b>にはあたらない</b>。
The movement is within statistical bounds and gives no cause for concern.
</pre>

Notice who is being addressed. `ことはない` comforts the person in front of you;
<code>にはあたらない</code> corrects an inference. That is also why
<code>にはあたらない</code> nearly always carries its reason, while `ことはない`
can stand alone with よ.

### ～にはあたらない vs ～には{f|及|およ}ばない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>にはあたらない</code> = the **reaction** is unwarranted.
<code>には{f|及|およ}ばない</code> = the **trouble** is unnecessary; please don't
bother.

</div>

<pre>
わざわざお{f|越|こ}しいただく<b>には{f|及|およ}びません</b>。
There's really no need for you to come all this way.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|遅刻|ちこく}は{f|一度|いちど}きりで、{f|処分|しょぶん}する<b>にはあたらない</b>。
It was a single instance of lateness and does not warrant disciplinary action.
</pre>

Swap them and both go wrong. Coming to visit is a courtesy someone is offering,
not a reaction to be judged; a disciplinary decision is a judgement, not an
inconvenience to be waved away. <code>には{f|及|およ}ばない</code> is polite;
<code>にはあたらない</code> is evaluative.

### {f|違反|いはん}にはあたらない vs {f|違反|いはん}ではない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ではない</code> flatly denies.
<code>にはあたらない</code> concedes that the case is close and rules that it
still falls outside.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|行為|こうい}は{f|規則|きそく}{f|違反|いはん}<b>ではない</b>。
What was done was not a breach of the rules.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|形式的|けいしきてき}には{f|問題|もんだい}が{f|残|のこ}るが、{f|規則|きそく}{f|違反|いはん}<b>にはあたらない</b>。
Formally some questions remain, but it does not amount to a breach of the rules.
</pre>

The second is what a committee writes when the answer was not obvious. It
implies deliberation, and it is why the phrase is so common in findings and
statements: it settles a borderline case without pretending the border was
never approached.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|驚|おどろ}いた<b>にはあたらない</b> → ✅ {f|驚|おどろ}く<b>にはあたらない</b>
  The verb stays in the dictionary form, even when the event is in the past.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|来|く}る<b>にはあたりません</b>。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は<b>お{f|越|こ}しには{f|及|およ}びません</b>。
  Coming is an action someone is kindly offering, not a reaction to be judged.
  For "no need to", use <code>には{f|及|およ}ばない</code>.

- ❌ {f|心配|しんぱい}するにはあたらない<b>です</b>。 → ✅ {f|心配|しんぱい}するには<b>あたりません</b>。
  This is a verb, so the polite form is あたりません. ないです clashes badly
  with the formality of the pattern.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}の{f|努力|どりょく}は{f|感心|かんしん}する<b>にあたる</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}の{f|努力|どりょく}は<b>{f|感心|かんしん}に{f|値|あたい}する</b>。
  There is no affirmative use of this pattern in modern Japanese. "Deserves
  admiration" is <code>{f|値|あたい}する</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|近|ちか}づいているのだから、{f|飛行機|ひこうき}が{f|欠航|けっこう}しても{f|驚|おどろ}く____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|驚|おどろ}く<b>にはあたらない</b>

The first clause supplies the reason and the second cancels the reader's
surprise, which is exactly what this pattern is for. Keep {f|驚|おどろ}く in the
dictionary form.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|運|はこ}びますので、{f|手伝|てつだ}っていただく（にはあたりません・には{f|及|およ}びません）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|手伝|てつだ}っていただく<b>には{f|及|およ}びません</b>。

The speaker is politely declining an offer of help - a courtesy, not a reaction.
<code>にはあたりません</code> would sound as though the offer had been judged
inappropriate, which is close to rude.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Sales fell slightly, but seasonal factors explain it and there is no cause for alarm."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|売上|うりあげ}はやや{f|減|へ}ったが、{f|季節|きせつ}{f|要因|よういん}によるもので、{f|懸念|けねん}する<b>にはあたらない</b>。

"No cause for alarm" plus a reason given in the same sentence is the natural home
of this pattern, and the register - a business or market comment - is right for
it. {f|心配|しんぱい}する would also work in place of {f|懸念|けねん}する in a
less formal report.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|一度|いちど}{f|注意|ちゅうい}されたくらいで、そんなに{f|落|お}ち{f|込|こ}んだにはあたらないよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|一度|いちど}{f|注意|ちゅうい}されたくらいで、そんなに{f|落|お}ち{f|込|こ}む<b>ことはない</b>よ。
"You were told off once - there's no need to take it so hard."

Two problems. The verb must be in the dictionary form, not the past. More
importantly, よ and this comforting tone mark the sentence as speech to a friend,
where <code>にはあたらない</code> sounds like a ruling from a committee;
`ことはない` is the natural choice.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|今回|こんかい}の{f|発言|はつげん}は{f|差別|さべつ}<b>ではない</b>。
(b) {f|今回|こんかい}の{f|発言|はつげん}は{f|差別|さべつ}<b>にはあたらない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a flat denial: whatever was said, it simply was not discriminatory. It
sounds like a person defending themselves, and it leaves no room for the
question.

(b) is a finding. It concedes that the remark was close enough to be examined,
and rules that it falls outside the category. That is why it is the wording used
in committee reports and official statements - and why, in a heated argument, it
can sound evasive rather than exonerating.

</details>
