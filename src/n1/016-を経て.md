# ～を経て

**Meaning** · after going through; by way of - names the years, the stages or the places that had to be passed through before the result arrived

<code>{f|経|へ}る</code> is a る-verb meaning "to pass through, to elapse", and
<code>を{f|経|へ}て</code> is its て-form doing connective work. The noun in front is
never a mere point in time: it is a **stretch or a sequence** that took effort,
patience or distance to get across - ten years, three rounds of screening, a change
of trains at Nagoya. The main clause is what came out the other side.

## Formation

A noun goes straight in front with を. There is no clause form; a verb has to be
nominalised first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Span of time | Noun + を{f|経|へ}て | {f|十年|じゅうねん} → {f|十年|じゅうねん}を{f|経|へ}て |
| Process, procedure | Noun + を{f|経|へ}て | {f|審査|しんさ} → {f|審査|しんさ}を{f|経|へ}て |
| Place on a route | Noun + を{f|経|へ}て | {f|名古屋|なごや} → {f|名古屋|なごや}を{f|経|へ}て |
| Nominalised clause | ～こと + を{f|経|へ}て | {f|議論|ぎろん}を{f|重|かさ}ねること → …を{f|経|へ}て |
| Modifying a noun | ～を{f|経|へ}た + Noun | {f|長|なが}い{f|議論|ぎろん}を{f|経|へ}た{f|結論|けつろん} |
| As the main verb | ～を{f|経|へ}る／{f|経|へ}ている | {f|三年|さんねん}を{f|経|へ}ている |
| Negative (stiff, written) | ～を{f|経|へ}ずに | {f|正式|せいしき}な{f|手続|てつづ}きを{f|経|へ}ずに |

<div class="note">

**Note** - two different verbs share the kanji.
<code>{f|経|へ}る</code> is transitive-ish and takes を: {f|三年|さんねん}を{f|経|へ}て.
<code>{f|経|た}つ</code> "time passes" takes が: {f|三年|さんねん}が{f|経|た}つ.
❌<code>{f|三年|さんねん}が{f|経|へ}て</code> mixes the two and is the standard error.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Firmly **written and formal**. Its natural habitats are news copy, company histories,
museum captions, academic prose and the career summary in a profile - "after posts at
A and B, joined C in 2019". In speech it appears in presentations and formal
introductions; between friends you would say
<code>{f|三年|さんねん}かかって</code> or <code>そのあとで</code>.

The pattern carries a quiet sense of **due process or hard-won time**. That is why
<code>{f|審査|しんさ}を{f|経|へ}て</code>, <code>{f|議論|ぎろん}を{f|経|へ}て</code>
and <code>{f|歳月|さいげつ}を{f|経|へ}て</code> sound so natural: each names something
you cannot skip. Put a short, effortless event in the slot and the sentence goes
strange - ❌<code>{f|昼食|ちゅうしょく}を{f|経|へ}て</code> makes lunch sound like an
ordeal.

The route sense is a separate flavour of the same idea and is common in transport and
travel writing: <code>ロンドン{f|経由|けいゆ}</code> for the ticket,
<code>ロンドンを{f|経|へ}て</code> for the narrative. Both are formal; only
<code>{f|経由|けいゆ}</code> appears on a departure board.

## Contrast

### ～を{f|経|へ}て vs ～てから／～のあとで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>てから</code> and <code>のあとで</code> report **sequence**.
<code>を{f|経|へ}て</code> reports that something was **gone through**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わっ<b>てから</b>、{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べた。
We had lunch after the meeting finished.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|二年|にねん}にわたる{f|交渉|こうしょう}<b>を{f|経|へ}て</b>、{f|両社|りょうしゃ}は{f|合意|ごうい}に{f|達|たっ}した。
After two years of negotiation the two companies reached agreement.
</pre>

The first is just what happened next; nobody had to endure the meeting for lunch to
occur. The second says the agreement exists **because** the two years were survived.
Note also the grammar: <code>てから</code> takes a verb,
<code>を{f|経|へ}て</code> takes a noun.

### ～を{f|経|へ}て vs ～を{f|通|つう}じて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|通|つう}じて</code> is the **medium** something happens
through. <code>を{f|経|へ}て</code> is a **stage** on the way that had to be crossed.

</div>

<pre>
{f|通訳|つうやく}<b>を{f|通|つう}じて</b>、{f|相手|あいて}の{f|考|かんが}えを{f|確|たし}かめた。
I confirmed what they thought through an interpreter.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|予選|よせん}<b>を{f|経|へ}て</b>、{f|決勝|けっしょう}に{f|進|すす}む{f|六|ろく}チームが{f|出|で}そろった。
Coming through the qualifiers, the six finalist teams are now settled.
</pre>

An interpreter is not a hurdle to be cleared, and a qualifying round is not a
channel of communication. Where both are possible - an intermediary organisation,
say - <code>を{f|通|つう}じて</code> frames it as the route the message took and
<code>を{f|経|へ}て</code> frames it as a step in a procedure.

### ～を{f|経|へ}て vs ～を{f|経由|けいゆ}して

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for **physical routes**, <code>{f|経由|けいゆ}</code> is the
everyday word and <code>を{f|経|へ}て</code> the literary one. For **processes and
years**, only <code>を{f|経|へ}て</code> works.

</div>

<pre>
ソウル<b>{f|経由|けいゆ}</b>のほうが{f|運賃|うんちん}が{f|安|やす}い。
Going via Seoul is cheaper.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一行|いっこう}は{f|険|けわ}しい{f|山道|やまみち}<b>を{f|経|へ}て</b>、ようやく{f|村|むら}にたどり{f|着|つ}いた。
The party came at last to the village by way of a steep mountain path.
</pre>

You cannot say ❌<code>{f|審査|しんさ}{f|経由|けいゆ}</code>:
<code>{f|経由|けいゆ}</code> is about geography and routing, not about procedure.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|五年|ごねん}が<b>{f|経|へ}て</b>、{f|町|まち}はすっかり{f|変|か}わった。 → ✅ {f|五年|ごねん}<b>を{f|経|へ}て</b>、{f|町|まち}はすっかり{f|変|か}わった。（or {f|五年|ごねん}が{f|経|た}って）
  <code>{f|経|へ}る</code> takes を; the が verb is <code>{f|経|た}つ</code>. Same kanji,
  different verbs, and mixing them is the commonest error with this pattern.

- ❌ {f|昼食|ちゅうしょく}<b>を{f|経|へ}て</b>{f|会議|かいぎ}を{f|再開|さいかい}した。 → ✅ {f|昼食|ちゅうしょく}<b>のあとで</b>{f|会議|かいぎ}を{f|再開|さいかい}した。
  Lunch is an interval, not something you come through. The slot wants a process or a
  long stretch.

- ❌ {f|試験|しけん}を<b>{f|経|へ}る{f|後|あと}で</b>、{f|結果|けっか}が{f|出|で}る。 → ✅ {f|試験|しけん}を<b>{f|経|へ}て</b>、{f|結果|けっか}が{f|出|で}る。
  The て-form already does the connecting; adding
  <code>{f|後|あと}で</code> is redundant and ungrammatical here.

- ❌ きのう{f|映画|えいが}<b>を{f|経|へ}て</b>{f|帰|かえ}った。 → ✅ きのう{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}てから{f|帰|かえ}った。
  Beyond the meaning problem, the register is wrong: casual weekend plans do not take
  a formal written connective.

## Practice

**1.** {f|三度|さんど}の{f|面接|めんせつ}____、ようやく{f|採用|さいよう}が{f|決|き}まった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|三度|さんど}の{f|面接|めんせつ}<b>を{f|経|へ}て</b>

Three rounds of interviews are stages that had to be crossed, and the hiring decision
is what came out the other end - the core use of the pattern. Note the を: the noun
is the thing passed through.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|香港|ホンコン}（を{f|経|へ}て・を{f|通|つう}じて）{f|東京|とうきょう}に{f|届|とど}く。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|香港|ホンコン}<b>を{f|経|へ}て</b>{f|東京|とうきょう}に{f|届|とど}く。

Hong Kong is a point on the route, so the pattern is
<code>を{f|経|へ}て</code>. <code>を{f|通|つう}じて</code> would make the city a medium
or an intermediary, as an interpreter or an agency would be.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "After twenty years abroad, she returned to her home town."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|二十年|にじゅうねん}の{f|海外|かいがい}{f|生活|せいかつ}<b>を{f|経|へ}て</b>、{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|故郷|こきょう}に{f|戻|もど}った。

Twenty years of living abroad is exactly the kind of long stretch the pattern was
made for, and the return reads as its outcome. The written register suits a sentence
that sounds like the opening of a profile.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|長|なが}い{f|議論|ぎろん}を{f|経|へ}る、{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|大|おお}きく{f|変|か}わった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|長|なが}い{f|議論|ぎろん}<b>を{f|経|へ}て</b>、{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|大|おお}きく{f|変|か}わった。

The connective form is the て-form. The dictionary form
<code>{f|経|へ}る</code> can only end a sentence or modify a following noun, as in
<code>{f|議論|ぎろん}を{f|経|へ}た{f|計画|けいかく}</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|審査|しんさ}<b>を{f|経|へ}て</b>、{f|補助金|ほじょきん}が{f|支給|しきゅう}された。
(b) {f|審査|しんさ}の<b>あとで</b>、{f|補助金|ほじょきん}が{f|支給|しきゅう}された。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) presents the screening as a required stage that the application went through, and
the payment as its proper outcome. It is the wording of an official document.

(b) merely puts the two events in order. It would still be true if the grant had been
paid for some unrelated reason after the screening happened to finish, and it sounds
like a person describing events rather than an institution describing procedure.

</details>
