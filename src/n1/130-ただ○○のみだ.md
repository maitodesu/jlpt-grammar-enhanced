# ただ○○のみだ

**Meaning** · that alone, and nothing besides - everything else has been stripped away and only this is left to do or to matter

`のみ` is the formal `だけ`, which the reader already has from N2. What `ただ` adds
in front of it is a **bracket**: it announces in advance that the sentence is about
to narrow to a single thing, and the `のみだ` at the end shuts the door. The result
is declarative and slightly grand - the sentence of someone summing up, deciding,
or resigning themselves.

## Formation

<code>ただ</code> is an adverb and simply sits at the front of the clause;
<code>のみ</code> is a particle attaching to a noun or to a plain-form verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain non-past) | ただ + Verb + のみだ | {f|待|ま}つ → ただ{f|待|ま}つのみだ |
| Verb (nominalised) | ただ + Verb + ことのみだ | {f|祈|いの}る → ただ{f|祈|いの}ることのみだった |
| Noun | ただ + Noun + のみだ | {f|結果|けっか} → ただ{f|結果|けっか}のみだ |
| Noun (slogan shape) | ただ + Noun + あるのみ | {f|前進|ぜんしん} → ただ{f|前進|ぜんしん}あるのみ |
| Written / formal | ただ ～ のみである | ただ{f|事実|じじつ}のみである |
| Polite | ただ ～ のみです | ただ{f|感謝|かんしゃ}するのみです |

<div class="note">

**Note** - adjectives sit awkwardly here. ❌<code>ただ{f|静|しず}かなのみだ</code> is
not idiomatic; for an adjective, use <code>だけ</code> -
<code>ただ{f|静|しず}かなだけだ</code>. Treat `のみ` as taking nouns and verbs.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
あとは<b>ただ{f|待|ま}つのみだ</b>。
All that is left now is to wait.
</pre>

<pre>
ここまで{f|来|き}たら、<b>ただ{f|進|すす}むのみです</b>。
Having come this far, we can only press on.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|準備|じゅんび}は{f|整|ととの}った。<b>ただ{f|本番|ほんばん}あるのみ</b>。
Preparations are complete. All that remains is the performance itself.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}にできたのは、<b>ただ{f|頭|あたま}を{f|下|さ}げることのみだった</b>。
All I could do was bow my head.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|職|しょく}に{f|求|もと}められているのは、<b>ただ{f|正確|せいかく}さのみである</b>。
What this job asks for is accuracy and nothing else.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|言葉|ことば}を{f|尽|つ}くしても{f|届|とど}かないのなら、<b>ただ{f|黙|だま}って{f|見守|みまも}るのみだ</b>。
If words will not reach him however many I use, there is nothing to do but watch in silence.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|洪水|こうずい}のあと、{f|住民|じゅうみん}にできたのは、<b>ただ{f|家族|かぞく}の{f|無事|ぶじ}を{f|祈|いの}ることのみだった</b>。
After the flood, all the residents could do was pray that their families were safe.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|議論|ぎろん}は{f|尽|つ}くした。{f|残|のこ}るは<b>ただ{f|実行|じっこう}あるのみである</b>。
The argument has been had. What remains is simply to carry it out.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Formal, written, and declamatory.** This is the register of speeches, mottoes,
editorials, formal reports and captains addressing a team. It also turns up in
sports writing and in the closing line of an essay, because it makes a good curtain.
In ordinary conversation it is out of place, and a sentence like
❌<code>{f|今日|きょう}はただ{f|寝|ね}るのみだよ</code> collides with itself: the
final よ is chatting while the `のみだ` is making a speech.

The pattern has **two temperatures**, and context decides which you hear.
<code>ただ{f|前|まえ}に{f|進|すす}むのみ</code> is resolve - everything else has been
settled and only the action remains. <code>ただ{f|待|ま}つのみだ</code> is
resignation - everything else is out of the speaker's hands. The grammar is the
same; the difference is whether the remaining thing is something you do or something
you endure.

<code>ただ ～ あるのみ</code> is worth learning as a unit. It takes a bare noun,
drops the copula entirely and ends the sentence dead:
<code>{f|努力|どりょく}あるのみ</code>, <code>{f|前進|ぜんしん}あるのみ</code>. This is
slogan Japanese - banners, training rooms, the last line of a pep talk - and it is
the one shape here that a learner can safely quote, because everybody recognises it
as a set phrase rather than as ordinary speech.

Finally, `ただ` can be dropped: <code>{f|待|ま}つのみだ</code> is grammatical.
Keeping it makes the narrowing explicit and is much the more common choice, which is
why the pattern is taught as a pair.

## Contrast

### ただ○○のみだ vs ～だけだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning, different register. `だけだ` is what people
say; `ただ○○のみだ` is what people write and declare.

</div>

<pre>
もう{f|待|ま}つ<b>だけだ</b>ね。
Nothing to do but wait now, is there.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|我々|われわれ}に{f|残|のこ}されているのは、<b>ただ{f|待|ま}つのみである</b>。
What is left to us is simply to wait.
</pre>

Both sentences describe the same evening. The first is two people in a waiting room;
the second is a paragraph in a novel about them. Note that `だけ` accepts adjectives
and casual endings, and `のみ` accepts neither comfortably.

### ただ○○のみだ vs ～しかない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `しかない` is about having **no choice**. `ただ○○のみだ` is
about **nothing else being left**, which is often the same situation seen with more
composure.

</div>

<pre>
{f|終電|しゅうでん}が{f|出|で}たので、{f|歩|ある}く<b>しかない</b>。
The last train's gone, so we've no choice but to walk.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|道|みち}は{f|一|ひと}つ。<b>ただ{f|歩|ある}き{f|続|つづ}けるのみだ</b>。
There is one road. Simply to keep walking is all there is.
</pre>

`しかない` is neutral and can sound annoyed. `ただ○○のみだ` is composed, even
dignified, and that composure is most of what the writer is buying. Where `しかない`
grumbles, `ただ○○のみだ` accepts.

### ただ○○のみだ vs ～のみならず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same particle, opposite jobs. `のみだ` **closes** the set at
one item; `のみならず` **opens** it - "not only this, but also".

</div>

<pre>
{f|被害|ひがい}を{f|受|う}けたのは、<b>ただ{f|一階|いっかい}のみだった</b>。
The damage was confined to the ground floor alone.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|被害|ひがい}は{f|一階|いっかい}<b>のみならず</b>{f|二階|にかい}にも{f|及|およ}んだ。
The damage was not confined to the ground floor but reached the first floor as well.
</pre>

Because the two look alike on the page, it is easy to read one as the other and get
the sentence exactly backwards. The tell is what follows: if another item comes
after `のみ`, it is `のみならず`; if the sentence ends, it is `のみだ`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ただ{f|結果|けっか}<b>だのみだ</b>。 → ✅ ただ{f|結果|けっか}<b>のみだ</b>。
  A noun attaches to <code>のみ</code> directly. The copula belongs at the end of the
  sentence, not in the middle of it.

- ❌ ただ{f|待|ま}つ<b>だけのみだ</b>。 → ✅ ただ{f|待|ま}つ<b>のみだ</b>。（or {f|待|ま}つ<b>だけだ</b>）
  `だけ` and `のみ` are the same word in two registers. Stacking them is like writing
  "only merely".

- ❌ この{f|部屋|へや}はただ{f|静|しず}か<b>なのみだ</b>。 → ✅ この{f|部屋|へや}はただ{f|静|しず}か<b>なだけだ</b>。
  Adjectives do not take `のみ` naturally. If the thing being narrowed is a quality
  rather than a noun or an action, use `だけ`.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}はただ{f|寝|ね}る<b>のみだよ</b>。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}はもう{f|寝|ね}る<b>だけだよ</b>。
  Register clash. Sentence-final よ, ね and the contracted forms of speech do not go
  with a pattern whose whole flavour is formal declaration.

## Practice

**1.** {f|全力|ぜんりょく}で{f|準備|じゅんび}してきた。あとは____{f|信|しん}じる____だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

あとは<b>ただ{f|信|しん}じるのみ</b>だ。

The first clause establishes that everything else is finished, which is exactly the
set-up `ただ○○のみだ` needs. The verb goes in plain non-past form directly before
<code>のみ</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|台風|たいふう}の{f|被害|ひがい}は{f|農地|のうち}（のみだ・のみならず）、{f|住宅|じゅうたく}にも{f|広|ひろ}がった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|農地|のうち}<b>のみならず</b>

The sentence continues after the particle with a second item, so the set is being
opened, not closed. <code>のみだ</code> would have to end the sentence there and
would state the opposite of what the writer means.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, in a written register: "The report contains no interpretation; it records the facts and nothing more."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}に{f|解釈|かいしゃく}は{f|含|ふく}まれておらず、{f|記|しる}されているのは<b>ただ{f|事実|じじつ}のみである</b>。

<code>のみである</code> rather than <code>のみだ</code> because the sentence is
written prose; <code>である</code> is the register the pattern is happiest in.
{f|記|しる}す is the verb for setting something down in a document.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|練習|れんしゅう}は{f|終|お}わった。ただ{f|本番|ほんばん}だあるのみ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|練習|れんしゅう}は{f|終|お}わった。<b>ただ{f|本番|ほんばん}あるのみ</b>。

<code>ただ ～ あるのみ</code> takes a bare noun. The inserted `だ` breaks the set
phrase, which is exactly the shape people recognise from banners and pep talks -
<code>{f|努力|どりょく}あるのみ</code>, <code>{f|前進|ぜんしん}あるのみ</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) もう{f|祈|いの}る<b>しかない</b>。
(b) {f|我々|われわれ}にできるのは<b>ただ{f|祈|いの}ることのみである</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a person at the end of their options, and it can carry frustration - the
tone of "well, what else is there".

(b) is composed and public. It is the sentence of a spokesperson, a narrator or a
letter, and the formality reframes the same helplessness as something accepted
rather than complained about. The choice between them is a choice about how much
dignity the speaker wants the situation to have.

</details>
