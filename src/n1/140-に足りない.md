# ～に足りない

**Meaning** · not worth doing; too small to deserve the trouble - beneath notice, beneath fear, beneath argument

This is the negative side of <code>に{f|足|た}る</code>, and it is used far more
often than the affirmative. It says the thing does **not clear the bar**: it is
not worth taking seriously, not worth being afraid of, not worth arguing about.
The most frequent form by a wide margin is
<code>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りない</code>, "trivial, negligible", which behaves
like a ready-made adjective.

## Formation

The verb in front is in the **dictionary form**. {f|足|た}りない is the negative
of the る-verb {f|足|た}りる; {f|足|た}らない is the negative of the う-verb
{f|足|た}る. Both appear, and the fixed phrases prefer {f|足|た}らない.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + に{f|足|た}りない | {f|恐|おそ}れる → {f|恐|おそ}れるに{f|足|た}りない |
| Verb (う-verb negative) | dictionary form + に{f|足|た}らない | {f|取|と}る → {f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}らない |
| Noun (する-noun) | noun + に{f|足|た}りない | {f|信頼|しんらい} → {f|信頼|しんらい}に{f|足|た}りない |
| Modifying a noun | ～に{f|足|た}りない + noun | {f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りない{f|問題|もんだい} |
| Past | ～に{f|足|た}りなかった | {f|論|ろん}じるに{f|足|た}りなかった |
| Polite | ～に{f|足|た}りません | {f|恐|おそ}れるに{f|足|た}りません |

<div class="note">

**Note** - do not confuse this with the ordinary {f|足|た}りない you learned at
N5. <code>お{f|金|かね}が{f|足|た}りない</code> means there is not enough money.
<code>{f|恐|おそ}れるに{f|足|た}りない</code> means the thing is not worth fearing.
The particle に and the dictionary-form verb in front are what mark the grammar
point.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
そんな{f|噂|うわさ}は{f|恐|おそ}れる<b>に{f|足|た}りない</b>。
Rumours like that are nothing to be afraid of.
</pre>

<pre>
これは<b>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りない</b>{f|問題|もんだい}だ。
This is a trivial issue.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|根拠|こんきょ}のない{f|批判|ひはん}なので、いちいち{f|論|ろん}じる<b>に{f|足|た}りない</b>。
The criticism has no basis, so it is not worth answering point by point.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|相手|あいて}の{f|実力|じつりょく}を{f|考|かんが}えれば、{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|敗戦|はいせん}は{f|驚|おどろ}く<b>に{f|足|た}りない</b>。
Given how strong the opposition are, this defeat is hardly a surprise.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|数字|すうじ}の{f|上|うえ}では<b>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}らない</b>{f|差|さ}だが、{f|現場|げんば}では{f|大|おお}きな{f|意味|いみ}を{f|持|も}つ。
On paper the gap is negligible, but on the ground it matters a great deal.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自分|じぶん}{f|一人|ひとり}の{f|力|ちから}など<b>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りない</b>と{f|思|おも}っていたが、{f|集|あつ}まれば{f|話|はなし}は{f|別|べつ}だった。
I used to think one person's efforts counted for nothing, but together it turned out to be another matter.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|証拠|しょうこ}と{f|呼|よ}ぶ<b>に{f|足|た}りない</b>{f|断片|だんぺん}をいくら{f|並|なら}べても、{f|主張|しゅちょう}は{f|通|とお}らないだろう。
No matter how many scraps too slight to be called evidence you line up, the argument will not carry.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|当時|とうじ}の{f|私|わたし}には{f|重大|じゅうだい}な{f|悩|なや}みだったが、{f|今|いま}{f|思|おも}えば<b>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}らない</b>ことだった。
At the time it weighed on me terribly; looking back, it was nothing at all.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Written and formal, and above all **dismissive**. The speaker is not describing a
size, they are refusing to take something seriously - which is why the pattern is
at home in editorials, criticism, court judgements and confident prose, and why
it can sting when applied to a person's work.

<code>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りない</code> and
<code>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}らない</code> are effectively one word meaning
"trivial", and they modify nouns freely:
{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}らない{f|意見|いけん}, {f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りない
{f|存在|そんざい}. Outside that phrase the verb slot is small and evaluative:
{f|恐|おそ}れる, {f|驚|おどろ}く, {f|論|ろん}じる, {f|数|かぞ}える,
{f|信頼|しんらい}する, {f|呼|よ}ぶ.

There is a self-deprecating use worth recognising. Talking about one's own effort
or contribution as {f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りないものですが is modest and gracious;
saying the same of someone else's is a hard judgement. The words do not change -
the target does.

The everyday spoken equivalents are ささいな, たいしたことではない and
つまらない.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|足|た}りない vs ～にはあたらない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|足|た}りない</code> judges the **thing**: it is too
slight to deserve this. <code>にはあたらない</code> judges the **reaction**: given
the circumstances, there is no call for it.

</div>

<pre>
あの{f|程度|ていど}の{f|失敗|しっぱい}は{f|騒|さわ}ぐ<b>に{f|足|た}りない</b>。
A slip on that scale is not worth making a fuss about.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|練習|れんしゅう}{f|不足|ぶそく}だったのだから、{f|負|ま}けても{f|驚|おどろ}く<b>にはあたらない</b>。
They were short of practice, so losing is nothing to be surprised at.
</pre>

The first weighs the failure and finds it small. The second accepts a reason -
they had not trained - and concludes that surprise is misplaced. This is why
<code>にはあたらない</code> so often follows からといって or のだから, while
<code>に{f|足|た}りない</code> needs no such support.

### {f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りない vs ささいな

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ささいな is a neutral "minor". <code>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りない</code>
adds a verdict: not merely small, but **not worth picking up**.

</div>

<pre>
ささいなことで{f|喧嘩|けんか}してしまった。
We ended up quarrelling over something minor.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|指摘|してき}は<b>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}らない</b>と{f|一蹴|いっしゅう}された。
His point was brushed aside as not worth considering.
</pre>

ささいな can describe your own small worry warmly.
<code>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}らない</code> in the same slot sounds cold, because
it does not measure - it dismisses.

### ～に{f|足|た}りない vs ～ほどのことはない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both say "not worth it".
<code>ほどのことはない</code> is ordinary spoken Japanese about a specific
occasion; <code>に{f|足|た}りない</code> is written judgement about a thing's
worth in general.

</div>

<pre>
かすり{f|傷|きず}だから、{f|病院|びょういん}に{f|行|い}く<b>ほどのことはない</b>。
It's only a scratch - not worth going to the hospital for.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|反論|はんろん}は{f|一顧|いっこ}する<b>に{f|足|た}りない</b>。
That objection does not merit a moment's consideration.
</pre>

Say the second to a friend about a scratch and you sound as though you are
delivering a ruling. Put the first in a written judgement and it sounds like
chat.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|取|と}るに<b>{f|足|た}るない</b>。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|取|と}るに<b>{f|足|た}らない</b>。
  {f|足|た}る is an う-verb: the negative is {f|足|た}らない.
  {f|足|た}りない (from {f|足|た}りる) is the other correct option.

- ❌ {f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りない<b>だ</b>{f|問題|もんだい} → ✅ <b>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りない</b>{f|問題|もんだい}
  {f|足|た}りない is already a negative verb form, so it modifies a noun on its
  own. Neither だ nor な belongs there.

- ❌ {f|時間|じかん}が{f|見|み}るに{f|足|た}りない。 → ✅ {f|時間|じかん}が{f|足|た}りない。
  If you mean "there is not enough of it", use plain {f|足|た}りない with が. The
  grammar point needs a dictionary-form verb plus に.

- ❌ {f|驚|おどろ}くに{f|足|た}りない<b>でした</b>。 → ✅ {f|驚|おどろ}くに<b>{f|足|た}りませんでした</b>。
  This is a verb, not an い-adjective, so the polite past is
  {f|足|た}りませんでした.

## Practice

**1.** {f|敵|てき}の{f|数|かず}は{f|多|おお}いが、{f|装備|そうび}を{f|見|み}れば{f|恐|おそ}れる____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|恐|おそ}れる<b>に{f|足|た}りない</b>

The sentence weighs the enemy and finds them beneath fear, which is the core use
of the pattern; {f|恐|おそ}れる is one of the small set of evaluative verbs it
takes.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|前日|ぜんじつ}に{f|熱|ねつ}を{f|出|だ}していたのだから、{f|欠場|けつじょう}は（{f|驚|おどろ}くに{f|足|た}りない・{f|驚|おどろ}くにはあたらない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|欠場|けつじょう}は<b>{f|驚|おどろ}くにはあたらない</b>。

The clause before it supplies a reason - a fever the day before - and the
sentence concludes that surprise is uncalled for. That is
<code>にはあたらない</code>. <code>{f|驚|おどろ}くに{f|足|た}りない</code> would
be judging the withdrawal itself as too trifling to notice, which is not what the
reason clause sets up.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Compared with the scale of the problem, our efforts so far are negligible."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|問題|もんだい}の{f|大|おお}きさに{f|比|くら}べれば、これまでの{f|努力|どりょく}など<b>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りない</b>。

<code>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りない</code> is the standard rendering of
"negligible", and など in front of it strengthens the dismissal. Because the
speaker is talking about their own side's efforts, the sentence reads as modest
rather than harsh.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}の{f|意見|いけん}は{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りないなことだと{f|思|おも}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}の{f|意見|いけん}は<b>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りない</b>ことだと{f|思|おも}います。
"I think his opinion is not worth taking up."

{f|足|た}りない is a verb in its negative form, so it attaches straight to こと.
The な comes from treating it as a な-adjective, which it is not.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|私|わたし}のしたことなど<b>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りません</b>。
(b) あなたのしたことなど<b>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}りません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is modest. Applied to your own contribution, the phrase is a polite way of
waving away thanks - roughly "it was nothing" - and a Japanese listener will hear
courtesy, not self-hatred.

(b) is an insult. The same dismissal aimed outward tells the listener their work
did not merit picking up at all. The grammar is identical in both; only the
target has moved, and that is enough to change the sentence from graceful to
cutting.

</details>
