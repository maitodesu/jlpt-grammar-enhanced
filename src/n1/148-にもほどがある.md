# ～にもほどがある

**Meaning** · there is a limit to that; you have gone too far - a reproach thrown at behaviour that has passed the point of acceptability

ほど is {f|程|ほど}, "degree, measure". The sentence says that even for the thing
named there is a **limit**, and the person in front of you has sailed past it.
This is not a description of excess - it is an accusation, and it is delivered
with feeling.

## Formation

Whatever is being criticised goes in front of にもほどがある.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | noun + にもほどがある | {f|冗談|じょうだん} → {f|冗談|じょうだん}にもほどがある |
| な-adjective (bare stem) | stem + にもほどがある | {f|失礼|しつれい} → {f|失礼|しつれい}にもほどがある |
| い-adjective | plain form + にもほどがある | {f|図々|ずうずう}しい → {f|図々|ずうずう}しいにもほどがある |
| Verb | plain form + にもほどがある | ふざける → ふざけるにもほどがある |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + にもほどがある | {f|考|かんが}えない → {f|考|かんが}えないにもほどがある |
| Polite | ～にもほどがあります | {f|勝手|かって} → {f|勝手|かって}にもほどがあります |

<div class="note">

**Note** - な-adjectives lose their な here.
✅{f|失礼|しつれい}にもほどがある, ❌{f|失礼|しつれい}なにもほどがある. What
precedes にも is treated as a bare noun.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Spoken, blunt and emotional.** It is a scolding, and it is usually said with
raised feeling: to a friend who has overstepped, to a family member, in a
complaint, in an angry post. Because it passes judgement on the listener's
character it is not something to say upward - to a boss or a client it is close
to unusable. In writing it appears in opinion pieces and letters of complaint,
where it lands hard.

The frame is often preceded by a なんて or とは clause naming the offence, and
that is the shape to learn:
<code>～なんて、～にもほどがある</code>. だろう or よ frequently follows, which
is another sign of how spoken it is.

The polite {f|失礼|しつれい}にもほどがあります exists and appears in written
complaints, but politeness cannot really soften the content - it is a formal
accusation rather than a mild one.

One modern extension is worth recognising. Younger speakers, and the internet
generally, apply the frame to **admirable** qualities as a joke:
かわいいにもほどがある, "too cute to be legal". The literal shape is unchanged -
"there's a limit" - and the humour comes from the mismatch. Recognise it, and
keep your own use to genuine complaints until you are sure of the tone.

## Contrast

### ～にもほどがある vs ～すぎる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `すぎる` **describes** an excess. にもほどがある
**accuses** someone of one.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|重|おも}<b>すぎる</b>。
This luggage is too heavy.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|遅刻|ちこく}して{f|謝|あやま}りもしないとは、いい{f|加減|かげん}<b>にもほどがある</b>。
Turning up late and not even apologising is sloppiness beyond excuse.
</pre>

Luggage cannot be blamed, so only `すぎる` fits the first. In the second,
`すぎる` would report a fact - {f|遅|おそ}すぎる, "too late" - while
にもほどがある delivers the reproach. Note also that `すぎる` attaches to a stem
and conjugates as a verb; にもほどがある is a fixed phrase and does not.

### ～にもほどがある vs ～も{f|大概|たいがい}にしろ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are angry.
<code>にもほどがある</code> **states** that a limit has been passed;
<code>も{f|大概|たいがい}にしろ</code> and いい{f|加減|かげん}にしろ **order**
the person to stop.

</div>

<pre>
{f|冗談|じょうだん}も<b>{f|大概|たいがい}にしろ</b>。
That's enough of the jokes.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人|ひと}のせいにするのは、{f|甘|あま}え<b>にもほどがある</b>。
Blaming other people is a childishness that has gone past its limit.
</pre>

The imperative forms are rougher and male-leaning in tone, and they are aimed at
the person directly. <code>にもほどがある</code> can be muttered about someone
absent, and it can appear in writing, which the imperatives cannot.

### ～にもほどがある vs ～には{f|限度|げんど}がある

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>には{f|限度|げんど}がある</code> states the limit as a
general truth, often as a **warning**. <code>にもほどがある</code> says the limit
has **already** been crossed.

</div>

<pre>
{f|我慢|がまん}<b>には{f|限度|げんど}がある</b>。
There is a limit to what a person can put up with.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎回|まいかい}{f|同|おな}じ{f|言|い}い{f|訳|わけ}をするとは、{f|人|ひと}を{f|馬鹿|ばか}にする<b>にもほどがある</b>。
The same excuse every single time - that is taking people for fools, and going well past the limit in doing it.
</pre>

The first can be a calm warning issued before anything has happened; it takes
{f|我慢|がまん}, {f|予算|よさん}, {f|体力|たいりょく} - things that run out. The
second is retrospective and personal, and it is always about behaviour.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|冗談|じょうだん}<b>にほどがある</b> → ✅ {f|冗談|じょうだん}<b>にもほどがある</b>
  The も is part of the expression - "even for a joke, there is a limit".

- ❌ {f|失礼|しつれい}<b>なにもほどがある</b> → ✅ {f|失礼|しつれい}<b>にもほどがある</b>
  な-adjectives appear in their bare stem form before にも.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、その{f|指示|しじ}は<b>{f|無茶|むちゃ}にもほどがあります</b>。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、その{f|指示|しじ}は<b>さすがに{f|難|むずか}しいかと{f|思|おも}います</b>。
  The polite ending does not make the accusation polite. Said upward, this ends
  conversations and occasionally careers.

- ❌ {f|荷物|にもつ}が{f|重|おも}い<b>にもほどがある</b>。 → ✅ {f|荷物|にもつ}が<b>{f|重|おも}すぎる</b>。
  A suitcase cannot be blamed for anything. The pattern needs a responsible
  agent behind the excess.

## Practice

**1.** {f|人|ひと}の{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|三年|さんねん}も{f|間違|まちが}え{f|続|つづ}けるなんて、{f|失礼|しつれい}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|失礼|しつれい}<b>にもほどがある</b>

The なんて clause names the offence and the pattern delivers the verdict, which
is the standard two-part shape. {f|失礼|しつれい} keeps its bare stem, with no
な.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}い（にもほどがある・すぎる）ので、{f|机|つくえ}をもう{f|一|ひと}つ{f|置|お}くのは{f|無理|むり}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|部屋|へや}は<b>{f|狭|せま}すぎる</b>ので…

The sentence is describing a room, and a room is nobody's fault. にもほどがある
would be accusing the room of misbehaviour, which only works as a joke.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Cancelling the day before, twice in a row - there's a limit to how inconsiderate one person can be."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|二回|にかい}{f|続|つづ}けて{f|前日|ぜんじつ}にキャンセルするなんて、{f|勝手|かって}<b>にもほどがある</b>。

The English "there's a limit to" maps straight onto the pattern, and なんて in
front supplies the offence. {f|身勝手|みがって} or {f|失礼|しつれい} would do
just as well in the slot.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: あんな{f|大|おお}きな{f|声|こえ}で{f|電話|でんわ}するなんて、{f|非常識|ひじょうしき}なにほどがある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ あんな{f|大|おお}きな{f|声|こえ}で{f|電話|でんわ}するなんて、{f|非常識|ひじょうしき}<b>にもほどがある</b>。
"Taking a call at that volume - there's a limit to how thoughtless you can be."

Two repairs: {f|非常識|ひじょうしき} is a な-adjective but appears bare before
にも, and the も cannot be dropped.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|冗談|じょうだん}<b>にもほどがある</b>。
(b) {f|冗談|じょうだん}<b>も{f|大概|たいがい}にしろ</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a judgement: the joking has passed the point where it can be defended.
The speaker may be talking to the joker, or complaining about them to someone
else, and the sentence works in writing as well as speech.

(b) is a command aimed squarely at the person - stop, now. It is rougher and
carries a more confrontational, male-leaning tone, and it only exists face to
face. If you are angry but do not want to issue orders, (a) is the safer of the
two.

</details>
