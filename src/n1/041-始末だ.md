# ～始末だ

**Meaning** · and in the end it came to this - the final, deplorable stage of a downhill run, delivered as a complaint. Read <code>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</code>

{f|始末|しまつ} on its own means "how a thing turned out; the state of affairs" -
the same noun as in {f|後始末|あとしまつ}, "clearing up after". As a sentence
ending, <code>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</code> takes a series of events that has been going
wrong and reports the point it finally reached. The speaker is never neutral about
it: the form is a verdict.

## Formation

The clause in front is a **plain non-past** verb. The tense of the whole sentence
sits on だ.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain non-past) | Verb + {f|始末|しまつ}だ | {f|怒鳴|どな}り{f|出|だ}す → {f|怒鳴|どな}り{f|出|だ}す{f|始末|しまつ}だ |
| Verb (negative) | ～ない + {f|始末|しまつ}だ | {f|返事|へんじ}もしない{f|始末|しまつ}だ |
| Pointing back at the outcome | この／その + {f|始末|しまつ}だ | {f|結局|けっきょく}この{f|始末|しまつ}だ |
| Past | ～{f|始末|しまつ}だった | {f|泣|な}き{f|出|だ}す{f|始末|しまつ}だった |
| Polite | ～{f|始末|しまつ}です | {f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}つ{f|始末|しまつ}です |
| Formal written | ～{f|始末|しまつ}である | {f|放置|ほうち}される{f|始末|しまつ}である |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the pattern is nearly always announced in advance by an escalation
marker: {f|挙|あ}げ{f|句|く}(に), しまいには, とうとう, ついには,
{f|最後|さいご}には. If you can hear one of those in the sentence,
{f|始末|しまつ}だ is what the listener is already waiting for at the end.

</div>

## Nuance & register

<code>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</code> is a **judgement**, not a report, and the
exasperation is the whole point of using it. It turns up in complaint columns, in
essays and novels, in a critical passage of a news feature, and in spoken grumbling
about a colleague or a relative. {f|始末|しまつ}です softens the delivery but not
the criticism; the polite form is used to be civil about someone while still saying
they were hopeless.

It is normally aimed at **somebody else**. Used about oneself it becomes
self-mocking, which works but is a deliberate effect. Used about a company or an
institution, it is the shape of a formal complaint.

The pattern needs a **run-up**. It sums up a sequence, so a single isolated event
cannot support it: ❌{f|財布|さいふ}を{f|落|お}とす{f|始末|しまつ}だ has nothing to
be the end of. It also cannot describe a good ending - there is no neutral or
positive {f|始末|しまつ}だ, and where the outcome is simply the point a process
reached, formal Japanese uses <code>に{f|至|いた}った</code> instead.

One more thing worth knowing: because it editorialises, it does not appear in the
neutral body of a report or a set of minutes. A company writing about its own
results uses plain statements; a journalist writing *about* that company may well
use {f|始末|しまつ}だ.

## Contrast

### ～{f|始末|しまつ}だ vs ～{f|羽目|はめ}になる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|羽目|はめ}になる</code> is about **you** being landed
with a job. <code>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</code> is about **the state things ended up
in**, usually because of someone else, and it condemns.

</div>

<pre>
{f|同僚|どうりょう}が{f|休|やす}んで、{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|受付|うけつけ}をする<b>{f|羽目|はめ}になった</b>。
My colleague was off, so I got stuck manning reception on my own.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|同僚|どうりょう}は{f|連絡|れんらく}もせずに{f|休|やす}み、{f|今|いま}では{f|電話|でんわ}にも{f|出|で}ない<b>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b>。
My colleague took time off without a word, and now doesn't even answer the phone.
</pre>

The first reports an inconvenience the speaker had to absorb; the second is a case
being built against somebody.

### ～{f|始末|しまつ}だ vs ～{f|挙|あ}げ{f|句|く}(に)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|挙|あ}げ{f|句|く}に</code> is a **connective** in the
middle of a sentence, "and after all that…". <code>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</code>
**ends** the sentence with the verdict. They are natural partners, not rivals.

</div>

<pre>
{f|散々|さんざん}{f|待|ま}たせた<b>{f|挙|あ}げ{f|句|く}に</b>、{f|約束|やくそく}を{f|忘|わす}れていたと{f|言|い}った。
After keeping me waiting for ages, he said he'd forgotten the arrangement.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|散々|さんざん}{f|待|ま}たせた{f|挙|あ}げ{f|句|く}に、{f|約束|やくそく}そのものを{f|忘|わす}れている<b>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b>。
After keeping me waiting for ages, he had forgotten the arrangement altogether.
</pre>

{f|挙|あ}げ{f|句|く}に takes a past-tense clause in front of it and can be followed
by any ending at all; {f|始末|しまつ}だ takes a non-past clause and can only be the
ending. Used together, as in the second example, they make the classic complaint
shape.

### ～{f|始末|しまつ}だ vs ～{f|有様|ありさま}だ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|有様|ありさま}だ</code> shows you the **wretched
picture**. <code>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</code> insists it is the **end point of a
process**, and assigns blame for it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}の{f|後|あと}、{f|畑|はたけ}は{f|足|あし}の{f|踏|ふ}み{f|場|ば}もない<b>{f|有様|ありさま}だった</b>。
After the typhoon there was nowhere to put your foot in the fields.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|手入|てい}れを{f|怠|おこた}り{f|続|つづ}け、{f|今|いま}では{f|草|くさ}に{f|埋|う}もれる<b>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b>。
Maintenance was neglected year after year, and now the place is buried in weeds.
</pre>

A storm-hit field is simply a state, and {f|有様|ありさま}だ can describe something
you are looking at with no story behind it. {f|始末|しまつ}だ needs the story, and
it holds someone responsible for it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|失敗|しっぱい}した<b>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b> → ✅ {f|失敗|しっぱい}する<b>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b>
  The clause before {f|始末|しまつ} is plain non-past. If the whole sentence needs
  the past, write {f|始末|しまつ}だった.

- ❌ {f|優勝|ゆうしょう}する<b>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b> → ✅ {f|優勝|ゆうしょう}するに<b>{f|至|いた}った</b>
  {f|始末|しまつ}だ only reports endings the speaker deplores. For a neutral or
  good outcome reached after a long process, use に{f|至|いた}る.

- ❌ {f|遅刻|ちこく}の<b>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b> → ✅ {f|遅刻|ちこく}する<b>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b>
  A bare noun plus の cannot stand in front of it. The only things that can appear
  there directly are この and その.

- ❌ {f|今朝|けさ}バスに{f|乗|の}り{f|遅|おく}れる<b>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b> → ✅ {f|今朝|けさ}はバスに{f|乗|の}り{f|遅|おく}れてしまった
  One isolated mishap has no downhill run behind it, and the heavy verdict makes the
  sentence sound absurd.

## Practice

**1.** {f|彼|かれ}は{f|約束|やくそく}を{f|破|やぶ}り{f|続|つづ}け、とうとう{f|電話|でんわ}にも{f|出|で}ない____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|電話|でんわ}にも{f|出|で}ない<b>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b>

とうとう has already told the listener that a downhill run is being summed up, and
the negative ない attaches to {f|始末|しまつ}だ with no change. The sentence is a
complaint, which is exactly what the pattern is for.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|部長|ぶちょう}が{f|急|きゅう}に{f|出張|しゅっちょう}になり、{f|私|わたし}が{f|代|か}わりに{f|発表|はっぴょう}する（{f|始末|しまつ}だった・{f|羽目|はめ}になった）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|私|わたし}が{f|代|か}わりに{f|発表|はっぴょう}する<b>{f|羽目|はめ}になった</b>。

The speaker was landed with a job by a single change of plan - that is
{f|羽目|はめ}になる. {f|始末|しまつ}だ would need a whole string of failures behind
it and would sound as though the speaker were condemning their own presentation.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He kept borrowing money from everyone, and in the end he stopped showing his face at the office."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}は{f|誰|だれ}かれ{f|構|かま}わず{f|金|かね}を{f|借|か}り、とうとう{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|顔|かお}を{f|出|だ}さなくなる<b>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b>。

"Kept borrowing … and in the end" is the escalation the pattern needs, and とうとう
signals it. Note that the final verb stays non-past
({f|出|だ}さなくなる), even though the story is in the past.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|遅刻|ちこく}を{f|繰|く}り{f|返|かえ}し、{f|挙|あ}げ{f|句|く}に{f|謝|あやま}らなかった{f|始末|しまつ}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|遅刻|ちこく}を{f|繰|く}り{f|返|かえ}し、{f|挙|あ}げ{f|句|く}に<b>{f|謝|あやま}りもしない{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b>。

The clause in front of {f|始末|しまつ}だ has to be non-past, so
{f|謝|あやま}らなかった must become {f|謝|あやま}らない - and adding も
({f|謝|あやま}りもしない) is the idiomatic way to underline that she did not even do
the minimum.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|店|みせ}は{f|客|きゃく}が{f|減|へ}り、{f|閉|し}めることになった。
(b) {f|店|みせ}は{f|客|きゃく}が{f|減|へ}り、とうとう{f|家賃|やちん}も{f|払|はら}えない<b>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports a decision in neutral language. Custom fell off and the shop is closing;
the sentence could appear in a local newspaper without editorialising.

(b) is a complaint or a lament. It picks the worst point the decline reached - not
even making the rent - and the form tells you the speaker is exasperated, whether at
the owner, the landlord or the world. It would sit oddly in a neutral news report.

</details>
