# さもないと

**Meaning** · otherwise; or else; if you don't - looks back at an instruction or a condition and names the bad thing that follows if it is not met

Pull it apart and it is transparent: `さ` is the old "so, that way" that survives in
そう and さよう, `も` is emphatic, `ない` negates, `と` makes the conditional. "And
if that is **not** how it goes ~". Everything after it is unwelcome; that is not a
tendency but a fixed part of the word.

## Usage

<code>さもないと</code> is a conjunctive adverb. It opens the clause that carries
the consequence, either after a full stop or after a comma, and it never changes
form and never attaches to anything.

What must come **before** it is a directive or a requirement: an imperative, a
request, a <code>べきだ</code>, a <code>なければならない</code>, a
<code>ほうがいい</code>, or a suggestion with <code>よう</code>. The word is
pointing back at a course of action and saying "take it".

What must come **after** it is an outcome the listener would rather avoid, and it
is usually non-past: <code>{f|遅|おく}れる</code>,
<code>{f|失|うしな}う</code>, <code>{f|無効|むこう}になる</code>,
<code>…ことになる</code>, <code>…てしまう</code>.

It has two sisters with the same meaning and a different weight:

- <code>さもないと</code> - the plainest of the three, and the only one you might
  hear said aloud.
- <code>さもなければ</code> - the standard written form; commonest in print.
- <code>さもなくば</code> - old and literary. Fiction, slogans, translated
  dialogue. Do not put it in an email.

<div class="note">

**Note** - `さも` also lives in a completely separate expression,
<code>さも…そうに</code> / <code>さも…ように</code>, meaning "just as if,
for all the world like": <code>さも{f|当然|とうぜん}のように{f|席|せき}に{f|座|すわ}った</code>.
Same little `さ`, unrelated job. Do not read one as the other.

</div>

<pre>

Let's leave now. Otherwise we won't make the last train.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}って{f|行|い}きなさい。<b>さもないと</b>ずぶ{f|濡|ぬ}れになるよ。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|期日|きじつ}までにご{f|連絡|れんらく}ください。<b>さもないと</b>お{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みは{f|無効|むこう}となります。

</pre>

We should replace the equipment while we still can. Otherwise, in a few years we will be in no position to compete.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>さもないと</code> is **formal and written-leaning**, and it is worth being
blunt about that, because its English translation is entirely ordinary. "Otherwise"
is neutral in English; <code>さもないと</code> is not neutral in Japanese. Its home
turf is notices, terms and conditions, safety warnings, editorials, subtitles and
dubbed dialogue, and the kind of speech that has been prepared in advance. A parent
or a boss may say it in earnest, and it lands as a genuine ultimatum.

In everyday conversation the same job is done by <code>そうしないと</code>,
<code>でないと</code>, <code>じゃないと</code> or by hanging `ないと` straight off
the verb. A friend telling you to hurry says
「{f|早|はや}く{f|行|い}かないと{f|遅|おく}れるよ」, not
「{f|早|はや}く{f|行|い}きなさい。さもないと…」.

Two consequences follow from the register. First, because it is stern, it carries
an implicit threat when the speaker controls the outcome: 「{f|静|しず}かにしろ。<b>さもないと</b>{f|出|で}て{f|行|い}ってもらう」
is a warning, not advice. Second, in fiction it is a stock marker of the villain,
the drill sergeant and the ultimatum, so overusing it in ordinary writing makes the
prose sound like a translation.

## Contrast

### さもないと vs そうしないと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register.
<code>そうしないと</code> is what people actually say; <code>さもないと</code> is
what gets written down.

</div>

<pre>
{f|薬|くすり}は{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|飲|の}んでね。<b>そうしないと</b>{f|効|き}かないよ。
Take the medicine every day, all right? It won't work otherwise.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|服用|ふくよう}は{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|決|き}まった{f|時間|じかん}に。<b>さもないと</b>{f|十分|じゅうぶん}な{f|効果|こうか}が{f|得|え}られません。
Take the dose at the same time each day; otherwise the full effect will not be obtained.
</pre>

There is one grammatical difference behind the register gap.
<code>そうしないと</code> contains する, so it needs the preceding clause to be an
**action**. <code>さもないと</code> has no verb in it and can follow a state as
well: 「{f|室温|しつおん}は{f|二十度|にじゅうど}{f|以下|いか}でなければならない。<b>さもないと</b>{f|機械|きかい}が{f|止|と}まる」.

### さもないと vs さもなければ・さもなくば

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one meaning, three heights.
<code>さもないと</code> < <code>さもなければ</code> < <code>さもなくば</code>,
from merely formal to frankly literary.

</div>

<pre>
{f|全員|ぜんいん}の{f|同意|どうい}が{f|要|い}る。<b>さもないと</b>{f|話|はなし}が{f|前|まえ}に{f|進|すす}まない。
We need everyone to agree. Otherwise the discussion goes nowhere.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|全員|ぜんいん}の{f|同意|どうい}を{f|得|え}なければならない。<b>さもなければ</b>、{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|白紙|はくし}に{f|戻|もど}る。
Unanimous agreement must be obtained; failing that, the plan goes back to square one.
</pre>

<code>さもなくば</code> belongs with it, one step further up:
<code>{f|降伏|こうふく}せよ、さもなくば{f|滅|ほろ}びる</code> reads as a line from
a historical drama, which is where you will meet it. Recognise it; do not write it.

### さもないと vs ～ないと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>さもないと</code> refers back to a **whole preceding
sentence**. Plain `ないと` negates **one named verb** inside its own sentence.

</div>

<pre>
{f|急|いそ}が<b>ないと</b>{f|遅|おく}れるよ。
You'll be late if you don't hurry.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|急|いそ}いだ{f|方|ほう}がいい。<b>さもないと</b>{f|遅|おく}れるよ。
You'd better hurry. Otherwise you'll be late.
</pre>

The two are not interchangeable in shape: `ないと` is a suffix and needs a verb
stem in front of it, while <code>さもないと</code> is a free word and needs a
sentence behind it. Trying to use the second as a suffix -
❌<code>{f|急|いそ}ぐさもないと</code> - is the single most common error with it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}しなさい。<b>さもないと</b>{f|上手|じょうず}になりますよ。 → ✅ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}しなさい。<b>さもないと</b>{f|上手|じょうず}になりませんよ。
  What follows <code>さもないと</code> has to be bad news. A desirable outcome
  contradicts the word itself.

- ❌ {f|走|はし}る<b>さもないと</b>{f|遅|おく}れる。 → ✅ {f|走|はし}ろう。<b>さもないと</b>{f|遅|おく}れる。
  It is an adverb, not a suffix. Finish the first sentence, then start the next one
  with it.

- ❌ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}い。<b>さもないと</b>{f|問題|もんだい}はない。 → ✅ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}い。<b>それ{f|以外|いがい}に</b>{f|問題|もんだい}はない。
  English "otherwise" has a second sense, "in other respects", and
  <code>さもないと</code> does not share it. Use
  <code>それ{f|以外|いがい}は</code> or <code>その{f|他|ほか}は</code>.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|雨|あめ}だった。<b>さもないと</b>{f|試合|しあい}があった。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|雨|あめ}だった。{f|雨|あめ}でなければ{f|試合|しあい}があったはずだ。
  <code>さもないと</code> needs a directive in front of it and points forward to a
  consequence. It cannot rewrite the past as a counterfactual.

## Practice

**1.** {f|荷物|にもつ}から{f|目|め}を{f|離|はな}さないでください。____{f|盗|ぬす}まれても{f|責任|せきにん}は{f|負|お}いかねます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>さもないと</b>

The first sentence is an instruction and the second is an unwelcome consequence of
ignoring it - exactly the frame this word sits in. The formal
<code>{f|負|お}いかねます</code> at the end confirms that the notice register fits.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 「ちょっと、{f|早|はや}く{f|準備|じゅんび}して。（さもないと・そうしないと）{f|置|お}いて{f|行|い}くよ」と{f|妹|いもうと}に{f|言|い}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>そうしないと</b>

The frame is a line spoken casually to a younger sister, and
<code>さもないと</code> would sound as though she were being read the terms of a
contract. Between family members the ordinary spoken forms
<code>そうしないと</code>, <code>でないと</code> and plain `ないと` are what get
used.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|時間|じかん}を{f|守|まも}ってください。<b>さもないと</b>{f|参加|さんか}をお{f|断|ことわ}りします。
(b) {f|時間|じかん}を{f|守|まも}ってくださいね。{f|守|まも}らないと{f|参加|さんか}できなくなっちゃいますよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a rule being stated. The register is that of a printed notice or an official
speaking on the record, and <code>お{f|断|ことわ}りします</code> makes the
consequence a decision the speaker will take. It is close to an ultimatum.

(b) is the same information delivered as a friendly warning. The `ね` and `よ`, the
contraction <code>ちゃいます</code>, and the potential
<code>{f|参加|さんか}できなくなる</code> all shift the blame onto circumstances
rather than the speaker. Nobody would feel threatened by it.

</details>
