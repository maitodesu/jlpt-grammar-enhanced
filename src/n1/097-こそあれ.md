# ～こそあれ

**Meaning** · there may be A, but…; there is A, if anything - grants that one thing exists, and uses that concession to deny or outweigh something else

`こそ` is the emphatic particle you already know from これこそ and
{f|今|いま}こそ. In classical Japanese a clause marked with `こそ` had to end its
verb in the {f|已然形|いぜんけい}, and ある's {f|已然形|いぜんけい} is あれ. The agreement rule died centuries ago;
this fossil did not. `こそあれ` is therefore *there certainly is A - and now here is
what follows from admitting that*.

## Formation

<code>あれ</code> is the verb ある, so what stands in front of it is a **noun** or
something wearing a noun's clothes.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + こそあれ | {f|違|ちが}い → {f|違|ちが}いこそあれ |
| Noun (predicative) | Noun + でこそあれ | {f|挑戦|ちょうせん} → {f|挑戦|ちょうせん}でこそあれ |
| な-adjective | ～で + こそあれ | {f|不便|ふべん} → {f|不便|ふべん}でこそあれ |
| い-adjective (rare) | ～く + こそあれ | {f|少|すく}ない → {f|少|すく}なくこそあれ |
| Variants | ～こそあれど / ～こそあるが | {f|差|さ}こそあれど |
| Modern equivalent | ～はあっても / ～はあるが | {f|差|さ}はあっても |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a **verb** cannot stand in front of `あれ`. An action takes the
sibling form <code>こそすれ</code>, built on the {f|已然形|いぜんけい} of `する`:
{f|増|ふ}えこそすれ, not ❌{f|増|ふ}えこそあれ. If what you are conceding *exists*,
use あれ; if it *happens*, use すれ.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - two shapes of sentence follow. Either the second clause simply carries on
in spite of the concession - {f|差|さ}こそあれ、{f|本質|ほんしつ}は{f|同|おな}じだ -
or it denies a rival item outright:
{f|感謝|かんしゃ}こそあれ、{f|恨|うら}みはない. The second is the more emphatic, and
it is the one that shades into <code>こそすれ</code>.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise this; never say it.** `こそあれ` is fossilised classical grammar living
on in modern **written** Japanese of a formal kind: editorials, prefaces, prepared
speeches, official statements, the summing-up paragraph of a report, and literary
prose. Its {f|已然形|いぜんけい} あれ is not a form of ある that modern Japanese produces anywhere
else. Used in conversation it does not sound "rather formal" - it sounds like
somebody has begun reciting, and the listener will notice.

What you should produce instead is <code>～はあっても</code> or
<code>～はあるが</code>. 「{f|多少|たしょう}の{f|違|ちが}いはあっても、{f|本質|ほんしつ}は{f|同|おな}じだ」
says exactly the same thing in the language people speak.

Two things `こそあれ` is good at, and worth recognising it by. First, **conceding a
small thing to protect a big one**: yes there is a delay, no the project is not
dead. Second, **rejecting an accusation by naming the opposite feeling**:
{f|感謝|かんしゃ}こそあれ、{f|恨|うら}みはない is close to a set formula in formal
apology and formal denial, and it is considerably more emphatic than a plain
{f|恨|うら}んでいません.

## Contrast

### ～こそあれ vs ～はあっても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same sentence, one in classical dress and one in modern.
<code>はあっても</code> is what you write in an email; `こそあれ` is what you read in
a leader column.

</div>

<pre>
{f|多少|たしょう}の{f|不満|ふまん}<b>はあっても</b>、やめるつもりはありません。
I have my complaints, but I have no intention of quitting.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|多少|たしょう}の{f|不満|ふまん}<b>こそあれ</b>、{f|職|しょく}を{f|辞|じ}する{f|意思|いし}はない。
Grievances there may be; of any intention to resign there is none.
</pre>

Notice how the rest of the sentence has to move with it. `こそあれ` drags the whole
register upward - {f|辞|じ}する rather than やめる,
{f|意思|いし}はない rather than つもりはありません - because a classical concessive
sitting in a colloquial sentence sounds like a costume half put on.

### ～こそあれ vs ～こそすれ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `あれ` is ある and takes **a noun**: something *exists*. `すれ`
is する and takes **a verb stem**: something *happens*. Same fossil, two verbs.

</div>

<pre>
{f|不安|ふあん}<b>こそあれ</b>、{f|後悔|こうかい}はしていない。
Anxious I may be, but I have no regrets.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|不安|ふあん}は{f|増|ふ}え<b>こそすれ</b>、{f|減|へ}ることはないだろう。
The anxiety will only grow; it certainly will not lessen.
</pre>

The first weighs one existing thing against another. The second predicts the
direction of a process and rules out the opposite direction. If you can put "there
is" in front of the English, you want あれ; if you can put "it will only", you want
すれ.

### ～こそあれ vs plain ～こそ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - it is the **same particle**, and `あれ` is the ending that the
old grammar demanded after it. Nothing about `こそ` has changed; only the verb ending
survives from the older language.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}<b>こそ</b>{f|決断|けつだん}すべき{f|時|とき}だ。
Now is precisely the moment to decide.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|決断|けつだん}の{f|遅|おそ}さ<b>こそあれ</b>、{f|方向|ほうこう}{f|自体|じたい}は{f|間違|まちが}っていない。
Slow in deciding they may have been; the direction itself is not wrong.
</pre>

In modern Japanese `こそ` simply spotlights the word it follows and the sentence
carries on normally. In `こそあれ` the spotlight is still there, but the sentence
must then end in あれ and hand over to a second clause. Seeing them as one particle
with a fossilised tail makes the form much easier to parse at reading speed - which
is the only speed at which you will meet it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|違|ちが}い<b>こそある</b>、{f|結論|けつろん}は{f|同|おな}じだ。 → ✅ {f|違|ちが}い<b>こそあれ</b>、{f|結論|けつろん}は{f|同|おな}じだ。
  The whole point of the form is the {f|已然形|いぜんけい} `あれ`. With plain `ある` the clause
  simply stops and the sentence falls apart.

- ❌ {f|増|ふ}え<b>こそあれ</b>、{f|減|へ}りはしない。 → ✅ {f|増|ふ}え<b>こそすれ</b>、{f|減|へ}りはしない。
  `あれ` is ある and needs something that exists. A verb stem takes `すれ`.

- ❌ {f|問題|もんだい}<b>こそあれ</b>。 → ✅ {f|問題|もんだい}<b>こそあれ</b>、{f|運営|うんえい}に{f|支障|ししょう}はない。
  It is a connective, not an ending. A second clause always follows.

- ❌ （to a friend）ちょっと{f|不満|ふまん}<b>こそあれ</b>、まあいいや。 → ✅ ちょっと{f|不満|ふまん}<b>はあるけど</b>、まあいいや。
  A register collision. Classical concord inside a sentence that ends in まあいいや
  is the linguistic equivalent of a dinner jacket with shorts.

## Practice

**1.** {f|表現|ひょうげん}の{f|違|ちが}い____、{f|二人|ふたり}の{f|言|い}っていることは{f|同|おな}じだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|表現|ひょうげん}の{f|違|ちが}い<b>こそあれ</b>、{f|二人|ふたり}の{f|言|い}っていることは{f|同|おな}じだ。

A small difference is granted in order to protect the larger claim of sameness -
the pattern's commonest job. The word in front is a noun, which is what `あれ`
requires.

</details>

**2.** Where would you meet this sentence?
{f|批判|ひはん}<b>こそあれ</b>、{f|当社|とうしゃ}の{f|姿勢|しせい}に{f|変|か}わりはございません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In a written corporate statement - a press release, a notice on a company website,
a formal reply to a complaint. The polite ございません and the classical `こそあれ`
belong to the same register.

You would not hear it in a meeting. Spoken, it would be
「{f|批判|ひはん}はありますが、{f|方針|ほうしん}は{f|変|か}えません」.

</details>

**3.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|苦労|くろう}<b>こそあれ</b>、この{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|選|えら}んだことを{f|後悔|こうかい}してはいない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|苦労|くろう}<b>はあっても</b>、この{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|選|えら}んだことを{f|後悔|こうかい}していない。

<code>はあっても</code> is the direct modern equivalent, and once the classical form
goes the rest of the sentence can relax too - していない rather than してはいない.
This is the substitution to make whenever you are tempted to produce `こそあれ`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|参加者|さんかしゃ}は{f|減|へ}り<b>こそあれ</b>、{f|増|ふ}えることはないだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|参加者|さんかしゃ}は{f|減|へ}り<b>こそすれ</b>、{f|増|ふ}えることはないだろう。

{f|減|へ}り is a verb stem - an action, not a thing that exists - so the form is
`こそすれ`, the {f|已然形|いぜんけい} of `する`. `あれ` is reserved for nouns.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|失敗|しっぱい}<b>はあったが</b>、{f|全体|ぜんたい}としては{f|成功|せいこう}だった。
(b) {f|失敗|しっぱい}<b>こそあれ</b>、{f|全体|ぜんたい}としては{f|成功|せいこう}であった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The propositional content is identical: there were failures, and the project
succeeded overall.

(a) is ordinary modern Japanese, usable in a meeting or an email, and it sounds like
somebody being straightforward.

(b) is the written verdict. `こそ` pushes hard on the concession - *failures there
certainly were* - which paradoxically strengthens the defence that follows, and the
classical あれ marks the sentence as a formal judgement rather than a remark. Expect
it in a report's conclusion or a chairman's statement, and expect であった rather
than だった to keep it company.

</details>
