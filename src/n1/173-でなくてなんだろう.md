# ～でなくてなんだろう

**Meaning** · if this is not ～, then what is it? - a rhetorical question used to assert, with feeling, that something is exactly that

The sentence is a question in form only. By daring the reader to supply another
label, the writer asserts the label they have just given:
<code>{f|奇跡|きせき}でなくてなんだろう</code> means "this is nothing other than a
miracle." It is a **written**
device - the closing line of a paragraph in an essay, a column, or a passage of
narration - and it needs an abstract, evaluative noun to work on.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (abstract) | noun + でなくてなんだろう | {f|奇跡|きせき} → {f|奇跡|きせき}でなくてなんだろう |
| With か | ～でなくてなんだろうか | {f|差別|さべつ}でなくてなんだろうか |
| More literary | ～でなくてなんであろう（か） | {f|幸|しあわ}せでなくてなんであろうか |
| Usual frame | これが～でなくてなんだろう | これが{f|愛|あい}でなくてなんだろう |
| Never an adjective | ❌{f|美|うつく}しいでなくてなんだろう | ✅{f|美|うつく}しさでなくてなんだろう |

<div class="note">

**Note - which nouns work.** The slot takes a **label a reader could argue
about**: {f|愛|あい}, {f|奇跡|きせき}, {f|運命|うんめい}, {f|勇気|ゆうき},
{f|幸|しあわ}せ, {f|才能|さいのう}, {f|差別|さべつ}, {f|矛盾|むじゅん},
{f|偽善|ぎぜん}, {f|暴力|ぼうりょく}, {f|無責任|むせきにん}. The whole force of the
pattern is "you would struggle to call this anything else", so a noun with nothing
to dispute makes it collapse: ❌これが{f|机|つくえ}でなくてなんだろう is a joke, not
a sentence.

</div>

A man they had given up on walked out of the hospital on his own two feet. Call it anything but a miracle.

The whole family around one table. It is an ordinary thing, and if it is not happiness, what is?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise this; do not say it.** It lives in print: newspaper columns, personal
essays, literary criticism, the narrator's voice in a novel, occasionally a
speech written to be moving. Spoken in conversation it sounds as though the
speaker had begun reciting. The plain modern ways to make the same point are
<code>まさに{f|奇跡|きせき}だ</code>,
<code>{f|奇跡|きせき}としか{f|言|い}いようがない</code> and
<code>{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|奇跡|きせき}だと{f|思|おも}う</code>, and those are what
you should produce.

Two forces are stacked in it. The rhetorical question shifts the burden onto the
reader - *you* try naming it something else - and the sentence-final だろう makes
the claim sound arrived at rather than declared. That combination is why it feels
emotional rather than argumentative, and why it works equally well for admiration
({f|奇跡|きせき}, {f|勇気|ゆうき}) and for indignation
({f|差別|さべつ}, {f|偽善|ぎぜん}).

<code>であろう</code> is the more literary of the two endings and dates the prose
slightly; it suits an essayist writing in a considered, old-fashioned voice.
Adding か -
<code>でなくてなんだろうか</code> - softens the sentence a little and makes it feel
more like a question genuinely put to the reader, though it is still not one that
expects an answer.

The pattern almost always comes **after** the evidence, as the last sentence of a
short paragraph. Placed first it has nothing to land on.

## Contrast

### ～でなくてなんだろう vs まさに～だ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>まさに～だ</code> is the modern, sayable equivalent, and
it asserts directly. <code>でなくてなんだろう</code> stages the same assertion as a
challenge, and belongs on a page.

</div>

<pre>
{f|三十年|さんじゅうねん}{f|一度|いちど}も{f|休|やす}まなかったという。これが<b>{f|努力|どりょく}でなくてなんだろう</b>。
Thirty years without missing a single day. If that is not dedication, what is?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三十年|さんじゅうねん}{f|休|やす}まずに{f|続|つづ}けたなんて、<b>まさに{f|努力|どりょく}の{f|人|ひと}だ</b>。
Keeping it up for thirty years without a break - he really is a model of hard work.
</pre>

Same admiration; only the second can be said aloud to a colleague without
sounding like a broadcast.

### ～でなくてなんだろう vs ～としか{f|言|い}いようがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both mean "there is no other word for it".
<code>としか{f|言|い}いようがない</code> is neutral, usable in speech and in
business writing; <code>でなくてなんだろう</code> is emotional and literary.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|時期|じき}に{f|値上|ねあ}げをするとは、{f|無神経|むしんけい}<b>としか{f|言|い}いようがない</b>。
Raising prices at a time like this can only be called insensitive.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|時期|じき}に{f|値上|ねあ}げをするのは、{f|無神経|むしんけい}<b>でなくてなんだろうか</b>。
To raise prices at a time like this - what is that if not insensitivity?
</pre>

The first would pass in a report or a meeting. The second reads as a columnist
inviting readers to be angry with them.

### ～でなくてなんだろう vs ～ではないだろうか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - these look alike and pull in opposite directions.
<code>ではないだろうか</code> is a **hedge**: "might it not be ～?"
<code>でなくてなんだろう</code> is the **strongest** assertion in the family.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|決定|けってい}は{f|拙速|せっそく}<b>ではないだろうか</b>。
Was this decision not perhaps taken rather too hastily?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}もなく{f|決|き}めてしまうのは、{f|独断|どくだん}<b>でなくてなんだろう</b>。
Deciding without a word of explanation - what is that if not high-handedness?
</pre>

The first offers the reader room to disagree and is common in careful writing and
in polite spoken argument. The second closes the door. Choosing the wrong one
turns a mild suggestion into an accusation, or an accusation into a shrug.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|美|うつく}しい<b>でなくてなんだろう</b> → ✅ {f|美|うつく}しさ<b>でなくてなんだろう</b>
  The slot needs a noun. Turn the adjective into a ～さ noun, or use
  <code>としか{f|言|い}いようがない</code>, which takes adjectives freely.

- ❌ これが{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}<b>でなくてなんだろう</b>。 → ✅ これが{f|奇跡|きせき}<b>でなくてなんだろう</b>。
  The noun must be a judgement someone could contest. With a concrete object there
  is nothing to assert, and the sentence reads as a riddle.

- ❌ これが{f|愛|あい}<b>でなくてなんですか</b>。 → ✅ これが{f|愛|あい}<b>でなくてなんだろう</b>。
  The ending is fixed: だろう or であろう, optionally with か. The polite ですか
  turns it into a real question, which is precisely what it is not.

- ❌ （in a meeting）その{f|進|すす}め{f|方|かた}は{f|独断|どくだん}でなくてなんだろう。 → ✅ （in a meeting）その{f|進|すす}め{f|方|かた}は{f|独断的|どくだんてき}ではないでしょうか。
  Register. In a room full of people this lands as a speech; the hedged question
  is how the same objection is raised in Japanese business talk.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to read this sentence? {f|見返|みかえ}りを{f|求|もと}めずに{f|十年|じゅうねん}{f|通|かよ}い{f|続|つづ}けた。これが{f|献身|けんしん}でなくてなんだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

At the end of a paragraph in an essay, a newspaper column or a piece of
narration. The giveaways are the evidence-then-verdict structure, the abstract
noun {f|献身|けんしん}, and the plain だろう ending, which no one uses this way in
speech. Read aloud in conversation it would sound like a recitation.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|見|み}ず{f|知|し}らずの{f|人|ひと}が{f|川|かわ}に{f|飛|と}び{f|込|こ}んで{f|助|たす}けたという。{f|勇気|ゆうき}でなくてなんだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|見|み}ず{f|知|し}らずの{f|人|ひと}が{f|川|かわ}に{f|飛|と}び{f|込|こ}んで{f|助|たす}けたそうです。<b>まさに{f|勇気|ゆうき}ある{f|行動|こうどう}だと{f|思|おも}います</b>。（or {f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|勇気|ゆうき}がありますね）

The content survives; the rhetorical staging does not. まさに and
{f|本当|ほんとう}に carry the emphasis in language that can be said to another
person.

</details>

**3.** Choose, and say why: {f|説明|せつめい}なしに{f|方針|ほうしん}を{f|変|か}えるのは（{f|不誠実|ふせいじつ}ではないだろうか・{f|不誠実|ふせいじつ}でなくてなんだろう）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are grammatical, and they are not equivalent.
{f|不誠実|ふせいじつ}<b>ではないだろうか</b> raises the possibility and leaves the
reader room to disagree - the safer choice in a report or a discussion.
{f|不誠実|ふせいじつ}<b>でなくてなんだろう</b> declares it, daring anyone to
propose another word, and belongs to a column written to persuade.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|三時間|さんじかん}も{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|続|つづ}けるなんて、{f|真面目|まじめ}でなくてなんだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|三時間|さんじかん}も{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|続|つづ}けるなんて、<b>{f|努力|どりょく}でなくてなんだろう</b>。（or {f|真面目|まじめ}さの{f|証|あかし}でなくてなんだろう）

{f|真面目|まじめ} is a な-adjective, and although it can be used as a noun the
sentence is weak with it: the pattern wants a weighty abstract label such as
{f|努力|どりょく} or {f|情熱|じょうねつ}. Note that the ～さ noun
{f|真面目|まじめ}さ is the way to keep the original word.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|結果|けっか}は{f|運|うん}がよかっただけだと{f|思|おも}います。
(b) この{f|結果|けっか}が{f|実力|じつりょく}でなくてなんだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a measured personal opinion, politely framed, and the listener is free to
take a different view.

(b) is written rhetoric. It asserts that the result was ability and dares the
reader to name anything else, and the だろう makes it sound like a conclusion the
writer has been driven to rather than a claim they are advancing. Put (b) into a
conversation and it will sound theatrical; put (a) into a stirring essay and it
will sound flat.

</details>
