# てっきり

**Meaning** · I was sure that ... - and I was wrong

`てっきり` is a confession. It reports a belief the speaker held with complete
confidence, and it only exists because that belief turned out to be false. There is
no version of this word in which you were right.

## Usage

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `てっきり` needs a **past-tense verb of believing** -
<code>と{f|思|おも}っていた</code>, <code>と{f|思|おも}い{f|込|こ}んでいた</code>,
<code>とばかり{f|思|おも}っていた</code> - and the sentence or the context must
reveal the correction.

</div>

The standard shapes:

- <code>てっきり X だと{f|思|おも}っていた</code>
- <code>てっきり X だと{f|思|おも}い{f|込|こ}んでいた</code> - stronger; you had
  talked yourself into it
- <code>てっきり X だとばかり{f|思|おも}っていた</code> - the most emphatic of the
  three
- <code>てっきり X だと{f|思|おも}っていたが、{f|実|じつ}は Y だった</code> - the full
  two-part sentence, with the correction spelled out

Because it lives in hindsight, the present and the future are closed to it:
❌<code>てっきり{f|明日|あした}だと{f|思|おも}う</code>. And because the belief has to
have been wrong, it cannot report an expectation that came true - that is
<code>やっぱり</code> in speech, <code>{f|案|あん}の{f|定|じょう}</code> in writing.

The belief is normally your own. You can report someone else's
（<code>{f|彼|かれ}はてっきり{f|中止|ちゅうし}になったと{f|思|おも}っていたらしい</code>）,
but the first person is the home use.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>てっきり</b>{f|今日|きょう}が{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りだと{f|思|おも}っていました。
I was quite sure the deadline was today.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電気|でんき}が{f|消|き}えていたので、<b>てっきり</b>{f|留守|るす}だと{f|思|おも}った。
The lights were off, so I assumed nobody was home.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|来|こ}ないから、<b>てっきり</b>{f|断|ことわ}られたものだと{f|思|おも}い{f|込|こ}んでいた。
There was no reply, so I'd convinced myself I had been turned down.
</pre>

<pre>
すみません、{f|会議|かいぎ}は<b>てっきり</b>{f|来週|らいしゅう}に{f|延|の}びたものとばかり{f|思|おも}っておりました。
I'm terribly sorry - I was quite certain the meeting had been pushed back to next week.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Ordinary spoken Japanese, and one of the genuinely conversational words in this
group. It is neutral for politeness: it sits happily in casual speech
（<code>てっきり{f|明日|あした}だと{f|思|おも}ってた</code>）and in humble business
Japanese （<code>てっきり…と{f|思|おも}っておりました</code>）, because the adverb
itself carries no formality - the verb ending does all that work.

Its most useful home is the apology. Explaining a mix-up, `てっきり` frames what
happened as an honest misunderstanding rather than carelessness, which is exactly
what you want when you have turned up on the wrong day. Note how naturally it pairs
with <code>すみません</code>.

There is a faint self-deprecating warmth to it. You are admitting you were
confidently wrong, and Japanese speakers use it lightly, often with a laugh. It
does not work as an accusation: `てっきり` about someone else's mistake sounds odd
unless you are reporting their thinking sympathetically.

## Contrast

### てっきり vs やっぱり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both start from an expectation you already had.
<code>やっぱり</code> says it was borne out; `てっきり` says it was not.

</div>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}りそうだと{f|思|おも}っていたら、<b>やっぱり</b>{f|降|ふ}ってきた。
I thought it looked like rain, and sure enough it started.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>てっきり</b>{f|雨|あめ}になると{f|思|おも}っていたのに、{f|一日|いちにち}{f|晴|は}れだった。
I was sure it was going to rain, and it stayed sunny all day.
</pre>

The two are mirror images, and the second half of the sentence is what tells them
apart: <code>やっぱり</code> is followed by the thing happening, `てっきり` by the
thing not happening. In writing, the confirming half is often
<code>{f|案|あん}の{f|定|じょう}</code> rather than
<code>やっぱり</code>; there is no written cousin for `てっきり`, which stays
conversational.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ てっきり{f|明日|あした}が{f|試験|しけん}だと{f|思|おも}います。 → ✅ てっきり{f|明日|あした}が{f|試験|しけん}だと<b>{f|思|おも}っていました</b>。
  The verb has to be in the past, and in the continuous
  <code>{f|思|おも}っていた</code> at that: the belief was held over a stretch of
  time and has since been corrected.

- ❌ {f|天気予報|てんきよほう}のとおり、てっきり{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}った。 → ✅ {f|天気予報|てんきよほう}のとおり、<b>{f|案|あん}の{f|定|じょう}</b>{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}った。
  Here the expectation came true, so `てっきり` is the wrong half of the pair.

- ❌ てっきり{f|彼|かれ}は{f|来|こ}ないだろう。 → ✅ <b>たぶん</b>{f|彼|かれ}は{f|来|こ}ないだろう。
  A fresh guess about the future is <code>たぶん</code> or <code>きっと</code>.
  `てっきり` can only look backwards at a belief you have already abandoned.

## Practice

**1.** {f|田中|たなか}さんが{f|白衣|はくい}を{f|着|き}ていたので、____{f|医者|いしゃ}だと{f|思|おも}っていました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>てっきり</b>{f|医者|いしゃ}だと{f|思|おも}っていました。

The sentence gives the evidence that misled the speaker - the white coat - and the
past continuous <code>{f|思|おも}っていました</code> signals that the belief has
since been overturned. Both are the standard scaffolding for this adverb.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: てっきり{f|彼女|かのじょ}が{f|来|く}ると{f|思|おも}っていて、{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|来|き}ました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ <b>やっぱり</b>{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|来|き}ました。／{f|思|おも}ったとおり{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|来|き}ました。

`てっきり` requires the belief to have been mistaken, so a sentence in which she did
turn up contradicts the adverb. When the expectation holds, the word you want is
<code>やっぱり</code> or <code>{f|思|おも}ったとおり</code>.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) てっきり{f|中止|ちゅうし}になったと{f|思|おも}っていました。
(b) {f|中止|ちゅうし}になったと{f|思|おも}っていました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both report the same past belief, but (a) adds two things. It says the speaker held
the belief with no doubt at all, and it announces in advance that the belief was
wrong - so the listener already knows an apology or a correction is coming.
Sentence (b) is neutral and could still turn out to be right.

</details>
