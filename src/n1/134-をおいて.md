# ～をおいて

**Meaning** · other than this one; setting this one aside there is nothing - said to nominate the only possible candidate

The verb is {f|置|お}く in its old sense of "set aside". Put something aside, look at
what is left, and find nothing: that is the shape of
<code>{f|彼|かれ}をおいてほかにいない</code>. In practice the pattern is a **piece of
advocacy**. It is how you say that a person is the only one for the job, or that now
is the only moment to act, and it is nearly always a compliment or a push.

## Formation

The particle is always `を`, whatever role the noun would otherwise have played, and
the predicate is always negative.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (person) | Noun + をおいて + ほかにいない | {f|彼|かれ} → {f|彼|かれ}をおいてほかにいない |
| Noun (thing) | Noun + をおいて + ほかにない | この{f|案|あん} → この{f|案|あん}をおいてほかにない |
| Noun (time) | Noun + をおいて + ～ない | {f|今|いま} → {f|今|いま}をおいてない |
| Without ほかに | Noun + をおいて + ない/いない | {f|彼女|かのじょ} → {f|彼女|かのじょ}をおいていない |
| Polite | ～をおいてほかにありません | {f|山口|やまぐち}さんをおいてほかにありません |
| Kanji spelling | ～を{f|置|お}いて | kana is the usual choice |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the sentence must end in a negative. There is no
<code>をおいて ～ ある</code>; the pattern exists only to say that nothing else
qualifies.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|役|やく}ができるのは、{f|彼|かれ}<b>をおいて</b>ほかにいない。
There is nobody but him who can play this part.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|決断|けつだん}するなら、{f|今|いま}<b>をおいて</b>ない。
If you're going to decide, it's now or never.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|相談|そうだん}できる{f|相手|あいて}は、{f|君|きみ}<b>をおいて</b>ほかにいなかった。
You were the only person I could have talked to.
</pre>

<pre>

She is the only researcher in the world who can reconstruct the lost records.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}を{f|受|う}け{f|継|つ}げるのは、{f|工房|こうぼう}に{f|残|のこ}った{f|弟子|でし}{f|一人|ひとり}<b>をおいて</b>ほかにいなかった。
The one apprentice left in the workshop was the only person who could carry the craft on.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|市場|しじょう}が{f|動|うご}き{f|始|はじ}めた{f|今|いま}<b>をおいて</b>、{f|参入|さんにゅう}の{f|好機|こうき}はない。
With the market finally moving, there will be no better moment to enter than this one.
</pre>

<pre>

For the address at our centenary there is nobody more fitting than this gentleman, who supported my predecessor for so long.

## Nuance & register

**Formal, emphatic, and public.** Its natural settings are introductions and
nominations, recommendation letters, speeches, editorials and advertising copy. In
conversation it appears when someone is being deliberately emphatic - pressing a
friend to act now, or paying a large compliment - and it always sounds like a
statement being made rather than a remark being dropped.

The pattern is **evaluative, not merely factual**. It does not just report that
alternatives are absent; it argues that they are, and it takes the speaker's side.
That is why it fits a nomination so well and why it grates on something small.
<code>{f|昼|ひる}ご{f|飯|はん}はこのコンビニをおいてほかにない</code> is not wrong so
much as absurd - the machinery is far too heavy for the load, and a listener will
hear it as a joke.

Two collocations account for most of what you will meet. With a **person**, the
frame is <code>～をおいてほかにいない</code>, and the point is fitness for a role.
With <code>{f|今|いま}</code>, the frame is <code>{f|今|いま}をおいてない</code> and
the point is urgency: this is the moment, do not wait. Both are worth learning whole.

The kanji spelling <code>を{f|置|お}いて</code> is correct and you will see it, but
kana dominates in modern text - partly because the kanji invites confusion with
<code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code>, which looks similar in meaning and behaves completely
differently.

## Contrast

### ～をおいて vs ～{f|以外|いがい}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `以外に` is the neutral "apart from" and can be used about
anything. `をおいて` argues for a single candidate, and needs a negative.

</div>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さん<b>{f|以外|いがい}に</b>、{f|中国語|ちゅうごくご}ができる{f|人|ひと}はいますか。
Is there anyone besides Tanaka who speaks Chinese?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|通訳|つうやく}を{f|頼|たの}めるのは{f|田中|たなか}さん<b>をおいて</b>ほかにいない。
Tanaka is the only person we could ask to interpret.
</pre>

`以外に` can head a question, a positive sentence or a list. `をおいて` can do none
of those - it is committed to the negative and to a single nominee, which is exactly
what makes it emphatic.

### ～をおいて vs ～を{f|除|のぞ}いて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - they look like twins and are not.
<code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code> means **excluding**, and the rest of the sentence
carries on positively. `をおいて` means **there is nothing else**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さん<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|出席|しゅっせき}した。
Everyone attended except Tanaka.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さん<b>をおいて</b>、{f|適任者|てきにんしゃ}はいない。
There is no suitable candidate other than Tanaka.
</pre>

The first removes Tanaka from a group; the second says the group consists of Tanaka.
Written in kanji as <code>を{f|置|お}いて</code> and <code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code>
they are one character apart, and getting them the wrong way round reverses the
sentence.

### ～をおいて vs ほかに○○ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ほかに○○ない` reports that a search found nothing.
`をおいて` names the exception first and then denies everything else. They combine
happily: <code>～をおいてほかにない</code>.

</div>

<pre>
{f|条件|じょうけん}に{f|合|あ}う{f|物件|ぶっけん}は<b>ほかになかった</b>。
There was nothing else that met the conditions.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|条件|じょうけん}に{f|合|あ}うのは、この{f|部屋|へや}<b>をおいてほかになかった</b>。
This flat was the only one that met the conditions.
</pre>

The difference is where the emphasis falls. `ほかに○○ない` leaves the surviving
candidate implicit; `をおいて` puts it in the spotlight with `を` and then clears the
stage. When you want to name someone, you want `をおいて`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|頼|たの}めるのは{f|彼|かれ}をおいてほかに<b>いる</b>。 → ✅ この{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|頼|たの}めるのは{f|彼|かれ}をおいてほかに<b>いない</b>。
  The pattern only exists with a negative predicate. A positive ending leaves the
  sentence saying the opposite of anything anyone means.

- ❌ {f|田中|たなか}さん<b>をおいて</b>、{f|全員|ぜんいん}{f|出席|しゅっせき}した。 → ✅ {f|田中|たなか}さん<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、{f|全員|ぜんいん}{f|出席|しゅっせき}した。
  "Excluding" is {f|除|のぞ}く. Using `をおいて` here produces a sentence that
  promises there is nobody but Tanaka and then contradicts itself.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}に<b>をおいて</b>ほかにいない。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}<b>をおいて</b>ほかにいない。
  The particle is `を` and it replaces whatever the noun would otherwise have taken.
  Stacking `に` in front of it is a common slip when the noun is the person a job is
  entrusted *to*.

- ❌ {f|傘|かさ}を{f|買|か}うなら、この{f|店|みせ}<b>をおいて</b>ほかにない。 → ✅ {f|傘|かさ}を{f|買|か}うなら、この{f|店|みせ}が{f|一番|いちばん}{f|安|やす}い。
  Grammatical, but the register is far too grand for the content. Save `をおいて`
  for nominations, appointments and genuinely decisive moments.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|難|むずか}しい{f|交渉|こうしょう}を{f|任|まか}せられるのは、{f|森|もり}さん____ほかに____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|森|もり}さん<b>をおいて</b>ほかに<b>いない</b>。

The noun takes `を` regardless of the role it plays in the sentence, and because the
candidate is a person the negative is <code>いない</code>. The whole frame
<code>～をおいてほかにいない</code> is best memorised as a unit.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|新人|しんじん}{f|三人|さんにん}（をおいて・を{f|除|のぞ}いて）、{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|研修|けんしゅう}を{f|終|お}えた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|新人|しんじん}{f|三人|さんにん}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>

The sentence carries on positively - everyone else finished - so what is needed is
"excluding", which is {f|除|のぞ}く. `をおいて` would require the sentence to end in a
negative and would claim that the three newcomers were the only people who existed.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If we are going to renegotiate the contract, there will never be a better moment than now."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|契約|けいやく}を{f|見直|みなお}すのであれば、{f|今|いま}<b>をおいて</b>ほかにありません。

<code>{f|今|いま}をおいて</code> is the fixed shape for urgency, and the polite
<code>ほかにありません</code> matches the business setting. Note that the English
"never" turns into the plain negative at the end - Japanese does not need a separate
word for it here.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|審査員|しんさいん}を{f|務|つと}めるのは、{f|先生|せんせい}にをおいてほかにいらっしゃいます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|審査員|しんさいん}を{f|務|つと}めるのは、{f|先生|せんせい}<b>をおいて</b>ほかに<b>いらっしゃいません</b>。

Two faults. The stray `に` in front of `をおいて` has to go, since the pattern takes
`を` alone. And the predicate must be negative - here the honorific negative
<code>いらっしゃいません</code>, which fits the respectful register the rest of the
sentence has set up.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|向|む}いているのは{f|佐藤|さとう}さん<b>だけです</b>。
(b) {f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|向|む}いているのは{f|佐藤|さとう}さん<b>をおいてほかにいません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) states a fact about the shortlist. It is neutral, and depending on tone it could
even be a complaint that the field is thin.

(b) is a recommendation. `をおいて` puts Sato forward and dismisses everyone else in
one movement, and the formality makes it sound like something said in a meeting or
written in a letter. If you want the sentence to *do* something rather than merely
report, (b) is the one.

</details>
