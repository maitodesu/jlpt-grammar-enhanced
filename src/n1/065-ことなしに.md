# ～ことなしに

**Meaning** · without doing A - and the point is that if you skip A, B is not going to happen

<code>ことなしに</code> is the verb version of <code>なしに</code>:
<code>こと</code> turns the verb into a noun, and に attaches it to the main clause.
It is not a neutral "without". It names A as an **unavoidable step** and then denies
the outcome - no honest look at the problem, no fix. Almost every real example ends
in a negative.

## Formation

The verb goes in the plain **dictionary form**. Nothing before
<code>ことなしに</code> is ever past or negative.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | Dictionary form + ことなしに | {f|認|みと}める → {f|認|みと}めることなしに |
| Verb (usual shape) | Dictionary form + ことなしには + negative | {f|変|か}わる → {f|変|か}わることなしには{f|解決|かいけつ}しない |
| する-verb | Noun + する + ことなしに | {f|努力|どりょく}する → {f|努力|どりょく}することなしに |
| Polite predicate | ～ことなしに … ません | {f|見|み}る → {f|見|み}ることなしに{f|判断|はんだん}できません |
| Noun in the slot instead | Noun + なしに（no こと） | {f|説明|せつめい} → {f|説明|せつめい}なしに |
| Positive-predicate relative | Dictionary form + ことなく | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}むことなく{f|働|はたら}く |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the sentence needs a **negative or impossibility** at the end:
～ない, ～できない, ～あり{f|得|え}ない, ～{f|難|むずか}しい. If the main clause is
plainly affirmative, what you want is <code>ことなく</code> or
<code>ずに</code>, not this.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|人|ひと}と{f|話|はな}す<b>ことなしに</b>、{f|会話|かいわ}は{f|上達|じょうたつ}しない。
Your conversation will not improve unless you talk to people.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|失敗|しっぱい}する<b>ことなしに</b>、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}は{f|生|う}まれない。
No new technology comes into being without failures along the way.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|現場|げんば}を{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|目|め}で{f|見|み}る<b>ことなしには</b>、{f|正|ただ}しい{f|判断|はんだん}はできません。
You cannot judge this correctly without seeing the site with your own eyes.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|痛|いた}みを{f|伴|ともな}う<b>ことなしに</b>{f|改革|かいかく}を{f|進|すす}めることはできない。
Reform cannot be carried through without pain.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|長年|ながねん}の{f|習慣|しゅうかん}を{f|見直|みなお}す<b>ことなしに</b>、{f|残業|ざんぎょう}を{f|減|へ}らすのは{f|無理|むり}だろう。
Cutting overtime is hopeless unless the habits of decades are reconsidered.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|非|ひ}を{f|認|みと}める<b>ことなしに</b>、{f|失|うしな}った{f|信頼|しんらい}を{f|取|と}り{f|戻|もど}すことはできない。
Trust once lost cannot be recovered without admitting your own fault.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|農薬|のうやく}に{f|頼|たよ}る<b>ことなしに</b>{f|大量|たいりょう}の{f|野菜|やさい}を{f|育|そだ}てるのは、{f|想像|そうぞう}{f|以上|いじょう}に{f|難|むずか}しい。
Growing vegetables at scale without relying on chemicals is harder than people imagine.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|誰|だれ}かを{f|傷|きず}つける<b>ことなしに</b>{f|真実|しんじつ}を{f|語|かた}ることは、できないのかもしれない。
Perhaps the truth cannot be told without hurting somebody.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Formal and **written**. Its home is argument: editorials, essays, policy papers,
academic prose, the serious part of a business proposal. What it contributes is a
rhetorical shape - it sets up a condition the reader would rather avoid, and then
closes the door. That is why the A slot so often holds something unwelcome:
{f|失敗|しっぱい}する, {f|痛|いた}みを{f|伴|ともな}う, {f|非|ひ}を
{f|認|みと}める, {f|犠牲|ぎせい}を{f|払|はら}う.

In speech the same idea is expressed with <code>～ないで</code>,
<code>～ずに</code> or a plain conditional. 「{f|練習|れんしゅう}しないと
{f|上手|じょうず}にならないよ」 is what a teacher says to a student;
{f|練習|れんしゅう}することなしに{f|上達|じょうたつ}はしない is what the same
teacher writes in a preface. Using it over coffee about a small matter -
{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}つことなしに{f|出|で}かけた - is not wrong so much as absurdly
heavy, like saying "absent the provision of an umbrella".

Two mechanical points that catch learners. The verb is always in the **dictionary
form**, however far in the past the situation is: {f|調|しら}べることなしに
{f|結論|けつろん}を{f|出|だ}してしまった is correct, and ❌{f|調|しら}べたこと
なしに is not. And the pattern is for **verbs only**. Where you have a plain noun -
{f|許可|きょか}, {f|予告|よこく}, {f|説明|せつめい} - drop the
<code>こと</code> and use <code>なしに</code>.

## Contrast

### ～ことなしに vs ～ことなく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ことなく</code> is a plain adverbial, "without ever doing",
and happily takes a **positive** predicate. <code>ことなしに</code> sets A up as a
**necessary condition** and denies what follows.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|一度|いちど}も{f|振|ふ}り{f|返|かえ}る<b>ことなく</b>{f|歩|ある}き{f|去|さ}った。
She walked away without once looking back.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|過去|かこ}を{f|振|ふ}り{f|返|かえ}る<b>ことなしに</b>、{f|同|おな}じ{f|失敗|しっぱい}は{f|防|ふせ}げない。
The same mistakes cannot be prevented without looking back at the past.
</pre>

This is the pair to get straight. The first sentence simply reports how she left, and
<code>ことなしに</code> would be wrong there because nothing is being denied. The
second could not use <code>ことなく</code> without losing the argument: the point is
not that people fail to look back, it is that not looking back makes prevention
impossible.

### ～ことなしに vs ～なしに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same pattern, different part of speech.
<code>ことなしに</code> takes a **verb**; <code>なしに</code> takes a **noun**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|住民|じゅうみん}への{f|説明|せつめい}<b>なしに</b>{f|計画|けいかく}が{f|決|き}まった。
The plan was settled with no explanation to residents.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|住民|じゅうみん}に{f|説明|せつめい}する<b>ことなしに</b>{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|進|すす}めるべきではない。
The plan should not be pushed forward without explaining it to residents.
</pre>

Many nouns can go either way, since {f|説明|せつめい} and
{f|説明|せつめい}する are the same word in two coats. Choose by what the rest of the
clause needs: a full verb phrase with an object and a particle forces
<code>ことなしに</code>, while a bare noun is lighter with <code>なしに</code>.

### ～ことなしに vs ～ないで／～ずに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the meanings overlap; the **register does not**.
<code>ずに</code> is everyday Japanese for any omission at all;
<code>ことなしに</code> is written argument about an omission that matters.

</div>

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}た<b>ずに</b>{f|出|で}かけて、ずぶ{f|濡|ぬ}れになった。
I went out without an umbrella and got soaked.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|原因|げんいん}を{f|突|つ}き{f|止|と}める<b>ことなしに</b>{f|対策|たいさく}を{f|立|た}てても{f|意味|いみ}がない。
There is no point drawing up measures without first pinning down the cause.
</pre>

The umbrella sentence cannot take <code>ことなしに</code>: the omission is trivial
and the predicate is not a denial. Going the other way is possible -
{f|原因|げんいん}を{f|突|つ}き{f|止|と}めずに would be understood - but the
sentence loses the formal, argued tone the writer was after.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|努力|どりょく}した<b>ことなしに</b>{f|成功|せいこう}はない。 → ✅ {f|努力|どりょく}する<b>ことなしに</b>{f|成功|せいこう}はない。
  The verb stays in the dictionary form. Tense is carried by the main clause only.

- ❌ {f|練習|れんしゅう}する<b>ことなしに</b>{f|上手|じょうず}になった。 → ✅ {f|練習|れんしゅう}する<b>ことなく</b>{f|上手|じょうず}になった。
  With a positive predicate the pattern is <code>ことなく</code>.
  <code>ことなしに</code> exists to deny the second half.

- ❌ {f|説明|せつめい}<b>ことなしに</b>{f|決|き}めてしまった。 → ✅ {f|説明|せつめい}<b>なしに</b>{f|決|き}めてしまった。
  A bare noun takes <code>なしに</code>. <code>こと</code> is only there to
  nominalise a verb that is not one already.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|朝|あさ}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べる<b>ことなしに</b>{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}た。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|朝|あさ}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べ<b>ずに</b>{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}た。
  Grammatical, but the weight is wrong. Everyday omissions belong to
  <code>ずに</code> and <code>ないで</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|信頼|しんらい}{f|関係|かんけい}を{f|築|きず}く____、{f|本音|ほんね}を{f|引|ひ}き{f|出|だ}すことはできない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|築|きず}く<b>ことなしに</b>

Building trust is presented as the step you cannot skip, and the main clause is a
denial of possibility - the standard shape for this pattern.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|彼|かれ}は{f|三十年|さんじゅうねん}{f|間|かん}、{f|一日|いちにち}も{f|休|やす}む（ことなく・ことなしに）{f|店|みせ}を{f|開|あ}け{f|続|つづ}けた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一日|いちにち}も{f|休|やす}む<b>ことなく</b>{f|店|みせ}を{f|開|あ}け{f|続|つづ}けた。

The main clause is affirmative - he kept the shop open - so the plain adverbial
<code>ことなく</code> is required. <code>ことなしに</code> needs something to deny.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "You cannot understand a country without learning something of its history."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

その{f|国|くに}の{f|歴史|れきし}を{f|学|まな}ぶ<b>ことなしに</b>、その{f|国|くに}を{f|理解|りかい}することはできない。

Learning the history is the unavoidable step; understanding is denied without it.
Note the dictionary form {f|学|まな}ぶ and the negative
できない at the end - both are obligatory.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|十分|じゅうぶん}に{f|議論|ぎろん}したことなしに、{f|結論|けつろん}を{f|急|いそ}ぐべきではない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|十分|じゅうぶん}に{f|議論|ぎろん}<b>することなしに</b>、{f|結論|けつろん}を{f|急|いそ}ぐべきではない。
"We should not rush to a conclusion without a full discussion."

The past form {f|議論|ぎろん}した cannot appear here. <code>こと</code> attaches to
the dictionary form, and the tense of the sentence lives in the final predicate.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|誰|だれ}にも{f|相談|そうだん}せ<b>ずに</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|辞|や}めた。
(b) {f|周|まわ}りに{f|相談|そうだん}する<b>ことなしに</b>{f|正|ただ}しい{f|決断|けつだん}はできない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) narrates one person's action. He quit, and he consulted nobody; the sentence is
neutral about whether that was wise, and the register is ordinary conversation.

(b) makes a general claim in written style: consulting others is a condition for
deciding well, and without it good decisions are impossible. It states a principle
rather than an event, and it would sound oddly grand as a remark about a friend's
resignation.

</details>
