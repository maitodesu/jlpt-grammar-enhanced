# ～としたことが

**Meaning** · I, of all people - dismay that someone who normally would never make this mistake has just made it. In practice the phrase is frozen into {f|私|わたし}としたことが.

The parts do not add up to anything useful; treat the whole string as one word. What
it does is set up a contrast between a person's usual competence and the blunder
they have just committed, and it is almost always the speaker talking about
themselves. Because it quietly claims *I am normally better than this*, it is a
phrase with a distinct flavour - theatrical, a little old-fashioned, and slightly
self-regarding.

## Formation

A person goes in front, followed directly by としたことが, and then the blunder.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| {f|私|わたし} (by far the commonest) | {f|私|わたし}としたことが | {f|私|わたし}としたことが、うっかりしていました |
| Other first-person words | {f|僕|ぼく} / おれ / わたくし + としたことが | {f|僕|ぼく}としたことが |
| Second person (reproach) | {f|君|きみ} / あなた + としたことが | {f|君|きみ}としたことが |
| Third person (narration) | {f|彼|かれ} / {f|彼女|かのじょ} / a name + としたことが | {f|彼女|かのじょ}としたことが |
| What follows | the mistake, usually with ～てしまう / ～とは / ～なんて | {f|忘|わす}れてしまいました、{f|間違|まちが}えるとは |
| Not possible | a non-human subject | ❌この{f|機械|きかい}としたことが |
| Not possible | a good outcome after it | ❌{f|私|わたし}としたことが、うまくいきました |

<div class="note">

**Note** - this is a **set phrase, not a productive pattern**. You cannot swap in
other tenses or particles: ❌<code>{f|私|わたし}とすることが</code>,
❌<code>{f|私|わたし}としたことは</code>, ❌<code>{f|私|わたし}としたことも</code>
are all wrong. The only slot that varies is the person in front, and even that slot
is filled by {f|私|わたし} the great majority of the time.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise it before you use it.** In modern Japanese this phrase lives mainly in
fiction: novels, television drama, manga, the line a capable character delivers when
they have been caught out. In real speech it does occur - from older speakers, and
in formal spoken apologies where the speaker wants to signal that the lapse is out
of character - but from a young speaker in a casual setting it sounds like a
performance. The plain ways to say the same thing are
<code>うっかりしていました</code>,
<code>{f|私|わたし}らしくないミスをしてしまいました</code>, and
<code>いつもならこんなことはないのですが</code>. Reach for one of those.

Two things to keep in mind if you ever do use it. First, it **claims competence**.
The whole force of the phrase is "this is not like me", so it fits a small,
uncharacteristic slip - a forgotten umbrella, a misread date, a name got wrong. Put
it in front of a serious failure and it sounds evasive, as though the speaker were
more concerned with their reputation than with the harm done. Second, in the second
person it is not really scolding: <code>{f|君|きみ}としたことが</code> expresses
surprise, and often something close to sympathy, from someone who thinks well of
the listener. A genuine reprimand uses different words.

The phrase is normally followed by <code>～てしまう</code>, or by a
<code>～とは</code> / <code>～なんて</code> clause that trails off without a main
verb - <code>{f|私|わたし}としたことが、なんという{f|失敗|しっぱい}を</code>. That
unfinished shape is part of its dramatic feel and is one of the easiest ways to
spot it on the page.

## Contrast

### ～としたことが vs うっかり～てしまいました

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>うっかり</code> plus an ordinary apology is the modern,
speakable version. It admits the slip without also announcing how competent you
usually are.

</div>

<pre>
すみません、<b>うっかり</b>{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|持|も}ってくるのを{f|忘|わす}れました。
Sorry - I completely forgot to bring the handout.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}<b>としたことが</b>、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|持|も}ってくるのを{f|忘|わす}れました。
I of all people forgot to bring the handout.
</pre>

Both apologise. The first is what a colleague says; the second adds a layer of
self-characterisation that, from a junior person, can read as pompous. If you want
the "not like me" note without the theatre, say
<code>{f|私|わたし}らしくないミスをしました</code>.

### ～としたことが vs ～ともあろうものが

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>としたことが</code> points **inward** at a lapse of your
own. <code>ともあろうものが</code> points **outward** at someone who has disgraced
a position of responsibility.

</div>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}<b>としたことが</b>、{f|時間|じかん}を{f|勘違|かんちが}いしておりました。
I of all people had the time wrong.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|責任者|せきにんしゃ}<b>ともあろうものが</b>、{f|時間|じかん}を{f|守|まも}らないとは。
For the person in charge to be late, of all people...
</pre>

The second difference is the weight of the fault.
<code>としたことが</code> covers a slip and is half an apology;
<code>ともあろうものが</code> covers a breach of duty and is an accusation. The
noun in front differs too - a personal pronoun for the first, a role or an
institution for the second.

### ～としたことが vs ～としては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - they share として and nothing else.
<code>としては</code> means *in my capacity as* or *for my part*, and is everyday
Japanese. <code>としたことが</code> is the frozen self-reproach.

</div>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}<b>としては</b>、この{f|案|あん}に{f|賛成|さんせい}です。
Speaking for myself, I'm in favour of this proposal.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}<b>としたことが</b>、この{f|案|あん}の{f|数字|すうじ}を{f|見落|みお}としていました。
I of all people missed the figures in this proposal.
</pre>

A third member of the family is worth adding, because learners mix it in:
<code>{f|私|わたし}にしては</code> means "for me", *by my standards*, and it works
in both directions -
<code>{f|私|わたし}にしてはうまく{f|書|か}けた</code>, "that came out well, for me".
Only <code>としたことが</code> is restricted to failure.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}<b>としては</b>、{f|鍵|かぎ}を{f|忘|わす}れてしまいました。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>としたことが</b>、{f|鍵|かぎ}を{f|忘|わす}れてしまいました。
  <code>としては</code> means "for my part" and cannot express the "of all people"
  sense. The two look alike and mean completely different things.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}<b>としたことが</b>、{f|今回|こんかい}はうまくいきました。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>にしては</b>、うまくいきました。
  The phrase only introduces a failure. For "that went well, considering it was me",
  the frame is <code>にしては</code>.

- ❌ この{f|機械|きかい}<b>としたことが</b>、{f|止|と}まってしまった。 → ✅ この{f|機械|きかい}が{f|止|と}まるなんて{f|珍|めずら}しい。
  Only a person can be held to a standard of character. A machine takes a plain
  expression of surprise.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}<b>としたことは</b>、{f|遅刻|ちこく}しました。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>としたことが</b>、{f|遅刻|ちこく}してしまいました。
  The が is fixed; the phrase does not take は or も. It is a set expression and
  cannot be taken apart.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to meet this line, and who is likely to be speaking? 「{f|私|わたし}としたことが、なんという{f|失態|しったい}を。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In fiction - a novel, a drama script, a manga panel - and most likely from a
character established as capable and rather proud of it. The unfinished ending
(なんという{f|失態|しったい}を, with the verb left off) and the formal word
{f|失態|しったい} both mark it as written dialogue rather than everyday speech.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|私|わたし}としたことが、{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|時間|じかん}を{f|間違|まちが}えておりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

すみません、うっかり{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|時間|じかん}を{f|間違|まちが}えていました。

<code>うっかり</code> carries the "careless slip" part, and a plain すみません does
the apologising. This is what you would actually say to a colleague; the original
would sound as if you were on stage.

</details>

**3.** Choose: {f|今回|こんかい}のテストは（{f|私|わたし}としたことが・{f|私|わたし}にしては）よくできたほうだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|私|わたし}にしては</b>よくできたほうだ。

The result was good, and <code>としたことが</code> can only introduce a failure.
<code>にしては</code> means "by my standards", and it happily reports a pleasant
surprise as well as a disappointing one.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部長|ぶちょう}としたことが、{f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|金|かね}を{f|使|つか}い{f|込|こ}んでいた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>ともあろうものが</b>、{f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|金|かね}を{f|使|つか}い{f|込|こ}んでいた。
"The department head, of all people, had been embezzling company money."

Two problems with the original. The subject is a role rather than a person the
speaker identifies with, and the offence is serious wrongdoing rather than a
careless slip - both of which point to <code>ともあろうものが</code>. Applying
<code>としたことが</code> to embezzlement makes it sound like a forgotten umbrella.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|君|きみ}としたことが、{f|数字|すうじ}を{f|見間違|みまちが}えるとはね。
(b) どうして{f|数字|すうじ}を{f|見|み}{f|間違|まちが}えたんだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is surprise rather than anger, and it contains a compliment: the speaker thinks
highly enough of the listener to find the mistake out of character. It is also
markedly literary, and in real life would come from an older speaker or a scripted
one.

(b) is a plain demand for an explanation, and depending on tone it can be a genuine
question or a telling-off. It carries no implication about the listener's usual
standard. If you want to soften a reprimand in real speech, the natural move is
{f|珍|めずら}しいね or いつもと{f|違|ちが}うね, not the pattern in (a).

</details>
