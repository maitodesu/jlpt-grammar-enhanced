# ～こととて

**Meaning** · because it is a case of ~; it being ~ - an old causal connective, now used almost only to excuse a shortcoming

`こととて` gives a reason, and the reason is nearly always an **apology in
advance**: we are out in the country, so there is nothing to offer you; it was all
so sudden, so nothing was ready; the child did not know any better. The form is
several centuries old and has survived into modern Japanese only inside that
apologetic frame. Recognise it in a formal letter or an older novel; when you need
to say the same thing, say `ので` or `ものですから`.

## Formation

<code>こと</code> is a noun, so what precedes it modifies a noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | ～の + こととて | {f|田舎|いなか} → {f|田舎|いなか}のこととて |
| な-adjective | ～な + こととて | {f|不慣|ふな}れな → {f|不慣|ふな}れなこととて |
| い-adjective | plain form + こととて | {f|慌|あわ}ただしい → {f|慌|あわ}ただしいこととて |
| Verb | plain form + こととて | {f|伺|うかが}う → {f|初|はじ}めて{f|伺|うかが}うこととて |
| Verb (negative, literary) | ～ぬ + こととて | {f|知|し}らない → {f|知|し}らぬこととて |
| Past | ～た + こととて | {f|急|きゅう}に{f|決|き}まった → {f|急|きゅう}に{f|決|き}まったこととて |
| Fixed opener | なにぶん + ～こととて | - → なにぶん{f|急|きゅう}なこととて |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the literary negative <code>～ぬ</code> is the giveaway.
<code>{f|知|し}らぬこととて</code> and <code>{f|慣|な}れぬこととて</code> are far more
frequent than the modern <code>{f|知|し}らないこととて</code>, because the whole
expression belongs to an older layer of the language and pulls the negative back
with it.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Read this one; do not use it.** `こととて` is archaic. Where you will actually meet
it is a narrow set of places: formal written apologies and letters of thanks, the
speech of an innkeeper or a host in fiction, historical novels and period drama, and
occasionally the prepared remarks of a much older speaker at a ceremony. Used by a
learner in ordinary conversation it does not sound polite - it sounds like costume
drama. The modern equivalents are `ので`, `ものですから`, `ため` and, in a formal
letter, <code>～につき</code>.

Almost every real example is an **excuse for a deficiency**. The second clause asks
for indulgence: お{f|許|ゆる}しください, ご{f|容赦|ようしゃ}ください,
{f|失礼|しつれい}{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げました, できませんでした. A `こととて`
clause followed by a neutral statement of fact - "it being Tuesday, the shop was
busy" - is not how the pattern behaves.

The surrounding language is old too, and it tends to arrive as a package: なにぶん
or なにぶんにも in front, the literary negative `ぬ` inside, humble verbs
({f|存|ぞん}じます, {f|伺|うかが}う, {f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げる) after. That whole
register is the real content of the page. If you can see the frame coming, you will
never misread `こととて` as anything but "because".

One practical warning: it looks like `ことで` and `ことだから`, and it is neither.
Do not let the surface similarity persuade you that it is available for everyday
reasons.

## Contrast

### ～こととて vs ～ので・～ものですから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning, three centuries apart. Write
`ものですから` for an apologetic reason and `ので` for a neutral one; keep
`こととて` for reading.

</div>

<pre>
{f|急|きゅう}な<b>こととて</b>、ご{f|挨拶|あいさつ}もできませんでした。
It being so sudden, I was unable even to pay my respects.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|急|きゅう}に{f|決|き}まった<b>ものですから</b>、ご{f|挨拶|あいさつ}もできませんでした。
Because it was decided at short notice, I wasn't able to come and greet you properly.
</pre>

The two sentences carry exactly the same apology. The second is what a person says
at a leaving party in 2026; the first is what appears in a printed letter, or in the
mouth of a character in a novel set eighty years ago. Note that `ものですから` is
already apologetic in itself, which is why it is the closest modern match -
`ので` alone is flatter.

### ～こととて vs ～ことだから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `こととて` = **because it is** the case that ~ (excuse).
`ことだから` = **knowing what ~ is like**, we can predict what follows.

</div>

<pre>
{f|田舎|いなか}の<b>こととて</b>、バスは{f|一時間|いちじかん}に{f|一本|いっぽん}しかありません。
This being the countryside, there is only one bus an hour.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}に{f|正確|せいかく}な{f|彼|かれ}の<b>ことだから</b>、もう{f|着|つ}いているはずだ。
Knowing how punctual he is, he must be there already.
</pre>

`ことだから` takes a person or thing whose character is already known to both
speakers, and leads to a **prediction**. `こととて` takes a circumstance and leads to
an **apology**. They are not variants of one pattern, and the resemblance is a trap
of spelling only.

### ～こととて vs ～とて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `こととて` is causal, "because". Bare `とて` is concessive,
"even; even if". The `こと` is doing all the work.

</div>

<pre>
{f|子供|こども}の<b>こととて</b>、{f|大目|おおめ}に{f|見|み}てやってください。
It was only a child, so please overlook it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子供|こども}の{f|言|い}うこと<b>とて</b>、{f|聞|き}き{f|流|なが}してよいものではない。
Even a child's words are not to be brushed aside.
</pre>

Both are literary, both are old, and they differ by one noun. Misread the first as
"even though it was a child" and the sentence turns from forgiveness into reproach.
When you meet `とて` in a text, look first for a `こと` in front of it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|田舎|いなか}<b>こととて</b>、{f|不便|ふべん}です。 → ✅ {f|田舎|いなか}<b>のこととて</b>、{f|不便|ふべん}でございます。
  A noun needs `の` before <code>こと</code>, and a な-adjective needs `な`. The
  pattern is a noun phrase, not a particle.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}の<b>こととて</b>、{f|遠足|えんそく}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になりました。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}の<b>ため</b>、{f|遠足|えんそく}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になりました。
  A neutral announcement has nothing to apologise for. `こととて` in a school notice
  reads as parody.

- ❌ すみません、{f|寝坊|ねぼう}した<b>こととて</b>{f|遅刻|ちこく}しました。 → ✅ すみません、{f|寝坊|ねぼう}してしまったものですから{f|遅刻|ちこく}しました。
  Spoken to a colleague, `こととて` sounds theatrical. Everyday spoken excuses take
  `ものですから` or `もので`.

- ❌ {f|慣|な}れない<b>こととて</b>、{f|自信|じしん}があります。 → ✅ {f|慣|な}れぬ<b>こととて</b>、{f|至|いた}らぬ{f|点|てん}も{f|多|おお}いかと{f|存|ぞん}じます。
  The second clause has to admit a shortcoming and ask for indulgence. A confident
  statement after `こととて` contradicts the whole expression.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to meet this sentence?
なにぶん{f|山中|さんちゅう}の<b>こととて</b>、ご{f|不便|ふべん}をおかけいたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In writing rather than speech: a notice pinned up in a mountain inn, a line in a
guest information sheet, or a letter from the proprietor. The なにぶん opener, the
humble おかけいたします and `こととて` together mark it as a prepared written
apology, not something a receptionist would say aloud.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese:
{f|急|きゅう}な<b>こととて</b>、{f|十分|じゅうぶん}な{f|準備|じゅんび}ができませんでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|急|きゅう}に{f|決|き}まった<b>ものですから</b>、{f|十分|じゅうぶん}な{f|準備|じゅんび}ができませんでした。

`ものですから` carries the same note of apology in living Japanese. Plain
<code>ので</code> would also be correct, though it sounds a shade more matter-of-fact
than the original.

</details>

**3.** Choose: {f|時間|じかん}に{f|厳|きび}しい{f|部長|ぶちょう}の（こととて・ことだから）、もう{f|会議室|かいぎしつ}で{f|待|ま}っているだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|部長|ぶちょう}の<b>ことだから</b>

The sentence predicts what a known person will have done, which is `ことだから`.
`こととて` would need an apology after it, and there is nothing here to apologise
for.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the register error: {f|昨日|きのう}{f|忙|いそが}しいこととて、メールの{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|遅|おく}れてごめん。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|昨日|きのう}{f|忙|いそが}しかった<b>もので</b>、メールの{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|遅|おく}れてごめん。

Two things clash. `こととて` cannot sit next to ごめん - one is period-drama
formality and the other is friend-to-friend speech - and a casual apology takes
<code>もので</code> or <code>ものだから</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain what the `こととて` contributes:
{f|初|はじ}めて{f|伺|うかが}う<b>こととて</b>、{f|勝手|かって}が{f|分|わ}からず{f|失礼|しつれい}をいたしました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It supplies the reason for the failing admitted in the second half: this was my first
visit, so I did not know how things are done here. Structurally it is doing no more
than `ので` would.

What it adds is register. The speaker is presenting the apology as a formal, composed
one - the kind written down or delivered in a prepared greeting - and the literary
{f|伺|うかが}う and いたしました around it belong to the same layer of language.

</details>
