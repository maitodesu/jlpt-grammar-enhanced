# どうにも～ない

**Meaning** · there is nothing to be done about it - no course of action would change this, so the speaker gives up

どう + に + も is "in any way at all", and the negative removes every one of those
ways. What the pattern expresses is **helplessness**: the speaker has looked at the
situation, or at their own feelings, and found no lever to pull. It is close to a
sigh in grammatical form.

## Formation

どうにも is an adverb and needs a negative predicate. The negative is very often a
potential.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (potential) | どうにも + ～(ら)れない | {f|眠|ねむ}る → どうにも{f|眠|ねむ}れない |
| Verb (する) | どうにも + できない | {f|我慢|がまん}する → どうにも{f|我慢|がまん}できない |
| Verb (plain negative) | どうにも + ～ない | {f|直|なお}る → どうにも{f|直|なお}らない |
| Polite | どうにも + ～ません | {f|納得|なっとく}する → どうにも{f|納得|なっとく}できません |
| Frozen: nothing can be done | ～は + どうにもならない | {f|過去|かこ} → {f|過去|かこ}はどうにもならない |
| Frozen: I can do nothing about it | ～を + どうにもできない | {f|自分|じぶん}の{f|性格|せいかく} → {f|自分|じぶん}の{f|性格|せいかく}をどうにもできない |
| Emphatic (casual) | どうにもこうにも + ～ない | {f|進|すす}む → どうにもこうにも{f|進|すす}まない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the two frozen phrases divide by particle.
<code>どうにもならない</code> is intransitive and takes が or は: the situation is
beyond help, with nobody named. <code>どうにもできない</code> takes を and names
someone who cannot fix it. ❌<code>{f|彼|かれ}をどうにもならない</code> mixes the two.

</div>

</pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

One part was missing and there was absolutely no way to put it together.

## Nuance & register

Everyday Japanese, common in speech and in writing that has a voice.
What it adds is **resignation**, and often a note of complaint. The speaker is not
neutrally reporting an impossibility; they have already run through the options and
come up empty, and the sentence carries that weariness.

A large share of real uses are about the speaker's own feelings refusing to
cooperate - どうにも{f|気|き}が{f|進|すす}まない, どうにも{f|好|す}きになれない,
どうにも{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}かない, どうにも{f|気|き}になる. The situation there is
internal, which is exactly the point: even your own mind is not something you can
simply decide about.

<code>どうにもこうにも</code> is the emphatic doubling and it is **casual**. It works
in conversation and in a chatty blog and looks out of place in a report, where the
neutral どうにも or a rephrasing is safer.

<code>どうにもならない</code> deserves separate mention because it has hardened into
a fixed phrase used on its own: 「もうどうにもならない」, "there's nothing for it
now". You will meet it after a て-form as well - {f|悔|く}やんでも
どうにもならない - where it becomes a way of telling someone to stop dwelling on
something.

## Contrast

### どうにも～ない vs どうしても～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>どうしても</code> is about **effort**: I tried hard and
still could not. <code>どうにも</code> is about **options**: there is no move
available at all.

</div>

<pre>
{f|名前|なまえ}が<b>どうしても</b>{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}せ<b>ない</b>。
I just cannot remember his name, however hard I try.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}の{f|人手|ひとで}では、<b>どうにも</b>{f|回|まわ}ら<b>ない</b>。
With the staff we have, it simply doesn't work, and nothing will make it.
</pre>

The first has a person straining at a task; the second has a situation with no way
out. There is also a use <code>どうにも</code> cannot copy:
<code>どうしても</code> takes affirmative predicates for strong determination -
どうしても{f|行|い}きたい, どうしても{f|会|あ}わせたい - and
❌どうにも{f|行|い}きたい is not Japanese.

### どうにも～ない vs なんとも～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>どうにも</code> denies that anything can be **done**.
<code>なんとも</code> denies that anything can be **said or felt** about it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|結果|けっか}が{f|出|で}るまでは<b>なんとも</b>{f|言|い}え<b>ません</b>。
Until the results are in I can't say anything either way.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|く}るなら、{f|予定|よてい}は<b>どうにも</b>{f|変|か}えられ<b>ない</b>。
If a typhoon is coming, the schedule is beyond changing.
</pre>

<code>なんとも</code> lives with verbs of speaking, thinking and feeling -
なんとも{f|言|い}えない, なんとも{f|思|おも}わない - and with the frozen
なんともない, "no problem, no harm done". It is about judgement.
<code>どうにも</code> is about action, which is why it pairs so naturally with
できない and ならない.

### どうにも～ない vs とても～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>とても</code> says the thing is **beyond reach**, usually
in scale or degree. <code>どうにも</code> says **no approach works**, whatever the
scale.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|値段|ねだん}は、{f|学生|がくせい}には<b>とても</b>{f|払|はら}え<b>ない</b>。
A student couldn't possibly pay that price.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値上|ねあ}げの{f|話|はなし}は{f|決|き}まってしまい、<b>どうにも</b>{f|止|と}められ<b>なかった</b>。
The price rise had already been decided and there was no stopping it.
</pre>

<code>とても</code> measures a gap - the price against the wallet.
<code>どうにも</code> reports that every avenue has been closed. Where a quantity or
a degree is in view, とても is the better fit; where the speaker has been casting
around for a way to act, どうにも is.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|会議|かいぎ}が<b>どうにも</b>{f|長引|ながび}いた。 → ✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}が<b>どうにも</b>{f|終|お}わら<b>なかった</b>。
  どうにも requires a negative. In an affirmative sentence it has no meaning to
  contribute.

- ❌ <b>どうにも</b>{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}したい。 → ✅ <b>どうしても</b>{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}したい。
  Strong positive determination is <code>どうしても</code>. どうにも cannot express
  wanting.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}の{f|性格|せいかく}を<b>どうにもならない</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}の{f|性格|せいかく}を<b>どうにもできない</b>。
  <code>ならない</code> is intransitive and takes は or が; with an を object the verb
  must be <code>できない</code>.

- ❌ （in a written report）{f|状況|じょうきょう}は<b>どうにもこうにも</b>{f|改善|かいぜん}しません。 → ✅ {f|状況|じょうきょう}は{f|改善|かいぜん}の{f|見通|みとお}しが{f|立|た}ちません。
  <code>どうにもこうにも</code> is conversational. In a formal document it reads as
  though the writer had given up mid-sentence.

## Practice

**1.** {f|隣|となり}の{f|部屋|へや}の{f|話|はな}し{f|声|ごえ}が{f|気|き}になって、____{f|集中|しゅうちゅう}できない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>どうにも</b>{f|集中|しゅうちゅう}できない

The speaker has presumably tried moving, closing the door and putting up with it, and
nothing helps - the helplessness this adverb carries. Note the obligatory negative
predicate.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|試合|しあい}には（どうにも・どうしても）{f|勝|か}ちたい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|試合|しあい}には<b>どうしても</b>{f|勝|か}ちたい。

This is determination, and the predicate is affirmative. どうにも works only with a
negative, so it cannot appear here at all.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I understand why she's angry, but there is nothing I can do about it now."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼女|かのじょ}が{f|怒|おこ}る{f|理由|りゆう}は{f|分|わ}かるが、{f|今|いま}となっては<b>どうにもならない</b>。

"There is nothing I can do about it now" is the frozen
<code>どうにもならない</code>. If you want to name yourself as the one who is
powerless, use {f|私|わたし}にはどうにもできない.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|問題|もんだい}をどうにもならないので、{f|専門家|せんもんか}に{f|相談|そうだん}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}を<b>どうにもできない</b>ので、{f|専門家|せんもんか}に{f|相談|そうだん}した。
（or この{f|問題|もんだい}<b>は</b>どうにもならないので…）

<code>ならない</code> cannot take an を object. Either switch the verb to
<code>できない</code> or switch the particle to は and let the problem be the subject.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|靴|くつ}は<b>とても</b>{f|履|は}け<b>ない</b>。
(b) この{f|靴|くつ}は<b>どうにも</b>{f|履|は}け<b>ない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) puts the shoes out of range. They are far too small, or so impractical that
wearing them is not a serious proposition - the sentence measures a gap.

(b) has the speaker fighting with them. They have loosened the laces, tried a thinner
sock, sat down and tried again, and the shoes still will not go on. The frustration
of having exhausted the options is what どうにも contributes.

</details>
