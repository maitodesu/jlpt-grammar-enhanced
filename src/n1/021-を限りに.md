# ～を限りに

**Meaning** · as of; with X as the last time - names the day, month or occasion after which something stops for good

Read <code>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</code> as drawing a line and shutting the door on the far
side of it. <code>{f|本日|ほんじつ}を{f|限|かぎ}りに{f|閉店|へいてん}</code> means today
is the last day of trading: the new state - not trading - runs from that boundary
onwards. The syllabus gloss "starting from" is only half the story, and the half it
leaves out is the important one. **Something ends here.**

A separate, frozen use of the same words is
<code>{f|声|こえ}を{f|限|かぎ}りに</code>, "at the top of one's voice", which belongs to
narrative prose.

## Formation

A noun goes in front with を. There is no verb form and no clause form.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Day, date | Noun + を{f|限|かぎ}りに | {f|本日|ほんじつ} → {f|本日|ほんじつ}を{f|限|かぎ}りに |
| Month, year, period | Noun + を{f|限|かぎ}りに | {f|今年度|こんねんど} → {f|今年度|こんねんど}を{f|限|かぎ}りに |
| Occasion, event | Noun + を{f|限|かぎ}りに | {f|今大会|こんたいかい} → {f|今大会|こんたいかい}を{f|限|かぎ}りに |
| Usual main clause | ～を{f|限|かぎ}りに + {f|終了|しゅうりょう}／{f|閉店|へいてん}／{f|引退|いんたい} | {f|営業|えいぎょう}を{f|終了|しゅうりょう}する |
| Frozen expression | {f|声|こえ}を{f|限|かぎ}りに + {f|叫|さけ}ぶ／{f|呼|よ}ぶ | {f|声|こえ}を{f|限|かぎ}りに{f|叫|さけ}んだ |
| Modern equivalent (spoken) | Noun + で | {f|今日|きょう}で{f|終|お}わり |
| Modern equivalent (notices) | Noun + をもって | {f|本日|ほんじつ}をもって{f|閉店|へいてん}いたします |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - three lookalikes, three different jobs.
<code>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</code> = this is the last time.
<code>に{f|限|かぎ}り</code> = only in this case
（{f|本日|ほんじつ}に{f|限|かぎ}り{f|半額|はんがく}）.
<code>{f|限|かぎ}り</code> = as long as it holds
（{f|体力|たいりょく}が{f|続|つづ}く{f|限|かぎ}り）.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|当店|とうてん}は{f|本日|ほんじつ}<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>{f|閉店|へいてん}いたします。
This shop will close for the last time today.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>、たばこはやめる。
As of today, I am finished with cigarettes.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|勝手|かって}ながら、{f|今月|こんげつ}<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>{f|配達|はいたつ}サービスを{f|終了|しゅうりょう}させていただきます。
We regret that our delivery service will end at the close of this month.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|選手|せんしゅ}は{f|今|こん}シーズン<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>{f|現役|げんえき}を{f|退|しりぞ}くと{f|発表|はっぴょう}した。
The player announced that he would retire at the end of this season.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|路線|ろせん}は{f|三月|さんがつ}{f|末|まつ}<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>{f|廃止|はいし}されることが{f|決|き}まった。
It has been decided that this line will be discontinued after the end of March.
</pre>

<pre>

The evening opening hours kept up since the shop was founded will end with this year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会長|かいちょう}は{f|今年度|こんねんど}<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>{f|職|しょく}を{f|辞|じ}する{f|意向|いこう}を{f|示|しめ}した。
The chairman indicated that he intends to leave his post at the end of this financial year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|少女|しょうじょ}は{f|声|こえ}<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>{f|助|たす}けを{f|求|もと}めた。
The girl cried out for help at the top of her voice.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise this one; do not reach for it.** Where you will actually meet it is on
printed notices in shop windows, in the closing paragraph of a formal announcement, in
sports and business news reporting a retirement or a withdrawal, and - in the
<code>{f|声|こえ}を{f|限|かぎ}りに</code> form - in novels. It is stiff, faintly
old-fashioned, and it belongs to the voice of an institution announcing a decision.
Said across a kitchen table it lands like a proclamation.

What a Japanese speaker says instead is simply **で**:
<code>この{f|店|みせ}、{f|今日|きょう}で{f|終|お}わりなんだって</code>,
<code>{f|部活|ぶかつ}は{f|今日|きょう}で{f|最後|さいご}だ</code>. In a written notice
today the usual choice is <code>をもって</code> -
<code>{f|本日|ほんじつ}をもって{f|営業|えいぎょう}を{f|終了|しゅうりょう}いたします</code>
- which sounds formal without sounding antique. Those two, で and をもって, are the
forms to produce.

The one place a learner might genuinely write
<code>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</code> is the theatrical personal vow:
<code>{f|今日|きょう}を{f|限|かぎ}りに…</code> announcing that you are giving something
up. Even there it is a deliberately dramatic choice, closer to "from this day forth"
than to ordinary speech, and it is normally said with a smile at its own grandeur.

<code>{f|声|こえ}を{f|限|かぎ}りに</code> is a separate matter: it is a fixed
collocation with {f|叫|さけ}ぶ, {f|呼|よ}ぶ and a few other verbs of calling out, it is
purely literary, and no one uses it about themselves.

## Contrast

### ～を{f|限|かぎ}りに vs ～で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning, opposite registers. <code>で</code> is what
people say; <code>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</code> is what notices print. **Use で.**

</div>

<pre>
このバイト、{f|今月|こんげつ}<b>で</b>やめるつもり。
I'm planning to quit this part-time job at the end of the month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本店|ほんてん}は{f|今月|こんげつ}<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>{f|営業|えいぎょう}を{f|終了|しゅうりょう}いたします。
The main branch will cease trading at the end of this month.
</pre>

Same month, same ending, two different worlds. The first is a conversation; the
second is a sign on a door. If you are speaking, で and
<code>{f|最後|さいご}</code> will carry everything you need:
<code>{f|今日|きょう}が{f|最後|さいご}です</code>.

### ～を{f|限|かぎ}りに vs ～をもって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are formal announcement wording.
<code>をもって</code> is the **current** one and is far more common today;
<code>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</code> sounds older and more emphatic.

</div>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|三月|さんがつ}{f|三十一日|さんじゅういちにち}<b>をもって</b>{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}らせていただきます。
Applications will close on 31 March.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大会|たいかい}は{f|今回|こんかい}<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>{f|終|お}わることになった。
This will be the last time the competition is held.
</pre>

<code>をもって</code> is also the wider word: it can mark a starting boundary
(<code>{f|四月|しがつ}{f|一日|ついたち}をもって{f|発効|はっこう}する</code>) and a means
(<code>{f|書面|しょめん}をもって{f|通知|つうち}する</code>), neither of which
<code>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</code> can do. If you are drafting a notice, reach for
<code>をもって</code>.

### {f|声|こえ}を{f|限|かぎ}りに vs {f|大声|おおごえ}で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|声|こえ}を{f|限|かぎ}りに</code> is narrative prose
meaning "with everything the voice had". In speech it is
<code>{f|大声|おおごえ}で</code> or <code>{f|力|ちから}いっぱい</code>.

</div>

<pre>
{f|男|おとこ}は{f|声|こえ}<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>{f|仲間|なかま}の{f|名|な}を{f|呼|よ}んだ。
The man called his companion's name at the top of his voice.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもたちが{f|大声|おおごえ}で{f|応援|おうえん}している。
The children are cheering at the top of their voices.
</pre>

The first sentence is a line of fiction and would be strange in conversation; the
second is what you would say watching a school sports day. Note too that this use has
nothing to do with endings - it is the same word
<code>{f|限|かぎ}り</code> in its other sense, "limit".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>{f|新|あたら}しい{f|生活|せいかつ}を{f|始|はじ}める。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}<b>から</b>{f|新|あたら}しい{f|生活|せいかつ}を{f|始|はじ}める。
  The pattern marks the **last** occasion of something, never the first. For a
  beginning, use から.

- ❌ この{f|割引|わりびき}は{f|本日|ほんじつ}<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>ご{f|利用|りよう}いただけます。 → ✅ この{f|割引|わりびき}は{f|本日|ほんじつ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}り</b>ご{f|利用|りよう}いただけます。
  "Only today" is <code>に{f|限|かぎ}り</code>. The two look almost the same and mean
  quite different things.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}を{f|限|かぎ}りにこのゲームやめるわ。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}<b>で</b>このゲームやめるわ。
  Not ungrammatical, but the register is broken in two: an antique announcement phrase
  followed by casual わ. In speech, で.

- ❌ {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|声|こえ}を{f|限|かぎ}りに<b>{f|話|はな}した</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|声|こえ}を{f|限|かぎ}りに<b>{f|叫|さけ}んだ</b>。
  The frozen phrase goes with verbs of shouting and calling, not with ordinary
  speaking.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to read this sentence, and what does it tell the reader?
{f|当店|とうてん}は{f|八月|はちがつ}{f|三十一日|さんじゅういちにち}<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>{f|閉店|へいてん}いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

On a printed notice in a shop window or on a shop's website - it is the formal
announcement register. It tells the reader that 31 August is the **final** day of
trading, not the day something new begins.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese, as you would say it to a friend:
{f|今日|きょう}を{f|限|かぎ}りに、{f|甘|あま}いものは{f|食|た}べないことにする。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|今日|きょう}<b>で</b>{f|甘|あま}いものはやめる。（or {f|今日|きょう}が{f|最後|さいご}にする）

The plain particle で carries the same boundary without the proclamation. Keeping
<code>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</code> in casual speech makes the sentence sound like a public
announcement about biscuits.

</details>

**3.** Choose: {f|本日|ほんじつ}（を{f|限|かぎ}りに・に{f|限|かぎ}り）、{f|全品|ぜんぴん}{f|二割引|にわりびき}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|本日|ほんじつ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}り</b>

The sign means "today only" - a restriction on when the discount applies.
<code>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</code> would announce that the shop is discounting for the very
last time in its history, which is a different piece of news.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|来月|らいげつ}を{f|限|かぎ}りに、{f|新|あたら}しいサービスを{f|開始|かいし}いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|来月|らいげつ}<b>より</b>、{f|新|あたら}しいサービスを{f|開始|かいし}いたします。

A launch is a beginning, and this pattern can only mark an ending. Formal notices use
より or <code>{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|一日|ついたち}をもって</code> for a starting boundary.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in register and effect:
(a) {f|本日|ほんじつ}<b>を{f|限|かぎ}りに</b>{f|営業|えいぎょう}を{f|終了|しゅうりょう}いたします。
(b) {f|今日|きょう}<b>で</b>お{f|店|みせ}は{f|終|お}わりです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the printed voice of the business itself. It is formal, a little old-fashioned,
and it carries a sense of finality suited to a closing announcement pinned to a door.

(b) is a person telling you the same fact, perhaps a member of staff at the counter.
It is the wording a learner should produce; (a) is the wording a learner should be
able to read.

</details>
