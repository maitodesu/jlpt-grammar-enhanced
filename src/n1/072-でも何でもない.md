# ～でも何でもない

**Meaning** · not X at all; nothing of the sort - a flat rebuttal of a label somebody has just applied

The frame is the ordinary "neither A nor B" negative with {f|何|なん} standing in for
B: "not X, and not anything else either". That is why it lands as a **rebuttal**
rather than a description. Somebody has called the thing a joke, an accident, an
apology - and this construction says no, it is not, and here is what it really is.

## Formation

Nouns and な-adjectives take でも{f|何|なん}でもない. い-adjectives take a different
frame, ～くも{f|何|なん}ともない, and the pairing of く with とも is fixed.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + でも{f|何|なん}でもない | {f|冗談|じょうだん} → {f|冗談|じょうだん}でも{f|何|なん}でもない |
| な-adjective | Stem + でも{f|何|なん}でもない | {f|失礼|しつれい} → {f|失礼|しつれい}でも{f|何|なん}でもない |
| い-adjective | ～く + も{f|何|なん}ともない | {f|面白|おもしろ}い → {f|面白|おもしろ}くも{f|何|なん}ともない |
| Verb (needs a nominaliser) | ～わけ／～つもり + でも{f|何|なん}でもない | {f|断|ことわ}る → {f|断|ことわ}るわけでも{f|何|なん}でもない |
| Polite | ～でも{f|何|なん}でもありません | {f|秘密|ひみつ} → {f|秘密|ひみつ}でも{f|何|なん}でもありません |
| Past | ～でも{f|何|なん}でもなかった | {f|偶然|ぐうぜん} → {f|偶然|ぐうぜん}でも{f|何|なん}でもなかった |
| Mid-sentence | ～でも{f|何|なん}でもなく、… | {f|結果|けっか} → {f|結果|けっか}でも{f|何|なん}でもなく |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the reading is **なん**, not なに. {f|何|なん} takes なん before で, だ
and の（{f|何|なん}でも, {f|何|なん}だ, {f|何|なん}の）and なに before が and か
（{f|何|なに}が, {f|何|なに}か）. Said aloud the tail of the phrase runs
でもなんでもない, and the い-adjective version runs くもなんともない.

</div>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

"Is it a secret?" "Not in the slightest. Nobody asked me, that's all."
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

She's no expert of any kind, and yet she happily tells everyone what to do.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

The **form** is register-neutral - the polite
でも{f|何|なん}でもありません is entirely at home in a formal complaint or a written
statement, and the plain form in conversation. What is marked is the **stance**. This
is a contradiction, and it presupposes that a label is on the table. Somebody called
it a joke, or the newspaper called it a coincidence, or the letter was described as
an apology; the sentence rejects that description outright.

That presupposition is a real constraint. Out of the blue, with nothing to push back
against, the expression falls flat - ❌{f|今日|きょう}は{f|雨|あめ}でも
{f|何|なん}でもない said to someone looking out of a window is simply odd, because no
one suggested it was raining. A useful test: if you could preface the English with
"That is not…" or "It was no…", the pattern fits.

Because it contradicts, it usually comes with a **correction attached**. The typical
shape is two clauses - でも{f|何|なん}でもない, then ただの… or
{f|実|じつ}は… saying what the thing actually is. Left without the second half it
can sound curt, which is sometimes exactly the effect wanted.

The い-adjective member of the family, ～くも{f|何|なん}ともない, is worth learning
separately. {f|痛|いた}くも{f|何|なん}ともない, {f|悔|くや}しくも
{f|何|なん}ともない, {f|怖|こわ}くも{f|何|なん}ともない are all common, and all deny a
feeling somebody has attributed to the speaker.

## Contrast

### ～でも{f|何|なん}でもない vs ～ではない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ではない</code> is a **statement**: X is not the case.
<code>でも{f|何|なん}でもない</code> is a **rebuttal**: X is not the case, and don't
suggest otherwise.

</div>

<pre>
これは{f|私|わたし}の{f|本|ほん}<b>ではありません</b>。
This is not my book.
</pre>

<pre>
これは{f|私|わたし}の{f|責任|せきにん}<b>でも{f|何|なん}でもありません</b>。
This is not my responsibility, and it's no use pretending it is.
</pre>

The first identifies whose book it is not, and carries no heat. The second answers an
accusation. Note that <code>ではない</code> will go anywhere, including in neutral
description, while the longer form needs something to be arguing with.

### ～でも{f|何|なん}でもない vs {f|何|なん}でもない (on its own)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with a noun in front it means "not X at all". **Standing alone**,
{f|何|なん}でもない means "it's nothing, never mind" - about a situation, not a label.

</div>

<pre>
「どうしたの?」「ううん、<b>{f|何|なん}でもない</b>」
"What's wrong?" "Nothing. Never mind."
</pre>

<pre>
{f|怪我|けが}といっても、{f|大|たい}したこと<b>でも{f|何|なん}でもない</b>。
It's called an injury, but it's nothing serious at all.
</pre>

The two look almost identical on the page and do quite different work. The bare
{f|何|なん}でもない deflects concern; the full pattern rejects a description. Listen
for whether a noun precedes it.

### ～でも{f|何|なん}でもない vs ～なんかじゃない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same rebuttal at two levels.
<code>なんかじゃない</code> is **casual speech**;
<code>でも{f|何|なん}でもない</code> is **stronger and register-flexible**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|友達|ともだち}<b>なんかじゃない</b>よ、ただのクラスメイト。
He's not a friend or anything - just someone in my class.
</pre>

<pre>
これは{f|値引|ねび}き<b>でも{f|何|なん}でもなく</b>、{f|元|もと}の{f|値段|ねだん}に{f|戻|もど}しただけです。
This is not a discount of any kind; the price has simply gone back to what it was.
</pre>

<code>なんか</code> carries a dismissive edge and belongs to conversation; putting it
in a written statement would sound flippant. The longer form keeps the force and
loses the flippancy, which is why it is the one that appears in complaints,
editorials and formal denials.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|面白|おもしろ}い<b>でも{f|何|なん}でもない</b> → ✅ {f|面白|おもしろ}<b>くも{f|何|なん}ともない</b>
  い-adjectives do not take で. They use the く form plus も{f|何|なん}ともない.

- ❌ {f|痛|いた}くも{f|何|なん}<b>でも</b>ない → ✅ {f|痛|いた}くも{f|何|なん}<b>とも</b>ない
  Within the い-adjective frame the pair is く … とも. Mixing the two frames is the
  single most common slip with this pattern.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|悪|わる}い{f|人|ひと}だと{f|言|い}う<b>でも{f|何|なん}でもない</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|悪|わる}いと{f|言|い}っている<b>わけでも{f|何|なん}でもない</b>。
  A verb cannot attach directly. Nominalise it first with
  <code>わけ</code>, <code>つもり</code> or <code>こと</code>.

- ❌ （unprompted, looking at the sky）{f|今日|きょう}は{f|雨|あめ}<b>でも{f|何|なん}でもない</b>。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っていない。
  There is nothing to rebut. The pattern needs a claim in the air; without one it
  sounds as if the speaker is arguing with themselves.

## Practice

**1.** 「あの{f|人|ひと}、{f|君|きみ}の{f|彼氏|かれし}?」「____よ。{f|兄|あに}の{f|友達|ともだち}」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼氏|かれし}<b>でも{f|何|なん}でもない</b>よ

A label has just been proposed and the speaker is throwing it out - the core use.
Note the correction that follows, which is how the pattern usually arrives.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|注射|ちゅうしゃ}は（{f|痛|いた}いでも{f|何|なん}でもなかった・{f|痛|いた}くも{f|何|なん}ともなかった）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|注射|ちゅうしゃ}は<b>{f|痛|いた}くも{f|何|なん}ともなかった</b>。

{f|痛|いた}い is an い-adjective, so it takes the く frame paired with とも. The
でも frame belongs to nouns and な-adjectives only.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "What he wrote was not a report at all - it was an advertisement."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}が{f|書|か}いたものは{f|報告|ほうこく}<b>でも{f|何|なん}でもなく</b>、ただの{f|宣伝|せんでん}だった。

The mid-sentence form なく joins the rebuttal to the correction, which is the shape
this pattern most often takes. ただの reinforces the dismissal.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|結果|けっか}は{f|運|うん}がよかったでも{f|何|なん}でもない。{f|準備|じゅんび}の{f|差|さ}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|結果|けっか}は{f|運|うん}<b>でも{f|何|なん}でもない</b>。{f|準備|じゅんび}の{f|差|さ}だ。
（or {f|運|うん}がよかった<b>わけでも{f|何|なん}でもない</b>）

A full clause cannot sit in front of でも. Either reduce it to the bare noun
{f|運|うん}, or nominalise with <code>わけ</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) それは{f|問題|もんだい}<b>ではありません</b>。
(b) それは{f|問題|もんだい}<b>でも{f|何|なん}でもありません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a calm assessment. The speaker has looked at the matter and judges that it
does not constitute a problem; a manager could say it reassuringly.

(b) pushes back. It assumes someone has been calling the thing a problem, and it
denies the label with some force - roughly "that is not a problem, nor anything
resembling one". The listener will hear disagreement, not reassurance.

</details>
