# ～を機に

**Meaning** · taking X as the opportunity to; now that X has happened - names the event a person decided to use as the moment for a change

<code>{f|機|き}</code> is the noun "opportunity, opening, the right moment". A sentence
built on <code>を{f|機|き}に</code> has two halves and a decision between them: the
event in front, and the deliberate change behind it. Marriage, a move, retirement, a
company's anniversary - the event does not force anything, it simply supplies a
convenient line to start from.

## Formation

A noun goes in front with を. A verb clause has to be nominalised with の or こと
first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event noun | Noun + を{f|機|き}に | {f|結婚|けっこん} → {f|結婚|けっこん}を{f|機|き}に |
| Emphatic | Noun + を{f|機|き}にして | {f|退職|たいしょく} → {f|退職|たいしょく}を{f|機|き}にして |
| Formal written twin | Noun + を{f|契機|けいき}に／として | {f|受賞|じゅしょう} → {f|受賞|じゅしょう}を{f|契機|けいき}として |
| Verb clause | plain past + の／こと + を{f|機|き}に | {f|転勤|てんきん}した → {f|転勤|てんきん}したのを{f|機|き}に |
| Pointing at the present occasion | これを{f|機|き}に | これを{f|機|き}に{f|禁煙|きんえん}する |
| Set greeting | これを{f|機|き}に{f|今後|こんご}とも… | これを{f|機|き}に{f|今後|こんご}ともよろしく |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the second half must be something **somebody chose to do**:
{f|始|はじ}める, やめる, {f|見直|みなお}す, {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}す, {f|改|あらた}める.
If the second half is an accident or a physical result, the pattern is wrong and で
or によって is what you want.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|結婚|けっこん}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、たばこをやめた。
I gave up smoking when I got married.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|入学|にゅうがく}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|一人暮|ひとりぐ}らしを{f|始|はじ}めた。
Starting university was my chance to begin living alone.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|定年|ていねん}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|夫婦|ふうふ}で{f|田舎|いなか}に{f|移|うつ}り{f|住|す}んだ。
On retiring, the couple moved out to the countryside.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|転勤|てんきん}したの<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|車|くるま}を{f|手放|てばな}すことにした。
Since I was being transferred anyway, I decided to get rid of the car.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大|おお}きな{f|病気|びょうき}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|食事|しょくじ}の{f|内容|ないよう}を{f|見直|みなお}した。
A serious illness was what prompted me to rethink what I eat.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|創立|そうりつ}{f|百|ひゃく}{f|周年|しゅうねん}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|社名|しゃめい}を{f|改|あらた}めることになった。
Marking its hundredth year, the company is to change its name.
</pre>

<pre>
これ<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|部署|ぶしょ}の{f|仕事|しごと}の{f|進|すす}め{f|方|かた}を{f|一度|いちど}{f|整理|せいり}してはどうでしょうか。
Why not take this as our chance to sort out how the team works?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|海外|かいがい}{f|赴任|ふにん}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}として</b>、{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|語学|ごがく}{f|研修|けんしゅう}の{f|制度|せいど}を{f|整|ととの}えた。
The posting abroad was the occasion for the company to put language training on a proper footing.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>を{f|機|き}に</code> is **formal but ordinary** - noticeably more careful than
everyday speech, and completely at home in a polite conversation, a self-introduction,
a New Year card, a company notice or a magazine profile. Learners often assume it is
stiff written grammar; in fact a colleague announcing a life change will use it
happily, because it is the compact way of saying "I used the occasion to…".

The event in front is usually **positive or at least neutral**, and it is very often a
milestone: {f|結婚|けっこん}, {f|出産|しゅっさん}, {f|就職|しゅうしょく},
{f|定年|ていねん}, {f|創立|そうりつ}{f|記念|きねん}. Illness and injury do appear -
<code>{f|入院|にゅういん}を{f|機|き}に{f|生活|せいかつ}を{f|改|あらた}めた</code> - but
notice what makes them work: the speaker turned the bad event into a starting point.
That is the constant. The event is treated as an **opening the speaker took**, never
as a blow that landed on them.

<code>{f|契機|けいき}</code> is the same word in a stiffer suit. It belongs to editorial
and academic writing and can describe impersonal social change -
<code>{f|震災|しんさい}を{f|契機|けいき}として{f|防災|ぼうさい}の{f|意識|いしき}が{f|高|たか}まった</code>
- where <code>を{f|機|き}に</code>, with its sense of somebody choosing, would be
slightly odd.

## Contrast

### ～を{f|機|き}に vs ～をきっかけに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>きっかけ</code> is a **trigger**, often accidental, and it
may set off something the person never intended. <code>{f|機|き}</code> is an
**opportunity**, and somebody deliberately took it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|友人|ゆうじん}の{f|一言|ひとこと}<b>をきっかけに</b>、{f|絵|え}を{f|描|か}き{f|始|はじ}めた。
A remark from a friend was what started me painting.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|退職|たいしょく}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|長年|ながねん}の{f|夢|ゆめ}だった{f|絵|え}を{f|習|なら}い{f|始|はじ}めた。
Retiring gave me the chance to take up the painting lessons I had always wanted.
</pre>

The stray remark was nobody's plan; the retirement was a known date the speaker built
on. This is also why <code>きっかけ</code> can head a sentence about something that
simply happened to somebody - a fear that took hold, a habit that crept in - while
<code>{f|機|き}に</code> always implies an act of will.

### ～を{f|機|き}に vs ～を{f|契機|けいき}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning, different register.
<code>{f|契機|けいき}</code> is the written, analytical one, and it can take an
impersonal subject.

</div>

<pre>
{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}し<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|家具|かぐ}をすべて{f|買|か}い{f|替|か}えた。
We replaced all the furniture when we moved.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|事件|じけん}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}として</b>、{f|制度|せいど}の{f|見直|みなお}しが{f|始|はじ}まった。
The incident became the occasion for a review of the system.
</pre>

Put <code>{f|契機|けいき}</code> into the furniture sentence and it sounds like a
government white paper about a sofa. Put <code>{f|機|き}に</code> into the second and
the sentence quietly claims that someone chose to use the incident, which a report
would avoid saying.

### ～を{f|機|き}に vs ～に{f|際|さい}して

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|際|さい}して</code> says you do the thing **appropriate
to** the occasion. <code>を{f|機|き}に</code> says you use the occasion to change
something **else**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|開店|かいてん}<b>に{f|際|さい}して</b>、{f|近所|きんじょ}の{f|方|かた}に{f|挨拶|あいさつ}に{f|回|まわ}った。
On opening the shop, we went round to greet the neighbours.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|開店|かいてん}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|朝|あさ}{f|型|がた}の{f|生活|せいかつ}に{f|変|か}えた。
Opening the shop was my chance to switch to getting up early.
</pre>

Greeting the neighbours is part of opening a shop; changing your sleeping hours is
not. That is the whole difference, and it decides which pattern a sentence needs.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|事故|じこ}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>{f|足|あし}を{f|骨折|こっせつ}した。 → ✅ {f|事故|じこ}<b>で</b>{f|足|あし}を{f|骨折|こっせつ}した。
  A broken leg is not a decision. The second half has to be something the person
  chose to do.

- ❌ {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}す<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|減|へ}らした。 → ✅ {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}したの<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|減|へ}らした。
  <code>{f|機|き}</code> is a noun, so a verb clause needs の or こと, and the past
  form is normal because the event has happened.

- ❌ {f|毎朝|まいあさ}のジョギング<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|体|からだ}が{f|軽|かる}くなった。 → ✅ ジョギングを{f|始|はじ}めてから、{f|体|からだ}が{f|軽|かる}くなった。
  A daily habit is not an occasion. The slot wants a one-off event with a date on it.

- ❌ {f|来月|らいげつ}{f|結婚|けっこん}する<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}します。 → ✅ {f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|結婚|けっこん}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}します。
  Future events are fine, but the grammar is not: use the event noun, or
  <code>{f|結婚|けっこん}するのを{f|機|き}に</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|子|こ}どもの{f|誕生|たんじょう}____、{f|保険|ほけん}を{f|見直|みなお}すことにした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|子|こ}どもの{f|誕生|たんじょう}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>

A milestone in front, a deliberate decision behind it - reviewing the insurance is
something the speaker chose to do, not something the birth caused directly.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|一本|いっぽん}の{f|映画|えいが}（を{f|機|き}に・をきっかけに）、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|医者|いしゃ}を{f|目指|めざ}すようになった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一本|いっぽん}の{f|映画|えいが}<b>をきっかけに</b>

A film he happened to see is an accidental trigger, and
<code>ようになった</code> describes a change that came over him rather than one he
planned. <code>を{f|機|き}に</code> would need a scheduled event and a deliberate act.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I took the move to Osaka as an opportunity to start studying Japanese again."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|大阪|おおさか}への{f|転勤|てんきん}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|日本語|にほんご}の{f|勉強|べんきょう}を{f|再開|さいかい}した。

"Took X as an opportunity to" is <code>を{f|機|き}に</code> almost word for word. The
main clause is a decision, which is what the pattern requires.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|台風|たいふう}を{f|機|き}に、{f|屋根|やね}のかわらが{f|飛|と}んでしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|台風|たいふう}<b>で</b>、{f|屋根|やね}のかわらが{f|飛|と}んでしまった。

Tiles blowing off is damage, not a choice, so the sentence needs the plain cause
marker で. A correct <code>を{f|機|き}に</code> version would have to continue with
something the owner decided, such as
<code>{f|屋根|やね}を{f|直|なお}すことにした</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|入院|にゅういん}<b>をきっかけに</b>、{f|酒|さけ}を{f|飲|の}まなくなった。
(b) {f|入院|にゅういん}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|酒|さけ}をやめた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports a change that settled in after the hospital stay. なくなった leaves it
open whether the speaker resolved on it or simply lost the habit.

(b) has the speaker making a decision and using the hospital stay as the line to draw
it at. やめた is deliberate, and the sentence sounds like something said with a
certain amount of pride.

</details>
