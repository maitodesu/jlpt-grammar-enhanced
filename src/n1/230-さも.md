# さも

**Meaning** · with every appearance of; just as though - describing how somebody
looked while doing something, often with a hint that it was a performance

`さも` never tells you what a person felt. It tells you what they looked like they
felt, as seen by someone watching them - and the watcher is usually not entirely
convinced.

## Usage

Like まるで, `さも` cannot stand on its own.

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `さも` must be answered by an appearance marker:
<code>さも～そうに／そうな</code>, <code>さも～ように／ような</code>, or
<code>さも～かのように</code>.

</div>

The three frames in order of frequency:

- <code>さも{f|楽|たの}しそうに{f|笑|わら}う</code> - the commonest, "laughing as if
  he were having a wonderful time"
- <code>さも{f|当然|とうぜん}のように{f|言|い}う</code> - "saying it as though it went
  without saying"
- <code>さも{f|見|み}てきたかのように{f|話|はな}す</code> - "talking as though he had
  been there to see it"

Almost always it modifies a **manner**: how the person laughed, said it, nodded,
handed something over. That is why the ending is so often
<code>そうに</code> or <code>ように</code> rather than <code>そうだ</code>.

One trap. <code>さもないと</code> and <code>さもなければ</code>, meaning "otherwise,
or else", are a different word - <code>{f|急|いそ}ごう。さもないと{f|遅|おく}れる</code>.
Same three sounds, no connection.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は<b>さも</b>おいしそうにビールを{f|飲|の}んだ。
He drank his beer with every appearance of enjoying it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子供|こども}は<b>さも</b>{f|得意|とくい}そうな{f|顔|かお}で{f|絵|え}を{f|見|み}せに{f|来|き}た。
The child brought the picture over to show me, looking terribly pleased with himself.
</pre>

<pre>

She took the documents off my desk as though it were the most natural thing in the world.
</pre>

<pre>

The man went on describing the accident as if he had been standing there watching it.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

A writer's word. `さも` belongs to fiction, essays and reported description; it is
the narrator standing back and reading a face. In conversation it is rare enough
that using it will make you sound like a novel, and the everyday alternatives are
<code>いかにも～そうに</code>, <code>{f|本当|ほんとう}に～そうに</code>, or simply
<code>～そうに</code> with no adverb at all.

The scepticism is the part learners miss. <code>さも</code> reports a display, and
in most contexts the display is being put on: the speaker is telling you that the
person *acted* delighted, *acted* as though they knew. It is not a rule -
<code>さも{f|嬉|うれ}しそうに</code> can describe genuine delight - but the suspicion
is close enough to the surface that describing your own boss this way is not
neutral.

Because of that, `さも` is a good tool for characterisation and a bad one for
compliments. If you mean somebody really did look happy, and you want no
implication of theatre, drop the adverb:
<code>{f|嬉|うれ}しそうに{f|笑|わら}った</code> says it cleanly.

## Contrast

### さも vs いかにも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>いかにも</code> endorses an appearance: that is exactly
what such a thing looks like. `さも` reports a display and often doubts it: he made
a great show of it.

</div>

<pre>
<b>いかにも</b>{f|高|たか}そうな{f|時計|とけい}をしていた。
He was wearing a watch that looked expensive, just as you'd imagine.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>さも</b>{f|高|たか}そうに{f|時計|とけい}を{f|見|み}せびらかしていた。
He was showing off the watch as though to say how expensive it was.
</pre>

The first is about the watch and how well it fits the type. The second is about the
man and the performance he is giving. Note too that
<code>いかにも</code> is comfortable in speech, while `さも` is not.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}はさも{f|嬉|うれ}しかった。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}はさも{f|嬉|うれ}し<b>そうだった</b>。
  The appearance marker is compulsory. `さも` describes the look on a face, so it
  cannot attach to a plain statement about the feeling itself.

- ❌ さも{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}りそうだ。 → ✅ <b>{f|今|いま}にも</b>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}りそうだ。
  `さも` reads people, not skies. For "it looks about to rain", use
  <code>{f|今|いま}にも</code>.

- ❌ {f|急|いそ}ごう、さも{f|遅|おく}れる。 → ✅ {f|急|いそ}ごう、<b>さもないと</b>{f|遅|おく}れる。
  "Or else" is <code>さもないと</code> or <code>さもなければ</code>, a separate
  expression. Bare `さも` cannot carry that meaning.

## Practice

**1.** {f|彼|かれ}は{f|一言|ひとこと}も{f|謝|あやま}らず、____{f|悪|わる}いのはこちらだと____{f|顔|かお}をしていた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}は{f|一言|ひとこと}も{f|謝|あやま}らず、<b>さも</b>{f|悪|わる}いのはこちらだと<b>いう</b>{f|顔|かお}をしていた。

The frame here is <code>さも～という{f|顔|かお}をする</code>, a very common way of
reporting an expression. The adverb sets up the appearance and
<code>という{f|顔|かお}</code> delivers it; without the second half the sentence has
nothing to land on.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼女|かのじょ}はさも{f|自信|じしん}がある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}はさも{f|自信|じしん}が<b>ありそうに{f|話|はな}した</b>。

Two repairs, both required by the same rule. The appearance marker
<code>そうに</code> has to appear, and because `さも` describes a manner, the
sentence wants a verb of doing - speaking, laughing, nodding - for the manner to
attach to.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}はさも{f|感動|かんどう}したように{f|話|はな}した。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|感動|かんどう}して{f|話|はな}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (b) states as fact that he was moved, and the speaker takes that at face
value. Sentence (a) reports only the outward show, and the adverb leans toward
disbelief: he spoke in the manner of a man who had been moved, and the writer is
inviting you to wonder whether he really was.

</details>
