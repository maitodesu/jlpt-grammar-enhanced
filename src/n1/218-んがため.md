# ～んがため

**Meaning** · for the sole purpose of; in order to - a heavy, archaic purpose clause, usually with a hint that the goal justified everything else

<code>んがため</code> is the classical volitional auxiliary `む` (written and
pronounced ん) + the old genitive particle `が` + ため: literally "for the sake of
[intending to do X]". It survives in speeches, editorials, court documents and
literary narration, and nowhere else. Read it as `ために`. Write `ために`.

## Formation

The base is the ない-stem (the classical {f|未然形|みぜんけい}) - the **opposite**
of <code>べく</code>, which takes the dictionary form. Mixing the two up is the
standard error.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ない-stem + んがため | {f|勝|か}たない → {f|勝|か}たんがため |
| Verb (る-verb) | ない-stem + んがため | {f|生|い}きない → {f|生|い}きんがため |
| Verb (する) | <b>せんがため</b> | {f|維持|いじ}しない → {f|維持|いじ}せんがため |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | <b>{f|来|こ}んがため</b> (rare) | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}んがため |
| With に | ～んがために | {f|守|まも}らんがために |
| Before a noun | ～んがための + noun | {f|得|え}んがための{f|行動|こうどう} |
| Sentence-final | ～んがためだ / ～んがためである | {f|勝|か}たんがためである |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the ん here is **not** the negative ん of
{f|分|わ}からん or {f|知|し}らん. It is the classical `む`, the ancestor of modern
`よう` in {f|行|い}こう and しよう. So
<code>{f|勝|か}たんがため</code> means "in order to win", never "in order not to
win". This is worth saying out loud once, because the shape is identical to the
dialect negative and learners regularly read the sentence backwards.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|生|い}き<b>んがため</b>に{f|働|はたら}く。
One works in order to live.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|勝|か}た<b>んがため</b>に{f|手段|しゅだん}を{f|選|えら}ばなかった。
He stopped at nothing in his determination to win.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|己|おのれ}の{f|利益|りえき}を{f|得|え}<b>んがため</b>の{f|行動|こうどう}にすぎない。
It is nothing more than conduct aimed at his own profit.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|真実|しんじつ}を{f|伝|つた}え<b>んがため</b>、{f|記者|きしゃ}たちは{f|危険|きけん}な{f|地域|ちいき}に{f|入|はい}った。
The reporters went into the dangerous districts in order to get the truth out.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|守|まも}ら<b>んがため</b>に、{f|事実|じじつ}を{f|隠|かく}したのではないか。
Was the truth not concealed simply in order to protect the company?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|政権|せいけん}を{f|維持|いじ}せ<b>んがため</b>の{f|政策|せいさく}だと、{f|野党|やとう}は{f|批判|ひはん}した。
The opposition attacked it as a policy designed purely to keep the government in office.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|次|つぎ}の{f|世代|せだい}に{f|豊|ゆた}かな{f|自然|しぜん}を{f|残|のこ}さ<b>んがため</b>、われわれは{f|今|いま}{f|決断|けつだん}しなければならない。
It is in order to leave a rich natural world to the next generation that we must decide now.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一族|いちぞく}の{f|名|な}を{f|守|まも}ら<b>んがため</b>に、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|生涯|しょうがい}{f|沈黙|ちんもく}を{f|通|とお}した。
To protect the name of his family he kept silent all his life.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**You will never say this.** <code>んがため</code> is the most archaic-sounding
purpose expression that still appears in modern text. It lives in political
speeches, newspaper editorials, court judgments, company statements of the gravest
kind, and historical fiction. Nobody says it. If you say
<code>{f|昼|ひる}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べんがために{f|出|で}かけた</code>, you
have said something no Japanese speaker would produce outside a joke. The word you
want is `ために`.

Beyond register, <code>んがため</code> has a **colour** that `ために` does not.
It presents the purpose as consuming - the only thing that mattered, everything
else subordinated to it. That is why it collocates so often with
{f|手段|しゅだん}を{f|選|えら}ばない ("stopping at nothing"), and why in journalism
it usually arrives with an accusation attached:
<code>{f|保身|ほしん}を{f|図|はか}らんがための{f|説明|せつめい}</code> is not a
neutral description. It can also be used admiringly - a doctor working
{f|人|ひと}を{f|救|すく}わんがため - but the intensity is always there.

Two structural points. The verb must be **volitional**: you cannot form
❌<code>{f|分|わ}からんがため</code>, because nobody decides to understand. And the
subject of the purpose is the subject of the main clause, as with
<code>べく</code>.

The family it belongs to is worth naming. The same classical `む` is behind
<code>んばかり</code> ("as if about to"), behind
<code>{f|言|い}わんとする</code> ("to be on the point of saying"), and behind the
everyday volitional {f|行|い}こう. What makes *this* pattern archaic is not the ん
but the `が` - a genitive particle modern Japanese has otherwise lost, surviving
only in fossils such as {f|我|わ}が{f|国|くに} and {f|我|わ}が{f|家|や}.

## Contrast

### ～んがため vs ～ために

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning; `ために` is the living form and
<code>んがため</code> is its ceremonial ancestor, with an added sense of "and
nothing else mattered".

</div>

<pre>
{f|家族|かぞく}を{f|養|やしな}う<b>ために</b>、{f|二|ふた}つの{f|仕事|しごと}をしている。
He works two jobs in order to support his family.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|家族|かぞく}を{f|養|やしな}わ<b>んがため</b>、{f|男|おとこ}は{f|夜|よる}も{f|働|はたら}き{f|続|つづ}けた。
It was to feed his family that the man went on working through the nights as well.
</pre>

Note the base as well as the register: `ために` follows the **dictionary form**
({f|養|やしな}う<b>ために</b>), <code>んがため</code> the **ない-stem**
({f|養|やしな}わ<b>んがため</b>). The first sentence is something a colleague could
tell you at lunch. The second has been written for a page, and it asks you to feel
the weight of the sacrifice.

### ～んがため vs ～べく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are literary purpose clauses.
<code>べく</code> is neutral and businesslike; <code>んがため</code> is emotional,
older, and often carries a judgement about the motive.

</div>

<pre>
{f|信頼|しんらい}を{f|回復|かいふく}す<b>べく</b>、{f|再発|さいはつ}{f|防止|ぼうし}{f|策|さく}をまとめた。
They drew up measures to prevent a recurrence, in order to restore confidence.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|信頼|しんらい}を{f|回復|かいふく}せ<b>んがため</b>の、{f|見|み}せかけの{f|対策|たいさく}にすぎない。
It is no more than a token measure got up in order to win back confidence.
</pre>

Swap them and something is lost each way. <code>べく</code> in the second sentence
would drain the sarcasm; <code>んがため</code> in the first would suggest the
company was desperate rather than orderly. Note also the bases -
{f|回復|かいふく}<b>す</b>べく but {f|回復|かいふく}<b>せ</b>んがため - which is the same
alternation as する → す / せ that runs through classical grammar.

### ～んがため vs ～ように

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ように` is for results you can only hope for, and it takes
potential and negative verbs. <code>んがため</code> is for goals you drive at, and
it takes neither.

</div>

<pre>
{f|失敗|しっぱい}し<b>ないように</b>、{f|何度|なんど}も{f|確|たし}かめた。
I checked over and over so as not to make a mistake.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|真相|しんそう}を{f|明|あき}らかにせ<b>んがため</b>、{f|市民|しみん}は{f|裁判|さいばん}を{f|起|お}こした。
The citizens brought the case in order to bring the truth to light.
</pre>

"In order not to" has no <code>んがため</code> form at all: the ん is a volitional,
and you cannot intend not to do something with it. Any negative purpose goes to
<code>ないように</code>, whatever the register - even a court judgment writes
<code>{f|生|しょう}じないように</code> rather than reaching for the classical.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|勝|か}つ<b>んがため</b> → ✅ {f|勝|か}た<b>んがため</b>
  The base is the ない-stem, not the dictionary form. Produce the `ない` form first
  ({f|勝|か}たない) and drop the ない.

- ❌ {f|維持|いじ}<b>しんがため</b> → ✅ {f|維持|いじ}<b>せんがため</b>
  `する` takes the classical negative base せ in front of `ん`, exactly as it does
  in <code>せず</code> and <code>せざるを{f|得|え}ない</code>.

- ❌ {f|忘|わす}れ<b>んがため</b>にメモした → ✅ {f|忘|わす}れ<b>ないように</b>メモした
  Two errors at once. There is no negative purpose form, and this is a homely
  everyday sentence that the classical ending has no business in.

- ❌ {f|来|き}<b>んがため</b> → ✅ {f|来|こ}<b>んがため</b>
  {f|来|く}る is irregular; its ない-stem is こ, as in {f|来|こ}ない. The same
  こ appears in {f|来|こ}させる and {f|来|こ}られる.

- ❌（{f|同僚|どうりょう}に）{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わ<b>んがため</b>に{f|走|はし}りました。 → ✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}う<b>ように</b>{f|走|はし}りました。
  Correctly formed, unusable. Said to a colleague this is not formality, it is
  costume.

## Practice

**1.** {f|社説|しゃせつ}の{f|一文|いちぶん}: {f|自|みずか}らの{f|立場|たちば}を{f|守|まも}____の{f|発言|はつげん}であったと{f|言|い}わざるを{f|得|え}ない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|守|まも}ら<b>んがため</b>の{f|発言|はつげん}

An editorial passing judgement on a politician's motive - the natural home of this
pattern. The base is the ない-stem {f|守|まも}ら, and the の is required because
what follows is a noun. Note the sarcastic edge:
<code>んがための</code> here means "purely in order to".

</details>

**2.** Where would you meet the following, and what is wrong with using it in an email to a colleague?
{f|情報|じょうほう}を{f|集|あつ}め<b>んがため</b>、{f|現地|げんち}に{f|人|ひと}を{f|送|おく}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

You would meet it in a newspaper article, a company history or a novel - written
narration reporting a deliberate act. In an email it fails twice over: the register
is a century too old for correspondence, and <code>んがため</code> hints that
gathering the information mattered more than anything else, which is not usually
what you mean. Write
{f|情報|じょうほう}を{f|集|あつ}めるために{f|現地|げんち}に{f|人|ひと}を{f|送|おく}りました.

</details>

**3.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|立|た}て{f|直|なお}さ<b>んがため</b>、{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|力|ちから}を{f|合|あ}わせた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|立|た}て{f|直|なお}す<b>ために</b>、{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|力|ちから}を{f|合|あ}わせた。

<code>んがため</code> → `ために`, and with it the base changes from the ない-stem
{f|立|た}て{f|直|なお}さ back to the dictionary form
{f|立|た}て{f|直|なお}す. Nothing else in the sentence needs touching, which is a
good illustration of how little the classical form actually adds.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|試験|しけん}に{f|合格|ごうかく}しんがために、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|勉強|べんきょう}しています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|試験|しけん}に{f|合格|ごうかく}する<b>ために</b>、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|勉強|べんきょう}しています。

The form ❌しんがため does not exist - する gives せんがため. But the deeper
problem is the sentence itself: ています tells you this is ordinary polite speech
about the speaker's own study routine, and no such sentence would ever carry
<code>んがため</code>. Fix the register, not the classical ending.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|票|ひょう}を{f|集|あつ}める<b>ために</b>{f|地方|ちほう}を{f|回|まわ}った。
(b) {f|票|ひょう}を{f|集|あつ}め<b>んがため</b>{f|地方|ちほう}を{f|回|まわ}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports a fact. Candidates tour the regions to gather votes; that is what
campaigning is, and the sentence carries no comment.

(b) is written, and it editorialises. <code>んがため</code> presents the vote as
the *only* thing the person cared about, so the reader hears an accusation - the
tour was cynical, the interest in the regions was not real. This is the standard
journalistic use of the pattern, and it is why you should not reach for it when you
simply mean "in order to".

</details>
