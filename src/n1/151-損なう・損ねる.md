# ～損なう / ～損ねる

**Meaning** · to fail in the attempt; to do something amiss - and, with a noun, to damage or impair it

{f|損|そこ}なう and {f|損|そこ}ねる are the same idea in two shapes. After a
ます-stem they say the attempt **went wrong or came to nothing**: you wrote it
badly, you reached for the train and did not catch it. After a noun with を they
say something valuable has been **spoiled** - health, trust, someone's mood.

## Formation

{f|損|そこ}なう is an う-verb ({f|損|そこ}なった, {f|損|そこ}ないます);
{f|損|そこ}ねる is a る-verb ({f|損|そこ}ねた, {f|損|そこ}ねます). The two are
interchangeable in most sentences.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (failed attempt) | ます-stem + {f|損|そこ}なう | {f|書|か}く → {f|書|か}き{f|損|そこ}なう |
| Verb (failed attempt) | ます-stem + {f|損|そこ}ねる | {f|乗|の}る → {f|乗|の}り{f|損|そこ}ねる |
| Noun (damage) | noun + を{f|損|そこ}なう | {f|健康|けんこう} → {f|健康|けんこう}を{f|損|そこ}なう |
| Noun (damage) | noun + を{f|損|そこ}ねる | {f|機嫌|きげん} → {f|機嫌|きげん}を{f|損|そこ}ねる |
| Past | ～{f|損|そこ}なった ／ ～{f|損|そこ}ねた | {f|取|と}り{f|損|そこ}ねた |
| Polite | ～{f|損|そこ}ないます ／ ～{f|損|そこ}ねます | {f|言|い}い{f|損|そこ}ねました |
| Risk of | ～を{f|損|そこ}なうおそれがある | {f|信用|しんよう}を{f|損|そこ}なうおそれがある |

<div class="note">

**Note** - which verb goes with which noun is largely fixed by habit. Feelings
take {f|損|そこ}ねる ({f|機嫌|きげん}を{f|損|そこ}ねる,
{f|気分|きぶん}を{f|損|そこ}ねる); health, trust, value and scenery take
{f|損|そこ}なう ({f|健康|けんこう}を{f|損|そこ}なう,
{f|信用|しんよう}を{f|損|そこ}なう). Neither pairing is a rule, but going against
it sounds slightly off.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Both uses are **ordinary Japanese**, neither literary nor casual. Between the two
verbs there is a slight tilt: {f|損|そこ}なう leans written and formal - reports,
warnings, notices - while {f|損|そこ}ねる is a little more conversational. In the
ます-stem use they are near-perfect substitutes, and speakers pick whichever
comes out.

In the failed-attempt sense the key is that **an attempt was made**. You reached
for the ball, you wrote the character, you went for the train. That is what
separates it from そびれる, where the chance never came round at all, and from
<code>～ようがない</code>, where no means of doing it existed in the first place.

{f|機嫌|きげん}を{f|損|そこ}ねる deserves its own line: it is the standard way to
say you have annoyed or offended someone, and it is everywhere in accounts of
office life, family life and customer service. It is gentler than
{f|怒|おこ}らせる - the other person has gone quiet and cool rather than shouted.

Two idioms to recognise. {f|死|し}に{f|損|そこ}なう is "to fail to die", used of
narrow escapes and, harshly, of oneself; and
{f|見|み}{f|損|そこ}なう has a second life meaning "to misjudge a person" -
{f|君|きみ}を{f|見|み}{f|損|そこ}なった, "I thought better of you", which is a
serious thing to say.

## Contrast

### ～{f|損|そこ}ねる vs ～そびれる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|損|そこ}ねる</code>: you **tried** and it went
wrong. <code>そびれる</code>: the **moment** passed and you never started.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新人|しんじん}の{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|聞|き}き<b>そびれた</b>まま、{f|歓迎会|かんげいかい}が{f|終|お}わった。
The welcome party ended without my ever finding the chance to ask the new starter's name.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|周|まわ}りがうるさくて、{f|集合|しゅうごう}{f|時間|じかん}を{f|聞|き}き<b>{f|損|そこ}ねた</b>。
It was so noisy around us that I failed to catch what time we were meeting.
</pre>

In the first, the question was never asked. In the second, the speaker was
listening and the information did not get through. The test is whether an
attempt took place.

### {f|書|か}き{f|損|そこ}なう vs {f|書|か}き{f|間違|まちが}える

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|間違|まちが}える</code> is about the **content**
being wrong. <code>{f|損|そこ}なう</code> is about the **execution** failing -
the thing came out spoiled.

</div>

<pre>
{f|日付|ひづけ}を{f|書|か}き<b>{f|間違|まちが}えて</b>、{f|来月|らいげつ}と{f|書|か}いてしまった。
I wrote the wrong date and put next month.
</pre>

<pre>
ペンがかすれて{f|署名|しょめい}を{f|書|か}き<b>{f|損|そこ}ない</b>、{f|用紙|ようし}をもらい{f|直|なお}した。
The pen skipped and ruined my signature, so I asked for a fresh form.
</pre>

A wrong date is correct writing of incorrect information; a smeared signature is
the right information written badly. Where English says "I made a mistake" for
both, Japanese separates them.

### ～{f|損|そこ}ねる vs ～{f|逃|のが}す

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|損|そこ}ねる</code>: your attempt failed.
<code>{f|逃|のが}す</code>: something slipped past you while you were there -
you overlooked it or let it go.

</div>

<pre>
{f|一瞬|いっしゅん}よそ{f|見|み}をして、{f|決勝|けっしょう}ゴールを{f|見|み}<b>{f|逃|のが}した</b>。
I looked away for a second and missed the winning goal.
</pre>

<pre>
ボールを{f|受|う}け{f|止|と}め<b>{f|損|そこ}ねて</b>、{f|後|うし}ろに{f|逸|そ}らしてしまった。
I failed to stop the ball and let it get past me.
</pre>

{f|逃|のが}す takes a chance or a sight that goes by -
{f|機会|きかい}を{f|逃|のが}す, {f|見逃|みのが}す, {f|聞|き}き{f|逃|のが}す -
and often implies inattention. {f|損|そこ}ねる implies effort that fell short.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|書|か}く<b>{f|損|そこ}なう</b> → ✅ {f|書|か}き<b>{f|損|そこ}なう</b>
  The base is the ます-stem, as with そびれる and かねる.

- ❌ {f|機嫌|きげん}を{f|損|そこ}な<b>て</b>しまった → ✅ {f|機嫌|きげん}を{f|損|そこ}な<b>って</b>しまった
  {f|損|そこ}なう is an う-verb, so its て-form is {f|損|そこ}なって.
  {f|損|そこ}ねる, a る-verb, gives {f|損|そこ}ねて - do not mix the two
  conjugations.

- ❌ {f|無理|むり}をして<b>{f|健康|けんこう}を{f|壊|こわ}した</b>。 → ✅ {f|無理|むり}をして<b>{f|健康|けんこう}を{f|損|そこ}なった</b>。
  {f|健康|けんこう} collocates with {f|損|そこ}なう. If you want
  {f|壊|こわ}す, the noun changes: {f|体|からだ}を{f|壊|こわ}した.

- ❌ {f|映画|えいが}の{f|最後|さいご}の{f|場面|ばめん}を<b>{f|見|み}{f|損|そこ}なった</b>。 → ✅ {f|映画|えいが}の{f|最後|さいご}の{f|場面|ばめん}を<b>{f|見逃|みのが}した</b>。
  A scene that went past while you were watching is
  {f|見逃|みのが}す. {f|見|み}{f|損|そこ}なう is heard mainly in its other
  sense, misjudging a person.

## Practice

**1.** バスの{f|中|なか}が{f|混|こ}んでいて、{f|降|お}りる{f|準備|じゅんび}が{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わず、{f|降|お}り____しまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|降|お}り<b>{f|損|そこ}ねて</b>しまった

The speaker was trying to get off and did not manage it in time, so the attempt
failed - {f|損|そこ}ねる. {f|降|お}り{f|損|そこ}なって would be equally correct
and sounds a shade more formal.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|言|い}い{f|方|かた}が{f|悪|わる}かったのか、{f|部長|ぶちょう}の{f|機嫌|きげん}を（{f|損|そこ}ねて・そびれて）しまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|部長|ぶちょう}の{f|機嫌|きげん}を<b>{f|損|そこ}ねて</b>しまった。

Only {f|損|そこ}ねる takes a noun with を and means "spoil, damage"; そびれる
attaches to a ます-stem and reports a missed opportunity, so it cannot take an
object at all. {f|機嫌|きげん}を{f|損|そこ}ねる is a fixed pairing worth
memorising.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Cutting corners on inspections risks damaging the trust we have built up."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|検査|けんさ}を{f|省略|しょうりゃく}することは、これまで{f|築|きず}いてきた{f|信頼|しんらい}を<b>{f|損|そこ}なう</b>おそれがある。

{f|信頼|しんらい} or {f|信用|しんよう} plus {f|損|そこ}なう is the standard
collocation for damaged trust, and
<code>～おそれがある</code> carries the "risks" of the English. The written
{f|損|そこ}なう suits this warning-notice register better than
{f|損|そこ}ねる.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|字|じ}を{f|書|か}く{f|損|そこ}なてしまったので、はじめから{f|書|か}き{f|直|なお}します。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|字|じ}を<b>{f|書|か}き{f|損|そこ}なって</b>しまったので、はじめから{f|書|か}き{f|直|なお}します。
"I made a mess of the writing, so I'll start again from the top."

Two repairs: the base is the ます-stem {f|書|か}き, not the dictionary form, and
{f|損|そこ}なう is an う-verb whose て-form is {f|損|そこ}なって.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|終電|しゅうでん}に{f|乗|の}り<b>{f|損|そこ}ねた</b>。
(b) {f|終電|しゅうでん}に{f|乗|の}り<b>そびれた</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) pictures the attempt: the speaker was on the platform, or running for it,
and the doors closed. Something was tried and it failed by a margin.

(b) pictures the evening: the speaker meant to catch it, the drinks went on, and
the moment to leave never arrived. Nothing was attempted at all. Both sentences
end with the speaker stranded, but they explain how differently, and a listener
will read the story behind them accordingly.

</details>
