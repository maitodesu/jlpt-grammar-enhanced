# ～まくる

**Meaning** · do it like crazy - over and over, in huge quantity, with no restraint at all. Loudly colloquial.

<code>まくる</code> is a live verb ({f|捲|まく}る, "to roll up, to turn over") that
has become a suffix meaning *and then some*. Attached to a verb stem it reports
that the action was done to excess: bought and bought, ate and ate, talked and
would not stop. It carries energy and a hint of no self-control, and it belongs to
casual speech and casual writing only. In this use it is written in kana.

## Formation

The stem is the ます-stem. <code>まくる</code> is a **う-verb** - まくった,
まくって, まくらない - and learners regularly conjugate it as though it were a
る-verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + まくる | {f|買|か}う → {f|買|か}いまくる |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + まくる | {f|食|た}べる → {f|食|た}べまくる |
| Verb (する) | し + まくる | {f|失敗|しっぱい}する → {f|失敗|しっぱい}しまくる |
| Past | ～まくった | {f|歌|うた}いまくった |
| て-form | ～まくって | {f|遊|あそ}びまくって |
| Negative | ～まくらない | {f|買|か}いまくらない |
| As a noun / predicate | ～まくり | {f|残業|ざんぎょう}する → {f|残業|ざんぎょう}しまくり |
| Not possible | uncontrollable states | ❌{f|分|わ}かりまくる |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the verb in front has to be a **deliberate action**. You can
{f|走|はし}りまくる, {f|書|か}きまくる or {f|笑|わら}いまくる, but not
❌{f|似|に}まくる or ❌{f|見|み}えまくる, because nobody controls resembling or
being visible. Jokey exceptions circulate online; do not build a rule on them.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|夏休|なつやす}みは{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|泳|およ}ぎ<b>まくった</b>。
I swam my head off every day of the summer holiday.
</pre>

<pre>
セールで{f|服|ふく}を{f|買|か}い<b>まくって</b>しまった。
I went completely overboard buying clothes in the sale.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}{f|前|まえ}は{f|過去問|かこもん}を{f|解|と}き<b>まくる</b>しかない。
Before the exam there's nothing for it but to hammer through past papers.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|会議|かいぎ}でしゃべり<b>まくる</b>から、いつも{f|時間|じかん}が{f|足|た}りなくなる。
He talks non-stop in meetings, so we always run out of time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新人|しんじん}のころは{f|失敗|しっぱい}し<b>まくって</b>、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|謝|あやま}っていた。
When I was new I messed up constantly and spent every day apologising.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|好|す}きなバンドの{f|曲|きょく}を{f|一日中|いちにちじゅう}{f|聞|き}き<b>まくって</b>いる。
My son has that band's songs on repeat all day long.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|夜|よる}はみんなでカラオケに{f|行|い}って、{f|終電|しゅうでん}まで{f|歌|うた}い<b>まくった</b>。
Last night we all went to karaoke and sang our lungs out until the last train.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|旅行|りょこう}{f|中|ちゅう}、{f|妹|いもうと}は{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|撮|と}り<b>まくって</b>いて、{f|気|き}づいたら{f|千枚|せんまい}を{f|超|こ}えていた。
My sister snapped away non-stop on the trip, and before we knew it she was past a thousand photos.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a **casual word and it sounds like one**. Its habitat is conversation
between friends, messages, social media, blogs, comedy, manga and variety
television. The polite form <code>～まくりました</code> exists and is grammatical,
but putting it in front of a superior is like saying "I smashed through the
paperwork" in a formal report - the politeness of ます does not cancel the
roughness of the word. In a work e-mail write
<code>{f|何度|なんど}も{f|確認|かくにん}しました</code> or
<code>{f|集中的|しゅうちゅうてき}に{f|進|すす}めました</code> instead.

The feeling it adds is **excess plus energy**. Very often the speaker is amused at
themselves - <code>{f|買|か}いまくった</code> is half a confession - or is
complaining with a grin about someone else who would not stop. It is not an insult,
and it is not vulgar; it is simply loud. It is used freely across ages and genders
in casual settings, though it leans younger and more urban, and it can sound
try-hard from a speaker who is otherwise being formal.

Two smaller points. <code>まくる</code> and
<code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code> both push an action to its limit, and they sit at
opposite ends of the register scale: if you want the dignified, written version of
"did all of it", the word is <code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code>. And
<code>～まくり</code>, the stem form, is used on its own as a predicate or a
label - <code>{f|今週|こんしゅう}は{f|残業|ざんぎょう}しまくりだ</code>,
<code>{f|勝|か}ちまくりのチーム</code> - which is even more colloquial than the
full verb.

## Contrast

### ～まくる vs ～{f|尽|つ}くす

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>まくる</code> counts how **wildly the doer went at it**
and is casual. <code>{f|尽|つ}くす</code> counts what was **used up** and is
formal and written.

</div>

<pre>
{f|棚|たな}のマンガを{f|読|よ}み<b>まくった</b>。
I plowed through the manga on that shelf.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|資料|しりょう}は{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}で{f|調|しら}べ<b>{f|尽|つ}くした</b>。
The materials were examined exhaustively within the week.
</pre>

The first could be said to a friend; the second could be written in a report.
Reversing them is jarring in both directions - a report does not say
{f|調|しら}べまくりました, and a chat about comics does not say
{f|読|よ}み{f|尽|つ}くした unless the speaker is being grand on purpose.

### ～まくる vs ～{f|続|つづ}ける

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～{f|続|つづ}ける</code> is neutral: the action went on.
<code>まくる</code> adds volume, disorder and an opinion about it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|三時間|さんじかん}{f|歩|ある}き<b>{f|続|つづ}けた</b>。
I kept walking for three hours.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|旅先|たびさき}で{f|歩|ある}き<b>まくった</b>ので、{f|足|あし}が{f|痛|いた}い。
I walked myself into the ground on that trip and now my feet hurt.
</pre>

<code>{f|続|つづ}ける</code> takes a measured duration comfortably;
<code>まくる</code> resists it, because it is not about a span of time but about
going at something without restraint. <code>{f|続|つづ}ける</code> is also
register-neutral and safe at work, which <code>まくる</code> is not.

### ～まくる vs ～てばかりいる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both say "does nothing but". <code>てばかりいる</code>
**disapproves**; <code>まくる</code> is usually **impressed or amused**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}はゲームをし<b>てばかりいる</b>。
My brother does nothing but play games. (and he should stop)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}はゲームをやり<b>まくって</b>いる。
My brother is gaming like a maniac.
</pre>

<code>てばかりいる</code> carries the sense that something else is being neglected,
so it is the natural choice for a parent's complaint. <code>まくる</code> just
reports the intensity; a speaker can use it about themselves with pleasure, which
<code>てばかりいる</code> cannot do.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|食|た}べる<b>まくる</b> → ✅ {f|食|た}べ<b>まくる</b>
  <code>まくる</code> joins to the ます-stem, like
  {f|食|た}べ{f|始|はじ}める.

- ❌ {f|遊|あそ}び<b>まくない</b> → ✅ {f|遊|あそ}び<b>まくらない</b>
  <code>まくる</code> is a う-verb. Its negative is まくらない, its past is
  まくった, its て-form is まくって.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|読|よ}み<b>まくりました</b>。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|資料|しりょう}にはひととおり{f|目|め}を{f|通|とお}しました。
  The ます ending does not make the word polite. Reporting to a superior needs
  neutral vocabulary.

- ❌ この{f|説明|せつめい}は{f|分|わ}かり<b>まくる</b>。 → ✅ この{f|説明|せつめい}はとても{f|分|わ}かりやすい。
  Understanding is not an action you perform repeatedly at will, so the suffix has
  nothing to attach to.

## Practice

**1.** {f|給料日|きゅうりょうび}のあと、ついネットで{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}を____しまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}を<b>しまくって</b>しまった

<code>する</code> becomes し before まくる, giving しまくる, and the て-form
しまくって joins to しまう for the note of regret. The whole sentence is casual,
which suits the word.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}には、「{f|各社|かくしゃ}の{f|事例|じれい}を（{f|調|しら}べまくりました・{f|調|しら}べ{f|尽|つ}くしました）」と{f|書|か}く。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|調|しら}べ<b>{f|尽|つ}くしました</b>

A written report needs the formal member of the pair.
<code>{f|調|しら}べまくりました</code> is grammatical but reads as if the writer
forgot which document they were in - the ます ending does not rescue a colloquial
verb.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "She ate non-stop at the party and regretted it the next day."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼女|かのじょ}はパーティーで{f|食|た}べ<b>まくって</b>、{f|次|つぎ}の{f|日|ひ}に{f|後悔|こうかい}した。

"Non-stop" with a note of excess and a rueful punchline is exactly what
<code>まくる</code> is for. The て-form links the two clauses; the register stays
casual throughout, which matches the situation.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|昨日|きのう}のライブで、ファンは{f|叫|さけ}ぶまくっていた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|昨日|きのう}のライブで、ファンは{f|叫|さけ}び<b>まくって</b>いた。
"At last night's gig the fans were screaming their heads off."

The join is to the ます-stem {f|叫|さけ}び, not the dictionary form
{f|叫|さけ}ぶ. This is the most common error with the pattern, because the
suffix looks like an independent verb.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|夏|なつ}は{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}み<b>まくった</b>。
(b) {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|夏|なつ}は{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}み<b>{f|続|つづ}けた</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is casual and enthusiastic: a big, disorderly pile of books, read at speed, and
the speaker is rather pleased about it. You would say this to a friend.

(b) is a flat statement that the reading went on. It could describe a calm, steady
habit, and it fits a written account or a conversation with someone you are not
close to. It also allows a stated duration
({f|三時間|さんじかん}{f|読|よ}み{f|続|つづ}けた) in a way that (a) does not.

</details>
