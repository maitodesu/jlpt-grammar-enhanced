# ～ないものでもない

**Meaning** · it isn't entirely out of the question; I suppose it could be done - a grudging yes, offered without enthusiasm and usually with a condition attached

Two negatives around <code>もの</code>: "it is not a thing that cannot be". What
survives the cancellation is a very small positive. The speaker is not agreeing, not
refusing, and not committing - they are conceding that the door is not locked. In
practice the sentence almost always goes on to say what would have to change first.

## Formation

The clause in front is **negative** and modifies <code>もの</code>, so it is in
noun-modifying shape. The verb is very often a potential.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | ない-form + ものでもない | {f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}ける → {f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}けないものでもない |
| Verb (potential) | ～ないものでもない | {f|行|い}ける → {f|行|い}けないものでもない |
| Verb (する) | しない + ものでもない | {f|検討|けんとう}する → {f|検討|けんとう}しないものでもない |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | <b>{f|来|こ}られない</b> + ものでもない | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}られないものでもない |
| い-adjective | ～くない + ものでもない | ない → ないものでもない |
| Polite | ～ないものでもありません | できない → できないものでもありません |
| Lighter, spoken | ～ないでもない ／ ～なくもない | {f|分|わ}かる → {f|分|わ}からないでもない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the pattern lives on **verbs**. Adjectives and nouns can be squeezed in,
but Japanese would rather use the lighter frames for them:
<code>おいしくなくもない</code>, <code>{f|嫌|きら}いでもない</code>. If you are
reaching for an adjective, reach for those instead.

</div>

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the ない **inside** is not optional and not decorative.
<code>{f|行|い}くものでもない</code> is a different pattern altogether, meaning "it
isn't the sort of thing one does".

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|一口|ひとくち}なら{f|食|た}べられ<b>ないものでもない</b>。
I could manage a mouthful, I suppose.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|急|いそ}げば{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わ<b>ないものでもない</b>。
If we get a move on, it isn't impossible that we make it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|言|い}い{f|分|ぶん}も{f|分|わ}から<b>ないものでもない</b>。
I can see his side of it, up to a point.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|条件|じょうけん}によっては、この{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}け<b>ないものでもない</b>。
Depending on the terms, I wouldn't necessarily turn the job down.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}をかければ{f|直|なお}せ<b>ないものでもない</b>が、{f|新|あたら}しく{f|買|か}ったほうが{f|安|やす}い。
Given time it could probably be repaired, but buying a new one would come cheaper.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}さえ{f|下|さ}げていただければ、{f|検討|けんとう}でき<b>ないものでもありません</b>。
If you were able to come down on price, it is not something we could not look at.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}でやれ<b>ないものでもない</b>が、{f|人手|ひとで}があるに{f|越|こ}したことはない。
I could do it on my own, but more hands would hardly hurt.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先方|せんぽう}が{f|条件|じょうけん}を{f|見直|みなお}してくるなら、{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いの{f|席|せき}に{f|着|つ}か<b>ないものでもない</b>。
If the other side revises its terms, we are not unwilling to sit down and talk.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Formal, adult, and deliberately unenthusiastic.** Its natural home is negotiation -
business meetings, letters between organisations, and the dialogue of a character in
a novel who is not going to say yes outright. It is not archaic and it is perfectly
sayable, but it is heavy, and among friends it sounds like a boardroom answer to a
simple question.

Three things it always carries:

| Feature | Consequence in the sentence |
|---|---|
| Reluctance | never used for something the speaker wants |
| A condition | ～なら, ～さえ～ば, {f|条件|じょうけん}によっては is usually present |
| A reservation to follow | a `が` clause very often comes next |

The reluctance is the part learners underestimate. Answering
<code>{f|手伝|てつだ}ってくれない？</code> with
<code>{f|手伝|てつだ}わないものでもないよ</code> is not a friendly yes. It says the
speaker will help if pressed and would rather not be - which is sometimes exactly
what you mean, and is a rude thing to say by accident.

Conversationally, the lighter cousins do the same job with a fraction of the weight.
<code>{f|分|わ}からないでもない</code> and <code>{f|分|わ}からなくもない</code> both
mean "I do sort of see it", and both are ordinary spoken Japanese. The `も` matters
in all of these: without it,
<code>できないものではない</code> is a genuine assertion that the thing *is*
possible, while <code>できないものでもない</code> merely declines to rule it out.

## Contrast

### ないものでもない vs ないことはない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both concede a small yes. `ないことはない` is **neutral and
conversational**; <code>ないものでもない</code> is **stiffer, more reluctant, and
expects a condition**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|海|うみ}なら{f|泳|およ}げ<b>ないことはない</b>よ。
I can swim well enough, if it's the sea.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|海|うみ}なら{f|泳|およ}げ<b>ないものでもない</b>が、{f|正直|しょうじき}{f|苦手|にがて}だ。
I could swim in the sea if I had to, though honestly I'd rather not.
</pre>

Said to a friend on a beach, the first is a normal answer and the second is a small
speech. The gap is register more than meaning, and it goes the other way in a
meeting: <code>ないことはありません</code> in a written reply to a client sounds
slightly light, where <code>ないものでもありません</code> sounds properly
non-committal.

### ないものでもない vs ないでもない・なくもない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the short forms are for **feelings and understanding**;
<code>ないものでもない</code> is for **actions and undertakings**.

</div>

<pre>
そう{f|言|い}いたくなる{f|気持|きも}ちは{f|分|わ}から<b>なくもない</b>。
I can see how you'd want to say that.
</pre>

<pre>
ご{f|事情|じじょう}が{f|事情|じじょう}ですので、{f|期限|きげん}を{f|延|の}ばせ<b>ないものでもありません</b>。
Given the circumstances, extending the deadline is not out of the question.
</pre>

<code>{f|分|わ}からなくもない</code> is a small sympathetic noise, and the heavier
pattern would be absurd in its place. Conversely, an extension of a deadline is
something an organisation grants or refuses, and there the weight is doing useful
work: it signals that the answer is not yet a yes.

### ないものでもない vs ないとも{f|限|かぎ}らない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ないものでもない</code> concedes what **could be done**.
<code>ないとも{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> warns of what **might happen**. Capacity
against contingency.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}なら{f|都合|つごう}をつけられ<b>ないものでもない</b>。
I could probably manage to make time next week.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}は{f|急|きゅう}な{f|出張|しゅっちょう}が{f|入|はい}ら<b>ないとも{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。
There's always a chance a trip comes up next week.
</pre>

Both are double negatives about next week, and they are doing opposite jobs. The
first is the speaker offering something, grudgingly. The second is the speaker
warning that the world may interfere. If the sentence is about what *you* will do,
it is this page's pattern; if it is about what may *befall* you, it is the other.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|手伝|てつだ}う<b>ものでもない</b>。 → ✅ {f|手伝|てつだ}わ<b>ないものでもない</b>。
  Without the inner ない you have written a different pattern - "helping isn't the
  sort of thing one does" - and told your colleague off instead of half-agreeing.

- ❌ ぜひ{f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}け<b>ないものでもありません</b>。 → ✅ ぜひ{f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}けさせてください。
  ぜひ is eagerness and the pattern is reluctance. The two cannot appear in the same
  sentence without sounding sarcastic.

- ❌ うん、{f|行|い}か<b>ないものでもないよ</b>。 → ✅ うん、{f|行|い}け<b>なくもないよ</b>。
  A friend asking whether you fancy coming along does not want a negotiating
  position. The light form carries the same hedge at the right weight.

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|難|むずか}しく<b>ないものでもない</b>から、{f|心配|しんぱい}いらないよ。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|難|むずか}しく<b>ない</b>から、{f|心配|しんぱい}いらないよ。
  Cancel the negatives and the original says the problem **is** difficult after all,
  which is no basis for telling somebody not to worry. Read your own sentence back
  with the negatives resolved before you commit to it.

## Practice

**1.** {f|納期|のうき}を{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}{f|延|の}ばしていただけるなら、お{f|受|う}けし____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お{f|受|う}けし<b>ないものでもありません</b>。

A condition is stated first and a grudging opening follows - the pattern's standard
shape in a business reply. The humble お{f|受|う}けする takes ない in the ordinary way
before <code>もの</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: その{f|気持|きも}ち、{f|分|わ}から（なくもない・ないものでもない）。ぼくも{f|同|おな}じ{f|経験|けいけん}をしたから。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|分|わ}から<b>なくもない</b>。

Sympathy between friends wants the light form. <code>ないものでもない</code> would
turn an expression of fellow feeling into a concession made across a table, and the
casual ぼく in the next clause makes the mismatch obvious.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, in the register of a business meeting: "If the schedule were reviewed, participation would not be out of the question for us."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|日程|にってい}を{f|見直|みなお}していただけるのであれば、{f|参加|さんか}でき<b>ないものでもありません</b>。

The condition comes first and the concession second, which is the order Japanese
prefers here. Note that "would not be out of the question" is the potential
<code>{f|参加|さんか}できない</code> inside the frame, not
<code>{f|参加|さんか}しない</code> - the speaker is talking about what is feasible,
not about what they intend.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|明日|あした}までにできるものでもないので、やってみます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|明日|あした}までにでき<b>ないものでもない</b>ので、やってみます。
"It isn't impossible by tomorrow, so I'll give it a go."

The inner ない has gone missing, and with it the meaning. As written, the sentence
says it is not the kind of thing one manages by tomorrow - and then volunteers to
manage it by tomorrow.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|一人|ひとり}でもでき<b>ます</b>。
(b) {f|一人|ひとり}でもでき<b>ないものでもありません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) accepts the job. The speaker says the work is within their reach and is offering
to do it, and a listener will stop worrying about it.

(b) accepts nothing. It says the work is not beyond reach, while making clear that
the speaker is not volunteering and would like the difficulty noticed. In a meeting
this is often a request for help disguised as an answer, and it is heard as one.

</details>
