# ～まみれ

**Meaning** · smeared all over with ～; covered in ～ - a substance clinging to the whole surface

`まみれ` comes from the verb {f|塗|まみ}れる, "to be smeared with", and it keeps that
verb's physical picture: mud, sweat, blood, oil, dust - something that **sticks to
the surface** of a person or a thing and coats it. It is heavier and more vivid than
`だらけ`, and it takes only substances, never counted-up flaws.

## Formation

`まみれ` attaches to a **bare noun** - no particle in front of it - and the result is
itself a **noun**. It predicates with だ/です, modifies a following noun with `の`,
and pairs constantly with なる.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (a substance) | Noun + まみれ | {f|泥|どろ} → {f|泥|どろ}まみれ |
| Predicate | ～まみれ + だ / です | {f|顔|かお}が{f|汗|あせ}まみれだ |
| Before a noun | ～まみれ + の + Noun | {f|油|あぶら}まみれの{f|手|て} |
| Becoming that way | ～まみれ + に + なる | ほこりまみれになる |
| Verb form | Noun + に + {f|塗|まみ}れる | {f|汗|あせ}にまみれて{f|働|はたら}く |
| Figurative | Noun (engulfing) + まみれ | {f|借金|しゃっきん}まみれ |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the base must be **stuff, not items**. {f|泥|どろ}・{f|汗|あせ}・{f|血|ち}・{f|油|あぶら}・{f|砂|すな}・ほこり are all natural.
Countable defects go to `だらけ` instead: {f|傷|きず}だらけ,
{f|間違|まちが}いだらけ, {f|人|ひと}だらけ - never ❌{f|間違|まちが}いまみれ.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the kanji spelling is {f|塗|まみ}れ, but the suffix is written in kana in
almost all modern text. The verb {f|塗|まみ}れる takes `に` for the substance, not
`を`: <code>{f|泥|どろ}にまみれる</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|転|ころ}んで、ズボンが<b>{f|泥|どろ}まみれ</b>になった。
I fell over and my trousers ended up smeared with mud.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|物置|ものおき}の{f|箱|はこ}はどれも<b>ほこりまみれ</b>だった。
Every one of the boxes in the shed was thick with dust.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}を{f|直|なお}していたら、{f|手|て}が<b>{f|油|あぶら}まみれ</b>になった。
I was fixing my bike and my hands got covered in oil.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|強|つよ}い{f|風|かぜ}の{f|中|なか}を{f|歩|ある}いてきたので、{f|髪|かみ}も{f|顔|かお}も<b>{f|砂|すな}まみれ</b>だ。
I walked here through a gale, so my hair and face are covered in sand.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|真夏|まなつ}の{f|工事|こうじ}{f|現場|げんば}で、{f|作業員|さぎょういん}は{f|皆|みな}<b>{f|汗|あせ}まみれ</b>だった。
On the building site in high summer, every one of the workers was drenched in sweat.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|映画|えいが}の{f|最後|さいご}の{f|場面|ばめん}で、{f|主人公|しゅじんこう}は<b>{f|血|ち}まみれ</b>のまま{f|立|た}ち{f|上|あ}がる。
In the final scene of the film, the hero gets to his feet still covered in blood.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|祖父|そふ}は{f|一生|いっしょう}、<b>{f|泥|どろ}にまみれて</b>この{f|田|た}を{f|守|まも}ってきた。
My grandfather spent his whole life in the mud, keeping these rice fields going.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会社|かいしゃ}はすでに<b>{f|借金|しゃっきん}まみれ</b>で、{f|再建|さいけん}はもう{f|難|むずか}しいと{f|言|い}われている。
The company is already drowning in debt, and people say it is past saving.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`まみれ` is vivid rather than formal, and it works in speech and writing alike -
though it is a shade more literary than `だらけ`, and a novel will reach for it where
a friend telling the same story might not. Its force comes from the picture: a whole
surface coated, unpleasantly and completely.

Usually that is a complaint, but not always, and the exception is worth knowing.
<code>{f|汗|あせ}にまみれて{f|働|はたら}く</code> - or
<code>{f|泥|どろ}にまみれて</code> - is a set image of honest, unglamorous labour, and
it is said with respect. That admiring use belongs to the **verb** form with `に`
much more than to the bare noun; <code>{f|汗|あせ}まみれだ</code> on its own is just a
description of being sweaty. `だらけ` has no equivalent warm use at all.

The figurative extension is limited and worth learning as vocabulary rather than
reasoning about: <code>{f|借金|しゃっきん}まみれ</code> is the common one, with
<code>{f|嘘|うそ}まみれ</code> and <code>{f|欲|よく}まみれ</code> turning up in
writing. The image is still "coated in it, and it will not come off", which is why
these all describe a discreditable condition rather than a quantity.

## Contrast

### ～まみれ vs ～だらけ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `まみれ` is a **substance smeared over a surface**. `だらけ` is
**a great many of something**, and the things can be counted.

</div>

<pre>
{f|提出|ていしゅつ}された{f|原稿|げんこう}は<b>{f|間違|まちが}いだらけ</b>だった。
The manuscript that came in was riddled with mistakes.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試合|しあい}が{f|終|お}わったとき、ユニフォームは<b>{f|泥|どろ}まみれ</b>だった。
By the end of the match, the uniform was caked in mud.
</pre>

Where the base is a genuine substance, both are often possible -
{f|泥|どろ}だらけ and {f|泥|どろ}まみれ are both good Japanese - and `まみれ` then
suggests a more even, thorough coating while `だらけ` suggests patches of it all
over. Where the base is countable, only `だらけ` works: ❌{f|傷|きず}まみれ,
❌{f|人|ひと}まみれ. And `だらけ` complains about quantity where `まみれ` complains
about the state of the surface.

### ～まみれ vs ～ずくめ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `まみれ` = coated in a substance, and unwelcome. `ずくめ` =
**everything, without exception, is X** - and it is happy to be good news.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今年|ことし}は{f|昇進|しょうしん}に{f|結婚|けっこん}に、<b>いいことずくめ</b>の{f|一年|いちねん}だった。
A promotion and a wedding - this year has been nothing but good news.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|草|くさ}を{f|刈|か}り{f|終|お}えたころには、{f|腕|うで}も{f|首|くび}も<b>{f|汗|あせ}まみれ</b>だった。
By the time the grass was cut, my arms and neck were dripping with sweat.
</pre>

The bases do not overlap at all: `ずくめ` takes abstractions and colours
(いいこと, {f|黒|くろ}, {f|規則|きそく}), `まみれ` takes physical stuff.
❌<code>いいことまみれ</code> and ❌<code>{f|泥|どろ}ずくめ</code> are both
impossible.

### ～まみれ vs ～{f|漬|づ}け

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `まみれ` coats the **outside**. <code>{f|漬|づ}け</code> is
**steeped in it**, immersed over a long stretch of time.

</div>

<pre>
{f|受験生|じゅけんせい}の{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|毎日|まいにち}<b>{f|勉強|べんきょう}{f|漬|づ}け</b>だ。
My son is sitting exams, and his days are nothing but study.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工場|こうじょう}から{f|戻|もど}ってきた{f|父|ちち}の{f|服|ふく}は<b>{f|油|あぶら}まみれ</b>だった。
My father's clothes were covered in oil when he got back from the plant.
</pre>

<code>{f|漬|づ}け</code> is the pickling verb {f|漬|つ}ける, and it keeps that sense
of being left to soak: <code>{f|薬|くすり}{f|漬|づ}け</code>,
<code>{f|借金|しゃっきん}{f|漬|づ}け</code>. Where both are possible - debt takes
either - `まみれ` pictures being covered in it and `漬け` pictures being submerged in
it for years.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|間違|まちが}い<b>まみれ</b>のレポート → ✅ {f|間違|まちが}い<b>だらけ</b>のレポート
  Mistakes are counted, not smeared. `まみれ` needs a substance.

- ❌ {f|泥|どろ}<b>まみれな</b>{f|靴|くつ} → ✅ {f|泥|どろ}<b>まみれの</b>{f|靴|くつ}
  `まみれ` produces a noun, and nouns modify with `の`. It is never a な-adjective.

- ❌ {f|汗|あせ}<b>をまみれて</b>{f|走|はし}った。 → ✅ {f|汗|あせ}<b>にまみれて</b>{f|走|はし}った。
  The verb {f|塗|まみ}れる is intransitive and marks the substance with `に`.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}は<b>いいことまみれ</b>だった。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}は<b>いいことずくめ</b>だった。
  `まみれ` cannot take an abstract noun, and it cannot report good news. For "nothing
  but good things", the word is `ずくめ`.

## Practice

**1.** {f|雨|あめ}の{f|中|なか}で{f|試合|しあい}をしたので、ユニフォームが____になった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|泥|どろ}まみれ</b>

Mud in the rain coats the whole kit, which is the core `まみれ` image, and the
change of state takes <code>になる</code>. {f|泥|どろ}だらけ would also pass here;
`まみれ` suggests the more thorough, evenly smeared version.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|中古|ちゅうこ}の{f|机|つくえ}は{f|安|やす}いが、{f|表面|ひょうめん}が{f|傷|きず}（だらけ・まみれ）だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|表面|ひょうめん}が{f|傷|きず}<b>だらけ</b>だ。

Scratches are countable marks, not a substance coating the surface, so `だらけ` is
the only option. ❌{f|傷|きず}まみれ is one of the commonest errors with this pair.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He came back from the garden with his hands covered in soil."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}は{f|手|て}を<b>{f|土|つち}まみれ</b>にして{f|庭|にわ}から{f|戻|もど}ってきた。

Soil on the hands is a substance on a surface. Note the transitive framing
<code>～を…まみれにする</code> alongside the intransitive
<code>～が…まみれになる</code>; either is fine here.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|長|なが}い{f|間|あいだ}{f|使|つか}っていなかったので、{f|棚|たな}の{f|上|うえ}はほこりまみれな{f|状態|じょうたい}だった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|長|なが}い{f|間|あいだ}{f|使|つか}っていなかったので、{f|棚|たな}の{f|上|うえ}はほこりまみれ<b>の</b>{f|状態|じょうたい}だった。

`まみれ` gives a noun, so it links to the following noun with `の`. The `な` comes
from treating it as a な-adjective, which it is not.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|汗|あせ}まみれ</b>だった。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|汗|あせ}にまみれて</b>{f|働|はたら}いていた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply describes his condition: he was drenched, and depending on context that
could be unpleasant - someone you would rather not sit next to on the train.

(b) is admiring. The `に` verb form followed by a verb of doing is a set image of
honest physical labour, and the sentence praises his effort rather than reporting
his state. Almost all the warm uses of this word take that shape.

</details>
