# ～まじき

**Meaning** · unbecoming; that must never be done - almost always in the frame "conduct unworthy of a [role]"

<code>まじき</code> is the noun-modifying form of `まじ`, the classical auxiliary
that negates `べし`: where `べし` said "ought to", `まじ` said "ought not to, surely
will not". Modern Japanese has kept exactly one construction alive -
<code>N + にあるまじき + noun</code>, "conduct unbecoming a doctor / a
teacher / a public servant". Recognise the frame; say
<code>{f|教師|きょうし}として{f|許|ゆる}されない{f|行為|こうい}</code> yourself.

## Formation

In practice only one verb appears in front of <code>まじき</code>, and that verb is
`ある`. The pattern is a frame with a slot, not a productive ending.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| The living frame | Noun + にあるまじき + noun | {f|教師|きょうし} → {f|教師|きょうし}にあるまじき{f|行為|こうい} |
| Same frame, として | Noun + としてあるまじき + noun | {f|社会人|しゃかいじん} → {f|社会人|しゃかいじん}としてあるまじき{f|発言|はつげん} |
| No preceding noun | あるまじき + こと / もの | あるまじきことが{f|起|お}きた |
| Other verbs (rare, literary) | dictionary form + まじき + noun | {f|許|ゆる}す → {f|許|ゆる}すまじき{f|罪|つみ} |
| Sentence-final classical form | ～まじ | {f|許|ゆる}すまじ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the nouns that fill the first slot are almost all **roles**:
{f|教師|きょうし}, {f|医師|いし}, {f|警察官|けいさつかん}, {f|議員|ぎいん},
{f|政治家|せいじか}, {f|社会人|しゃかいじん}, {f|指導者|しどうしゃ}, プロ. The nouns
that fill the second slot are a similarly short list:
{f|行為|こうい}, {f|発言|はつげん}, {f|言動|げんどう}, {f|態度|たいど},
{f|振|ふ}る{f|舞|ま}い, こと. Learning those two lists is more useful than learning
a rule.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
それは{f|教師|きょうし}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|行為|こうい}だ。
That is conduct unbecoming a teacher.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|警察官|けいさつかん}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|振|ふ}る{f|舞|ま}いだった。
It was behaviour unworthy of a police officer.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|社会人|しゃかいじん}として<b>あるまじき</b>{f|発言|はつげん}だと{f|批判|ひはん}された。
He was criticised for a remark unbecoming a working adult.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}は、これを{f|医師|いし}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|判断|はんだん}であったと{f|指摘|してき}している。
The report identifies this as a decision unworthy of a physician.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|見出|みだ}しには「{f|議員|ぎいん}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|言動|げんどう}」と{f|大|おお}きく{f|出|で}ていた。
The headline ran, in large type, "Conduct unbecoming a member of the assembly".
</pre>

<pre>
{f|起|お}きてはならないこと、いや、<b>あるまじき</b>ことが{f|起|お}きてしまった。
Something that should not have happened - that could not be allowed to happen - has happened.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|相手|あいて}を{f|侮|あなど}るような{f|態度|たいど}は、{f|勝負|しょうぶ}の{f|場|ば}に{f|立|た}つ{f|者|もの}に<b>あるまじき</b>ものである。
An attitude that belittles one's opponent is unworthy of anyone who steps into a contest.
</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

**You will never say this.** <code>あるまじき</code> is the language of press
statements, disciplinary notices, newspaper headlines and formal apologies. It is
how an institution condemns one of its own in public. In conversation it does not
occur, and a learner who tries it will produce something that sounds like a
subtitle. The sentence you actually want is
<code>{f|先生|せんせい}としてどうかと{f|思|おも}います</code> or
<code>{f|教師|きょうし}のすることではない</code>.

Two consequences of that register. First, the pattern is **heavy**: it accuses
someone of failing at their office, not merely of behaving badly. Applying it to a
small lapse is not a small overstatement, it is a category error -
<code>{f|遅刻|ちこく}は{f|学生|がくせい}にあるまじき{f|行為|こうい}だ</code>
reads as a joke about a headmaster. Second, it is almost never used **to** the
person. It is used *about* them, to a third party or to the public.

<code>まじき</code> is a {f|連体形|れんたいけい}, a noun-modifying form,
so a noun has to follow. There is no ❌<code>あるまじきだ</code> and no
❌<code>あるまじきです</code>; if you have nothing concrete to put after it, the
generic こと or もの fills the slot.

The bare classical ending <code>まじ</code> does still surface, but only in slogans
and titles - the shape of {f|許|ゆる}すまじ, "let this never be permitted", on a
banner or a memorial. Treat it as something you read, once, and understand.

## Contrast

### ～にあるまじき vs ～べきではない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>べきではない</code> judges the **action**:
this should not be done. <code>にあるまじき</code> judges the action **against a
role**: someone in that position has disgraced it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|生徒|せいと}の{f|前|まえ}でそんな{f|言|い}い{f|方|かた}をする<b>べきではない</b>。
You shouldn't speak like that in front of the pupils.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|生徒|せいと}の{f|前|まえ}でのその{f|発言|はつげん}は、{f|教師|きょうし}に<b>あるまじき</b>ものだ。
That remark, made in front of pupils, is unbecoming a teacher.
</pre>

The first can be said privately, by a colleague, over coffee. The second is a
verdict; it names the office and finds the person unworthy of it, and it therefore
belongs in a written report or a public statement. If the person you are talking
about has no relevant role, <code>あるまじき</code> is simply unavailable -
❌<code>{f|人|ひと}にあるまじき</code> does not work the way "inhuman" does in
English.

### ～にあるまじき vs ～らしくない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `らしくない` says the behaviour is **out of character**.
<code>にあるまじき</code> says it is **a disgrace to the office**. One is mild
surprise; the other is condemnation.

</div>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}に{f|遅|おく}れるなんて、{f|彼|かれ}<b>らしくない</b>ね。
Turning up late - that's not like him at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|記録|きろく}を{f|書|か}き{f|換|か}えたことは、{f|研究者|けんきゅうしゃ}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|行為|こうい}である。
Altering the records is an act unworthy of a researcher.
</pre>

`らしくない` is everyday spoken Japanese and can even be affectionate. It also
attaches happily to a person's name, which <code>あるまじき</code> cannot:
❌<code>{f|田中|たなか}さんにあるまじき</code> is wrong, because the slot wants a
**role**, not an individual. Say
{f|教師|きょうし}にあるまじき or {f|上司|じょうし}にあるまじき.

### ～にあるまじき vs ～てはならない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>てはならない</code> is a **prohibition** aimed forward:
do not do this. <code>にあるまじき</code> is a **judgement** aimed backward: this
has been done, and it was a disgrace.

</div>

<pre>
{f|個人|こじん}{f|情報|じょうほう}を{f|外部|がいぶ}に{f|出|だ}し<b>てはならない</b>。
Personal data must not be released outside the organisation.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|個人|こじん}{f|情報|じょうほう}を{f|外部|がいぶ}に{f|出|だ}したのは、{f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|行為|こうい}だった。
Releasing personal data was an act unworthy of the officer responsible.
</pre>

`てはならない` is live formal Japanese: it appears in rules, contracts and
regulations, and you should be able to write it. <code>あるまじき</code> only ever
comments on what has already occurred. This is why the two so often appear in the
same document, one in the rulebook and the other in the statement announcing that
the rule was broken.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ その{f|発言|はつげん}は{f|教師|きょうし}に<b>あるまじきだ</b>。 → ✅ その{f|発言|はつげん}は{f|教師|きょうし}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|発言|はつげん}だ。／ ✅ ～に<b>あるまじき</b>ものだ。
  <code>まじき</code> modifies a noun and cannot end a sentence. When no specific
  noun is available, use こと or もの.

- ❌ {f|田中|たなか}さんに<b>あるまじき</b>{f|行為|こうい} → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|行為|こうい}
  The slot before に takes a role or a status, not a personal name. The whole force
  of the pattern comes from measuring a person against their office.

- ❌ {f|遅刻|ちこく}するのは{f|学生|がくせい}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|行為|こうい}だ。 → ✅ {f|遅刻|ちこく}は{f|学生|がくせい}としてよくない。
  Not ungrammatical, but wildly out of proportion. Reserve
  <code>あるまじき</code> for things that would be reported.

- ❌ {f|教師|きょうし}に<b>あるまじく</b>{f|行動|こうどう}した → ✅ {f|教師|きょうし}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|行動|こうどう}をした
  `まじ` has no living adverbial form in modern Japanese. The only shape you will
  meet is <code>まじき</code> in front of a noun.

- ❌（{f|本人|ほんにん}に{f|向|む}かって）それは{f|社会人|しゃかいじん}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|態度|たいど}ですよ。 → ✅ それは{f|社会人|しゃかいじん}として{f|恥|は}ずかしい{f|態度|たいど}ですよ。
  Said to someone's face, the pattern is not stern but stagy. Japanese delivers
  this criticism in the third person, in writing.

## Practice

**1.** {f|新聞|しんぶん}の{f|見出|みだ}し: 「{f|警察官|けいさつかん}に____{f|行為|こうい}、{f|署長|しょちょう}が{f|謝罪|しゃざい}」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|警察官|けいさつかん}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|行為|こうい}

The frame is Noun + にあるまじき + noun, and a headline is exactly where it lives.
Note that the verb in the frame is always ある - you are not choosing a verb, you
are filling two noun slots.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|今日|きょう}の{f|田中|たなか}さん、{f|全然|ぜんぜん}{f|話|はな}さないね。（{f|田中|たなか}さんにあるまじきね・{f|田中|たなか}さんらしくないね）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|田中|たなか}さん<b>らしくないね</b>。

Two reasons. The slot before <code>にあるまじき</code> wants a role, not a
person's name; and the situation is a friend noticing something mildly unusual,
which is `らしくない` territory. <code>あるまじき</code> would turn a quiet
afternoon into a disciplinary hearing.

</details>

**3.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|指導者|しどうしゃ}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|発言|はつげん}である。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|指導者|しどうしゃ}として{f|言|い}ってはいけないことを{f|言|い}った。
Or, a little more formally: {f|指導者|しどうしゃ}として{f|許|ゆる}されない{f|発言|はつげん}だ。

<code>あるまじき</code> unpacks into "as a leader" + "must not". Note that the
modern version needs a verb, because the classical form was doing the work of a
whole clause in one word.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: あの{f|判断|はんだん}は{f|専門家|せんもんか}にあるまじきでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ あの{f|判断|はんだん}は{f|専門家|せんもんか}に<b>あるまじき</b>ものでした。
Or: あの{f|判断|はんだん}は{f|専門家|せんもんか}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|判断|はんだん}でした。

<code>まじき</code> must be followed by a noun; it cannot take です or でした
directly. The generic もの is the standard repair when no specific noun suggests
itself.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|上司|じょうし}として{f|不適切|ふてきせつ}な{f|発言|はつげん}だったと{f|思|おも}います。
(b) {f|上司|じょうし}に<b>あるまじき</b>{f|発言|はつげん}である。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a real sentence a real person could say, in a meeting or in a written
comment. It is critical but leaves room: {f|不適切|ふてきせつ} is a measured word,
and と{f|思|おも}います marks it as the speaker's view.

(b) is a public verdict. である rather than です already tells you it is written
rather than spoken, and <code>あるまじき</code> removes the speaker altogether:
nobody is offering an opinion, the office itself is said to have been disgraced.
You would meet (b) in a newspaper or an official statement, and you would not hear
it in a room.

</details>
