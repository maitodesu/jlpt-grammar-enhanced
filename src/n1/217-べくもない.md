# ～べくもない

**Meaning** · there is no possibility of doing it; cannot possibly - the thing is ruled out by the situation, not merely difficult

<code>べくもない</code> is <code>べく</code> (the adverbial form of classical `べし`)
+ `も` + `ない`: "there is not even the *possibility* of doing it". It is a written,
literary way of closing a door, and it attaches to a short list of verbs about
knowing, hoping, matching and disputing. Read it as
<code>{f|知|し}りようがない</code> or <code>{f|望|のぞ}めない</code>; write those
instead.

## Formation

Like every member of the `べし` family, it follows the **dictionary form**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | dictionary form + べくもない | {f|知|し}る → {f|知|し}るべくもない |
| Verb (る-verb) | dictionary form + べくもない | {f|比|くら}べる → {f|比|くら}べるべくもない |
| Verb (する) | すべくもない / するべくもない | {f|想像|そうぞう}する → {f|想像|そうぞう}すべくもない |
| Past | ～べくもなかった | {f|望|のぞ}む → {f|望|のぞ}むべくもなかった |
| Polite | ～べくもありません | {f|及|およ}ぶ → {f|及|およ}ぶべくもありません |
| Classical variant of the verb | {f|比|くら}ぶ + べくもない | {f|比|くら}ぶべくもない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the verb slot is not free. In practice you will meet perhaps six verbs
there: {f|知|し}る, {f|望|のぞ}む, {f|及|およ}ぶ, {f|比|くら}べる,
{f|疑|うたが}う, {f|争|あらそ}う. Anything else - ❌{f|食|た}べるべくもない,
❌{f|行|い}くべくもない - is not a stylistic slip but a phrase that does not exist.
Learn the collocations, not a rule.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|当時|とうじ}の{f|事情|じじょう}は、もはや{f|知|し}る<b>べくもない</b>。
The circumstances of the time are now beyond any possibility of knowing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|勝利|しょうり}など{f|望|のぞ}む<b>べくもなかった</b>。
Victory had never been anything one could hope for.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|素人|しろうと}の{f|腕|うで}では、{f|職人|しょくにん}の{f|技|わざ}に{f|及|およ}ぶ<b>べくもない</b>。
An amateur's hand cannot begin to approach a craftsman's skill.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|両者|りょうしゃ}の{f|規模|きぼ}は{f|比|くら}べる<b>べくもない</b>。
The two are not remotely comparable in scale.
</pre>

<pre>
これだけの{f|記録|きろく}が{f|残|のこ}っている{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|事実|じじつ}は{f|疑|うたが}う<b>べくもない</b>。
With this much on record, the facts are beyond any possibility of doubt.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|山|やま}あいの{f|小|ちい}さな{f|工場|こうじょう}が、{f|海外|かいがい}の{f|大手|おおて}と{f|価格|かかく}で{f|争|あらそ}う<b>べくもない</b>。
A small workshop in the hills cannot possibly compete on price with the overseas giants.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|焼|や}け{f|落|お}ちた{f|屋敷|やしき}の{f|中|なか}から、{f|母|はは}の{f|手紙|てがみ}を{f|見|み}つけ{f|出|だ}す<b>べくもなかった</b>。
There was no hope of finding his mother's letters among the ruins of the burnt house.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|当時|とうじ}の{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}では、これほどの{f|精度|せいど}は{f|実現|じつげん}す<b>べくもありませんでした</b>。
With the technology of the day, precision on this scale was simply out of the question.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**You will never say this.** <code>べくもない</code> is a literary and formal
written form: essays, histories, novels, editorials, the occasional written
apology. In conversation it does not occur, and there is no casual version of it.
When you want to say the same thing out loud, Japanese gives you
<code>{f|知|し}りようがない</code>, <code>{f|知|し}るはずがない</code>,
<code>とても{f|望|のぞ}めない</code>, <code>{f|比|くら}べものにならない</code>.

The feeling it carries is **resignation**, not frustration. It says the possibility
was never on the table - because the evidence is gone, because the gap in scale is
too wide, because time has passed. That is why it so often has もはや or もう or
{f|今|いま}となっては in front of it, and why it fits historical writing so
naturally. It does *not* express refusal or unwillingness; it is not the classical
version of ❌"I won't".

There is no affirmative <code>べくもある</code>, and no question form. The
expression exists only in this negative shape. It also cannot take a person as the
one being denied permission - it is about possibility, not permission, so
<code>{f|入|はい}るべくもない</code> would mean "there is no way in", never "you may
not enter". That last sense is <code>{f|入|はい}るべからず</code>, the other
negative of the same classical word.

## Contrast

### ～べくもない vs ～ようがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both say "there is no way to". `ようがない` is ordinary modern
Japanese and can be spoken; <code>べくもない</code> is its literary counterpart and
takes only a handful of verbs.

</div>

<pre>
{f|連絡先|れんらくさき}を{f|聞|き}いていないので、{f|知|し}らせ<b>ようがない</b>。
I didn't get their contact details, so there's no way to let them know.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|当時|とうじ}の{f|人|ひと}が{f|何|なに}を{f|思|おも}っていたかは、{f|知|し}る<b>べくもない</b>。
What the people of that time were thinking is beyond our knowing.
</pre>

`ようがない` attaches to the ます-stem ({f|知|し}らせ<b>ようがない</b>) and takes any
verb at all. <code>べくもない</code> attaches to the dictionary form and takes
almost none. If you are unsure whether the verb you want is allowed with
<code>べくもない</code>, that uncertainty is itself the answer: use `ようがない`.

### ～べくもない vs ～はずがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `はずがない` is a **judgement about likelihood**: given what I
know, that can't be so. <code>べくもない</code> is a statement that the
**possibility does not exist** in the world.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}がそんなうそをつく<b>はずがない</b>。
There's no way he would tell a lie like that.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|記録|きろく}が{f|失|うしな}われた{f|今|いま}、{f|正確|せいかく}な{f|数字|すうじ}は{f|知|し}る<b>べくもない</b>。
Now that the records are lost, the exact figures are beyond recovery.
</pre>

`はずがない` is about the speaker's reasoning and is used constantly in speech.
<code>べくもない</code> makes no claim about the speaker at all: the door is shut by
circumstances. This is also why `はずがない` can be wrong (the person did lie after
all) in a way that <code>べくもない</code> cannot.

### ～べくもない vs ～{f|得|え}ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are formal negatives of possibility.
<code>～{f|得|え}ない</code> is **productive** - it attaches to almost any
ます-stem and you can use it in a report. <code>べくもない</code> is a closed set of
frozen phrases.

</div>

<pre>
そのような{f|結論|けつろん}は{f|受|う}け{f|入|い}れ<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>。
Such a conclusion is not one we can accept.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|両者|りょうしゃ}の{f|実力|じつりょく}は{f|比|くら}べる<b>べくもない</b>。
There is no comparing the ability of the two.
</pre>

Note the different bases: <code>{f|得|え}ない</code> follows the ます-stem
({f|受|う}け{f|入|い}れ), <code>べくもない</code> the dictionary form. Of the two,
<code>{f|得|え}ない</code> is the one to learn actively - it appears in modern
business and academic writing and nobody would call it archaic. Reserve
<code>べくもない</code> for reading.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|知|し}ら<b>べくもない</b> → ✅ {f|知|し}る<b>べくもない</b>
  Everything in the `べし` family attaches to the dictionary form. The ない-stem
  looks right because the meaning is negative, but the negation lives in the
  `ない` at the end.

- ❌ {f|想像|そうぞう}<b>しべくもない</b> → ✅ {f|想像|そうぞう}<b>すべくもない</b>
  `する` becomes す (or, less commonly, stays する) before <code>べく</code>. There
  is no し form here.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>べくもない</b>。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>はずがない</b>。
  The pattern is not a way of predicting. It belongs to knowing, hoping, matching
  and disputing, and a weather forecast is none of those.

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|解決|かいけつ}す<b>べくもある</b>。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|解決|かいけつ}できる。
  There is no affirmative. The expression exists only as
  <code>べくもない</code> / <code>べくもなかった</code>.

- ❌（{f|友人|ゆうじん}に）その{f|店|みせ}の{f|場所|ばしょ}は{f|知|し}る<b>べくもないよ</b>。 → ✅ その{f|店|みせ}、どこにあるか{f|分|わ}からないよ。
  The よ at the end and the classical ending cannot occupy the same sentence. In
  speech, use {f|分|わ}からない or {f|知|し}りようがない.

## Practice

**1.** {f|随筆|ずいひつ}の{f|一節|いっせつ}: {f|祖父|そふ}が{f|何|なに}を{f|考|かんが}えてこの{f|家|いえ}を{f|建|た}てたのか、{f|今|いま}となっては____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|知|し}る<b>べくもない</b>

{f|今|いま}となっては ("now, at this remove") is the standard lead-in for this
pattern, and {f|知|し}る is the verb it most often takes. The whole sentence is
written in the reflective register of an essay - which is exactly where
<code>べくもない</code> lives.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|会員|かいいん}カードを{f|忘|わす}れたので、{f|割引|わりびき}を{f|受|う}け（べくもない・ようがない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|割引|わりびき}を{f|受|う}け<b>ようがない</b>。

Two reasons. The base is wrong for <code>べくもない</code>, which needs the
dictionary form {f|受|う}ける; and more importantly this is an everyday situation at
a shop counter, where `ようがない` is the natural form and
<code>べくもない</code> would be absurd.

</details>

**3.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: あの{f|二|ふた}つのチームの{f|力|ちから}は{f|比|くら}べる<b>べくもない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

あの{f|二|ふた}つのチームの{f|力|ちから}は{f|比|くら}べものにならない。
Or, more plainly still: あの{f|二|ふた}つのチームでは、{f|力|ちから}が{f|全然|ぜんぜん}{f|違|ちが}う。

<code>{f|比|くら}べものにならない</code> is the everyday idiom that carries exactly
this meaning and can be said out loud. Note the reading {f|二|ふた}つ - the native
numeral, not ❌につ.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|昔|むかし}のことなので、{f|正確|せいかく}な{f|日付|ひづけ}は{f|知|し}らべくもありません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|昔|むかし}のことなので、{f|正確|せいかく}な{f|日付|ひづけ}は{f|知|し}る<b>べくもありません</b>。

The base must be the dictionary form {f|知|し}る. The ない-stem {f|知|し}ら is what
`ない` and `ず` take, not what <code>べく</code> takes. Note the reading
{f|日付|ひづけ} - rendaku turns つけ into づけ after the noun.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|真相|しんそう}は{f|知|し}る<b>べくもない</b>。
(b) {f|真相|しんそう}は{f|知|し}りたくない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is about possibility, and the speaker has no say in it. The evidence is gone,
the witnesses are dead, the door is shut - and the tone is one of resignation
rather than complaint. It reads as a line from an essay or a history.

(b) is about desire: the speaker could find out and is choosing not to. That is a
completely different sentence, and it is ordinary spoken Japanese. Learners
sometimes reach for (a) intending (b), because both come out as "I can't know" in a
rough translation.

</details>
