# いかにも

**Meaning** · exactly as you would expect; the very picture of

`いかにも` says that something matches the mental image of its type so closely that
it is almost a caricature. A shop that is *so* Showa. An excuse that is *so* him.
The word carries that "so" - and the small smile that comes with it.

## Usage

`いかにも` is an adverb and comes first in the phrase it colours. It is nearly
always followed by a marker of resemblance or appearance:

- <code>いかにも～らしい</code>: <code>いかにも{f|彼|かれ}らしい{f|答|こた}え</code>
- <code>いかにも～そうだ／そうな</code>: <code>いかにも{f|高|たか}そうな{f|車|くるま}</code>
- <code>いかにも～という／といった</code>: <code>いかにも{f|職人|しょくにん}という{f|手|て}</code>
- directly before a descriptive noun phrase:
  <code>いかにも{f|日本|にほん}{f|的|てき}な{f|風景|ふうけい}</code>

The judgement is about **type membership**, not about intensity. That is why
❌<code>いかにもおいしい</code> is wrong where
<code>いかにもおいしそうだ</code> is right: the word comments on how something
presents itself, not on how it actually is.

There is a second, archaic use in which `いかにも` stands alone meaning "indeed,
quite so" - the reply of a retainer in a period drama. Recognise it; do not deploy
it. In a meeting the modern equivalent is
<code>おっしゃるとおりです</code>.

## Nuance & register

Usable in speech, unlike most of the adverbs around it in this chapter. It is
mildly evaluative and a shade bookish, but nothing about
<code>いかにも{f|高|たか}そう</code> or <code>いかにも{f|彼|かれ}らしい</code> would
raise an eyebrow in conversation. You will meet it constantly in reviews, travel
writing and character description.

The edge to watch is the caricature. Saying
<code>いかにも{f|先生|せんせい}らしい{f|人|ひと}</code> can be affectionate - "every
inch the teacher" - or dismissive - "a schoolteacher type" - and which one lands
depends on tone and context. Applied to a person's clothes, accent or manner it can
easily read as mockery, so it is safer aimed at things and scenes than at people
you have just met.

Note also that `いかにも` frequently means the appearance is *only* an appearance,
especially with <code>そうな</code>: <code>いかにも{f|強|つよ}そうな{f|犬|いぬ}</code>
is a dog that looks tough, with no promise that it is. That reading is one small
step from the doubting <code>さも</code>, and the two words meet there.

## Contrast

### いかにも vs さも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `いかにも` says the thing is a perfect specimen of its type.
<code>さも</code> says the person put on a display. `いかにも` describes; `さも`
suspects.

</div>

<pre>
<b>いかにも</b>{f|真面目|まじめ}そうな{f|学生|がくせい}が{f|前|まえ}の{f|席|せき}に{f|座|すわ}った。
A student who looked the very picture of diligence sat down in the front row.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は<b>さも</b>{f|真面目|まじめ}そうに{f|話|はな}を{f|聞|き}いていた。
He listened with every show of taking it seriously.
</pre>

The first sentence is about a type and how well the student fits it; the second is
about a performance being given to an audience. `いかにも` also modifies nouns
freely, while <code>さも</code> wants a manner and a verb -
<code>さも{f|真面目|まじめ}そうな{f|学生|がくせい}</code> is possible but reads as an
accusation.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|料理|りょうり}はいかにもおいしい。 → ✅ この{f|料理|りょうり}はいかにもおいし<b>そうだ</b>。
  The adverb comments on how something looks or seems. To say the food really is
  delicious, use <code>{f|本当|ほんとう}に</code>.

- ❌ 「いかにも。」（agreeing in a meeting）→ ✅ <b>おっしゃるとおりです。</b>
  The standalone "indeed" is period-drama Japanese. In a modern office it will get
  a laugh, which may not be the one you wanted.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}はいかにも{f|平気|へいき}なふりをして{f|嘘|うそ}をついた。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}は<b>さも</b>{f|平気|へいき}なように{f|嘘|うそ}をついた。
  When the point is that somebody is faking a manner, the word is
  <code>さも</code>. `いかにも` would put the emphasis on how convincingly typical
  he looked, which is not the accusation intended.

## Practice

**1.** {f|窓|まど}から{f|海|うみ}が{f|見|み}えて、____{f|夏|なつ}の{f|旅行|りょこう}____{f|部屋|へや}だった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|窓|まど}から{f|海|うみ}が{f|見|み}えて、<b>いかにも</b>{f|夏|なつ}の{f|旅行|りょこう}<b>らしい</b>{f|部屋|へや}だった。

The two blanks are one frame: the adverb opens the "perfect specimen" judgement and
<code>らしい</code> attaches it to the noun. Without the ending the sentence has no
resemblance marker for `いかにも` to strengthen.

</details>

**2.** Choose いかにも or さも, and say why:
{f|彼|かれ}は____{f|自分|じぶん}が{f|考|かんが}えたかのように、{f|私|わたし}の{f|案|あん}を{f|説明|せつめい}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>さも</b>{f|自分|じぶん}が{f|考|かんが}えたかのように{f|説明|せつめい}した。

The sentence accuses him of passing off someone else's idea, and that reading of a
put-on display is <code>さも</code>'s job. The
<code>かのように</code> ending is another signal: it is the frame
<code>さも</code> keeps company with.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) いかにも{f|元気|げんき}そうな{f|子|こ}だ。
(b) {f|元気|げんき}な{f|子|こ}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (b) simply states that the child is lively. Sentence (a) reports the
impression the child gives and measures it against a type - this is what a healthy,
lively child looks like - and it leaves a small gap between the look and the fact,
which is why it can be said of a child you have only just glimpsed.

</details>
