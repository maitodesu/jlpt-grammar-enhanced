# ～を境に

**Meaning** · with that as the dividing line, things on one side are not like things on the other

{f|境|さかい} is a border - the word in {f|国境|こっきょう} and
{f|境目|さかいめ}. <code>を{f|境|さかい}に</code> draws that border across time (or,
less often, across a map) and asserts a contrast: before this point, one state of
affairs; after it, a different one. The pattern's whole job is the **before and
after**, so a sentence that does not change anything has no use for it.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (event) | noun + を{f|境|さかい}に | {f|卒業|そつぎょう} → {f|卒業|そつぎょう}を{f|境|さかい}に |
| Noun (point in time) | noun + を{f|境|さかい}に | {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|春|はる} → {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|春|はる}を{f|境|さかい}に |
| Noun (place) | noun + を{f|境|さかい}に | {f|川|かわ} → {f|川|かわ}を{f|境|さかい}に |
| Longer variant | noun + を{f|境|さかい}にして | {f|震災|しんさい}を{f|境|さかい}にして |
| Formal variant | noun + を{f|境|さかい}として | 1990{f|年|ねん}を{f|境|さかい}として |
| Verb clause | ～のを + を… → ～のを{f|境|さかい}に | {f|入院|にゅういん}した → {f|入院|にゅういん}したのを{f|境|さかい}に |
| Second clause | a change of state, usually past or ～ている | {f|減|へ}り{f|続|つづ}けている |
| Not possible | verb directly + を{f|境|さかい}に | ❌{f|卒業|そつぎょう}するを{f|境|さかい}に |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the spatial use is real and worth recognising:
<code>{f|川|かわ}を{f|境|さかい}に{f|東西|とうざい}で{f|文化|ぶんか}が{f|違|ちが}う</code>,
"the culture differs east and west of the river". The logic is identical; only the
axis has changed from time to space.

</div>

After graduation the two of them stopped keeping in touch.
</pre>

That accident was the dividing line; his life was quite different afterwards.
</pre>

<pre>

<pre>

<pre>

The earthquake was a watershed: attitudes to disaster preparedness in this town are not what they were.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>を{f|境|さかい}に</code> is **neutral, mildly formal and completely modern**. It
belongs to news reports, company documents, academic writing and careful speech,
and it is exactly the phrase a presenter reaches for when a graph changes direction.
Nothing about it is literary or old-fashioned; it is simply a shade more considered
than <code>から</code>.

It is also **emotionally neutral**. Unlike
<code>てからというもの</code>, which lets the speaker's feelings show, this pattern
stands back and observes: the change may be a blessing, a disaster or a statistic,
and the sentence does not say which. That objectivity is why it goes so well with
numbers and dates.

Two things it needs. A **genuine contrast** - if the situation before and after is
the same, the border is imaginary and the sentence is odd. And a **point**, not a
period: {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|春|はる} works, ここ{f|十年|じゅうねん} does not,
because a decade has no edge to draw a line at.

It says nothing about anyone's intentions. When the subject deliberately used the
event as an occasion to act - "I took my fiftieth birthday as a chance to start
running" - the pattern you want is <code>を{f|機|き}に</code>, not this one.

## Contrast

### ～を{f|境|さかい}に vs ～を{f|機|き}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|機|き}に</code> means somebody **seized the occasion**
and did something on purpose. <code>を{f|境|さかい}に</code> only reports that things
became different from that point.

</div>

<pre>
{f|定年|ていねん}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|夫婦|ふうふ}で{f|田舎|いなか}に{f|移|うつ}り{f|住|す}むことにした。
We decided to take my retirement as the moment to move to the countryside.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|定年|ていねん}<b>を{f|境|さかい}に</b>、{f|父|ちち}は{f|急|きゅう}に{f|老|ふ}け{f|込|こ}んだ。
After he retired my father aged suddenly.
</pre>

Nobody chose to age, so the second sentence cannot take
<code>を{f|機|き}に</code>. Going the other way, the first *could* take
<code>を{f|境|さかい}に</code>, but it would stop presenting the move as a decision
and merely note that the couple's address changed. Volition is the whole difference.

### ～を{f|境|さかい}に vs ～{f|以来|いらい}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|以来|いらい}</code> emphasises the **span** running from
then to now. <code>を{f|境|さかい}に</code> emphasises the **contrast** between the
two sides of the line.

</div>

<pre>
{f|開店|かいてん}<b>{f|以来|いらい}</b>、この{f|味|あじ}を{f|守|まも}り{f|続|つづ}けています。
We have kept this same flavour ever since the shop opened.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|開店|かいてん}<b>を{f|境|さかい}に</b>、{f|通|とお}りの{f|人通|ひとどお}りが{f|増|ふ}えた。
Since the shop opened, there have been noticeably more people on the street.
</pre>

The first sentence has no before-and-after at all - the point is continuity - so
<code>を{f|境|さかい}に</code> would contradict it. That is the test: if what you are
stressing is that nothing has changed since, use {f|以来|いらい}.

### ～を{f|境|さかい}に vs ～を{f|皮切|かわき}りに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</code> means "starting with", and what
follows is a **series** of similar things. <code>を{f|境|さかい}に</code> means
"from that point on", and what follows is a **different state**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大阪|おおさか}{f|公演|こうえん}<b>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</b>、{f|全国|ぜんこく}{f|十|じゅっ}か{f|所|しょ}を{f|回|まわ}る。
Starting with the Osaka show, they will play ten venues nationwide.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大阪|おおさか}{f|公演|こうえん}<b>を{f|境|さかい}に</b>、チケットの{f|売|う}れ{f|行|ゆ}きが{f|変|か}わった。
The Osaka show was the turning point for ticket sales.
</pre>

In the first, the Osaka show is the first item on a list. In the second it is a
hinge, and what comes after is not more shows but a different situation. Only
<code>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</code> implies that similar events follow.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|卒業|そつぎょう}する<b>を{f|境|さかい}に</b>、{f|東京|とうきょう}を{f|離|はな}れた。 → ✅ {f|卒業|そつぎょう}したの<b>を{f|境|さかい}に</b>、{f|東京|とうきょう}を{f|離|はな}れた。
  A verb has to be nominalised with の first, or replaced by the noun
  {f|卒業|そつぎょう} on its own.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}<b>を{f|境|さかい}に</b>、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|走|はし}ります。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}から{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|走|はし}ります。（or これ<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>）
  A resolution about your own behaviour is not an observed change. Either drop the
  pattern or switch to <code>を{f|機|き}に</code>.

- ❌ この{f|十年|じゅうねん}<b>を{f|境|さかい}に</b>、{f|人口|じんこう}が{f|減|へ}っている。 → ✅ この{f|十年|じゅうねん}、{f|人口|じんこう}が{f|減|へ}り{f|続|つづ}けている。
  A ten-year stretch is a period, not a boundary. The pattern needs a point you
  could mark on a line.

- ❌ {f|開業|かいぎょう}<b>を{f|境|さかい}に</b>、{f|同|おな}じサービスを{f|続|つづ}けている。 → ✅ {f|開業|かいぎょう}{f|以来|いらい}、{f|同|おな}じサービスを{f|続|つづ}けている。
  Nothing changed, so there is no border. Continuity belongs to
  {f|以来|いらい}.

## Practice

**1.** {f|台風|たいふう}____、{f|海岸|かいがん}の{f|地形|ちけい}はすっかり{f|変|か}わってしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|台風|たいふう}<b>を{f|境|さかい}に</b>

A single event acting as a hinge, with a clear difference before and after, and no
suggestion that anybody intended it. すっかり{f|変|か}わってしまった supplies exactly
the contrast the pattern requires.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|結婚|けっこん}（を{f|境|さかい}に・を{f|機|き}に）、{f|郊外|こうがい}に{f|家|いえ}を{f|買|か}うことにした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|結婚|けっこん}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|郊外|こうがい}に{f|家|いえ}を{f|買|か}うことにした。

ことにした marks a deliberate decision, and taking an occasion as a reason to act is
<code>を{f|機|き}に</code>. <code>を{f|境|さかい}に</code> reports a change from
outside and does not sit with a stated intention.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Sales began to recover from the second half of last year."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨年|さくねん}の{f|後半|こうはん}<b>を{f|境|さかい}に</b>、{f|売|う}り{f|上|あ}げは{f|回復|かいふく}に{f|向|む}かった。

A date acting as a turning point in a trend is the pattern's home ground, and the
neutral, faintly formal tone matches a business report. {f|昨年|さくねん} rather
than {f|去年|きょねん} keeps the register consistent.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|工場|こうじょう}が{f|閉|し}まるを{f|境|さかい}に、{f|町|まち}の{f|人口|じんこう}が{f|減|へ}り{f|始|はじ}めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|工場|こうじょう}が{f|閉|し}まったの<b>を{f|境|さかい}に</b>、{f|町|まち}の{f|人口|じんこう}が{f|減|へ}り{f|始|はじ}めた。

を requires a noun in front of it. Nominalise the clause with の - and use the past
form, since the closure has already happened.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|入学|にゅうがく}<b>を{f|境|さかい}に</b>、{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|口数|くちかず}が{f|減|へ}った。
(b) {f|入学|にゅうがく}し<b>てからというもの</b>、{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|口数|くちかず}が{f|減|へ}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the observer's sentence. It draws a line at the moment of starting school and
notes that the boy on one side of it is not the boy on the other. It is cool, and it
would fit a teacher's report as easily as a parent's remark.

(b) is the parent's sentence. It stresses that the quietness has gone on ever since,
and it lets a note of worry or wistfulness through. The facts are the same; the
distance is not.

</details>
