# ～ぐるみ

**Meaning** · the whole of ～ together; involving every member of ～ - a group acting as one body

`ぐるみ` attaches to a word naming a group of people and says that **all of it** is
in on the thing: not one member of the family but the family as a unit, not one
employee but the company. The image is of the whole lot being wrapped up together,
and the sentence usually goes on to say either how admirable that cooperation is or
how damning the complicity is.

## Formation

`ぐるみ` attaches to a **bare noun** and the result is a **noun**, which in practice
appears with `で` before a verb or `の` before a noun. The initial consonant is
always voiced: ぐるみ, never くるみ.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (a group) | Noun + ぐるみ | {f|家族|かぞく} → {f|家族|かぞく}ぐるみ |
| Before a verb | ～ぐるみ + で | {f|町|まち}ぐるみで{f|取|と}り{f|組|く}む |
| Before a noun | ～ぐるみ + の + Noun | {f|組織|そしき}ぐるみの{f|不正|ふせい} |
| Fixed expression | {f|身|み} + ぐるみ | {f|身|み}ぐるみはがされる |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the base must name a **group**: {f|家族|かぞく}・{f|町|まち}・{f|村|むら}・{f|地域|ちいき}・{f|会社|かいしゃ}・{f|組織|そしき}・{f|業界|ぎょうかい}・{f|国|くに}.
Individuals do not attach - ❌<code>{f|私|わたし}ぐるみ</code>,
❌<code>{f|二人|ふたり}ぐるみ</code>. The one exception is the frozen
<code>{f|身|み}ぐるみ</code>, "everything one has on", which survives almost only in
<code>{f|身|み}ぐるみはがされる</code> (be stripped of everything).

</div>

## Nuance & register

`ぐるみ` is register-neutral and equally at home in conversation and in news copy.
What varies enormously is the **evaluation**, and it comes entirely from the noun in
front, not from the suffix.

The warm family: <code>{f|家族|かぞく}ぐるみの{f|付|つ}き{f|合|あ}い</code>,
<code>{f|地域|ちいき}ぐるみの{f|見守|みまも}り</code>,
<code>{f|町|まち}ぐるみの{f|取|と}り{f|組|く}み</code>. These praise a community for
acting together, and they are the standard vocabulary of local-government leaflets
and of ordinary friendly conversation.

The damning family: <code>{f|会社|かいしゃ}ぐるみ</code>,
<code>{f|組織|そしき}ぐるみ</code>, <code>{f|業界|ぎょうかい}ぐるみ</code>. These are
so heavily associated with scandal reporting that they now carry the accusation on
their own. If you say <code>{f|組織|そしき}ぐるみでした</code> in a meeting, everyone
in the room hears "and that is much worse than one rogue employee" - the phrase is
the difference between an individual's mistake and institutional guilt, which in
Japanese public life is exactly the line an investigation is trying to draw.

<code>{f|身|み}ぐるみ</code> is a different animal: a frozen idiom, faintly
old-fashioned, and used almost only with はがす / はがされる, literally of robbery
and figuratively of losing everything in a business failure or a divorce.

## Contrast

### ～ぐるみ vs ～ごと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ぐるみ` is a **group of people** all taking part.
`ごと` is a **physical thing included**, eaten or carried along with what it is
attached to.

</div>

<pre>
りんごは{f|皮|かわ}<b>ごと</b>{f|食|た}べたほうが{f|栄養|えいよう}がある。
Apples are more nutritious eaten skin and all.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|問題|もんだい}には{f|地域|ちいき}<b>ぐるみ</b>で{f|取|と}り{f|組|く}む{f|必要|ひつよう}がある。
That problem needs the whole area to tackle it together.
</pre>

Both translate as "and all", which is why they get swapped, but they are not
interchangeable in either direction. ❌<code>{f|皮|かわ}ぐるみ{f|食|た}べる</code> and
❌<code>{f|地域|ちいき}ごと{f|取|と}り{f|組|く}む</code> are both wrong. The test: if
the "whole" is people who are choosing to act, it is `ぐるみ`; if it is a physical
object swallowed up along with something else, it is `ごと`.

### ～ぐるみ vs ～{f|全体|ぜんたい}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|全体|ぜんたい}</code> simply states the **extent** of
something. `ぐるみ` says every member is **actively involved**, and adds either
praise or blame.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会社|かいしゃ}{f|全体|ぜんたい}の{f|売上|うりあげ}は{f|去年|きょねん}より{f|伸|の}びている。
Sales across the company as a whole are up on last year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会社|かいしゃ}<b>ぐるみ</b>で{f|数字|すうじ}を{f|操作|そうさ}していたことが{f|分|わ}かった。
It emerged that the figures had been manipulated with the whole company in on it.
</pre>

The first is a measurement and is completely neutral. The second is an accusation.
This is why replacing {f|全体|ぜんたい} with `ぐるみ` in a report is never a purely
stylistic change - you have added complicity to the sentence.

### ～ぐるみ vs ～と{f|一緒|いっしょ}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>と{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</code> = X **accompanies** me on
this occasion. `ぐるみ` = the two **groups relate to each other as groups**, as a
standing arrangement.

</div>

<pre>
{f|日曜日|にちようび}は{f|家族|かぞく}<b>と{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</b>{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}った。
On Sunday I went shopping with my family.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|夫婦|ふうふ}とは{f|十年|じゅうねん}{f|来|らい}、{f|家族|かぞく}<b>ぐるみ</b>で{f|付|つ}き{f|合|あ}っている。
We've been friends with that couple as families for ten years now.
</pre>

The first is one outing; the family came along. The second describes a relationship
in which the children, the grandparents and everyone else are part of the connection.
`ぐるみ` is about the standing shape of things, which is why it so rarely appears in
a sentence about a single occasion.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|家族|かぞく}ぐるみ<b>に</b>{f|旅行|りょこう}した。 → ✅ {f|家族|かぞく}ぐるみ<b>で</b>{f|旅行|りょこう}した。
  Before a verb the particle is `で` - the group is the agent doing it together.

- ❌ みかんを{f|皮|かわ}<b>ぐるみ</b>{f|食|た}べる → ✅ みかんを{f|皮|かわ}<b>ごと</b>{f|食|た}べる
  `ぐるみ` needs a group of people. A skin included with the fruit is `ごと`.

- ❌ {f|町|まち}ぐるみ<b>な</b>{f|運動|うんどう} → ✅ {f|町|まち}ぐるみ<b>の</b>{f|運動|うんどう}
  The result is a noun and modifies with `の`, never `な`.

- ❌ {f|兄|あに}と{f|二人|ふたり}<b>ぐるみ</b>で{f|店|みせ}を{f|始|はじ}めた。 → ✅ {f|兄|あに}と{f|二人|ふたり}<b>で</b>{f|店|みせ}を{f|始|はじ}めた。
  Two named individuals are not a "whole group". `ぐるみ` needs a collective noun -
  a family, a town, an organisation.

## Practice

**1.** {f|空|あ}き{f|家|や}の{f|見回|みまわ}りは、{f|自治会|じちかい}が{f|中心|ちゅうしん}となって{f|地域|ちいき}____{f|行|おこな}っている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|地域|ちいき}<b>ぐるみで</b>

The neighbourhood association leads it but the whole area takes part, which is the
cooperative use of `ぐるみ`. Before a verb it takes `で`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|検査|けんさ}{f|結果|けっか}の{f|改|かい}ざんは、{f|一部|いちぶ}の{f|社員|しゃいん}ではなく{f|会社|かいしゃ}（{f|全体|ぜんたい}・ぐるみ）で{f|行|おこな}われていた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会社|かいしゃ}<b>ぐるみ</b>で{f|行|おこな}われていた。

The sentence explicitly rejects "a few employees" in favour of collective guilt,
which is exactly what `ぐるみ` adds. <code>{f|全体|ぜんたい}</code> would state the
extent without the charge of complicity, and it also does not take `で` this way.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The two families have been close since our children were small."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|子|こ}どもが{f|小|ちい}さい{f|頃|ころ}から、<b>{f|家族|かぞく}ぐるみ</b>で{f|親|した}しくしている。

"The two families" as units, not two individuals, is the standing-relationship use
of `ぐるみ`. <code>{f|家族|かぞく}と{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</code> would mean your own
family came along on some occasion.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|土地|とち}では、{f|昔|むかし}から{f|村|むら}ぐるみな{f|助|たす}け{f|合|あ}いが{f|続|つづ}いている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|土地|とち}では、{f|昔|むかし}から{f|村|むら}ぐるみ<b>の</b>{f|助|たす}け{f|合|あ}いが{f|続|つづ}いている。

<code>{f|村|むら}ぐるみ</code> is a noun, so it links to the following noun with
`の`. `な` would require it to be a な-adjective, which it is not.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|工場|こうじょう}<b>{f|全体|ぜんたい}</b>で{f|安全|あんぜん}{f|対策|たいさく}を{f|見直|みなお}した。
(b) {f|工場|こうじょう}<b>ぐるみ</b>で{f|安全|あんぜん}{f|対策|たいさく}を{f|見直|みなお}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a neutral report of scope: the review covered every part of the plant.

(b) stresses that everyone from the floor to management was personally involved. In
a positive context that reads as commendable collective effort; but because
`ぐるみ` is so strongly associated with scandal, in a sentence about safety failures
readers may hear a hint that the plant as a body was implicated. When you only mean
"across the whole plant", <code>{f|全体|ぜんたい}</code> is the safer word.

</details>
