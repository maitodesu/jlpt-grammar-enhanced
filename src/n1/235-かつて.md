# かつて

**Meaning** · once; formerly - and, with a negative, never yet

`かつて` places something in a past that is **finished**. It does not simply say
"earlier"; it says "there was a time when this was so, and that time is over". In the
negative it flips into "not once in all the time up to now", which is where the fixed
phrase かつてない, "unprecedented", comes from.

## Usage

An adverb of the written language. It normally stands early in the clause, and the
clause it governs is in the **past**. Three shapes cover nearly everything.

- **Plain past.** <code>かつて ～た / ～ていた</code>: この{f|町|まち}はかつて
  {f|城下町|じょうかまち}だった. The contrast with the present is built in, and it is
  often made explicit with `は`: <code>かつては～が、{f|今|いま}は～</code>.
- **Before a noun, with `の`.** <code>かつての</code> = "former, one-time":
  かつての{f|同僚|どうりょう}, かつての{f|栄光|えいこう}, かつての{f|自分|じぶん}.
- **With a negative.** <code>かつて～ない</code> = "never yet". The commonest form by
  far is the compressed <code>かつてない</code> before a noun -
  かつてない{f|規模|きぼ}, かつてない{f|経験|けいけん} - and the emphatic
  <code>{f|未|いま}だかつて～ない</code> in longer sentences.

The past it points at is an **indefinite era**, not a dated occasion. That is why a
precise time word beside it jars: かつて{f|三年前|さんねんまえ}に is wrong in the way
that "once, three years ago" is odd in English. It is also happiest with states and
long situations - {f|住|す}んでいた, {f|栄|さか}えていた, ～たことがある - rather than
with a single instant.

The kanji spellings <code>{f|嘗|かつ}て</code> and <code>{f|曽|かつ}て</code> exist and
you may meet them in older books, but modern text uses kana.

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|辺|あた}りは<b>かつて</b>{f|田|た}んぼだった。
This area was once rice paddies.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼女|かのじょ}は<b>かつて</b>の{f|同僚|どうりょう}で、{f|今|いま}は{f|大学|だいがく}で{f|教|おし}えている。
She is a former colleague of mine, and now she teaches at a university.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今年|ことし}の{f|夏|なつ}は<b>かつて</b>ない{f|暑|あつ}さになるおそれがある。
There is a risk that this summer will be hotter than any before it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|港町|みなとまち}として<b>かつて</b>は{f|栄|さか}えていたが、{f|鉄道|てつどう}が{f|通|とお}ってからは{f|人|ひと}の{f|流|なが}れがすっかり{f|変|か}わってしまった。
It flourished in its day as a port town, but once the railway came through the flow of people changed completely.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`かつて` is a **written** word with a slightly elevated, retrospective tone. Where you
will meet it: history writing and museum captions, newspaper features, company
histories, documentary narration, novels, and the prepared part of a speech
("{f|私|わたし}もかつてこの{f|教室|きょうしつ}で{f|学|まな}びました"). It suits
subjects with some weight - a town, an industry, a person's earlier life - and sounds
inflated over trivia.

Spoken Japanese does the same job with {f|昔|むかし} and {f|以前|いぜん}, or with
nothing at all: {f|前|まえ}はよく{f|行|い}ってたんだけどね. A learner who says
かつてラーメンが{f|好|す}きでした to a friend has produced a grammatical sentence that
sounds like a voice-over.

The one part of the word that *is* alive in speech is the negative. かつてない has
escaped into ordinary use - sports commentary, advertising copy,
かつてない{f|規模|きぼ}の{f|災害|さいがい} in a news bulletin - and no one hears it as
literary. The full {f|未|いま}だかつて form stays formal.

There is also a faint note of loss about it. Because it frames the past as closed,
`かつて` tends to attach to things that have declined, ended or changed beyond
recognition. It is not a rule, but it is a strong tendency, and it is why
かつての{f|自分|じぶん} usually introduces someone the speaker no longer is.

## Contrast

### かつて vs {f|昔|むかし} vs {f|以前|いぜん}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|以前|いぜん}</code> = at some earlier point, possibly last
week. <code>{f|昔|むかし}</code> = long ago, spoken, warm. `かつて` = in a former era
that is now over, written.

</div>

<pre>
<b>{f|以前|いぜん}</b>この{f|店|みせ}に{f|来|き}たことがあります。
I've been to this shop before.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|昔|むかし}</b>はこの{f|川|かわ}でよく{f|泳|およ}いだものだ。
We used to swim in this river a lot in the old days.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>かつて</b>この{f|島|しま}には{f|三千人|さんぜんにん}が{f|暮|く}らしていた。
Three thousand people once lived on this island.
</pre>

The first sentence cannot take `かつて`: a previous visit to a shop is not an era.
The second could take it, but the loss of {f|昔|むかし} would take the warmth with it -
{f|昔|むかし} invites the listener into a shared memory, `かつて` reports from a
distance. The third is `かつて` territory precisely because the island's populated
period is over, and swapping in {f|昔|むかし} would make a documentary sentence sound
like chat.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>かつて</b>{f|先週|せんしゅう}{f|田中|たなか}さんに{f|会|あ}いました。 → ✅ <b>{f|以前|いぜん}</b>{f|田中|たなか}さんに{f|会|あ}いました。
  A dated, recent occasion is exactly what `かつて` cannot describe. It needs an
  indefinite former period.

- ❌ {f|父|ちち}は<b>かつて</b>{f|銀行|ぎんこう}に{f|勤|つと}めています。 → ✅ {f|父|ちち}は<b>かつて</b>{f|銀行|ぎんこう}に{f|勤|つと}めていた。
  The clause must be past. `かつて` says the situation has ended, so a present-tense
  verb contradicts it in the same sentence.

- ❌ {f|今|いま}まで<b>かつて</b>こんなに{f|驚|おどろ}いた。 → ✅ {f|今|いま}まで<b>かつて</b>こんなに{f|驚|おどろ}いたことはない。
  In this frame `かつて` is half of a negative pair and cannot be left hanging. Finish
  it with ない.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}のライブ、<b>かつて</b>{f|楽|たの}しかったよ。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}のライブ、{f|今|いま}までで{f|一番|いちばん}{f|楽|たの}しかったよ。
  Two faults at once: casual register, and `かつて` being read as "extremely" rather
  than "formerly". It is never an intensifier.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|工場|こうじょう}は____、{f|町|まち}で{f|最|もっと}も{f|大|おお}きな{f|職場|しょくば}だった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|工場|こうじょう}は<b>かつて</b>、{f|町|まち}で{f|最|もっと}も{f|大|おお}きな{f|職場|しょくば}だった。

An indefinite former period, a past-tense clause, and an implied "not any more" - the
three things the word wants. Adding `は` after it,
<code>かつては</code>, would sharpen the contrast with the present still further.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|去年|きょねん}（かつて・{f|以前|いぜん}）お{f|送|おく}りした{f|資料|しりょう}をご{f|覧|らん}ください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|去年|きょねん}<b>{f|以前|いぜん}</b>お{f|送|おく}りした{f|資料|しりょう}

Only {f|以前|いぜん} can sit beside a dated, recent occasion such as
{f|去年|きょねん}. `かつて` would frame last year as a closed historical age, which is
absurd for a document you emailed, and it would clash with the business register of
お{f|送|おく}りした and ご{f|覧|らん}ください as well.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}は<b>かつて</b>の{f|名|めい}{f|選手|せんしゅ}だ。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|昔|むかし}の{f|名|めい}{f|選手|せんしゅ}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the natural sentence, and it says two things at once: he was a great player,
and that is over. Depending on the tone it can be respectful (a legend) or unkind (he
is finished), but the "no longer" is always audible.

(b) is odd in a way worth noticing. <code>{f|昔|むかし}の</code> before a noun tends to
mean "of the old days" as a type - {f|昔|むかし}の{f|建物|たてもの}, an old-style
building - so (b) drifts towards "a great player of a bygone era" rather than "a man
who used to be a great player". For a person's own former status, `かつての` is the
phrase.

</details>
