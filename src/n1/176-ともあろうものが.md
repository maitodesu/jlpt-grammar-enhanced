# ～ともあろうものが

**Meaning** · someone who is supposed to be a X, of all people - said with indignation about a person or body whose position should have prevented exactly this. The criticism is never optional; it is built into the pattern.

The literal parts are <code>と</code> (quotative) + <code>も</code> +
<code>あろう</code> (the old volitional of ある) + <code>もの</code> - roughly
"a person who would even be called a X". What the phrase does in practice is set
up a gap: the title carries a standard, the behaviour fell below it, and the
speaker is disappointed. It is the language of editorials and of formal
reprimands, not of conversation.

## Formation

A noun naming a **position of standing or responsibility** goes in front. What
follows the が is always a negative judgement.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (a role) | Noun + ともあろうものが | {f|大臣|だいじん} → {f|大臣|だいじん}ともあろうものが |
| Noun (respectful) | Noun + ともあろう{f|方|かた}が | {f|専門家|せんもんか} → {f|専門家|せんもんか}ともあろう{f|方|かた}が |
| Noun (kanji spelling) | Noun + ともあろう{f|者|もの}が | {f|指導者|しどうしゃ}ともあろう{f|者|もの}が |
| Noun (an organisation) | Noun + ともあろう + institution word + が | {f|大学|だいがく}ともあろう{f|機関|きかん}が |
| Second half | a criticism, very often ～とは / ～なんて | ～とは{f|情|なさ}けない、～とは{f|何事|なにごと}か |
| Not possible | a noun with no status attached | ❌{f|子|こ}どもともあろうものが |
| Not possible | praise after it | ❌～ともあろうものが、{f|見事|みごと}な{f|対応|たいおう}だった |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the が is the ordinary subject marker, not the "but" が. Replacing it
with は breaks the phrase: ❌<code>{f|大臣|だいじん}ともあろうものは</code>. The
whole string <code>ともあろうものが</code> behaves like one frozen unit, and the
only part that really varies is the noun after ともあろう -
もの / {f|者|もの} / {f|人|ひと} / {f|方|かた} / {f|機関|きかん} / {f|会社|かいしゃ} / {f|国|くに}.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise it; write around it.** This is the voice of a newspaper editorial, a
formal complaint, a courtroom summing-up, or an angry senior person in a period
drama. It is not impossible in speech - an older speaker can produce
<code>{f|先生|せんせい}ともあろう{f|人|ひと}が</code> when genuinely appalled - but
in ordinary conversation it lands as pompous or theatrical. The everyday ways to
say the same thing are <code>{f|大臣|だいじん}なのに</code>,
<code>{f|大臣|だいじん}ほどの{f|人|ひと}が</code>, or, in a franker register,
<code>{f|仮|かり}にも{f|大臣|だいじん}だろう</code>. Use one of those and keep
<code>ともあろうものが</code> for reading.

Three things it always carries. It **must** be followed by criticism; there is no
admiring use. The noun in front **must** carry status or responsibility - a
minister, a teacher, a doctor, a police force, a broadcaster, a national team. And
the speaker is positioned as an outsider passing judgement, which is why it is
awkward about oneself: for a blunder of your own, Japanese uses
<code>{f|私|わたし}としたことが</code> instead.

The respectful variant <code>ともあろう{f|方|かた}が</code> is worth noticing,
because it is not a softening. Wrapping a rebuke in honorific vocabulary makes it
colder and more formal, not gentler - it is the tone of a letter of protest to
someone senior.

## Contrast

### ～ともあろうものが vs ～なのに / ～ほどの{f|人|ひと}が

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>なのに</code> and <code>ほどの{f|人|ひと}が</code> are
the modern, speakable versions. They say the same thing without the editorial page.

</div>

<pre>
{f|医者|いしゃ}<b>なのに</b>、{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|健康|けんこう}には{f|無関心|むかんしん}なんだね。
He's a doctor, and yet he takes no interest in his own health.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|医者|いしゃ}<b>ともあろうものが</b>、{f|患者|かんじゃ}の{f|記録|きろく}を{f|放置|ほうち}していたという。
A doctor - a doctor of all people - is said to have left patient records unattended.
</pre>

Both point at the same mismatch. The first is something you say to a friend; the
second is something a committee says in public. If you are speaking rather than
writing, use the first.

### ～ともあろうものが vs ～ともなると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ともなると</code> says *once you reach that position,
naturally this follows*. <code>ともあろうものが</code> says *given that position,
this should never have happened*.

</div>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>ともなると</b>、{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|数|かず}も{f|一気|いっき}に{f|増|ふ}える。
Once you make department head, the number of meetings shoots up.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>ともあろうものが</b>、{f|部下|ぶか}の{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|間違|まちが}えるとは。
For a department head to get his own staff's names wrong...
</pre>

<code>ともなると</code> is neutral, even admiring, and is followed by a
consequence. <code>ともあろうものが</code> is always followed by a failing. They
share a shape and share nothing else.

### ～ともあろうものが vs ～としたことが

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ともあろうものが</code> points **outward** at someone
whose office they have disgraced. <code>としたことが</code> points **inward** at
the speaker's own uncharacteristic slip.

</div>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}<b>としたことが</b>、{f|大事|だいじ}な{f|書類|しょるい}を{f|忘|わす}れてしまいました。
How careless of me - I of all people have left the important papers behind.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|責任者|せきにんしゃ}<b>ともあろうものが</b>、{f|報告|ほうこく}を{f|怠|おこた}っていた。
The person in charge, of all people, had been neglecting to report.
</pre>

The second difference is the weight of the fault.
<code>としたことが</code> covers a lapse - a forgotten umbrella, a slip of the
tongue - and is half an apology. <code>ともあろうものが</code> covers a breach of
the duty attached to a position, and is an accusation.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|山田|やまだ}さん<b>ともあろうものが</b>、すばらしい{f|発表|はっぴょう}でした。 → ✅ さすが{f|山田|やまだ}さんですね、すばらしい{f|発表|はっぴょう}でした。
  The pattern cannot introduce praise. For "as one would expect of X", the word is
  さすが.

- ❌ {f|弟|おとうと}<b>ともあろうものが</b>、{f|宿題|しゅくだい}を{f|忘|わす}れた。 → ✅ {f|弟|おとうと}<b>ときたら</b>、また{f|宿題|しゅくだい}を{f|忘|わす}れたんだ。
  A younger brother carries no office to fall short of. The noun in front has to
  denote status or responsibility.

- ❌ {f|大臣|だいじん}ともあろうもの<b>は</b>、{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|避|さ}けた。 → ✅ {f|大臣|だいじん}ともあろうもの<b>が</b>、{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|避|さ}けた。
  The が is fixed. Swapping in は turns the phrase into a general statement about a
  class of people and the indignation disappears.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}<b>ともあろうものが</b>、{f|寝坊|ねぼう}しました。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>としたことが</b>、{f|寝坊|ねぼう}しました。
  Applied to yourself, the pattern claims your own eminence, which is either
  self-important or a joke. Use としたことが for your own mistakes.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to meet this sentence, and what tone does it carry? {f|公共|こうきょう}{f|放送|ほうそう}<b>ともあろうものが</b>、{f|事実|じじつ}の{f|確認|かくにん}を{f|怠|おこた}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In print - a newspaper editorial, a magazine column, a formal letter of complaint,
or the written findings of a review panel. The tone is cold public indignation: the
writer is holding an institution to the standard its own status implies. Spoken
aloud to a colleague it would sound like a performance.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|校長|こうちょう}{f|先生|せんせい}<b>ともあろう{f|方|かた}が</b>、{f|約束|やくそく}を{f|忘|わす}れるとは。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|校長|こうちょう}{f|先生|せんせい}<b>ほどの{f|方|かた}が</b>{f|約束|やくそく}を{f|忘|わす}れるなんて、{f|意外|いがい}ですね。

<code>ほどの{f|方|かた}が</code> keeps the respect and the surprise while dropping
the editorial register, and なんて carries the "of all people" flavour in
conversation. Adding {f|意外|いがい}ですね softens an accusation into a remark.

</details>

**3.** Choose: {f|社長|しゃちょう}（ともなると・ともあろうものが）、{f|海外|かいがい}{f|出張|しゅっちょう}も{f|月|つき}に{f|何度|なんど}もある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|社長|しゃちょう}<b>ともなると</b>

The second half is a neutral consequence of holding the post, not a failing, so it
needs <code>ともなると</code>. <code>ともあろうものが</code> would promise a
criticism that never arrives, and the sentence would break off unfinished.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|裁判官|さいばんかん}ともあろうものが、{f|立派|りっぱ}な{f|判断|はんだん}をした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ さすが{f|裁判官|さいばんかん}だけあって、{f|立派|りっぱ}な{f|判断|はんだん}をした。
"As you would expect of a judge, it was a fine decision."

The original praises, and this pattern only ever introduces a fault. If you want
to say the behaviour matched the position, the frames are
<code>さすが</code> and <code>だけあって</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|私|わたし}としたことが、{f|数字|すうじ}を{f|間違|まちが}えてしまいました。
(b) {f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}ともあろうものが、{f|数字|すうじ}を{f|間違|まちが}えるとは。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the speaker about the speaker. It admits a slip while implying it is out of
character, and it functions as part of an apology - the listener is expected to
say never mind.

(b) is one person judging another. It treats the same mistake as a failure of
professional duty and invites no forgiveness; it belongs in a written review or a
formal dressing-down. Note also that (b) needs a role noun, so
{f|担当者|たんとうしゃ} rather than a personal name works best.

</details>
