# ～ないではおかない / ～ずにはおかない

**Meaning** · it is bound to have that effect; I will not let it rest until I do it - read <code>ないではおかない</code> / <code>ずにはおかない</code>

おく here is "to leave a thing as it is". The double negative therefore says "will
not leave it undone", and two quite different sentences come out of that one shape.
With a verb of **effect** - usually a causative - it is a statement of inevitability:
the film *will* move you, and there is no leaving the audience alone. With a verb of
the speaker's **own action** it is a vow: I am not letting this go.

## Formation

Both endings sit on the ない-stem, exactly as with ずには{f|済|す}まない.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ～ない stem + ないでは／ずにはおかない | {f|招|まね}かない → {f|招|まね}かずにはおかない |
| Verb (る-verb) | same | {f|与|あた}えない → {f|与|あた}えないではおかない |
| Verb (する) | **せずにはおかない**／しないではおかない | {f|究明|きゅうめい}する → {f|究明|きゅうめい}せずにはおかない |
| Causative (the commonest shape) | ～させずにはおかない | {f|考|かんが}えさせる → {f|考|かんが}えさせずにはおかない |
| Past | ～ずにはおかなかった | {f|驚|おどろ}かせずにはおかなかった |
| Modifying a noun | ～ずにはおかない + Noun | {f|追及|ついきゅう}せずにはおかない{f|構|かま}え |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the two senses take different subjects. **Inevitability** needs a
non-human subject and an effect on somebody else, which is why the verb is nearly
always a causative ({f|感動|かんどう}させる, {f|考|かんが}えさせる) or a verb of
consequence ({f|招|まね}く, {f|与|あた}える, {f|引|ひ}き{f|起|お}こす). The **vow**
needs a first-person subject and a verb of deliberate action. A polite form exists,
<code>ずにはおきません</code>, but it is rare - this pattern lives in plain-form
written Japanese.

</div>

この{f|映画|えいが}は{f|見|み}る{f|者|もの}を{f|泣|な}かせ<b>ずにはおかない</b>。
This film cannot fail to move its audience to tears.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|一言|ひとこと}は{f|人|ひと}を{f|傷|きず}つけ<b>ないではおかない</b>。
A remark like that is bound to wound.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値上|ねあ}げの{f|発表|はっぴょう}は、{f|利用者|りようしゃ}の{f|不満|ふまん}を{f|招|まね}か<b>ずにはおかない</b>。
The announcement of the price rise is certain to provoke discontent among users.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|文章|ぶんしょう}は、{f|読|よ}む{f|者|もの}に{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|生|い}き{f|方|かた}を{f|考|かんが}えさせ<b>ずにはおかない</b>。
His writing makes the reader think about how they are living, whether they wish to or not.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|真相|しんそう}は{f|必|かなら}ず{f|明|あき}らかにし<b>ないではおかない</b>。
I will get to the bottom of this, whatever it takes.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|遺族|いぞく}は、{f|事故|じこ}の{f|責任|せきにん}を{f|追及|ついきゅう}せ<b>ずにはおかない</b>{f|構|かま}えだ。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|判決|はんけつ}は、{f|同|おな}じような{f|裁判|さいばん}に{f|影響|えいきょう}を{f|与|あた}え<b>ずにはおかない</b>だろう。

</pre>

Quietly told though it was, the testimony could not but leave its hearers shaken.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is **written, rhetorical Japanese**. Book reviews, film write-ups, editorials,
criticism, courtroom reporting and speeches are where it lives. Spoken aloud in
ordinary conversation it sounds theatrical, and the vow sense in particular has the
ring of a period drama or a politician at a microphone.

Of the two senses, **inevitability is far and away the commoner** in modern text.
It is the reviewer's phrase: the writer wants to say that the work has an effect on
you which you do not get to opt out of, and the double negative delivers exactly
that. The vow sense survives in headlines about investigations
({f|徹底|てってい}{f|究明|きゅうめい}せずにはおかない) and in fiction, but a learner
should treat it as something to recognise. In speech you would say
{f|必|かなら}ず～する or きっと～させる.

ないでは and ずには are interchangeable here, with ずには a shade more literary. The
form is stronger than {f|必|かなら}ず～する: it does not merely predict the effect,
it says the thing is incapable of *not* producing it.

## Contrast

### ～ずにはおかない vs ～ずには{f|済|す}まない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - **おかない**: the subject will not stop until it has had its
effect on **someone else**. **{f|済|す}まない**: the subject is the one under
pressure, and the world will not let **them** off.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}は、{f|関係者|かんけいしゃ}に{f|見直|みなお}しを{f|迫|せま}ら<b>ずにはおかない</b>。
This report is bound to force those involved into a rethink.
</pre>

<pre>
これだけの{f|指摘|してき}を{f|受|う}けた{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|関係者|かんけいしゃ}は{f|見直|みなお}しをせ<b>ずには{f|済|す}まない</b>。
Faced with criticism on this scale, those involved will not be allowed to leave things as they are.
</pre>

Same event, opposite grammar. In the first the report is doing the pushing; in the
second the people are being pushed. Identify who is squeezed and the choice makes
itself.

### ～ずにはおかない vs ～させる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the plain causative <code>させる</code> reports that the effect
**happens**. <code>させずにはおかない</code> adds that it **cannot fail to** happen,
and lifts the sentence into literary register.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|小説|しょうせつ}は{f|読者|どくしゃ}を{f|感動|かんどう}<b>させる</b>。
This novel moves its readers.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|小説|しょうせつ}は{f|読者|どくしゃ}を{f|感動|かんどう}<b>させずにはおかない</b>。
This novel cannot leave a reader unmoved.
</pre>

The first is a plain statement and could appear anywhere. The second is a review
sentence: it claims the effect is inescapable, and the rhetoric is doing as much
work as the meaning. Note that the causative is not optional - the effect has to
land on somebody.

### ～ずにはおかない vs ～ずにはいられない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - **おかない**: something out there will not leave **people**
alone. **いられない**: *I* cannot leave *myself* alone - my own feelings force the
action.

</div>

<pre>
その{f|光景|こうけい}を{f|見|み}て、{f|声|こえ}をかけ<b>ずにはいられなかった</b>。
Seeing that, I couldn't stop myself saying something.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|光景|こうけい}は、{f|見|み}る{f|者|もの}に{f|言葉|ことば}を{f|失|うしな}わせ<b>ずにはおかない</b>。
A sight like that leaves anyone who sees it lost for words.
</pre>

The two look almost identical and share a stem. The test is the subject: a person
with feelings takes いられない, and a thing with an effect takes おかない.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|感動|かんどう}<b>しずにはおかない</b> → ✅ {f|感動|かんどう}<b>させずにはおかない</b>
  Two repairs at once. する takes the old stem せ before ずに, and the sense
  requires the causative because the emotion belongs to somebody else.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}はこの{f|映画|えいが}に{f|感動|かんどう}せ<b>ずにはおかなかった</b> → ✅ {f|私|わたし}はこの{f|映画|えいが}に{f|感動|かんどう}せ<b>ずにはいられなかった</b>
  Your own reaction takes いられない. おかない would mean you were determined to
  force yourself to be moved.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}までに{f|宿題|しゅくだい}をやら<b>ずにはおかない</b> → ✅ {f|明日|あした}までに{f|宿題|しゅくだい}をやらないわけにはいかない
  Homework is an obligation, not a vow to make the world answer for something. The
  mismatch of scale makes the sentence comic.

- ❌ {f|招|まね}く<b>ずにはおかない</b> → ✅ {f|招|まね}か<b>ずにはおかない</b>
  The base is the ない-form stem, not the dictionary form.

## Practice

**1.** {f|今回|こんかい}の{f|決定|けってい}は、{f|現場|げんば}に{f|大|おお}きな{f|混乱|こんらん}を{f|招|まね}か____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|混乱|こんらん}を{f|招|まね}か<b>ずにはおかない</b>

The decision is the subject, the confusion lands on somebody else, and the writer is
claiming the consequence is unavoidable - the inevitability sense. Note the ない stem
{f|招|まね}か.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|彼|かれ}の{f|演奏|えんそう}は、{f|聞|き}く{f|人|ひと}を{f|驚|おどろ}かせ（ずにはおかない・ずにはいられない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|聞|き}く{f|人|ひと}を{f|驚|おどろ}かせ<b>ずにはおかない</b>。

The subject is the performance and the effect falls on the listeners, so おかない is
right. いられない would need a person as subject and their own feeling as the verb -
{f|驚|おどろ}かずにはいられない, "I couldn't help being astonished".

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "A photograph like this is bound to make people think about the war."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

このような{f|写真|しゃしん}は、{f|人々|ひとびと}に{f|戦争|せんそう}について{f|考|かんが}えさせ<b>ずにはおかない</b>。

"Is bound to make people think" is the causative {f|考|かんが}えさせる plus the
pattern. This is the review-and-editorial register the form belongs to, so the plain
form is correct even in polite writing.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|事件|じけん}の{f|真相|しんそう}を{f|究明|きゅうめい}しずにはおかない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|事件|じけん}の{f|真相|しんそう}を{f|究明|きゅうめい}<b>せずにはおかない</b>。
（or {f|究明|きゅうめい}<b>しないではおかない</b>。）

する takes the classical stem せ before ずに. The modern し only works with the
other ending, しないでは. This sentence is the vow sense, and it reads like a
headline or a line of dialogue rather than something said at a desk.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|報道|ほうどう}は{f|世論|よろん}に{f|影響|えいきょう}を{f|与|あた}えるだろう。
(b) この{f|報道|ほうどう}は{f|世論|よろん}に{f|影響|えいきょう}を{f|与|あた}え<b>ずにはおかない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a prediction, and a hedged one. The reporting will probably have an effect;
the writer allows that it might not.

(b) removes the hedge and adds rhetoric. It says the reporting is *incapable* of
leaving opinion untouched, and the form itself signals that the sentence comes from
an editorial or a piece of commentary rather than a news report.

</details>
