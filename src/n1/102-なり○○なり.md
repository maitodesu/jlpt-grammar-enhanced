# ～なり～なり

**Meaning** · A or B - or anything else along those lines; pick one and get on with it

`なり…なり` puts two options on the table and hands the choice to the other person.
The two are **samples**, not the whole menu: whatever else would do the same job is
included by implication. The sentence it lives in is nearly always a suggestion, a
request, an instruction or a permission - the speaker is telling someone that a
decision is theirs to make, and that any reasonable option will do.

## Formation

Each option carries its own `なり`, and the pair is normally rounded off with
`する`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + なり | {f|売|う}る → {f|売|う}るなり{f|捨|す}てるなり |
| Noun | Noun + なり | メールなり{f|電話|でんわ}なり |
| Noun + particle | Noun + なり + に／で／へ | {f|先輩|せんぱい}なり{f|上司|じょうし}なりに |
| Closing the pair | ～なり～なり + する／してください／したらどうか | {f|読|よ}むなり{f|聞|き}くなりしてください |
| Question word | {f|何|なん}なりと／どこなりと | {f|何|なん}なりとお{f|申|もう}し{f|付|つ}けください |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the verb in front of `なり` is always **plain non-past**.
❌<code>{f|売|う}ったなり{f|捨|す}てたなり</code> is impossible, because the pattern
looks at options still open, not at things already done.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - a single `なり` after a dictionary-form verb is a completely different
pattern meaning "the moment that", as in
<code>{f|座|すわ}るなり{f|眠|ねむ}ってしまった</code>. The alternatives reading needs
the **pair**. If you see only one `なり`, read it as "the moment".

</div>

</pre>

</pre>

<pre>

Rather than carrying it all alone, why not see a doctor or talk to your family - start with whatever you can manage.

## Nuance & register

The register is neutral: `なり…なり` appears in polite requests and in blunt
advice alike, and it is at home in both speech and writing. What it does have is a
**direction**. Because it hands over a decision, it fits speech aimed downwards or
sideways - to a junior, a friend, a family member, a customer being given options -
far more comfortably than speech aimed upwards. Laying out
<code>{f|電車|でんしゃ}で{f|行|い}くなりタクシーを{f|使|つか}うなりしてください</code>
for your own manager sounds as if you were assigning them homework. To a superior,
<code>どちらでも{f|構|かま}いません</code> or
<code>ご{f|都合|つごう}に{f|合|あ}わせます</code> is the safer shape.

There is often a note of mild impatience underneath, especially with
`したらどうですか` or `してほしい`: the speaker thinks the other person has been
sitting on a problem that any of several obvious moves would solve. Said warmly it
is encouragement; said flatly it is a nudge.

<code>{f|何|なん}なりと</code> is worth learning as a unit. It is service language -
<code>ご{f|用|よう}がありましたら{f|何|なん}なりとお{f|申|もう}し{f|付|つ}けください</code>
- and belongs to hotels, shops and formal correspondence.

One frozen relative to recognise but not to build on:
<code>{f|大|だい}なり{f|小|しょう}なり</code>, "to a greater or lesser degree". It is
a set phrase, and its {f|大|だい}・{f|小|しょう} slots cannot be filled with
anything else.

## Contrast

### ～なり～なり vs ～か～か

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `か` presents a **closed** choice: these are the options. `なり`
presents **samples**: these, or anything similar.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}まではバス<b>か</b>{f|電車|でんしゃ}で{f|来|き}てください。
Come to the venue by bus or by train.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}まではバス<b>なり</b>{f|電車|でんしゃ}<b>なり</b>で{f|来|き}てください。
Get to the venue by bus, or train, or however you like.
</pre>

The first has quietly ruled out driving. The second has not: it names two ways of
travelling and leaves the rest of the world open. That is also why `か` is fine in
a plain statement of fact - {f|彼|かれ}はバスか{f|電車|でんしゃ}で{f|来|き}た -
where `なり` cannot go.

### ～なり～なり vs ～たり～たり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たり…たり` samples things that **happen**, often several of
them. `なり…なり` samples options of which the listener picks **one**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|休|やす}みの{f|日|ひ}は{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}ん<b>だり</b>{f|散歩|さんぽ}し<b>たり</b>している。
On days off I read, go for walks, that sort of thing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|気分転換|きぶんてんかん}に{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}む<b>なり</b>{f|散歩|さんぽ}する<b>なり</b>したらどう。
Why not read something or go for a walk, to clear your head?
</pre>

The first describes a life; both activities are real and both recur. The second
proposes a remedy, and the listener is expected to do one of them, not both. This
is the pair learners mix up most, because the English "or" and "and" both come out
vague in translation - decide first whether anyone is choosing.

### ～なり～なり vs ～にしろ～にしろ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なり…なり` says **pick either**. `にしろ…にしろ` says it makes
no difference which, because the same thing is true of both.

</div>

<pre>
{f|参加|さんか}する<b>にしろ</b>{f|欠席|けっせき}する<b>にしろ</b>、{f|金曜|きんよう}までに{f|返事|へんじ}をください。
Whether you're coming or not, let me have an answer by Friday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}はメール<b>なり</b>{f|電話|でんわ}<b>なり</b>でお{f|願|ねが}いします。
Send your answer by email or phone, whichever you prefer.
</pre>

`にしろ` concedes the branches and then states what holds across them; the listener
is not being invited to choose between them at all. `なり` does nothing but invite
the choice.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}る<b>なり</b>{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}をする<b>なり</b>した。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}<b>たり</b>{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}をし<b>たり</b>した。
  Yesterday is over and nobody is choosing anything. `なり` needs an open decision;
  a report of what happened takes `たり`.

- ❌ {f|捨|す}てた<b>なり</b>{f|売|う}った<b>なり</b>してください。 → ✅ {f|捨|す}てる<b>なり</b>{f|売|う}る<b>なり</b>してください。
  The verb before `なり` stays in the dictionary form. Tense is carried by the
  `する` that closes the pattern.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|新幹線|しんかんせん}を{f|使|つか}う<b>なり</b>{f|飛行機|ひこうき}にする<b>なり</b>してください。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|新幹線|しんかんせん}でも{f|飛行機|ひこうき}でも、ご{f|都合|つごう}のよろしい{f|方|ほう}をお{f|選|えら}びください。
  Grammatically sound, socially wrong. Handing a superior a menu of actions has an
  instructing tone that the polite ending cannot cancel.

- ❌ {f|困|こま}ったら{f|相談|そうだん}する<b>なり</b>してください。 → ✅ {f|困|こま}ったら{f|相談|そうだん}する<b>なり</b>{f|連絡|れんらく}をくれる<b>なり</b>してください。
  The alternatives reading needs two. A lone `なり` after a dictionary form is read
  as "the moment you consult someone", which is not what was meant.

## Practice

**1.** {f|荷物|にもつ}が{f|多|おお}いなら、{f|宅配便|たくはいびん}で{f|送|おく}る____{f|駅|えき}のロッカーに{f|預|あず}ける____したらどうですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|送|おく}る<b>なり</b>{f|預|あず}ける<b>なり</b>

Two suggestions offered as samples, in a sentence whose whole point is that the
listener should choose one. Note that both verbs stay in the dictionary form and
`する` carries the ending.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|週末|しゅうまつ}は{f|洗濯|せんたく}を（するなり・したり）{f|掃除|そうじ}を（するなり・したり）して{f|過|す}ごした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|洗濯|せんたく}を<b>したり</b>{f|掃除|そうじ}を<b>したり</b>して{f|過|す}ごした

This is a report of a weekend that has already happened, and both jobs got done.
`なり` would need an unmade decision in front of it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If you can't reach me, leave a message with the office or text my mobile."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|連絡|れんらく}がつかないときは、{f|事務所|じむしょ}に{f|伝言|でんごん}を{f|残|のこ}す<b>なり</b>{f|携帯|けいたい}にメッセージを{f|送|おく}る<b>なり</b>してください。

Two routes offered to the listener, with others tacitly allowed - which is exactly
what `なり…なり` adds over a plain <code>か</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|体調|たいちょう}が{f|悪|わる}いなら、{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}るなり{f|休|やす}むなりしましたか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|体調|たいちょう}が{f|悪|わる}いなら、{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}る<b>なり</b>{f|休|やす}む<b>なり</b>したらどうですか。

The pattern proposes; it does not ask about the past. Endings like
`したらどうですか`, `してください`, `したほうがいい` and `してほしい` fit it;
a past question does not.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|質問|しつもん}があれば、メールか{f|電話|でんわ}でお{f|願|ねが}いします。
(b) {f|質問|しつもん}があれば、メールなり{f|電話|でんわ}なりでお{f|願|ねが}いします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) names the two accepted channels and, by saying nothing else, implies they are
the accepted ones. A reader would not turn up at the desk in person.

(b) names two as examples and leaves the door open - fax, the contact form,
catching you in the corridor. It also sounds a shade more relaxed, because the
speaker is declining to specify.

</details>
