# ～極まる / ～極まりない

**Meaning** · extremely; utterly; beyond any limit - a verdict that some quality has gone as far as it can go, almost always a bad one

Both halves of this pair mean the same thing, which is the first surprise:
<code>{f|危険|きけん}{f|極|きわ}まる</code> and
<code>{f|危険|きけん}{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> are both "dangerous in the extreme".
<code>{f|極|きわ}まる</code> says the quality *reaches* its limit;
<code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> says there *is no* limit to it. Japanese arrives at
the same place from opposite directions. What matters more than the logic is the
tone: this is the language of a written complaint, an editorial or a formal
protest, and it is a **judgement passed on something**, not a report of how the
speaker feels.

## Formation

The base is a な-adjective **stem** - no な, no い-adjective.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| な-adjective (stem) | stem + {f|極|きわ}まりない | {f|失礼|しつれい}な → {f|失礼|しつれい}{f|極|きわ}まりない |
| な-adjective (stem) | stem + {f|極|きわ}まる | {f|危険|きけん}な → {f|危険|きけん}{f|極|きわ}まる |
| Noun of quality | noun + {f|極|きわ}まりない | {f|迷惑|めいわく} → {f|迷惑|めいわく}{f|極|きわ}まりない |
| Before a noun | ～{f|極|きわ}まる／{f|極|きわ}まりない + noun | {f|危険|きけん}{f|極|きわ}まりない{f|行為|こうい} |
| Past | ～{f|極|きわ}まりなかった | {f|退屈|たいくつ}{f|極|きわ}まりなかった |
| Polite | ～{f|極|きわ}まりないです／～{f|極|きわ}まりありません | {f|失礼|しつれい}{f|極|きわ}まりありません |
| Lexical verb | {f|感|かん}{f|極|きわ}まる = be overcome with emotion | {f|感|かん}{f|極|きわ}まって{f|泣|な}き{f|出|だ}す |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> is treated as an い-adjective, so it
ends a written sentence on its own with no だ: ❌<code>{f|危険|きけん}{f|極|きわ}まりないだ</code>.
The polite forms exist but are uncommon, because the pattern lives in writing,
where a plain ending is normal. Of the two,
<code>～{f|極|きわ}まりありません</code> is the stiffer and fits a formal spoken
protest.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
あの{f|運転|うんてん}は{f|危険|きけん}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>。
That driving is dangerous in the extreme.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|店員|てんいん}の{f|態度|たいど}は{f|失礼|しつれい}<b>{f|極|きわ}まる</b>ものだった。
The clerk's attitude was utterly rude.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|前|まえ}の{f|日|ひ}に{f|予定|よてい}を{f|変|か}えられるのは{f|迷惑|めいわく}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>。
Having the schedule changed on us the day before is a thorough nuisance.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|同|おな}じ{f|説明|せつめい}が{f|三|さん}{f|時間|じかん}{f|続|つづ}き、{f|退屈|たいくつ}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりなかった</b>。
The same explanation went on for three hours; it was crushingly dull.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|安全|あんぜん}{f|帯|たい}もつけずに{f|屋根|やね}に{f|登|のぼ}るとは、{f|危険|きけん}<b>{f|極|きわ}まる</b>{f|行為|こうい}だ。
Climbing onto the roof without even a harness is an act of sheer recklessness.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}が{f|決|き}まった{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}、キャプテンは<b>{f|感|かん}{f|極|きわ}まって</b>{f|声|こえ}を{f|詰|つ}まらせた。
The moment the win was confirmed, the captain was overcome and his voice caught.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|点検|てんけん}の{f|記録|きろく}を{f|残|のこ}さずに{f|運行|うんこう}を{f|続|つづ}けていたのなら、ずさん<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>{f|管理|かんり}だ。
If they kept the service running without recording any inspections, the management was slapdash beyond words.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|席|せき}で{f|部下|ぶか}を{f|名指|なざ}しで{f|笑|わら}い{f|者|もの}にするなど、{f|無礼|ぶれい}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>{f|振|ふ}る{f|舞|ま}いである。
To single out a subordinate for ridicule in a meeting is conduct of the utmost discourtesy.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is **formal written Japanese**: newspaper columns, letters of complaint,
company statements, courtroom argument, the narrator's voice in a novel. In
conversation it lands as theatrical, and speakers who use it in speech are usually
doing it for effect - imitating an editorial to be funny, or being deliberately
severe.

The important restriction is not grammatical but lexical: **the adjective is nearly
always a critical one.** The stems that genuinely occur are a short list -
{f|危険|きけん}, {f|失礼|しつれい}, {f|無礼|ぶれい}, {f|非常識|ひじょうしき},
{f|不愉快|ふゆかい}, {f|不謹慎|ふきんしん}, {f|迷惑|めいわく}, {f|退屈|たいくつ},
{f|卑劣|ひれつ}, {f|滑稽|こっけい}, {f|身勝手|みがって}, {f|無責任|むせきにん},
{f|面倒|めんどう}, ずさん. {f|贅沢|ぜいたく}{f|極|きわ}まりない is the one
common near-positive case, and even there the speaker is half-scolding the
extravagance. Attaching it to a happy word does not work: nobody says
❌{f|幸|しあわ}せ{f|極|きわ}まりない.

Watch the direction of the sentence. <code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> **judges a
thing** - an act, a piece of work, someone's manner. It does not report what is
happening inside the speaker. That job belongs to
<code>～{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code>, <code>～{f|極|きわ}み</code> and
<code>～を{f|禁|きん}じ{f|得|え}ない</code>, and the difference is invisible in an
English translation with "extremely" in it.

<code>{f|感|かん}{f|極|きわ}まる</code> is a separate word that happens to contain
the same verb, and it is worth learning on its own. It is a plain intransitive
verb meaning "be overwhelmed by emotion", it is not critical at all, and it is
mostly used in the て-form: {f|感|かん}{f|極|きわ}まって{f|泣|な}く,
{f|感|かん}{f|極|きわ}まって{f|言葉|ことば}が{f|出|で}ない. It appears in ordinary
news reporting about weddings, retirements and medal ceremonies.

## Contrast

### ～{f|極|きわ}まる vs ～{f|極|きわ}まりない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same meaning. <code>～{f|極|きわ}まる</code> is the older
and slightly more clipped of the two, and is most at home directly before a noun;
<code>～{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> is the commoner choice and the safer default.

</div>

<pre>
{f|不謹慎|ふきんしん}<b>{f|極|きわ}まる</b>{f|発言|はつげん}に{f|抗議|こうぎ}が{f|集|あつ}まった。
The grossly insensitive remark drew a wave of protest.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|言|い}い{f|訳|わけ}は{f|身勝手|みがって}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>。
That excuse is selfish in the extreme.
</pre>

Do not read the negative form as a negative meaning. ❌"is not extreme" is never
what <code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> says; the {f|極|きわ}まり that is missing is
the *limit*, not the quality.

### ～{f|極|きわ}まりない vs ～この{f|上|うえ}ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>この{f|上|うえ}ない</code> is the neutral scale-topper and
works for good things. <code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> carries a scowl.

</div>

<pre>
{f|皆様|みなさま}にお{f|越|こ}しいただき、この<b>{f|上|うえ}ない</b>{f|喜|よろこ}びです。
Having you all here is a joy beyond measure.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|手続|てつづ}きが{f|三|さん}{f|度|ど}もやり{f|直|なお}しになり、{f|面倒|めんどう}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>。
The paperwork was sent back three times - it is an utter pain.
</pre>

Swap them and the first sentence becomes a complaint about your own guests. With
い-adjectives, <code>この{f|上|うえ}ない</code> also has the escape hatch
<code>～ことこの{f|上|うえ}ない</code>
（{f|嬉|うれ}しいことこの{f|上|うえ}ない）, which
<code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> does not.

### ～{f|極|きわ}まりない vs ～の{f|極|きわ}み

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - grammar first: <code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> follows an
**adjective stem** and ends the sentence like an adjective;
<code>の{f|極|きわ}み</code> follows a **noun** and *is* a noun. Then register:
<code>の{f|極|きわ}み</code> is ceremonial and often describes the speaker's own
state.

</div>

<pre>
{f|警備|けいび}の{f|甘|あま}さは{f|無責任|むせきにん}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>。
The laxness of the security is the height of irresponsibility.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|長年|ながねん}の{f|仲間|なかま}を{f|失|うしな}い、{f|痛恨|つうこん}<b>の{f|極|きわ}み</b>です。
Losing a colleague of many years is a matter of the deepest regret.
</pre>

The second sentence is a speaker reporting their own grief in public, and
<code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> could not replace it. The first is a verdict on
somebody else's work, and <code>の{f|極|きわ}み</code> would sound like a eulogy for
the security guards.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|危険|きけん}な<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b> → ✅ {f|危険|きけん}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>
  The base is the bare stem. The な that a な-adjective normally needs before a
  noun is not used here.

- ❌ {f|嬉|うれ}しい<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b> → ✅ {f|嬉|うれ}しい{f|限|かぎ}りだ／{f|嬉|うれ}しいことこの{f|上|うえ}ない
  い-adjectives do not take this pattern at all, and a positive feeling wants a
  different construction anyway.

- ❌ この{f|状況|じょうきょう}は{f|危険|きけん}{f|極|きわ}まりない<b>だ</b>。 → ✅ この{f|状況|じょうきょう}は{f|危険|きけん}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>。
  <code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> is already the predicate; adding だ is like
  writing ❌{f|高|たか}いだ.

- ❌ {f|合格|ごうかく}して<b>{f|感|かん}{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>。 → ✅ {f|合格|ごうかく}して<b>{f|感|かん}{f|極|きわ}まった</b>。
  <code>{f|感|かん}{f|極|きわ}まる</code> is a fixed verb and has no
  <code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> counterpart.

- ❌ ホテルの{f|部屋|へや}は{f|快適|かいてき}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりなかった</b>。 → ✅ ホテルの{f|部屋|へや}は{f|快適|かいてき}この{f|上|うえ}なかった。
  Grammatically well formed, and still wrong: the pattern is a criticism, so
  praising a hotel room with it reads as sarcasm.

## Practice

**1.** {f|説明|せつめい}も{f|謝罪|しゃざい}もないまま{f|工事|こうじ}を{f|始|はじ}めるとは、{f|非常識|ひじょうしき}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|非常識|ひじょうしき}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>（or {f|非常識|ひじょうしき}{f|極|きわ}まる{f|行為|こうい}だ）

{f|非常識|ひじょうしき} is a な-adjective, so the stem attaches directly with no な,
and the sentence is a verdict on someone else's behaviour - the pattern's home
ground.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|孫|まご}の{f|成長|せいちょう}を{f|見|み}られて、{f|嬉|うれ}しい（{f|限|かぎ}りです・{f|極|きわ}まりないです）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|嬉|うれ}しい<b>{f|限|かぎ}りです</b>。

Two reasons. <code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> cannot follow an い-adjective, and
even if the adjective were swapped for a な-adjective the pattern would be wrong
here: this is the speaker's own happy feeling, which is
<code>～{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code> territory.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Cancelling on the morning of the event with no word of apology is thoroughly discourteous."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|謝罪|しゃざい}の{f|言葉|ことば}もなく{f|当日|とうじつ}の{f|朝|あさ}にキャンセルするのは、{f|失礼|しつれい}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>。

"Thoroughly discourteous" is exactly what the pattern is for: a critical
な-adjective stem plus <code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code>, with no だ at the end.
{f|無礼|ぶれい}{f|極|きわ}まりない is also fine and is harsher again.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: その{f|対応|たいおう}は{f|不誠実|ふせいじつ}な{f|極|きわ}まりないだと{f|思|おも}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ その{f|対応|たいおう}は{f|不誠実|ふせいじつ}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>と{f|思|おも}います。
"I think that response was utterly insincere."

Two repairs: drop the な before the pattern, and drop the だ after it, since
<code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> already behaves as an い-adjective. Note that the
と{f|思|おも}います frame is what softens an otherwise very sharp line.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|返事|へんじ}は{f|失礼|しつれい}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|返事|へんじ}は{f|失礼|しつれい}この{f|上|うえ}ない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both say the reply was as rude as a reply can be, and either could appear in a
written complaint. (a) is the angrier and the more formal: it reads as a
judgement being placed on record, and it is the version you would expect in a
newspaper column or a letter from a lawyer.

(b) tops out the same scale with less heat. <code>この{f|上|うえ}ない</code> is a
neutral superlative that also works with pleasant words
（この{f|上|うえ}ない{f|光栄|こうえい}）, so in (b) the severity comes from
{f|失礼|しつれい} alone rather than from the construction as well.

</details>
