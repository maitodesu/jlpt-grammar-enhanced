# ～(よ)うが○○まいが / ～(よ)うと○○まいと

**Meaning** · whether one does it or not - both branches lead to the same place, so the choice is irrelevant

The same verb appears twice: once in the volitional, once in `まい`, which is the
**negative volitional** - "won't / shall not". Put the two side by side and you
have covered the entire field, yes and no, with nothing left over. The main clause
then holds no matter which branch turns out to be true.

## Formation

`が` and `と` are interchangeable, but the two halves must **match**: `が…が` or
`と…と`, never one of each.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | volitional + が／と ＋ dictionary + まい | {f|降|ふ}る → {f|降|ふ}ろうが{f|降|ふ}るまいが |
| Verb (る-verb) | volitional ＋ dictionary or stem + まい | {f|信|しん}じる → {f|信|しん}じようと{f|信|しん}じまいと |
| Verb (する) | しよう ＋ しまい／すまい／するまい | {f|参加|さんか}する → {f|参加|さんか}しようが{f|参加|さんか}しまいが |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | {f|来|こ}よう ＋ {f|来|こ}まい／{f|来|く}るまい | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ようと{f|来|こ}まいと |
| ある | あろう ＋ あるまい | ある → あろうがあるまいが |
| Noun / な-adjective | ～であろうと ＋ ～ではなかろうと | {f|会員|かいいん} → {f|会員|かいいん}であろうとなかろうと |

<div class="note">

**Note** - る-verbs allow two shapes of `まい`. <code>{f|信|しん}じまい</code>
(stem) is the older and more literary one; <code>{f|信|しん}じるまい</code>
(dictionary form) is what most people write today. Both are accepted, and the pair
reads naturally either way.

</div>

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the verb has to be the **same verb** on both sides. This is a
yes-or-no fork, not a list of two different activities. ❌<code>{f|行|い}こうが{f|休|やす}むまいが</code>
is not a possible sentence.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が<b>{f|降|ふ}ろうが{f|降|ふ}るまいが</b>、{f|明日|あした}は{f|出|で}かけます。
Whether it rains or not, I am going out tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}が<b>{f|来|こ}ようと{f|来|こ}まいと</b>、{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|時間|じかん}どおりに{f|始|はじ}めます。
Whether he turns up or not, the meeting starts on time.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|信|しん}じようが{f|信|しん}じまいが</b>、これは{f|実際|じっさい}にあったことだ。
Believe it or not, this actually happened.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本人|ほんにん}が<b>{f|望|のぞ}もうと{f|望|のぞ}むまいと</b>、うわさは{f|広|ひろ}がっていくものだ。
Whether the person wants it or not, a rumour spreads all the same.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|当日|とうじつ}<b>{f|参加|さんか}しようが{f|参加|さんか}しまいが</b>、{f|会費|かいひ}は{f|払|はら}う{f|決|き}まりになっている。
Whether you show up on the day or not, the rules say you pay the fee.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|売|う}れようが{f|売|う}れまいが</b>、{f|自分|じぶん}が{f|作|つく}りたいものを{f|作|つく}り{f|続|つづ}けたい。
Sell or not sell, I want to go on making what I want to make.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|周囲|しゅうい}が<b>{f|認|みと}めようと{f|認|みと}めまいと</b>、{f|彼女|かのじょ}が{f|残|のこ}した{f|功績|こうせき}は{f|消|き}えない。
Whether the people around her acknowledge it or not, what she achieved does not vanish.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|景気|けいき}が<b>{f|回復|かいふく}しようがしまいが</b>、{f|来年度|らいねんど}の{f|予算|よさん}は{f|削|けず}られることになりそうだ。
Whether or not the economy picks up, next year's budget looks set to be cut.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a **formal, emphatic** construction, and it belongs to writing, speeches,
editorials and firm statements in meetings. The everyday version of the same idea
is <code>{f|降|ふ}っても{f|降|ふ}らなくても</code>, and that is what you say to a
friend.

The `まい` half is worth pausing on. On its own, `まい` is the classical negative
volitional - <code>もう{f|言|い}うまい</code>, "I shall say no more" - and in
modern speech that use is genuinely archaic: it survives in novels, in older
characters' dialogue, and in the set phrase
<code>あるまいし</code>. Inside this doubled frame, though, `まい` is alive and
well, because there is no other way to build the shape. Recognise the two uses as
the same word wearing different clothes.

The attitude the pattern carries is **indifference bordering on dismissal**. It
says the other side's decision has no purchase on what happens next, which can be
brave (「{f|売|う}れようが{f|売|う}れまいが」) or cold, depending on whose choice
you are brushing aside. Used about a person standing in front of you - 「{f|君|きみ}が{f|来|こ}ようと{f|来|こ}まいと」 -
it is blunt, and will be heard that way.

## Contrast

### ～(よ)うが○○まいが vs ～ても～なくても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register.
<code>～ても～なくても</code> is ordinary Japanese; the volitional-plus-`まい`
version is formal and considerably more emphatic.

</div>

<pre>
{f|行|い}っ<b>ても{f|行|い}かなくても</b>、{f|結果|けっか}は{f|同|おな}じだよ。
Whether you go or not, the result's the same.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|行|い}こ<b>うが{f|行|い}くまいが</b>、{f|結果|けっか}は{f|変|か}わらない。
Go or do not go - the result will not change.
</pre>

The first could be said across a kitchen table. The second sounds like a verdict.
When you are not sure which to use in speech, the `ても～なくても` version is never
wrong.

### ～(よ)うが○○まいが vs ～かどうかにかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>かどうかにかかわらず</code> is **neutral and
administrative**: it states that a condition is not a factor.
<code>(よ)うが○○まいが</code> is **personal and emphatic**: it brushes the
condition aside.

</div>

<pre>
{f|経験|けいけん}が<b>あるかどうかにかかわらず</b>、{f|応募|おうぼ}できます。
You may apply regardless of whether you have experience.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|経験|けいけん}が<b>あろうがあるまいが</b>、やる{f|気|き}がなければ{f|続|つづ}かない。
Experience or no experience, without motivation nobody lasts.
</pre>

Swap them and you can hear the problem. In a job advertisement the volitional
version sounds as though the company is arguing with the reader; in the second
sentence <code>かかわらず</code> would drain out the speaker's opinion, which was
the whole point.

### ～(よ)うが○○まいが vs ～(よ)うが on its own

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the doubled form covers exactly **two** branches, yes and no.
The single form covers an **open range**, and normally has a question word or a
scale adverb in front of it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|何|なに}を<b>{f|言|い}おうと</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|耳|みみ}を{f|貸|か}さないだろう。
Whatever you say, he will not listen.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|意見|いけん}を<b>{f|言|い}おうと{f|言|い}うまいと</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|耳|みみ}を{f|貸|か}さないだろう。
Whether you voice an opinion or keep quiet, he will not listen.
</pre>

The first sentence is about the content of what you say and admits infinitely many
fillings. The second is about a single binary decision - speak, or do not speak.
If you can complete the sentence with a question word, use the single form.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|行|い}こうが<b>{f|行|い}かないが</b> → ✅ {f|行|い}こうが<b>{f|行|い}くまいが</b>
  The negative half is built with `まい`, not with the plain negative. A
  <code>ない</code> here leaves the `が` with nothing to do.

- ❌ {f|行|い}こう<b>が</b>{f|行|い}くまい<b>と</b> → ✅ {f|行|い}こう<b>が</b>{f|行|い}くまい<b>が</b>
  Choose `が` or `と` and use the same one twice.

- ❌ <b>{f|来|き}ようが{f|来|き}まいが</b> → ✅ <b>{f|来|こ}ようが{f|来|こ}まいが</b>
  {f|来|く}る is irregular. Both halves take the こ stem here; the alternative
  negative is <code>{f|来|く}るまい</code>, with the dictionary form intact.

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>だろうが{f|学生|がくせい}じゃあるまいが</b> → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>であろうとなかろうと</b>
  Nouns do not take `まい`. They use
  <code>であろうとなかろうと</code>, built on <code>である</code> and its negative.

- ❌ {f|来|こ}ようが{f|来|こ}まいが、ぜひ{f|来|き}てください。 → ✅ {f|時間|じかん}があれば、ぜひ{f|来|き}てください。
  The pattern declares the branch irrelevant, so it cannot be followed by an
  invitation that depends on that very branch.

## Practice

**1.** {f|試合|しあい}に____、この{f|一年|いちねん}の{f|練習|れんしゅう}は{f|無駄|むだ}にならない。（{f|勝|か}つ）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|試合|しあい}に<b>{f|勝|か}とうが{f|勝|か}つまいが</b>（or {f|勝|か}とうと{f|勝|か}つまいと）

<code>{f|勝|か}つ</code> is an う-verb, so the volitional is
<code>{f|勝|か}とう</code> and the `まい` half keeps the dictionary form. The main
clause holds on both branches, which is what the pattern requires.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|会員|かいいん}（であろうとなかろうと・であろうがあるまいが）、{f|入場|にゅうじょう}{f|料|りょう}は{f|同|おな}じです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会員|かいいん}<b>であろうとなかろうと</b>、{f|入場|にゅうじょう}{f|料|りょう}は{f|同|おな}じです。

<code>{f|会員|かいいん}</code> is a noun, and nouns pair
<code>であろう</code> with <code>なかろう</code>. The `まい` form belongs to verbs.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Whether the client agrees or not, we cannot change the delivery date."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|先方|せんぽう}が<b>{f|納得|なっとく}しようがしまいが</b>、{f|納期|のうき}を{f|変|か}えることはできません。

<code>{f|納得|なっとく}する</code> is a する-verb, so the pair is
<code>しよう</code> plus <code>しまい</code>. The formal register of the pattern
suits a sentence about contractual terms.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}が{f|謝|あやま}ろうが{f|許|ゆる}すまいが、{f|私|わたし}の{f|気持|きも}ちは{f|変|か}わらない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}が<b>{f|謝|あやま}ろうが{f|謝|あやま}るまいが</b>、{f|私|わたし}の{f|気持|きも}ちは{f|変|か}わらない。

The two halves must be the same verb; as written, the sentence forks between "he
apologises" and "someone does not forgive", which is not a fork at all. If you
really want two different actions, use
<code>{f|謝|あやま}ろうと{f|黙|だま}っていようと</code> - the doubled
<code>(よ)うと</code> without `まい`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|参加|さんか}し<b>てもしなくても</b>、{f|連絡|れんらく}だけはください。
(b) {f|参加|さんか}し<b>ようがしまいが</b>、{f|連絡|れんらく}だけはください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a normal, friendly request. It simply removes the condition: come or don't
come, just let me know either way.

(b) says the same thing with a hard edge. The formality of the volitional pair
makes the sentence sound like a rule being laid down, and the dismissive flavour of
the construction - your decision does not matter to me, only the message does - can
read as irritation. To a colleague, (a) is almost always the right choice.

</details>
