# ～んばかりに

**Meaning** · as if about to; all but - it looked exactly as though X were going to happen, and it did not

<code>んばかり</code> is the classical `む` ("would, is about to") + ばかり ("just
about"). It describes an **appearance**: the branches looked as though they would
snap, the hall looked as though it would burst. The crucial point is that the thing
does not actually happen. This is descriptive written Japanese - narration,
reportage, scene-setting. Read it as <code>{f|今|いま}にも～そう</code>; write that
instead.

## Formation

The base is the ない-stem (the classical {f|未然形|みぜんけい}), the same base
<code>んがため</code> takes.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ない-stem + んばかり | {f|泣|な}かない → {f|泣|な}かんばかり |
| Verb (る-verb) | ない-stem + んばかり | あふれない → あふれんばかり |
| Verb (する) | <b>せんばかり</b> | {f|爆発|ばくはつ}しない → {f|爆発|ばくはつ}せんばかり |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | <b>{f|来|こ}んばかり</b> (rare) | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}んばかり |
| Adverbial, before a verb | ～んばかりに | {f|泣|な}かんばかりに{f|頼|たの}んだ |
| Before a noun | ～んばかりの | {f|割|わ}れんばかりの{f|拍手|はくしゅ} |
| Sentence-final | ～んばかりだ / ～んばかりであった | あふれんばかりだった |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the collocations are half the pattern. Four are so common they are worth
learning as vocabulary: <code>{f|割|わ}れんばかりの{f|拍手|はくしゅ}</code>
(thunderous applause), <code>あふれんばかりの{f|笑顔|えがお}</code> (a beaming
smile), <code>{f|泣|な}かんばかりに</code> (almost in tears), and
<code>{f|言|い}わんばかりに</code> (as if to say).

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|割|わ}れ<b>んばかりの</b>{f|拍手|はくしゅ}が{f|起|お}こった。
Applause broke out loud enough to bring the roof down.
</pre>

<pre>
あふれ<b>んばかりの</b>{f|笑顔|えがお}で{f|迎|むか}えてくれた。
She greeted us with a smile that was almost spilling over.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|泣|な}か<b>んばかりに</b>{f|頭|あたま}を{f|下|さ}げた。
She bowed her head, on the very edge of tears.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|枝|えだ}は{f|折|お}れ<b>んばかりに</b>{f|実|み}をつけていた。
The branches were so laden with fruit that they looked ready to snap.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|人|ひと}であふれ、{f|壁|かべ}が{f|押|お}し{f|出|だ}され<b>んばかり</b>であった。
The hall was so packed that the walls themselves seemed about to give way.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|少年|しょうねん}は{f|飛|と}び{f|上|あ}がら<b>んばかりに</b>{f|喜|よろこ}んだ。
The boy was so delighted he all but leapt into the air.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|老人|ろうじん}は{f|何|なに}か{f|言|い}わ<b>んばかりに</b>{f|口|くち}を{f|開|ひら}きかけたが、{f|結局|けっきょく}{f|黙|だま}ったままだった。
The old man opened his mouth as though about to say something, but in the end kept his silence.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}の{f|知|し}らせに、{f|町|まち}じゅうが{f|沸|わ}き{f|返|かえ}ら<b>んばかりの</b>{f|騒|さわ}ぎとなった。
The news of the championship set off an uproar that seemed to lift the whole town off the ground.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**You will never say this.** <code>んばかり</code> is descriptive prose: novels,
essays, feature journalism, match reports, the caption under a photograph. Nobody
uses it in conversation. The spoken equivalent is
<code>{f|今|いま}にも{f|泣|な}きそうだった</code>,
<code>{f|落|お}ちそうなくらい</code>, <code>すごい{f|拍手|はくしゅ}だった</code> -
and those are what you should be producing.

The single most important thing about the pattern is that **the event does not
occur**. <code>{f|泣|な}かんばかりに{f|頼|たの}んだ</code> means she begged
almost in tears - she did not cry. If she cried, the sentence is
<code>{f|泣|な}きながら{f|頼|たの}んだ</code>. Learners regularly use
<code>んばかり</code> for things that did happen, which is not a matter of degree
but a factual error.

It is also **visual**. <code>んばかり</code> reports what an observer could see or
hear at that moment: a face, a crowd, a gesture, a sound. It does not work for a
forecast (❌{f|明日|あした}{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}らんばかりだ), for an internal
state nobody could observe, or for a general tendency. If you cannot point to the
scene, the pattern does not apply.

<code>{f|言|い}わんばかりに</code> deserves separate mention. It is by far the
commonest use, and it means "as if to say" - the person did not speak, but their
manner said it for them:
<code>{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}れと{f|言|い}わんばかりに{f|時計|とけい}を{f|見|み}た</code>.
Note that it needs a quoted phrase and と in front of it, which is where it meets -
and gets confused with - <code>とばかりに</code>.

## Contrast

### ～んばかりに vs {f|今|いま}にも～そう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning, different medium.
<code>{f|今|いま}にも～そうだ</code> is what you say; <code>んばかり</code> is what
a writer prints.

</div>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもは<b>{f|今|いま}にも{f|泣|な}きそう</b>な{f|顔|かお}をしていた。
The child looked as though she was about to burst into tears.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもは{f|泣|な}き{f|出|だ}さ<b>んばかりの</b>{f|顔|かお}で{f|立|た}っていた。
The child stood there with a face on the very brink of tears.
</pre>

Note the bases. `そう` follows the ます-stem ({f|泣|な}き<b>そう</b>);
<code>んばかり</code> follows the ない-stem ({f|泣|な}か<b>んばかり</b>). If you are
writing rather than reading, use the first: it is shorter, universally understood,
and carries no risk of sounding like a nineteenth-century novel.

### ～んばかりに vs ～とばかりに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>んばかり</code> describes an **appearance**: it looked as
though it would happen. <code>とばかりに</code> reports an **unspoken message**: his
manner said "now!".

</div>

<pre>
{f|皿|さら}は{f|落|お}ち<b>んばかりに</b>{f|棚|たな}のふちに{f|乗|の}っていた。
The plate sat on the edge of the shelf, on the point of falling.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}だ<b>とばかりに</b>、{f|犬|いぬ}が{f|皿|さら}に{f|飛|と}びついた。
Seizing the moment, the dog lunged at the plate.
</pre>

The forms look alike because both end in ばかりに, but the pieces in front differ.
<code>んばかり</code> takes a **verb stem** ({f|落|お}ち + んばかり);
<code>とばかりに</code> takes a **quotation** with と ({f|今|いま}だ + とばかりに).
The bridge between them is <code>と{f|言|い}わんばかりに</code>, which is literally
"as if about to say ~" and is close in meaning to <code>とばかりに</code>.

### ～んばかりに vs ～ばかりに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a completely different word. `ばかりに` (N3) means "just
because ~, and it turned out badly". <code>んばかりに</code> means "as if about
to". Only the ん distinguishes them.

</div>

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}って{f|来|こ}なかった<b>ばかりに</b>、ずぶぬれになった。
Just because I hadn't brought an umbrella, I got soaked through.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|嵐|あらし}の{f|中|なか}を、{f|飛|と}ばされ<b>んばかりに</b>{f|歩|ある}いていた。
He walked on through the storm, looking as if he might be blown off his feet.
</pre>

Watch the base, which resolves it every time: `ばかりに` follows a **plain past or
plain form** ({f|来|こ}なかった<b>ばかりに</b>), while <code>んばかりに</code> follows
a **bare ない-stem** with no ない ({f|飛|と}ばされ<b>んばかりに</b>). If you can see a
complete verb form in front of it, it is the N3 pattern; if the verb looks
unfinished, it is this one.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|泣|な}く<b>んばかりに</b> → ✅ {f|泣|な}か<b>んばかりに</b>
  The base is the ない-stem. Produce {f|泣|な}かない first and drop the ない.

- ❌ {f|感謝|かんしゃ}<b>しんばかり</b> → ✅ {f|感謝|かんしゃ}<b>せんばかり</b>
  `する` gives せ before every classical ending of this kind - the same せ as in
  <code>せず</code> and <code>せんがため</code>.

- ❌ {f|涙|なみだ}があふれ<b>んばかりに</b>あふれた。 → ✅ {f|涙|なみだ}があふれ<b>んばかり</b>だった。
  The pattern says the thing *nearly* happened. Reporting that it then happened
  contradicts it.

- ❌ もう{f|帰|かえ}れ<b>んばかりに</b>、{f|店員|てんいん}が{f|片付|かたづ}け{f|始|はじ}めた。 → ✅ もう{f|帰|かえ}れ<b>と{f|言|い}わんばかりに</b>、{f|店員|てんいん}が{f|片付|かたづ}け{f|始|はじ}めた。
  A quoted phrase needs と{f|言|い}わんばかりに (or <code>とばかりに</code>).
  <code>んばかり</code> alone attaches to a verb stem, not to a quotation.

- ❌（{f|友|とも}だちに）お{f|腹|なか}が{f|割|わ}れ<b>んばかりに</b>{f|笑|わら}ったよ。 → ✅ お{f|腹|なか}が{f|痛|いた}くなるくらい{f|笑|わら}ったよ。
  Correctly built, but no one talks like this. In speech, ～くらい and
  {f|今|いま}にも do all the work.

## Practice

**1.** {f|試合|しあい}{f|後|ご}の{f|記事|きじ}: {f|逆転|ぎゃくてん}{f|勝利|しょうり}の{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}、スタジアムは{f|割|わ}れ____{f|歓声|かんせい}に{f|包|つつ}まれた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|割|わ}れ<b>んばかりの</b>{f|歓声|かんせい}

Before a noun the form is <code>んばかりの</code>, not
<code>んばかりに</code>. <code>{f|割|わ}れんばかりの</code> is one of the fixed
collocations, normally with {f|拍手|はくしゅ} or {f|歓声|かんせい} - and a match
report is exactly where you meet it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|部長|ぶちょう}は「もう{f|帰|かえ}れ」（とばかりに・んばかりに）{f|時計|とけい}を{f|見|み}た。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

「もう{f|帰|かえ}れ」<b>とばかりに</b>{f|時計|とけい}を{f|見|み}た。

What precedes is a quoted phrase, so it needs と. <code>んばかり</code> attaches to
a verb stem instead: the version with ん would have to be
「もう{f|帰|かえ}れ」と{f|言|い}わんばかりに, with {f|言|い}わ supplying the stem.

</details>

**3.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|彼|かれ}は{f|泣|な}か<b>んばかりの</b>{f|声|こえ}で{f|事情|じじょう}を{f|話|はな}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}は{f|今|いま}にも{f|泣|な}きそうな{f|声|こえ}で{f|事情|じじょう}を{f|話|はな}した。

<code>{f|今|いま}にも～そうだ</code> is the everyday equivalent, and note that the
base changes with it: `そう` takes the ます-stem {f|泣|な}き, while
<code>んばかり</code> took the ない-stem {f|泣|な}か.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|喜|よろこ}ぶんばかりに、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|何度|なんど}も{f|手|て}を{f|振|ふ}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|喜|よろこ}ば<b>んばかりに</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|何度|なんど}も{f|手|て}を{f|振|ふ}った。
More natural still: {f|飛|と}び{f|上|あ}がら<b>んばかりに</b>{f|喜|よろこ}んで、{f|何度|なんど}も{f|手|て}を{f|振|ふ}った。

The base must be the ない-stem {f|喜|よろこ}ば, not the dictionary form. The second
version is better Japanese because <code>んばかり</code> wants a **visible**
near-event - leaping into the air - rather than the emotion itself.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|母|はは}は{f|泣|な}か<b>んばかりに</b>{f|喜|よろこ}んだ。
(b) {f|母|はは}は{f|泣|な}きながら{f|喜|よろこ}んだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says she did **not** cry. She was visibly on the edge of it, and the writer is
describing what an onlooker would have seen. The sentence belongs to written
narration.

(b) says the tears came. It is plain modern Japanese and could be said out loud to
anyone. The two are not stronger and weaker versions of the same statement - they
report different facts, and choosing (a) when you mean (b) tells the reader
something untrue.

</details>
