# ゆえに

**Meaning** · therefore; hence; it follows that - the "therefore" of proofs, judgements and formal argument

{f|故|ゆえ}に stands at the head of a sentence and presents what follows as the
**necessary consequence** of what has just been stated. It is not "so" in the
conversational sense; it is the word a mathematician writes at the end of a proof
and a court writes before its ruling. Reach for it only when the step really is a
deduction, and only in writing that can carry the weight.

## Usage

Two shapes, and it is worth keeping them apart.

As a **conjunction**, ゆえに opens a sentence or follows a comma, with the premise in
front of it: <code>{f|前提|ぜんてい}が{f|正|ただ}しくない。ゆえに、{f|結論|けつろん}も{f|正|ただ}しくない。</code>
This is the sense the word is usually taught for, and it is the one you will meet in
philosophy, mathematics, logic textbooks, legal judgements and formal essays.
そのゆえに is not used; the plain ゆえに, or それゆえ / それゆえに, is the form.

As a **postposition**, it attaches to what caused something and means "because of".
A noun takes `の` or joins directly: <code>{f|貧|まず}しさゆえに</code>,
<code>{f|病気|びょうき}のゆえに</code>. A verb or adjective takes `が`:
<code>{f|若|わか}いがゆえに</code>, <code>{f|知|し}らなかったがゆえに</code>. The `が`
is the old genitive, the same one inside <code>わがくに</code>; without it a verb in
front of ゆえに sounds unfinished to a Japanese ear, so learn
<code>～がゆえに</code> as one unit.

The bare noun {f|故|ゆえ} - "reason, grounds" - survives in a handful of set phrases:
<code>{f|故|ゆえ}あって</code> ("for reasons of my own"),
<code>{f|何|なに}{f|故|ゆえ}</code> ("why", literary). The character is read **ゆえ**
here; the こ of {f|故人|こじん} and {f|故障|こしょう} is a different word entirely.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}れば{f|地面|じめん}は{f|濡|ぬ}れる。{f|地面|じめん}は{f|濡|ぬ}れていない。<b>ゆえに</b>、{f|雨|あめ}は{f|降|ふ}らなかった。
If it rains the ground gets wet. The ground is not wet. Therefore it did not rain.
</pre>

The data are insufficient; the conclusion is therefore reserved.
</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

ゆえに is **written, formal and faintly philosophical**. Spoken aloud in ordinary
conversation it sounds either pompous or like a joke - a student announcing
<code>ゆえに、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}く</code> is being funny on
purpose, and the humour depends on everyone recognising the register. In speech the
word for "therefore" is `だから`; in a business email it is `そのため`; in a report or
a paper it is `したがって`.

Among those neighbours the ordering is worth having. `だから` is spoken and neutral.
`そのため` states a practical cause and is the everyday written choice. `したがって`
is the standard connective of academic and business writing, and is what most
learners actually want when they reach for ゆえに. `よって` is a step stiffer and
belongs to formal documents, resolutions and mathematical proofs. ゆえに sits beside
`よって`, with an added flavour of pure reasoning: it is the word chosen for the
Japanese rendering of Descartes' cogito, and that association clings to it. These are
tendencies rather than boundaries, and a single essay may use several of them.

One consequence of that flavour: ゆえに wants a conclusion that **follows**, not one
that merely happened next. <code>ゆえに{f|明日|あした}は{f|晴|は}れるだろう</code> is
odd, because a guess about the weather is not deduced from anything. Where the link
is real-world cause and effect rather than inference, `そのため` is the honest word.

The `がゆえに` form is common in a slightly different place: writing about people and
society, where it introduces a quality that both explains and slightly excuses -
<code>{f|真面目|まじめ}であるがゆえに{f|苦|くる}しむ</code>. That use is still formal,
but it appears in newspaper columns and novels as well as in academic prose.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|疲|つか}れた。<b>ゆえに</b>、{f|今日|きょう}はもう{f|寝|ね}る。 → ✅ {f|疲|つか}れた。<b>だから</b>{f|今日|きょう}はもう{f|寝|ね}る。
  Nothing is being deduced and nobody is being addressed formally. ゆえに in a diary
  entry or a text message reads as a parody of scholarship.

- ❌ {f|経験|けいけん}が{f|少|すく}ない<b>ゆえに</b>、{f|失敗|しっぱい}も{f|多|おお}い。 → ✅ {f|経験|けいけん}が{f|少|すく}ない<b>がゆえに</b>、{f|失敗|しっぱい}も{f|多|おお}い。
  After a verb or an adjective the `が` is expected. Nouns are the exception:
  <code>{f|経験|けいけん}{f|不足|ぶそく}ゆえに</code> is fine as it stands.

- ❌ {f|事故|じこ}が{f|多|おお}い。<b>そのゆえに</b>、{f|信号|しんごう}が{f|設置|せっち}された。 → ✅ {f|事故|じこ}が{f|多|おお}い。<b>そのため</b>、{f|信号|しんごう}が{f|設置|せっち}された。
  ❌<code>そのゆえに</code> is not Japanese. The demonstrative form is
  <code>それゆえ</code> or <code>それゆえに</code>, and for a practical cause like this
  one `そのため` is the natural choice anyway.

- ❌ この{f|漢字|かんじ}は「こに」と{f|読|よ}む。 → ✅ この{f|漢字|かんじ}は「ゆえに」と{f|読|よ}む。
  The character <code>故</code> has two lives. In {f|故障|こしょう} and {f|事故|じこ}
  it is こ; standing alone before に it is **ゆえ**.

## Practice

**1.** Choose the natural connective: {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|接近|せっきん}しています。（ゆえに・そのため）、{f|明日|あした}の{f|見学|けんがく}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}といたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>そのため</b>、{f|明日|あした}の{f|見学|けんがく}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}といたします。

This is an announcement about a practical arrangement, not a deduction, and the
audience is being addressed politely rather than argued with. ゆえに would make a
cancellation notice sound like the closing line of a proof.

</details>

**2.** Translate into Japanese, in the register of a written argument: "All the witnesses gave the same account. It follows that the defendant's statement is untrue."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|証人|しょうにん}は{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|同|おな}じ{f|証言|しょうげん}をしている。<b>ゆえに</b>、{f|被告|ひこく}の{f|供述|きょうじゅつ}は{f|事実|じじつ}ではないと{f|考|かんが}えられる。

"It follows that" is a deduction from stated premises, which is exactly what ゆえに
marks, and the legal setting supports the formality. <code>したがって</code> would
also be correct and slightly less stiff.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|優|やさ}しい<b>ので</b>、{f|人|ひと}に{f|頼|たよ}られる。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|優|やさ}しい<b>がゆえに</b>、{f|損|そん}をすることが{f|多|おお}い。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain spoken statement of cause: he is kind, so people lean on him. It could
be said about a colleague over lunch and carries no particular attitude.

(b) is written, and the `がゆえに` frames his kindness as the very quality that works
against him. There is a note of "precisely because" in it, and often a hint of
sympathy or irony - the sentence is being composed rather than said.

</details>
