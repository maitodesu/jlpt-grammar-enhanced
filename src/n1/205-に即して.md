# ～に即して

**Meaning** · in keeping with; fitted to - the action or judgement is shaped to match a reality as it actually stands, rather than applied from a template

<code>{f|即|そく}する</code> means "to be right up against something, to fit it
exactly, to leave no gap". The noun in front is a **state of affairs**:
{f|実情|じつじょう}, {f|実態|じったい}, {f|現状|げんじょう}, {f|事実|じじつ},
{f|時代|じだい}, the particular case in hand. The sentence then says that what was
done was cut to the shape of that reality. The unspoken contrast is always with
something rigid and off-the-shelf - a rule applied blindly, a plan drawn up in an
office far from the site.

## Formation

<code>{f|即|そく}する</code> is a する-verb and marks the reality it fits with `に`.
The pattern takes a **noun** only; a clause has to be packaged into one first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + に{f|即|そく}して | {f|実情|じつじょう} → {f|実情|じつじょう}に{f|即|そく}して |
| Noun (stiffer, documents) | Noun + に{f|即|そく}し | {f|現状|げんじょう} → {f|現状|げんじょう}に{f|即|そく}し |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + に{f|即|そく}した + Noun | {f|実態|じったい} → {f|実態|じったい}に{f|即|そく}した{f|運用|うんよう} |
| Same, non-past shape | Noun + に{f|即|そく}する + Noun | {f|時代|じだい} → {f|時代|じだい}に{f|即|そく}する{f|教育|きょういく} |
| As the predicate | Noun + に{f|即|そく}している | {f|事実|じじつ} → {f|事実|じじつ}に{f|即|そく}している |
| Negative | Noun + に{f|即|そく}さない + Noun | {f|実情|じつじょう} → {f|実情|じつじょう}に{f|即|そく}さない{f|規則|きそく} |
| Nominalised clause | ～という + Noun + に{f|即|そく}して | {f|事実|じじつ} → {f|人口|じんこう}が{f|減|へ}ったという{f|事実|じじつ}に{f|即|そく}して |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the spelling. <code>即</code> is the kanji for fitting reality:
{f|事実|じじつ}に{f|即|そく}した, {f|現場|げんば}に{f|即|そく}した. Some publishers'
style guides use <code>則</code> - the kanji of {f|規則|きそく} - when the thing fitted
is a law or a norm rather than a reality, and the reading stays そくして either way.
Unless you are copy-editing to a house style, write <code>即</code> for realities and use
<code>に{f|則|のっと}って</code> when you mean obeying a rule; that keeps the two
apart cleanly.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|現状|げんじょう}<b>に{f|即|そく}して</b>{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|見|み}{f|直|なお}す。
We will revise the plan to fit the situation as it now stands.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|説明|せつめい}は{f|事実|じじつ}<b>に{f|即|そく}していない</b>。
That account does not match the facts.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|法律|ほうりつ}は{f|時代|じだい}<b>に{f|即|そく}した</b>{f|形|かたち}に{f|改|あらた}められるべきだ。
The law ought to be amended into a form that fits the times.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|地域|ちいき}の{f|実情|じつじょう}<b>に{f|即|そく}した</b>{f|支援|しえん}が{f|求|もと}められている。
What is called for is support tailored to conditions in each locality.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|規則|きそく}をそのまま{f|当|あ}てはめるのではなく、{f|個々|ここ}の{f|事情|じじょう}<b>に{f|即|そく}して</b>{f|判断|はんだん}します。
Rather than applying the rule as it stands, we judge each case on its actual circumstances.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|現場|げんば}の{f|実態|じったい}<b>に{f|即|そく}さない</b>{f|指示|しじ}は、{f|結局|けっきょく}{f|守|まも}られない。
Instructions that do not match conditions on the ground end up being ignored.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|客様|きゃくさま}のご{f|事情|じじょう}<b>に{f|即|そく}して</b>、{f|返済|へんさい}{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|組|く}み{f|直|なお}すことも{f|可能|かのう}です。
We can also rebuild the repayment plan around your individual circumstances.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}は、{f|現地|げんち}で{f|見|み}てきたこと<b>に{f|即|そく}して</b>{f|書|か}かれている。
This report is written to match what was actually seen on site.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Like its neighbours in this group, `に即して` lives in official prose: policy papers,
committee reports, administrative guidance, contracts, the speech a director gives
when announcing a change of course. It is not conversational. Where a report writes
<code>{f|実情|じつじょう}に{f|即|そく}した{f|対応|たいおう}</code>, a colleague in the
corridor says <code>{f|実際|じっさい}の{f|様子|ようす}を{f|見|み}て{f|決|き}める</code>
or simply <code>{f|現場|げんば}に{f|合|あ}わせて</code>. That last one -
<code>～に{f|合|あ}わせて</code> - is the everyday equivalent to reach for.

The pattern carries an argumentative edge that the dictionary gloss hides. Saying
<code>{f|実態|じったい}に{f|即|そく}した{f|制度|せいど}が{f|必要|ひつよう}だ</code> is a
polite way of saying the present system is detached from reality. It is a standard
move in Japanese policy writing, and a reader hears the criticism even though no one
has been named.

The `に即し` form belongs to written documents; `に即して` is what gets read aloud.
There is no casual contraction, and no form of this that sounds natural in speech
between friends.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|即|そく}して vs ～に{f|則|のっと}って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に{f|即|そく}して</code> fits itself to a **reality**.
<code>～に{f|則|のっと}って</code> obeys a **rule**. Reality can be untidy; a rule
cannot.

</div>

<pre>
{f|処分|しょぶん}は{f|就業|しゅうぎょう}{f|規則|きそく}<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>{f|決|き}めた。
The disciplinary measure was decided in accordance with the work rules.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|処分|しょぶん}は{f|本人|ほんにん}の{f|事情|じじょう}<b>に{f|即|そく}して</b>{f|決|き}めた。
The disciplinary measure was decided in the light of his actual circumstances.
</pre>

These two sentences describe opposite defences. The first says the outcome was not
the speaker's choice - the rulebook produced it. The second says the rulebook was
not applied mechanically. Notice that they can even conflict:
<code>{f|実情|じつじょう}に{f|即|そく}して</code> is often precisely a decision *not*
to follow the standard procedure to the letter.

### ～に{f|即|そく}して vs ～に{f|基|もと}づいて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に{f|基|もと}づいて</code> **derives** the result from an
authority or a body of evidence. <code>～に{f|即|そく}して</code> **shapes** the result
so that it fits a situation.

</div>

<pre>
{f|調査|ちょうさ}{f|結果|けっか}<b>に{f|基|もと}づいて</b>{f|新|あたら}しい{f|基準|きじゅん}を{f|作|つく}った。
We drew up the new standard on the basis of the survey findings.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|現場|げんば}の{f|状況|じょうきょう}<b>に{f|即|そく}した</b>{f|基準|きじゅん}に{f|作|つく}り{f|直|なお}した。
We rewrote the standard so that it fits conditions on the ground.
</pre>

The first sentence tells you where the content came from; the second tells you what
the content now matches. That is why <code>{f|基|もと}づいて</code> naturally takes
data, laws and agreements, and <code>{f|即|そく}して</code> naturally takes
{f|実情|じつじょう}, {f|実態|じったい} and {f|現状|げんじょう}. Neither list is a hard
boundary, but the direction of the relation is.

### ～に{f|即|そく}して vs ～に{f|応|おう}じて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～に{f|応|おう}じて</code> **varies** as the noun varies:
change the input, change the output. <code>～に{f|即|そく}して</code> stays
**faithful** to one situation, without any implication of a scale.

</div>

<pre>
{f|年齢|ねんれい}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|料金|りょうきん}が{f|変|か}わります。
The fee varies according to age.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|利用者|りようしゃ}の{f|実態|じったい}<b>に{f|即|そく}して</b>{f|料金|りょうきん}{f|体系|たいけい}を{f|見|み}{f|直|なお}しました。
We revised the fee structure to match how users actually use the service.
</pre>

<code>{f|応|おう}じて</code> needs something that comes in degrees or types - age,
income, the season, the size of an order. <code>{f|即|そく}して</code> needs a single
concrete reality to be true to. They overlap in phrases like
<code>{f|場合|ばあい}に{f|応|おう}じて</code> and
<code>ケースに{f|即|そく}して</code>, but even there the first means "it depends" and
the second means "we look at what the case actually is".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}のご{f|希望|きぼう}<b>に{f|即|そく}して</b>{f|日程|にってい}を{f|決|き}めます。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}のご{f|希望|きぼう}<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|日程|にってい}を{f|決|き}めます。
  A wish is not a state of affairs. Wishes, plans and policies are lines you keep to,
  which is <code>{f|沿|そ}って</code>.

- ❌ {f|実情|じつじょう}<b>を{f|即|そく}して</b> → ✅ {f|実情|じつじょう}<b>に{f|即|そく}して</b>
  <code>{f|即|そく}する</code> is intransitive here and takes `に`. The `を` version is
  a straightforward particle slip, and it is the most common one on this pattern.

- ❌ {f|昔話|むかしばなし}<b>に{f|即|そく}して</b>{f|映画|えいが}を{f|作|つく}った。 → ✅ {f|昔話|むかしばなし}<b>をもとに</b>{f|映画|えいが}を{f|作|つく}った。
  A folk tale is raw material you rework, not a reality you conform to. Use
  <code>をもとに</code> when something new is being built out of the noun.

- ❌ {f|現状|げんじょう}に<b>{f|即|そっ}して</b> → ✅ {f|現状|げんじょう}に<b>{f|即|そく}して</b>
  Before し the reading is そく, with no gemination. <code>即</code> does geminate in
  {f|即決|そっけつ} and {f|即興|そっきょう} - before k-sounds - which is where the
  wrong reading comes from.

## Practice

**1.** {f|外国人|がいこくじん}{f|住民|じゅうみん}が{f|増|ふ}えているので、{f|窓口|まどぐち}の{f|対応|たいおう}も{f|地域|ちいき}の{f|実情|じつじょう}____{f|変|か}えていく{f|必要|ひつよう}がある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|地域|ちいき}の{f|実情|じつじょう}<b>に{f|即|そく}して</b>{f|変|か}えていく

{f|実情|じつじょう} is the archetypal noun for this pattern, and the sentence is
arguing that the current counter service does not fit what the area is actually like.
<code>に{f|即|そく}した{f|対応|たいおう}に{f|変|か}えていく</code> would also work.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|判決|はんけつ}は{f|事実|じじつ}（に{f|即|そく}している・に{f|応|おう}じている）とは{f|言|い}えない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|事実|じじつ}<b>に{f|即|そく}している</b>とは{f|言|い}えない。

The complaint is that the ruling does not match what actually happened - a question
of faithfulness to one reality, not of varying with a scale.
<code>{f|応|おう}じて</code> would need something gradable like the severity of the
offence.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Rules drawn up thirty years ago no longer fit the way people work today."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|三十年|さんじゅうねん}{f|前|まえ}に{f|作|つく}られた{f|規則|きそく}は、もう{f|今|いま}の{f|働|はたら}き{f|方|かた}<b>に{f|即|そく}していない</b>。

The predicate form <code>に{f|即|そく}していない</code> is the natural way to say
that something has come adrift from reality, and it is a stock phrase in reports
arguing for reform.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|規則|きそく}に{f|即|そく}して{f|処分|しょぶん}が{f|決|き}められたので、{f|文句|もんく}は{f|言|い}えない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|規則|きそく}<b>に{f|則|のっと}って</b>{f|処分|しょぶん}が{f|決|き}められたので、{f|文句|もんく}は{f|言|い}えない。

The second half - "so you cannot complain" - only follows if the rulebook was
*obeyed*, and that is <code>{f|則|のっと}って</code>. With
<code>{f|即|そく}して</code> the sentence would be saying the rules were bent to fit
the case, which is exactly what someone might complain about.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|現場|げんば}の{f|意見|いけん}<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b>{f|制度|せいど}を{f|設計|せっけい}した。
(b) {f|現場|げんば}の{f|実態|じったい}<b>に{f|即|そく}して</b>{f|制度|せいど}を{f|設計|せっけい}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the views of the people on the ground were heard and taken into account
before designing the system. It is a claim about process, and it leaves open how much
of what they said survived into the design.

(b) says the finished system matches what actually happens on the ground. It is a
claim about the result. You can honestly say (a) about a system that turned out to be
unworkable; you cannot say (b) about one.

</details>
