# いずれにせよ

**Meaning** · whichever it turns out to be; at any rate; in any case

`いずれにせよ` is a connective that does one precise job: it takes a set of
possibilities that has just been laid out, declares that the choice between them does
not matter, and states the thing that holds **on every branch**. It does not resolve
the question - it retires it.

## Usage

A sentence connective. It stands at the head of the sentence, or of the second clause,
and is normally followed by a comma.

It is built out of いずれ ("which of them") + `に` + せよ, the literary imperative of
する - the same せよ as in なんにせよ and {f|何|なに}をするにせよ. That imperative is
concessive: "let it be whichever it likes".

**It needs alternatives in front of it.** This is the part learners miss. `いずれにせよ`
is not a general-purpose "anyway"; without two or more possibilities already in the
listener's mind it has nothing to sweep aside, and the sentence sounds like the answer
to a question nobody asked. Those possibilities can be supplied in three ways:

- Spelled out as a disjunction: <code>～か～か</code>, <code>～にせよ～にせよ</code>,
  <code>～であれ～であれ</code>.
- Left open as an uncertainty:
  {f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}うかどうかは{f|分|わ}からないが、いずれにせよ…
- Left implicit because two people have just been disagreeing about them.

What follows is a **conclusion, an obligation or a decision** - the invariant. Typical
endings are {f|必要|ひつよう}がある, ～なければならない,
<code>ことに{f|変|か}わりはない</code>, ～だろう.

The same expression comes in a register ladder, and they are otherwise
interchangeable: <code>いずれにせよ</code> and <code>いずれにしろ</code> are the formal
written pair, <code>いずれにしても</code> is neutral and usable aloud, and
<code>どちらにしても</code>, <code>どっちにしても</code> and <code>どっちみち</code>
are casual. Note that the どちら forms allow only two options; the いずれ forms allow
any number.

## Nuance & register

This is a **written and formal** connective. Its home is reports, minutes, editorials,
business email and prepared remarks - anywhere an argument is being tidied up. In
speech, the same move is made with いずれにしても, or more casually with
どっちにしても, どっちみち or とにかく. A learner who says いずれにせよ over coffee
will be understood perfectly and will sound like a memo.

The せよ ending is what makes it formal, and it is worth knowing that this is a fossil
rather than a live imperative. Nobody is being told to do anything.

`いずれにせよ` also carries a mild **closing-down** force, and that is a social fact
worth watching. Because it announces that the preceding distinctions are irrelevant,
using it while someone senior is still setting out those distinctions can read as
impatience - "whatever the case, the fact remains…". In writing this is efficient; in
a meeting it can be sharp. A softer alternative when you do not want to cut anyone off
is どちらにしても or a simple ただ.

One trap has nothing to do with register. **いずれ on its own is a different word.** It
means "sometime, in due course" - いずれまたお{f|会|あ}いしましょう,
いずれ{f|分|わ}かることだ - and has no connective function at all. Dropping the
にせよ does not shorten the phrase; it replaces it.

## Contrast

### いずれにせよ vs とにかく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `いずれにせよ` says the conclusion **holds on every branch**, so
the branches can be set aside. とにかく says "never mind that for now" and pushes the
question away without claiming anything about it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|理由|りゆう}はあとで{f|聞|き}く。<b>とにかく</b>{f|病院|びょういん}へ{f|行|い}こう。
I'll hear the reasons later. Right now, let's just get to the hospital.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事故|じこ}か{f|故障|こしょう}か、<b>いずれにせよ</b>{f|運行|うんこう}の{f|再開|さいかい}には{f|時間|じかん}がかかる。
Accident or breakdown - either way, it will be a while before services resume.
</pre>

The first is a decision to postpone a question; the reasons still matter, just not
yet. The second is a claim about logic: whichever explanation turns out to be right,
the delay follows. とにかく is spoken, urgent and often a little impatient;
`いずれにせよ` is written, calm and argumentative. Only `いずれにせよ` requires
alternatives to have been raised, which is why とにかく can open a conversation and
`いずれにせよ` cannot.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ （{f|会話|かいわ}の{f|冒頭|ぼうとう}で）<b>いずれにせよ</b>、{f|明日|あした}は{f|九時|くじ}{f|集合|しゅうごう}です。 → ✅ <b>とにかく</b>{f|明日|あした}は{f|九時|くじ}{f|集合|しゅうごう}です。
  Opened cold, the phrase points back at alternatives that were never mentioned. It
  can only follow something.

- ❌ {f|中止|ちゅうし}か{f|延期|えんき}か、<b>いずれにせよ</b>まだ{f|決|き}まっていない。 → ✅ {f|中止|ちゅうし}か{f|延期|えんき}か、<b>いずれにせよ</b>{f|参加者|さんかしゃ}への{f|連絡|れんらく}は{f|必要|ひつよう}だ。
  What follows must be the thing that is true on both branches. Restating the
  uncertainty undoes the whole point of the connective.

- ❌ <b>いずれ</b>、この{f|案|あん}は{f|見直|みなお}すべきだ。 → ✅ <b>いずれにせよ</b>、この{f|案|あん}は{f|見直|みなお}すべきだ。
  Bare いずれ means "sometime", so the first sentence says the plan should be revised
  at some point in the future - a different claim entirely.

- ❌ （{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}に）<b>どっちみち</b>{f|追加|ついか}の{f|調査|ちょうさ}が{f|必要|ひつよう}である。 → ✅ （{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}に）<b>いずれにせよ</b>{f|追加|ついか}の{f|調査|ちょうさ}が{f|必要|ひつよう}である。
  The meaning is identical; the register is not. どっちみち in a written report is as
  jarring as "either way, whatever" in a formal English one.

## Practice

**1.** {f|自分|じぶん}で{f|直|なお}すか{f|業者|ぎょうしゃ}に{f|頼|たの}むか、____{f|費用|ひよう}はかかる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>いずれにせよ</b>{f|費用|ひよう}はかかる

The two options are laid out in front, and what follows is the thing that is true of
both of them. That is the shape the connective requires: alternatives, then the
invariant.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: {f|参加者|さんかしゃ}が{f|何人|なんにん}になるか{f|分|わ}かりませんが、いずれ{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|広|ひろ}めに{f|押|お}さえておきましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|参加者|さんかしゃ}が{f|何人|なんにん}になるか{f|分|わ}かりませんが、<b>いずれにせよ</b>{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|広|ひろ}めに{f|押|お}さえておきましょう。

Bare いずれ means "at some point in the future", which turns the sentence into a plan
to book a room eventually. The intended meaning - "however many people come" - needs
the full connective. In speech, いずれにしても would be the more comfortable choice.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) <b>とにかく</b>{f|一度|いちど}{f|会|あ}って{f|話|はな}しましょう。
(b) <b>いずれにせよ</b>{f|一度|いちど}{f|会|あ}って{f|話|はな}す{f|必要|ひつよう}があります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) brushes the discussion aside and proposes action. It is spoken, warm and slightly
impatient: whatever we have been arguing about, let's just meet. It works even if no
alternatives were on the table.

(b) reasons. It assumes the listener has raised two or more possible courses, and it
argues that a meeting is required under all of them. The register is that of an email
or a meeting summary, and the effect is to close the branch of the discussion rather
than to postpone it. Said to a superior mid-explanation, (b) can sound like an
interruption in a way (a) does not.

</details>
