# ～ではあるまいし

**Meaning** · it's not as if ～; it isn't as though you were ～ - dismisses a false premise, and then tells the listener off on the strength of it

`まい` is the classical negative conjecture: <code>あるまい</code> is "surely is
not". Add the reason-giving `し` and you get "since it surely is not the case that
～". Nothing that follows is neutral. The speaker has decided the listener is
behaving as though something absurd were true, and the second half of the sentence
says so - *you are not a child, so do it yourself*.

## Formation

A **noun** in front, bare, with no `だ`. A clause has to be made noun-like first, with
`の` or `わけ`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + ではあるまいし | {f|子供|こども} → {f|子供|こども}ではあるまいし |
| Noun, contracted | Noun + じゃあるまいし | {f|子供|こども} → {f|子供|こども}じゃあるまいし |
| な-adjective | stem + ではあるまいし | {f|苦手|にがて} → {f|苦手|にがて}ではあるまいし |
| Verb clause | plain form + の／わけ + ではあるまいし | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}くわけではあるまいし |
| い-adjective clause | plain form + の + ではあるまいし | {f|忙|いそが}しい → {f|忙|いそが}しいのではあるまいし |
| Variant | ～でもあるまいし | {f|医者|いしゃ} → {f|医者|いしゃ}でもあるまいし |
| What must follow | a reproach, an order, or "so obviously ～" | ～し、{f|自分|じぶん}でやりなさい |
| Modern equivalent | ～ではないのだから／～じゃないんだから | {f|子供|こども}じゃないんだから |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the `し` at the end is the ordinary reason-giving `し`, so the
sentence is **unfinished on purpose**. Something has to come after it, and that
something is never a compliment.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise this one; do not deploy it.** <code>あるまい</code> is a classical form
that died out of ordinary Japanese long ago, and it survives here only as a fossil.
Where you will meet it is fiction and manga dialogue, film subtitles, scripted
scolding by an older character, and a handful of set complaints that everybody
knows - <code>{f|子供|こども}じゃあるまいし</code>,
<code>{f|神様|かみさま}じゃあるまいし</code>,
<code>{f|馬鹿|ばか}じゃあるまいし</code>. Outside those fixed phrases, a learner who
produces it will sound as though they had learned Japanese from a period drama.

**Say <code>{f|子供|こども}じゃないんだから</code> instead.** That is the living form,
it means exactly the same thing, and it is what a real Japanese speaker uses when
they are exasperated.

There is a second reason for caution, and it has nothing to do with the age of the
form. The pattern is **rude by construction**. It tells the listener that they have
been acting on a premise so silly it needs stating out loud, and then instructs them.
It is used downward and sideways - parent to child, senior to junior, one friend to
another - and never, in any register, upward. A junior employee who says
<code>{f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|新人|しんじん}じゃあるまいし</code> has not made a
grammatical error; they have made a career one. Where the target is the speaker
themselves - <code>{f|神様|かみさま}じゃあるまいし</code> - it is not rude at all,
just weary, which is why that particular phrase is the one you hear most.

## Contrast

### ではあるまいし vs ではないんだから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning. <code>ではあるまいし</code> is the literary
fossil; <code>じゃないんだから</code> is what people actually say. Use the second and
recognise the first.

</div>

<pre>
{f|子供|こども}<b>じゃないんだから</b>、いちいち{f|言|い}われなくても{f|分|わ}かるでしょう。
You're not a child - you shouldn't need telling every time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子供|こども}<b>じゃあるまいし</b>、いちいち{f|言|い}われなくても{f|分|わ}かるだろう。
You're hardly a child; you should manage without being told each time.
</pre>

Both scold. The second sounds written, or performed - it belongs in a novel, or in
the mouth of a character whose speech is being marked as old-fashioned or theatrical.
Note that the modern version also lets you soften the ending
(<code>でしょう</code>, <code>よね</code>), which the fossil does not: `まい` pulls
the whole sentence into the plain, blunt register and keeps it there.

### ではあるまいし vs ではあるまいか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same `まい`, doing opposite jobs. With `し` it **gives a
reason** and scolds. With `か` it **asks a rhetorical question** and asserts, in the
careful voice of an essay.

</div>

<pre>
{f|専門家|せんもんか}<b>じゃあるまいし</b>、そこまで{f|詳|くわ}しく{f|説明|せつめい}する{f|必要|ひつよう}はない。
We're not specialists - there's no need to go into that much detail.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|変化|へんか}こそが{f|問題|もんだい}の{f|根|ね}にあるの<b>ではあるまいか</b>。
Might this change not be the root of the problem?
</pre>

Learners meeting the two on the same day mix them up constantly, and the endings are
the only signal. `し` leans forward into a complaint; `か` leans back into a
proposition, and it is at home in serious writing in a way that
<code>あるまいし</code> never is.

### ではあるまいし vs ならともかく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both dismiss a hypothetical. <code>ではあるまいし</code> says the
premise is **plainly false**; `ならともかく` says it **would have made a difference
if true**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|急|きゅう}な{f|用事|ようじ}<b>ならともかく</b>、そんなことで{f|会議|かいぎ}を{f|抜|ぬ}けるのはよくない。
If it were something urgent that would be one thing, but walking out of a meeting for that is not on.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|急|きゅう}な{f|用事|ようじ}<b>ではあるまいし</b>、{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わるまで{f|待|ま}てばいい。
It's hardly an emergency; you can wait until the meeting is over.
</pre>

`ならともかく` grants the hypothetical a hearing before setting it aside, which makes
it much the more usable of the two in ordinary life - it is neutral in register and
carries no reproach of its own. <code>ではあるまいし</code> refuses the hypothetical
outright and adds contempt for having entertained it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|子供|こども}だ<b>ではあるまいし</b> → ✅ {f|子供|こども}<b>ではあるまいし</b>
  The noun goes in bare. `だ` and <code>ではあるまい</code> are two attempts at the
  same copula, and they cannot both be there.

- ❌ {f|外国|がいこく}へ{f|行|い}く<b>ではあるまいし</b> → ✅ {f|外国|がいこく}へ{f|行|い}く<b>わけではあるまいし</b>
  The pattern needs a noun in front of it. A verb clause has to be nominalised first,
  with `わけ` or `の`.

- ❌ {f|課長|かちょう}、{f|新人|しんじん}<b>じゃあるまいし</b>、もう{f|少|すこ}し{f|早|はや}くお{f|願|ねが}いします。 → ✅ {f|課長|かちょう}、{f|恐|おそ}れ{f|入|い}りますが、もう{f|少|すこ}しお{f|急|いそ}ぎいただけますでしょうか。
  The grammar is intact and the sentence is unusable. This pattern scolds downward
  only; aimed at a superior it is an insult, whatever politeness is bolted onto the
  end of it.

- ❌ {f|子供|こども}<b>じゃあるまいし</b>、よくできたね。 → ✅ {f|子供|こども}<b>じゃあるまいし</b>、これくらいできて{f|当然|とうぜん}だ。
  What follows has to be a reproach or an "obviously, then" conclusion. Praise after
  it reads as sarcasm, because the first half has already denied the listener any
  credit.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to meet this sentence, and what does it tell you about the speaker? {f|探偵|たんてい}<b>じゃあるまいし</b>、そんなことまで{f|調|しら}べられるものか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Fiction, manga or a drama script - or a real speaker quoting that style for effect.
The sentence is a set-shape complaint aimed at the speaker's own situation ("I'm not
a detective"), so it is weary rather than rude, and `ものか` at the end confirms the
literary, performed register. In life the same line would come out as
<code>{f|探偵|たんてい}じゃないんだから、そんなことまで{f|調|しら}べられないよ</code>.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|学生|がくせい}<b>ではあるまいし</b>、{f|時間|じかん}の{f|管理|かんり}くらいできるだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|学生|がくせい}<b>じゃないんだから</b>、{f|時間|じかん}の{f|管理|かんり}くらいできるでしょう。

<code>ではあるまいし</code> maps directly onto
<code>じゃないんだから</code>: same premise, same reason-giving function, same
reproach. Softening <code>だろう</code> to <code>でしょう</code> is optional, but it
is available only in the modern version - which is one practical reason to prefer it.

</details>

**3.** Choose, and say why: {f|人手|ひとで}が{f|足|た}りないの（ではあるまいか・ではあるまいし）、{f|募集|ぼしゅう}を{f|考|かんが}えたほうがよいのではないか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|人手|ひとで}が{f|足|た}りないの<b>ではあるまいか</b>、…

The sentence is a careful written suggestion, not a telling-off: it floats a
proposition and then recommends something. That is the `か` of the rhetorical
question. <code>ではあるまいし</code> would assert that the staffing is obviously
adequate, which contradicts the second half of the sentence.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|山|やま}に{f|登|のぼ}るではあるまいし、そんな{f|靴|くつ}はいらないよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|山|やま}に{f|登|のぼ}る<b>わけではあるまいし</b>、そんな{f|靴|くつ}はいらないよ。

The pattern attaches to a noun, and <code>{f|登|のぼ}る</code> is a verb; `わけ` (or
`の`) has to be inserted to give <code>ではあるまいし</code> something to sit on. In
conversation you would drop the whole fossil and say
<code>{f|山|やま}に{f|登|のぼ}るわけじゃないんだから</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain who could say (b) to whom, and why (a) is the safer sentence:
(a) {f|新人|しんじん}<b>じゃないんですから</b>、そろそろ{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}ですよね。
(b) {f|新人|しんじん}<b>じゃあるまいし</b>、そろそろ{f|一人|ひとり}でやってくれ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b) can only be said downward: a manager to a subordinate, a senior to a junior, or
a coach to a player - and even then it stings, because the first half denies the
listener the excuse of inexperience before the second half issues an order. The
classical <code>あるまい</code> adds a curt, stagey edge on top.

(a) carries the same content with the politeness and the tag question doing the
social work. It can be said sideways between colleagues, it leaves the listener room
to disagree, and no one will hear it as a rebuke unless they have earned one.

</details>
