# ともすれば

**Meaning** · apt to; prone to; all too easily - warns that people or things slide in a particular unwelcome direction unless something holds them back

<code>ともすれば</code> is frozen out of an old conditional - roughly "if it does even
that much" - and it has kept the conditional's flavour: given half a chance, this is
what happens. It announces a **drift**, usually a drift away from where the speaker
thinks we ought to be, and it is nearly always followed by `がちだ`, `やすい` or
`かねない` at the end of the sentence.

## Formation

<code>ともすれば</code> is an adverb. Nothing attaches to it; what it needs is a
**partner at the end of the clause**, and choosing that partner is the whole of the
grammar.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Clause ending in がちだ | ともすれば … ～がちだ | ともすれば{f|感情的|かんじょうてき}になりがちだ |
| Clause ending in やすい | ともすれば … ～やすい | ともすれば{f|見落|みお}とされやすい |
| Clause ending in かねない | ともすれば … ～かねない | ともすれば{f|誤解|ごかい}を{f|招|まね}きかねない |
| Clause ending in てしまう | ともすれば … ～てしまう | ともすれば{f|後回|あとまわ}しにしてしまう |
| Placement | topic + は + ともすれば … | {f|専門家|せんもんか}はともすれば{f|前提|ぜんてい}を{f|疑|うたが}わなくなる |
| Variant, equally common | ともすると | ともすると{f|忘|わす}れられがちだ |
| Variant, more literary | ややもすれば／ややもすると | ややもすれば{f|独善|どくぜん}に{f|陥|おちい}りやすい |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the adverb cannot carry the meaning on its own. A flat predicate after it
- <code>ともすれば{f|忘|わす}れる</code> - leaves the sentence sounding unfinished,
because <code>ともすれば</code> has promised a tendency and only
`がちだ`, `やすい`, `かねない` or `てしまう` delivers one.

</div>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise it; say something else.** <code>ともすれば</code> belongs to written
Japanese that lectures a little: newspaper editorials and columns, the preface to a
report, essays, ceremonial speeches, safety and quality guidance, textbook
commentary. Spoken, it turns up in a lecture or a formal address and almost nowhere
else. Dropped into conversation it sounds like someone quoting an editorial at you.
What a speaker actually says is `～がちだ`, `～やすい`,
<code>つい～してしまう</code>, or - if the point is a specific slip - うっかりすると.

Three properties are worth holding on to.

**The drift is undesirable.** Nobody writes
❌<code>ともすれば{f|健康|けんこう}になりやすい</code>. The pattern exists to warn, so
the outcome must be one the writer would rather avoid.

**The subject is general.** {f|人|ひと}は, {f|我々|われわれ}は,
{f|日本人|にほんじん}は, {f|組織|そしき}は, この{f|種|しゅ}の{f|議論|ぎろん}は - the
sentence is usually about a class, not about Yamada on Tuesday. Applied to one named
person, it becomes a rather grand way of complaining.

**It often includes the speaker.** Much of its work is moralising in the
first-person plural: *we* let this happen, and we should watch for it. That gentle
self-implication is a large part of why the pattern survives in speeches, where
saying "you people are careless" would be impossible.

Among the variants, <code>ともすると</code> is the least marked and reads most
naturally in modern prose; <code>ともすれば</code> is a shade more formal, and
<code>ややもすれば</code> and <code>ややもすると</code> are the most literary of the
four, with a slightly older ring. Treat that as a gradient, not a rule - all four are
alive, and writers use them interchangeably.

## Contrast

### ともすれば vs ～がちだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `がちだ` is the **predicate** and can stand alone.
<code>ともすれば</code> is only an **adverb** and needs `がちだ` (or a relative) to
finish the job. If you want one of the two, want `がちだ`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|最近|さいきん}、{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|長|なが}くなり<b>がち</b>です。
Meetings have been running long lately.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|議論|ぎろん}が{f|白熱|はくねつ}すると、<b>ともすれば</b>{f|本来|ほんらい}の{f|目的|もくてき}を{f|見失|みうしな}い<b>がち</b>だ。
Once a discussion gets heated, the original purpose is all too easily lost sight of.
</pre>

The first sentence is ordinary spoken Japanese; the second is written commentary,
and dropping <code>ともすれば</code> from it would cost nothing but the editorial
tone. That is the honest summary: <code>ともすれば</code> adds emphasis and register,
not information. Learners should produce the first shape and be able to read the
second.

### ともすれば vs ややもすれば・ともすると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different weight.
<code>ともすると</code> ≦ <code>ともすれば</code> ≦ <code>ややもすれば</code> in
how bookish they sound.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|制度|せいど}は、<b>ともすると</b>{f|形|かたち}だけのものになってしまう。
This system all too easily ends up being a formality and nothing more.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|権力|けんりょく}を{f|持|も}つ{f|者|もの}は、<b>ややもすれば</b>{f|批判|ひはん}を{f|嫌|きら}うようになる。
Those who hold power become prone to disliking criticism.
</pre>

Nothing changes in the meaning if you swap them. The second sentence simply sounds
as though it comes from an older essay, and <code>ややもすれば</code> is where a
writer goes when the sentence is already moralising and wants to sound as if it has
history behind it.

### ともすれば vs うっかりすると・へたをすると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - these are the **spoken** members of the family.
<code>うっかりすると</code> = if your attention slips, on a particular occasion.
<code>へたをすると</code> = if it goes badly, worst case.
<code>ともすれば</code> = as a general standing tendency.

</div>

<pre>
<b>うっかりすると</b>{f|乗|の}り{f|過|す}ごすから、{f|寝|ね}ないでね。
If you're not careful you'll miss the stop, so don't fall asleep.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|同|おな}じ{f|作業|さぎょう}が{f|続|つづ}くと、<b>ともすれば</b>{f|確認|かくにん}が{f|甘|あま}くなりがちだ。
When the same task goes on and on, checks are all too apt to get sloppy.
</pre>

The first is advice to one person about one train; the second is a general claim
about repetitive work, of the kind that belongs in a safety briefing. This is the
pair to remember, because it is the swap a learner most needs: what you want in
conversation is almost always <code>うっかりすると</code> or
<code>へたをすると</code>, not <code>ともすれば</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>ともすれば</b>{f|昨日|きのう}{f|寝坊|ねぼう}した。 → ✅ {f|最近|さいきん}<b>ともすれば</b>{f|寝坊|ねぼう}しがちだ。
  The pattern states a standing tendency, so it cannot report one completed event.

- ❌ <b>ともすれば</b>{f|試験|しけん}に{f|合格|ごうかく}しやすい。 → ✅ {f|準備|じゅんび}すれば{f|試験|しけん}に{f|合格|ごうかく}しやすい。
  The outcome has to be one the writer is warning against. With good news in it, the
  sentence reads as a warning about something desirable, which makes no sense.

- ❌ <b>ともすれば</b>{f|人|ひと}は{f|人|ひと}を{f|見|み}た{f|目|め}で{f|判断|はんだん}する。 → ✅ <b>ともすれば</b>{f|人|ひと}は{f|人|ひと}を{f|見|み}た{f|目|め}で{f|判断|はんだん}し<b>がちだ</b>。
  Finish the sentence with `がちだ`, `やすい` or `かねない`. Without one the
  adverb is left dangling.

- ❌ {f|時間|じかん}が<b>ともすれば</b>、{f|品質|ひんしつ}が{f|落|お}ちる。 → ✅ {f|時間|じかん}に{f|追|お}われると、<b>ともすれば</b>{f|品質|ひんしつ}が{f|落|お}ちる。
  Although it ends in `～ば`, it is a fixed adverb, not a live conditional. Nothing
  can be conditioned by it, and no clause attaches in front of it.

- ❌ ごめん、<b>ともすれば</b>{f|返事|へんじ}を{f|忘|わす}れちゃうんだ。 → ✅ ごめん、つい{f|返事|へんじ}を{f|忘|わす}れちゃうんだ。
  Apologising to a friend in editorial register is its own kind of comedy. In casual
  speech use <code>つい～してしまう</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|遠|とお}くの{f|災害|さいがい}は、{f|報道|ほうどう}が{f|減|へ}ると<b>ともすれば</b>{f|忘|わす}れられ____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|忘|わす}れられ<b>がちだ</b>（or {f|忘|わす}れられ<b>やすい</b>）

<code>ともすれば</code> has to be answered by a tendency ending. Both
<code>がちだ</code> and <code>やすい</code> work here; the passive
<code>{f|忘|わす}れられる</code> keeps the sentence impersonal, which suits the
editorial register the adverb brings with it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|夜道|よみち}は{f|暗|くら}いから、（ともすれば・うっかりすると）{f|段差|だんさ}に{f|気|き}づかないよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

（うっかりすると）{f|段差|だんさ}に{f|気|き}づかないよ。

This is one person being warned about one dark path, and the `よ` ending marks it as
speech. <code>うっかりすると</code> is the spoken fit.
<code>ともすれば</code> generalises about human nature and would sound like a public
safety notice delivered to a friend.

</details>

**3.** Rewrite in ordinary spoken Japanese: {f|在宅|ざいたく}{f|勤務|きんむ}では、<b>ともすれば</b>{f|生活|せいかつ}と{f|仕事|しごと}の{f|区別|くべつ}が{f|曖昧|あいまい}になりがちである。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|家|いえ}で{f|働|はたら}いていると、{f|仕事|しごと}とプライベートの{f|区別|くべつ}がつかなくなりがちなんだよね。

Three things change together: the adverb goes, `である` becomes a spoken ending, and
the vocabulary drops a register. `がちだ` alone carries the whole meaning - which is
the point of the exercise, since it shows how little
<code>ともすれば</code> was contributing beyond tone.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|新人|しんじん}の{f|田中|たなか}さんはともすれば{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}で{f|緊張|きんちょう}してしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|新人|しんじん}の{f|田中|たなか}さんは、{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}で{f|緊張|きんちょう}してしまったようだ。
Or, if you want the tendency: ✅ {f|新人|しんじん}のうちは、ともすれば{f|会議|かいぎ}で{f|緊張|きんちょう}してしまいがちだ。

Two problems. The sentence describes one meeting yesterday, and it describes one
named person - both of which fight the adverb's job of stating a general drift. Cut
it, or generalise the sentence around it.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|長|なが}くなり<b>がちだ</b>。
(b) {f|会議|かいぎ}は<b>ともすれば</b>{f|長|なが}くなり<b>がちだ</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain observation about how meetings have been going. Anyone could say it
to a colleague in the corridor.

(b) says the same thing, and frames it as a general truth about meetings that we
ought to be guarding against. The adverb adds a note of warning and lifts the
sentence into written or platform register, so it fits a report on how the company
runs its meetings and sounds heavy in the corridor.

</details>
