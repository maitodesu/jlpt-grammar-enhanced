# 言わずもがな

**Meaning** · needless to say - and, in its older sense, better left unsaid. One fixed phrase with two nearly opposite readings.

{f|言|い}わずもがな is a fossil: {f|言|い}わ (the imperfective stem of
{f|言|い}う) + ず (negative) + もがな, an Old Japanese particle of wishing. Literally
"would that it were not said". From that one root the phrase has grown two senses that
pull in opposite directions - **"so obvious that saying it is unnecessary"** and **"a
remark that should not have been made"** - and modern Japanese keeps both.

## Formation

The phrase does not conjugate and it does not attach productively to other verbs. What
varies is the frame it sits in, and the frame is what tells you which sense is meant.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (listing sense) | Noun + は + {f|言|い}わずもがな（、Noun + も…） | {f|英語|えいご} → {f|英語|えいご}は{f|言|い}わずもがな |
| Before a noun | {f|言|い}わずもがな + の + Noun | こと → {f|言|い}わずもがなのこと |
| As predicate | {f|言|い}わずもがなだ／である／であろう | - → {f|言|い}わずもがなである |
| Concessive lead-in | {f|言|い}わずもがなだが／のことだが、～ | - → {f|言|い}わずもがなだが、{f|確認|かくにん}は{f|必要|ひつよう}だ |
| Historical parse (not productive) | Verb imperfective stem + ず + もがな | {f|言|い}う → {f|言|い}わずもがな |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|言|い}わずもがなの＋noun</code> is almost always the
critical sense, "an uncalled-for ~". <code>Noun+は{f|言|い}わずもがな</code> followed
by a second listed item is almost always the "needless to say" sense. The last row of
the table is history, not a pattern: あらずもがな survives in classical texts, but you
cannot build {f|行|い}かずもがな or {f|見|み}ずもがな in modern Japanese.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise this one; do not deploy it.** {f|言|い}わずもがな belongs to written
Japanese of a fairly literary cast: essays and newspaper columns, book reviews and
commentary, the prepared portion of a formal address, and narration in fiction. It
also survives in the set noun phrase <code>{f|言|い}わずもがなのこと</code>. It is not
archaic in the way <code>～べからず</code> is - an educated reader meets it without
blinking - but it is not spoken. Say it in conversation and you will sound as though
you are reading aloud from a column.

The modern equivalents are ordinary and entirely adequate. For "needless to say", use
<code>{f|言|い}うまでもなく</code>, <code>{f|言|い}うまでもない</code> or もちろん. For
"better left unsaid", use <code>{f|余計|よけい}な{f|一言|ひとこと}</code>,
<code>{f|言|い}わなくてもいいこと</code> or <code>{f|言|い}わなければよかった</code>.

There is a second and stronger reason to keep it in the reading pile. Because the two
senses coexist, a sentence built around it can be **misread as its own opposite**. If
you tell a colleague それは{f|言|い}わずもがなですね intending "that goes without
saying", there is a real chance of being heard as "you should not have said that" -
which is a rebuke. Writers avoid the ambiguity by choosing the frame carefully; a
learner has no reason to take the risk when
{f|言|い}うまでもありません is sitting right there.

When you meet it in text, the frame disambiguates almost perfectly. A の and a
following noun means the critical sense. A は and a second listed item means the
"needless to say" sense. A bare predicate - {f|言|い}わずもがなである - is usually the
"needless to say" sense in an essay, and the critical sense when the subject is a
remark someone made.

One last observation about tone. In the listing sense the phrase does a little more
than <code>～はもちろん</code> would: it flatters the reader slightly, treating the
first item as beneath discussion between people like us. That faint air of shared
sophistication is part of why it is a columnist's word.

## Contrast

### {f|言|い}わずもがな vs {f|言|い}うまでもなく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same meaning in the "needless to say" sense, two registers
apart. <code>{f|言|い}うまでもなく</code> is the living form and is safe everywhere,
including speech.

</div>

<pre>
{f|健康|けんこう}が{f|大切|たいせつ}であることは<b>{f|言|い}わずもがな</b>である。
That health matters is, of course, beyond stating.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|健康|けんこう}が{f|大切|たいせつ}であることは<b>{f|言|い}うまでもない</b>。
It goes without saying that health matters.
</pre>

The content is identical. The first belongs in a printed essay; the second belongs
anywhere, from a report to a conversation. Note also that
<code>{f|言|い}うまでもなく</code> has only the one sense, so it can never be
misheard as a criticism - which is the practical argument for preferring it.

### {f|言|い}わずもがな vs ～はもちろん・～はもとより

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - all three list a taken-for-granted item and then add a further one.
<code>～はもちろん</code> is neutral, <code>～はもとより</code> is formal, and
{f|言|い}わずもがな is formal **and** literary, with a note of "we need hardly discuss
this one".

</div>

<pre>
{f|給料|きゅうりょう}<b>はもちろん</b>、{f|職場|しょくば}の{f|雰囲気|ふんいき}も{f|大事|だいじ}だ。
Salary matters, obviously, but so does the atmosphere of the workplace.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|給料|きゅうりょう}は<b>{f|言|い}わずもがな</b>、{f|職場|しょくば}の{f|雰囲気|ふんいき}こそが{f|定着|ていちゃく}を{f|左右|さゆう}する。
Pay is beyond discussion; it is the atmosphere of the workplace that decides whether people stay.
</pre>

Structurally the two are the same move. What changes is who is speaking: the first
sentence could be said over lunch, the second is the voice of an article. If you find
yourself wanting {f|言|い}わずもがな in this frame, write <code>～はもちろん</code> in
speech and <code>～はもとより</code> in a formal document, and you will never be wrong.

### {f|言|い}わずもがな vs {f|余計|よけい}な{f|一言|ひとこと}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for the *critical* sense, the modern phrase is
<code>{f|余計|よけい}な{f|一言|ひとこと}</code>. It is blunt, spoken, and carries no
risk of being taken the other way round.

</div>

<pre>
それは<b>{f|言|い}わずもがな</b>の{f|一言|ひとこと}だった。
That was a remark better left unsaid.
</pre>

<pre>
それは<b>{f|余計|よけい}な{f|一言|ひとこと}</b>だったね。
That was one word too many, wasn't it.
</pre>

The first is how a novelist reports it; the second is how a friend tells you that you
went too far. There is a difference of stance as well:
{f|言|い}わずもがな judges the remark from a distance, almost with regret, while
{f|余計|よけい}な{f|一言|ひとこと} is a direct reproach. If you are actually telling
someone off, you want the second.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|行|い}か<b>ずもがな</b>の{f|旅|たび}だった。 → ✅ {f|行|い}かなくてもよかった{f|旅|たび}だった。
  もがな is not a productive suffix in modern Japanese. Outside the fixed
  {f|言|い}わずもがな and the classical あらずもがな, it does not attach to verbs at
  all.

- ❌ {f|言|い}わずもがな<b>に</b>、{f|準備|じゅんび}が{f|必要|ひつよう}だ。 → ✅ <b>{f|言|い}うまでもなく</b>、{f|準備|じゅんび}が{f|必要|ひつよう}だ。
  The phrase takes no `に`. As a sentence-opening adverbial, the modern
  <code>{f|言|い}うまでもなく</code> is what you want.

- ❌ （{f|会議|かいぎ}で{f|上司|じょうし}に）「それは{f|言|い}わずもがなですね。」 → ✅ 「それはもちろんですね。」
  Even setting the register aside, this is risky. The listener may hear the critical
  sense - "that was something you should not have said" - and you will have rebuked
  your manager by accident.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}{f|休|やす}みだよ。{f|言|い}わずもがなだけどね。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}{f|休|やす}みだよ。{f|言|い}うまでもないけどね。
  Casual speech and a literary fossil in the same breath. The phrase has no casual
  form; it simply does not belong in the sentence.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to meet this sentence, and who is speaking?
{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}の{f|進歩|しんぽ}は<b>{f|言|い}わずもがな</b>、{f|制度|せいど}の{f|設計|せっけい}こそが{f|問|と}われている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In print: a newspaper column, a magazine essay, or the opening of an argumentative
report. The voice is a commentator addressing a general but educated reader.

Two things mark it. The listing frame
<code>～は{f|言|い}わずもがな、～こそ</code> is a writer's construction, and the
sentence assumes agreement about the first item instead of arguing for it. Nobody
speaks this way in a meeting.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese:
{f|準備|じゅんび}の{f|大切|たいせつ}さは<b>{f|言|い}わずもがな</b>である。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|準備|じゅんび}が{f|大切|たいせつ}なことは<b>{f|言|い}うまでもない</b>。

<code>{f|言|い}うまでもない</code> carries exactly the same claim and is usable in
speech, in email and in writing alike. もちろん{f|準備|じゅんび}は{f|大切|たいせつ}だ
would also do, at a more conversational level.

</details>

**3.** Which sense is each one, and how can you tell?
(a) {f|彼|かれ}の<b>{f|言|い}わずもがな</b>の{f|一言|ひとこと}が{f|事態|じたい}をこじらせた。
(b) {f|費用|ひよう}の{f|問題|もんだい}は<b>{f|言|い}わずもがな</b>、{f|時間|じかん}も{f|足|た}りない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the critical sense: a remark that would have been better not made. The giveaway
is the frame - <code>{f|言|い}わずもがなの</code> in front of a noun - reinforced by a
consequence, こじらせた, that only makes sense if the remark did harm.

(b) is the listing sense: cost goes without saying, and time is short as well. The
giveaway is `は` followed by a second item that is being *added*. When you see a second
item, read "needless to say"; when you see の plus a noun, read "better left unsaid".

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the register error:
「ごめん、{f|昨日|きのう}のあれ、{f|言|い}わずもがなだったよね。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ 「ごめん、{f|昨日|きのう}のあれ、{f|余計|よけい}な{f|一言|ひとこと}だったよね。」

The sense is right - the speaker is apologising for something they should not have
said - but the phrase is literary and ごめん and よね are not. In a spoken apology, use
{f|余計|よけい}なことを{f|言|い}った or {f|言|い}わなきゃよかった.

</details>

**5.** Explain what {f|言|い}わずもがな contributes here, and why a learner should not
produce this sentence:
{f|基礎|きそ}{f|研究|けんきゅう}の{f|重要性|じゅうようせい}は<b>{f|言|い}わずもがな</b>だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It asserts that the importance of basic research is beyond argument, and it does so
with a small flourish: the phrase treats the point as settled among readers of the
writer's sort. <code>{f|言|い}うまでもない</code> would make the same claim without the
flourish.

A learner should leave it alone for two reasons. It is written register, so it will
sound wrong out loud. And it is ambiguous - stripped of the essayistic context, a
listener could take {f|言|い}わずもがなだ as "that was a thing better not said", which
turns a statement of agreement into a complaint about the previous speaker.

</details>
