# ～にかたくない

**Meaning** · it is not hard to imagine; one can readily infer it - a written formula for "anyone can see how they must have felt"

{f|難|かた}い is the literary adjective "difficult", the same {f|難|かた} that
appears voiced in ～がたい. <code>{f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|かた}くない</code> is
therefore "not difficult to imagine", and it is one of a very small number of
frozen phrases in which this word still lives. The writer is inviting the reader
to supply the picture themselves.

## Formation

The noun slot is tiny in practice: {f|想像|そうぞう}, {f|察|さっ}する,
{f|推察|すいさつ}. Both a noun and a dictionary-form verb are possible before に.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun ({f|想像|そうぞう} etc.) | noun + に{f|難|かた}くない | {f|想像|そうぞう} → {f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|かた}くない |
| Verb | dictionary form + に{f|難|かた}くない | {f|察|さっ}する → {f|察|さっ}するに{f|難|かた}くない |
| Verb ({f|想像|そうぞう}する) | dictionary form + に{f|難|かた}くない | {f|想像|そうぞう}する → {f|想像|そうぞう}するに{f|難|かた}くない |
| Polite | ～に{f|難|かた}くありません | {f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|かた}くありません |
| Kana spelling | ～にかたくない | {f|想像|そうぞう}にかたくない |
| Typical frame | ～ことは ～に{f|難|かた}くない | {f|反発|はんぱつ}が{f|出|で}ることは{f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|かた}くない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - {f|難|かた}くない, read **かたくない**, is not
{f|難|むずか}しくない. They share a kanji and nothing else: the first is this
literary formula, the second is the everyday "is not difficult".

</div>

<pre>

Reading the letters of the period, it is not difficult to infer how isolated he was within his field.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise this one; do not use it.** It belongs to newspaper editorials,
opinion columns, formal reports, eulogies and speeches - written Japanese
addressed to an audience. Said out loud in ordinary conversation it lands like a
leader-column sentence dropped into a chat, and a listener will hear it as
posturing. What you should say instead is
<code>{f|想像|そうぞう}がつく</code>, or
<code>{f|簡単|かんたん}に{f|想像|そうぞう}できる</code>.

Two things are worth noticing when you meet it. First, it is a **rhetorical
move**, not a report: the writer asserts that the conclusion is obvious and so
declines to argue for it. Editorials use it to slide past the weakest link in an
argument, and reading it that way is a useful habit.

Second, it is almost always about **someone else's feelings or reaction** -
distress, anger, relief, the fallout of a decision. It is not used for facts you
could simply check. ❌{f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ることは
{f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|かた}くない is wrong not grammatically but in kind.

The one place it does show up in speech is prepared, ceremonial speech: at a
memorial or a retirement address, ご{f|心痛|しんつう}は{f|察|さっ}するに
{f|難|かた}くありません is entirely at home. That register - podium, script,
audience - is the test.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|難|かた}くない vs {f|想像|そうぞう}がつく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, opposite settings.
<code>{f|想像|そうぞう}がつく</code> is what a person says;
<code>{f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|かた}くない</code> is what a column prints.

</div>

<pre>
あの{f|人|ひと}が{f|怒|おこ}ったのは<b>{f|想像|そうぞう}がつく</b>よ。
I can imagine he was angry.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|関係者|かんけいしゃ}の{f|落胆|らくたん}は<b>{f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|かた}くない</b>。
The dismay among those involved is not hard to imagine.
</pre>

If you are speaking, use the first. <code>{f|想像|そうぞう}がつく</code> also
takes the negative freely - {f|想像|そうぞう}がつかない, "I can't imagine" -
whereas the literary phrase has no natural affirmative counterpart:
❌{f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|かた}い is not said.

### ～に{f|難|かた}くない vs ～がたい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same kanji points in opposite directions.
<code>に{f|難|かた}くない</code> = **easy** to imagine. ～がたい = **hard** to do,
attached to a ます-stem.

</div>

<pre>
あの{f|温厚|おんこう}な{f|人|ひと}が{f|怒鳴|どな}ったとは、<b>{f|信|しん}じがたい</b>。
That so mild a man should have shouted is hard to believe.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|周囲|しゅうい}が{f|驚|おどろ}いたことは<b>{f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|かた}くない</b>。
That those around him were startled is not hard to imagine.
</pre>

Learners meeting {f|難|かた} for the first time in both patterns often read
<code>に{f|難|かた}くない</code> as "cannot", because ～がたい feels negative.
It is the reverse: the negative ending makes the sentence say the inference is
easy.

### ～に{f|難|かた}くない vs {f|言|い}うまでもない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|難|かた}くない</code> says the reader can **work it
out**. <code>{f|言|い}うまでもない</code> says it is so obvious that **saying it
is a waste of breath**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|安全|あんぜん}が{f|最優先|さいゆうせん}であることは<b>{f|言|い}うまでもない</b>。
It goes without saying that safety comes first.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|現場|げんば}が{f|混乱|こんらん}したことは<b>{f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|かた}くない</b>。
The confusion on the ground is not hard to imagine.
</pre>

The first states a shared premise. The second hands the reader a scene and asks
them to fill it in. Only <code>に{f|難|かた}くない</code> can do that, which is
why it takes feelings and reactions rather than principles.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|想像|そうぞう}に<b>{f|難|むずか}しくない</b> → ✅ {f|想像|そうぞう}に<b>{f|難|かた}くない</b>
  The reading is かたくない. {f|難|むずか}しくない is a different, ordinary word
  and does not form this phrase.

- ❌ {f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|かた}くない<b>だ</b>。 → ✅ {f|想像|そうぞう}に<b>{f|難|かた}くない</b>。
  {f|難|かた}くない is already the negative of an い-adjective, so だ cannot
  follow. The polite form is {f|難|かた}くありません.

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}の{f|答|こた}えは{f|分|わ}かる<b>に{f|難|かた}くない</b>。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}の{f|答|こた}えは<b>すぐ{f|分|わ}かる</b>。
  The slot in front takes {f|想像|そうぞう}, {f|察|さっ}する,
  {f|推察|すいさつ} and little else. It is not a general "is easy to".

- ❌ (to a friend, about a delayed train) {f|遅|おく}れて{f|怒|おこ}ったのは{f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|かた}くないね。 → ✅ {f|遅|おく}れて{f|怒|おこ}ったのは<b>{f|想像|そうぞう}がつくね</b>。
  Correct grammar, wrong room. In conversation the literary phrase sounds like
  reading aloud from a newspaper.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to meet this sentence - (a) a message to a friend, (b) a newspaper editorial, (c) a children's picture book?
{f|被災地|ひさいち}の{f|方々|かたがた}のご{f|苦労|くろう}は{f|察|さっ}する<b>に{f|難|かた}くない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**(b), a newspaper editorial** - or a speech, a formal report, a letter of
condolence. The phrase is written, public Japanese about other people's feelings.
To a friend you would say {f|想像|そうぞう}がつく, and a children's book would
not use {f|察|さっ}する at all.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|彼|かれ}が{f|悩|なや}んでいたことは{f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|かた}くない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}が{f|悩|なや}んでいたのは<b>{f|想像|そうぞう}がつく</b>。
（or {f|簡単|かんたん}に{f|想像|そうぞう}できる）

<code>{f|想像|そうぞう}がつく</code> carries the whole meaning and is what a
speaker would actually say; dropping ことは for のは also brings the sentence
down into the spoken register.

</details>

**3.** Choose: あの{f|慎重|しんちょう}な{f|彼|かれ}が{f|辞表|じひょう}を{f|出|だ}したとは（{f|信|しん}じがたい・{f|信|しん}じるに{f|難|かた}くない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|信|しん}じがたい</b>。

The sentence is about something **hard** to believe, so it needs ～がたい.
<code>に{f|難|かた}くない</code> would claim the opposite, that believing it is
easy - and in any case the pattern does not take {f|信|しん}じる.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|住民|じゅうみん}が{f|不安|ふあん}を{f|感|かん}じたことは{f|想像|そうぞう}に{f|難|むずか}しくないです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|住民|じゅうみん}が{f|不安|ふあん}を{f|感|かん}じたことは{f|想像|そうぞう}に<b>{f|難|かた}くありません</b>。
"It is not hard to imagine that the residents felt uneasy."

Two repairs: the reading is かたく, not むずかしく, and a phrase this formal takes
ありません rather than ないです. If the setting is conversation, abandon the
pattern altogether and say {f|想像|そうぞう}がつきます.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|苦労|くろう}は{f|想像|そうぞう}<b>に{f|難|かた}くない</b>。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|苦労|くろう}は{f|想像|そうぞう}<b>もつかない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the reader can picture it easily - the writer is claiming common ground
and moving on. It is sympathetic but slightly distant, and it belongs on a
printed page.

(b) says the opposite: what he went through is **beyond** the speaker's power to
imagine. It is spoken Japanese, and it expresses awe or helplessness rather than
easy agreement. Note that the negative of the literary phrase is not the way to
express (b); the two use different verbs entirely.

</details>
