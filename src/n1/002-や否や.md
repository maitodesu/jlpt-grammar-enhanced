# ～や否や

**Meaning** · no sooner had one thing happened than the next did - a written, newspaper-and-novel way of saying "the moment that"

Read <code>や{f|否|いな}や</code> as やいなや. It is a survival from classical
Japanese: <code>や</code> is the old exclamatory particle and
<code>{f|否|いな}や</code> is literally "or not", so the phrase once meant "hardly
had it, or had it not". Nothing of that literal sense is left. What remains is a
compressed way of welding two events together, and it belongs to the page, not to
the mouth.

## Formation

The verb in front is in the dictionary form, whatever the tense of the sentence.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | dictionary form + や{f|否|いな}や | {f|止|と}まる → {f|止|と}まるや{f|否|いな}や |
| Verb (る-verb) | dictionary form + や{f|否|いな}や | {f|届|とど}ける → {f|届|とど}けるや{f|否|いな}や |
| Verb (する) | dictionary form + や{f|否|いな}や | {f|発表|はっぴょう}する → {f|発表|はっぴょう}するや{f|否|いな}や |
| Verb (passive) | dictionary form + や{f|否|いな}や | {f|発売|はつばい}される → {f|発売|はつばい}されるや{f|否|いな}や |
| Shortened variant | dictionary form + や | {f|結|むす}ばれる → {f|結|むす}ばれるや |
| Second clause | past tense, a completed event | {f|降|お}り{f|始|はじ}めた |
| Not possible | past form in front | ❌{f|止|と}まったや{f|否|いな}や |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the bare <code>～や</code> is the same pattern with the tail cut off. It
is even more literary, and in modern text it appears mostly in historical writing
and in headlines. The <code>{f|否|いな}や</code> half is also written in kana as
<code>やいなや</code>; both spellings are standard.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**This is a form you read, not one you use.** Its home is newspaper copy, magazine
features, company histories, historical writing and literary fiction - text whose
job is to compress a sequence of events. In conversation it is genuinely strange:
a Japanese speaker who said <code>{f|帰|かえ}るや{f|否|いな}や</code> to a friend
would sound as though they were reading a headline aloud, and the listener would
probably laugh. Learn to recognise it, and say
<code>{f|帰|かえ}ったとたん</code> or <code>{f|帰|かえ}ってすぐ</code> yourself.

The one setting where a speaker might reach for it is a prepared, formal spoken
text - a speech, a memorial address, a documentary narration. Even there it is a
deliberately literary flourish rather than neutral choice.

Two grammatical consequences of that written character are worth noting. First,
the second clause reports a **fact that occurred**, so no requests, invitations,
volitional forms or plain negatives can follow: ❌<code>{f|着|つ}くや{f|否|いな}や{f|電話|でんわ}してください</code>.
Second, unlike <code>なり</code>, the two clauses **may have different subjects** -
the train stops, the passengers move - which is exactly why it suits reportage.

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if the sentence would fit under a photograph in a newspaper,
<code>や{f|否|いな}や</code> is possible. If it would fit in a text message, it is
not.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～や{f|否|いな}や vs ～たとたん（に）

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>たとたん</code> is the **modern equivalent**, and the one
to actually use. It says the same thing in ordinary Japanese and works in speech,
email and writing alike.

</div>

<pre>
{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|呼|よ}ばれ<b>たとたん</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|顔|かお}を{f|赤|あか}くした。
The instant his name was called, he went red.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|名前|なまえ}が{f|読|よ}み{f|上|あ}げられる<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b>、{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|静|しず}まり{f|返|かえ}った。
The name was no sooner read out than the hall fell completely silent.
</pre>

Both sentences are correct Japanese; they simply come from different worlds. Note
the form change as well: <code>たとたん</code> takes the **past** form in front,
<code>や{f|否|いな}や</code> the dictionary form. Rewriting one as the other means
changing the verb, not just swapping the tail.

### ～や{f|否|いな}や vs ～が{f|早|はや}いか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - these two are near-identical, and no reliable meaning
difference separates them. <code>が{f|早|はや}いか</code> leans towards fiction and a
single vivid human action; <code>や{f|否|いな}や</code> leans towards reportage and
tolerates impersonal events.

</div>

<pre>
{f|少年|しょうねん}はベルが{f|鳴|な}る<b>が{f|早|はや}いか</b>、かばんをつかんで{f|教室|きょうしつ}を{f|出|で}て{f|行|い}った。
The bell had barely rung before the boy grabbed his bag and was out of the classroom.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|規制|きせい}が{f|緩和|かんわ}される<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b>、{f|各社|かくしゃ}が{f|市場|しじょう}に{f|参入|さんにゅう}した。
No sooner were the rules relaxed than firms started entering the market.
</pre>

Swap them and neither sentence becomes wrong, only slightly less idiomatic. Do not
invent a distinction here; treat them as stylistic neighbours and recognise both.

### ～や{f|否|いな}や vs ～なり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>なり</code> follows **one person** through two actions.
<code>や{f|否|いな}や</code> links **two events**, whoever or whatever performs them.

</div>

<pre>
{f|父|ちち}は{f|新聞|しんぶん}を{f|置|お}く<b>なり</b>、だまって{f|外|そと}へ{f|出|で}て{f|行|い}った。
My father put the paper down and walked out without a word.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試合|しあい}が{f|終|お}わる<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b>、スタンドから{f|人|ひと}があふれ{f|出|だ}した。
The match had barely finished before people came pouring out of the stands.
</pre>

The second sentence is impossible with <code>なり</code>: the match does not empty
the stands, and the subject changes across the comma. <code>なり</code> is also the
milder of the two on the register scale - narrative but not journalistic.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|授業|じゅぎょう}が{f|終|お}わった<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b> → ✅ {f|授業|じゅぎょう}が{f|終|お}わる<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b>
  The dictionary form is fixed. Learners reach for the past because the English is
  past, but the tense lives in the second clause.

- ❌ {f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}く<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b>{f|連絡|れんらく}してください。 → ✅ {f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}いたら、すぐ{f|連絡|れんらく}してください。
  The pattern reports events that happened. A request cannot follow it.

- ❌ ただいま。{f|家|いえ}に{f|着|つ}く<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b>、おなかがすいちゃった。 → ✅ {f|家|いえ}に{f|着|つ}いたとたん、おなかがすいちゃった。
  Not a grammar error, a register error - and the more damaging kind. Newspaper
  vocabulary inside a casual sentence sounds like a joke.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b>、{f|毎回|まいかい}{f|傘|かさ}を{f|買|か}ってしまう。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ると、{f|毎回|まいかい}{f|傘|かさ}を{f|買|か}ってしまう。
  A single past event only. Habits take と or たびに.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to meet this sentence: 「{f|工場|こうじょう}の{f|門|もん}が{f|開|ひら}く<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b>、{f|人々|ひとびと}がなだれ{f|込|こ}んだ。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In writing - a news report, a magazine feature, a work of fiction or a company
history. The construction compresses two events into one line, which is what
reportage and narration want. Nobody would produce this sentence in conversation;
there they would say <code>{f|門|もん}が{f|開|ひら}いたとたん</code>.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|電話|でんわ}が{f|鳴|な}る<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b>、{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|受話器|じゅわき}を{f|取|と}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|電話|でんわ}が{f|鳴|な}っ<b>たとたん</b>、{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|受話器|じゅわき}を{f|取|と}った。（or {f|鳴|な}るとすぐに）

The two forms mean the same thing; only the register changes. Notice that the verb
in front has to move from the dictionary form to the past form when you switch.

</details>

**3.** Find and fix the error: {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|始|はじ}まったや{f|否|いな}や、{f|議論|ぎろん}が{f|白熱|はくねつ}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|始|はじ}まる<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b>、{f|議論|ぎろん}が{f|白熱|はくねつ}した。

<code>や{f|否|いな}や</code> takes the dictionary form, never the past. The rest of
the sentence is fine: two different subjects across the comma is normal here, and
{f|白熱|はくねつ}する is written-register vocabulary that matches the connective.

</details>

**4.** Choose the sentence that could actually appear in speech, and say why the
other could not:
(a) {f|先生|せんせい}が{f|教室|きょうしつ}に{f|入|はい}る<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b>、みんな{f|静|しず}かになった。
(b) {f|先生|せんせい}が{f|教室|きょうしつ}に{f|入|はい}っ<b>たとたん</b>、みんな{f|静|しず}かになった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b). It is neutral Japanese and fits a conversation between students.

(a) is grammatically perfect but written-language only. Spoken aloud between
friends it lands as mock-formal, the way "no sooner had the teacher entered" would
in an English text message.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|判定|はんてい}が{f|出|で}る<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b>、{f|観客|かんきゃく}は{f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}った。
(b) {f|判定|はんてい}が{f|出|で}てから、{f|観客|かんきゃく}は{f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) presses the two events together and colours the standing-up as an immediate,
almost reflexive reaction to the decision. It also sounds like reportage - you can
hear the sports page.

(b) only orders them: the decision came, and afterwards people left. A minute may
have passed, and no reaction is implied. It is also plain, everyday Japanese.

</details>
