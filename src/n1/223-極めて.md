# 極めて

**Meaning** · extremely; in the highest degree

Read <code>{f|極|きわ}めて</code>. It is the strongest of the ordinary "very"
words and also the stiffest: it lives in reports, editorials and official
statements, not in conversation.

## Usage

<code>{f|極|きわ}めて</code> is an adverb. It stands directly in front of the word
it intensifies, and that word describes a **quality**:

- い-adjective: <code>{f|極|きわ}めて{f|高|たか}い{f|確率|かくりつ}</code>
- な-adjective: <code>{f|極|きわ}めて{f|困難|こんなん}な{f|状況|じょうきょう}</code> - the `な` still appears before a noun
- adverbial phrase: <code>{f|極|きわ}めて{f|順調|じゅんちょう}に{f|進|すす}んでいる</code>
- a stative predicate: <code>{f|極|きわ}めて{f|限|かぎ}られている</code>

What it does **not** do is intensify an ordinary action verb. ❌<code>{f|極|きわ}めて{f|疲|つか}れた</code>
is not Japanese; that job belongs to <code>{f|非常|ひじょう}に</code>, とても or すごく.
Nor is it a noun: there is no ❌<code>{f|極|きわ}めての{f|問題|もんだい}</code> and no
❌<code>{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|極|きわ}めてだ</code>.

The word is historically the て-form of <code>{f|極|きわ}める</code>, "to take
something to its limit", and that is where the force comes from: not "very" but
"at the far end of the scale". Its habitual partners are evaluative -
<code>{f|重要|じゅうよう}</code>, <code>{f|困難|こんなん}</code>,
<code>{f|深刻|しんこく}</code>, <code>{f|危険|きけん}</code>, まれ,
<code>{f|少|すく}ない</code>, <code>{f|遺憾|いかん}</code>.

## Examples

<pre>
このやり{f|方|かた}は<b>{f|極|きわ}めて</b>{f|危険|きけん}だ。
This approach is extremely dangerous.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|成功|せいこう}の{f|可能性|かのうせい}は<b>{f|極|きわ}めて</b>{f|低|ひく}いと{f|言|い}わざるを{f|得|え}ない。
We have to say that the chances of success are extremely low.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|対応|たいおう}は<b>{f|極|きわ}めて</b>{f|遺憾|いかん}であると、{f|大臣|だいじん}は{f|述|の}べた。
The minister said that the handling of the matter was extremely regrettable.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|規則|きそく}が{f|現場|げんば}に{f|与|あた}える{f|影響|えいきょう}は<b>{f|極|きわ}めて</b>{f|大|おお}きいと{f|考|かんが}えられる。
The new rules are likely to have an extremely large impact on the people doing the work.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Written and formal, with almost no life in casual speech. You will meet it in
newspapers, academic papers, company announcements, court documents and prepared
remarks. Said out loud over dinner, <code>{f|今日|きょう}は{f|極|きわ}めて{f|暑|あつ}いね</code>
sounds like a weather bureau reading its own bulletin; the natural sentence is
<code>{f|今日|きょう}はすごく{f|暑|あつ}いね</code>.

If you need the idea in speech, the ladder runs roughly すごく（casual）→ とても
（neutral polite）→ <code>{f|非常|ひじょう}に</code>（formal but sayable）→
<code>{f|極|きわ}めて</code>（written）. These are tendencies rather than hard
boundaries: <code>{f|極|きわ}めて</code> does turn up in speech, but the speech in
question is a press conference or a lecture.

One collocation is worth learning whole. <code>{f|極|きわ}めて{f|遺憾|いかん}である</code>
is the standard formula of official displeasure - a government protesting, a
company apologising. It is stronger than it looks in translation, and it is the
sentence you will hear on the evening news.

## Contrast

### {f|極|きわ}めて vs {f|非常|ひじょう}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are formal intensifiers, but
<code>{f|非常|ひじょう}に</code> can be spoken and can intensify verbs of feeling,
while <code>{f|極|きわ}めて</code> is a writer's word that only takes quality
words.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}のあと、<b>{f|非常|ひじょう}に</b>{f|疲|つか}れました。
I was very tired after yesterday's meeting.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事故|じこ}の{f|原因|げんいん}を{f|特定|とくてい}するのは<b>{f|極|きわ}めて</b>{f|難|むずか}しい。
Identifying the cause of the accident is extremely difficult.
</pre>

A speaker thanking you can say <code>{f|非常|ひじょう}にありがたいです</code>; nobody
says ❌<code>{f|極|きわ}めてありがたいです</code>. Going the other way, a written
report can use either, and <code>{f|極|きわ}めて</code> will read as the graver of
the two.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|極|きわ}めて{f|歩|ある}いた。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は<b>{f|非常|ひじょう}に</b>たくさん{f|歩|ある}いた。
  <code>{f|極|きわ}めて</code> intensifies a quality, not an action. Put an
  adjective or a quantity word in front of it and the sentence works again.

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|極|きわ}めての{f|重要|じゅうよう}だ。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は<b>{f|極|きわ}めて{f|重要|じゅうよう}</b>だ。
  No `の`. It is an adverb attaching straight onto the adjective, exactly like
  とても.

- ❌ このケーキ、{f|極|きわ}めておいしい！ → ✅ このケーキ、<b>すごく</b>おいしい！
  Grammatical, but the register is wrong by a wide margin. Nobody praises a cake
  in the language of a ministry statement.

## Practice

**1.** {f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}より：この{f|数値|すうち}は____{f|信頼性|しんらいせい}が{f|低|ひく}く、{f|再調査|さいちょうさ}が{f|必要|ひつよう}である。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|数値|すうち}は<b>{f|極|きわ}めて</b>{f|信頼性|しんらいせい}が{f|低|ひく}く、{f|再調査|さいちょうさ}が{f|必要|ひつよう}である。

A written report with a plain <code>である</code> ending is exactly the habitat of
this word, and <code>{f|低|ひく}い</code> is the kind of evaluative adjective it
takes. すごく would clash with the surrounding style.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: この{f|夏|なつ}は{f|極|きわ}めて{f|汗|あせ}をかいた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|夏|なつ}は<b>{f|非常|ひじょう}に</b>{f|汗|あせ}をかいた。

<code>{f|汗|あせ}をかく</code> is an action, and <code>{f|極|きわ}めて</code> cannot
intensify one. Either swap in <code>{f|非常|ひじょう}に</code>, or rebuild the
sentence around an adjective, as in <code>{f|汗|あせ}の{f|量|りょう}が{f|極|きわ}めて{f|多|おお}かった</code>.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|案|あん}は{f|非常|ひじょう}に{f|難|むずか}しいです。
(b) この{f|案|あん}は{f|極|きわ}めて{f|困難|こんなん}である。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both say the plan is very hard, and neither is more "true" than the other. What
differs is the room they are spoken in. Sentence (a) is polite spoken Japanese and
could be said to a colleague; sentence (b), with the written adverb, the Sino-Japanese
adjective and the <code>である</code> ending, is a line from a document, and saying it
aloud would sound like reading a verdict.

</details>
