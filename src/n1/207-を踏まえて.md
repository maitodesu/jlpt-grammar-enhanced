# ～を踏まえて

**Meaning** · taking X as the footing; in the light of - X is something already established, and whatever comes next was decided with X firmly underfoot

<code>{f|踏|ふ}まえる</code> is literally "to plant your feet on". What goes in front is
a settled thing you cannot now ignore: last meeting's discussion, a survey result, a
change in the law, the lesson of a failure. The sentence then says that the next
step was taken **with that in view**. It does not say the result was made *out of*
X, and it does not say the result is *authorised* by X. It says X was taken fully
into account.

## Formation

<code>{f|踏|ふ}まえる</code> is a る-verb ({f|一段|いちだん}/ichidan/Group 2) and is
transitive, so its object is marked with `を`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + を{f|踏|ふ}まえて | {f|結果|けっか} → {f|結果|けっか}を{f|踏|ふ}まえて |
| Noun (stiffer, documents) | Noun + を{f|踏|ふ}まえ | {f|議論|ぎろん} → {f|議論|ぎろん}を{f|踏|ふ}まえ |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + を{f|踏|ふ}まえた + Noun | {f|反省|はんせい} → {f|反省|はんせい}を{f|踏|ふ}まえた{f|計画|けいかく} |
| Emphasising the step order | Noun + を{f|踏|ふ}まえた{f|上|うえ}で | {f|意見|いけん} → ご{f|意見|いけん}を{f|踏|ふ}まえた{f|上|うえ}で |
| Clause | ～という{f|点|てん} / こと + を{f|踏|ふ}まえて | - → {f|人手|ひとで}が{f|足|た}りないことを{f|踏|ふ}まえて |
| Polite (spoken, business) | Noun + を{f|踏|ふ}まえまして | ご{f|指摘|してき} → ご{f|指摘|してき}を{f|踏|ふ}まえまして |
| Negative | Noun + を{f|踏|ふ}まえずに | {f|実情|じつじょう} → {f|実情|じつじょう}を{f|踏|ふ}まえずに |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the `を` is not optional and not interchangeable.
❌<code>{f|議論|ぎろん}に{f|踏|ふ}まえて</code> is the single most common error on this
pattern, produced by analogy with its `に` neighbours
<code>に{f|基|もと}づいて</code> and <code>に{f|沿|そ}って</code>.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`を踏まえて` is the most usable member of this group. It belongs to the same world -
committee papers, official notices, formal speeches, the minutes of a board meeting -
but unlike <code>に{f|則|のっと}って</code> or <code>に{f|照|て}らして</code> it is
genuinely spoken. In a Japanese office you will hear
<code>{f|前回|ぜんかい}のご{f|指摘|してき}を{f|踏|ふ}まえまして</code> out loud several
times a week, and it is worth being able to produce.

What it is not is casual. Between friends the same idea is
<code>そのへんも{f|考|かんが}えて</code>,
<code>{f|前|まえ}の{f|話|はなし}も{f|入|い}れて</code> or simply
<code>{f|参考|さんこう}にして</code>. Say
<code>{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|話|はなし}を{f|踏|ふ}まえて{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}こう</code>
and you have turned a drink into an agenda item.

Two things it quietly signals. First, respect: naming what somebody said and then
saying you built on it is a standard way of crediting them, which is why
<code>ご{f|意見|いけん}を{f|踏|ふ}まえまして</code> works so well as a reply to
criticism. Second, sequence - the noun came first, the decision came after. The
<code>{f|上|うえ}で</code> variant makes that ordering explicit, and it is the shape
to reach for when you want to insist that you did not decide before listening.

## Contrast

### ～を{f|踏|ふ}まえて vs ～をもとに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>をもとに</code> names the **material** something new was
made out of. <code>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</code> names a **consideration** that was kept in
view; the result is not made of it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|実話|じつわ}<b>をもとに</b>{f|映画|えいが}が{f|作|つく}られた。
The film was made from a true story.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試写会|ししゃかい}の{f|反応|はんのう}<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b>{f|結末|けつまつ}を{f|変|か}えた。
They changed the ending in the light of the preview audience's reaction.
</pre>

The true story is in the film; the audience reaction is not. That is the whole test.
❌<code>{f|実話|じつわ}を{f|踏|ふ}まえた{f|映画|えいが}</code> sounds as though the film
merely bore the true story in mind while being about something else. Conversely
❌<code>{f|反応|はんのう}をもとに{f|結末|けつまつ}を{f|変|か}えた</code> suggests the new
ending was assembled out of audience comments.

### ～を{f|踏|ふ}まえて vs ～に{f|基|もと}づいて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|基|もと}づいて</code> means the result **answers to**
its base and may not depart from it. <code>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</code> means the base was
weighed - and you may still have decided otherwise.

</div>

<pre>
{f|契約|けいやく}{f|書|しょ}<b>に{f|基|もと}づいて</b>{f|費用|ひよう}を{f|請求|せいきゅう}します。
We will invoice the costs under the terms of the contract.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|契約|けいやく}{f|書|しょ}の{f|内容|ないよう}<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b>{f|今後|こんご}の{f|対応|たいおう}を{f|考|かんが}えます。
We will work out how to proceed with the contract's terms in mind.
</pre>

The first is a legal claim; if the invoice departs from the contract it is simply
wrong. The second promises only that the contract will be read. This is exactly why a
company under pressure writes
<code>ご{f|意見|いけん}を{f|踏|ふ}まえて{f|検討|けんとう}します</code> rather than
<code>ご{f|意見|いけん}に{f|基|もと}づいて</code> - the weaker verb commits it to
nothing.

### ～を{f|踏|ふ}まえて vs ～を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</code> **assumes** X to be true and
builds on it; if X turns out false the whole plan collapses.
<code>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</code> merely **factors X in**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|値上|ねあ}げ<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|来年度|らいねんど}の{f|予算|よさん}を{f|組|く}んだ。
We built next year's budget on the assumption of a price rise.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値上|ねあ}げの{f|可能性|かのうせい}<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b>{f|来年度|らいねんど}の{f|予算|よさん}を{f|組|く}んだ。
We built next year's budget with the possibility of a price rise in view.
</pre>

If the rise never happens, the first budget has to be rewritten and the second does
not. Note also the tense difference hiding in the two: a {f|前提|ぜんてい} can be
something that has not happened yet, while what you {f|踏|ふ}まえる is normally
already on the record.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|議論|ぎろん}<b>に{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b> → ✅ {f|議論|ぎろん}<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b>
  The verb is transitive. Its `に` neighbours in this group pull learners the wrong
  way, but <code>{f|踏|ふ}まえる</code> never takes `に`.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b>{f|日程|にってい}を{f|決|き}めた。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>ことを{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b>{f|日程|にってい}を{f|決|き}めた。
  A clause has to be nominalised with こと or repackaged as
  <code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}</code>, <code>{f|点|てん}</code> or
  <code>{f|事実|じじつ}</code> before it can be an object.

- ❌ {f|実話|じつわ}<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえた</b>{f|小説|しょうせつ} → ✅ {f|実話|じつわ}<b>をもとにした</b>{f|小説|しょうせつ}
  Source material takes <code>をもとに</code>. <code>{f|踏|ふ}まえる</code> would
  say the novel is about something else and merely remembered the true story.

- ❌ {f|週末|しゅうまつ}の{f|天気|てんき}<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b>、{f|遊|あそ}びに{f|行|い}こうよ。 → ✅ {f|週末|しゅうまつ}の{f|天気|てんき}<b>を{f|見|み}て</b>、{f|遊|あそ}びに{f|行|い}こうよ。
  Correct grammar, wrong register. Inviting a friend out is not a policy decision,
  and the formal verb turns the invitation into a memo.

## Practice

**1.** {f|今回|こんかい}の{f|事故|じこ}____、{f|安全|あんぜん}{f|点検|てんけん}の{f|回数|かいすう}を{f|増|ふ}やすことにした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|事故|じこ}<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b>、{f|回数|かいすう}を{f|増|ふ}やすことにした。

The accident is an established fact that forced a rethink, and the decision was taken
with it in view. <code>を{f|踏|ふ}まえ</code> is the shape a written notice would use.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|祖母|そぼ}の{f|日記|にっき}（を{f|踏|ふ}まえて・をもとに）{f|一冊|いっさつ}の{f|本|ほん}を{f|書|か}いた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|祖母|そぼ}の{f|日記|にっき}<b>をもとに</b>{f|一冊|いっさつ}の{f|本|ほん}を{f|書|か}いた。

The diary is the material the book was made out of, so <code>をもとに</code> is the
only natural choice. <code>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</code> would mean the writer had read the
diary and borne it in mind while writing about something else. Note the counter:
{f|一冊|いっさつ}, with gemination.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Taking your comments into account, we will submit a revised proposal next week."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

いただいたご{f|意見|いけん}<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえまして</b>、{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|修正|しゅうせい}{f|案|あん}を{f|提出|ていしゅつ}いたします。

<code>を{f|踏|ふ}まえまして</code> is the polite spoken form and is exactly what this
sentence wants - a business reply that credits the other side. The plain
<code>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</code> would be fine in an email.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|市民|しみん}の{f|要望|ようぼう}に{f|踏|ふ}まえて、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|公園|こうえん}を{f|作|つく}ることになった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|市民|しみん}の{f|要望|ようぼう}<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b>、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|公園|こうえん}を{f|作|つく}ることになった。

The particle is `を`. If you want a `に` pattern here,
<code>{f|要望|ようぼう}に{f|応|こた}えて</code> works and is warmer - it says the
request was granted, not merely considered.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ご{f|指摘|してき}<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b>{f|検討|けんとう}いたします。
(b) ご{f|指摘|してき}<b>に{f|基|もと}づいて</b>{f|修正|しゅうせい}いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) promises that the criticism will be taken into account during consideration. It
is courteous and it commits to nothing: the outcome may be no change at all.

(b) says the correction will be made to match what was pointed out. The criticism now
functions as the specification. In Japanese business correspondence the gap between
these two sentences is the difference between a polite holding reply and an
undertaking.

</details>
