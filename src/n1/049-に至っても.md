# ～に至っても

**Meaning** · even after things had come to that; even at this late stage - and still nothing changed. Read <code>に{f|至|いた}っても</code>

に{f|至|いた}る gives you the point a process finally reached. Add ても and the
sentence becomes a concession: things had gone all the way to *that*, and the
response that should have come still did not. What follows is nearly always negative
or critical, and the writer's impatience is audible.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (a stage or a time) | Noun + に{f|至|いた}っても | {f|当日|とうじつ} → {f|当日|とうじつ}に{f|至|いた}っても |
| Verb (dictionary form) | Verb + に{f|至|いた}っても | {f|死者|ししゃ}が{f|出|で}る → {f|死者|ししゃ}が{f|出|で}るに{f|至|いた}っても |
| Fixed phrase | {f|今|いま}に{f|至|いた}っても | {f|今|いま}に{f|至|いた}っても{f|説明|せつめい}がない |
| Emphatic, written | ～に{f|至|いた}ってもなお | …に{f|至|いた}ってもなお{f|変|か}わらない |
| Sibling: "only when it got that far" | ～に{f|至|いた}って | {f|事故|じこ}が{f|起|お}きるに{f|至|いた}って、ようやく… |
| Sibling: "as for the worst case" | ～に{f|至|いた}っては | {f|彼|かれ}に{f|至|いた}っては{f|返事|へんじ}もしない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - three siblings differ only by the particle, and the particle
carries the whole meaning. <code>に{f|至|いた}って</code> = *only when it finally
got that far* did something happen. <code>に{f|至|いた}っては</code> = *as for that
one*, picking out the most extreme case. <code>に{f|至|いた}っても</code> = *even
though it got that far*, and still nothing. Learn them as a set or you will pick the
wrong one under time pressure.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Formal written Japanese**: reporting, criticism, official complaint, the
discussion section of a report. It is not conversational, and it is not neutral -
the whole point of choosing ても over a plain ても clause is to say that the stage
reached was **extreme enough that a response was overdue**.

It therefore pairs naturally with なお, いまだに, {f|依然|いぜん}として, まだ,
{f|一切|いっさい} and with a negative predicate. If your sentence has no frustrated
expectation in it, this is the wrong pattern.

The everyday equivalents are ordinary. In speech you would say
<code>こんなことになっても、まだ…</code> or simply
<code>ここまで{f|来|き}ても</code>. There is also the spoken idiom
<code>この{f|期|ご}に{f|及|およ}んで</code>, "at this late stage, after all
this" - close in feeling and much more colloquial. Note its reading: {f|期|ご},
not き.

One more caution. The stage named in front of に{f|至|いた}っても has to be
**serious**. A trivial time point makes the sentence pompous rather than pointed,
which is why real examples cluster around deadlines that have arrived, trials that
have opened, accidents that have repeated and damage that has already been done.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|至|いた}っても vs ～に{f|至|いた}って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|至|いた}って</code>: the response finally **did**
come, but only at that late stage. <code>に{f|至|いた}っても</code>: even at that
stage, it **still did not**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|死者|ししゃ}が{f|出|で}るに<b>{f|至|いた}って</b>、ようやく{f|対策|たいさく}が{f|取|と}られた。
It took a death before any measures were finally taken.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|死者|ししゃ}が{f|出|で}るに<b>{f|至|いた}っても</b>、{f|対策|たいさく}は{f|取|と}られなかった。
Even after a death, no measures were taken.
</pre>

Both are critical, and they criticise different things: the first the delay, the
second the total absence of a response. ようやく belongs with に{f|至|いた}って;
まだ and いまだに belong with に{f|至|いた}っても.

### ～に{f|至|いた}っても vs ～に{f|至|いた}っては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|至|いた}っては</code> picks out **the worst example
in a list** - "and as for X…". <code>に{f|至|いた}っても</code> marks **a stage
reached in time** and concedes it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|遅|おそ}い{f|人|ひと}は{f|多|おお}いが、{f|彼|かれ}に<b>{f|至|いた}っては</b>{f|一度|いちど}も{f|返|かえ}してこない。
Plenty of people are slow to reply, but as for him, he has never replied once.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|催促|さいそく}を{f|三度|さんど}{f|送|おく}るに<b>{f|至|いた}っても</b>、{f|返事|へんじ}はなかった。
Even after three reminders had been sent, there was no reply.
</pre>

は needs something to contrast against - a group of cases, of which one is the
extreme. も needs a point on a timeline that ought to have triggered a response.
Getting these two round the wrong way is the commonest error in the family.

### ～に{f|至|いた}っても vs ～ても・にもかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - all three concede. The plain <code>ても</code> and
<code>にもかかわらず</code> are **neutral**; <code>に{f|至|いた}っても</code> adds
that the point conceded is an **extreme one**, reached after a long build-up.

</div>

<pre>
{f|何度|なんど}{f|注意|ちゅうい}し<b>ても</b>、{f|直|なお}らなかった。
However often he was warned, it made no difference.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|処分|しょぶん}を{f|受|う}けるに<b>{f|至|いた}っても</b>、{f|態度|たいど}は{f|変|か}わらなかった。
Even when it came to a formal reprimand, his attitude did not change.
</pre>

If your English is simply "even though", ても is enough. Reach for
に{f|至|いた}っても only when you want the reader to feel how far things had gone.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|事故|じこ}が{f|起|お}きたに<b>{f|至|いた}っても</b> → ✅ {f|事故|じこ}が{f|起|お}きるに<b>{f|至|いた}っても</b>
  The verb before に{f|至|いた}る stays in the dictionary form, however far in the
  past the event is.

- ❌ {f|事故|じこ}が{f|起|お}きるに<b>{f|至|いた}っても</b>、ようやく{f|対策|たいさく}が{f|取|と}られた → ✅ {f|事故|じこ}が{f|起|お}きるに<b>{f|至|いた}って</b>、ようやく{f|対策|たいさく}が{f|取|と}られた
  ても requires a **frustrated** expectation. If the response finally did arrive,
  the particle is plain に{f|至|いた}って.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}に<b>{f|至|いた}っても</b>{f|挨拶|あいさつ}もしない → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}に<b>{f|至|いた}っては</b>{f|挨拶|あいさつ}もしない
  Singling out a person as the extreme case takes は.

- ❌ お{f|昼|ひる}に<b>{f|至|いた}っても</b>お{f|腹|なか}がすかない → ✅ お{f|昼|ひる}になってもお{f|腹|なか}がすかない
  Lunchtime is not a grave stage in a deteriorating situation. The heavy form makes
  a small observation sound like a public inquiry.

## Practice

**1.** {f|工事|こうじ}の{f|完成|かんせい}{f|予定|よてい}{f|日|び}____、{f|作業|さぎょう}は{f|半分|はんぶん}も{f|進|すす}んでいなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|完成|かんせい}{f|予定|よてい}{f|日|び}<b>に{f|至|いた}っても</b>

The completion date is the stage that should have forced progress, and the negative
predicate {f|進|すす}んでいなかった supplies the frustrated expectation. Note the
noun attaches directly, with no する.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|遅刻|ちこく}する{f|社員|しゃいん}は{f|何人|なんにん}かいるが、{f|新人|しんじん}の{f|田中|たなか}さん（に{f|至|いた}っては・に{f|至|いた}っても）{f|連絡|れんらく}すらしない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|田中|たなか}さん<b>に{f|至|いた}っては</b>{f|連絡|れんらく}すらしない。

The first clause sets up a group of latecomers and the second picks out the worst of
them, which is exactly what は does here. も would need a point in time, not a
person.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Even after the matter had gone to court, the company still issued no apology."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|裁判|さいばん}になる<b>に{f|至|いた}っても</b>、{f|会社|かいしゃ}はいまだに{f|謝罪|しゃざい}していない。

"Even after it had gone to court" is the extreme stage, and いまだに supplies the
"still" that the pattern almost always wants. The dictionary form なる is correct in front of に{f|至|いた}っても.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会社|かいしゃ}が{f|倒産|とうさん}するに{f|至|いた}っても、ようやく{f|社長|しゃちょう}は{f|責任|せきにん}を{f|認|みと}めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会社|かいしゃ}が{f|倒産|とうさん}するに<b>{f|至|いた}って</b>、ようやく{f|社長|しゃちょう}は{f|責任|せきにん}を{f|認|みと}めた。

ようやく says that the response did eventually come, which contradicts the ても.
Drop the も and the sentence works: it took the bankruptcy before he would admit
anything. Keeping も would require a negative ending, such as
{f|認|みと}めなかった.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|三年|さんねん}{f|経|た}っ<b>ても</b>、{f|補償|ほしょう}の{f|話|はなし}は{f|進|すす}んでいない。
(b) {f|事故|じこ}から{f|三年|さんねん}が{f|過|す}ぎるに<b>{f|至|いた}っても</b>、{f|補償|ほしょう}の{f|話|はなし}は{f|進|すす}んでいない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain, neutral concession. Three years have passed and nothing has moved;
the sentence reports it and could appear in any register.

(b) frames those three years as a point that had been reached after a long and
visible process, and the heavier form carries the writer's judgement: by this stage
the delay is no longer explicable, it is a failure. It belongs in a critical article
or a formal complaint, and it would sound stilted in conversation.

</details>
