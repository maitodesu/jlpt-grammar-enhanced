# ～だに

**Meaning** · even; so much as - "merely to do this is already X", or, with a negative, "did not do it even a little"

`だに` is an Old Japanese particle meaning "even, at least". In the modern language
it is not a live piece of grammar: it survives inside a short list of frozen
expressions - <code>{f|想像|そうぞう}するだに</code>,
<code>{f|微動|びどう}だにしない</code>, <code>{f|予想|よそう}だにしなかった</code> -
and almost nowhere else. Learn to read it; write `さえ`, `だけでも` or
<code>{f|少|すこ}しも～ない</code> instead.

## Formation

Two shapes, and each is limited to particular words.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (dictionary form) + emotive predicate | ～するだに + {f|恐|おそ}ろしい etc. | {f|想像|そうぞう}する → {f|想像|そうぞう}するだに{f|恐|おそ}ろしい |
| Verb (dictionary form) | {f|聞|き}く・{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}す + だに | {f|聞|き}く → {f|聞|き}くだに{f|腹立|はらだ}たしい |
| する-noun + だに + negative する | ～だにしない | {f|予想|よそう} → {f|予想|よそう}だにしなかった |
| Noun (fixed set) + だに + negative する | ～だにしない | {f|微動|びどう} → {f|微動|びどう}だにしない |
| Continuative | ～だにせず | {f|微動|びどう} → {f|微動|びどう}だにせず |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the whole productive inventory is roughly this:
<code>{f|想像|そうぞう}するだに</code>, <code>{f|聞|き}くだに</code>,
<code>{f|考|かんが}えるだに</code>, <code>{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}すだに</code>,
<code>{f|想像|そうぞう}だにしない</code>, <code>{f|予想|よそう}だにしない</code>,
<code>{f|微動|びどう}だにしない</code>, <code>{f|一顧|いっこ}だにしない</code>.
Anything you build outside that list will read as a mistake rather than as
elevated style.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|想像|そうぞう}する<b>だに</b>{f|恐|おそ}ろしい。
It is terrifying merely to imagine.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|石|いし}の{f|像|ぞう}は{f|微動|びどう}<b>だに</b>しなかった。
The stone statue did not stir in the slightest.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|聞|き}く<b>だに</b>{f|腹立|はらだ}たしい{f|話|はなし}である。
It is a story that angers you just to hear it.
</pre>

<pre>
こんな{f|結末|けつまつ}になろうとは、{f|予想|よそう}<b>だに</b>しなかった。
That it would end like this I had not foreseen for a moment.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大臣|だいじん}は{f|記者|きしゃ}たちの{f|抗議|こうぎ}を{f|一顧|いっこ}<b>だに</b>せず、{f|車|くるま}に{f|乗|の}り{f|込|こ}んだ。
The minister got into the car without so much as a glance at the reporters' protests.
</pre>

<pre>

The screaming of that night is painful even to recall.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|焼|や}け{f|野原|のはら}に{f|立|た}っていた{f|人々|ひとびと}は、この{f|街|まち}が{f|半世紀|はんせいき}{f|後|ご}にこうなるとは{f|想像|そうぞう}<b>だに</b>しなかっただろう。
The people who stood in those burnt-out fields can hardly have imagined that the city would look like this half a century later.
</pre>

<pre>

However often the presiding judge prompted him, the witness would not speak, and sat staring ahead without moving a muscle.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise this one; do not produce it.** `だに` is a survival from classical
Japanese, and outside the fixed expressions listed above it is dead. Where you
will actually meet it: literary narration, memoirs and war writing, newspaper
commentary, courtroom and historical reporting, and the occasional deliberately
grand line in a speech. In ordinary modern Japanese, say
<code>{f|想像|そうぞう}するだけでも{f|恐|おそ}ろしい</code> and
<code>{f|少|すこ}しも{f|動|うご}かなかった</code>.

There is one real exception, and it is worth knowing. <b>{f|微動|びどう}だにしない</b> **has escaped into everyday speech.** People say it about a jammed door, a frozen
screen or a stubborn opinion, and it does not sound literary at all - it sounds
like a set phrase, which is what it is.
<code>{f|一顧|いっこ}だにしない</code> and
<code>{f|予想|よそう}だにしなかった</code> stay firmly in writing.

The verb use leans towards **unpleasant** feeling. The predicates that follow it
are {f|恐|おそ}ろしい, {f|身|み}の{f|毛|け}がよだつ, {f|腹立|はらだ}たしい and
{f|胸|むね}が{f|痛|いた}む - things you would rather not dwell on. That is a strong tendency rather than an absolute rule, but a cheerful
<code>{f|想像|そうぞう}するだに{f|楽|たの}しい</code> is rare enough that a reader
would notice it.

One more surface point: `だに` is almost never written in kanji, and there is no
polite form of it. The politeness of the sentence lives in the final predicate -
<code>{f|微動|びどう}だにしませんでした</code> is perfectly possible.

## Contrast

### ～だに vs ～だけでも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だけでも` is the modern equivalent of the verb use. Same
meaning, no archaic flavour, and it attaches to any verb you like.

</div>

<pre>
{f|想像|そうぞう}する<b>だに</b>{f|恐|おそ}ろしい{f|光景|こうけい}だった。
It was a scene terrible merely to imagine.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|想像|そうぞう}する<b>だけでも</b>{f|恐|おそ}ろしい{f|光景|こうけい}だった。
It was a scene that was terrible just to imagine.
</pre>

The second is what you should say. The first is what you should be able to read.
The gap between them is not meaning but distance: `だに` makes the sentence sound
like narration, and in the middle of a conversation that distance registers as
theatrical.

### ～だにしない vs {f|少|すこ}しも～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for the negative use, the modern replacement is
<code>{f|少|すこ}しも</code>, <code>まったく</code> or
<code>{f|夢|ゆめ}にも</code> plus a negative.

</div>

<pre>
ドアは{f|微動|びどう}<b>だに</b>しなかった。
The door did not budge an inch.
</pre>

<pre>
ドアは<b>まったく</b>{f|動|うご}かなかった。
The door did not move at all.
</pre>

For <code>{f|予想|よそう}だにしなかった</code> the everyday version is
<code>{f|夢|ゆめ}にも{f|思|おも}わなかった</code>, and for
<code>{f|一顧|いっこ}だにしない</code> it is
<code>{f|見向|みむ}きもしない</code>. Learning those three pairs is most of what
this page is for.

### ～だに vs ～すら・～さえ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `すら` and `さえ` attach freely to nouns and are alive. `だに`
attaches to a closed list and is not. They are not interchangeable in either
direction.

</div>

<pre>
{f|水|みず}<b>すら</b>{f|飲|の}まずに{f|歩|ある}き{f|続|つづ}けた。
He kept walking without so much as drinking water.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|想像|そうぞう}する<b>だに</b>{f|恐|おそ}ろしい{f|道|みち}のりだった。
It was a journey terrible even to imagine.
</pre>

❌<code>{f|水|みず}だに{f|飲|の}まずに</code> is not Japanese, and
❌<code>{f|想像|そうぞう}するすら</code> is not either. `すら` wants a noun; `だに`
wants one of its handful of licensed partners. Where both are possible - and there
is almost nowhere - `だに` is the older and heavier of the two.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|音|おと}<b>だに</b>{f|聞|き}こえなかった。 → ✅ {f|音|おと}<b>すら</b>{f|聞|き}こえなかった。
  `だに` does not attach to ordinary nouns. Only the frozen partners -
  {f|微動|びどう}, {f|一顧|いっこ}, {f|予想|よそう}, {f|想像|そうぞう} - take it.

- ❌ {f|想像|そうぞう}する<b>だに</b>しなかった。 → ✅ {f|想像|そうぞう}<b>だに</b>しなかった。
  The two shapes are separate. Before the negative <code>しない</code> the base is
  the bare noun, not the verb.

- ❌ {f|微動|びどう}だに<b>する</b>。 → ✅ {f|微動|びどう}だに<b>しない</b>。
  The negative use only exists as a negative. There is no affirmative
  <code>{f|微動|びどう}だにする</code>.

- ❌ そのケーキ、{f|想像|そうぞう}する<b>だに</b>おいしそうだね。 → ✅ そのケーキ、{f|想像|そうぞう}する<b>だけでも</b>おいしそうだね。
  Wrong on two counts: the register is casual, and the feeling is a pleasant one.
  Both cut against what `だに` is for.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to meet this sentence, and what would the everyday
version be? {f|首相|しゅしょう}は{f|野党|やとう}の{f|要求|ようきゅう}を{f|一顧|いっこ}<b>だに</b>しなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A newspaper political report, or a commentary column - it is written register, and
`だに` is doing the work of a cold, formal verdict.

Everyday: {f|首相|しゅしょう}は{f|野党|やとう}の{f|要求|ようきゅう}を<b>まったく{f|相手|あいて}にしなかった</b>、
or {f|見向|みむ}きもしなかった.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|当時|とうじ}の{f|人々|ひとびと}は、{f|人|ひと}が{f|月|つき}に{f|立|た}つ{f|日|ひ}が{f|来|く}るとは{f|想像|そうぞう}<b>だに</b>しなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|当時|とうじ}の{f|人々|ひとびと}は、{f|人|ひと}が{f|月|つき}に{f|立|た}つ{f|日|ひ}が{f|来|く}るとは<b>{f|夢|ゆめ}にも{f|思|おも}わなかった</b>。

<code>{f|想像|そうぞう}だにしなかった</code> and
<code>{f|夢|ゆめ}にも{f|思|おも}わなかった</code> are the literary and the everyday
version of one idea. Note that <code>{f|来|く}る</code> keeps its dictionary form
before とは - the archaism is in `だに` alone.

</details>

**3.** Choose, and say why: この{f|机|つくえ}、{f|重|おも}すぎて（{f|微動|びどう}だにしない・{f|一顧|いっこ}だにしない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|微動|びどう}<b>だにしない</b>

<code>{f|微動|びどう}</code> is a tiny movement, so the phrase means "will not budge",
which is what a heavy desk does. <code>{f|一顧|いっこ}</code> is a single glance, and
that phrase means to ignore a person or a request. This is also the one `だに`
expression that is fine in casual speech.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|戦争|せんそう}の{f|話|はなし}は{f|聞|き}くだに{f|嬉|うれ}しくなる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|戦争|せんそう}の{f|話|はなし}は{f|聞|き}く<b>だに</b>{f|恐|おそ}ろしい。

The construction is sound; the emotion is not. <code>{f|聞|き}くだに</code> introduces
something you would rather not have heard, so it needs a predicate like
{f|恐|おそ}ろしい or {f|腹立|はらだ}たしい. Pairing it with {f|嬉|うれ}しい reads as a
sentence written by someone matching a pattern rather than meaning it.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in register between:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|裏切|うらぎ}りは{f|予想|よそう}<b>だに</b>しなかった。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|裏切|うらぎ}りは{f|予想|よそう}もしていなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The content is identical. (a) is written Japanese: memoir, novel, a formal
statement read from paper. Spoken aloud in conversation it sounds staged.

(b) is what a person says. Note that the modern version also lets you use
<code>ていない</code> for the continuing state, which the frozen `だに` phrase
cannot - <code>{f|予想|よそう}だにしていなかった</code> is possible but rare, and the
plain past is the shape you will actually see.

</details>
