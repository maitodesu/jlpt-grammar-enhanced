# ～ときている

**Meaning** · and on top of that it's ~; and since it's ~ (of all things) - adds the fact that clinches the case

`ときている` throws one more fact onto a pile and lets it land. The speaker has
already listed one or two things; this is the last item, delivered with a small
thump, and it is the one that settles the matter. Depending on what is being piled
up, the effect is exasperation ("and the boss repeats himself, on top of it all") or
delight ("cheap, fast, *and* delicious"). It is spoken Japanese with an attitude in
it, and it is written both in kana and, in print, as
<code>と{f|来|き}ている</code>.

## Formation

The clause in front is plain. Nouns and な-adjectives attach bare.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | noun + ときている | {f|夜勤|やきん} → {f|夜勤|やきん}ときている |
| な-adjective | stem + ときている | {f|下手|へた} → {f|下手|へた}ときている |
| い-adjective | plain form + ときている | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}いときている |
| Verb | plain form + ときている | {f|遅|おく}れる → {f|遅|おく}れるときている |
| Verb (negative) | ～ない + ときている | {f|払|はら}わない → {f|払|はら}わないときている |
| Contracted (speech) | ～ときてる | {f|狭|せま}い → {f|狭|せま}いときてる |
| Followed by a reason | ～ときているから | {f|遠|とお}い → {f|遠|とお}いときているから |
| Conditional variant | ～ときては | {f|留守|るす} → {f|留守|るす}ときては |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the {f|来|く}る inside it is the ordinary verb "come", frozen in the
て-form. That is why the whole thing is <code>と{f|来|き}ている</code> and never
❌<code>と{f|来|こ}ている</code>. Nothing in the sentence is actually arriving; think
of it as "and then along comes the fact that…".

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|安|やす}くて{f|早|はや}い。しかもうまい<b>ときている</b>。
Cheap and quick - and it tastes good into the bargain.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|隣|となり}の{f|犬|いぬ}は{f|夜中|よなか}に{f|吠|ほ}える。おまけに{f|人|ひと}を{f|見|み}ると{f|飛|と}びつく<b>ときている</b>。
The dog next door barks in the middle of the night - and it jumps at anyone it sees, too.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|仕事|しごと}が{f|速|はや}い{f|上|うえ}に、{f|字|じ}まできれい<b>ときている</b>。
He works fast, and on top of that he has beautiful handwriting.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}は{f|駅|えき}から{f|遠|とお}い<b>ときているから</b>、{f|客|きゃく}が{f|入|はい}らないのも{f|無理|むり}はない。
The place is miles from the station on top of everything, so no wonder it gets no customers.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}い、{f|日当|ひあ}たりは{f|悪|わる}い、{f|家賃|やちん}は{f|高|たか}い<b>ときている</b>。よく{f|借|か}り{f|手|て}がついたものだ。

Our section chief talks about nothing but golf - and tells you the same story three times, at that.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りは{f|明日|あした}、{f|資料|しりょう}はまだ{f|届|とど}かない、{f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}は{f|出張中|しゅっちょうちゅう}<b>ときては</b>、{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わせようがない。
Deadline tomorrow, materials still not here, and the person in charge away on a trip - there's no way to make it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}は{f|据|す}え{f|置|お}き、{f|量|りょう}はそのまま、それでいて{f|味|あじ}は{f|以前|いぜん}より{f|良|よ}くなった<b>ときている</b>んですから、{f|売|う}れないはずがありません。
Same price, same portion, and better-tasting than before on top of that - of course it sells.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a **spoken** pattern, and a colourful one. It lives in conversation, in
column-writing that imitates conversation, in comedy routines and in the grumbling
half of a phone call. It is not rude, but it is never neutral: using `ときている`
means you have taken a position on the facts you are listing.

The characteristic shape is a **list with a punchline**. One or two facts are laid
out with `し`, `上に`, `おまけに` or just a full stop, and then the last one arrives
with `ときている`. Because it is the closer, it usually carries the most extreme
item. Put it on the mildest fact in the list and the sentence deflates.

It also normally **stays plain** even when the surrounding conversation is polite.
Speakers reach for <code>～ときているんです</code> or
<code>～ときているものですから</code> rather than ❌<code>ときています</code>, which
sounds like a joke about being formal. In a written report it is out of place
altogether; there you would write `～上に` or `しかも`.

One more feature worth knowing: the subject of a `ときている` sentence is almost
always **someone or something other than the speaker**. Turning it on yourself -
<code>{f|私|わたし}は{f|不器用|ぶきよう}ときている</code> - is possible, but it comes
out as deliberate self-mockery, the voice of an essayist making fun of himself.

## Contrast

### ～ときている vs ～とあって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `とあって` is a **reporter's** cause: neutral, written, no
judgement. `ときている` is a **talker's** cause: colloquial, evaluative, and always
piling on.

</div>

<pre>
{f|人手|ひとで}{f|不足|ぶそく}<b>とあって</b>、{f|店|みせ}は{f|営業|えいぎょう}{f|時間|じかん}を{f|短縮|たんしゅく}した。
Short of staff, the shop cut its opening hours.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人|ひと}は{f|足|た}りない、{f|機械|きかい}は{f|古|ふる}い<b>ときている</b>。もう{f|回|まわ}らないよ。
Not enough people, and clapped-out machines on top of that. It just doesn't work any more.
</pre>

You could not put the second sentence in a news bulletin, and the first would sound
oddly stiff in a break room. Note too that `ときている` almost demands company: it
wants at least one other fact beside it, while `とあって` is perfectly happy alone.

### ～ときている vs ～ときたら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ときたら` puts a **topic** up front, ready to be complained
about. `ときている` puts a **fact** at the end, as the last straw.

</div>

<pre>
うちの{f|息子|むすこ}<b>ときたら</b>、{f|休|やす}みの{f|日|ひ}は{f|昼|ひる}まで{f|寝|ね}ている。
My son, honestly - on days off he sleeps until noon.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|休|やす}みの{f|日|ひ}は{f|昼|ひる}まで{f|寝|ね}ている、{f|起|お}きても{f|手伝|てつだ}わない<b>ときている</b>。
He sleeps until noon on his days off, and when he does get up he doesn't lift a finger.
</pre>

They share a register and often appear in the same breath, but they occupy different
slots in the sentence. `ときたら` replaces `は`; `ときている` replaces the final
`だ`. Learners who mix them produce ❌<code>うちの{f|息子|むすこ}ときている、…</code>,
which leaves the sentence with a predicate and no subject.

### ～ときている vs ～{f|上|うえ}に・しかも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `上に` and `しかも` add another item **neutrally**. `ときている`
adds it and simultaneously says *and that settles it*.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}い{f|上|うえ}に、{f|窓|まど}が{f|一|ひと}つもない。
This room is cramped, and it has no windows.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}い。{f|窓|まど}が{f|一|ひと}つもない<b>ときている</b>。
Cramped - and not a single window, at that.
</pre>

The information is identical; the second sentence has a speaker in it. Note that
`上に` can add a good thing to a good thing quite blandly, whereas `ときている` will
colour even praise with a kind of "would you believe it" astonishment.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|店|みせ}は{f|安|やす}いだ<b>ときている</b>。 → ✅ この{f|店|みせ}は{f|安|やす}い<b>ときている</b>。
  い-adjectives attach in plain form. There is never a `だ` before `ときている`, and
  nouns attach bare as well: <code>{f|夜勤|やきん}ときている</code>.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}<b>ときている</b>、{f|約束|やくそく}を{f|守|まも}ったことがない。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}<b>ときたら</b>、{f|約束|やくそく}を{f|守|まも}ったことがない。
  A topic at the head of the sentence is `ときたら`. `ときている` can only close a
  clause.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っている<b>ときている</b>ので、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}って{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っている<b>ので</b>、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}って{f|行|い}きます。
  A single plain reason has nothing to be piled on top of. `ときている` needs a list
  behind it and an attitude in it.

- ❌ {f|交通|こうつう}の{f|便|べん}も{f|悪|わる}い<b>ときています</b>。 → ✅ {f|交通|こうつう}の{f|便|べん}も{f|悪|わる}い<b>ときているんです</b>。
  The pattern resists ます. If you need politeness, wrap it in `んです` and leave the
  `ときている` itself plain.

## Practice

**1.** {f|給料|きゅうりょう}は{f|安|やす}い、{f|残業|ざんぎょう}は{f|多|おお}い、おまけに{f|上司|じょうし}は{f|話|はなし}を{f|聞|き}いてくれない____。よく{f|続|つづ}けているものだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|聞|き}いてくれない<b>ときている</b>

Three complaints in a row, with the last one delivered as the clincher, is the exact
shape this pattern was made for. The verb stays in plain negative form in front of
it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: あの{f|人|ひと}（ときたら・ときている）、{f|人|ひと}の{f|話|はなし}を{f|最後|さいご}まで{f|聞|き}かない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

あの{f|人|ひと}<b>ときたら</b>

The phrase sits at the head of the sentence marking the topic to be complained
about, which is `ときたら`. `ときている` would have to come at the end, after the
predicate.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The hotel was old, the food was cold, and the staff were rude on top of it all."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ホテルは{f|古|ふる}い、{f|料理|りょうり}は{f|冷|つめ}たい、{f|係|かかり}の{f|人|ひと}は{f|失礼|しつれい}<b>ときている</b>。

"On top of it all" attached to the last item of a grumbling list is `ときている`
almost word for word. {f|失礼|しつれい} is a な-adjective, so it attaches with no `だ`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|息子|むすこ}は{f|勉強|べんきょう}もしないし、{f|部屋|へや}も{f|片付|かたづ}けないときていますから、{f|困|こま}っています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|息子|むすこ}は{f|勉強|べんきょう}もしないし、{f|部屋|へや}も{f|片付|かたづ}けない<b>ときているんです</b>から、{f|困|こま}っています。

The content is right; only the politeness is misplaced. `ときています` sounds like a
parody of formal speech, so keep the pattern plain and let `んです` carry the
politeness.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|地域|ちいき}はバスの{f|本数|ほんすう}が{f|少|すく}ない{f|上|うえ}に、{f|終電|しゅうでん}も{f|早|はや}い。
(b) この{f|地域|ちいき}はバスの{f|本数|ほんすう}が{f|少|すく}ない。{f|終電|しゅうでん}も{f|早|はや}い<b>ときている</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a neutral description. It would be at home in a guidebook, a survey, or an
estate agent's honest summary, and the speaker's feelings are not on display.

(b) is someone talking. The early last train is offered as the final blow, and the
listener is expected to react - to sympathise, or to agree that living there would
be impossible. The facts are the same; only (b) has a complaint inside it.

</details>
