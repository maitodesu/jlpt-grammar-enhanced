# あくまでも

**Meaning** · (1) purely; merely; nothing more than - and (2) to the very end,
without giving an inch

Two senses that look unrelated share one idea: a line that does not move. In sense
1 the line is around a claim - this is only that, do not read more into it. In
sense 2 it is around a position - I will not shift from it.

## Usage

`あくまで` and `あくまでも` are the same word; the `も` adds emphasis and is very
common in sense 1. It is written in kana in practice, though the kanji
<code>{f|飽|あ}くまで</code> exists.

**Sense 1 - limiting a claim.** The frame is `あくまでも` + a noun + `だ／です`, or
`あくまでも` + a clause + <code>だけだ／にすぎない</code>. The nouns that turn up
here are worth learning as a set: <code>{f|一例|いちれい}</code>,
<code>{f|目安|めやす}</code>, <code>{f|参考|さんこう}</code>,
<code>{f|予定|よてい}</code>, <code>{f|個人|こじん}の{f|意見|いけん}</code>. This is
the sense you meet daily in meetings, emails and small print.

**Sense 2 - holding out.** Here `あくまで` modifies a verb of persistence or
resistance: <code>{f|争|あらそ}う</code>, <code>{f|否定|ひてい}する</code>,
<code>{f|反対|はんたい}する</code>, <code>{f|貫|つらぬ}く</code>,
<code>{f|認|みと}めない</code>. The meaning is "right through to the end, whatever
happened", so a sentence that goes on to say the person gave in contradicts itself.

What it never means is "very". The dictionary gloss "to the utmost degree" tempts
learners into ❌あくまでも{f|疲|つか}れた, which is not Japanese.

## Examples

<pre>
これは<b>あくまでも</b>{f|目安|めやす}です。
This is no more than a rough guide.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>あくまで</b>{f|私|わたし}{f|個人|こじん}の{f|意見|いけん}ですが、この{f|案|あん}には{f|無理|むり}があると{f|思|おも}います。
Speaking purely for myself, I think this plan is a stretch.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|数字|すうじ}は<b>あくまでも</b>{f|現時点|げんじてん}のもので、{f|来月|らいげつ}には{f|変|か}わる{f|可能性|かのうせい}があります。
The figures are only as of today, and they may well change next month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会社|かいしゃ}{f|側|がわ}は<b>あくまで</b>{f|責任|せきにん}を{f|認|みと}めず、{f|裁判|さいばん}は{f|五年|ごねん}に{f|及|およ}んだ。
The company refused to admit responsibility to the very end, and the trial dragged on for five years.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Sense 1 is formal but entirely sayable, and it is one of the most useful pieces of
working Japanese in this group. A consultant presenting a forecast, a colleague
offering an opinion without wanting to be quoted, a website disclaiming its own
figures - all of them reach for `あくまでも`. It protects the speaker, and Japanese
professional speech does a great deal of that.

Sense 2 is more declamatory. It suits reporting on disputes, trials and
negotiations, and in the first person it sounds firm to the point of stubborn:
<code>{f|私|わたし}はあくまで{f|反対|はんたい}します</code> is not a soft objection.
Choose it deliberately.

There is a mild politeness effect worth noticing. Prefacing an opinion with
<code>あくまで{f|個人|こじん}の{f|意見|いけん}ですが</code> lowers the temperature of
what follows, rather like "just my two cents". Dropping it makes the same sentence
sound like a ruling.

## Contrast

### あくまでも vs {f|一応|いちおう}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both hedge, but they hedge different things.
<code>{f|一応|いちおう}</code> lowers the *thoroughness* of what you did;
`あくまでも` limits the *status* of what you are claiming.

</div>

<pre>
<b>{f|一応|いちおう}</b>{f|確認|かくにん}しましたが、{f|見落|みお}としがあるかもしれません。
I've had a look, for what it's worth, though I may have missed something.
</pre>

<pre>
これは<b>あくまでも</b>{f|確認|かくにん}のための{f|連絡|れんらく}で、{f|決定|けってい}ではありません。
This message is purely to confirm; it is not a decision.
</pre>

The first speaker admits the check may have been shallow. The second makes no
admission at all about the quality of anything - they are drawing a boundary
around what the message *is*. Learners reach for
<code>{f|一応|いちおう}</code> when they mean the boundary, and end up sounding
unsure of their own work.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}はあくまでも{f|疲|つか}れた。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}は<b>とても</b>{f|疲|つか}れた。
  `あくまでも` is not an intensifier. The "to the utmost" in the dictionary gloss
  is about persistence, not about degree.

- ❌ あくまでの{f|予想|よそう}です。 → ✅ <b>あくまでも</b>{f|予想|よそう}です。
  No `の` between the adverb and the noun predicate. Say `あくまで` or `あくまでも`
  and continue straight into the noun.

- ❌ あくまで{f|反対|はんたい}しましたが、{f|最後|さいご}は{f|賛成|さんせい}しました。 → ✅ {f|最初|さいしょ}は{f|反対|はんたい}しましたが、{f|最後|さいご}は{f|賛成|さんせい}しました。
  Sense 2 means the position never moved. Following it with a change of mind
  cancels the word you just used.

## Practice

**1.** ____{f|例|れい}ですので、{f|実際|じっさい}の{f|金額|きんがく}とは{f|異|こと}なります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>あくまでも</b>{f|例|れい}ですので、{f|実際|じっさい}の{f|金額|きんがく}とは{f|異|こと}なります。

Classic sense 1: the speaker is fencing off the example so that nobody treats it as
a quotation. Note the shape - adverb, noun, copula - which is the frame to
memorise.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}はあくまでも{f|上手|じょうず}にピアノを{f|弾|ひ}く。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}は<b>とても</b>{f|上手|じょうず}にピアノを{f|弾|ひ}く。

The sentence is trying to use `あくまでも` as "extremely", which it cannot do. If
you did want `あくまで` here it would have to mean persistence, as in
<code>あくまで{f|自分|じぶん}のやり{f|方|かた}で{f|弾|ひ}く</code>, "he insists on
playing it his own way" - a completely different claim.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼女|かのじょ}はあくまで{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|説|せつ}を{f|曲|ま}げなかった。
(b) {f|彼女|かのじょ}はしばらく{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|説|せつ}を{f|曲|ま}げなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (a) says she never gave way at all, and the adverb adds a note of
admiration or exasperation depending on who is speaking - it is the sense-2 use,
about a position held to the end. Sentence (b) merely reports a stretch of time and
quietly implies that she gave way afterwards.

</details>
