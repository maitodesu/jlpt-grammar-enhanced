# 可能性がある

**Meaning** · there is a possibility that ～ - read *かのうせいがある*; the neutral, measurable way of saying "might", and the one that survives in writing

<code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}</code> is a noun - *possibility* - so the whole expression
is literally "a possibility of ～ exists". That noun is what makes the pattern
useful. A possibility can be high or low, can be ruled out, can be admitted; none of
that is available to `かもしれない`, which is a bare ending with no handles on it.

## Usage

The clause in front modifies <code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}</code> as a noun, so it is in
noun-modifying shape: a verb or い-adjective goes in plain, a な-adjective takes `な`,
and a noun takes `の`. <code>{f|来週|らいしゅう}になる{f|可能性|かのうせい}</code>,
<code>{f|不十分|ふじゅうぶん}な{f|可能性|かのうせい}</code>,
<code>{f|故障|こしょう}の{f|可能性|かのうせい}</code>. Because it is a noun, the verb
at the end is free to vary, and that is where most of the expressive work happens:

| Shape | Force |
|---|---|
| ～{f|可能性|かのうせい}がある | a possibility exists - the plain statement |
| ～{f|可能性|かのうせい}が{f|高|たか}い／{f|低|ひく}い | likely / unlikely |
| ～{f|可能性|かのうせい}も{f|十分|じゅうぶん}にある | quite conceivable |
| ～{f|可能性|かのうせい}は{f|否定|ひてい}できない | cannot be ruled out |
| ～{f|可能性|かのうせい}はない／ゼロだ | flatly impossible |
| ～{f|可能性|かのうせい}が{f|出|で}てきた | a possibility has arisen |

The one collocation to memorise is <b>{f|高|たか}い／{f|低|ひく}い</b> for degree.
Japanese measures a possibility by height, never by amount, so
❌<code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}が{f|多|おお}い</code> is wrong however natural "a big
possibility" feels in English.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|雪|ゆき}になる<b>{f|可能性|かのうせい}があります</b>。
There is a chance it will turn to snow tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|情報|じょうほう}は{f|古|ふる}い<b>{f|可能性|かのうせい}がある</b>。
That information may well be out of date.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|長引|ながび}く<b>{f|可能性|かのうせい}が{f|高|たか}い</b>ので、{f|夕食|ゆうしょく}の{f|予約|よやく}は{f|遅|おそ}めにしておきます。
The meeting is quite likely to run over, so I'll book dinner for a bit later.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|同|おな}じ{f|不具合|ふぐあい}が{f|他|ほか}の{f|機種|きしゅ}でも{f|起|お}きる<b>{f|可能性|かのうせい}は{f|否定|ひてい}できません</b>。
We cannot rule out the possibility that the same fault occurs on other models as well.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Formal, and neutral about whether the outcome is welcome.** It belongs to
weather bulletins, medical explanations, engineering reports, risk assessments and
careful business speech. In relaxed conversation the same idea comes out as
`かもしれない`, and reaching for
<code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}があります</code> over lunch makes you sound as though you
were briefing a committee on your own afternoon.

Its neutrality is the thing to hold on to. A championship win, a promotion and a
train delay can all be a <code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}</code>; only the last of the
three can be an <code>{f|恐|おそ}れ</code>. Slotted onto the scale the book has been
building since N4, it sits at the low end, but with a different voice from its
neighbours:

| Expression | Confidence | Voice |
|---|---|---|
| ～かもしれない | might, could | a person weighing it up |
| ～{f|可能性|かのうせい}がある | there is a possibility | an assessment, good news or bad |
| ～{f|恐|おそ}れがある | there is a risk | an assessment, bad news only |
| ～{f|可能性|かのうせい}が{f|高|たか}い | probably | an assessment with the odds attached |
| ～だろう／でしょう | probably | a person's general guess |
| ～はずだ | should be | grounds you can point to |
| ～に{f|違|ちが}いない | must be | evidence plus conviction |

Two turns of phrase are worth knowing because they are so common in careful speech.
<code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}は{f|否定|ひてい}できない</code> is how a spokesperson
concedes something without conceding it, and
<code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}が{f|出|で}てきた</code> reports that a possibility has
newly appeared - the sentence a doctor or a project manager says when the situation
has just changed.

Finally, keep the "potential" sense separate.
<code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}を{f|秘|ひ}めた{f|若|わか}い{f|選手|せんしゅ}</code> means a
player with promise in them, not a player who might exist. That
<code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}</code> has no clause in front of it, which is how you tell
the two apart at a glance.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}の{f|可能性|かのうせい}が<b>{f|多|おお}い</b>。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}の{f|可能性|かのうせい}が<b>{f|高|たか}い</b>。
  A possibility is measured by height in Japanese. <code>{f|多|おお}い</code> would
  mean there were numerous separate possibilities.

- ❌ {f|地元|じもと}のチームが{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}する<b>{f|恐|おそ}れがある</b>。 → ✅ {f|地元|じもと}のチームが{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}する<b>{f|可能性|かのうせい}がある</b>。
  <code>{f|恐|おそ}れ</code> only ever points at something unwelcome. Used of your own
  team winning, it is either a joke or a slip.

- ❌ {f|今晩|こんばん}{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}く<b>{f|可能性|かのうせい}があります</b>。 → ✅ {f|今晩|こんばん}{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}く<b>かもしれません</b>。
  Grammatical, and oddly detached: you are forecasting your own evening from the
  outside, as though your movements were a weather system.

- ❌ {f|事故|じこ}が{f|起|お}きる<b>{f|可能性|かのうせい}がある</b>{f|恐|おそ}れがあります。 → ✅ {f|事故|じこ}が{f|起|お}きる<b>{f|恐|おそ}れがあります</b>。
  Two hedges of the same kind stacked on one clause. Pick whichever fits the
  outcome and stop there.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|薬|くすり}は{f|眠気|ねむけ}が{f|出|で}る____がありますので、{f|運転|うんてん}は{f|控|ひか}えてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|眠気|ねむけ}が{f|出|で}る<b>{f|可能性|かのうせい}</b>がありますので…

A pharmacist's warning is exactly the register, and the plain form
<code>{f|出|で}る</code> goes straight in front of the noun.
<code>{f|恐|おそ}れ</code> would also be possible here and would sound graver;
<code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}</code> keeps it factual.

</details>

**2.** Choose: え、{f|明日|あした}のライブ、{f|中止|ちゅうし}になる（{f|可能性|かのうせい}があるらしいよ・かもしれないらしいよ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|中止|ちゅうし}になる<b>{f|可能性|かのうせい}があるらしいよ</b>。

`かもしれない` is a guess of the speaker's own and cannot be handed over to `らしい`,
which reports somebody else's words. <code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}がある</code> is a
statement about the world, so it can be quoted and reported freely - one practical
advantage of a noun over an ending.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|再発|さいはつ}する<b>{f|可能性|かのうせい}は{f|低|ひく}いです</b>。
(b) {f|再発|さいはつ}する<b>{f|可能性|かのうせい}は{f|否定|ひてい}できません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is reassurance with a number behind it. The speaker has weighed the odds and is
telling you they are in your favour, while stopping short of a promise.

(b) is the opposite move made with the same politeness. Nothing has been estimated;
the speaker is refusing to rule the thing out, which in Japanese as in English is
how a professional warns you without predicting. Hearing (b), a listener braces.

</details>
