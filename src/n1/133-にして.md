# ～にして

**Meaning** · at that point and not before; or, of a span, in no more than that - and by extension, only someone of that standing

`にして` is the classical `に` plus `して`, and modern Japanese keeps it as a marker
of a **striking point on a scale**. Two uses dominate. With an age or an attempt
number it means "only at that point": <code>{f|三度目|さんどめ}にしてようやく</code>,
it took until the third try. With a very short span it means "in no more than that":
<code>{f|一夜|いちや}にして</code>, overnight, just like that.

## Formation

`にして` always follows a noun. Which sense you get depends entirely on what kind of
noun it is.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Age or ordinal | Noun + にして + {f|初|はじ}めて／ようやく | {f|七十歳|ななじゅっさい} → {f|七十歳|ななじゅっさい}にして{f|初|はじ}めて |
| Short time span | Noun + にして | {f|一瞬|いっしゅん} → {f|一瞬|いっしゅん}にして |
| Person or standing | Noun + にして + {f|初|はじ}めて | {f|職人|しょくにん} → {f|職人|しょくにん}にして{f|初|はじ}めて{f|可能|かのう}だ |
| Dual role | Noun + にして + Noun | {f|学者|がくしゃ} → {f|学者|がくしゃ}にして{f|詩人|しじん} |
| Set phrase | {f|今|いま}にして{f|思|おも}えば | - → {f|今|いま}にして{f|思|おも}えば{f|軽率|けいそつ}だった |
| Written variant | ～にしてはじめて | {f|苦労|くろう}を{f|知|し}る{f|人|ひと}にしてはじめて |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the time-span use is largely **collocational**. The nouns that occur are
a small set: {f|一瞬|いっしゅん}, {f|一夜|いちや}, {f|一日|いちにち},
{f|一年|いちねん}, and occasionally わずか plus a number.
<code>{f|一瞬|いっしゅん}にして</code> and <code>{f|一夜|いちや}にして</code> are the
two you will meet most.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|三度目|さんどめ}<b>にして</b>ようやく{f|合格|ごうかく}した。
It was only on the third attempt that I finally passed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工場|こうじょう}は{f|火事|かじ}で{f|一夜|いちや}<b>にして</b>{f|焼|や}け{f|落|お}ちた。
The factory burned to the ground in a single night.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|祖母|そぼ}は{f|七十歳|ななじゅっさい}<b>にして</b>{f|初|はじ}めてパソコンに{f|触|さわ}った。
My grandmother touched a computer for the first time at the age of seventy.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|噂|うわさ}は{f|一瞬|いっしゅん}<b>にして</b>{f|町中|まちじゅう}に{f|広|ひろ}がった。
The rumour was all over town in an instant.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}<b>にして</b>{f|思|おも}えば、あのとき{f|断|ことわ}っておくべきだった。
Looking back on it now, I should have refused at the time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|創業|そうぎょう}{f|五年目|ごねんめ}<b>にして</b>、{f|会社|かいしゃ}はようやく{f|黒字|くろじ}に{f|転|てん}じた。

</pre>

<pre>

Reaching this level becomes possible only for a craftsman who has trained for many years.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|震災|しんさい}の{f|夜|よる}、{f|見慣|みな}れた{f|街並|まちな}みが{f|一夜|いちや}<b>にして</b>{f|消|き}えてしまったことを、{f|今|いま}でも{f|信|しん}じられずにいる。
On the night of the earthquake the familiar streets vanished overnight, and I still cannot quite believe it.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`にして` is **formal and written**, and it survives in speech mainly through set
phrases. <code>{f|今|いま}にして{f|思|おも}えば</code> is the most useful of them and
is entirely normal in conversation; <code>{f|一瞬|いっしゅん}にして</code> is close
behind. Beyond those, the pattern belongs to news copy, biography, obituaries,
company histories and formal speeches. In everyday Japanese the ordinary particles
`で` and `に` do the same work with no ceremony.

What `にして` adds over `で` is **remark**. <code>{f|一年|いちねん}で{f|完成|かんせい}した</code>
reports a duration; <code>{f|一年|いちねん}にして{f|完成|かんせい}した</code> says a
year was astonishingly little. The same applies to ages:
<code>{f|三十歳|さんじゅっさい}で{f|独立|どくりつ}した</code> is a fact, while
<code>{f|三十歳|さんじゅっさい}にして</code> tells you the writer finds the age worth
commenting on - and with an age it nearly always wants <code>{f|初|はじ}めて</code>
or <code>ようやく</code> in support.

The "person or standing" use is worth flagging because it looks like the others and
is not about time at all. <code>{f|彼|かれ}にして{f|初|はじ}めてできることだ</code>
means only someone of his calibre could manage it - and a close relative of it,
<code>{f|彼|かれ}にしてこの{f|失敗|しっぱい}か</code>, means the opposite kind of
surprise: even *he* got it wrong. Both are literary and both depend heavily on
context, so read them carefully before deciding which way round the compliment runs.

Finally, do not confuse `にして` with `にしては`, which is a different pattern
altogether. The one extra `は` changes "at, in" into "for a ...", and the two are a
standard exam trap.

## Contrast

### ～にして vs ～で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `で` is the neutral particle for a span or a point.
`にして` is `で` plus a raised eyebrow.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|三年|さんねん}<b>で</b>{f|終|お}わった。
The construction was finished in three years.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|大|だい}{f|工事|こうじ}は、わずか{f|三年|さんねん}<b>にして</b>{f|完成|かんせい}を{f|見|み}た。
This enormous project reached completion in a mere three years.
</pre>

The second sentence is doing something the first is not: it is asking you to be
impressed. Use `で` unless the span really is the point, because `にして` on an
unremarkable duration reads as writing that is trying too hard.

### ～にして vs ～になって{f|初|はじ}めて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning for the age use.
`になって初めて` is the everyday form; `にして初めて` is the written one.

</div>

<pre>
{f|四十歳|よんじゅっさい}<b>になって{f|初|はじ}めて</b>{f|泳|およ}げるようになった。
I only learned to swim when I got to forty.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|四十歳|よんじゅっさい}<b>にして{f|初|はじ}めて</b>{f|泳|およ}ぎを{f|覚|おぼ}えた。
It was at forty that he first learned to swim.
</pre>

Both are correct and mean the same thing. The first is what you say about yourself
over dinner; the second is what a profile in a magazine says about someone. If you
are speaking, take the first.

### ～にして vs ～にしては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `にして` marks a point on a scale. `にしては` means "for a ～",
setting up a judgement that defies expectation. Different patterns, one letter
apart.

</div>

<pre>
{f|子供|こども}<b>にしては</b>よく{f|書|か}けている。
For a child, it's very well written.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|十歳|じゅっさい}<b>にして</b>プロの{f|舞台|ぶたい}に{f|立|た}った。
At the age of ten he was already on a professional stage.
</pre>

`にしては` compares against a standard and usually softens praise or criticism;
`にして` simply plants a flag at a point and lets its earliness or lateness speak.
Swapping them produces sentences that are grammatical and mean the wrong thing,
which is exactly why the pair turns up in tests.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}<b>にして</b>{f|終|お}わった。 → ✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}<b>で</b>{f|終|お}わった。
  Half an hour is an ordinary length for a meeting, so there is nothing to remark on.
  Reserve `にして` for spans the reader is meant to find striking.

- ❌ {f|子供|こども}<b>にして</b>よく{f|書|か}けている。 → ✅ {f|子供|こども}<b>にしては</b>よく{f|書|か}けている。
  Dropping the `は` changes the pattern. Without it the sentence tries to place a
  child on a time scale and fails.

- ❌ {f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|六時|ろくじ}<b>にして</b>{f|起|お}きる。 → ✅ {f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|六時|ろくじ}<b>に</b>{f|起|お}きる。
  A clock time takes plain `に`. `にして` needs a duration, an age or an ordinal,
  not a point in the day.

- ❌ {f|三十歳|さんじゅっさい}<b>にして</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|入|はい}った。 → ✅ {f|三十歳|さんじゅっさい}<b>で</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|入|はい}った。
  With an age, `にして` leans on <code>{f|初|はじ}めて</code> or
  <code>ようやく</code> and implies the age is remarkable. On a neutral fact it
  leaves the reader waiting for a point that never arrives.

## Practice

**1.** {f|四度目|よんどめ}の{f|挑戦|ちょうせん}____、ついに{f|頂上|ちょうじょう}に{f|立|た}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|四度目|よんどめ}の{f|挑戦|ちょうせん}<b>にして</b>、ついに{f|頂上|ちょうじょう}に{f|立|た}った。

An ordinal plus `にして` is the "only at that point" use, and ついに or ようやく
downstream is the usual companion. The sentence says the first three attempts
failed without having to mention them.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|新人|しんじん}（にして・にしては）{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}いた{f|受|う}け{f|答|こた}えだった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|新人|しんじん}<b>にしては</b>{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}いた{f|受|う}け{f|答|こた}えだった。

The sentence measures the performance against what you would expect of a newcomer,
which is `にしては`. <code>{f|新人|しんじん}にして</code> would have to mean
something like "it takes a newcomer to manage that", which is not what a listener
would understand here.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The town's entire economy collapsed overnight when the mine closed."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|炭鉱|たんこう}が{f|閉|と}じられ、{f|町|まち}の{f|経済|けいざい}は{f|一夜|いちや}<b>にして</b>{f|崩|くず}れ{f|去|さ}った。

"Overnight" in the sense of a sudden collapse is exactly
<code>{f|一夜|いちや}にして</code> - one of the fixed collocations, and one that
carries the drama the English has. A plain <code>{f|一晩|ひとばん}で</code> would
sound like a literal measurement of hours.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|祖父|そふ}は{f|八十歳|はちじゅっさい}にして{f|新聞|しんぶん}を{f|読|よ}んでいる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|祖父|そふ}は{f|八十歳|はちじゅっさい}<b>にして</b>なお{f|新聞|しんぶん}を{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|読|よ}んでいる。
（or simply {f|八十歳|はちじゅっさい}<b>で</b>{f|新聞|しんぶん}を{f|読|よ}んでいる）

Reading a newspaper at eighty is not in itself remarkable, so the pattern is left
without a point. Either add the word that supplies the surprise - なお, "still" -
or drop `にして` for the neutral `で`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|二十歳|はたち}<b>で</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|立|た}ち{f|上|あ}げた。
(b) {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|二十歳|はたち}<b>にして</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|立|た}ち{f|上|あ}げた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) records the age at which she founded the company. It is neutral, and it would
sit comfortably in a CV.

(b) comments on it. `にして` tells you the writer thinks twenty is startlingly
young, and the sentence now belongs in a profile or an introduction rather than in a
list of facts. Note the reading: {f|二十歳|はたち} is the special native reading for
the age, not ❌にじゅっさい.

</details>
