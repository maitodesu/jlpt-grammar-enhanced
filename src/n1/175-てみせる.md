# ～てみせる

**Meaning** · I will do it, and you will see me do it - a declaration of resolve made in front of an audience. The same shape also has a plainer sense: to do something in order to demonstrate it to someone.

Both senses come from the same idea: the action is performed **for someone to
see**. When the verb is in the past and the subject is a third person, that is
usually literal demonstration - the teacher wrote the character out so the class
could watch. When the verb is non-past and the subject is *I*, the seeing becomes
figurative and the sentence turns into a vow: doubt me if you like, I am going to
do this.

## Formation

<code>みせる</code> attaches to the て-form. It is a る-verb and conjugates
normally. In this construction it is almost always written in kana.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | て-form + みせる | {f|勝|か}つ → {f|勝|か}ってみせる |
| Verb (る-verb) | て-form + みせる | {f|続|つづ}ける → {f|続|つづ}けてみせる |
| Verb (する) | して + みせる | {f|成功|せいこう}する → {f|成功|せいこう}してみせる |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | {f|来|き}て + みせる | {f|戻|もど}ってくる → {f|戻|もど}ってきてみせる |
| Polite | ～てみせます | {f|合格|ごうかく}してみせます |
| Past (demonstration) | ～てみせた | {f|書|か}いてみせた |
| Common companions | {f|必|かなら}ず、{f|絶対|ぜったい}に、{f|今度|こんど}こそ | {f|必|かなら}ず{f|勝|か}ってみせる |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the resolve sense does not take a negative verb. To vow *not* to do
something, Japanese uses a different frame:
<code>{f|二度|にど}とタバコは{f|吸|す}わない</code>, or
<code>{f|禁煙|きんえん}してみせる</code> stated positively. ❌<code>{f|吸|す}わないでみせる</code>
is not Japanese.

</div>

## Nuance & register

The resolve sense is **loud**. It says the speaker expects to be doubted and is
answering the doubt in advance, so it always implies an obstacle, a sceptical
audience, or a previous failure. That is why it lives where it does: sports
interviews, election speeches, curtain lines in drama, comic-book declarations,
and the sort of thing a person says to themselves at three in the morning. Between
friends it is fine, but it is not casual small talk - saying
<code>{f|今夜|こんや}はカレーを{f|作|つく}ってみせる</code> about dinner is a joke,
because there is nothing to prove.

Politeness works normally: <code>～てみせます</code> is perfectly usable in a press
conference or a formal pledge, and does not soften the force at all - if anything
the polite form in front of an audience sounds more like a commitment on the
record. The plain <code>～てみせる</code> in speech leans a little male and a
little dramatic, but it is not restricted, and in narration and quoted thought it
is neutral. A woman saying <code>{f|絶対|ぜったい}に{f|見返|みかえ}してみせる</code>
sounds exactly as determined as a man saying it.

The demonstration sense is register-neutral and very common, especially with
<code>～てみせてくれる</code> ("showed me how by doing it"). Keep the two apart by
looking at tense and person: past plus third person is nearly always
demonstration, non-past plus first person is nearly always resolve.

## Contrast

### ～てみせる vs ～てみる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～てみる</code> is *I'll try it and see what happens*.
<code>～てみせる</code> is *I'll do it and you will see*. One is tentative; the
other is the opposite of tentative.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しいアプリを{f|使|つか}っ<b>てみます</b>。
I'll give the new app a go.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しいアプリで{f|結果|けっか}を{f|出|だ}し<b>てみせます</b>。
I'll get results with the new app - just wait.
</pre>

The single kana difference reverses the confidence level. This is the error that
matters most: a learner who means "let me try" and produces
<code>～てみせる</code> has announced a challenge to the room.

### ～てみせる vs ～つもりだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>つもりだ</code> reports a plan.
<code>～てみせる</code> makes a promise **to someone**, with pride staked on it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来年|らいねん}、{f|留学|りゅうがく}する<b>つもりです</b>。
I'm planning to study abroad next year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来年|らいねん}こそ{f|留学|りゅうがく}し<b>てみせます</b>。
Next year I'm getting on that plane, whatever it takes.
</pre>

<code>つもり</code> is information: you could say it to an immigration officer.
<code>～てみせる</code> is a stance, and it needs a reason to be defiant - a
rejection last year, a family that said no. Used with no such background it sounds
overheated.

### ～てみせる vs ～てやる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both can express defiance. <code>～てみせる</code> aims at
**proving something**; the defiant <code>～てやる</code> aims at **getting
even**, and is rougher.

</div>

<pre>
いつか{f|見返|みかえ}し<b>てやる</b>。
One day I'll show them. (with an edge of spite)
</pre>

<pre>
いつか{f|見返|みかえ}し<b>てみせる</b>。
One day I'll prove them wrong.
</pre>

<code>やる</code> in this use is blunt and can sound aggressive, and it has an
entirely separate everyday meaning of doing something for someone below you
({f|犬|いぬ}にえさをやる). <code>みせる</code> keeps the focus on the achievement
rather than on the other person's comeuppance, so it is the safer of the two in
mixed company.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|勝|か}る<b>てみせる</b> → ✅ {f|勝|か}っ<b>てみせる</b>
  <code>みせる</code> attaches to the て-form, never the dictionary form. (The verb
  is {f|勝|か}つ, so the て-form is {f|勝|か}って.)

- ❌ この{f|料理|りょうり}を{f|食|た}べ<b>てみせます</b>か。 → ✅ この{f|料理|りょうり}を{f|食|た}べ<b>てみます</b>。
  If you mean "I'll try it and see", the pattern is <code>～てみる</code>.
  <code>てみせる</code> would mean you were eating it to prove a point, and it does
  not form ordinary questions in the resolve sense.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っ<b>てみせる</b>。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っていく。
  There is nothing to prove about carrying an umbrella. The pattern needs an
  obstacle or a doubter.

- ❌ タバコを{f|吸|す}わない<b>でみせる</b>。 → ✅ {f|絶対|ぜったい}に{f|禁煙|きんえん}し<b>てみせる</b>。
  The vow has to be stated as something you will do, not as something you will
  refrain from; the negative form does not attach.

## Practice

**1.** {f|今回|こんかい}は{f|失敗|しっぱい}したが、{f|次|つぎ}は{f|必|かなら}ず{f|成功|せいこう}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|成功|せいこう}<b>してみせる</b>（or してみせます）

The previous failure supplies exactly the background the pattern needs: the speaker
is answering an implied doubt. Note the する verb gives して, not ❌しって.

</details>

**2.** Choose: すみません、この{f|靴|くつ}を{f|履|は}い（てみて・てみせて）もいいですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|履|は}い<b>てみて</b>もいいですか。

You are asking to try the shoes on. <code>てみせて</code> would be asking the shop
assistant to put them on and model them for you, which is a different request -
and with もいいですか attached it comes out as nonsense.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Everyone says it's impossible, but I'm going to finish it in a month."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

みんな{f|無理|むり}だと{f|言|い}うが、{f|一|いっ}か{f|月|げつ}で{f|終|お}わらせ<b>てみせる</b>。

The English "I'm going to ... " against everyone's doubt is precisely the resolve
sense. Keep {f|終|お}わらせる (transitive, "bring to an end") rather than
{f|終|お}わる, because the speaker is the one doing it.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|母|はは}はケーキの{f|作|つく}り{f|方|かた}を{f|説明|せつめい}しながら、{f|卵|たまご}を{f|割|わ}ってみせるくれた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|母|はは}はケーキの{f|作|つく}り{f|方|かた}を{f|説明|せつめい}しながら、{f|卵|たまご}を{f|割|わ}っ<b>てみせて</b>くれた。
"My mother explained how to make the cake, cracking an egg to show me."

To add くれる you need the て-form of みせる, which is みせて. This is the
demonstration sense, and <code>～てみせてくれる</code> is its most common shape.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|来年|らいねん}までにこの{f|資格|しかく}を{f|取|と}ろうと{f|思|おも}っています。
(b) {f|来年|らいねん}までにこの{f|資格|しかく}を{f|取|と}っ<b>てみせます</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a stated intention, and a mild one. The speaker is telling you what is on
their plan; if it slips a year, nothing much has been broken.

(b) is a pledge with the speaker's credibility attached, made to a listener who is
assumed to have doubts - a boss who said the exam was too hard, a family that
suggested giving up. It sounds strong and slightly combative, and if the speaker
misses the deadline they will feel it. Use (a) unless you actually mean to stake
something.

</details>
