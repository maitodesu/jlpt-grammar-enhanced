# ほかに○○ない

**Meaning** · there is no other one - the alternatives have been looked for and there are none

`ほかに` is "elsewhere, besides", and the negative at the end of the clause closes
the door on it. The pattern is a statement about the **search**: someone has
considered the other candidates, the other routes, the other shops, and reports
back that nothing else qualifies. Depending on what is being ruled out, the result
is praise ("nobody else could do it") or resignation ("there is nothing else we can
try").

## Formation

<code>ほか</code> is a noun - it is written {f|他|ほか} or {f|外|ほか} in kanji, but
kana is the usual choice. A noun in front of it therefore needs `の`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing (bare) | ほかに + Noun + は + ない | ほかに{f|方法|ほうほう}はない |
| Noun | Noun + の + ほかに ～ ない | {f|彼|かれ} → {f|彼|かれ}のほかに{f|適任者|てきにんしゃ}はいない |
| Animate noun | ほかに ～ は + いない | ほかに{f|頼|たよ}れる{f|人|ひと}はいない |
| Verb (plain) | Verb + ほかに ～ ない | {f|待|ま}つ → {f|待|ま}つほかに{f|手|て}はない |
| Noun-modifying clause | Clause + ことは + ほかにない | {f|言|い}いたいことはほかにない |
| Polite | ～ほかに ～ ありません / いません | ほかに{f|質問|しつもん}はありません |

<div class="note">

**Note** - use <code>ない</code> for things and <code>いない</code> for people. The
mistake ❌<code>{f|彼|かれ}のほかに{f|適任者|てきにんしゃ}はない</code> is common and
sounds odd in the same way "there is no such person available" would if you said
"there is not".

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
すみません、<b>ほかに{f|方法|ほうほう}はありません</b>。
I'm sorry, but there is no other way.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|問題|もんだい}を{f|解|と}ける{f|人|ひと}は、{f|彼|かれ}の<b>ほかにいない</b>。
There is nobody but him who can solve this problem.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>ほかに{f|言|い}いたいことはない</b>ので、これで{f|終|お}わります。
I have nothing else I want to say, so I will stop there.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|出|で}られる{f|人|ひと}は、{f|私|わたし}の<b>ほかに{f|一人|ひとり}もいなかった</b>。
Apart from me there was not a single person free to attend the meeting.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|終電|しゅうでん}が{f|行|い}ってしまった{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|歩|ある}く<b>ほかに{f|手|て}はない</b>。
Now that the last train has gone, there is nothing for it but to walk.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|味|あじ}を{f|出|だ}せる{f|店|みせ}は、{f|市内|しない}には<b>ほかにない</b>と{f|思|おも}う。
I don't think there is another shop in the city that can produce this flavour.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|予算|よさん}が{f|限|かぎ}られている{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|来年度|らいねんど}に{f|先送|さきおく}りする<b>ほかに{f|選択肢|せんたくし}はない</b>。
Given that the budget is limited, we have no option other than to postpone it to next year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|条件|じょうけん}の{f|近|ちか}い{f|物件|ぶっけん}はいくつも{f|見|み}たが、{f|駅|えき}まで{f|徒歩|とほ}{f|三分|さんぷん}という{f|点|てん}でここに{f|勝|まさ}るものは<b>ほかになかった</b>。
We looked at several flats on similar terms, but nothing else beat this one for being three minutes from the station.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The pattern is **register-neutral**. <code>ほかに{f|方法|ほうほう}はない</code> is at
home in a boardroom, in a shop and between friends; the politeness lives entirely in
the ending you choose, <code>ない</code> or <code>ありません</code>. In writing you
may see it in kanji as {f|他|ほか} or, more old-fashionedly, {f|外|ほか}, but kana is
the modern default and nothing is lost by it.

What the pattern adds over a plain negative is the sense of a **completed search**.
<code>いい{f|店|みせ}がない</code> just says there is no good shop.
<code>ほかにいい{f|店|みせ}はない</code> says you have been round them all and this is
the verdict. That is why it is so common in recommendations and in resigned
decisions, and why it sounds strange applied to something nobody would have looked
for: <code>ほかに{f|空気|くうき}はない</code> is not a sentence with a use.

Note the small emphatic variants. <code>ほかに{f|一人|ひとり}もいない</code> and
<code>ほかに{f|何|なに}もない</code> put a counter or a question word in the middle
and harden the denial. And <code>ほかでもない</code>, which you will meet as a fixed
phrase - <code>ほかでもない{f|君|きみ}の{f|頼|たの}みだから</code>, "because it is
none other than you asking" - is built from the same {f|他|ほか} and means "precisely
this one", the positive side of the same coin.

## Contrast

### ほかに○○ない vs しか○○ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `しか～ない` attaches to the thing that **remains**.
`ほかに～ない` denies that anything **else** exists. Same fact from two sides.

</div>

<pre>
{f|残|のこ}っているのは、この{f|一|ひと}つ<b>しかない</b>。
This one is all that's left.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|使|つか}えそうなものは、これの<b>ほかになかった</b>。
There was nothing else that looked usable.
</pre>

`しか` is the everyday form and needs a specific item in front of it, which is why
it cannot open a sentence. `ほかに` can stand alone -
<code>ほかにありませんか</code>, "is there nothing else?" - and is the natural choice
when the other candidates, not the survivor, are the point.

### ほかに○○ない vs ～ほかない・～よりほかない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ほかに○○ない` says **no other thing exists**.
`～ほかない` says **there is nothing to do but** - it is about a course of action,
and always follows a verb.

</div>

<pre>
{f|待|ま}つ<b>よりほかない</b>。
There's nothing to do but wait.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|待|ま}つ{f|以外|いがい}に、できることは<b>ほかにない</b>。
Apart from waiting, there is nothing else we can do.
</pre>

The two overlap heavily, and in the second sentence the difference is mostly one of
weight. Where they part company is that `～ほかない` needs a verb and expresses
obligation, while `ほかに○○ない` can take a noun -
<code>{f|候補|こうほ}はほかにない</code> - and expresses absence.

### ほかに○○ない vs ～{f|以外|いがい}に○○ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - all but interchangeable. `以外に` is tighter and more written;
watch the particle, because `ほか` takes `の` after a noun and `以外` does not.

</div>

<pre>
{f|山田|やまだ}さん<b>{f|以外|いがい}に</b>{f|経験者|けいけんしゃ}はいません。
There is nobody with experience other than Yamada.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|山田|やまだ}さんの<b>ほかに</b>{f|経験者|けいけんしゃ}はいません。
There is nobody with experience apart from Yamada.
</pre>

❌<code>{f|山田|やまだ}さんほかに</code> and ❌<code>{f|山田|やまだ}さんの{f|以外|いがい}に</code>
are both wrong, and swapping the particles is the usual way learners get caught.
Meaning-wise, `以外に` is slightly the drier of the two and dominates contracts,
notices and academic prose.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}<b>ほかに</b>{f|適任者|てきにんしゃ}はいない。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}<b>のほかに</b>{f|適任者|てきにんしゃ}はいない。
  <code>ほか</code> is a noun, so a noun in front of it links with `の`. This is the
  single commonest slip, caused by interference from `以外に`, which takes no
  particle.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}のほかに{f|適任者|てきにんしゃ}は<b>ない</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}のほかに{f|適任者|てきにんしゃ}は<b>いない</b>。
  People take <code>いない</code>. Things take <code>ない</code>.

- ❌ ほかに{f|方法|ほうほう}が<b>ある</b>。 → ✅ ほかに{f|方法|ほうほう}が<b>ありますか</b>。
  In a plain affirmative, `ほかに` is simply the adverb "besides" and this is no
  longer the pattern. The pattern lives on the negative, or on a question expecting
  one.

- ❌ これ{f|以外|いがい}に<b>ほかに</b>{f|案|あん}はない。 → ✅ これ{f|以外|いがい}に{f|案|あん}はない。
  <code>{f|以外|いがい}に</code> and <code>ほかに</code> say the same thing. Using
  both is a doubled negative frame that native speakers hear as clumsy.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|任|まか}せられるのは、{f|田中|たなか}さん____ほかに____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|田中|たなか}さん<b>のほかにいない</b>。

The noun before <code>ほか</code> needs `の`, and because the missing item is a
person, the negative existence verb is <code>いない</code>, not <code>ない</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}には{f|卵|たまご}（しか・ほかに）なかったので、{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|卵|たまご}<b>しか</b>なかったので

`しか～ない` attaches directly to what survived - the eggs - and that is what the
sentence is about. To use `ほかに` you would have to rewrite it so the missing things
are the subject: <code>{f|卵|たまご}{f|以外|いがい}は{f|何|なに}もなかった</code>, or
<code>{f|卵|たまご}のほかには{f|何|なに}もなかった</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I've eaten ramen all over the country, and there is no bowl anywhere to beat this one."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|全国|ぜんこく}のラーメンを{f|食|た}べ{f|歩|ある}いてきたが、これに{f|勝|まさ}るものは<b>ほかにない</b>。

The first clause supplies the completed search that `ほかに○○ない` presupposes,
which is exactly why the pattern fits here and a bare negative would not.
<code>{f|食|た}べ{f|歩|ある}く</code> is the idiomatic verb for eating your way round
a lot of places.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|急|いそ}いでいるので、タクシーで{f|行|い}くほかにしかない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|急|いそ}いでいるので、タクシーで{f|行|い}く<b>ほかない</b>。
（or {f|行|い}く<b>しかない</b>）

Two patterns have been welded together. Pick one: <code>{f|行|い}くほかない</code> or
<code>{f|行|い}くしかない</code>, both meaning "there's nothing for it but to go by
taxi". <code>ほかに</code> plus <code>しか</code> in the same clause is not a
possible shape.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ほかに{f|質問|しつもん}はありませんか。
(b) {f|質問|しつもん}はありませんか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b) opens the floor: it is asked once, and it invites any question at all.

(a) can only be asked after at least one question has already been taken. `ほかに`
looks back at what has been covered and asks what is left over, so as the opening
line of a meeting it would leave the room briefly confused about what they had
missed.

</details>
