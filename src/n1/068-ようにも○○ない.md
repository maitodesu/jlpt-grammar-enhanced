# (よ)うにも～ない

**Meaning** · I would if I could, but I can't - the will is there and the circumstances block it

The volitional says "let me do it"; にも concedes that; the potential negative shuts
it down. So {f|帰|かえ}ろうにも{f|帰|かえ}れない is not "I didn't go home" but "I
wanted to go home and there was no way to". The obstacle is outside the speaker, and
it is normally named in the same sentence.

## Formation

The **same verb twice**: volitional first, potential negative second.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | Volitional + にも + potential negative | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}もうにも{f|休|やす}めない |
| Verb (る-verb) | ～よう + にも + ～られない | {f|出|で}る → {f|出|で}ようにも{f|出|で}られない |
| Verb (する) | **しよう**にも + **できない** | {f|連絡|れんらく}する → {f|連絡|れんらく}しようにもできない |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | <b>{f|来|こ}よう</b>にも <b>{f|来|こ}られない</b> | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ようにも{f|来|こ}られない |
| Polite | ～ようにも … ません | {f|買|か}う → {f|買|か}おうにも{f|買|か}えません |
| Past | ～ようにも … なかった | {f|下|お}りる → {f|下|お}りようにも{f|下|お}りられなかった |
| With the reason attached | ～て／～ので + ～ようにも … ない | {f|熱|ねつ}がある → {f|熱|ねつ}があって{f|起|お}きようにも{f|起|お}きられない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - う-verb / る-verb are the two regular classes（{f|五段|ごだん} /
{f|一段|いちだん}, Group 1 / Group 2）. Watch the two irregulars: する pairs
しよう with できない, not ❌されない, and {f|来|く}る gives
{f|来|こ}ようにも{f|来|こ}られない - the こ stem in both halves.

</div>

<pre>
{f|熱|ねつ}があって、{f|起|お}き<b>ようにも{f|起|お}きられない</b>。
I've got a fever - I can't get up however much I want to.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|終電|しゅうでん}が{f|行|い}ってしまって、{f|帰|かえ}ろ<b>うにも{f|帰|かえ}れない</b>。
The last train's gone, so there's no getting home now.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電話|でんわ}{f|番号|ばんごう}を{f|知|し}らないので、{f|連絡|れんらく}し<b>ようにもできない</b>。
I don't have his number, so I can't get in touch even if I want to.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|忙|いそが}しすぎて、{f|休|やす}も<b>うにも{f|休|やす}めない</b>{f|日|ひ}が{f|続|つづ}いている。
It's been one day after another where I couldn't take a break even if I tried.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雪|ゆき}で{f|道|みち}がふさがれ、{f|山|やま}を{f|下|お}り<b>ようにも{f|下|お}りられなかった</b>。
Snow had blocked the path and there was no way down off the mountain.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部品|ぶひん}がもう{f|作|つく}られていないので、{f|直|なお}そ<b>うにも{f|直|なお}せない</b>{f|状態|じょうたい}です。
The parts aren't made any more, so it simply can't be repaired.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|停電|ていでん}でエレベーターが{f|止|と}まり、{f|上|うえ}の{f|階|かい}へ{f|上|あ}がろ<b>うにも{f|上|あ}がれなかった</b>。

</pre>

<pre>

The records were all destroyed, so there is no way to investigate what happened then.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and genuinely useful: it fits conversation, a business email and a
news report equally. The feeling it carries is **frustration** - the speaker is
willing, sometimes desperate, and something outside them has closed the door. That
is why a reason clause almost always sits in front of it
({f|熱|ねつ}があって, {f|終電|しゅうでん}が{f|行|い}ってしまって,
{f|雪|ゆき}で). A bare
{f|帰|かえ}ろうにも{f|帰|かえ}れない with no explanation sounds unfinished.

It follows that the pattern cannot be used when you simply do not want to do the
thing. ❌{f|行|い}きたくないから{f|行|い}こうにも{f|行|い}けない contradicts itself;
if the reluctance is internal, the sentence is just {f|行|い}かない or
{f|行|い}きたくない. The same goes for a plain lack of skill - the block has to be
circumstantial. {f|緊張|きんちょう}して{f|話|はな}そうにも{f|話|はな}せなかった is
fine, because nerves are treated as something that happened to the speaker.

The second half is normally the **potential negative of the same verb**. A closely
related negative clause is also idiomatic where repeating the verb would be odd -
{f|買|か}おうにも{f|売|う}っていない, "I'd buy one if anyone sold them" - but the
repeated-verb version is the safe default and the one tested.

Two forms to keep straight in speech: う-verbs give the volitional in お-う
（{f|休|やす}もう, {f|帰|かえ}ろう, {f|買|か}おう）, and their potential is the
え-stem（{f|休|やす}める, {f|帰|かえ}れる, {f|買|か}える）. Getting one right and the
other wrong is the usual slip.

## Contrast

### (よ)うにも～ない vs ～たくても～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>たくても</code> foregrounds the **wish**;
<code>(よ)うにも</code> foregrounds the **attempt**, and is a little more formal.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会|あ}い<b>たくても</b>、もう{f|会|あ}え<b>ない</b>。
I'd love to see her, but I can't any more.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|書類|しょるい}がそろわず、{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}も<b>うにも{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}めない</b>。
The paperwork isn't complete, so there's no applying even if we try.
</pre>

The overlap is large and both are correct in most sentences.
<code>たくても</code> is the everyday choice and needs a subject with feelings, which
is why it fits the first sentence so well. <code>(よ)うにも</code> works equally for
an organisation or an impersonal situation, where "wanting" would be strange.

### (よ)うにも～ない vs ～ようがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ようがない</code> says there is **no method**, full stop.
<code>(よ)うにも～ない</code> says **this particular obstacle** is in the way, and
usually names it.

</div>

<pre>
ここまで{f|壊|こわ}れると、もう{f|直|なお}し<b>ようがない</b>。
Broken this badly, there's no fixing it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工具|こうぐ}を{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|貸|か}してしまって、{f|直|なお}そ<b>うにも{f|直|なお}せない</b>。
I've lent out all my tools, so I can't repair it even though I'd like to.
</pre>

The two are easy to confuse because both attach to a verb stem and both end in ない.
<code>ようがない</code> takes the **ます-stem**（{f|直|なお}し-）and makes a flat
statement about impossibility; this pattern takes the **volitional**
（{f|直|なお}そう-）and implies a thwarted intention. If nobody in the world could do
it, use <code>ようがない</code>.

### (よ)うにも～ない vs ～わけにはいかない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>(よ)うにも～ない</code> = **cannot**, physically or
practically. <code>わけにはいかない</code> = **could, but must not** - duty, custom
or conscience forbids it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大事|だいじ}な{f|会議|かいぎ}だから、{f|休|やす}む<b>わけにはいかない</b>。
It's an important meeting, so I can't very well take the day off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|代|か}わりの{f|人|ひと}がいなくて、{f|休|やす}も<b>うにも{f|休|やす}めない</b>。
There's nobody to cover for me, so taking a day off is out of the question.
</pre>

In the first the speaker could stay home and has decided that would be wrong. In the
second staying home is not available at all. Learners reach for
<code>わけにはいかない</code> when they mean simple impossibility; the test is whether
anyone would be annoyed with you if you did it anyway.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|行|い}こうにも{f|行|い}か<b>ない</b>。 → ✅ {f|行|い}こうにも{f|行|い}け<b>ない</b>。
  The second verb must be the **potential** negative. With the plain negative the
  sentence just says you didn't go.

- ❌ {f|運動|うんどう}する<b>ようにも</b> → ✅ {f|運動|うんどう}し<b>ようにも</b>
  The volitional of する is しよう. The dictionary form cannot appear here.

- ❌ {f|連絡|れんらく}しようにも{f|連絡|れんらく}<b>されない</b>。 → ✅ {f|連絡|れんらく}しようにも<b>できない</b>。
  The potential of する is できる. されない is the passive and says something quite
  different.

- ❌ {f|行|い}きたくないから、{f|行|い}こうにも{f|行|い}けない。 → ✅ {f|行|い}きたくないから、{f|行|い}かない。
  The pattern requires that you *want* to and are stopped. An internal reluctance
  makes it self-contradictory.

## Practice

**1.** パスポートを{f|忘|わす}れてきたので、{f|飛行機|ひこうき}に{f|乗|の}ろ____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|乗|の}ろ<b>うにも{f|乗|の}れない</b>

The forgotten passport is the external obstacle, and the speaker plainly wants to
board. Note the shape: volitional {f|乗|の}ろう, then the potential negative
{f|乗|の}れない.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|頼|たの}まれた{f|仕事|しごと}だから、{f|断|ことわ}る（わけにはいかない・ようにもできない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|断|ことわ}る<b>わけにはいかない</b>。

Refusing is perfectly possible here; it would just be unacceptable. That is
<code>わけにはいかない</code>. The other pattern is for cases where refusing is not
available at all.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The road was flooded, so we couldn't get to the hospital however hard we tried."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|道|みち}が{f|水|みず}に{f|浸|つ}かってしまい、{f|病院|びょういん}へ{f|行|い}こ<b>うにも{f|行|い}けなかった</b>。

The flood is the named obstacle, the will is obvious, and the past tense goes on the
final negative - {f|行|い}こう never changes.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|資料|しりょう}がないので、{f|説明|せつめい}しようにも{f|説明|せつめい}しられない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|資料|しりょう}がないので、{f|説明|せつめい}し<b>ようにもできない</b>。
"Without the materials, I can't explain it even if I try."

❌しられない is not a form. する has the suppletive potential できる, so the second
half is できない - or, if you prefer to keep the verb visible,
{f|説明|せつめい}のしようがない.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|忙|いそが}しくて{f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}け<b>ない</b>。
(b) {f|忙|いそが}しくて{f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}こ<b>うにも{f|行|い}けない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply reports the fact. Work is busy and the trip is not happening; the speaker
may or may not mind.

(b) puts the wanting into the sentence. It says the speaker has been trying to make
the trip work and keeps running into the same wall, and it invites sympathy in a way
that (a) does not.

</details>
