# ～羽目になる

**Meaning** · to end up having to do something you would rather not - read <code>{f|羽目|はめ}になる</code>

{f|羽目|はめ} began as the boarding on a wall and now means a fix, a spot you
cannot back out of. <code>{f|羽目|はめ}になる</code> says that a chain of events -
usually one you set off yourself - has landed you with a job nobody wanted. There is
almost always a note of "and it serves me right" in it.

## Formation

The verb in front stays in the **dictionary form**, however far in the past the
event is. Tense is carried by なる alone.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (dictionary form) | Verb + {f|羽目|はめ}になる | {f|払|はら}う → {f|払|はら}う{f|羽目|はめ}になる |
| Past result | Verb + {f|羽目|はめ}になった | {f|歩|ある}く → {f|歩|ある}く{f|羽目|はめ}になった |
| Polite | Verb + {f|羽目|はめ}になりました | {f|謝|あやま}る → {f|謝|あやま}る{f|羽目|はめ}になりました |
| Pointing back at a situation | こんな／そんな + {f|羽目|はめ}になる | どうしてこんな{f|羽目|はめ}になったのか |
| Stiffer variant | Verb + {f|羽目|はめ}に{f|陥|おちい}る | {f|返済|へんさい}に{f|追|お}われる{f|羽目|はめ}に{f|陥|おちい}る |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|羽目|はめ}を{f|外|はず}す</code>, "to let loose, to go
overboard", uses the same noun but is a completely separate idiom. The two turn up
together often enough to be worth keeping apart:
{f|羽目|はめ}を{f|外|はず}して{f|羽目|はめ}になる is a real sequence of events, not a
pun.

</div>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>{f|羽目|はめ}になる</code> is **conversational**. It belongs to talking about
your day, to blog posts and essays, to the story you tell at the office on Monday
morning. Nothing about it is stiff, and
<code>{f|羽目|はめ}になりました</code> is perfectly usable in a polite work email
describing a mishap.

The tone is **rueful rather than bitter**. Used about yourself it is
self-deprecating: the sentence in front of it names the small mistake that caused
the trouble - {f|忘|わす}れて, {f|逃|のが}して, {f|負|ま}けて, ～たら, ～せいで.
Used about someone else it sounds sympathetic, not accusing; you are on their side.
This is the opposite of <code>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</code>, which passes judgement.

Two limits are worth remembering. First, the outcome must be **unwelcome** - the
pattern cannot report good luck, and a happy ending in front of
{f|羽目|はめ}になる reads as sarcasm. Second, the outcome is normally a **task you
are stuck performing**, not merely a bad state. Something you *do* fits
({f|歩|ある}く, {f|払|はら}う, {f|謝|あやま}る); something that merely *happens to*
you sits badly, so ❌{f|風邪|かぜ}を{f|引|ひ}く{f|羽目|はめ}になった is odd where
{f|風邪|かぜ}を{f|引|ひ}いてしまった is natural.

The scale is domestic. Missing a train, redoing the furniture, chairing a meeting
you did not volunteer for - these are {f|羽目|はめ} events. For an evacuation or a
factory closure, written Japanese reaches for
<code>を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる</code> instead.

## Contrast

### ～{f|羽目|はめ}になる vs ～ことになる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ことになる</code> is **neutral**: it reports that
something has been arranged or has turned out that way.
<code>{f|羽目|はめ}になる</code> adds that it was a **nuisance** and that the
speaker walked into it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|大阪|おおさか}の{f|支店|してん}で{f|働|はたら}く<b>ことになりました</b>。
It's been arranged that I'll work at the Osaka branch from next month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|誰|だれ}も{f|手|て}を{f|挙|あ}げず、{f|私|わたし}が{f|司会|しかい}をする<b>{f|羽目|はめ}になった</b>。
Nobody put their hand up, so I got stuck chairing it.
</pre>

Swap them and the meaning shifts audibly. ことになった about the chairing would
sound like a normal appointment; {f|羽目|はめ}になった about the transfer would
tell your colleagues you are unhappy about it, which may be more than you wanted to
say.

### ～{f|羽目|はめ}になる vs ～を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same idea, two different worlds.
<code>{f|羽目|はめ}になる</code> is spoken, personal and small-scale.
<code>を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる</code> is written, impersonal and serious.

</div>

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}を{f|忘|わす}れて、{f|雨|あめ}の{f|中|なか}を{f|走|はし}る<b>{f|羽目|はめ}になった</b>。
I forgot my umbrella and ended up running through the rain.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大雨|おおあめ}のため、{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|中断|ちゅうだん}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>。
Heavy rain forced the works to be suspended.
</pre>

The grammar differs too: {f|羽目|はめ}になる takes a **verb**,
を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる takes a **noun**.

### ～{f|羽目|はめ}になる vs ～{f|始末|しまつ}だ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|羽目|はめ}になる</code> is about being **landed with
a job**. <code>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</code> is about the **disgraceful state** a whole
run of events finally reached, and it passes judgement.

</div>

<pre>
{f|後輩|こうはい}のミスで、{f|休日|きゅうじつ}に{f|出勤|しゅっきん}する<b>{f|羽目|はめ}になった</b>。
A junior colleague's mistake meant I had to come in on my day off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|後輩|こうはい}は{f|遅刻|ちこく}を{f|繰|く}り{f|返|かえ}し、とうとう{f|連絡|れんらく}もしない<b>{f|始末|しまつ}だ</b>。
He kept turning up late, and in the end stopped even letting anyone know.
</pre>

The first sentence is about the speaker's inconvenience; the second is a verdict on
somebody else's behaviour. {f|始末|しまつ}だ also needs a downhill run behind it,
where {f|羽目|はめ}になる is happy with a single blunder.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|歩|ある}いた<b>{f|羽目|はめ}になった</b> → ✅ {f|歩|ある}く<b>{f|羽目|はめ}になった</b>
  The verb stays in the dictionary form. Only なる takes the past tense.

- ❌ {f|宝|たから}くじが{f|当|あ}たって、{f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}く<b>{f|羽目|はめ}になった</b> → ✅ …{f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}くことになった
  Winning a holiday is not a fix you are stuck in. Good news cannot take
  {f|羽目|はめ}.

- ❌ {f|風邪|かぜ}を{f|引|ひ}く<b>{f|羽目|はめ}になった</b> → ✅ {f|風邪|かぜ}を{f|引|ひ}いてしまった
  Catching a cold is something that happens to you, not a task you were forced to
  carry out.

- ❌ {f|自分|じぶん}で{f|決|き}めて{f|留学|りゅうがく}する<b>{f|羽目|はめ}になった</b> → ✅ {f|自分|じぶん}で{f|決|き}めて{f|留学|りゅうがく}することにした
  A choice you made and wanted is the exact opposite of {f|羽目|はめ}.

## Practice

**1.** {f|寝坊|ねぼう}して、{f|会議|かいぎ}にタクシーで{f|向|む}かう____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

タクシーで{f|向|む}かう<b>{f|羽目|はめ}になった</b>

Oversleeping is the blunder, and the expensive taxi is the unwanted job it landed
the speaker with. Note the dictionary form {f|向|む}かう, not {f|向|む}かった.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|来年|らいねん}から{f|本社|ほんしゃ}で{f|働|はたら}く（{f|羽目|はめ}になりました・ことになりました）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|本社|ほんしゃ}で{f|働|はたら}く<b>ことになりました</b>。

Announcing a transfer to colleagues calls for the neutral ことになる.
{f|羽目|はめ}になる would tell everyone you resent the move - fine among close
friends, awkward in an announcement.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I put off writing the report, and ended up staying at the office until midnight."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}を{f|後回|あとまわ}しにして、{f|夜中|よなか}まで{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|残|のこ}る<b>{f|羽目|はめ}になった</b>。

"Ended up staying" is exactly {f|残|のこ}る{f|羽目|はめ}になった - the speaker's
own procrastination caused it, which is the classic shape for this pattern.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|道|みち}に{f|迷|まよ}って、{f|一時間|いちじかん}も{f|歩|ある}いた{f|羽目|はめ}になりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|道|みち}に{f|迷|まよ}って、{f|一時間|いちじかん}も<b>{f|歩|ある}く{f|羽目|はめ}になりました</b>。

The verb before {f|羽目|はめ} must be the plain non-past. The past tense belongs on
なる, and here the polite なりました already carries it.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|友人|ゆうじん}の{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}しを{f|手伝|てつだ}う<b>ことになった</b>。
(b) {f|友人|ゆうじん}の{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}しを{f|手伝|てつだ}う<b>{f|羽目|はめ}になった</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply reports the arrangement. The speaker may well have offered, and nothing
in the sentence says how they feel about it.

(b) says they were manoeuvred into it - they mentioned they were free, or nobody
else answered the message - and they are not thrilled. The listener will expect the
story of how it happened, and will probably laugh.

</details>
