# ～ても始まらない

**Meaning** · it's no use doing that; that won't get us anywhere - the activity produces no forward movement, so stop and do something else

Literally: even if you do it, **nothing starts**. That word {f|始|はじ}まる is the
whole character of the expression. It does not say the act is stupid or that the
world is hopeless; it says no progress comes out of it, and it therefore points
straight at whatever should be done instead. In practice it is almost always
encouragement.

## Formation

The て-form of a **verb**, plus も, plus {f|始|はじ}まらない. Adjectives and nouns do
not enter this pattern.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + も{f|始|はじ}まらない | {f|悩|なや}む → {f|悩|なや}んでも{f|始|はじ}まらない |
| する-verb | ～しても{f|始|はじ}まらない | {f|心配|しんぱい}する → {f|心配|しんぱい}しても{f|始|はじ}まらない |
| Verb phrase | Noun + を + て-form + も… | {f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}う → {f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}っても{f|始|はじ}まらない |
| With てみる | ～てみても{f|始|はじ}まらない | {f|考|かんが}える → {f|考|かんが}えてみても{f|始|はじ}まらない |
| Polite | ～ても{f|始|はじ}まりません | {f|焦|あせ}る → {f|焦|あせ}っても{f|始|はじ}まりません |
| Usual continuation | …から／…ので + a suggestion | {f|迷|まよ}う → {f|迷|まよ}っても{f|始|はじ}まらないから、{f|決|き}めよう |
| Not available | い-adjective, な-adjective, noun | ❌{f|寒|さむ}くても{f|始|はじ}まらない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the verb slot is narrow. It holds **unproductive mental and
verbal activity**: {f|悩|なや}む, {f|考|かんが}える, {f|心配|しんぱい}する,
{f|焦|あせ}る, {f|迷|まよ}う, {f|慌|あわ}てる, {f|後悔|こうかい}する,
{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}う, {f|責|せ}める, {f|泣|な}く. A genuinely productive
action in that slot sounds odd, because such an action *would* start something.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Conversational and neutral. The plain form is what friends and colleagues say to each
other, and {f|始|はじ}まりません fits a polite meeting without sounding stiff. What
is distinctive is the **direction it faces**: forward. Because the literal claim is
that nothing gets started, the sentence naturally hands over to a proposal, and in
real speech it very often arrives glued to one -
「{f|悩|なや}んでも{f|始|はじ}まらないから、{f|明日|あした}{f|聞|き}いてみよう」.

That makes it a **kind** thing to say. Told that your worrying is useless, you might
bristle; told that worrying is not going to start anything and that here is the next
step, you generally do not. Speakers use it to break a stalemate - in a meeting that
has turned into recrimination, at a kitchen table at two in the morning.

The verb restriction is the main thing to internalise. The pattern wants an activity
that spins on the spot: fretting, hesitating, arguing, apportioning blame. Put a
productive verb in and the sentence becomes strange, because
{f|掃除|そうじ}しても{f|始|はじ}まらない seems to claim that cleaning starts nothing,
which is false unless you mean cleaning is beside the point. Where you want to
dismiss a productive action, ～ても{f|意味|いみ}がない or
～てもどうにもならない is the tool.

Two form notes. The も is not optional: {f|悩|なや}んで{f|始|はじ}まらない, without
it, reads as a cause-and-effect statement and makes no sense. And the pattern is
normally in the present tense, because it states a general judgement rather than
reporting an outcome; the past
{f|始|はじ}まらなかった exists but is rare and usually means something more literal.

## Contrast

### ～ても{f|始|はじ}まらない vs ～てもどうにもならない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|始|はじ}まらない</code> says **this activity produces
nothing**, and expects a better activity next.
<code>どうにもならない</code> says **the situation itself will not shift**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|言|い}い{f|争|あらそ}っ<b>ても{f|始|はじ}まらない</b>。{f|条件|じょうけん}を{f|一|ひと}つずつ{f|見|み}ていこう。
Arguing gets us nowhere. Let's go through the terms one at a time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工場|こうじょう}はもう{f|閉|と}じられていて、{f|今|いま}から{f|抗議|こうぎ}し<b>てもどうにもならない</b>。
The factory has already closed; protesting at this point will change nothing.
</pre>

The first assumes there is still a way forward and is looking for it. The second
reports that the way forward is gone. That is also why
<code>どうにもならない</code> accepts any verb at all while
<code>{f|始|はじ}まらない</code> keeps to its narrow list: one is about the world,
the other is about how you are spending your time.

### ～ても{f|始|はじ}まらない vs ～てもしかたがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>しかたがない</code> **consoles**: there is no help for it,
so don't blame yourself. <code>{f|始|はじ}まらない</code> **prods**: this isn't
moving us, so let's do something.

</div>

<pre>
{f|終|お}わったことだから、{f|落|お}ち{f|込|こ}ん<b>でもしかたがないよ</b>。
It's over and done with - there's no point being down about it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|落|お}ち{f|込|こ}ん<b>でも{f|始|はじ}まらない</b>から、そろそろ{f|次|つぎ}の{f|準備|じゅんび}をしよう。
Being down about it isn't getting us anywhere, so let's start getting ready for the next one.
</pre>

Both are said to the same dejected person, and the difference is what the speaker
wants from them. <code>しかたがない</code> asks for acceptance;
<code>{f|始|はじ}まらない</code> asks for movement. If the next sentence out of your
mouth is a suggestion, this is the one you want.

### ～ても{f|始|はじ}まらない vs ～ても{f|意味|いみ}がない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|意味|いみ}がない</code> judges the act **meaningless**
in itself, and applies to anything. <code>{f|始|はじ}まらない</code> says only that
it **produces no progress**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|答|こた}えを{f|写|うつ}すだけでは、{f|宿題|しゅくだい}をやっ<b>ても{f|意味|いみ}がない</b>。
Just copying the answers means there's no point doing the homework at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|結論|けつろん}の{f|出|で}ない{f|話|はなし}を{f|続|つづ}け<b>ても{f|始|はじ}まらない</b>。
There is no sense going on with a discussion that isn't reaching a conclusion.
</pre>

<code>{f|意味|いみ}がない</code> is the broader and blunter of the two. It can be
said of an object, a rule, a whole plan, and it can sound dismissive of the person
who proposed it. <code>{f|始|はじ}まらない</code> criticises no one; it just observes
that the clock is running.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|悩|なや}ん<b>で{f|始|はじ}まらない</b>。 → ✅ {f|悩|なや}ん<b>でも{f|始|はじ}まらない</b>。
  The も is part of the pattern. Without it the sentence reads as cause and effect -
  "it doesn't start because you worry" - which means nothing.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っ<b>ても{f|始|はじ}まらない</b>。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っ<b>てもどうにもならない</b>。
  The slot holds something a person is *doing* pointlessly. Weather is not an
  activity anyone can be talked out of.

- ❌ {f|寒|さむ}く<b>ても{f|始|はじ}まらない</b>。 → ✅ {f|寒|さむ}くても{f|仕方|しかた}がない。
  Adjectives and nouns do not enter this pattern at all. It takes verbs only.

- ❌ {f|部屋|へや}を{f|掃除|そうじ}し<b>ても{f|始|はじ}まらない</b>。 → ✅ {f|部屋|へや}を{f|掃除|そうじ}し<b>ても{f|意味|いみ}がない</b>。
  Cleaning is productive, so saying it starts nothing is close to a contradiction.
  For dismissing a real action, use {f|意味|いみ}がない or どうにもならない.

## Practice

**1.** {f|結果|けっか}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}まで{f|分|わ}からないのだから、{f|今|いま}から{f|心配|しんぱい}し____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|心配|しんぱい}し<b>ても{f|始|はじ}まらない</b>（よ）

Worrying is the archetypal verb for this pattern, and the sentence is comforting
someone rather than dismissing them. Note the する-verb form
{f|心配|しんぱい}しても.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|契約|けいやく}はもう{f|終|お}わっているので、{f|今|いま}から{f|条件|じょうけん}を{f|変|か}えてほしいと{f|言|い}っ（ても{f|始|はじ}まらない・てもどうにもならない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|言|い}っ<b>てもどうにもならない</b>。

The contract is closed - the situation itself has stopped moving, which is
<code>どうにもならない</code>. <code>{f|始|はじ}まらない</code> would imply there is
still somewhere better to direct the effort.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "There's no use blaming each other. Let's work out what to do next."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お{f|互|たが}いを{f|責|せ}め{f|合|あ}っ<b>ても{f|始|はじ}まらない</b>。{f|次|つぎ}にどうするか{f|考|かんが}えよう。

Mutual blame is exactly the wheel-spinning this pattern names, and the suggestion
that follows is the shape it almost always comes in.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|準備|じゅんび}が{f|足|た}りなくても{f|始|はじ}まらないので、あきらめました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|準備|じゅんび}が{f|足|た}りず、どうにもならなかったので、あきらめました。
"We weren't ready enough and there was nothing to be done, so we gave up."

Two problems. {f|足|た}りない is a state rather than a futile activity, and the
conclusion is surrender - but <code>{f|始|はじ}まらない</code> exists to point at a
next step, so it clashes with あきらめました.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|後悔|こうかい}し<b>てもしかたがない</b>。
(b) {f|後悔|こうかい}し<b>ても{f|始|はじ}まらない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) lets the listener off. It says the regret is understandable but pointless, and
invites them to stop being hard on themselves. Nothing is asked of them.

(b) is asking for something. It says the regret is not moving anybody forward, and
the listener will expect the speaker's next sentence to be a proposal. Same comfort,
different destination.

</details>
