# ～にかこつけて

**Meaning** · using ~ as a pretext; on the excuse of ~ - the stated reason is a fig leaf for what the person wanted to do anyway

`にかこつけて` names the excuse, not the motive. Someone had already decided to open
the good wine, take the afternoon off, or cut the department; the birthday, the
business trip or the bad quarter simply came along and gave them cover. The pattern
carries the speaker's judgement with it: **you can see through the excuse**. Turned
on yourself it is wry rather than harsh, which is how it is most often used.

## Formation

The verb is かこつける, a る-verb, almost always met in this て-form. It is written in
kana in practice; the kanji spelling {f|託|かこ}つける exists but is rare.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | noun + にかこつけて | {f|出張|しゅっちょう} → {f|出張|しゅっちょう}にかこつけて |
| Verb | plain form + の / こと + にかこつけて | {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}す → {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}すのにかこつけて |
| Written (clause-linking) | ～にかこつけ | {f|不況|ふきょう} → {f|不況|ふきょう}にかこつけ、… |
| Modifying a noun | ～にかこつけた + noun | {f|視察|しさつ} → {f|視察|しさつ}にかこつけた{f|旅行|りょこう} |
| Progressive habit | ～にかこつけては | {f|残業|ざんぎょう} → {f|残業|ざんぎょう}にかこつけては |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the excuse goes before `にかこつけて`; the thing actually wanted
goes after it. Get them the wrong way round and the sentence says the opposite of
what you mean.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`にかこつけて` is **neutral in politeness but never neutral in attitude**. It works
in conversation among adults, in essays and columns, and in news writing about
governments and companies; it is not stiff, and it is not slang. What it always
supplies is the speaker's verdict that the stated reason is not the real one.

The strength of the criticism depends entirely on who the subject is. About
**yourself** it is light and self-deprecating - admitting cheerfully that you wanted
the wine anyway. About a **friend or family member** it is teasing. About a
**company, a government or an opponent** it is a genuine accusation, and in that use
it appears in editorials and investigative reporting with real force.

Two boundaries are worth marking. The excuse has to be **plausible on its face**:
`にかこつけて` describes a cover story that would pass, not a transparent lie. And
the thing done afterwards is normally **mildly indulgent or self-serving**, not
criminal. Serious wrongdoing takes stronger words -
<code>～を{f|口実|こうじつ}に</code>, <code>～を{f|装|よそお}って</code>,
<code>～と{f|偽|いつわ}って</code>.

The bare verb かこつける does exist outside this pattern, but you will rarely need
it. Learn the て-form as a unit, together with the written variant `にかこつけ`,
which is how newspapers write it.

## Contrast

### ～にかこつけて vs ～を{f|口実|こうじつ}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same manoeuvre at two temperatures. `にかこつけて` is
colloquial and often affectionate; <code>を{f|口実|こうじつ}に</code> is colder and
suits a deliberate, calculated excuse.

</div>

<pre>
{f|健康|けんこう}{f|診断|しんだん}<b>にかこつけて</b>、{f|午後|ごご}は{f|休|やす}ませてもらった。
I used the medical check-up as an excuse to take the afternoon off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|安全|あんぜん}{f|対策|たいさく}<b>を{f|口実|こうじつ}に</b>、{f|反対|はんたい}{f|派|は}の{f|集会|しゅうかい}が{f|禁止|きんし}された。
Under the pretext of safety measures, the opposition's rallies were banned.
</pre>

{f|口実|こうじつ} is a noun meaning "pretext", so it can also be used on its own -
<code>{f|口実|こうじつ}を{f|作|つく}る</code>, <code>ただの{f|口実|こうじつ}だ</code> -
whereas `にかこつけて` only ever works as an adverbial phrase attached to a verb. For
the small indulgences of daily life, `にかこつけて` is the more idiomatic choice.

### ～にかこつけて vs ～を{f|理由|りゆう}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|理由|りゆう}に</code> is **neutral**: it states the
grounds given, with no comment on whether they were sincere. `にかこつけて` says they
were not.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大雪|おおゆき}<b>を{f|理由|りゆう}に</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|延期|えんき}された。
The match was postponed on account of the heavy snow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大雪|おおゆき}<b>にかこつけて</b>、{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|一日|いちにち}サボった。
I used the heavy snow as an excuse to skip a day's training.
</pre>

Notice that the first sentence takes no position at all - the snow may well have been
the whole story. Swapping in `にかこつけて` accuses somebody. This is why
<code>を{f|理由|りゆう}に</code> is the safe form in a report and `にかこつけて`
is not.

### ～にかこつけて vs ～にかまけて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - they sound almost alike and mean quite different things.
`にかこつけて` = X is my **excuse** for doing what I wanted. `にかまけて` = X has
**swallowed** me, and I have neglected everything else.

</div>

<pre>
{f|仕事|しごと}<b>にかこつけて</b>、{f|親戚|しんせき}の{f|集|あつ}まりを{f|欠席|けっせき}した。
I used work as an excuse to get out of the family gathering.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|仕事|しごと}<b>にかまけて</b>、{f|親戚|しんせき}づき{f|合|あ}いをおろそかにしてきた。
I let work take me over, and neglected my relatives for years.
</pre>

In the first the speaker did not want to go and used work as cover; the work may not
even have been urgent. In the second the work was real and consuming, and the fault
is neglect rather than dishonesty. One sentence admits a small deception, the other
admits a long-running failure.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ワインを{f|開|あ}けること<b>にかこつけて</b>、{f|誕生日|たんじょうび}を{f|祝|いわ}った。 → ✅ {f|誕生日|たんじょうび}<b>にかこつけて</b>、ワインを{f|開|あ}けた。
  The order is fixed: excuse first, real object of desire second. Reversed, the
  sentence claims you invented a birthday to justify a bottle.

- ❌ {f|病気|びょうき}<b>にかこつけて</b>、{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|休|やす}まなければならなかった。 → ✅ {f|病気|びょうき}の<b>ため</b>、{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|休|やす}まなければならなかった。
  A genuine reason does not take this pattern. `にかこつけて` always implies the
  reason was a cover.

- ❌ {f|出張|しゅっちょう}する<b>にかこつけて</b>、{f|観光|かんこう}もしてきた。 → ✅ {f|出張|しゅっちょう}する<b>のにかこつけて</b>、{f|観光|かんこう}もしてきた。
  A verb clause needs `の` or `こと` to turn it into a noun before `に`. Nouns attach
  directly.

- ❌ {f|震災|しんさい}<b>にかこつけて</b>、{f|被害者|ひがいしゃ}から{f|金|かね}をだまし{f|取|と}った。 → ✅ {f|震災|しんさい}を<b>{f|口実|こうじつ}に</b>、{f|被害者|ひがいしゃ}から{f|金|かね}をだまし{f|取|と}った。
  The pattern is too light for outright fraud. Serious deception wants
  {f|口実|こうじつ}, {f|装|よそお}う or {f|偽|いつわ}る.

## Practice

**1.** {f|同窓会|どうそうかい}____、{f|久|ひさ}しぶりに{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}の{f|休暇|きゅうか}を{f|取|と}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|同窓会|どうそうかい}<b>にかこつけて</b>

A reunion is a perfectly presentable reason for a day off, but not for a whole week -
which is exactly the gap the pattern points at. The noun attaches with nothing
between it and `に`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|新|あたら}しいゲーム（にかこつけて・にかまけて）、{f|試験|しけん}{f|勉強|べんきょう}がまったく{f|進|すす}んでいない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|新|あたら}しいゲーム<b>にかまけて</b>

Nothing is being excused here; the game has simply eaten the study time. That is
`にかまけて`. `にかこつけて` would require the game to be the stated reason for doing
something else the speaker wanted.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The minister used the inspection as a pretext to visit his home town."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|大臣|だいじん}は{f|視察|しさつ}<b>にかこつけて</b>{f|故郷|こきょう}を{f|訪|おとず}れた。

"Used ~ as a pretext" is the pattern's core meaning, and a politician is a typical
subject for its critical use. In a newspaper the same sentence would often appear
with the written form {f|視察|しさつ}にかこつけ.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|く}るにかこつけて、{f|授業|じゅぎょう}が{f|休講|きゅうこう}になった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|く}る<b>ため</b>、{f|授業|じゅぎょう}は{f|休講|きゅうこう}になった。

Two problems. A verb clause would need <code>のにかこつけて</code>, and more
importantly a typhoon is a real reason for cancelling classes, so no pretext is
involved at all.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|経費|けいひ}{f|削減|さくげん}<b>を{f|理由|りゆう}に</b>、{f|社員|しゃいん}{f|旅行|りょこう}を{f|取|と}りやめた。
(b) {f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|経費|けいひ}{f|削減|さくげん}<b>にかこつけて</b>、{f|社員|しゃいん}{f|旅行|りょこう}を{f|取|と}りやめた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply reports the grounds the company gave. The writer takes no view; cost
cutting may have been the honest and sufficient reason.

(b) accuses. It says management had wanted to be rid of the staff trip anyway and
seized on the budget as cover. The facts reported are the same, but (b) could not
appear in a neutral news item - it belongs to a column, or to someone grumbling in
the corridor.

</details>
