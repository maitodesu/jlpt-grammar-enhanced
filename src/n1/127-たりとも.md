# ～たりとも

**Meaning** · not even one; not a single - takes the smallest possible quantity and denies even that

<code>たりとも</code> is the classical copula <code>たり</code> plus the concessive
<code>とも</code>: "even if it be one". What is left in modern Japanese is a
declaration form. It sits on <code>{f|一|いち}</code> plus a counter, and the
predicate that follows is always a refusal, a denial or a prohibition -
<code>{f|一日|いちにち}たりとも{f|忘|わす}れない</code>, "not for one single day".
Recognise it; in ordinary Japanese say <code>{f|一日|いちにち}も{f|忘|わす}れない</code>.

## Formation

The number is essentially always <code>{f|一|いち}</code>. The predicate must be
negative or prohibitive.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| {f|一|いち} + counter | ～たりとも + negative | {f|一日|いちにち} → {f|一日|いちにち}たりとも{f|休|やす}まない |
| {f|一|いち} + counter | ～たりとも + potential negative | {f|一分|いっぷん} → {f|一分|いっぷん}たりとも{f|待|ま}てない |
| {f|一|いち} + counter | ～たりとも + prohibition | {f|一歩|いっぽ} → {f|一歩|いっぽ}たりとも{f|引|ひ}くな |
| {f|一|いち} + counter | ～たりとも + わけにはいかない | {f|一人|ひとり} → {f|一人|ひとり}たりとも{f|失|うしな}うわけにはいかない |
| {f|何人|なんぴと} | ～たりとも + prohibition | {f|何人|なんぴと} → {f|何人|なんぴと}たりとも{f|立|た}ち{f|入|い}るべからず |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the counters that actually occur are a short list:
{f|一日|いちにち}, {f|一瞬|いっしゅん}, {f|一分|いっぷん}, {f|一度|いちど},
{f|一人|ひとり}, {f|一円|いちえん}, {f|一滴|いってき}, {f|一歩|いっぽ},
{f|一言|ひとこと}. Watch the readings: {f|一人|ひとり} not いちにん, and
{f|一分|いっぷん} with gemination. <code>{f|何人|なんぴと}たりとも</code> is a frozen
legal-sounding phrase meaning "no person whatsoever"; it is also read
なんびと, and older texts have なにびと.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|一日|いちにち}<b>たりとも</b>{f|忘|わす}れたことはない。
Not for a single day have I forgotten.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一分|いっぷん}<b>たりとも</b>{f|遅|おく}れられない。
We cannot be even one minute late.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一瞬|いっしゅん}<b>たりとも</b>{f|気|き}を{f|抜|ぬ}いてはならない。
You must not let your concentration slip for so much as an instant.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|何人|なんぴと}<b>たりとも</b>、{f|許可|きょか}なくこの{f|区域|くいき}に{f|立|た}ち{f|入|い}ることを{f|禁|きん}ずる。
No person whatsoever may enter this area without permission.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|預|あず}かった{f|命|いのち}である。{f|一人|ひとり}<b>たりとも</b>{f|欠|か}けさせるわけにはいかない。
These are lives entrusted to us. We cannot afford to lose a single one.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|砂漠|さばく}を{f|越|こ}える{f|間|あいだ}、{f|水|みず}は{f|一滴|いってき}<b>たりとも</b>{f|無駄|むだ}にできなかった。
Crossing the desert, we could not waste even one drop of water.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|公金|こうきん}を{f|預|あず}かる{f|立場|たちば}として、{f|一円|いちえん}<b>たりとも</b>{f|使途|しと}を{f|曖昧|あいまい}にすることは{f|許|ゆる}されません。
Entrusted as we are with public money, we cannot leave the use of a single yen unaccounted for.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|創業|そうぎょう}から{f|四十年|よんじゅうねん}、{f|父|ちち}が{f|店|みせ}の{f|鍵|かぎ}を{f|開|あ}けなかった{f|日|ひ}は{f|一日|いちにち}<b>たりとも</b>なかった。
In the forty years since he founded it, there was not one single day my father failed to unlock the shop.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**This is a form to recognise, not to deploy.** Its natural homes are notices and
signage, contracts and regulations, formal apologies and pledges, courtroom and
parliamentary speech, coaches and commanders addressing a team or a unit, and
fiction that imitates any of those. Say it across a dinner table and it will land
as a joke, because it sounds like a vow. The everyday equivalent is
<code>{f|一日|いちにち}も</code>, <code>{f|一度|いちど}も</code>,
<code>{f|少|すこ}しも</code> or <code>まったく</code> plus a negative.

Two hard structural facts. First, **the number is {f|一|いち}**. Japanese does not build
❌<code>{f|三日|みっか}たりとも</code> or ❌<code>{f|五人|ごにん}たりとも</code>; the
whole point is the smallest conceivable unit, and a larger number destroys it.
Second, **the predicate must be negative or prohibitive.** A positive sentence with
`たりとも` in it is simply broken.

The feeling the pattern carries is *resolve*. It is not neutral counting - it is a
speaker committing to something absolutely, or forbidding something absolutely. That
is why <code>{f|一円|いちえん}たりとも</code> turns up in speeches about public money
and <code>{f|一歩|いっぽ}たりとも{f|引|ひ}かない</code> in speeches about
negotiations. When you meet it in a text, the question to ask is not "what does
this quantity mean" but "what is this person swearing to".

## Contrast

### ～たりとも vs {f|一|いち}～も～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `も` is the everyday form of the same idea. `たりとも` adds
solemnity and nothing else to the meaning.

</div>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}は{f|一日|いちにち}<b>も</b>{f|休|やす}まなかった。
I did not take a single day off last year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}は{f|一日|いちにち}<b>たりとも</b>{f|休|やす}まなかった。
Not for one day last year did I rest.
</pre>

Identical facts. The first is a person reporting; the second is a person declaring.
If you are unsure which you want, you want the first - `も` is never wrong here,
whereas `たりとも` in a casual context always is.

### ～たりとも vs ～すら・～さえ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `すら` and `さえ` pick out an extreme **item**. `たりとも`
denies a minimum **quantity**, and needs a number and a counter in front of it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|水|みず}<b>すら</b>{f|残|のこ}っていなかった。
There was not even water left.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|水|みず}は{f|一滴|いってき}<b>たりとも</b>{f|残|のこ}っていなかった。
There was not one drop of water left.
</pre>

The first says the category was empty; the second measures the emptiness. That is
why ❌<code>{f|水|みず}たりとも</code> fails - there is no quantity in it - and why
<code>{f|一滴|いってき}すら</code> is fine but sounds less like a vow.

### ～たりとも vs ～といえども

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both come from the same classical concessive, but
`といえども` means "even though it is X" and takes any noun or clause.
`たりとも` is restricted to <code>{f|一|いち}</code> plus a counter.

</div>

<pre>
{f|専門家|せんもんか}<b>といえども</b>{f|間違|まちが}えることはある。
Even a specialist can be wrong.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一度|いちど}<b>たりとも</b>{f|間違|まちが}えたことはない。
He has never been wrong even once.
</pre>

`といえども` concedes a category and then contradicts it; `たりとも` counts to one
and stops there. They are cousins in origin and are not substitutable in either
direction.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|三日|みっか}<b>たりとも</b>{f|休|やす}めない。 → ✅ {f|一日|いちにち}<b>たりとも</b>{f|休|やす}めない。
  The numeral has to be <code>{f|一|いち}</code>. Any larger quantity contradicts
  the whole point of the expression.

- ❌ {f|一日|いちにち}<b>たりとも</b>{f|休|やす}んだ。 → ✅ {f|一日|いちにち}<b>たりとも</b>{f|休|やす}まなかった。
  The pattern only exists with a negative or a prohibition. There is no affirmative
  use.

- ❌ {f|一人|いちにん}<b>たりとも</b>{f|逃|に}がすな。 → ✅ {f|一人|ひとり}<b>たりとも</b>{f|逃|に}がすな。
  A reading error rather than a grammar one, but a conspicuous one:
  {f|一人|ひとり} and {f|二人|ふたり} keep their native readings.

- ❌ ごめん、{f|一分|いっぷん}<b>たりとも</b>{f|待|ま}てないんだけど。 → ✅ ごめん、{f|一分|いっぷん}<b>も</b>{f|待|ま}てないんだけど。
  Register clash. An apology, a contraction and a sentence-final んだけど do not
  belong in the same sentence as a formal vow.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to read this, and what is it doing? {f|何人|なんぴと}<b>たりとも</b>この{f|線|せん}を{f|越|こ}えてはならない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

A notice or a barrier sign - a construction site, a restricted zone, a courtroom -
or fiction imitating one. <code>{f|何人|なんぴと}たりとも</code> is the frozen legal
phrase for "no person whatsoever", and the sentence is a prohibition addressed to
the world at large rather than to anyone in particular.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary spoken Japanese: {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|一度|いちど}<b>たりとも</b>{f|休|やす}まなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|一度|いちど}<b>も</b>{f|休|やす}まなかった。

Swap `たりとも` for `も` and the sentence becomes something a person would say
about a teammate. The meaning does not move at all; only the solemnity goes.

</details>

**3.** Fill in a counter that fits: {f|交渉|こうしょう}では、こちらから____たりとも{f|譲|ゆず}るつもりはない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一歩|いっぽ}たりとも (also {f|一|いち}ミリたりとも in looser writing)

<code>{f|一歩|いっぽ}も{f|譲|ゆず}らない</code>, "not give an inch", is the standard
image for negotiation, and `たりとも` raises it to a declaration. Note the
gemination: {f|一歩|いっぽ}, not ❌いちほ.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|一円|いちえん}たりとも{f|大切|たいせつ}に{f|使|つか}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|一円|いちえん}<b>たりとも</b>{f|無駄|むだ}に<b>しません</b>。

The sentence is positive, and `たりとも` cannot live in a positive sentence. Turn the
promise around into a denial - "we will not waste even one yen" - and the pattern
works. Alternatively drop the pattern: <code>{f|一円|いちえん}{f|一円|いちえん}を{f|大切|たいせつ}に{f|使|つか}います</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|一瞬|いっしゅん}<b>たりとも</b>{f|目|め}を{f|離|はな}さなかった。
(b) {f|一瞬|いっしゅん}<b>も</b>{f|目|め}を{f|離|はな}さなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both mean the speaker never looked away. (b) is a report: it could describe watching
a film, a child, or a football match, in any register.

(a) sounds like testimony or narration - a nurse describing a night at a bedside, a
witness in court, a line in a novel. The archaic particle frames the act as a duty
discharged without fail, which is more weight than most situations want.

</details>
