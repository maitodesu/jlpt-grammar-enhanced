# ～ところから

**Meaning** · from the fact that; on the strength of the observation that ~ - the noticed detail that a name, a nickname or a conclusion came out of

`ところから` points at one **observable feature** of a thing and says that this
feature is where something else came from. Overwhelmingly, what came from it is a
**name**: a mountain is called such-and-such because it looks like Fuji, a district
kept its old name because smiths once worked there. Its second job is to give the
evidence behind an inference - the light is on, *from which* we know he is home. In
both uses the speaker is explaining an origin to someone who did not know it, which
is why the pattern lives in captions, guidebooks and reference works.

## Formation

<code>ところ</code> is a noun, so what precedes it is in noun-modifying shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + ところから | {f|似|に}ている → {f|似|に}ているところから |
| Verb (past) | ～た + ところから | {f|集|あつ}まった → {f|集|あつ}まったところから |
| い-adjective | plain form + ところから | {f|赤|あか}い → {f|赤|あか}いところから |
| な-adjective | ～な + ところから | {f|静|しず}かな → {f|静|しず}かなところから |
| Noun | ～である + ところから | {f|名産地|めいさんち} → {f|名産地|めいさんち}であるところから |
| Noun (past) | ～だった + ところから | {f|沼地|ぬまち}だった → {f|沼地|ぬまち}だったところから |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the naming use has a fixed second half. Look for
<code>～と{f|呼|よ}ばれる</code>, <code>～と{f|呼|よ}ばれるようになった</code>,
<code>～という{f|名|な}がついた</code> or <code>～と{f|名付|なづ}けられた</code>. If you
learn the whole frame together, the pattern becomes almost automatic to recognise.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`ところから` is **written and explanatory**. Its home is the caption under a museum
exhibit, the etymology paragraph of a dictionary entry, a guidebook, a company
history, or a news report summarising what investigators have concluded. Nobody says
it while chatting; in speech the same idea comes out as `～から` or
<code>～ので、それで</code>.

The tone is neutral and slightly scholarly. There is no emotion in it at all, which
is part of why it fits reference writing so well: the writer is not persuading, only
recording where something came from.

Two structural habits are worth memorising. First, the second clause is almost
always **passive or descriptive** - {f|呼|よ}ばれる, つけられた, {f|残|のこ}っている, {f|見|み}られている.
The pattern explains how things came to be called or regarded as they are, not what
anyone decided to do. Second, the thing in front of `ところから` is a **noticeable
feature**, something you could point at: a shape, a colour, a sound, a number, a
mark on the ground. An abstract state works badly.

`ことから` does the same work and is considerably more common; treat `ところから` as
its slightly more literary variant, with the extra suggestion that the speaker is
pointing at one particular *aspect* of the thing.

## Contrast

### ～ところから vs ～ことから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the two are interchangeable in most naming sentences. `ことから`
is the **ordinary** choice and takes any kind of grounds; `ところから` is more
**literary** and prefers a concrete, observable feature.

</div>

<pre>
{f|白|しろ}い{f|花|はな}を{f|咲|さ}かせる<b>ことから</b>、この{f|木|き}は{f|雪柳|ゆきやなぎ}と{f|呼|よ}ばれる。
It bears white blossom, which is how the shrub came to be called "snow willow".
</pre>

<pre>
{f|石|いし}の{f|表面|ひょうめん}に{f|波|なみ}のような{f|模様|もよう}がある<b>ところから</b>、この{f|岩|いわ}は{f|波岩|なみいわ}と{f|名付|なづ}けられた。
The wave-like pattern on its surface is what gave this rock the name "wave rock".
</pre>

Where they part company is breadth. `ことから` happily takes an abstract or negative
reason - <code>{f|前例|ぜんれい}がないことから、{f|判断|はんだん}は{f|見送|みおく}られた</code>
- while `ところから` on the same sentence sounds as though you were pointing at a
non-existent precedent. If there is nothing to point at, use `ことから`.

### ～ところから vs ～から・～ので

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `から` and `ので` explain **why something was done**.
`ところから` explains **what an observation gave rise to**: a name, a nickname, or a
conclusion.

</div>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っていた<b>から</b>、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}って{f|出|で}た。
It was raining, so I took an umbrella out with me.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}が{f|濡|ぬ}れている<b>ところから</b>、{f|外|そと}は{f|雨|あめ}らしいと{f|判断|はんだん}した。
From the wetness of the umbrella I judged that it must be raining outside.
</pre>

Notice the direction. In the first sentence the rain causes an action; in the second
the wet umbrella is evidence, and what follows is not an action but a **conclusion**.
❌<code>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っていたところから、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}って{f|出|で}た</code>
is the standard error - reasons for your own behaviour are `から` territory.

### ～ところから (grounds) vs ～ところから (starting point)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if `ところ` means a **stage or moment**, then `から` is the
ordinary "from": the story starts *from* that point. If `ところ` is a feature you
could point at, it is the grounds pattern.

</div>

<pre>
{f|物語|ものがたり}は{f|主人公|しゅじんこう}が{f|島|しま}に{f|着|つ}いた<b>ところから</b>{f|始|はじ}まる。
The story begins from the point where the hero arrives on the island.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|主人公|しゅじんこう}が{f|島|しま}の{f|言葉|ことば}を{f|知|し}っていた<b>ところから</b>、{f|読者|どくしゃ}は{f|彼|かれ}の{f|過去|かこ}を{f|察|さっ}する。
Because the hero already knows the island's language, the reader guesses at his past.
</pre>

The two are spelled identically and are told apart only by what follows. A verb of
starting, continuing or resuming - {f|始|はじ}まる, {f|再開|さいかい}する,
{f|話|はな}し{f|直|なお}す - means you are looking at a point on a timeline. A verb of
naming, judging or inferring means you are looking at grounds. If neither fits, read
the sentence again before deciding.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|静|しず}か<b>ところから</b>、この{f|町|まち}は「{f|眠|ねむ}る{f|町|まち}」と{f|呼|よ}ばれる。 → ✅ {f|静|しず}か<b>なところから</b>、この{f|町|まち}は「{f|眠|ねむ}る{f|町|まち}」と{f|呼|よ}ばれる。
  <code>ところ</code> is a noun, so a な-adjective needs its `な`, and a noun needs
  <code>である</code>.

- ❌ {f|寒|さむ}かった<b>ところから</b>、{f|暖房|だんぼう}をつけた。 → ✅ {f|寒|さむ}かった<b>ので</b>、{f|暖房|だんぼう}をつけた。
  The second clause has to be something concluded or named, not something the
  speaker decided to do.

- ❌ この{f|島|しま}は{f|形|かたち}が{f|靴|くつ}に{f|似|に}ている<b>ところから</b>、{f|多|おお}くの{f|観光客|かんこうきゃく}を{f|集|あつ}めている。 → ✅ この{f|島|しま}は{f|形|かたち}が{f|靴|くつ}に{f|似|に}ている<b>ことから</b>「{f|靴島|くつじま}」と{f|呼|よ}ばれている。
  Attracting tourists is a consequence, not an origin. `ところから` needs the second
  clause to be the name or judgement that grew out of the observation.

- ❌ {f|明|あ}かりがついている<b>ところで</b>、{f|中|なか}に{f|誰|だれ}かいるようだ。 → ✅ {f|明|あ}かりがついている<b>ところから</b>、{f|中|なか}に{f|誰|だれ}かいるようだ。
  Only `から` gives the "grounds" reading. <code>ところで</code> and
  <code>ところに</code> belong to the temporal ところ family and change the meaning
  completely.

## Practice

**1.** {f|貝|かい}の{f|化石|かせき}がたくさん{f|見|み}つかった____、この{f|丘|おか}は{f|昔|むかし}{f|海|うみ}の{f|底|そこ}だったと{f|考|かんが}えられている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|見|み}つかった<b>ところから</b>

Shell fossils are a concrete find, and what follows is a conclusion drawn from them
rather than an action taken. That is the evidential use of the pattern, and the verb
stays in plain past form.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|予算|よさん}が{f|足|た}りない（ところから・ことから）、{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|来年|らいねん}に{f|延期|えんき}された。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|足|た}りない<b>ことから</b>

A budget shortfall is an abstract circumstance, not a feature you could point at, so
`ことから` is the fit. `ところから` would also need a name or judgement in the second
clause, and a postponement is neither.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The bridge is painted bright red, which is how it came to be known as the Red Bridge."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|橋|はし}が{f|真|ま}っ{f|赤|か}に{f|塗|ぬ}られている<b>ところから</b>、この{f|橋|はし}は{f|赤橋|あかばし}と{f|呼|よ}ばれるようになった。

"Which is how it came to be known as" is the naming frame the pattern lives in, and
the colour is exactly the kind of visible feature it wants in front. Keep the second
clause passive.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|新|あたら}しい{f|駅|えき}ができたところから、この{f|辺|あた}りの{f|地価|ちか}が{f|上|あ}がった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|新|あたら}しい{f|駅|えき}ができた<b>ことで</b>、この{f|辺|あた}りの{f|地価|ちか}が{f|上|あ}がった。
"Land prices around here went up because the new station was built."

A rise in land prices is a real-world effect, not a name or an inference, so the
grounds pattern does not apply. <code>ことで</code> or <code>ため</code> states the
cause plainly.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|部長|ぶちょう}が{f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}った<b>ところから</b>おかしくなった。
(b) {f|部長|ぶちょう}が{f|途中|とちゅう}で{f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}った<b>ところから</b>、{f|話|はなし}はすでに{f|決|き}まっていたと{f|推測|すいそく}できる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) uses `ところ` as a **point in time**: the meeting started going wrong from the
moment the manager left his seat. `から` here is the ordinary "from", and the
sentence is about a timeline.

(b) uses the **grounds** pattern: his leaving is treated as evidence, and the second
clause is what the observer infers from it. The giveaway is the verb at the end -
{f|推測|すいそく}できる is an inference, while おかしくなった in (a) is just what
happened next.

</details>
