# ～を前提に

**Meaning** · on the premise that; on the assumption of - X is treated as settled, and everything that follows is built on top of it

{f|前提|ぜんてい} is "premise" in the logician's sense: the thing you take as given
before you start. <code>～を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</code> announces that assumption
openly. The point of saying it aloud is that everyone can then see what the plan
rests on - and what would collapse if the assumption turned out to be false. That
makes it the language of contracts, negotiations, budgets and project proposals.

## Formation

The pattern takes a **noun**; a clause is packaged with こと or という{f|前提|ぜんてい}.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に | {f|参加|さんか} → {f|参加|さんか}を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に |
| Noun (stiffer, documents) | Noun + を{f|前提|ぜんてい}として | {f|値上|ねあ}げ → {f|値上|ねあ}げを{f|前提|ぜんてい}として |
| Fuller variant | Noun + を{f|前提|ぜんてい}にして | {f|結婚|けっこん} → {f|結婚|けっこん}を{f|前提|ぜんてい}にして |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + を{f|前提|ぜんてい}とした + Noun | {f|在宅|ざいたく}{f|勤務|きんむ} → {f|在宅|ざいたく}{f|勤務|きんむ}を{f|前提|ぜんてい}とした{f|制度|せいど} |
| Same, non-past shape | Noun + を{f|前提|ぜんてい}とする + Noun | {f|返済|へんさい} → {f|返済|へんさい}を{f|前提|ぜんてい}とする{f|契約|けいやく} |
| Clause | ～こと + を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に | {f|来|く}る → {f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|来|く}ることを{f|前提|ぜんてい}に |
| Clause, spoken | ～という{f|前提|ぜんてい}で | {f|届|とど}く → {f|明日|あした}{f|届|とど}くという{f|前提|ぜんてい}で |

<div class="note">

**Note** - after a **bare noun** the particle is `に` or `として`, not `で`:
❌<code>{f|結婚|けっこん}を{f|前提|ぜんてい}で</code>. `で` appears only in the clause
pattern <code>～という{f|前提|ぜんてい}で</code>, where {f|前提|ぜんてい} is a full noun
of its own.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|結婚|けっこん}<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|付|つ}き{f|合|あ}っています。
We are seeing each other with a view to marriage.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|参加|さんか}<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}として</b>{f|話|はなし}を{f|進|すす}めます。
We will proceed on the assumption that you are taking part.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|見|み}{f|積|つ}もりは、{f|材料|ざいりょう}{f|費|ひ}が{f|上|あ}がらないこと<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}にしている</b>。
This estimate assumes that material costs will not rise.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|在宅|ざいたく}{f|勤務|きんむ}<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}とした</b>{f|制度|せいど}に{f|作|つく}り{f|直|なお}す{f|必要|ひつよう}がある。
The system needs to be rebuilt around the assumption of working from home.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|返|かえ}すこと<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|貸|か}したのに、{f|一年|いちねん}{f|経|た}っても{f|連絡|れんらく}がない。
I lent it on the understanding that it would come back, and a year on I have heard nothing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|全員|ぜんいん}{f|集|あつ}まる<b>という{f|前提|ぜんてい}で</b>{f|会場|かいじょう}を{f|押|お}さえてしまった。
We went ahead and booked the venue assuming everyone would be there.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値上|ねあ}げ<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|話|はなし}が{f|進|すす}んでいることに、{f|現場|げんば}は{f|納得|なっとく}していない。
The staff on the ground are unhappy that the discussion is proceeding on the assumption of a price rise.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|将来|しょうらい}{f|人口|じんこう}が{f|増|ふ}えること<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|作|つく}られた{f|道路|どうろ}が、いまはほとんど{f|使|つか}われずに{f|残|のこ}っている。
Roads built on the premise of future population growth now stand almost unused.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This belongs with the rest of the group in contracts, official notices and formal
meetings - a clause reading
<code>{f|支払|しはら}いを{f|前提|ぜんてい}として</code> is doing real legal work - but
it is more usable than its neighbours, because negotiating is something people do out
loud. <code>それを{f|前提|ぜんてい}に{f|考|かんが}えます</code> is ordinary business
speech, and <code>という{f|前提|ぜんてい}で</code> is common enough in a working
conversation to be worth producing.

One phrase has escaped into everyday life altogether:
<code>{f|結婚|けっこん}を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に{f|付|つ}き{f|合|あ}う</code>. It is a fixed
expression, it is said in ordinary conversation, and it is heard as sincere rather
than legalistic. Outside that phrase, the casual equivalents are
<code>～つもりで</code> and <code>～ということにして</code>:
<code>{f|来|く}るつもりで{f|準備|じゅんび}しておく</code>.

What the pattern is quietly doing is limiting liability. Announcing the premise means
that if it fails, the plan can be reopened without anyone being at fault. That is why
it turns up so often in the awkward parts of a negotiation, and why a Japanese
counterpart who says
<code>{f|来年度|らいねんど}の{f|予算|よさん}がつくことを{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</code>
has just told you, politely, that nothing is guaranteed.

## Contrast

### ～を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に vs ～を{f|踏|ふ}まえて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a {f|前提|ぜんてい} is **assumed and may not yet be true**; if it
fails, everything built on it falls. What you <code>{f|踏|ふ}まえる</code> is
**already established** and is merely taken into account.

</div>

<pre>
{f|補助金|ほじょきん}が{f|出|で}ること<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|立|た}てた。
We drew up the plan assuming the subsidy would come through.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|補助金|ほじょきん}の{f|審査|しんさ}{f|結果|けっか}<b>を{f|踏|ふ}まえて</b>{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|立|た}てた。
We drew up the plan in the light of the outcome of the subsidy review.
</pre>

The first plan has a hole in it if the money does not arrive; the second was made
after the answer was known. Notice the tense hidden in each: a premise usually points
forward, while what you {f|踏|ふ}まえる has already happened.

### ～を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に vs ～に{f|基|もと}づいて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|基|もと}づいて</code> stands on something that
**exists** - a law, a contract, a set of figures.
<code>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</code> stands on something **granted for the sake of
argument**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|契約|けいやく}{f|書|しょ}<b>に{f|基|もと}づいて</b>{f|費用|ひよう}を{f|負担|ふたん}します。
We will bear the costs in accordance with the contract.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|契約|けいやく}が{f|結|むす}ばれること<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|準備|じゅんび}を{f|進|すす}めます。
We will get on with preparations on the assumption that a contract will be signed.
</pre>

In the first, the contract is signed and sitting on the desk. In the second, it is
not, and the sentence says so. Learners often reach for
<code>に{f|基|もと}づいて</code> when the thing does not exist yet, which quietly
overstates the case; if it has not happened, you want
<code>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</code>.

### ～を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に vs ～という{f|条件|じょうけん}で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a {f|条件|じょうけん} is a **requirement imposed on the other
party**, and failing it has consequences for them. A {f|前提|ぜんてい} is a **shared
assumption** the plan is built around, with nobody obliged to deliver it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}までに{f|返|かえ}すという<b>{f|条件|じょうけん}で</b>{f|貸|か}した。
I lent it on condition that it be returned by next month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}までに{f|返|かえ}ってくること<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|次|つぎ}の{f|予定|よてい}を{f|組|く}んだ。
I arranged what comes next assuming it would be back by next month.
</pre>

The first sentence makes a demand of the borrower; the second only describes how the
lender planned. They are often both true at once, which is why they get confused - but
only <code>{f|条件|じょうけん}</code> gives you grounds for complaint.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|結婚|けっこん}<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}で</b>{f|付|つ}き{f|合|あ}う → ✅ {f|結婚|けっこん}<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|付|つ}き{f|合|あ}う
  A bare noun takes `に` or `として`. `で` belongs to
  <code>～という{f|前提|ぜんてい}で</code>, where the noun is the head of its own
  phrase.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|準備|じゅんび}する → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>ことを{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|準備|じゅんび}する
  A clause has to be nominalised with こと before `を`.

- ❌ {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|売|う}り{f|上|あ}げ<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|計算|けいさん}した。 → ✅ {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|売|う}り{f|上|あ}げ<b>に{f|基|もと}づいて</b>{f|計算|けいさん}した。
  Last year's figures are a fact, not an assumption. Reserve
  <code>{f|前提|ぜんてい}</code> for things that are not yet settled.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|会|あ}いましょう。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}{f|会|あ}えるという<b>{f|前提|ぜんてい}で</b>{f|準備|じゅんび}しておきます。
  A bare time word is not a premise. The assumption has to be a proposition - that
  something will be the case.

## Practice

**1.** {f|工事|こうじ}が{f|三月|さんがつ}までに{f|終|お}わること____、{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}しの{f|日|ひ}を{f|決|き}めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|終|お}わること<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>、{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}しの{f|日|ひ}を{f|決|き}めた。

The building work is not finished yet, so it is an assumption rather than a fact, and
the moving date depends on it entirely. Note the こと: the clause must be nominalised.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|実績|じっせき}（を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に・に{f|基|もと}づいて）{f|今年|ことし}の{f|目標|もくひょう}を{f|決|き}めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|実績|じっせき}<b>に{f|基|もと}づいて</b>{f|決|き}めた。

Last year's results are on the record. A premise is something granted in advance
because it is not yet known, and there is nothing unknown about last year.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The budget was drawn up on the assumption that the number of visitors would double."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|来場者|らいじょうしゃ}{f|数|すう}が{f|倍|ばい}になること<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|予算|よさん}が{f|組|く}まれた。

A forecast that may or may not come true is a textbook {f|前提|ぜんてい}.
<code>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}として</code> would suit a written budget document even
better.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|届|とど}くを{f|前提|ぜんてい}で{f|作業|さぎょう}を{f|進|すす}めています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|届|とど}くという<b>{f|前提|ぜんてい}で</b>{f|作業|さぎょう}を{f|進|すす}めています。

Two repairs in one. A clause cannot sit directly before `を`, and after a bare noun
the particle would have to be `に`. Wrapping the clause in
<code>という{f|前提|ぜんてい}で</code> fixes both at once, and it is the shape you
would actually hear in a meeting.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|全員|ぜんいん}{f|出席|しゅっせき}すること<b>を{f|前提|ぜんてい}に</b>{f|会場|かいじょう}を{f|選|えら}んだ。
(b) {f|全員|ぜんいん}{f|出席|しゅっせき}するという<b>{f|条件|じょうけん}で</b>{f|会場|かいじょう}を{f|借|か}りた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is about the planner's own reasoning: the venue was chosen for a full turnout, and
if half the people stay away the room is simply too big. Nobody has been asked to
promise anything.

(b) is about a requirement someone imposed. Full attendance is a term of the booking,
and failing it means the arrangement can be withdrawn. (a) risks embarrassment; (b)
risks losing the hall.

</details>
