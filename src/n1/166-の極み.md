# ～の極み

**Meaning** · the height of; the very peak of - a state that has gone as far as it is possible to go, said in formal, ceremonial Japanese

{f|極|きわ}み is a noun meaning "the furthest point", so
<code>～の{f|極|きわ}み</code> is literally "the summit of ～". In practice it is
not a free construction at all: it attaches to a small set of abstract nouns and
almost always appears in prepared, public language - a statement to the press, a
speech of thanks, a message of condolence, or a magazine paragraph about an
extraordinarily good meal.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (feeling or state) | noun + の{f|極|きわ}み | {f|遺憾|いかん} → {f|遺憾|いかん}の{f|極|きわ}み |
| Ending a sentence | ～の{f|極|きわ}みだ／です／である | {f|痛恨|つうこん}の{f|極|きわ}みです |
| Reaching that point | ～の{f|極|きわ}みに{f|達|たっ}する | {f|疲労|ひろう}の{f|極|きわ}みに{f|達|たっ}する |
| Passing a verdict | ～の{f|極|きわ}みと{f|言|い}える | {f|贅沢|ぜいたく}の{f|極|きわ}みと{f|言|い}える |
| Never after an adjective | ❌{f|嬉|うれ}しいの{f|極|きわ}み | ✅{f|喜|よろこ}びの{f|極|きわ}み |

<div class="note">

**Note - the collocations that are actually live.** The noun slot is not open.
What you will really meet is {f|遺憾|いかん}の{f|極|きわ}み (regret, in an official
statement), {f|痛恨|つうこん}の{f|極|きわ}み (bitter regret over a loss),
{f|感激|かんげき}の{f|極|きわ}み, {f|光栄|こうえい}の{f|極|きわ}み,
{f|贅沢|ぜいたく}の{f|極|きわ}み, {f|疲労|ひろう}の{f|極|きわ}み,
{f|混乱|こんらん}の{f|極|きわ}み, {f|悲|かな}しみの{f|極|きわ}み and
{f|無礼|ぶれい}の{f|極|きわ}み. Outside that neighbourhood the phrase stops sounding
like Japanese: ❌{f|仕事|しごと}の{f|極|きわ}み, ❌{f|天気|てんき}の{f|極|きわ}み.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise this one; do not deploy it.** Its natural habitats are a company
apology read from a sheet of paper, a politician's comment to reporters, an
acceptance speech, a funeral address, and the copy in a travel or food magazine.
If you say <code>{f|疲労|ひろう}の{f|極|きわ}みです</code> to a friend who asks
whether you are tired, you have quoted a press release at them. The ordinary
modern way to say the same thing is
<code>{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|残念|ざんねん}です</code>,
<code>この{f|上|うえ}なく{f|嬉|うれ}しいです</code>,
<code>{f|最高|さいこう}の{f|贅沢|ぜいたく}です</code> or simply
<code>とても{f|疲|つか}れました</code> - and that is what you should produce.

Two of these collocations carry more freight than the dictionary suggests.
<code>{f|遺憾|いかん}の{f|極|きわ}み</code> is the formula an organisation uses when
it wants to sound gravely displeased without conceding anything; Japanese readers
hear it as institutional, sometimes evasive, and it is often aimed at *someone
else's* conduct rather than at the speaker's own fault.
<code>{f|痛恨|つうこん}の{f|極|きわ}み</code>, by contrast, is genuinely personal
regret - a mistake you will not forgive yourself for - and it is the standard line
from a coach after a loss or an official who missed something.

Grammatically the phrase is a **noun**, which is why it takes だ, です, である or
でございます at the end, and why it can be followed by に{f|達|たっ}する or
にある. The very formal ending
<code>{f|悲|かな}しみの{f|極|きわ}みであります</code> belongs to spoken addresses;
であります is a podium form and would be strange anywhere else.

## Contrast

### ～の{f|極|きわ}み vs ～{f|極|きわ}まりない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>の{f|極|きわ}み</code> follows a **noun** and is itself a
noun; <code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> follows a な-adjective **stem** and ends the
sentence like an adjective. In meaning, <code>の{f|極|きわ}み</code> usually
describes a state the speaker is in, while
<code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> passes judgement on something outside.

</div>

<pre>
{f|司会者|しかいしゃ}の{f|言|い}い{f|方|かた}は{f|無礼|ぶれい}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>。
The way the host put it was insufferably rude.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|招|まね}いた{f|客|きゃく}を{f|立|た}たせたままにするとは<b>{f|無礼|ぶれい}の{f|極|きわ}み</b>だ。
To leave an invited guest standing is the height of discourtesy.
</pre>

{f|無礼|ぶれい} is one of the few words that takes both, so the pair is a clean
minimal one. The first sentence is a live reaction; the second sounds like a line
from an essay or a period drama.

### ～の{f|極|きわ}み vs ～の{f|至|いた}り

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are ceremonial, and they are not interchangeable.
<code>の{f|至|いた}り</code> reports a **feeling welling up in the speaker** and is
tied to a handful of humble set phrases; <code>の{f|極|きわ}み</code> can describe
a **situation** - fatigue, confusion, extravagance - with no speaker inside it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|皆様|みなさま}の{f|前|まえ}でお{f|話|はなし}できますことは、<b>{f|光栄|こうえい}の{f|至|いた}り</b>です。
To be able to speak before you all is an honour indeed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|混乱|こんらん}<b>の{f|極|きわ}み</b>で、{f|案内|あんない}の{f|声|こえ}も{f|届|とど}かなかった。
The venue was in utter chaos, and the announcements could not be heard.
</pre>

❌<code>{f|混乱|こんらん}の{f|至|いた}り</code> is not Japanese: nobody *feels*
confusion as a swelling emotion in the way they feel honour or embarrassment.
{f|光栄|こうえい} is the one noun that takes both, and
{f|光栄|こうえい}の{f|至|いた}り is the more old-fashioned and more deferential of
the two.

### ～の{f|極|きわ}み vs the modern equivalent

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - in anything you actually write or say, use
<code>この{f|上|うえ}なく</code>, <code>{f|非常|ひじょう}に</code>,
<code>{f|本当|ほんとう}に</code> or <code>{f|最高|さいこう}の</code>. Keep
<code>の{f|極|きわ}み</code> for reading.

</div>

<pre>
{f|温泉|おんせん}に{f|入|はい}ってから{f|冷|つめ}たいビールを{f|飲|の}む。<b>{f|贅沢|ぜいたく}の{f|極|きわ}み</b>だ。
A hot spring and then a cold beer: the last word in indulgence.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|温泉|おんせん}のあとのビールって、<b>{f|最高|さいこう}の{f|贅沢|ぜいたく}</b>だよね。
A beer after the hot spring is just the best kind of luxury, isn't it.
</pre>

The content is identical; the first is written for a page and the second is said
to a person. Note that the casual version also drops the noun-heavy shape - real
speech prefers {f|最高|さいこう}, すごく and めちゃくちゃ to any of this.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|嬉|うれ}しい<b>の{f|極|きわ}み</b> → ✅ <b>{f|喜|よろこ}びの{f|極|きわ}み</b>／{f|嬉|うれ}しい{f|限|かぎ}りだ
  The slot takes a noun. An い-adjective has to be nominalised first, and in
  everyday Japanese <code>～{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code> is the better fit anyway.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}の{f|試験|しけん}は<b>{f|疲労|ひろう}の{f|極|きわ}み</b>でした。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}の{f|試験|しけん}は{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|疲|つか}れました。
  Grammatical, but it puts podium language into a chat about a test. This is the
  characteristic learner error with the pattern.

- ❌ この<b>{f|極|きわ}み</b>の{f|料理|りょうり} → ✅ <b>{f|贅沢|ぜいたく}の{f|極|きわ}み</b>のような{f|料理|りょうり}
  {f|極|きわ}み is not an adjective and cannot modify a noun on its own.

- ❌ {f|遺憾|いかん}の{f|極|きわ}み<b>する</b> → ✅ {f|遺憾|いかん}の{f|極|きわ}み<b>です</b>
  It is a noun phrase, so it takes the copula. The only verb that regularly
  follows it is {f|達|たっ}する, in ～の{f|極|きわ}みに{f|達|たっ}する.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to meet this sentence? {f|多|おお}くの{f|皆様|みなさま}にご{f|迷惑|めいわく}をおかけしたことは、{f|遺憾|いかん}の{f|極|きわ}みであります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In a prepared public statement - a press conference, an apology posted on a
company website, or a speech read from notes. The giveaways are
{f|遺憾|いかん}の{f|極|きわ}み itself, the humble ご{f|迷惑|めいわく}をおかけした,
and the podium copula であります. Nobody speaks like this to a colleague.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|久|ひさ}しぶりに{f|友人|ゆうじん}に{f|会|あ}えて、{f|感激|かんげき}の{f|極|きわ}みでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|久|ひさ}しぶりに{f|友人|ゆうじん}に{f|会|あ}えて、<b>{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|嬉|うれ}しかったです</b>。（or {f|感動|かんどう}しました）

Meeting a friend is an everyday event, and the ceremonial noun phrase is far too
heavy for it. <code>{f|感激|かんげき}の{f|極|きわ}み</code> would fit a reunion
described in a speech at a formal gathering, not a report to a friend.

</details>

**3.** Choose: {f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}の{f|説明|せつめい}は{f|無責任|むせきにん}（の{f|極|きわ}み・{f|極|きわ}まりない）だった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are possible, and they differ. <b>{f|無責任|むせきにん}の{f|極|きわ}み</b>だった
treats the explanation as reaching the summit of irresponsibility - the noun
phrase makes it sound like a written verdict. <b>{f|無責任|むせきにん}{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>
would need the だった removed, because
<code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> is already adjectival:
{f|無責任|むせきにん}{f|極|きわ}まりなかった.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|山頂|さんちょう}からの{f|景色|けしき}は{f|美|うつく}しいの{f|極|きわ}みでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|山頂|さんちょう}からの{f|景色|けしき}は<b>{f|美|うつく}しさの{f|極|きわ}み</b>でした。（more natural: この{f|上|うえ}なく{f|美|うつく}しかったです）

<code>の{f|極|きわ}み</code> needs a noun in front of it, so the adjective has to
become {f|美|うつく}しさ. Even then the sentence reads like guidebook copy; in
speech, この{f|上|うえ}なく{f|美|うつく}しかった or ものすごくきれいでした is what a
person would say.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|今回|こんかい}の{f|判断|はんだん}ミスは{f|痛恨|つうこん}の{f|極|きわ}みです。
(b) {f|今回|こんかい}の{f|判断|はんだん}ミスは{f|遺憾|いかん}の{f|極|きわ}みです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the speaker owning the error. {f|痛恨|つうこん} is personal, burning regret,
so the sentence reads as "this was my mistake and it will stay with me" - the line
a manager or a coach gives after a loss.

(b) distances the speaker from it. {f|遺憾|いかん} is institutional displeasure,
and Japanese listeners routinely hear it as the language of an organisation
registering disapproval rather than accepting blame. Used about one's own error it
can sound evasive, which is exactly why it is so common in official statements.

</details>
