# ～に至るまで

**Meaning** · all the way to; right down to; right up until - marks the far end of a range and insists that the range really does reach that far

<code>{f|至|いた}る</code> is a formal verb meaning "to reach, to arrive at", so
<code>に{f|至|いた}るまで</code> is literally "as far as the point where it reaches X".
Plain <code>まで</code> would already give the endpoint; what this adds is the
speaker's astonishment or emphasis - the list goes on further than you would expect,
or the state has held for longer than you would think. It very often answers a から
earlier in the sentence.

## Formation

A noun goes in front. <code>{f|至|いた}る</code> is a う-verb ({f|五段|ごだん}), so the form here
is the plain <code>{f|至|いた}る</code> plus <code>まで</code>.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (thing, category) | Noun + に{f|至|いた}るまで | {f|靴|くつ}の{f|色|いろ} → {f|靴|くつ}の{f|色|いろ}に{f|至|いた}るまで |
| Paired with から | ～から～に{f|至|いた}るまで | {f|子|こ}ども → {f|子|こ}どもからお{f|年寄|としよ}りに{f|至|いた}るまで |
| Noun (point in time) | Time + に{f|至|いた}るまで | {f|今日|こんにち} → {f|今日|こんにち}に{f|至|いた}るまで |
| Verb clause (written) | dictionary + に{f|至|いた}るまで | {f|完成|かんせい}する → {f|完成|かんせい}するに{f|至|いた}るまで |
| Modifying a noun | ～に{f|至|いた}るまでの + Noun | {f|現在|げんざい}に{f|至|いた}るまでの{f|歩|あゆ}み |
| Related pattern (reaching) | ～に{f|至|いた}る／～に{f|至|いた}って | {f|解決|かいけつ}に{f|至|いた}る |

<div class="note">

**Note** - in <code>{f|今日|こんにち}に{f|至|いた}るまで</code> the word is read
**こんにち**, "the present day", not きょう. きょう would mean this particular
Tuesday, which is not what a sentence spanning a company's history is about.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Formal, and much more common in **writing** than in speech: reports, inquiries,
museum panels, company profiles, feature journalism. In conversation the same
emphasis comes out as plain <code>まで</code> with the intonation doing the work, or
as <code>～まで{f|全部|ぜんぶ}</code>.

Two flavours share the form. The **exhaustive** one runs across a set of things and
says the set is complete to its farthest, smallest or most unexpected member; it
loves the company of すべて, {f|全|すべ}て, {f|細|こま}かく, どれも. The **temporal**
one runs from a starting point to now and says the state has held that whole time;
<code>{f|今日|こんにち}に{f|至|いた}るまで</code> and
<code>{f|現在|げんざい}に{f|至|いた}るまで</code> are close to fixed expressions.

There is a rhetorical edge to the exhaustive use. Picking a trivial endpoint - the
angle of the pens, the colour of the socks - is a way of showing that the control was
excessive or the attention obsessive. Used of a person's supervision it can be quietly
critical, so choose the endpoint with care.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|至|いた}るまで vs ～まで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>まで</code> states the endpoint.
<code>に{f|至|いた}るまで</code> states the endpoint **and marks it as remarkable**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}<b>まで</b>{f|歩|ある}いて{f|十分|じゅっぷん}かかります。
It is a ten-minute walk to the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|規則|きそく}は{f|髪|かみ}の{f|長|なが}さ<b>に{f|至|いた}るまで</b>{f|細|こま}かく{f|決|き}められている。
The rules are laid down in detail, down to the length of your hair.
</pre>

The first sentence has no rhetoric in it and
<code>に{f|至|いた}るまで</code> would be absurd there - an ordinary destination of an
ordinary walk. The test: if you could insert "even" or "right down to" in the English
without changing the meaning, the longer form is available.

### ～に{f|至|いた}るまで vs ～から～にかけて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>にかけて</code> spreads something **loosely across** a range
of time or space. <code>に{f|至|いた}るまで</code> runs to a **precise far edge** and
claims everything up to it is included.

</div>

<pre>
{f|夕方|ゆうがた}から{f|夜|よる}<b>にかけて</b>、{f|強|つよ}い{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るでしょう。
Heavy rain is expected from evening into the night.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昭和|しょうわ}の{f|初|はじ}めから{f|戦後|せんご}<b>に{f|至|いた}るまで</b>、この{f|工場|こうじょう}は{f|動|うご}き{f|続|つづ}けた。
From the early Showa years right through to the postwar period, this factory kept running.
</pre>

<code>にかけて</code> is vague on purpose - the rain will not start at a stated
minute. <code>に{f|至|いた}るまで</code> is the opposite: it fixes the far end and
asserts continuity all the way there.

### ～に{f|至|いた}るまで vs ～に{f|至|いた}って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|至|いた}るまで</code> is about a **range**.
<code>に{f|至|いた}って</code> is about a **threshold finally crossed**, usually a bad
one, and usually with the sense "only when it got this far".

</div>

<pre>
{f|退院|たいいん}から{f|今日|こんにち}<b>に{f|至|いた}るまで</b>、{f|薬|くすり}は{f|飲|の}み{f|続|つづ}けている。
From leaving hospital up to today, I have kept taking the medicine.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|死者|ししゃ}が{f|出|で}る<b>に{f|至|いた}って</b>、{f|会社|かいしゃ}はようやく{f|操業|そうぎょう}を{f|止|と}めた。
Only when there were deaths did the company finally halt operations.
</pre>

Same verb, opposite jobs. If a まで is present you are looking at the range use; if
the sentence has ようやく, はじめて or a note of blame, it is the threshold use.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|会社|かいしゃ}<b>に{f|至|いた}るまで</b>{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}で{f|行|い}く。 → ✅ {f|会社|かいしゃ}<b>まで</b>{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}で{f|行|い}く。
  An ordinary destination takes plain まで. The pattern needs a range whose far end
  is worth remarking on.

- ❌ {f|五時|ごじ}<b>に{f|至|いた}るまで</b>{f|待|ま}った。 → ✅ {f|五時|ごじ}<b>まで</b>{f|待|ま}った。
  Same problem with clock time. A stretch that runs to the present -
  <code>{f|今日|こんにち}に{f|至|いた}るまで</code> - is fine, because the point is how
  long it has lasted.

- ❌ {f|創業|そうぎょう}から{f|今日|きょう}<b>に{f|至|いた}るまで</b> → ✅ {f|創業|そうぎょう}から{f|今日|こんにち}<b>に{f|至|いた}るまで</b>
  Not a grammar error but a reading error, and a common one. In this phrase {f|今日|こんにち} is
  read こんにち, "the present day".

- ❌ {f|細部|さいぶ}<b>を{f|至|いた}るまで</b>{f|作|つく}り{f|込|こ}まれている。 → ✅ {f|細部|さいぶ}<b>に{f|至|いた}るまで</b>{f|作|つく}り{f|込|こ}まれている。
  <code>{f|至|いた}る</code> is a verb of arrival, so the point reached is marked with
  に, never を.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|美術館|びじゅつかん}では、{f|絵|え}の{f|並|なら}べ{f|方|かた}から{f|壁|かべ}の{f|色|いろ}____、すべて{f|作家|さっか}{f|自身|じしん}が{f|決|き}めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|壁|かべ}の{f|色|いろ}<b>に{f|至|いた}るまで</b>

から earlier in the sentence sets up the near end of the range, and the wall colour is
the unexpectedly small far end - the classic exhaustive use. すべて confirms it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|月曜日|げつようび}（まで・に{f|至|いた}るまで）に{f|返事|へんじ}をください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|月曜日|げつようび}<b>まで</b>に{f|返事|へんじ}をください。

A deadline is an ordinary endpoint with nothing remarkable about it, so plain までに
is correct. <code>に{f|至|いた}るまで</code> would suggest Monday was an astonishing
distance away.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He remembers everything, from the names of the guests down to what they ate."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}は{f|客|きゃく}の{f|名前|なまえ}から{f|何|なに}を{f|食|た}べたか<b>に{f|至|いた}るまで</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えている。

"From … down to" is the から … <code>に{f|至|いた}るまで</code> frame, and a detail as
small as what someone ate is exactly the kind of far edge the pattern is used to
point at.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|朝|あさ}から{f|夜|よる}に{f|至|いた}るまで{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|朝|あさ}から{f|夜|よる}<b>に{f|至|いた}るまで</b>{f|続|つづ}いた。

The frame promises a range that is filled all the way to its far end, so the main
clause has to describe something that continued or was covered. A one-off
cancellation cannot fill a range; with {f|中止|ちゅうし} you would simply say
<code>{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|書類|しょるい}の{f|字|じ}の{f|大|おお}きさ<b>まで</b>{f|注意|ちゅうい}する。
(b) {f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|書類|しょるい}の{f|字|じ}の{f|大|おお}きさ<b>に{f|至|いた}るまで</b>{f|注意|ちゅうい}する。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) already says the boss goes as far as font size, and in speech that is enough; the
surprise is carried by まで and by how you say it.

(b) is heavier and more written. It presents the font size as the outermost item in a
long list of things he checks, and the extra weight tips the sentence towards
criticism - this is a man who cannot leave anything alone.

</details>
