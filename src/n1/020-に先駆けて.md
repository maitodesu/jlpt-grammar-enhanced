# ～に先駆けて

**Meaning** · ahead of everyone else; in advance of - does something before the rivals do, or before the main event opens to everybody

<code>{f|先駆|さきが}ける</code> means "to go on ahead, to lead the way". The pattern has
two faces that come from the same idea. With **people or organisations** in front of
it, it says you got there first: before the other companies, before the rest of the
country, before the world. With an **event** in front of it, it says something was
done in advance of that event, usually for a limited group - a press preview, a
pre-launch trial.

## Formation

A noun goes in front with に. There is no clause form.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Rivals, others | Noun (people, bodies) + に{f|先駆|さきが}けて | {f|他社|たしゃ} → {f|他社|たしゃ}に{f|先駆|さきが}けて |
| The world, the country | Noun (group) + に{f|先駆|さきが}けて | {f|世界|せかい} → {f|世界|せかい}に{f|先駆|さきが}けて |
| Coming event | Noun (event) + に{f|先駆|さきが}けて | {f|発売|はつばい} → {f|発売|はつばい}に{f|先駆|さきが}けて |
| Modifying a noun | ～に{f|先駆|さきが}けた + Noun | {f|時代|じだい}に{f|先駆|さきが}けた{f|試|こころ}み |
| As the main verb | ～に{f|先駆|さきが}ける | {f|他社|たしゃ}に{f|先駆|さきが}ける |
| Noun form | ～の{f|先駆|さきが}け | この{f|分野|ぶんや}の{f|先駆|さきが}け |
| Stiffer written twin | ～に{f|先|さき}んじて | {f|各国|かっこく}に{f|先|さき}んじて |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the reading here is **さきがけて**, the native verb. The same two kanji are
read せんく in <code>{f|先駆者|せんくしゃ}</code> "pioneer" and
<code>{f|先駆的|せんくてき}</code>. Reading it ❌せんくけて is a common slip.

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is **press-release and news Japanese**. Product announcements, corporate
histories, city council statements, museum publicity, and any writing that wants to
claim a first. It carries a note of **prestige**: saying
<code>{f|世界|せかい}に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</code> about your own product is a boast,
and it is meant to be.

In the "before an event" use the prestige softens into privilege - the people at the
preview are getting in early, ahead of the general public. That flavour is worth
keeping in mind, because it is what separates this pattern from a neutral "before".
Ordinary preparation before a meeting is not "getting in ahead of" anybody, and takes
<code>に{f|先立|さきだ}って</code> or plain <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code>.

Spoken use is limited to formal settings - a presentation, a company announcement, a
guided tour. Among friends the idea comes out as
<code>{f|人|ひと}より{f|先|さき}に</code> or <code>いちばん{f|早|はや}く</code>.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|先駆|さきが}けて vs ～に{f|先立|さきだ}って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|先立|さきだ}って</code> is a neutral, formal **before**,
usually about preparation. <code>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</code> adds that you are
**ahead of others** and that this is worth noting.

</div>

<pre>
{f|工事|こうじ}<b>に{f|先立|さきだ}って</b>、{f|住民|じゅうみん}への{f|説明会|せつめいかい}が{f|開|ひら}かれた。
Prior to the works, a briefing was held for local residents.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大手|おおて}{f|各社|かくしゃ}<b>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</b>、この{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|週|しゅう}{f|休|きゅう}{f|三日|みっか}{f|制|せい}を{f|導入|どうにゅう}した。
Ahead of the big firms, this company brought in a four-day week.
</pre>

The residents' briefing beats nobody; it is simply a step that came first.
<code>に{f|先立|さきだ}って</code> is by far the more common of the two in ordinary
formal writing, and it is the safer choice whenever no competition is involved.

### ～に{f|先駆|さきが}けて vs ～より{f|前|まえ}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>より{f|前|まえ}に</code> only orders two things in time.
<code>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</code> claims a lead.

</div>

<pre>
{f|兄|あに}より{f|前|まえ}に{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}た。
I left the house before my brother did.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|国|くに}の{f|制度|せいど}<b>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</b>、この{f|市|し}は{f|独自|どくじ}の{f|支援|しえん}を{f|始|はじ}めた。
Ahead of any national scheme, the city started support of its own.
</pre>

Leaving the house first is not an achievement, and
<code>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</code> there would sound absurdly grand. The pattern needs
a lead that means something - over an industry, a country, an era.

### ～に{f|先駆|さきが}けて vs ～に{f|先|さき}んじて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - near-identical in meaning.
<code>に{f|先|さき}んじて</code> is the stiffer, more literary of the two and is at
home in editorial and historical writing.

</div>

<pre>
{f|他国|たこく}<b>に{f|先|さき}んじて</b>、その{f|国|くに}は{f|条約|じょうやく}に{f|署名|しょめい}した。
Ahead of the other nations, the country signed the treaty.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|業界|ぎょうかい}<b>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</b>、この{f|工場|こうじょう}は{f|生産|せいさん}の{f|自動|じどう}{f|化|か}を{f|進|すす}めた。
Ahead of the industry, this plant pushed on with automating production.
</pre>

Either would pass in either sentence. As a tendency,
<code>{f|先|さき}んじて</code> reads as older and more formal, and it is the one you
are more likely to meet in a history book than in a product announcement.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|会議|かいぎ}<b>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</b>{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|配|くば}った。 → ✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}<b>に{f|先立|さきだ}って</b>{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|配|くば}った。
  Handing out papers is preparation, not a race won. Use
  <code>に{f|先立|さきだ}って</code> when nobody is being beaten to anything.

- ❌ {f|弟|おとうと}<b>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</b>{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べた。 → ✅ {f|弟|おとうと}より{f|先|さき}に{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べた。
  Both the scale and the register are wrong. Everyday precedence between two people
  takes <code>より{f|先|さき}に</code>.

- ❌ {f|新製品|しんせいひん}を{f|発売|はつばい}する<b>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</b> → ✅ {f|新製品|しんせいひん}の{f|発売|はつばい}<b>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</b>
  The slot takes a noun. Turn the verb into an event noun, or use
  <code>{f|発売|はつばい}する{f|前|まえ}に</code>.

- ❌ {f|他社|たしゃ}に<b>{f|先駆|せんく}けて</b> → ✅ {f|他社|たしゃ}に<b>{f|先駆|さきが}けて</b>
  A reading error, not a grammar one: せんく belongs to
  <code>{f|先駆者|せんくしゃ}</code>, while the verb is さきがける.

## Practice

**1.** その{f|病院|びょういん}は{f|県内|けんない}____、{f|夜間|やかん}の{f|小児|しょうに}{f|診療|しんりょう}を{f|始|はじ}めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|県内|けんない}の{f|他|ほか}の{f|病院|びょういん}<b>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</b>

The hospital is being credited with doing something before anyone else in the
prefecture, which is exactly the claim this pattern makes. Note that the noun in front
names the group being led.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|試合|しあい}（に{f|先駆|さきが}けて・に{f|先立|さきだ}って）、{f|選手|せんしゅ}{f|全員|ぜんいん}で{f|会場|かいじょう}の{f|清掃|せいそう}を{f|行|おこな}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|試合|しあい}<b>に{f|先立|さきだ}って</b>

Cleaning the venue is a preparatory step, and no one is being beaten to it.
<code>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</code> would imply the players cleaned up before some
rival team could.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Ahead of the rest of the industry, the company stopped using plastic packaging."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

その{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|業界|ぎょうかい}<b>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</b>、プラスチックの{f|包装|ほうそう}をやめた。

"Ahead of the rest of the industry" is the group being led, so it takes に and the
pattern follows. The sentence reads as the kind of claim a company would put in its
own announcement.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|新|あたら}しいアプリを{f|公開|こうかい}するに{f|先駆|さきが}けて、{f|社員|しゃいん}が{f|試|ため}している。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|新|あたら}しいアプリの{f|公開|こうかい}<b>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</b>、{f|社員|しゃいん}が{f|試|ため}している。

The pattern takes a noun, so the verb clause has to become the event noun
<code>{f|公開|こうかい}</code>. The meaning is fine: staff getting early access before
the public release is the privileged-preview use.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|開館|かいかん}<b>に{f|先立|さきだ}って</b>、{f|報道|ほうどう}{f|関係者|かんけいしゃ}が{f|館内|かんない}を{f|見学|けんがく}した。
(b) {f|開館|かいかん}<b>に{f|先駆|さきが}けて</b>、{f|報道|ほうどう}{f|関係者|かんけいしゃ}が{f|館内|かんない}を{f|見学|けんがく}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply places the press visit before the opening. It is the neutral wording a
schedule or a minute would use.

(b) adds that the press got in ahead of everybody else, and treats that early access
as something worth mentioning. It is the wording a publicity department would choose.

</details>
