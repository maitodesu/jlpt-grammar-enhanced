# ～とみられる

**Meaning** · is believed to be ～; is thought to have ～; is expected to ～ - the estimate that stands until somebody confirms it

Written <code>と{f|見|み}られる</code> or, in news copy, usually in kana. The
literal shape is "is seen as", and the seeing is done by nobody in particular -
investigators, analysts, the people whose business it is to know. That is what the
pattern is for: it lets a writer put a number, a cause or a forecast on the page
**without asserting it**, because the facts are not in yet.

## Formation

The clause in front is quoted, so it takes the plain form and `と`. Nouns and
な-adjectives go in bare or with `だ`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + とみられる | {f|増|ふ}える → {f|増|ふ}えるとみられる |
| Verb (past) | plain past + とみられる | {f|逃|に}げた → {f|逃|に}げたとみられる |
| い-adjective | plain form + とみられる | {f|難|むずか}しい → {f|難|むずか}しいとみられる |
| な-adjective | stem or ～だ + とみられる | {f|深刻|しんこく} → {f|深刻|しんこく}（だ）とみられる |
| Noun | bare or ～だ + とみられる | {f|漏電|ろうでん} → {f|漏電|ろうでん}（だ）とみられる |
| The prevailing estimate | ～とみられている | {f|回復|かいふく}する → {f|回復|かいふく}するとみられている |
| Modifying a noun | ～とみられる + Noun | {f|犯人|はんにん} → {f|犯人|はんにん}とみられる{f|男|おとこ} |
| Polite | ～とみられます | {f|遅|おく}れる → {f|遅|おく}れるとみられます |

<div class="note">

**Note** - do not confuse this with <code>～が{f|見|み}られる</code>, which has no
`と` and means "is observed, can be seen":
<code>{f|回復|かいふく}の{f|兆|きざ}しが{f|見|み}られる</code>, "signs of a recovery
are visible". The `と` is the whole difference. With it you have an estimate; without
it you have an observation.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|犯人|はんにん}はまだ{f|近|ちか}くにいる<b>とみられる</b>。
The culprit is believed to be still in the area.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}には{f|結論|けつろん}が{f|出|で}る<b>とみられている</b>。
A conclusion is expected next week.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|出火|しゅっか}の{f|原因|げんいん}は{f|漏電|ろうでん}<b>とみられています</b>。
The fire is believed to have been started by an electrical fault.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}にはおよそ3{f|万人|まんにん}が{f|集|あつ}まった<b>とみられる</b>。
Around thirty thousand people are thought to have gathered at the venue.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|制度|せいど}は{f|来年度|らいねんど}から{f|実施|じっし}される<b>とみられる</b>。
The new system is expected to come into force from next year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|長雨|ながあめ}の{f|影響|えいきょう}で、ことしの{f|収穫|しゅうかく}は{f|例年|れいねん}を{f|下回|したまわ}る<b>とみられています</b>。
Because of the long spell of rain, this year's harvest is expected to fall short of the usual level.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|交渉|こうしょう}は{f|難航|なんこう}しており、{f|年内|ねんない}の{f|決着|けっちゃく}は{f|難|むずか}しい<b>とみられる</b>。
The talks are going badly, and a settlement within the year is thought unlikely.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|海外|かいがい}{f|市場|しじょう}の{f|需要|じゅよう}が{f|戻|もど}ったことが、{f|今期|こんき}の{f|増益|ぞうえき}につながった<b>とみられている</b>。
The return of demand in overseas markets is thought to be behind this term's rise in profits.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is **newspaper Japanese**, and once you have noticed it you will hear it in
every bulletin. Its home is the sentence that reports a cause, a casualty figure,
an identification or a timetable before any of them are official. It is equally at
home in analysts' notes, company outlooks and government projections.

Two directions, one form:

| Looking at | Example shape | English |
|---|---|---|
| Something already over | ～した／～だったとみられる | is thought to have ～ |
| Something still to come | ～する／～になるとみられる | is expected to ～ |

The forward-looking use is the one learners tend to miss. <code>{f|発表|はっぴょう}は
{f|来月|らいげつ}になるとみられる</code> is not "it seems it will be next month" in
the sense of a personal hunch; it is the considered expectation of the people who
follow such things.

`ている` again marks the estimate as one that stands and is shared -
<code>とみられている</code> is "the current view is". Without `ている`, the sentence
reads as the writer's own assessment given the material, and in a news report the
two are used almost interchangeably.

The pattern is **impersonal to the point that you cannot get into it**:
❌<code>{f|私|わたし}は…とみられる</code> is not a modest way to say "I think". If
you need to say that a report's conclusion is your own,
<code>と{f|考|かんが}えています</code> or
<code>と{f|判断|はんだん}しました</code> does the job. Conversationally, none of this
applies - a friend's late arrival is `らしい` or `みたい`, never
<code>とみられる</code>.

## Contrast

### とみられる vs とされる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>とみられる</code> is a **provisional estimate** that later
facts may overturn. <code>とされる</code> is a **settled convention or ruling** that
would take an authority to change.

</div>

<pre>
{f|被害|ひがい}は{f|数億円|すうおくえん}に{f|上|のぼ}る<b>とみられている</b>。
The damage is believed to run to several hundred million yen.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|品種|ひんしゅ}は{f|寒|さむ}さに{f|強|つよ}い<b>とされている</b>。
This variety is held to be resistant to cold.
</pre>

The damage figure is somebody's working estimate, and tomorrow's paper may carry a
different one. The variety's cold-hardiness is what growers and catalogues have
agreed on for years. Ask whether the statement is waiting to be confirmed or has
already been accepted.

### とみられる vs と{f|考|かんが}えられる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</code> is **reasoned** and belongs
to argument. <code>とみられる</code> is **estimated** and belongs to reporting.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|数値|すうち}の{f|変化|へんか}は{f|測定|そくてい}{f|方法|ほうほう}の{f|違|ちが}いによるもの<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</b>。
The change in these figures is likely to be down to a difference in how they were measured.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|感染|かんせん}の{f|拡大|かくだい}は{f|来月|らいげつ}まで{f|続|つづ}く<b>とみられる</b>。
The spread of infection is expected to continue into next month.
</pre>

The first sentence is doing intellectual work in front of you: it offers an
explanation and stands behind the inference. The second is passing on a projection
without showing any workings at all. That is why an academic paper is full of
<code>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</code> and a news bulletin is full of
<code>とみられる</code>.

### とみられる vs ようだ・らしい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical job, different height. `ようだ` and `らしい` are one
person's read of the evidence; <code>とみられる</code> is the read of an unnamed
professional body.

</div>

<pre>
{f|隣|となり}の{f|家|いえ}、もう{f|誰|だれ}も{f|住|す}んでいない<b>みたい</b>だね。
Looks like nobody lives next door any more.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|空|あ}き{f|家|や}は{f|今後|こんご}さらに{f|増|ふ}える<b>とみられる</b>。
The number of empty houses is expected to rise further.
</pre>

Say the second one to a neighbour over the fence and you sound as though you were
reading the evening news aloud. Put the first into a government white paper and the
paper has no authority. The content is close; the voice is not.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|来年|らいねん}{f|景気|けいき}が{f|良|よ}くなる<b>とみられます</b>。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|来年|らいねん}{f|景気|けいき}が{f|良|よ}くなる<b>とみています</b>。
  The passive deletes the observer, so you cannot be the observer. The active
  <code>とみている</code> is exactly how an analyst states their own forecast.

- ❌ {f|去年|きょねん}より{f|改善|かいぜん}<b>とみられる</b>。 → ✅ {f|去年|きょねん}より{f|改善|かいぜん}が<b>{f|見|み}られる</b>。
  "An improvement can be seen" is the <code>が{f|見|み}られる</code> construction, with no `と`
  at all. Adding `と` turns an observation into an estimate about something else.

- ❌ ゆうべの{f|地震|じしん}で{f|建物|たてもの}が{f|倒|たお}れる<b>とみられている</b>。 → ✅ ゆうべの{f|地震|じしん}で{f|建物|たてもの}が{f|倒|たお}れた<b>とみられている</b>。
  Tense sits **inside** the quotation. For something that has already happened the
  quoted clause must be past, however the English is phrased.

- ❌ {f|木村|きむら}さん、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|来|こ}ない<b>とみられるよ</b>。 → ✅ {f|木村|きむら}さん、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|来|こ}ない<b>みたいだよ</b>。
  Nothing is wrong with the grammar; it is the register that fails. Reporting on a
  colleague to a colleague is not a press briefing.

## Practice

**1.** {f|事故|じこ}を{f|起|お}こした{f|車|くるま}は{f|盗|ぬす}まれたもの____。（news report）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|盗|ぬす}まれたもの<b>とみられています</b>。

Police have an early view and no confirmation yet, which is what
<code>とみられる</code> exists for. `もの` gives a noun for the `と` to attach to, so
`だ` is optional here.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|地域|ちいき}のかつての{f|主産業|しゅさんぎょう}は{f|漁業|ぎょぎょう}だった（とされている・とみられている）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|漁業|ぎょぎょう}だった<b>とされている</b>。

Local history that has been settled for generations is a convention, not a working
estimate. <code>とみられている</code> would suggest the question was live and someone
was still investigating it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, in the register of a news bulletin: "The number of visitors is expected to top last year's figure."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|来場者数|らいじょうしゃすう}は{f|昨年|さくねん}を{f|上回|うわまわ}る<b>とみられます</b>。

"Is expected to" going forward in time is the second of the pattern's two
directions. Keep the verb in the plain non-past inside the quotation; the
expectation lives in <code>とみられる</code>, not in the verb.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|各社|かくしゃ}の{f|決算|けっさん}を{f|見|み}る{f|限|かぎ}り、{f|私|わたし}は{f|回復|かいふく}は{f|来年|らいねん}まで{f|待|ま}たなければならないとみられます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|各社|かくしゃ}の{f|決算|けっさん}を{f|見|み}る{f|限|かぎ}り、{f|回復|かいふく}は{f|来年|らいねん}まで{f|待|ま}たなければならない<b>とみられます</b>。
"On the evidence of the companies' results, a recovery looks likely to have to wait until next year."

The only repair needed is deleting {f|私|わたし}は. The pattern's grammar has already
removed the person doing the judging; naming yourself as well leaves the sentence
with two incompatible viewpoints. If the assessment must be marked as your own,
switch to <code>とみています</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|停電|ていでん}の{f|原因|げんいん}は{f|落雷|らくらい}<b>とみられている</b>。
(b) {f|停電|ていでん}の{f|原因|げんいん}は{f|落雷|らくらい}<b>だった</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a report filed while the investigation is still open. It commits nobody: if
the cause turns out to be a failed transformer, the sentence was not wrong, it was
provisional. This is why bulletins are written this way.

(b) states the cause as established fact. A reporter writes it only after the power
company has said so, and a listener hearing it treats the matter as closed.

</details>
