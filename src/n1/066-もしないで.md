# ～もしないで

**Meaning** · without even doing; without so much as - the bare minimum was skipped, and the speaker is annoyed about it

Take the ます-stem, wedge in a も, and finish with <code>しないで</code>:
{f|見|み}ないで becomes {f|見|み}もしないで. That も is the "even" of
{f|水|みず}も{f|飲|の}まない. So the pattern does not merely report an omission - it
says the person did not do the one obvious minimum thing, and then went ahead
anyway. It is a complaint almost every time.

## Formation

The base is the **ます-stem**, and も sits between the stem and しないで.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + もしないで | {f|謝|あやま}る → {f|謝|あやま}りもしないで |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + もしないで | {f|調|しら}べる → {f|調|しら}べもしないで |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | **き** + もしないで | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|き}もしないで |
| する-verb | Noun + もしないで | {f|掃除|そうじ}する → {f|掃除|そうじ}もしないで |
| Formal / written | ます-stem + もせずに | {f|読|よ}む → {f|読|よ}みもせずに |
| Ending the sentence | ます-stem + もしない | {f|見|み}る → {f|見|み}もしない |
| Contrastive sibling | ます-stem + はしない | {f|見|み}る → {f|見|み}はしない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - う-verb / る-verb are the two regular classes（{f|五段|ごだん} /
{f|一段|いちだん}, Group 1 / Group 2）. {f|来|く}る is irregular and its stem here is
**き**, the same stem as {f|来|き}ます - not こ, which belongs to
{f|来|こ}ない and {f|来|こ}られる.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|謝|あやま}り<b>もしないで</b>{f|帰|かえ}って{f|行|い}った。
He left without so much as an apology.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|中身|なかみ}を{f|見|み}<b>もしないで</b>{f|捨|す}てないでよ。
Don't throw it out without even looking inside.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自分|じぶん}では{f|何|なに}もやり<b>もしないで</b>、{f|文句|もんく}ばかり{f|言|い}っている。
He does nothing himself and yet complains non-stop.
</pre>

<pre>
うちの{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|部屋|へや}の{f|掃除|そうじ}<b>もしないで</b>、ゲームばかりしている。
My son plays games all day and doesn't even tidy his room.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}を{f|読|よ}み<b>もせずに</b>{f|組|く}み{f|立|た}てるから、こういうことになる。
This is what happens when you assemble it without even reading the instructions.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事情|じじょう}を{f|確|たし}かめ<b>もしないで</b>{f|人|ひと}を{f|疑|うたが}うのはよくない。
Suspecting someone without even checking the facts is not on.
</pre>

<pre>

Without even coming over to say hello, he launched straight into terms.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|口|くち}をつけ<b>もしないで</b>「まずい」と{f|決|き}めつけるのは、さすがに{f|失礼|しつれい}だと{f|思|おも}う。
Writing the food off as bad without so much as tasting it is, I have to say, rude.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The form is register-neutral - it fits casual speech and ordinary polite speech
alike, and <code>もせずに</code> covers formal writing. What is heavily marked is the
**attitude**. This construction accuses. It sets up a minimum standard, reports that
it was not met, and then names the thing the person did instead, which is usually
worse: complaining, deciding, throwing something away, suspecting somebody.

Because of that, it is rarely used about oneself except in self-reproach -
{f|調|しら}べもしないで{f|買|か}ってしまった, "I bought it without even checking,
idiot that I am". Used about a listener it is direct criticism, and softening
particles do not really rescue it; if you want to be gentle, drop the も and say
{f|調|しら}べないで.

The も is doing real work. {f|読|よ}まないで just says the thing was not read;
{f|読|よ}みもしないで says not one line was read, when reading it was the obvious
first step. Note also the sentence-final use, which is the same construction
standing alone: 「あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|謝|あやま}りもしない」 - "he doesn't even
apologise". The near-twin <code>はしない</code>（{f|謝|あやま}りはしない）swaps the
"even" for a contrast: he does other things, but apologising is not among them.

One structural point. With a する-verb, も goes straight after the noun -
{f|掃除|そうじ}もしないで, {f|連絡|れんらく}もしないで,
{f|確認|かくにん}もしないで. This looks like a different pattern and is not; the noun
is simply standing where the ます-stem would.

## Contrast

### ～もしないで vs ～ないで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ないで</code> reports the omission.
<code>もしないで</code> adds **"not even"**, and with it a judgement.

</div>

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}た<b>ないで</b>{f|出|で}かけた。
I went out without taking an umbrella.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|天気|てんき}{f|予報|よほう}を{f|見|み}<b>もしないで</b>{f|出|で}かけるから、いつも{f|濡|ぬ}れるんだ。
You get soaked every time because you go out without even checking the forecast.
</pre>

The first is a fact about the morning. The second is a scolding, and the も is what
makes it one. If you find yourself wanting to add "even" in the English, the も
belongs in the Japanese.

### ～もしないで vs ～もせずに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical in meaning. <code>しないで</code> is **spoken**,
<code>せずに</code> is **written and formal**. The disapproval is the same in both.

</div>

<pre>
{f|下調|したしら}べ<b>もしないで</b>{f|面接|めんせつ}に{f|行|い}ったの。
You went to the interview without doing any research at all?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|現地|げんち}を{f|確認|かくにん}<b>もせずに</b>{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}をまとめたことが{f|問題|もんだい}である。
The problem is that the report was compiled without even inspecting the site.
</pre>

Note that <code>せずに</code> uses the old negative stem せ of する - never
❌しずに. Everything else about the two forms is the same, so pick by the register
of the surrounding text.

### ～もしないで vs ろくに～ないで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>もしないで</code> says the act happened **not at all**.
<code>ろくに～ないで</code> says it happened but **not properly**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|聞|き}き<b>もしないで</b>{f|判断|はんだん}した。
He made up his mind without hearing the explanation at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}を<b>ろくに</b>{f|聞|き}か<b>ないで</b>{f|判断|はんだん}した。
He made up his mind having barely listened to the explanation.
</pre>

In the first he did not listen for a second; in the second he sat through some of it
with half an ear. Both disapprove, which is why they feel interchangeable, but they
make different factual claims - and if the person genuinely did nothing at all,
ろくに understates it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|謝|あやま}る<b>もしないで</b> → ✅ {f|謝|あやま}り<b>もしないで</b>
  The base is the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|来|こ}<b>もしないで</b> → ✅ {f|来|き}<b>もしないで</b>
  {f|来|く}る is irregular. Its ます-stem is き; こ belongs to
  {f|来|こ}ない and {f|来|こ}よう.

- ❌ {f|調|しら}べ<b>もしなくて</b>{f|買|か}った。 → ✅ {f|調|しら}べ<b>もしないで</b>{f|買|か}った。
  <code>なくて</code> gives a reason ("because it wasn't"); "without doing" is
  <code>ないで</code>.

- ❌ {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|化粧|けしょう}<b>もしないで</b>きれいだ。 → ✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|化粧|けしょう}をし<b>なくても</b>きれいだ。
  The pattern is not "even without doing X". It is "without even doing X", and it
  needs something the speaker disapproves of in the second half.

## Practice

**1.** {f|値段|ねだん}を{f|確|たし}かめ____{f|買|か}ってしまい、あとで{f|後悔|こうかい}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|確|たし}かめ<b>もしないで</b>

Checking the price is the obvious minimum step, and skipping it led to something the
speaker regrets - the standard shape, here turned on the speaker himself.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|彼|かれ}はメールを（{f|読|よ}まないで・{f|読|よ}みもしないで）{f|削除|さくじょ}したらしい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

メールを<b>{f|読|よ}みもしないで</b>{f|削除|さくじょ}したらしい。

Deleting a message unread is precisely the "not even" case, and the speaker is
clearly not impressed. {f|読|よ}まないで would be a flat report with no comment in it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "She threw the letter away without even opening it."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼女|かのじょ}はその{f|手紙|てがみ}を{f|開|あ}け<b>もしないで</b>{f|捨|す}ててしまった。

Opening it is the minimum act that was skipped, so も goes after the ます-stem
{f|開|あ}け. しまう adds the sense that this was a waste or a shame.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}を{f|見|み}るもせずに、{f|会議|かいぎ}で{f|批判|ひはん}を{f|始|はじ}めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}を<b>{f|見|み}もせずに</b>、{f|会議|かいぎ}で{f|批判|ひはん}を{f|始|はじ}めた。
"The department head started criticising it in the meeting without even looking at the report."

The dictionary form {f|見|み}る cannot appear before もせずに. The base is the
ます-stem {f|見|み}, giving {f|見|み}もせずに.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|連絡|れんらく}<b>しないで</b>{f|欠席|けっせき}した。
(b) {f|連絡|れんらく}<b>もしないで</b>{f|欠席|けっせき}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) states what happened: he was absent and did not get in touch. It could be said by
someone simply passing on information.

(b) says a phone call was the least he could have done and he did not even manage
that. The も turns a fact into an accusation, and a listener would take the speaker
to be annoyed with him.

</details>
