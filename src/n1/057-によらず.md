# ～によらず

**Meaning** · regardless of ~; not determined by ~ - the thing named is not what decides the matter

<code>による</code> means "depends on, is due to, is by means of".
<code>によらず</code> is simply its negative, left hanging so the sentence can
continue: "not depending on ~, ...". Which of the three senses of
<code>よる</code> is being negated decides how the phrase reads - most often
"not decided by", sometimes "not by means of", and in one very common set phrase,
"not as it appears".

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + によらず | {f|年齢|ねんれい} → {f|年齢|ねんれい}によらず |
| Noun (means) | Noun + によらず | {f|手術|しゅじゅつ} → {f|手術|しゅじゅつ}によらず |
| Set phrase | {f|見|み}かけによらず | {f|見|み}かけによらず{f|力持|ちからも}ちだ |
| Set phrase | {f|何事|なにごと}によらず | {f|何事|なにごと}によらず{f|慎重|しんちょう}だ |
| Sentence-final | ～によらない | {f|人|ひと}は{f|見|み}かけによらない |
| Formal variant | ～のいかんによらず | {f|理由|りゆう}のいかんによらず |

<div class="note">

**Note** - no `の` before <code>によらず</code>. The `の` belongs to
<code>いかん</code>, which is a noun: <code>{f|理由|りゆう}のいかんによらず</code>
but <code>{f|理由|りゆう}によらず</code>.

</div>

</pre>

</pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>によらず</code> is a **written form** for the "regardless of" sense. It is
the ordinary register of notices, adverts, prospectuses, company statements and
reports, and it is comfortable in prepared speech. It is a step milder than
<code>のいかんによらず</code>, which is document language proper, and a step
stiffer than <code>に{f|関係|かんけい}なく</code>, which is what you would say to
a colleague.

The set phrase <code>{f|見|み}かけによらず</code> is the exception and belongs to
everyday speech, so it is worth separating out. It does **not** mean "regardless of
appearance". It means "contrary to appearances", and it always introduces something
surprising about the person or thing described:
<code>{f|見|み}かけによらず{f|辛|から}いですよ</code>, said of a mild-looking
curry. The proverb form is <code>{f|人|ひと}は{f|見|み}かけによらない</code> or,
more old-fashioned, <code>{f|人|ひと}は{f|見|み}かけによらぬもの</code>. Note
that it is a comment on somebody, and a fairly personal one, so it is friendly
between equals and rude upwards.

The "not by means of" sense - <code>{f|力|ちから}によらず</code>,
<code>{f|手術|しゅじゅつ}によらず</code>,
<code>{f|従来|じゅうらい}の{f|方法|ほうほう}によらず</code> - is the most formal
of the three and the rarest in speech. It reads as technical or literary, and
often has a `で` phrase after it naming the means actually used.

<code>{f|何事|なにごと}によらず</code> is a small fixed adverbial: "in anything
you care to name". Slightly old-fashioned in flavour, common in the mouths of older
speakers and in essays.

## Contrast

### ～によらず vs ～にかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>によらず</code> takes a noun naming a **criterion**
that might have decided the matter. <code>にかかわらず</code> is happiest with a
**pair or a yes-or-no**, and can take a clause.

</div>

<pre>
{f|出席|しゅっせき}するしない<b>にかかわらず</b>、{f|返信|へんしん}をお{f|願|ねが}いします。
Please reply whether or not you plan to attend.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|経験|けいけん}の{f|有無|うむ}<b>にかかわらず</b>、{f|研修|けんしゅう}は{f|全員|ぜんいん}{f|受|う}けていただきます。
Everyone takes the training, experienced or not.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|成績|せいせき}<b>によらず</b>、{f|全員|ぜんいん}に{f|修了証|しゅうりょうしょう}が{f|渡|わた}される。
Every participant receives a certificate, whatever their marks.
</pre>

<code>{f|成績|せいせき}にかかわらず</code> is also possible; what does not work
is the reverse - ❌<code>{f|出席|しゅっせき}するしないによらず</code> is
awkward, because <code>によらず</code> wants a single noun standing for a scale.
Where a scale exists (marks, age, weather, price), take
<code>によらず</code>; where the choice is binary, take
<code>にかかわらず</code>.

### ～によらず vs ～を{f|問|と}わず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> means "we do not **ask** about
it", so it fits eligibility and categories. <code>によらず</code> means "it is
not what **decides** the outcome", so it fits how a judgement is made.

</div>

<pre>
{f|学歴|がくれき}・{f|経験|けいけん}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>、やる{f|気|き}のある{f|方|かた}を{f|募集|ぼしゅう}します。
Education and experience immaterial - we are looking for motivated people.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|採用|さいよう}は{f|学歴|がくれき}<b>によらず</b>、{f|面接|めんせつ}での{f|受|う}け{f|答|こた}えで{f|判断|はんだん}します。
Hiring is not based on educational background; we judge on how candidates answer at interview.
</pre>

The first sentence sets the entry conditions. The second explains the basis of the
decision, and it is followed by what the basis actually *is* - a very common shape
for <code>によらず</code>, and one <code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> does not take
naturally.

### ～によらず vs ～によっては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same verb <code>よる</code>, opposite polarity.
<code>によっては</code> = **in some cases it does** decide.
<code>によらず</code> = it does **not** decide, in any case.

</div>

<pre>
{f|季節|きせつ}<b>によっては</b>、この{f|道|みち}は{f|通|とお}れないことがある。
At some times of year this road is impassable.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|季節|きせつ}<b>によらず</b>、この{f|道|みち}は{f|一年中|いちねんじゅう}{f|通|とお}れる。
The road is open all year round, whatever the season.
</pre>

Learning them as a pair is the efficient move, because the noun in front is
identical and only the tail changes. If you can hear the difference between "some
seasons" and "no season makes a difference", the whole family - including
<code>のいかんでは</code> and <code>のいかんによらず</code> - follows the same
split.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|参加|さんか}する<b>によらず</b> → ✅ {f|参加|さんか}する<b>かどうかにかかわらず</b>
  <code>によらず</code> takes a noun. For a clause, use
  <code>かどうかにかかわらず</code> or
  <code>かどうかに{f|関係|かんけい}なく</code>.

- ❌ {f|見|み}かけ<b>によらず</b>{f|採用|さいよう}します。 → ✅ {f|外見|がいけん}<b>に{f|関係|かんけい}なく</b>{f|採用|さいよう}します。
  <code>{f|見|み}かけによらず</code> is frozen with the meaning "contrary to
  appearances". Used for "we don't care what you look like" it says something quite
  different, and a little strange.

- ❌ {f|成績|せいせき}<b>のによらず</b> → ✅ {f|成績|せいせき}<b>によらず</b>
  No `の`. Learners add one after meeting
  <code>のいかんによらず</code>, where the `の` is there because
  <code>いかん</code> is a noun.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}<b>によらず</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|行|おこな}われる。 → ✅ {f|天候|てんこう}<b>によらず</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|行|おこな}われる。
  The noun has to name a variable - something with a range of possible values.
  {f|雨|あめ} is one particular condition, not a scale, so the natural forms are
  <code>{f|雨|あめ}でも</code> or <code>{f|雨天|うてん}にかかわらず</code>.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|図書館|としょかん}は{f|市内|しない}にお{f|住|す}まいかどうか____、どなたでもご{f|利用|りよう}になれます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

かどうか<b>に{f|関係|かんけい}なく</b>（or かどうか<b>にかかわらず</b>）

The trap is that the blank follows a <code>かどうか</code> clause, and
<code>によらず</code> does not attach to one. With a yes-or-no in front, the
natural tails are <code>にかかわらず</code> and
<code>に{f|関係|かんけい}なく</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|給料|きゅうりょう}は{f|年齢|ねんれい}（によらず・によっては）、{f|仕事|しごと}の{f|成果|せいか}で{f|決|き}まる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|年齢|ねんれい}<b>によらず</b>

The second half names what pay *is* decided by, so the first half has to say what
it is *not* decided by. <code>によっては</code> would claim that age does decide
it in some cases, which the rest of the sentence then contradicts.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He looks delicate, but in fact he hardly ever gets ill."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|見|み}かけによらず</b>{f|丈夫|じょうぶ}で、めったに{f|病気|びょうき}をしない。

"Looks ~ but in fact" is exactly what the set phrase
<code>{f|見|み}かけによらず</code> carries, and it goes in front of the
surprising quality rather than after it. Note that the English "but" disappears -
the contrast is already inside the phrase.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|制度|せいど}は{f|収入|しゅうにゅう}のによらず、{f|子育|こそだ}て{f|世帯|せたい}すべてが{f|対象|たいしょう}となります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|制度|せいど}は{f|収入|しゅうにゅう}<b>によらず</b>、{f|子育|こそだ}て{f|世帯|せたい}すべてが{f|対象|たいしょう}となります。

Delete the `の`. <code>によらず</code> attaches straight to the noun; the `の`
would only be correct in front of <code>いかん</code>, as in
<code>{f|収入|しゅうにゅう}のいかんによらず</code>, which is the same sentence in
document register.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼女|かのじょ}は<b>{f|見|み}かけによらず</b>よく{f|食|た}べる。
(b) {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|見|み}かけ<b>に{f|関係|かんけい}なく</b>よく{f|食|た}べる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is idiomatic and means she eats far more than you would guess from looking at
her. The speaker is registering a mild surprise, and the sentence is a compliment
of sorts among friends.

(b) is grammatical but nearly nonsensical. It says her appetite is not affected by
her appearance - as though the two might have been connected and turned out not to
be. The lesson is that <code>{f|見|み}かけによらず</code> is a fixed expression
with its own meaning, not a free combination of {f|見|み}かけ and
<code>によらず</code>.

</details>
