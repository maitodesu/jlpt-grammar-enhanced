# ～もさることながら

**Meaning** · A is impressive enough in itself, but B is what really deserves the attention

さる is the classical {f|然|さ}る, "such, of that sort", and
<code>さることながら</code> means roughly "while being a thing that is indeed so".
The pattern therefore **grants** A - it is real, it is worth mentioning, nobody is
denying it - and then moves the spotlight on to B. Whatever the sentence is about,
B is the point.

## Formation

A noun, or a clause nominalised with こと. The `も` is part of the pattern and
cannot be dropped.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + もさることながら | {f|値段|ねだん}もさることながら |
| Clause | ～こと + もさることながら | {f|安|やす}いこともさることながら |
| な-adjective idea | ～な + こと + もさることながら | {f|便利|べんり}なこともさることながら |
| Second clause | B が／は + the real point | …{f|品質|ひんしつ}のよさが{f|評価|ひょうか}されている |
| Polite | unchanged; politeness sits at the end | …{f|印象|いんしょう}{f|的|てき}でした |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the weight always falls on the **second** half. If you find yourself
wanting to stress A, you have the wrong pattern; use
<code>{f|特|とく}に</code> or put A in the second slot instead.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Formal, and comfortable in both writing and careful speech. Reviews, introductions
of a speaker, awards citations, product copy, a manager's summing-up, a piece of
criticism - anywhere someone is weighing two merits and deciding which to put
first. It is a shade too dressed-up for chat: telling a friend that a ramen shop's
{f|味|あじ}もさることながら its {f|量|りょう} is impressive is the register of a
magazine, not of a lunch break. There, 「{f|味|あじ}もいいけど…」 does the job.

The commonest use by a long way is **praise**. Because it concedes A before
promoting B, it lets a speaker compliment two things without seeming to gush, and
it is the standard way of saying "yes, the obvious virtue - but look at this one".
It handles problems just as correctly ({f|費用|ひよう}もさることながら…), and there
the effect is of a measured warning: A is bad enough, and B is worse.

One social use worth noticing. Praising a person's effort with
<code>{f|本人|ほんにん}の{f|努力|どりょく}もさることながら</code> and then crediting
someone else is a graceful move in Japanese: it avoids taking anything away from
them while giving the credit where the speaker thinks it belongs. Reversed - B
first, A conceded afterwards - the same content sounds grudging.

The pattern is fixed. <code>さることながら</code> without the `も` is not idiomatic,
and <code>さる</code> cannot be replaced with the modern {f|然|しか}り or anything
else.

## Contrast

### ～もさることながら vs ～はもちろん

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `はもちろん` says A **goes without saying**, and adds B as
another true thing. `もさることながら` says A is **genuinely notable**, and then
ranks B above it.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|大学|だいがく}は{f|研究|けんきゅう}{f|実績|じっせき}<b>はもちろん</b>、{f|就職|しゅうしょく}{f|支援|しえん}にも{f|力|ちから}を{f|入|い}れている。
The university puts effort into careers support as well as, of course, its research record.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|大学|だいがく}は{f|研究|けんきゅう}{f|実績|じっせき}<b>もさることながら</b>、{f|学生|がくせい}への{f|支援|しえん}の{f|手厚|てあつ}さで{f|知|し}られている。
The university's research record speaks for itself, but what it is known for is how well it looks after its students.
</pre>

In the first, both facts sit level and A is simply assumed. In the second there is a
ranking, and B wins it. That ranking is the whole reason to choose this pattern;
if you do not want one, `はもちろん` is lighter and easier.

### ～もさることながら vs ～だけでなく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だけでなく` **adds**. `もさることながら` **weighs**, and hands
the verdict to B.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|語数|ごすう}が{f|多|おお}い<b>だけでなく</b>、{f|例文|れいぶん}も{f|豊富|ほうふ}だ。
This dictionary has a large vocabulary and plenty of example sentences too.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|語数|ごすう}の{f|多|おお}さ<b>もさることながら</b>、{f|例文|れいぶん}の{f|質|しつ}が{f|群|ぐん}を{f|抜|ぬ}いている。
The number of entries is impressive, but the quality of the examples is in another class.
</pre>

`だけでなく` leaves the two merits equal, and is register-neutral into the bargain.
`もさることながら` is formal and takes a position. Notice that it also prefers
**nouns of quality** - {f|多|おお}さ, {f|質|しつ}, よさ - where `だけでなく` is
happy with a plain adjective clause.

### ～もさることながら vs ～にもまして

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both promote the second item. `もさることながら` compares **two
different things**; `にもまして` compares the **same thing** against an earlier
state or another holder of it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今年|ことし}の{f|夏|なつ}は{f|去年|きょねん}<b>にもまして</b>{f|暑|あつ}い。
This summer is even hotter than last year's.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|暑|あつ}さ<b>もさることながら</b>、{f|湿気|しっけ}のひどさが{f|体|からだ}にこたえる。
The heat is bad enough, but it is the humidity that wears you down.
</pre>

`にもまして` needs a single scale - heat, this year against last. `もさることながら`
needs two candidates for one title - heat and humidity, competing to be the thing
worth complaining about. They are easy to keep apart once you ask what is being
compared with what.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|車|くるま}は{f|安|やす}い<b>もさることながら</b>、{f|燃費|ねんぴ}もいい。 → ✅ この{f|車|くるま}は{f|安|やす}い<b>こともさることながら</b>、{f|燃費|ねんぴ}のよさが{f|光|ひか}る。
  An adjective clause needs こと before the pattern, and the second half should be
  the stronger claim, not a casual addition.

- ❌ {f|味|あじ}<b>さることながら</b>、{f|盛|も}り{f|付|つ}けも{f|美|うつく}しい。 → ✅ {f|味|あじ}<b>もさることながら</b>、{f|盛|も}り{f|付|つ}けの{f|美|うつく}しさに{f|驚|おどろ}いた。
  The `も` is not optional. Without it the phrase is not idiomatic Japanese.

- ❌ {f|給料|きゅうりょう}のよさ<b>もさることながら</b>、{f|会社|かいしゃ}が{f|近|ちか}いのもまあいい。 → ✅ {f|会社|かいしゃ}が{f|近|ちか}いこと<b>もさることながら</b>、{f|給料|きゅうりょう}のよさが{f|決|き}め{f|手|て}になった。
  The second half has to be the heavier one. Following the pattern with a shrug
  wastes it, and the sentence lands upside down.

- ❌ このケーキ、{f|見|み}た{f|目|め}<b>もさることながら</b>、めっちゃおいしいよ。 → ✅ このケーキ、{f|見|み}た{f|目|め}もいいけど、{f|味|あじ}が{f|本当|ほんとう}にいいよ。
  Formal pattern, slang ending. めっちゃ and <code>さることながら</code> cannot
  share a sentence.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|美術館|びじゅつかん}は{f|作品|さくひん}の{f|質|しつ}____、{f|建物|たてもの}{f|自体|じたい}が{f|見|み}る{f|価値|かち}のある{f|名作|めいさく}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|質|しつ}<b>もさることながら</b>

The collection is granted first, and the building is promoted to the main point -
the ranking the pattern exists to express. Note the noun of quality
（{f|質|しつ}）in front of it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|会場|かいじょう}には{f|社員|しゃいん}（もさることながら・はもちろん）、{f|取引先|とりひきさき}の{f|方|かた}も{f|大勢|おおぜい}{f|集|あつ}まった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|社員|しゃいん}<b>はもちろん</b>

Nothing is being ranked here; the sentence just reports who came, taking the staff
for granted. `もさることながら` would promise a comparison of merits that the second
half never delivers.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The design is certainly striking, but what sells this phone is how long the battery lasts."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

デザインの{f|良|よ}さ<b>もさることながら</b>、この{f|機種|きしゅ}が{f|売|う}れているのはバッテリーの{f|持|も}ちのよさゆえだ。

"Certainly … but what really" is the shape the pattern was made for. The English
concession becomes `も…ながら`, and the real reason lands in the second clause.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|新|あたら}しい{f|校舎|こうしゃ}は{f|広|ひろ}いさることながら、{f|明|あか}るくて{f|気持|きも}ちがいい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|新|あたら}しい{f|校舎|こうしゃ}は{f|広|ひろ}い<b>こともさることながら</b>、{f|明|あか}るさが{f|印象|いんしょう}{f|的|てき}だ。

Two repairs: an い-adjective needs こと to become a noun, and the `も` must be
there. The second clause has also been strengthened, since the pattern expects the
weight to fall there.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|歌唱力|かしょうりょく}はもちろん、{f|表現力|ひょうげんりょく}もすばらしい。
(b) {f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|歌唱力|かしょうりょく}もさることながら、{f|表現力|ひょうげんりょく}に{f|引|ひ}き{f|込|こ}まれた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) treats her singing as beyond question and adds that her expressiveness is
excellent too. Two compliments, level with each other.

(b) makes a judgement. The singing is acknowledged as genuinely impressive - not
merely assumed - and then set below the expressiveness, which is what actually
gripped the speaker. (b) sounds like a review; (a) could be said in a corridor.

</details>
