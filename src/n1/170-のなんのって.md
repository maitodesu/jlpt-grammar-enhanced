# ～のなんのって

**Meaning** · you would not believe how ～; ～ is putting it mildly - spoken storytelling emphasis, thrown in mid-anecdote

The shape is a quotation that gives up on itself: "whether you call it ～ or call
it anything else…". A speaker uses it in the middle of telling a story, to signal
that the ordinary adjective they just reached for is not going to be enough, and
then usually goes straight on to what happened as a result.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective | plain form + のなんのって | {f|痛|いた}い → {f|痛|いた}いのなんのって |
| Verb | plain form + のなんのって | {f|泣|な}く → {f|泣|な}くのなんのって |
| な-adjective | ～な + のなんのって | {f|大変|たいへん}な → {f|大変|たいへん}なのなんのって |
| Shorter variant | ～のなんの | {f|辛|から}い → {f|辛|から}いのなんの |
| Louder variant | ～のなんのったらない | {f|寒|さむ}いのなんのったらない |
| Different pattern | ～のなんのと + {f|言|い}う／{f|文句|もんく} | {f|高|たか}いのなんのと{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}う |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the last row is a genuinely different construction and catches learners
out. <code>～のなんのと</code> followed by a verb of speaking means "going on about
X and this and that", and it is dismissive of the *speaker being quoted*:
{f|狭|せま}いのなんのと{f|文句|もんく}ばかり{f|言|い}っている, "he does nothing but
complain that it's cramped and so on". No emphasis of degree is involved.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**This is spoken Japanese, and it is marked.** You will hear it in anecdotes over
dinner, on variety television, in comedy routines and in the dialogue of novels
and manga; it turns up in casual written storytelling, blogs and essays, precisely
because those imitate speech. It leans older and chatty - the voice of someone
warming to a good story - and a learner who drops it into a work conversation will
sound as though they are performing. **Recognise it; say something else.**

The ordinary equivalents are simple.
<code>ものすごく{f|痛|いた}くて、しばらく{f|動|うご}けなかった</code> or
<code>{f|痛|いた}くてたまらなかった</code> carry the same content with no register
risk. In writing, <code>{f|非常|ひじょう}に</code> or a plain
<code>とても</code> does the work.

Structurally, notice that the pattern is a **parenthesis, not a predicate**. It
does not end a proper sentence so much as break one off: the speaker states the
quality, waves it away as inadequate, and then delivers the evidence. That is why
almost every real example has a second clause after it. It can be left hanging -
「もう、{f|暑|あつ}いのなんのって。」 - but then the listener is expected to fill in
the rest, which only works face to face.

The って is the casual quotative, so the whole thing is casual by construction.
There is no polite version: ❌{f|痛|いた}いのなんのってです does not exist. If the
sentence needs です or ます, this pattern is the wrong choice.

## Contrast

### ～のなんのって vs the modern equivalent

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - produce <code>ものすごく～て</code>,
<code>～てたまらない</code> or <code>{f|本当|ほんとう}に～</code>. Keep
<code>のなんのって</code> for understanding what you hear.

</div>

<pre>
{f|階段|かいだん}で{f|転|ころ}んで、{f|恥|は}ずかしい<b>のなんのって</b>。
I fell over on the stairs - I have never been so embarrassed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|階段|かいだん}で{f|転|ころ}んでしまって、<b>{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|恥|は}ずかしかった</b>です。
I fell over on the stairs and I was really embarrassed.
</pre>

The first is a friend telling a story; the second can be said to anyone, including
your boss. Both are natural Japanese, but only one of them is safe to produce
before you have a native ear for register.

### ～のなんのって vs ～といったらない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are colloquial exclamations of degree.
<code>といったらない</code> can stand as a complete sentence and is often a
verdict; <code>のなんのって</code> is a mid-story aside that expects a follow-up
clause.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|季節|きせつ}の{f|花粉|かふん}のひどさ<b>といったらない</b>。
The pollen at this time of year is beyond anything.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|花粉|かふん}がひどい<b>のなんのって</b>、{f|一日中|いちにちじゅう}くしゃみが{f|止|と}まらなかった。
The pollen was so bad that I sneezed all day long.
</pre>

<code>といったらない</code> also attaches happily to a ～さ noun and appears in
print; <code>のなんのって</code> is tied to the speaking voice and to the anecdote
it is embedded in.

### ～のなんのって vs ～のなんのと{f|言|い}う

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a verb of **speaking or complaining** after のなんのと changes
the meaning completely: not "extremely", but "going on about X and everything
else".

</div>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|狭|せま}いのなんの<b>と{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}って</b>、{f|部屋|へや}を{f|替|か}えろとうるさい。
My son keeps complaining that it's cramped and so on, and pesters me to change rooms.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}い<b>のなんのって</b>、ベッドを{f|置|お}いたら{f|何|なに}も{f|入|はい}らなかった。
The room was so small that once the bed was in, nothing else would fit.
</pre>

In the first, someone else is doing the talking and the speaker is unimpressed by
it. In the second, the speaker is doing the talking and the room really was tiny.
The signal is the と plus a speech verb.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|暑|あつ}い<b>のなんのってです</b>。 → ✅ {f|暑|あつ}い<b>のなんのって</b>。／{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|暑|あつ}かったです。
  The って is casual quotation; the pattern has no polite form at all.

- ❌ {f|大変|たいへん}<b>のなんのって</b> → ✅ {f|大変|たいへん}<b>なのなんのって</b>
  A な-adjective keeps its な before の, exactly as it does before のだ.

- ❌ ({f|会議|かいぎ}で) {f|今回|こんかい}の{f|遅|おく}れは{f|深刻|しんこく}なのなんのって。 → ✅ ({f|会議|かいぎ}で) {f|今回|こんかい}の{f|遅|おく}れは{f|極|きわ}めて{f|深刻|しんこく}です。
  Register. In a meeting this sounds like a comedian's aside; the formal register
  wants {f|極|きわ}めて or {f|非常|ひじょう}に.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|給料|きゅうりょう}が{f|安|やす}い<b>のなんのって</b>{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}っている。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|給料|きゅうりょう}が{f|安|やす}い<b>のなんのと</b>{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}っている。
  With a speech verb the particle is と, not って, and the meaning shifts to "going
  on about the pay and everything else".

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to hear this, and from whom? 「もう、{f|待|ま}たされたのなんのって、{f|二時間|にじかん}だよ、{f|二時間|にじかん}。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In casual conversation - a friend or family member telling you about their day,
probably with some indignation. Everything about it is spoken: もう as an opener,
the のなんのって aside, the bare だよ, and the repetition of
{f|二時間|にじかん} for effect. It would never appear in a written report.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in neutral modern Japanese: {f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|暑|あつ}いのなんのって、{f|途中|とちゅう}で{f|外|そと}に{f|出|で}てしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会場|かいじょう}は<b>{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|暑|あつ}くて</b>、{f|途中|とちゅう}で{f|外|そと}に{f|出|で}てしまいました。（or {f|暑|あつ}くてたまらず）

The information does not change; only the performance does.
{f|暑|あつ}くてたまらず is the slightly more formal written option, and both can be
said to someone you are not close to.

</details>

**3.** Which of these is the "complaining" pattern rather than the emphatic one?
(a) {f|部屋|へや}が{f|寒|さむ}いのなんのって、{f|毛布|もうふ}を{f|二枚|にまい}{f|使|つか}った。
(b) {f|部屋|へや}が{f|寒|さむ}いのなんのと{f|言|い}って、{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|帰|かえ}ってしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b). The と plus {f|言|い}って marks it as a report of someone else's grumbling:
"saying it was cold and I don't know what else, my son went home." The speaker is
sceptical about the excuse.

(a) is the emphatic pattern: the room really was freezing, and the two blankets
are the proof.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: あの{f|映画|えいが}の{f|最後|さいご}の{f|場面|ばめん}は{f|感動的|かんどうてき}のなんのってでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ あの{f|映画|えいが}の{f|最後|さいご}の{f|場面|ばめん}は<b>{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|感動的|かんどうてき}でした</b>。（casual: {f|感動的|かんどうてき}<b>なのなんのって</b>、{f|涙|なみだ}が{f|止|と}まらなかった）

Two problems. {f|感動的|かんどうてき} is a な-adjective and needs な before の. More
importantly the polite でした cannot be bolted onto a wholly casual pattern - pick
one register and stay in it.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|試験|しけん}が{f|難|むずか}しい<b>のなんのって</b>、{f|半分|はんぶん}も{f|解|と}けなかった。
(b) {f|試験|しけん}の{f|難|むずか}しさ<b>といったらなかった</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are casual and both say the exam was brutal. (a) is mid-anecdote: the speaker
is talking to someone, brushing the word {f|難|むずか}しい aside as inadequate and
immediately producing the evidence. Cut the second clause off and the sentence
feels unfinished.

(b) is a complete exclamation and could close a paragraph. It is also the one you
might meet in print - an essay or a blog post - whereas (a) really belongs to the
speaking voice.

</details>
