# ～じみた

**Meaning** · reeking of ～; looking exactly like ～ - a hostile description, and literally, stained with ～

`じみた` is the past form of the verb `じみる`, which is {f|染|し}みる, "to soak in,
to stain", voiced after a noun. Both of its senses come straight out of that image.
Physically, <code>{f|汗|あせ}じみたシャツ</code> is a shirt the sweat has soaked into
and discoloured. Figuratively, <code>{f|子供|こども}じみた{f|言|い}い{f|訳|わけ}</code>
is an excuse with childishness soaked all the way through it - and the speaker is
not being kind.

## Formation

`じみる` attaches to a **bare noun** and produces a **る-verb** ({f|一段|いちだん} / ichidan /
Group 2). In practice you will almost never meet the dictionary form: the quality
is already there, so the useful forms are ～じみた before a noun and ～じみている as a
predicate.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + じみる (る-verb) | {f|子供|こども} → {f|子供|こども}じみる |
| Modifying a noun | ～じみた + Noun | {f|芝居|しばい} → {f|芝居|しばい}じみた{f|態度|たいど} |
| As a predicate | ～じみている | その{f|話|はなし}は{f|狂気|きょうき}じみている |
| Negative | ～じみない | {f|所帯|しょたい}じみない |
| Becoming that way | ～じみてくる | だんだん{f|所帯|しょたい}じみてくる |
| Literal stain | Noun (substance) + じみた | {f|油|あぶら} → {f|油|あぶら}じみた{f|作業着|さぎょうぎ} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the figurative bases are nouns naming something undignified:
{f|子供|こども}・{f|芝居|しばい}・{f|狂気|きょうき}・{f|説教|せっきょう}・{f|言|い}い{f|訳|わけ}・{f|年寄|としよ}り・{f|所帯|しょたい}.
The literal bases are substances that stain: {f|汗|あせ}・{f|油|あぶら}・{f|垢|あか}.
It is usually written in kana; the kanji spelling {f|染|し}みた exists but is
uncommon in this suffixed use.

</div>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

`じみた` is **a judgement, not a description**, and the judgement is negative every
time. <code>{f|子供|こども}じみた</code> does not mean "childlike" in any warm sense;
it means petty, immature, beneath the person. Because of that, it cannot be turned
into a compliment by choosing a flattering base noun -
❌<code>{f|大人|おとな}じみた</code> is not how you praise a mature child; that is
<code>{f|大人|おとな}びた</code>.

Register-wise it is ordinary Japanese, at home in both speech and writing.
<code>{f|子供|こども}じみたことを{f|言|い}うな</code> is something one adult really
says to another in an argument, and it stings. It is not literary in the way `めく`
is.

Two members need a warning. <code>{f|狂気|きょうき}じみた</code> is strong language
about a plan or an act; applied to a person it is close to an accusation of
madness, so keep it for schemes and behaviour. <code>{f|所帯|しょたい}じみる</code>
means "worn down and narrowed by domestic life", and it has a long history of being
aimed at married women in particular - it is unflattering enough that it is best
recognised rather than deployed, and safest of all used about yourself, as in the
last example above.

## Contrast

### ～じみた vs ～めいた

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ～めいた = **a hint** of it, and can be neutral or even
attractive. ～じみた = **saturated** with it, and is always an attack.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|返事|へんじ}には<b>{f|冗談|じょうだん}めいた</b>{f|響|ひび}きがあった。
There was a half-joking note in his reply.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}での{f|提案|ていあん}はほとんど<b>{f|冗談|じょうだん}じみて</b>いた。
His proposal at the meeting was barely short of a joke.
</pre>

The first is a light observation; the second says the proposal could not be taken
seriously. Note also that <code>{f|謎|なぞ}めいた{f|女性|じょせい}</code> is a
compliment while ❌<code>{f|謎|なぞ}じみた{f|女性|じょせい}</code> does not work at
all - the heavy suffix will not take an attractive noun.

### ～じみた vs ～っぽい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `っぽい` is a light, casual "-ish" that attaches to almost
anything. `じみた` attaches to a short list and delivers a verdict.

</div>

<pre>
その{f|言|い}い{f|方|かた}、ちょっと<b>{f|子供|こども}っぽい</b>よ。
The way you put that is a bit childish.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大|だい}の{f|大人|おとな}が{f|順番|じゅんばん}で{f|争|あらそ}うなんて<b>{f|子供|こども}じみて</b>いる。
Grown adults squabbling over whose turn it is - it's infantile.
</pre>

`っぽい` is softenable ({f|子供|こども}っぽくてかわいい is possible); `じみた` is
not. `っぽい` also has meanings `じみた` does not have at all: a tendency
({f|忘|わす}れっぽい, "forgetful") and a colour or material
({f|白|しろ}っぽい, "whitish").

### ～じみた vs ～まみれ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for the literal sense: `じみた` is a stain that has **soaked in
and dried**. `まみれ` is a substance **sitting on the surface right now**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|練習|れんしゅう}が{f|終|お}わり、{f|全員|ぜんいん}が<b>{f|汗|あせ}まみれ</b>でベンチに{f|座|すわ}っていた。
Practice over, they were all sitting on the bench drenched in sweat.
</pre>

<pre>
ロッカーには{f|去年|きょねん}の<b>{f|汗|あせ}じみた</b>ユニフォームが{f|残|のこ}っていた。
Last year's sweat-stained uniform was still in the locker.
</pre>

You wash `まみれ` off; `じみた` is what is left when you did not. This is also why
`まみれ` can be washed away in the next sentence, while a ～じみた garment is
usually described as being thrown out.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|子供|こども}<b>じみるな</b>{f|態度|たいど} → ✅ {f|子供|こども}<b>じみた</b>{f|態度|たいど}
  `じみる` is a verb. It modifies a noun in its plain past form, since the quality
  has already set in.

- ❌ {f|娘|むすめ}はすっかり{f|大人|おとな}<b>じみて</b>きた。（as praise） → ✅ {f|娘|むすめ}はすっかり{f|大人|おとな}<b>びて</b>きた。
  `じみた` cannot praise. To say a child is growing up nicely, use `びる`.

- ❌ {f|走|はし}ってきたので、シャツが{f|汗|あせ}<b>じみて</b>いる。 → ✅ {f|走|はし}ってきたので、シャツが{f|汗|あせ}<b>まみれだ</b>。
  Fresh sweat is on the surface, so it is `まみれ`. `じみた` is an old stain that has
  set into the cloth.

- ❌ {f|所帯|しょたい}<b>じみらない</b>{f|暮|く}らし → ✅ {f|所帯|しょたい}<b>じみない</b>{f|暮|く}らし
  `じみる` is a る-verb, not an う-verb: the negative simply drops `る`.

## Practice

**1.** {f|大人|おとな}どうしの{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いなのに、{f|途中|とちゅう}から____{f|悪口|わるぐち}の{f|言|い}い{f|合|あ}いになってしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|子供|こども}じみた</b>{f|悪口|わるぐち}

Adults descending into name-calling is exactly the petty, beneath-you quality
`じみた` marks, and the contrast with {f|大人|おとな}どうし makes the criticism the
point of the sentence.

</details>

**2.** Choose: その{f|話|はなし}し{f|方|かた}には、どこか（{f|芝居|しばい}めいた・{f|芝居|しばい}じみた）ところがあって{f|面白|おもしろ}かった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

どこか<b>{f|芝居|しばい}めいた</b>ところがあって{f|面白|おもしろ}かった。

{f|面白|おもしろ}かった makes this positive, and どこか makes it faint - both point
to ～めいた. ～じみた is a verdict, and it cannot sit in a sentence that ends by
saying you enjoyed it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He came home in oil-stained overalls and sat straight down on the sofa."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|油|あぶら}じみた</b>{f|作業着|さぎょうぎ}のまま{f|帰|かえ}ってきて、そのままソファに{f|座|すわ}った。

The literal stain sense: oil that has soaked into the cloth over time, not oil
spilled a moment ago. Fresh oil all over him would be
<code>{f|油|あぶら}まみれ</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}の{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|狂気|きょうき}じみるだと{f|皆|みな}が{f|言|い}っていた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}の{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|狂気|きょうき}<b>じみている</b>と{f|皆|みな}が{f|言|い}っていた。

`じみる` is a verb and cannot take `だ`. The state that the quality has settled into
is ～じみている, and that is the form used as a predicate.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|部屋|へや}は<b>{f|生活|せいかつ}{f|感|かん}</b>があった。
(b) {f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|部屋|へや}は<b>{f|所帯|しょたい}じみて</b>いた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is neutral or even warm: the room shows that someone actually lives there -
books left open, a mug on the table.

(b) is unkind. It says the room, and by extension its owner, has been flattened by
domestic routine: nothing there for pleasure, everything worn and practical.
`じみた` never describes without judging, which is why (b) would be rude to say to
her face.

</details>
