# ～放題

**Meaning** · as much as you like; left to run wild - marks something as unrestrained, either because the restraint was paid off or because nobody bothered

Read <code>{f|放題|ほうだい}</code>. The kanji is {f|放|ほう} "to let loose", and that
single idea splits into two very different halves. Attached to verbs of consuming or
using, it is the language of menus and price plans:
<code>{f|食|た}べ{f|放題|ほうだい}</code>, "all you can eat". Attached to verbs of
neglect or of doing as one pleases, it is a **complaint**:
<code>{f|荒|あ}れ{f|放題|ほうだい}</code>, "left to fall apart";
やりたい{f|放題|ほうだい}, "doing whatever they liked, and no one stopped them".

## Formation

The base is the ます-stem, or the たい-form when the point is that someone did as they
pleased. The result is always a **noun**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb ます-stem (paid service) | ～{f|放題|ほうだい} | {f|食|た}べる → {f|食|た}べ{f|放題|ほうだい} |
| Verb ます-stem (left unchecked) | ～{f|放題|ほうだい} | {f|荒|あ}れる → {f|荒|あ}れ{f|放題|ほうだい} |
| Verb たい-form | ～たい{f|放題|ほうだい} | {f|言|い}う → {f|言|い}いたい{f|放題|ほうだい} |
| する-verbs | し{f|放題|ほうだい}／したい{f|放題|ほうだい} | {f|使用|しよう} → {f|使用|しよう}し{f|放題|ほうだい} |
| Modifying a noun | ～{f|放題|ほうだい}の + {f|名詞|めいし} | {f|伸|の}び{f|放題|ほうだい}の{f|草|くさ} |
| As predicate | ～{f|放題|ほうだい}だ／です | {f|部屋|へや}は{f|散|ち}らかし{f|放題|ほうだい}だ |
| Established nouns | {f|食|た}べ／{f|飲|の}み／{f|使|つか}い／{f|乗|の}り + {f|放題|ほうだい} | {f|二時間|にじかん}{f|飲|の}み{f|放題|ほうだい} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the reading never voices: ほうだい, not ❌ぼうだい, in every compound. And
the result is a **noun**, so it takes だ, の and が like any other -
<code>{f|食|た}べ{f|放題|ほうだい}の{f|店|みせ}</code>,
<code>{f|食|た}べ{f|放題|ほうだい}が{f|付|つ}いている</code> - and can never be
conjugated as a verb.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise the suffix; produce only the fixed compounds.** Everything below sorts
into two piles, and only one of them is a learner's to use.

**The service compounds are ordinary vocabulary.** {f|食|た}べ{f|放題|ほうだい},
{f|飲|の}み{f|放題|ほうだい}, {f|乗|の}り{f|放題|ほうだい},
{f|使|つか}い{f|放題|ほうだい}, {f|読|よ}み{f|放題|ほうだい},
かけ{f|放題|ほうだい} are nouns you will meet on menus, rail passes and phone
contracts, and asking <code>{f|飲|の}み{f|放題|ほうだい}は{f|付|つ}きますか</code>
is perfectly normal Japanese. Learn them as words.

**The suffix behind them is not yours to use freely.** In advertising copy it is
semi-productive inside one frame - a flat fee that buys unlimited use of something -
which is where {f|学|まな}び{f|放題|ほうだい} and {f|見|み}{f|放題|ほうだい} come
from. Outside that frame, coining is where learners go wrong. ❌
<code>{f|週末|しゅうまつ}は{f|寝|ね}{f|放題|ほうだい}だ</code> for "I can sleep as
much as I like at the weekend" reads like a hotel advertisement. The ordinary ways to
say it are <code>{f|好|す}きなだけ{f|寝|ね}られる</code> and
<code>いくらでも{f|寝|ね}ていい</code>, and those are what a learner should produce.

**The other pile is a judgement, and a hard one.**
{f|荒|あ}れ{f|放題|ほうだい}, {f|伸|の}び{f|放題|ほうだい},
{f|散|ち}らかし{f|放題|ほうだい} accuse someone of neglect;
やりたい{f|放題|ほうだい} and {f|言|い}いたい{f|放題|ほうだい} accuse someone of
acting without restraint because nobody could stop them. They are the vocabulary of
newspaper criticism, of complaints about landlords and governments and badly behaved
guests, and of narration describing an abandoned place. Said about a person to their
face, やりたい{f|放題|ほうだい} is close to calling them a tyrant, and no politeness
ending will soften it - <code>やりたい{f|放題|ほうだい}ですね</code> to a manager is
not a comment on their autonomy, it is an accusation. If you mean someone works
freely, say <code>{f|自由|じゆう}に{f|進|すす}めていらっしゃいますね</code>.

So: recognise the whole pattern, buy the {f|食|た}べ{f|放題|ほうだい}, and leave the
coining and the accusations to native speakers.

## Contrast

### ～{f|放題|ほうだい} vs ～っぱなし

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `っぱなし` = **one action was not undone** (the light left on,
the window left open). <code>{f|放題|ほうだい}</code> = **a state was allowed to run
to its extreme** over time.

</div>

<pre>
エアコンを<b>つけっぱなし</b>で{f|出|で}かけてしまった。
I went out and left the air conditioning on.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|手入|てい}れをしないので、{f|庭|にわ}は{f|荒|あ}れ<b>{f|放題|ほうだい}</b>だ。
Nobody looks after it, so the garden has gone completely wild.
</pre>

`っぱなし` points at a single unfinished action and is everyday spoken Japanese -
{f|置|お}きっぱなし, {f|出|だ}しっぱなし, {f|立|た}ちっぱなし.
<code>{f|放題|ほうだい}</code> points at the result of long neglect and sounds
stronger and more literary. A light left on all night is
<code>つけっぱなし</code>; a house left empty for a decade is
<code>{f|荒|あ}れ{f|放題|ほうだい}</code>.

### ～{f|放題|ほうだい} vs {f|好|す}きなだけ・いくらでも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - this is the swap to remember.
<code>{f|好|す}きなだけ</code> and <code>いくらでも</code> are the **ordinary,
productive** ways to say "as much as you like". <code>{f|放題|ほうだい}</code>
outside its fixed compounds is advertising language.

</div>

<pre>
{f|遠慮|えんりょ}しないで、<b>{f|好|す}きなだけ</b>{f|取|と}ってください。
Don't hold back - take as much as you want.
</pre>

<pre>
このパスがあれば、{f|市内|しない}のバスは{f|乗|の}り<b>{f|放題|ほうだい}</b>です。
With this pass you can ride the city buses as much as you like.
</pre>

The first is what you say to a guest at your table; the second is what the tourist
office prints. Try to say the first sentence with
<code>{f|放題|ほうだい}</code> - ❌<code>{f|取|と}り{f|放題|ほうだい}です</code> - and
you have turned your dinner into a buffet promotion.

### ～たい{f|放題|ほうだい} vs ～ままに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ままに</code> ({f|思|おも}うままに, なすがままに) is
**neutral or literary**: as one wishes, as it happens.
<code>たい{f|放題|ほうだい}</code> adds that **nobody restrained them**, and
condemns it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|定年後|ていねんご}は{f|思|おも}う<b>ままに</b>{f|絵|え}を{f|描|か}いて{f|暮|く}らしている。
Since retiring he has lived painting as he pleases.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|管理人|かんりにん}がいないのをいいことに、{f|住民|じゅうみん}はやり<b>たい{f|放題|ほうだい}</b>だった。
With no caretaker on site, the residents did whatever they liked.
</pre>

Swap them and the retiree becomes a menace while the residents become a picture of
quiet freedom. <code>{f|思|おも}うままに</code> is admiring;
<code>やりたい{f|放題|ほうだい}</code> is an indictment, and the giveaway is usually
in the clause before it - some absence of supervision that the sentence is blaming.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|食|た}べる<b>{f|放題|ほうだい}</b> → ✅ {f|食|た}べ<b>{f|放題|ほうだい}</b>
  The base is the ます-stem: {f|食|た}べ, {f|飲|の}み, {f|使|つか}い,
  {f|乗|の}り. Not the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|焼肉|やきにく}を{f|食|た}べ<b>{f|放題|ほうだい}しました</b>。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|食|た}べ<b>{f|放題|ほうだい}</b>の{f|焼肉屋|やきにくや}に{f|行|い}きました。
  It is a noun, not a verb. You go to a
  <code>{f|食|た}べ{f|放題|ほうだい}の{f|店|みせ}</code>, or the meal
  <code>は{f|食|た}べ{f|放題|ほうだい}でした</code>.

- ❌ {f|土曜日|どようび}は{f|家|いえ}で{f|寝|ね}<b>{f|放題|ほうだい}</b>だ。 → ✅ {f|土曜日|どようび}は{f|家|いえ}で{f|好|す}きなだけ{f|寝|ね}られる。
  Coining outside the paid-service frame produces advertising copy about your own
  Saturday. Use <code>{f|好|す}きなだけ</code> or <code>いくらでも</code>.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}はやりたい<b>{f|放題|ほうだい}</b>ですね。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|自由|じゆう}に{f|進|すす}めていらっしゃいますね。
  やりたい{f|放題|ほうだい} says a person acts unchecked because no one can stop
  them. Aimed at anyone present, it is an accusation.

- ❌ {f|髪|かみ}が{f|伸|の}び<b>{f|放題|ほうだい}</b>で、うらやましいです。 → ✅ {f|髪|かみ}が{f|長|なが}くてうらやましいです。
  <code>{f|伸|の}び{f|放題|ほうだい}</code> means unkempt, not enviably long. The
  neglect branch never compliments.

## Practice

**1.** {f|一年|いちねん}{f|以上|いじょう}{f|誰|だれ}も{f|手入|てい}れをしていないので、{f|生|い}け{f|垣|がき}は{f|伸|の}び____になっている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|伸|の}び<b>{f|放題|ほうだい}</b>になっている

Long neglect plus an unchecked result is the criticising branch of the pattern. Note
the clue in the first half: sentences with this branch almost always name the absence
of care that allowed it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|温泉|おんせん}に{f|入|はい}った{f|後|あと}、{f|冷|つめ}たい{f|水|みず}を（{f|飲|の}み{f|放題|ほうだい}・{f|好|す}きなだけ）{f|飲|の}んだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|冷|つめ}たい{f|水|みず}を<b>{f|好|す}きなだけ</b>{f|飲|の}んだ。

<code>{f|飲|の}み{f|放題|ほうだい}</code> is a noun naming a service you have paid
for; it cannot describe how much you personally drank.
<code>{f|好|す}きなだけ</code> is the ordinary adverbial way to say "as much as I
liked".

</details>

**3.** Where would you expect to read each of these? (a) {f|一泊|いっぱく}{f|二食|にしょく}{f|付|つ}き、ドリンク<b>{f|飲|の}み{f|放題|ほうだい}</b>。 (b) {f|工場|こうじょう}は{f|閉鎖|へいさ}され、{f|敷地|しきち}は{f|荒|あ}れ<b>{f|放題|ほうだい}</b>のまま{f|残|のこ}された。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is hotel or travel-agency copy - a plan with two meals and unlimited drinks. It
is commercial vocabulary and completely neutral.

(b) is journalism or narration: a closed factory, a site nobody maintains. The same
suffix here carries neglect and implied blame, and the sentence would sit in a news
report or a novel rather than in conversation.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|旅館|りょかん}では{f|温泉|おんせん}に{f|入|はい}る{f|放題|ほうだい}できます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|旅館|りょかん}では{f|温泉|おんせん}に{f|何度|なんど}でも{f|入|はい}れます。
Or, in advertising style: ✅ {f|温泉|おんせん}{f|入|はい}り<b>{f|放題|ほうだい}</b>。

Two problems: the base must be the ます-stem, not
<code>{f|入|はい}る</code>, and <code>{f|放題|ほうだい}</code> is a noun and cannot
be followed by <code>できます</code>. In ordinary speech the natural repair drops
the pattern altogether.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|自由|じゆう}に{f|意見|いけん}を{f|言|い}っている。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|言|い}いたい{f|放題|ほうだい}</b>{f|言|い}っている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is neutral and can be praise: he speaks his mind, and an organisation where that
is possible is a healthy one.

(b) says he says whatever he likes because nobody holds him to account - the
responsibility is missing, and the speaker resents it. It belongs to a complaint
about a colleague, a commentator or a politician, and it is not a sentence to use
about someone who can hear you.

</details>
