# ～かいもなく

**Meaning** · for all the effort, to no avail - work was put in, aimed squarely at one result, and that result did not come

The noun is {f|甲斐|かい}, "worth, avail, something to show for it". `かいもなく`
therefore says *there was not even that much to show for it*. Three things have to
be true for the pattern to fit: somebody made a real effort, the effort was aimed at
a particular outcome, and the outcome failed. Take any one away and the sentence
sounds wrong.

## Formation

The effort goes in front, either as a past-tense clause or as a noun with `の`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (past) | plain past + かいもなく | {f|看病|かんびょう}した → {f|看病|かんびょう}したかいもなく |
| Noun | Noun + のかいもなく | {f|努力|どりょく} → {f|努力|どりょく}のかいもなく |
| Shorter variant | ～かいなく | {f|説得|せっとく}のかいなく |
| Positive counterpart | ～かいあって / かいがあって | {f|練習|れんしゅう}した → {f|練習|れんしゅう}したかいあって |
| As a predicate | ～かいがある / かいがない | {f|待|ま}った → {f|待|ま}ったかいがあった |
| Kanji spelling | ～{f|甲斐|かい}もなく | {f|治療|ちりょう}の{f|甲斐|かい}もなく |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the verb in front is **past**. ❌<code>{f|練習|れんしゅう}するかいもなく</code>
is wrong: the effort has to be finished before you can report that it bought you
nothing.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - both spellings are standard, and news copy tends to prefer the kanji
{f|甲斐|かい}. The same noun voices to `がい` when it is welded onto a verb stem:
やりがい (work worth doing), {f|生|い}きがい (what makes life worth living),
{f|働|はたら}きがい. Those are ordinary everyday words and carry none of the gloom of
`かいもなく`.

</div>

## Nuance & register

The adverbial `かいもなく` leans **written and formal**. It is standard in news
reports, obituaries, company announcements and the reflective register of an essay;
in conversation the same thought comes out as
{f|頑張|がんば}ったのに{f|無駄|むだ}だった or せっかく…したのに. The related predicate
{f|甲斐|かい}がある / {f|甲斐|かい}がない, by contrast, is completely ordinary spoken
Japanese - {f|待|ま}った{f|甲斐|かい}があった, "the wait was worth it".

<code>{f|懸命|けんめい}の{f|治療|ちりょう}の{f|甲斐|かい}もなく</code> is close to a
fixed formula in Japanese news writing and in the announcement of a death, and it is
worth knowing simply because you will meet it. Because of that association the
pattern carries real weight, and using it about something trivial - a missed bus, a
cold coffee - sounds either childishly dramatic or deliberately comic.

The emotion is **disappointment, not blame**. The effort is granted as genuine; what
the sentence laments is that it bought nothing. If you want to accuse somebody, this
is the wrong pattern.

## Contrast

### ～かいもなく vs ～かいあって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one noun, two outcomes. `かいあって` = the effort paid off.
`かいもなく` = it did not. Learning them as a pair is the fastest way to keep the
direction straight.

</div>

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|走|はし}った<b>かいあって</b>、タイムが{f|大|おお}きく{f|縮|ちぢ}んだ。
All that daily running paid off - my time came down sharply.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|走|はし}った<b>かいもなく</b>、タイムはほとんど{f|変|か}わらなかった。
All that daily running got me nowhere; my time barely moved.
</pre>

The `も` in `かいもなく` is doing real work: *not even* the worth. Drop it and you
get `かいなく`, which is the same meaning in a slightly tighter, more literary
package. There is no ❌`かいもあって`.

### ～かいもなく vs ～のに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `のに` covers any expectation that was let down. `かいもなく`
needs **invested effort** that was aimed at the very thing that failed.

</div>

<pre>
{f|晴|は}れる{f|予報|よほう}だった<b>のに</b>、{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}した。
The forecast said clear, and then it started raining.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|何度|なんど}も{f|練習|れんしゅう}した<b>かいもなく</b>、{f|本番|ほんばん}で{f|間違|まちが}えた。
For all that rehearsing, I fluffed it on the night.
</pre>

Nobody worked at making the forecast come true, so ❌<code>{f|予報|よほう}のかいもなく</code>
does not work. The other direction is easier: almost anything you can say with
`かいもなく` can also be said with `のに`, at a lower register and with the loss of
the "wasted effort" colouring.

### ～かいもなく vs ～にもかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `にもかかわらず` concedes a **circumstance**, and the outcome may
be good, bad or neutral. `かいもなく` concedes an **effort**, and the outcome is
always the failure of what that effort was for.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大雨|おおあめ}<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|催|もよお}しは{f|予定|よてい}どおり{f|行|おこな}われた。
In spite of the downpour, the event went ahead as planned.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|入念|にゅうねん}な{f|準備|じゅんび}の<b>かいもなく</b>、{f|催|もよお}しは{f|途中|とちゅう}で{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。
For all the careful preparation, the event was abandoned halfway through.
</pre>

Swap them and the first sentence stops making sense: rain is not an effort anybody
made. The second could take `にもかかわらず` and would still be grammatical, but the
sting - all that work, gone - drains out of it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|努力|どりょく}した<b>かいもなく</b>、{f|合格|ごうかく}した。 → ✅ {f|努力|どりょく}した<b>かいあって</b>、{f|合格|ごうかく}した。
  A good outcome takes `かいあって`. `かいもなく` announces that the effort bought
  nothing, so pairing it with success contradicts itself.

- ❌ {f|練習|れんしゅう}する<b>かいもなく</b>、{f|負|ま}けた。 → ✅ {f|練習|れんしゅう}した<b>かいもなく</b>、{f|負|ま}けた。
  The clause is past. The effort has to be over before you can report that it
  yielded nothing.

- ❌ {f|努力|どりょく}<b>かいもなく</b> → ✅ {f|努力|どりょく}の<b>かいもなく</b>
  <code>かい</code> is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs `の`.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}の<b>かいもなく</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}のため、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。
  Rain is a cause, not an effort somebody made. Only something a person did on
  purpose, hoping for a result, can go in front of <code>かい</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|一年|いちねん}かけて{f|準備|じゅんび}した____、{f|大会|たいかい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一年|いちねん}かけて{f|準備|じゅんび}した<b>かいもなく</b>、{f|大会|たいかい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。

A year of preparation, aimed at one event, and the event never happened - effort,
target and failure are all present. The verb in front stays in the plain past.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|単語|たんご}を{f|覚|おぼ}えた（かいもなく・かいあって）、{f|読|よ}む{f|速|はや}さがかなり{f|上|あ}がった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|単語|たんご}を{f|覚|おぼ}えた<b>かいあって</b>、{f|読|よ}む{f|速|はや}さがかなり{f|上|あ}がった。

The reading speed went up, so the effort was repaid. `かいもなく` is only for the
case where the hoped-for result never arrived.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "In spite of a month of rehearsals, the play closed after three days."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一|いっ}か{f|月|げつ}にわたる{f|稽古|けいこ}の<b>{f|甲斐|かい}もなく</b>、{f|芝居|しばい}は{f|三日|みっか}で{f|打|う}ち{f|切|き}られた。

The rehearsals are effort aimed at a run of performances, and the run collapsed -
`かいもなく` almost writes itself. A noun phrase in front takes `の`, and
{f|三日|みっか} is the native reading of the counter.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|渋滞|じゅうたい}の{f|甲斐|かい}もなく、{f|開演|かいえん}に{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|早|はや}めに{f|出|で}た<b>{f|甲斐|かい}もなく</b>、{f|開演|かいえん}に{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わなかった。
"For all that leaving early, I missed the curtain."

A traffic jam is what got in the way, not the effort that was wasted. Whatever sits
in front of <code>{f|甲斐|かい}</code> has to be the thing the speaker did on
purpose. If you want the jam as the cause, write
<code>{f|渋滞|じゅうたい}のため</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|手術|しゅじゅつ}をした<b>のに</b>、{f|痛|いた}みは{f|消|き}えなかった。
(b) {f|手術|しゅじゅつ}の<b>{f|甲斐|かい}もなく</b>、{f|痛|いた}みは{f|消|き}えなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is what a patient says. `のに` registers a personal grievance - I went through
that, and here I still am - and it is ordinary spoken Japanese.

(b) is what a written account says. It presents the operation as an intervention
undertaken in order to remove the pain, and reports coolly that it achieved nothing.
The regret is there, but it is the regret of a narrator rather than a complaint, and
the register is a clear step higher.

</details>
