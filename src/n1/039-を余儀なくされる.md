# ～を余儀なくされる

**Meaning** · to be forced into something; to be left with no alternative - read <code>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる</code>

{f|余儀|よぎ} is an old noun meaning "another way", so {f|余儀|よぎ}ない is "there is
no other way". <code>を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる</code> is the passive of that: the
subject is the person or organisation that has been left without an option, and the
noun in front of を is the unwelcome thing they now have to accept. It is the
sentence a news bulletin uses when weather, money or an accident has decided
something for somebody.

## Formation

The pattern takes a **noun**, almost always an action noun - the する of a
する-verb is simply dropped.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (action) | Noun + を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる | {f|中止|ちゅうし} → {f|中止|ちゅうし}を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる |
| する-verb | drop する | {f|休業|きゅうぎょう}する → {f|休業|きゅうぎょう}を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる |
| Noun with a modifier | ～の + Noun + を… | {f|計画|けいかく}の{f|変更|へんこう}を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる |
| Past | ～を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた | {f|撤退|てったい}を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた |
| Ongoing state | ～を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされている | {f|避難|ひなん}{f|生活|せいかつ}を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされている |
| Polite | ～を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされました | {f|延期|えんき}を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされました |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the grammatical subject is the **victim**, not the cause. The cause goes
in a separate phrase with で, のため, により or a preceding clause:
<code>{f|大雨|おおあめ}で、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</code>.
Putting the cause in the subject slot needs the causative
<code>を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくさせる</code> instead.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|雪|ゆき}のため、{f|登山|とざん}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>。
The snow forced the climb to be called off.
</pre>

<pre>
けがで、その{f|選手|せんしゅ}は{f|引退|いんたい}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>。
An injury forced the player into retirement.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|都合|つごう}で、{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|転勤|てんきん}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされました</b>。
Circumstances at the company left her no choice but to transfer.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|地震|じしん}の{f|後|あと}、{f|数千人|すうせんにん}が{f|車|くるま}での{f|生活|せいかつ}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされている</b>。
Since the earthquake, several thousand people have been living in their cars with nowhere else to go.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|燃料|ねんりょう}{f|価格|かかく}の{f|高騰|こうとう}により、{f|航空|こうくう}{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|路線|ろせん}の{f|縮小|しゅくしょう}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>。
Soaring fuel prices forced the airline to cut back its network.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|水道管|すいどうかん}の{f|工事|こうじ}で、{f|近|ちか}くの{f|店|みせ}は{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}の{f|休業|きゅうぎょう}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされました</b>。
Work on the water main forced the nearby shops to close for a week.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|資金|しきん}が{f|集|あつ}まらず、{f|劇団|げきだん}は{f|公演|こうえん}の{f|延期|えんき}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>。
The funding never came together, and the theatre company had to postpone the run.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部品|ぶひん}の{f|供給|きょうきゅう}が{f|止|と}まったため、{f|多|おお}くの{f|工場|こうじょう}が{f|生産|せいさん}{f|計画|けいかく}の{f|見直|みなお}しを<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされている</b>。
With the supply of parts cut off, many plants are having to rewrite their production plans.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is **formal written Japanese**, and its natural home is news copy, company
announcements, annual reports and official statements. In speech it appears in
meetings and interviews, never over lunch. Among friends the same idea comes out as
<code>{f|中止|ちゅうし}するしかなかった</code> or
<code>{f|中止|ちゅうし}になっちゃった</code>.

Three things travel with it. The outcome is **bad for the subject** - nobody is
{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる into a promotion they wanted. The cause is **external and
serious**: weather, illness, money, an accident, a regulation. And the tone is
**neutral and impersonal**: the form reports the situation rather than complaining
about it, which is why a company can use it about itself without sounding as though
it is making excuses.

Because of that seriousness, the pattern is badly out of scale for small
inconveniences. <code>{f|昼食|ちゅうしょく}はコンビニの{f|弁当|べんとう}を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</code>
is not wrong so much as comic - it dresses a sandwich up as a disaster. Some writers
use exactly that mismatch for humour, but recognise the joke rather than make it.

The expression is frozen. You will practically never meet the active
<code>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくする</code> on its own; what exists in real text is this
passive and the causative <code>を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくさせる</code>. The adjective
survives separately in the set phrase
<code>{f|余儀|よぎ}ない{f|事情|じじょう}</code>, "unavoidable circumstances".

## Contrast

### ～を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる vs ～ざるを{f|得|え}ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる</code> takes a **noun** and
reports the outcome from outside, like a bulletin.
<code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> takes a **verb** and speaks from inside the
decision, with the speaker's reluctance audible.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大雪|おおゆき}で、{f|大会|たいかい}は{f|延期|えんき}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>。
Heavy snow forced the tournament to be postponed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大雪|おおゆき}なので、{f|大会|たいかい}を{f|延期|えんき}せ<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</b>。
With snow like this we have no choice but to postpone the tournament.
</pre>

The first is what the newspaper prints; the second is what the organiser says at the
meeting. Note the grammar difference too: を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる cannot take a
verb, and ざるを{f|得|え}ない cannot take a bare noun.

### ～を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる vs ～を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくさせる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - **される** puts the sufferer in the subject slot; **させる**
puts the cause there. Same event, opposite ends of the sentence.

</div>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}で、{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|中断|ちゅうだん}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>。
The typhoon meant the works had to be suspended.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}が{f|工事|こうじ}の{f|中断|ちゅうだん}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくさせた</b>。
The typhoon forced a suspension of the works.
</pre>

Getting these the wrong way round is the single most common error with the pair.
Ask who is being pushed: if it is the subject of your sentence, use された.

### ～を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる vs ～させられる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the causative-passive <code>させられる</code> means **a person
made me**, and it resents them. <code>を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされる</code> means
**circumstances left no opening**, and blames nobody.

</div>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|残業|ざんぎょう}を<b>させられた</b>。
The section head made me work late.
</pre>

<pre>
システム{f|障害|しょうがい}で、{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|残業|ざんぎょう}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>。
A system failure left everyone with no option but to work late.
</pre>

<code>させられる</code> needs someone to point at, and it carries a grudge. The
{f|余儀|よぎ}なく form deliberately has no villain, which is why organisations
reach for it when they would rather not name one.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|帰|かえ}るを<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b> → ✅ {f|帰国|きこく}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>（or {f|帰|かえ}らざるを{f|得|え}なかった）
  を needs a noun in front of it. A dictionary-form verb cannot sit there; either
  find the matching action noun or switch pattern.

- ❌ {f|中止|ちゅうし}するを<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b> → ✅ {f|中止|ちゅうし}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>
  With a する-verb, drop the する. This is the most frequent slip in exam answers.

- ❌ {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|試合|しあい}の{f|中止|ちゅうし}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b> → ✅ {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|試合|しあい}の{f|中止|ちゅうし}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくさせた</b>
  The cause cannot be the subject of the passive. Either flip to the causative, or
  demote the typhoon: {f|台風|たいふう}で{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた.

- ❌ {f|希望|きぼう}の{f|大学|だいがく}への{f|進学|しんがく}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b> → ✅ {f|希望|きぼう}の{f|大学|だいがく}に{f|進|すす}むことができた
  The pattern reports damage. Applied to something the subject wanted, it says the
  opposite of what you mean.

## Practice

**1.** {f|工場|こうじょう}の{f|火事|かじ}により、{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|生産|せいさん}の{f|停止|ていし}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|生産|せいさん}の{f|停止|ていし}<b>を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>

The fire is an external disaster and the company is the party left without options,
so the passive is right. Note that the cause sits in the により phrase, not in the
subject slot.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|急|きゅう}な{f|雨|あめ}で、{f|運動会|うんどうかい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}を（{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた・{f|余儀|よぎ}なくさせた）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|中止|ちゅうし}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>。

The subject of the sentence is {f|運動会|うんどうかい} - the thing that suffered
the cancellation. The causative would need the rain itself as subject:
{f|急|きゅう}な{f|雨|あめ}が{f|運動会|うんどうかい}の{f|中止|ちゅうし}を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくさせた.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Because of the shortage of staff, the shop was forced to shorten its opening hours."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|人手|ひとで}{f|不足|ぶそく}のため、{f|店|みせ}は{f|営業|えいぎょう}{f|時間|じかん}の{f|短縮|たんしゅく}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>。

"Was forced to shorten" becomes the action noun {f|短縮|たんしゅく} plus the
passive. Note the reading {f|人手|ひとで}{f|不足|ぶそく}: {f|不足|ふそく} takes
rendaku and becomes ぶそく in this compound.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|大雨|おおあめ}が{f|多|おお}くの{f|住民|じゅうみん}に{f|避難|ひなん}を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|大雨|おおあめ}が{f|多|おお}くの{f|住民|じゅうみん}に{f|避難|ひなん}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくさせた</b>。
（or {f|大雨|おおあめ}で、{f|多|おお}くの{f|住民|じゅうみん}が{f|避難|ひなん}を{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた。）

The rain is the cause and sits in the subject slot, so the verb has to be the
causative. Keeping された would mean the rain itself was the one left with no
choice.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|上司|じょうし}に{f|休日|きゅうじつ}{f|出勤|しゅっきん}を<b>させられた</b>。
(b) {f|納期|のうき}の{f|関係|かんけい}で、{f|休日|きゅうじつ}{f|出勤|しゅっきん}を<b>{f|余儀|よぎ}なくされた</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) names a culprit. The boss made the speaker come in, and the causative-passive
carries the resentment openly; you would say it to a friend, not in a report.

(b) has no culprit at all. The delivery date is what closed off the alternatives,
and the sentence is written in the neutral, formal register a company would use
about itself. It also sounds more serious - a genuine constraint rather than an
unreasonable manager.

</details>
