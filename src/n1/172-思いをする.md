# ～思いをする

**Meaning** · to go through something that leaves you feeling ～ - {f|嫌|いや}な{f|思|おも}いをする is to have a thoroughly unpleasant time of it

The {f|思|おも}い here is not a thought but a **felt experience**. The pattern
describes what an episode was like from the inside for the person who lived
through it: not "I was embarrassed" as a state, but "I went through something
embarrassing". Its causative twin,
<code>{f|思|おも}いをさせる</code>, is the standard way of apologising for having
put someone through it.

## Usage

An adjective describing the feeling goes in front, and {f|思|おも}い takes を with
する. い-adjectives attach in their plain form -
{f|悔|くや}しい{f|思|おも}いをする, {f|心細|こころぼそ}い{f|思|おも}いをする -
and な-adjectives keep their な: {f|嫌|いや}な{f|思|おも}いをする,
{f|不安|ふあん}な{f|思|おも}いをする. A short descriptive phrase can fill the same
slot, which is where the set expression
{f|肩身|かたみ}の{f|狭|せま}い{f|思|おも}いをする, "to feel small in front of
others", comes from.

The past {f|思|おも}いをした is by far the commonest form, since the pattern
narrates something that has already happened. The causative
{f|思|おも}いをさせる shifts the experience onto someone else and is almost
obligatory in apologies:
{f|嫌|いや}な{f|思|おも}いをさせてしまい、{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません.
Passive and potential forms are not used with it.

The adjectives that occur are ones that describe how a situation felt to a person:
{f|嫌|いや}な, つらい, {f|悲|かな}しい, {f|寂|さび}しい, {f|恥|は}ずかしい,
{f|悔|くや}しい, {f|心細|こころぼそ}い, こわい, {f|痛|いた}い, {f|不安|ふあん}な,
{f|不快|ふかい}な. Most are negative. The positive members are
{f|楽|たの}しい{f|思|おも}いをする and the idiom
いい{f|思|おも}いをする, which does not mean "have a nice time" - see below.

Everyone around me had done it before, and I was the only one feeling like an intruder.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The expression is **register-neutral** and extremely common. It works in
conversation, in essays, in customer emails and in formal apologies, and it does
not sound stiff in any of them. What it does carry is a viewpoint: the subject is
the person who *underwent* the experience, so it is a natural fit for stories about
being on the receiving end - of rudeness, of a mistake, of bad luck.

<code>{f|思|おも}いをさせる</code> is worth drilling separately, because Japanese
apologies almost always take that shape. You do not apologise for the fact that
the customer was upset; you apologise for having caused them to go through
something unpleasant:
ご{f|不便|ふべん}な{f|思|おも}いをおかけしました is a stiffer variant of the same
move, and both are heard daily in service settings.

Keep <code>いい{f|思|おも}いをする</code> at arm's length. It does not mean "have a
good time" - it means to do rather well out of something, usually at others'
expense, and it is said with a raised eyebrow.

<pre>
{f|苦労|くろう}したのは{f|現場|げんば}で、{f|上|うえ}の{f|人|ひと}たちだけが<b>いい{f|思|おも}いをした</b>。
The people on the ground did the suffering, and only those at the top did well out of it.
</pre>

Finally, do not confuse the {f|思|おも}い of this pattern with the verb
{f|思|おも}う. ～と{f|思|おも}う reports an opinion;
～{f|思|おも}いをする reports an experience. They share a kanji and nothing else.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|嫌|いや}<b>{f|思|おも}いをした</b> → ✅ {f|嫌|いや}<b>な{f|思|おも}いをした</b>
  {f|嫌|いや} and {f|不安|ふあん} are な-adjectives and keep their な before
  {f|思|おも}い.

- ❌ {f|悔|くや}しい{f|思|おも}いを<b>{f|思|おも}った</b>。 → ✅ {f|悔|くや}しい{f|思|おも}いを<b>した</b>。
  The verb is する. {f|思|おも}いを{f|思|おも}う is a double of the same word and is
  not Japanese.

- ❌ お{f|客様|きゃくさま}に{f|嫌|いや}な{f|思|おも}いを<b>しました</b>。 → ✅ お{f|客様|きゃくさま}に{f|嫌|いや}な{f|思|おも}いを<b>させてしまいました</b>。
  If the person who suffered is somebody else, the verb has to be causative.
  Without it the sentence says that you were the one having a bad time.

- ❌ {f|旅行|りょこう}で{f|美味|おい}しい<b>{f|思|おも}いをした</b>。 → ✅ {f|旅行|りょこう}で{f|美味|おい}しいものをたくさん{f|食|た}べた。
  The adjective must describe how an experience felt emotionally, not a property
  of a thing. Taste, price and size do not go in the slot.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}はみんなで<b>いい{f|思|おも}いをしましょう</b>。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}はみんなで{f|楽|たの}しみましょう。
  いい{f|思|おも}いをする implies pocketing an advantage, so as an invitation it
  sounds like a proposal to profit at someone's expense.

## Practice

**1.** {f|終電|しゅうでん}を{f|逃|のが}して{f|知|し}らない{f|町|まち}で{f|一人|ひとり}になり、こわい____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

こわい<b>{f|思|おも}いをした</b>

The sentence narrates an episode the speaker lived through, which is exactly what
<code>{f|思|おも}いをする</code> is for, and the past form is the one that
almost always appears. こわかった alone would state the feeling;
こわい{f|思|おも}いをした frames the whole night as the experience.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: {f|説明|せつめい}が{f|足|た}りず、{f|参加者|さんかしゃ}の{f|皆様|みなさま}に{f|不安|ふあん}{f|思|おも}いをしました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|説明|せつめい}が{f|足|た}りず、{f|参加者|さんかしゃ}の{f|皆様|みなさま}に<b>{f|不安|ふあん}な{f|思|おも}いをさせてしまいました</b>。
"Our explanation was inadequate, and we caused the participants anxiety."

Two repairs. {f|不安|ふあん} is a な-adjective and needs its な. And the people who
felt the anxiety were the participants, not the speaker, so the verb must be the
causative させる - the standard shape of a Japanese apology.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|去年|きょねん}は{f|寂|さび}しかった。
(b) {f|去年|きょねん}は{f|寂|さび}しい{f|思|おも}いをした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports a state: last year, the speaker felt lonely. It says nothing about why
or about what happened.

(b) frames last year as something the speaker was *put through*. The phrase
implies circumstances - a move, an absence, being left out - and invites the
listener to ask about them. It also leans slightly towards the speaker having been
on the receiving end of events rather than simply having a mood, which is why
{f|思|おも}いをさせる works so naturally when someone else is responsible.

</details>
