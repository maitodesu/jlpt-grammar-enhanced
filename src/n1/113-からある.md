# ～からある

**Meaning** · a good X of them; no less than X - a floor put under a figure the speaker finds striking

`から` here is the から of "from": the amount **starts** at that figure and may well
be higher. What makes the pattern more than arithmetic is the admiration built into
it. <code>30キロからある{f|荷物|にもつ}</code> is not a measurement but a reaction -
that case must weigh thirty kilos, and look who is carrying it. It cannot be used
of a small amount, because there would be nothing to react to.

## Formation

A number and its counter come first. Which of the three endings you use depends on
what is being measured.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Weight, length, size, quantity | number + counter + からある | 30キロからある{f|荷物|にもつ} |
| Price | number + counter + からする | 5{f|万円|まんえん}からするワイン |
| Modifying a noun (esp. people) | number + counter + からの + Noun | 500{f|人|にん}からの{f|観客|かんきゃく} |
| As the predicate | …は + number + からある | その{f|石|いし}は50キロからある |
| Past | ～からあった／からした | 100{f|万円|まんえん}からした |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>からある</code> for how big, <code>からする</code> for how
much it cost, <code>からの</code> when the phrase is modifying a following noun.
Mixing the first two is the standard error:
❌<code>3{f|万円|まんえん}からある{f|時計|とけい}</code> →
✅<code>3{f|万円|まんえん}からする{f|時計|とけい}</code>.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Written, or spoken in a storytelling register: newspaper copy, reportage, a memoir,
an anecdote being told properly. It is not stiff - somebody describing a monstrous
fish or an unbelievable queue may well reach for it - but in ordinary conversation
the everyday emphatic is `も`: <code>30キロもある</code>.

Three things it insists on.

**The figure must be large.** Large for the thing, that is: two metres is large for
a fish, five centimetres is large for a pile of paper, and a million yen is large
for a watch. ❌<code>1キロからある{f|砂糖|さとう}</code> fails not because a kilo is
small in the abstract but because nobody is impressed by it.

**There must be a number.** ❌<code>たくさんからある</code> and
❌<code>{f|大勢|おおぜい}からの{f|人|ひと}</code> are impossible. The pattern puts a
floor under a *figure*, so a figure has to be there.

**The speaker is reacting.** A specification sheet does not say
<code>からある</code>; it says {f|以上|いじょう}. The moment the pattern appears,
somebody is impressed, appalled or showing off.

<code>からの</code> is the form to watch for, because it is the one that turns up in
headlines and opening paragraphs -
<code>{f|数百人|すうひゃくにん}からの{f|観客|かんきゃく}</code> - and because it
modifies a noun rather than standing as a predicate.

## Contrast

### ～からある vs ～も

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `も` reacts to an **exact figure**: it weighs all of thirty
kilos. `からある` reacts to a **minimum**: thirty at least, possibly more.

</div>

<pre>
このスーツケース、20キロ<b>も</b>あるよ。
This suitcase weighs twenty kilos!
</pre>

<pre>
20キロ<b>からある</b>スーツケースを{f|階段|かいだん}で{f|運|はこ}び{f|上|あ}げた。
He hauled a twenty-kilo-plus suitcase up the stairs.
</pre>

The first speaker has weighed it, or has just picked it up and is telling you. The
second is estimating from the outside and rounding downwards for safety. `も` is
also the one you can say to a friend; `からある` in that first sentence would sound
like a line of narration.

### ～からある vs ～{f|以上|いじょう}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> is **neutral and precise**: X or
more, and X counts. `からある` is **subjective**: X at the very least, and the
speaker is impressed.

</div>

<pre>
{f|体重|たいじゅう}が20キロ<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>の{f|犬|いぬ}は{f|預|あず}かれません。
We cannot take dogs weighing twenty kilos or more.
</pre>

<pre>
20キロ<b>からある</b>{f|大型犬|おおがたけん}を{f|二頭|にとう}{f|飼|か}っている。
He keeps two big dogs, twenty kilos each if they're an ounce.
</pre>

The first is a rule, and rules need a threshold that can be applied by anyone. The
second is a description with an attitude in it. Put `からある` into a set of
regulations and it reads as though the drafter were marvelling at the dogs.

### ～からある vs ～ほど・～くらい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ほど` and `くらい` give an **approximation**: about that much,
maybe less. `からある` gives a **floor**: that much at minimum.

</div>

<pre>
{f|荷物|にもつ}は20キロ<b>くらい</b>あった。
The luggage weighed about twenty kilos.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|荷物|にもつ}は20キロ<b>からあった</b>。
The luggage was twenty kilos if it was a gram.
</pre>

They point in opposite directions from the same number. `くらい` allows eighteen;
`からある` does not, and hints at twenty-five. If your English has "about", you want
`くらい`; if it has "no less than", "a good", or "and more", you want `からある`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|時計|とけい}は50{f|万円|まんえん}<b>からある</b>。 → ✅ この{f|時計|とけい}は50{f|万円|まんえん}<b>からする</b>。
  Money takes `からする`. `からある` is for how much there is of something, not for
  what it costs.

- ❌ <b>たくさんからの</b>{f|人|ひと}が{f|集|あつ}まった。 → ✅ <b>3000{f|人|にん}からの</b>{f|人|ひと}が{f|集|あつ}まった。
  The pattern needs an actual figure. With a vague quantifier there is no floor to
  put under anything.

- ❌ {f|砂糖|さとう}を1キロ<b>からある</b>{f|買|か}ってきた。 → ✅ {f|砂糖|さとう}を1キロ<b>も</b>{f|買|か}ってきた。
  Two faults: a kilo of sugar is not an impressive quantity, and `からある` modifies
  a noun or stands as a predicate - it cannot sit adverbially in front of a verb
  like this.

- ❌ 500{f|人|にん}<b>からある</b>{f|観客|かんきゃく}が{f|詰|つ}めかけた。 → ✅ 500{f|人|にん}<b>からの</b>{f|観客|かんきゃく}が{f|詰|つ}めかけた。
  People take いる, not ある, so the ある form jars when the noun counted is human.
  For crowds the idiom is `からの`.

## Practice

**1.** {f|台風|たいふう}で、{f|樹齢|じゅれい}{f|百年|ひゃくねん}の{f|木|き}が{f|倒|たお}れ、{f|太|ふと}さ1メートル____{f|幹|みき}が{f|道|みち}をふさいだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1メートル<b>からある</b>{f|幹|みき}

A trunk a metre thick and more, described by someone who saw it. The figure is
large for the thing, and the phrase modifies a following noun - the two conditions
the pattern needs.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|絵|え}は{f|画廊|がろう}で300{f|万円|まんえん}（からある・からする）そうだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

300{f|万円|まんえん}<b>からする</b>そうだ

A price, so `からする`. `からある` would be talking about how much painting there
is, which is not a thing anyone measures in yen.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Two hundred people and more were queuing outside the hall before dawn."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|夜明|よあ}け{f|前|まえ}から、200{f|人|にん}<b>からの</b>{f|人|ひと}が{f|会場|かいじょう}の{f|外|そと}に{f|並|なら}んでいた。

"And more" after a round number is the giveaway, and because the phrase modifies
{f|人|ひと} the form wanted is `からの` rather than `からある`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会員|かいいん}が10{f|人|にん}からある{f|場合|ばあい}は、{f|部屋|へや}を{f|無料|むりょう}でお{f|使|つか}いいただけます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会員|かいいん}が10{f|人|にん}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>の{f|場合|ばあい}は、{f|部屋|へや}を{f|無料|むりょう}でお{f|使|つか}いいただけます。

A condition in a set of terms needs a threshold anyone can apply, and ten is not a
figure to marvel at. {f|以上|いじょう} is neutral and precise, which is exactly what
a rule requires.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|荷物|にもつ}は30キロくらいありそうだ。
(b) {f|荷物|にもつ}は30キロからありそうだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is an estimate around thirty: it might turn out to be twenty-six, and the
speaker would not be surprised.

(b) sets thirty as the lower bound and expects to be proved right on the high side.
It also carries a reaction the first sentence does not - the speaker is weighing up
whether anyone can lift the thing.

</details>
