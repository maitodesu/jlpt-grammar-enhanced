# ～も顧みず / ～を顧みず

**Meaning** · without a backward glance at; regardless of - the subject sets aside something that genuinely had a claim on their attention, and presses on

{f|顧|かえり}みる is "to look back at, to give thought to". The negative
<code>{f|顧|かえり}みず</code> therefore says the person did not turn their head at
all. What they did not turn their head towards is never trivial: their own safety,
their health, their reputation, their family. That is why the same phrase can read
as heroism in one sentence and as neglect in the next - it depends entirely on who
was going to be hurt.

## Formation

A noun goes in front, marked with `を` or `も`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (plain object) | Noun + を{f|顧|かえり}みず | {f|危険|きけん} → {f|危険|きけん}を{f|顧|かえり}みず |
| Noun (emphatic) | Noun + も{f|顧|かえり}みず | {f|身|み}の{f|危険|きけん} → {f|身|み}の{f|危険|きけん}も{f|顧|かえり}みず |
| With に | ～も{f|顧|かえり}みずに | {f|採算|さいさん}を{f|顧|かえり}みずに |
| Modifying a noun | ～を{f|顧|かえり}みない + Noun | {f|家庭|かてい}を{f|顧|かえり}みない{f|父親|ちちおや} |
| Ending a sentence | ～を{f|顧|かえり}みなかった | {f|忠告|ちゅうこく}を{f|顧|かえり}みなかった |
| Longer variant | ～を{f|顧|かえり}みることなく | {f|批判|ひはん}を{f|顧|かえり}みることなく |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `を` is the ordinary object marker; `も` replaces it and adds
*not even this*. That is why the emphatic set phrases -
<code>{f|身|み}の{f|危険|きけん}も{f|顧|かえり}みず</code>,
<code>{f|世間体|せけんてい}も{f|顧|かえり}みず</code> - take `も`. Both particles
occur with the same nouns, and the choice is one of weight, not of grammar.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - two verbs are read かえりみる. {f|顧|かえり}みる is *to look back at
something outside yourself*, and it is the kanji this pattern uses.
{f|省|かえり}みる is *to examine your own conduct* -
{f|我|わ}が{f|身|み}を{f|省|かえり}みる. They overlap in the bare sense of looking
back, but for self-reflection the standard spelling is {f|省|かえり}みる.

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is **written, formal Japanese**. It belongs to news copy, eulogies, citations
for bravery, biography, court reporting and the more serious kind of criticism. It
is not archaic - the verb {f|顧|かえり}みる is current - but the bare `ず` negative
is a literary connective, and in conversation the same ideas come out as
<code>{f|危|あぶ}ないのも{f|気|き}にせず</code> or
<code>{f|家族|かぞく}をほったらかしにして</code>.

Which way it reads depends on **who suffers**. When what is set aside is the
subject's own stake - their safety, their life, their good name - the sentence
salutes them, and {f|身|み}の{f|危険|きけん}も{f|顧|かえり}みず is close to a fixed
phrase in reports of rescues. When what is set aside is an obligation owed to
somebody else - a family, a workforce, a set of accounts - the same words become an
accusation. {f|家庭|かてい}を{f|顧|かえり}みない is one of the standard Japanese
criticisms of a certain kind of working life.

Because of that weight, the phrase does not scale down. Nobody writes
❌<code>{f|眠気|ねむけ}を{f|顧|かえり}みず</code>: sleepiness is not something you owe
anything to. And, as with most Japanese praise formulas, applying it to yourself
sounds like a boast rather than a report.

## Contrast

### ～も{f|顧|かえり}みず vs ～をものともせず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `をものともせず` is about an **obstacle in the way** that failed
to stop them. <code>{f|顧|かえり}みず</code> is about **something of their own** they
left behind - and it may be something they should not have.

</div>

<pre>
{f|吹雪|ふぶき}<b>をものともせず</b>、{f|隊|たい}は{f|尾根|おね}を{f|越|こ}えた。
Undaunted by the blizzard, the party crossed the ridge.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|身|み}の{f|危険|きけん}も<b>{f|顧|かえり}みず</b>、{f|隊|たい}は{f|尾根|おね}を{f|越|こ}えた。
With no thought for their own safety, the party crossed the ridge.
</pre>

The blizzard is out there, pushing back; their safety is theirs, and they put it
down. That is why `をものともせず` is unmixed admiration while
<code>{f|顧|かえり}みず</code> can carry an undertone of *they should not have had
to* - or, with a different object, of *they had no business doing that*.
`をものともせず` has no critical use at all.

### ～も{f|顧|かえり}みず vs ～もかまわず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `もかまわず` is not minding something **in the immediate
surroundings**, and it is everyday language. <code>{f|顧|かえり}みず</code> is not
minding a **duty or a danger**, and it is formal.

</div>

<pre>
{f|服|ふく}が{f|汚|よご}れるの<b>もかまわず</b>、{f|彼|かれ}はしゃがみ{f|込|こ}んで{f|猫|ねこ}を{f|抱|だ}き{f|上|あ}げた。
Never mind his clothes, he crouched down and picked the cat up.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|評判|ひょうばん}を<b>{f|顧|かえり}みず</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|内部|ないぶ}の{f|不正|ふせい}を{f|告発|こくはつ}した。
Careless of his own reputation, he blew the whistle on the wrongdoing inside.
</pre>

Getting your trousers dirty is a small thing you decline to fuss about; your
reputation is a serious asset you decide to spend. Slotting a trivial noun into
<code>{f|顧|かえり}みず</code> sounds pompous, and slotting a grave one into
`もかまわず` sounds flippant.

### ～も{f|顧|かえり}みず vs ～をよそに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - who owns the thing being ignored. `をよそに` ignores **other
people's** worry or objection. <code>{f|顧|かえり}みず</code> ignores something that
is the subject's **own** - their safety, their name, their household.

</div>

<pre>
{f|母|はは}の{f|反対|はんたい}<b>をよそに</b>、{f|兄|あに}は{f|山|やま}へ{f|向|む}かった。
Ignoring our mother's objections, my brother set off for the mountains.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|体調|たいちょう}も<b>{f|顧|かえり}みず</b>、{f|兄|あに}は{f|山|やま}へ{f|向|む}かった。
Giving no thought to how unwell he was, my brother set off for the mountains.
</pre>

Both sentences describe the same departure, and they blame different things. The
first has the brother overriding somebody; the second has him overriding himself.
A useful test: if you can put a possessor like {f|周囲|しゅうい}の or {f|親|おや}の
in front of the noun, `をよそに` is available; if the noun is
{f|自分|じぶん}の something, it is <code>{f|顧|かえり}みず</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|危険|きけん}に<b>{f|顧|かえり}みず</b> → ✅ {f|危険|きけん}<b>を{f|顧|かえり}みず</b>（or {f|危険|きけん}<b>も{f|顧|かえり}みず</b>）
  {f|顧|かえり}みる takes a direct object. The particle is `を`, or `も` for
  emphasis - never `に`.

- ❌ {f|大雨|おおあめ}を<b>{f|顧|かえり}みず</b>、{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}した。 → ✅ {f|大雨|おおあめ}<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}した。
  Rain is not something you owe consideration to. Only a duty, a risk or a stake of
  your own can go in front of the verb.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|家族|かぞく}を<b>{f|顧|かえり}みず</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|家族|かぞく}を<b>{f|顧|かえり}みなかった</b>。
  `ず` is a connective and cannot close a sentence. Finish with
  <code>{f|顧|かえり}みない</code> or <code>{f|顧|かえり}みなかった</code>.

- ❌ {f|自分|じぶん}の{f|行動|こうどう}を<b>{f|顧|かえり}みて</b>{f|反省|はんせい}した。 → ✅ {f|自分|じぶん}の{f|行動|こうどう}を<b>{f|省|かえり}みて</b>{f|反省|はんせい}した。
  For examining your **own conduct**, the standard kanji is {f|省|かえり}みる. Same
  reading, different character, and editors do correct it.

## Practice

**1.** {f|消防士|しょうぼうし}は{f|身|み}の{f|危険|きけん}____、{f|煙|けむり}の{f|中|なか}へ{f|飛|と}び{f|込|こ}んだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|身|み}の{f|危険|きけん}<b>も{f|顧|かえり}みず</b>、{f|煙|けむり}の{f|中|なか}へ{f|飛|と}び{f|込|こ}んだ。

What is set aside is the firefighter's own safety, so the sentence reads as praise.
<code>{f|身|み}の{f|危険|きけん}も{f|顧|かえり}みず</code> with `も` is close to
fixed in reports of this kind.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|人目|ひとめ}（もかまわず・も{f|顧|かえり}みず）、{f|彼女|かのじょ}はホームで{f|泣|な}き{f|続|つづ}けた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|人目|ひとめ}<b>もかまわず</b>、{f|彼女|かのじょ}はホームで{f|泣|な}き{f|続|つづ}けた。

Being seen is a matter of the immediate surroundings, not a duty or a danger, and
the scene is everyday. <code>{f|顧|かえり}みず</code> would make weeping on a
platform sound like an act of self-sacrifice.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He worked on with no regard for his own health, and collapsed in the end."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|健康|けんこう}を<b>{f|顧|かえり}みず</b>{f|働|はたら}き{f|続|つづ}け、ついに{f|倒|たお}れてしまった。

The thing disregarded is his own body, which is exactly the noun class this pattern
wants, and the `を` marks it as a plain object. Note the double edge: the sentence
sounds devoted and reckless at the same time, and that ambiguity is native to the
phrase.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|社長|しゃちょう}は{f|社員|しゃいん}の{f|生活|せいかつ}を{f|顧|かえり}みず。{f|工場|こうじょう}を{f|閉|と}じた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|社長|しゃちょう}は{f|社員|しゃいん}の{f|生活|せいかつ}を<b>{f|顧|かえり}みず</b>、{f|工場|こうじょう}を{f|閉|と}じた。
"With no thought for his employees' livelihoods, the president closed the plant."

The full stop is the error. `ず` links one clause to the next, so it takes a comma;
a full stop leaves the first half hanging. Note also that the object here is other
people's living, so the sentence is an accusation, not a tribute.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|危険|きけん}を<b>{f|顧|かえり}みず</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|海|うみ}に{f|飛|と}び{f|込|こ}んだ。
(b) {f|波|なみ}の{f|高|たか}さ<b>をものともせず</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|海|うみ}に{f|飛|と}び{f|込|こ}んだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) puts the risk on his side of the ledger. He had something to lose - his life -
and he chose not to weigh it. Readers hear courage, and a shadow of recklessness
behind it.

(b) puts the waves on the other side of the ledger, as an opponent that failed to
impress him. There is no shadow: `をものともせず` only ever admires. If the sentence
is going into a citation for bravery, (a) is the one that acknowledges what he
risked.

</details>
