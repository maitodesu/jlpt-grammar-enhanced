# とはいうものの

**Meaning** · having said that; be that as it may - the speaker repeats a claim already on the table, allows it, and then reports the part of reality that does not match it

Read it literally and it explains itself: `と` (that) + `は` (as for) +
{f|言|い}う (say) + `ものの` (although). *Although one says that.* Something has been
asserted - by the speaker a moment ago, by an official notice, by common opinion -
and this word picks the assertion up whole before setting the awkward facts beside
it.

## Usage

Two positions, both very common.

- **Opening a sentence**, after your own preceding statement, followed by a comma.
  This is the shape you will meet most often:
  {f|準備|じゅんび}は{f|整|ととの}った。とはいうものの、{f|不安|ふあん}は{f|残|のこ}る。
- **After a plain clause**, joining the two halves inside one sentence:
  {f|便利|べんり}だとはいうものの、{f|値段|ねだん}が{f|高|たか}すぎる。

い-adjectives and verbs go in plain, with no `だ` - ❌{f|安|やす}いだとはいうものの.
Nouns and な-adjectives usually keep the `だ`, and a bare noun in front of it is
possible but is the slot where `とはいえ` is the idiomatic choice.

What follows is always a **statement of how things stand**, and it must undercut the
claim rather than agree with it. If nothing has been asserted, there is nothing for
`とはいうものの` to concede, and the sentence will read as though its first half has
gone missing.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|夏|なつ}は{f|終|お}わった。<b>とはいうものの</b>、{f|暑|あつ}さは{f|続|つづ}いている。
Summer is over. That said, the heat is still with us.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|便利|べんり}だ<b>とはいうものの</b>、この{f|機能|きのう}は{f|誰|だれ}も{f|使|つか}っていない。
Handy, they say - but nobody actually uses this feature.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大学|だいがく}に{f|入|はい}った<b>とはいうものの</b>、{f|授業|じゅぎょう}にはほとんど{f|出|で}ていないらしい。
He got into university, apparently, though he hardly ever goes to lectures.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|規則|きそく}で{f|禁止|きんし}されている<b>とはいうものの</b>、{f|実際|じっさい}に{f|守|まも}っている{f|部署|ぶしょ}はほとんどない。
The rules forbid it, true enough, but there is barely a department that observes them.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This belongs to **considered language**: essays, columns, reports, the reflective
middle of a business email. It also survives in careful speech - a presentation, an
interview, a person thinking aloud on the radio - because it buys the speaker a
moment while they turn the argument round. Among friends the same move is made with
`そうはいっても`, `とはいっても` or a plain `でも`.

Roughly, and only roughly: `とはいえ` reads as the most written of the family,
`とはいうものの` a step less so, `とはいっても` a step less again, and
`そうはいっても` is at home in speech. Treat that as a tendency; all four turn up
in print and none of them is wrong out loud.

The tone is honest rather than adversarial. Because the speaker has just conceded
the point in full, the objection that follows lands as fairness, not as
contradiction - which is why writers reach for it when they are about to complicate
their own argument. Overused, it starts to sound as though the writer cannot commit
to anything.

## Contrast

### とはいうものの vs ～ものの

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ものの` attaches to the fact itself. `とはいうものの` attaches
to the **claim about** the fact - somebody said it, and the speaker is quoting it
back before dissenting.

</div>

<pre>
{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}んだ<b>ものの</b>、{f|返事|へんじ}は{f|来|こ}なかった。
I applied, but no reply ever came.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|誰|だれ}でも{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}める<b>とはいうものの</b>、{f|条件|じょうけん}は{f|細|こま}かい。
Anyone may apply, so they say - but the conditions are fiddly.
</pre>

In the first sentence the applying simply happened; the speaker reports it and then
reports the shortfall. In the second, "anyone may apply" is somebody's line - a
poster, a website, a recruiter - and the sentence sets it against the small print.
That quoting layer is what the extra <code>とは{f|言|い}う</code> adds, and it is why
`とはいうものの` can stand alone at the head of a sentence while bare `ものの` cannot.

### とはいうものの vs そうはいっても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same sentence in two registers. `そうはいっても` is what you
say; `とはいうものの` is what you write.

</div>

<pre>
{f|節約|せつやく}しないとね。<b>そうはいっても</b>、{f|外食|がいしょく}はやめられないよ。
We ought to economise. Even so, I can't give up eating out.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|節約|せつやく}が{f|必要|ひつよう}である。<b>とはいうものの</b>、{f|生活|せいかつ}の{f|質|しつ}を{f|落|お}とすわけにはいかない。
Economising is necessary. That said, we cannot let our standard of living slip.
</pre>

`そうはいっても` points back with そう, so it needs the previous remark to be
physically there in the conversation - it is a reply. `とはいうものの` can look back
at a whole paragraph, or at an opinion nobody in the room has actually voiced, which
is exactly what a written argument needs.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|安|やす}いだ<b>とはいうものの</b>、{f|質|しつ}が{f|悪|わる}い。 → ✅ {f|安|やす}い<b>とはいうものの</b>、{f|質|しつ}が{f|悪|わる}い。
  `と` is the quotative particle and takes a plain form. い-adjectives never carry
  `だ`.

- ❌ それは{f|言|い}い<b>とはいうものの</b>だ。 → ✅ それは{f|言|い}い{f|過|す}ぎ<b>というものだ</b>。
  <code>というものだ</code> ("that is what you would call…") is a different
  expression with no concessive force. The `は` is what makes this one concessive.

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>ものの</b>、{f|責任|せきにん}はある。 → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}だ<b>とはいうものの</b>、{f|責任|せきにん}はある。
  Bare `ものの` cannot sit straight on a noun; it needs `である`, or the whole
  `とはいうものの` frame.

- ❌ <b>とはいうものの</b>、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るそうです。（as the first thing said） → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るそうです。
  With nothing asserted beforehand, there is nothing to concede. The word is a
  reply, even when the thing it replies to is the writer's own last sentence.

## Practice

**1.** {f|給料|きゅうりょう}は{f|悪|わる}くない。____、{f|残業|ざんぎょう}が{f|多|おお}すぎて{f|続|つづ}けられない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>とはいうものの</b>、{f|残業|ざんぎょう}が{f|多|おお}すぎて{f|続|つづ}けられない。

The speaker has just granted that the pay is decent and now takes the job apart on
other grounds. A concession already made, then the reality that spoils it, is
exactly this word's shape.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural expression for a message to a close friend:
{f|禁煙|きんえん}すると{f|決|き}めたんだ。（とはいうものの・そうはいっても）、{f|一本|いっぽん}くらいいいよね。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>そうはいっても</b>、{f|一本|いっぽん}くらいいいよね。

The register is casual - `んだ`, `よね`, a friend - and そう points straight back at
what was just said. `とはいうものの` would read as though the speaker were writing a
column about their own smoking.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|海|うみ}が{f|近|ちか}い<b>ものの</b>、{f|泳|およ}げる{f|場所|ばしょ}はない。
(b) {f|海|うみ}が{f|近|ちか}い<b>とはいうものの</b>、{f|泳|およ}げる{f|場所|ばしょ}はない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports two facts about the place from the speaker's own observation: the sea is
close by, and there is nowhere to swim. It is a flat, slightly deflated description.

(b) treats "the sea is close by" as somebody's selling point - an estate agent, a
brochure, a friend who recommended the town - and answers it. The listener hears a
claim being tested, and a faint note of the speaker having been oversold.

</details>
