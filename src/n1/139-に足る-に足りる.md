# ～に足る / ～に足りる

**Meaning** · worth doing; enough to deserve it - the thing clears the bar that the action demands

{f|足|た}る and {f|足|た}りる both mean "to suffice". Put after an action, they say
that something is **good enough to justify** that action: worth trusting, worth
respecting, worth examining. This is not a statement that you have enough of
something - it is a verdict that the thing has earned the action.

## Formation

{f|足|た}る is the older う-verb form of {f|足|た}りる (a る-verb). Both are current
in this pattern; {f|足|た}る is the stiffer of the two.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + に{f|足|た}る | {f|信頼|しんらい}する → {f|信頼|しんらい}するに{f|足|た}る |
| Verb | dictionary form + に{f|足|た}りる | {f|尊敬|そんけい}する → {f|尊敬|そんけい}するに{f|足|た}りる |
| Noun (する-noun) | noun + に{f|足|た}る | {f|信頼|しんらい} → {f|信頼|しんらい}に{f|足|た}る |
| Modifying a noun | ～に{f|足|た}る + noun | {f|尊敬|そんけい}に{f|足|た}る{f|人物|じんぶつ} |
| As a predicate | ～に{f|足|た}るものだ | {f|評価|ひょうか}するに{f|足|た}るものだ |
| Negative | ～に{f|足|た}りない ／ ～に{f|足|た}らない | {f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}らない |
| Polite | ～に{f|足|た}ります ／ ～に{f|足|た}りません | {f|検討|けんとう}に{f|足|た}ります |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the two verbs have separate negatives. {f|足|た}りる gives
<code>{f|足|た}りない</code>; {f|足|た}る is an う-verb, so it gives
<code>{f|足|た}らない</code>, never ❌<code>{f|足|た}るない</code>. Both negatives
are used with this pattern.

</div>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

Formal and almost entirely **written**: reports, reviews, editorials, court
language, references and speeches of appreciation. In speech it appears only in
prepared remarks. The everyday equivalent is
<code>{f|価値|かち}がある</code> or <code>{f|十分|じゅうぶん}だ</code>.

The verbs that go in front are a narrow, evaluative set:
{f|信頼|しんらい}する, {f|尊敬|そんけい}する, {f|評価|ひょうか}する,
{f|検討|けんとう}する, {f|賞賛|しょうさん}する, {f|注目|ちゅうもく}する,
{f|信|しん}じる, {f|語|かた}る. All of them are acts of judgement. An ordinary
preference does not fit the slot: ❌この{f|店|みせ}は{f|行|い}くに{f|足|た}る is
not Japanese, it is a translation of "worth going to".

Two shapes carry most of the real usage. One is the noun-modifying frame
<code>{f|信頼|しんらい}に{f|足|た}る{f|情報|じょうほう}</code>, "information one
can rely on", which is standard in journalism. The other is the negative -
<code>{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}らない</code>, "trivial" - which is commoner than any
affirmative use and is treated as a pattern in its own right.

Between the two verbs, {f|足|た}る leans literary and fixed and {f|足|た}りる
leans slightly plainer; in the set phrases ({f|信頼|しんらい}に{f|足|た}る,
{f|取|と}るに{f|足|た}らない) only {f|足|た}る sounds right.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|足|た}る vs ～にたえる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|足|た}る</code> says the thing has **earned** the
action. `にたえる` says the thing **survives** the action - it holds up when you
actually look, listen or read.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|論文|ろんぶん}は{f|引用|いんよう}する<b>に{f|足|た}る</b>{f|内容|ないよう}を{f|備|そな}えている。
This paper has enough substance to be worth citing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|素人|しろうと}の{f|作品|さくひん}だが、{f|十分|じゅうぶん}{f|鑑賞|かんしょう}<b>にたえる</b>。
It's an amateur piece, but it holds up perfectly well as something to look at.
</pre>

The first is a verdict on merit; the second is about withstanding scrutiny. Where
they overlap - a book worth reading - both are possible, but `にたえる` implies
the thing survives a demanding audience, while <code>に{f|足|た}る</code> implies
it deserves that audience's time in the first place.

### ～に{f|足|た}る vs ～の{f|価値|かち}がある

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same idea, different room.
<code>{f|価値|かち}がある</code> is what you say out loud;
<code>に{f|足|た}る</code> is what you write in the report.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|映画|えいが}は{f|一度|いちど}{f|見|み}る<b>{f|価値|かち}がある</b>よ。
This film is worth seeing once.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|当該|とうがい}{f|事業|じぎょう}は{f|継続|けいぞく}する<b>に{f|足|た}る</b>と{f|判断|はんだん}した。
We concluded that the project in question merits continuation.
</pre>

<code>{f|価値|かち}がある</code> also takes anything at all as its subject - a
film, a trip, a coffee. <code>に{f|足|た}る</code> stays with things that are
being formally assessed.

### ～に{f|足|た}る vs ～だけの

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だけの` measures an **amount** that is sufficient.
<code>に{f|足|た}る</code> passes a **judgement** that something is worthy.

</div>

<pre>
{f|家族|かぞく}を{f|養|やしな}う<b>だけの</b>{f|収入|しゅうにゅう}はある。
I earn enough to support a family.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|家族|かぞく}を{f|養|やしな}う<b>に{f|足|た}る</b>{f|覚悟|かくご}が{f|君|きみ}にあるのか。
Do you have the resolve that supporting a family deserves?
</pre>

Money is counted, so `だけの` fits it. Resolve is assessed, so
<code>に{f|足|た}る</code> fits that. Swapping them makes the income sound noble
and the resolve sound measurable.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|信頼|しんらい}に<b>{f|足|た}るない</b> → ✅ {f|信頼|しんらい}に<b>{f|足|た}らない</b>
  {f|足|た}る is an う-verb, so its negative is {f|足|た}らない, exactly like
  {f|取|と}る → {f|取|と}らない. (The る-verb {f|足|た}りる gives
  {f|足|た}りない, which is also correct here.)

- ❌ {f|信頼|しんらい}に{f|足|た}りる<b>の</b>{f|人|ひと} → ✅ {f|信頼|しんらい}に<b>{f|足|た}りる</b>{f|人|ひと}
  This is a verb modifying a noun directly. No の comes between them.

- ❌ この{f|店|みせ}のラーメンは{f|食|た}べる<b>に{f|足|た}る</b>。 → ✅ この{f|店|みせ}のラーメンは{f|食|た}べる<b>{f|価値|かち}がある</b>。
  The pattern is for formal evaluation, not for recommending lunch. English
  "worth ～ing" covers both; Japanese does not.

- ❌ この{f|案|あん}は{f|検討|けんとう}する<b>に{f|足|た}るです</b>。 → ✅ この{f|案|あん}は{f|検討|けんとう}する<b>に{f|足|た}ります</b>。
  {f|足|た}る is a verb, so です cannot follow it. Use {f|足|た}ります, or
  <code>に{f|足|た}るものです</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|複数|ふくすう}の{f|資料|しりょう}で{f|裏付|うらづ}けられており、{f|信頼|しんらい}____{f|情報|じょうほう}だと{f|言|い}える。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|信頼|しんらい}<b>に{f|足|た}る</b>{f|情報|じょうほう}

The frame <code>{f|信頼|しんらい}に{f|足|た}る + noun</code> is the standard way
to say "information one can rely on" in written Japanese, and the corroboration
mentioned first is exactly the ground such a judgement rests on.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|旅費|りょひ}を{f|払|はら}う（に{f|足|た}る・だけの）{f|貯金|ちょきん}がやっとできた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|旅費|りょひ}を{f|払|はら}う<b>だけの</b>{f|貯金|ちょきん}

Savings are an amount, and the sentence is counting whether there is enough of
them. <code>に{f|足|た}る</code> would turn a bank balance into a moral verdict
on the trip.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "There is not yet enough evidence to warrant reopening the investigation."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|捜査|そうさ}を{f|再開|さいかい}する<b>に{f|足|た}る</b>{f|証拠|しょうこ}はまだない。

"Enough to warrant" is the core sense of the pattern, and an official judgement
about whether a threshold has been met is exactly the register it belongs to.
<code>に{f|足|た}りる</code> would also be grammatical, but {f|足|た}る is the
form used in this kind of writing.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}の{f|話|はなし}は{f|証拠|しょうこ}がなく、{f|信|しん}じるに{f|足|た}るないと{f|思|おも}う。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}の{f|話|はなし}は{f|証拠|しょうこ}がなく、{f|信|しん}じるに<b>{f|足|た}りない</b>と{f|思|おも}う。
"There's no evidence behind what he says, so I don't think it is worth believing."

❌{f|足|た}るない does not exist. Use either the る-verb negative
{f|足|た}りない or the う-verb negative {f|足|た}らない; both are current, and
{f|足|た}りない is the safer default.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|作品|さくひん}は{f|鑑賞|かんしょう}<b>にたえる</b>。
(b) この{f|作品|さくひん}は{f|鑑賞|かんしょう}する<b>に{f|足|た}る</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the work **holds up** when you actually sit down in front of it. It
often carries a faint concession - it may be an amateur piece, or an old one, but
it does not embarrass itself.

(b) says the work has **earned** your attention: it is good enough that looking
at it is justified. This is the language of a formal review, and it praises more
directly than (a) does.

</details>
