# ～なしに

**Meaning** · without; with no X at all - something went ahead although X, which should have come first, never happened

<code>なしに</code> is the noun {f|無|な}し with に. It attaches to a **noun** and says
the main action took place unaccompanied by it. What the pattern adds over a plain
negative is a faint charge of impropriety: the nouns that fill the slot -
{f|予告|よこく}, {f|断|ことわ}り, {f|許可|きょか}, {f|連絡|れんらく},
{f|説明|せつめい} - are things somebody was owed and did not get.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + なしに | {f|予告|よこく} → {f|予告|よこく}なしに |
| Noun (emphatic) | {f|何|なん}の + Noun + もなしに | {f|説明|せつめい} → {f|何|なん}の{f|説明|せつめい}もなしに |
| As a precondition | Noun + なしには + negative | {f|辞書|じしょ} → {f|辞書|じしょ}なしには{f|読|よ}めない |
| Before a noun | Noun + なしの + Noun | {f|予約|よやく} → {f|予約|よやく}なしの{f|来店|らいてん} |
| Clause | Verb (plain) + こと + なしに | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}むことなしに |
| Neutral counterpart | Noun + なしで | {f|砂糖|さとう} → {f|砂糖|さとう}なしで |
| Sentence-final | Noun + なしだ | {f|問題|もんだい} → {f|問題|もんだい}なしだ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>なしに</code> takes a noun and nothing else. To put a verb in front
of it you must nominalise first, which produces
<code>ことなしに</code> - a related but noticeably heavier pattern with rules of its
own. For a plain "without doing", ordinary
<code>～ないで</code> or <code>～ずに</code> is what a Japanese speaker reaches for.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|何|なん}の{f|連絡|れんらく}も<b>なしに</b>{f|休|やす}んだ。
He took the day off without a word to anyone.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|予告|よこく}<b>なしに</b>{f|料金|りょうきん}が{f|変|か}わることがあります。
Charges may change without notice.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|断|ことわ}り<b>なしに</b>{f|人|ひと}の{f|引|ひ}き{f|出|だ}しを{f|開|あ}けないでください。
Please don't open other people's drawers without asking.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

</pre>

<pre>
{f|専門|せんもん}{f|用語|ようご}が{f|多|おお}いので、{f|辞書|じしょ}<b>なしには</b>この{f|論文|ろんぶん}は{f|読|よ}めない。
There is so much jargon that you can't read this paper without a dictionary.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|挨拶|あいさつ}も<b>なしに</b>{f|帰|かえ}ってしまうなんて、いつもの{f|彼|かれ}らしくない。

</pre>

<pre>

The company decided to close the plant without explaining anything to its workforce.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>なしに</code> leans **written and formal**. Its natural homes are notices,
contracts, terms of service, warning signs and reported complaints - anywhere an
obligation was skipped and somebody is putting that on the record.
{f|予告|よこく}なしに and {f|許可|きょか}なしに are close to fixed expressions in
that kind of text.

It works in speech, but only where the speaker is being pointed. 「{f|何|なん}の
{f|説明|せつめい}もなしに{f|決|き}められた」 is a complaint, and it is heard as one.
For a neutral "he left without eating", speech uses
<code>{f|食|た}べないで</code> or <code>{f|食|た}べずに</code>, and
<code>なしに</code> would sound as though you were filing a grievance about the
missed meal.

The emphatic frame {f|何|なん}の…もなしに is worth learning as a unit. The も is
not optional decoration - it is what produces "not so much as a", and dropping it
leaves the sentence sounding half-built.

Register-wise, <code>なしで</code> is the everyday sibling and
<code>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</code> the casual one; <code>なしに</code> sits above both. In a
negative sentence, <code>なしには</code> and <code>なしでは</code> both occur and
overlap heavily - {f|辞書|じしょ}なしには{f|読|よ}めない and
{f|辞書|じしょ}なしでは{f|読|よ}めない are both good Japanese, with the first a
little more written.

## Contrast

### ～なしに vs ～ないで／～ずに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>なしに</code> takes a **noun**; <code>ないで</code> and
<code>ずに</code> take a **verb**. Where both are possible,
<code>なしに</code> is the more formal, and hints that the omission was improper.

</div>

<pre>
{f|朝|あさ}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べ<b>ないで</b>{f|出|で}かけた。
I went out without eating breakfast.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|上司|じょうし}への{f|報告|ほうこく}<b>なしに</b>{f|契約|けいやく}を{f|結|むす}んだ。
He signed the contract without reporting it to his manager.
</pre>

Notice that the first sentence is neutral - nobody is at fault for skipping
breakfast - and that swapping in {f|朝|あさ}ご{f|飯|はん}なしに would make it sound
oddly grave. The second could be rewritten as
{f|報告|ほうこく}しないで, which would soften it into a plain description of what
happened.

### ～なしに vs ～なしで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>なしで</code> is about doing without a **thing**: a tool,
an ingredient, a companion. <code>なしに</code> is about proceeding without an
**act** that ought to have come first.

</div>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|補助|ほじょ}{f|輪|りん}<b>なしで</b>{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}に{f|乗|の}れるようになった。
My son can ride a bike without training wheels now.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|住民|じゅうみん}の{f|同意|どうい}<b>なしに</b>{f|工事|こうじ}が{f|始|はじ}まった。
Construction started without the residents' consent.
</pre>

Reverse them and both go wrong in the same way. ❌{f|補助|ほじょ}{f|輪|りん}なしに
treats a piece of equipment as a missed procedure; ❌{f|同意|どうい}なしで is not
impossible, but it flattens a serious omission into a shrug. In negative sentences
the two draw much closer together, and there the choice is register alone.

### ～なしに vs ～{f|抜|ぬ}きで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</code> means X was **deliberately left
out**, usually as a choice the speaker approves of. <code>なしに</code> simply
reports that X did not happen.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}はお{f|世辞|せじ}<b>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</b>{f|話|はな}しましょう。
Let's talk today with the flattery left out.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|十分|じゅうぶん}な{f|検討|けんとう}<b>なしに</b>{f|結論|けつろん}が{f|出|だ}された。
A conclusion was reached without adequate consideration.
</pre>

<code>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</code> is casual and cheerful - ワサビ{f|抜|ぬ}きで,
{f|冗談|じょうだん}{f|抜|ぬ}きで - and it implies an agent choosing to omit
something. <code>なしに</code> is neutral about who chose, which is exactly why it
suits the language of complaints and contracts.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|説明|せつめい}する<b>なしに</b>{f|帰|かえ}った。 → ✅ {f|説明|せつめい}<b>なしに</b>{f|帰|かえ}った。
  A verb cannot sit in front of it. Use the bare noun, or nominalise:
  {f|説明|せつめい}することなしに.

- ❌ {f|砂糖|さとう}<b>なしに</b>コーヒーを{f|飲|の}む。 → ✅ {f|砂糖|さとう}<b>なしで</b>コーヒーを{f|飲|の}む。
  A missing ingredient is a thing, not a skipped obligation, so
  <code>なしで</code> is the fit.

- ❌ {f|予約|よやく}<b>なしに</b>のお{f|客|きゃく}{f|様|さま} → ✅ {f|予約|よやく}<b>なしの</b>お{f|客|きゃく}{f|様|さま}
  In front of a noun the form is <code>なしの</code>. に is for modifying a verb.

- ❌ {f|何|なん}の{f|連絡|れんらく}<b>なしに</b>{f|来|こ}なかった。 → ✅ {f|何|なん}の{f|連絡|れんらく}<b>もなしに</b>{f|来|こ}なかった。
  The emphatic frame is {f|何|なん}の…も…なしに. Without the も it reads as an
  unfinished sentence.

## Practice

**1.** {f|工事|こうじ}の{f|音|おと}がひどい。{f|管理人|かんりにん}から{f|何|なん}の____{f|始|はじ}まった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|何|なん}の{f|知|し}らせ<b>もなしに</b>{f|始|はじ}まった

The complaint frame is {f|何|なん}の + noun + も + なしに, and a notice from the
building manager is exactly the kind of owed act this pattern marks as missing.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|眼鏡|めがね}（なしに・なしで）{f|新聞|しんぶん}を{f|読|よ}んでいる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|眼鏡|めがね}<b>なしで</b>{f|新聞|しんぶん}を{f|読|よ}んでいる。

Glasses are a piece of equipment, not a procedure somebody skipped, so this is
<code>なしで</code>. <code>なしに</code> would imply she was supposed to put her
glasses on first and improperly failed to.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "They cut down the trees without consulting anyone in the neighbourhood."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|近所|きんじょ}の{f|人|ひと}への{f|相談|そうだん}<b>なしに</b>、{f|木|き}が{f|切|き}られてしまった。

{f|相談|そうだん} is an action-noun and consultation was owed here, which is the
core case for <code>なしに</code>. The passive plus しまう carries the speaker's
disapproval, matching the pattern's tone.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|社員|しゃいん}に{f|知|し}らせるなしに{f|制度|せいど}を{f|変|か}えた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|社員|しゃいん}に<b>{f|知|し}らせることなしに</b>{f|制度|せいど}を{f|変|か}えた。
（or {f|社員|しゃいん}への{f|通知|つうち}<b>なしに</b>）

<code>なしに</code> needs a noun. Either add <code>こと</code> to nominalise the
verb, or replace the verb with the corresponding noun, which is the more natural
choice in written Japanese.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|何|なに}も{f|言|い}わ<b>ずに</b>{f|部屋|へや}を{f|出|で}て{f|行|い}った。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|一言|ひとこと}の{f|説明|せつめい}も<b>なしに</b>{f|部屋|へや}を{f|出|で}て{f|行|い}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain narration. He said nothing and left; the sentence does not judge him,
and it could equally describe someone who was upset or simply in a hurry.

(b) states that an explanation was owed and was not given. The noun
{f|説明|せつめい}, the emphatic {f|一言|ひとこと}の…も, and the formality of
<code>なしに</code> all point the same way, and the listener hears a grievance.

</details>
