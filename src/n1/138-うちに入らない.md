# ～うちに入らない

**Meaning** · doesn't count as; can't be called that - the thing falls short of the category it is being put in

<code>うち</code> here is a *range*, the set of things a word covers. To say
<code>{f|料理|りょうり}のうちに{f|入|はい}らない</code> is to say the thing does not
get inside the boundary of "cooking" at all. This is not a judgement about quality on
a scale; it is a refusal of membership, and that bluntness is what the pattern is
for.

## Formation

A noun naming the category goes in front, linked with `の`. The verb is
{f|入|はい}る, and it is always negative.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (the category) | Noun + のうちに{f|入|はい}らない | {f|料理|りょうり} → {f|料理|りょうり}のうちに{f|入|はい}らない |
| With a concessive lead-in | ～くらいでは + Noun + のうちに{f|入|はい}らない | {f|歩|ある}くくらいでは{f|運動|うんどう}のうちに{f|入|はい}らない |
| Emphatic | Noun + のうちにも{f|入|はい}らない | {f|失敗|しっぱい}のうちにも{f|入|はい}らない |
| Polite | ～のうちに{f|入|はい}りません | {f|苦労|くろう}のうちに{f|入|はい}りません |
| Past | ～のうちに{f|入|はい}らなかった | {f|当時|とうじ}は{f|贅沢|ぜいたく}のうちに{f|入|はい}らなかった |
| Affirmative (rarer) | ～のうちに{f|入|はい}る | これも{f|節約|せつやく}のうちに{f|入|はい}る |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the reading is <b>はいらない</b>. {f|入|い}る and {f|入|はい}る are the
same character and different verbs; ❌<code>うちに{f|入|い}らない</code> is a reading
error, not a variant.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
こんなの、{f|料理|りょうり}の<b>うちに{f|入|はい}らない</b>よ。
This doesn't count as cooking.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}まで{f|歩|ある}くくらいでは、{f|運動|うんどう}の<b>うちに{f|入|はい}りません</b>。
Walking as far as the station hardly counts as exercise.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一泊|いっぱく}しただけでは、{f|旅行|りょこう}の<b>うちに{f|入|はい}らない</b>{f|気|き}がする。
One night away doesn't really feel like it counts as a trip.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}の{f|遅|おく}れなど、この{f|路線|ろせん}では{f|遅延|ちえん}の<b>うちに{f|入|はい}らない</b>。
On this line a thirty-minute delay does not even register as a delay.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}のミスは、{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|一件|いっけん}に{f|比|くら}べれば{f|失敗|しっぱい}の<b>うちにも{f|入|はい}らない</b>。
Compared with last year's business, his slip doesn't even begin to count as a failure.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}{f|練習|れんしゅう}しただけで{f|弾|ひ}けるようになったのなら、{f|苦労|くろう}の<b>うちに{f|入|はい}らない</b>。
If you could play it after a week's practice, that hardly counts as hard work.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}の{f|冬|ふゆ}を{f|知|し}っている{f|人|ひと}にとっては、{f|東京|とうきょう}の{f|寒|さむ}さなど{f|寒|さむ}さの<b>うちに{f|入|はい}らない</b>らしい。
For anyone who knows a Hokkaido winter, apparently Tokyo's cold does not count as cold at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|挨拶|あいさつ}と{f|天気|てんき}の{f|話|はなし}をしただけで、{f|込|こ}み{f|入|い}った{f|相談|そうだん}は{f|一切|いっさい}していないのだから、{f|面談|めんだん}の<b>うちに{f|入|はい}らない</b>だろう。
We exchanged greetings and talked about the weather and nothing more involved than that, so it can hardly count as a meeting.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The pattern is **spoken and unceremonious**. It lives in conversation, in blogs and
in dialogue; <code>{f|入|はい}りません</code> keeps it polite enough for the office,
but even then it stays informal in flavour and would look out of place in a report.
There the equivalent would be <code>とは{f|言|い}えない</code> or
<code>{f|該当|がいとう}しない</code>.

Its tone is **dismissive**, and who is being dismissed decides how it lands. Applied
to **yourself** it is modest and very common:
<code>{f|私|わたし}の{f|英語|えいご}なんて、{f|話|はな}せるうちに{f|入|はい}りません</code>
is a standard piece of self-deprecation and will be heard as good manners. Applied
to **your own hardship** it is stoic - the flood, the sixteen-hour day, whatever it
was, does not count next to what somebody else went through.

Applied to **another person's effort**, it is rude, and there is no polite ending
that fixes it. Telling a colleague that their all-nighter
<code>{f|苦労|くろう}のうちに{f|入|はい}りません</code> is not modesty transferred; it
is a straightforward insult. Native speakers know this and use the pattern about
others mainly in teasing between equals, or about things rather than people.

Two smaller points. <code>うちにも{f|入|はい}らない</code>, with the extra `も`,
hardens the refusal to "does not even begin to count". And the affirmative
<code>うちに{f|入|はい}る</code> does exist -
<code>{f|水|みず}やりも{f|世話|せわ}のうちに{f|入|はい}る</code> - but it is far less
common, and it is the negative that gets taught because the negative is what people
say.

## Contrast

### ～うちに{f|入|はい}らない vs ～とは{f|言|い}えない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both deny a label. `とは言えない` is measured and hedged;
`うちに入らない` is flat and colloquial.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|結果|けっか}は{f|成功|せいこう}<b>とは{f|言|い}えない</b>。
This result cannot be called a success.
</pre>

<pre>
あんなもの、{f|成功|せいこう}の<b>うちに{f|入|はい}らない</b>。
That doesn't count as a success at all.
</pre>

The first could go in a written review; it leaves room for the result to have been
partly good. The second closes the door and comes with an audible shrug. When you
need to be careful, `とは言えない` is the one.

### ～うちに{f|入|はい}らない vs ～に{f|過|す}ぎない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `に過ぎない` says the thing **is** X and no more.
`うちに入らない` says it is **not even** X.

</div>

<pre>
これは{f|一|ひと}つの{f|例|れい}<b>に{f|過|す}ぎない</b>。
This is no more than one example.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|例|れい}が{f|一|ひと}つでは、{f|証拠|しょうこ}の<b>うちに{f|入|はい}らない</b>。
A single example doesn't count as evidence.
</pre>

`に過ぎない` grants the category and limits the size of the claim inside it;
`うちに入らない` refuses the category outright. Choosing the wrong one flips how
generous the sentence sounds.

### ～うちに{f|入|はい}らない vs ～ほどではない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ほどではない` compares **degree**: not to that extent.
`うちに入らない` denies **membership**: not in that class at all.

</div>

<pre>
{f|痛|いた}いけれど、{f|病院|びょういん}に{f|行|い}く<b>ほどではない</b>。
It hurts, but not enough to go to hospital.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|程度|ていど}の{f|痛|いた}みは、けがの<b>うちに{f|入|はい}らない</b>。
Pain at this level doesn't count as an injury.
</pre>

`ほどではない` keeps the thing on the scale and places it low; `うちに入らない` takes
it off the scale. Note also that `ほどではない` follows a verb or an adjective, where
`うちに入らない` wants a noun plus `の`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ あんなのは{f|料理|りょうり}<b>うちに{f|入|はい}らない</b>。 → ✅ あんなのは{f|料理|りょうり}<b>のうちに{f|入|はい}らない</b>。
  <code>うち</code> is a noun, so the category in front of it links with `の`.

- ❌ うちに{f|入|い}らない → ✅ うちに<b>{f|入|はい}らない</b>
  The verb is {f|入|はい}る, "to go in", not {f|入|い}る. The same character spells
  both, and the wrong reading is the most audible error a learner can make with this
  pattern.

- ❌ {f|運動|うんどう}のうちに{f|入|はい}らない<b>だ</b>。 → ✅ {f|運動|うんどう}のうちに{f|入|はい}ら<b>ない</b>。
  The ending is already a verb. There is no copula to add.

- ❌ {f|先輩|せんぱい}の{f|徹夜|てつや}なんて、{f|苦労|くろう}のうちに<b>{f|入|はい}りませんね</b>。 → ✅ {f|徹夜|てつや}まで{f|大変|たいへん}でしたね。
  Grammatically impeccable and socially disastrous. Used about somebody else's
  effort, the polite ending does not soften it - it makes the dismissal sound
  deliberate.

## Practice

**1.** {f|近所|きんじょ}を{f|一周|いっしゅう}するだけでは、ジョギングの____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ジョギングの<b>うちに{f|入|はい}らない</b>。

The <code>だけでは</code> clause sets up something that falls short, and the pattern
denies it entry to the category. Note the `の` linking the category noun to
<code>うち</code>, and the reading はいらない.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|熱|ねつ}は{f|少|すこ}しあるが、{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|休|やす}む（ほどではない・うちに{f|入|はい}らない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|休|やす}む<b>ほどではない</b>。

The sentence measures a temperature against a threshold, which is a question of
degree. `うちに入らない` needs a category noun plus `の` in front of it and would have
nothing to attach to here.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, in casual speech: "Two people turning up doesn't count as a party."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|二人|ふたり}{f|集|あつ}まったくらいじゃ、パーティーの<b>うちに{f|入|はい}らないよ</b>。

<code>くらいじゃ</code> is the casual contraction of <code>くらいでは</code> and is
the natural lead-in for this pattern. Note the reading {f|二人|ふたり}, not
❌にじん.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|一日|いちにち}{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}の{f|散歩|さんぽ}は{f|運動|うんどう}うちに{f|入|い}らないだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|一日|いちにち}{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}の{f|散歩|さんぽ}は{f|運動|うんどう}<b>のうちに{f|入|はい}らない</b>。

Three faults in one short sentence: the missing `の` after the category noun, the
wrong reading of {f|入|はい}る, and a stray copula after a verb that is already the
predicate.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|私|わたし}の{f|中国語|ちゅうごくご}なんて、{f|話|はな}せる<b>うちに{f|入|はい}りません</b>。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|中国語|ちゅうごくご}なんて、{f|話|はな}せる<b>うちに{f|入|はい}りません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is modesty, and it is what a Japanese speaker would expect to hear after being
complimented on a language. The politeness and the self-deprecation reinforce each
other, and nobody takes it as a literal claim.

(b) is an insult. Turned on a third person, the same words become a verdict on
somebody else's ability, and the polite ending makes it worse rather than better -
it now sounds considered. If you want to say his Chinese is limited, use
<code>まだ{f|勉強|べんきょう}{f|中|ちゅう}だそうです</code> or something equally
indirect.

</details>
