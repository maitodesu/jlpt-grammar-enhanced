# ～やしない

**Meaning** · doesn't ... at all; certainly won't - a flat, blunt negative with feeling behind it

<code>やしない</code> is <code>はしない</code> with the は worn down to や, the same
softening that turns やはり into やっぱり. The meaning is an ordinary negative plus
emphasis and irritation: {f|分|わ}かりやしない is "he hasn't the first idea". It is a
**voice** rather than a register - blunt, informal, and noticeably dated. Recognise
it in fiction and say the plain negative yourself.

## Formation

The base is the **ます-stem**, as with <code>はしない</code>.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + やしない | {f|分|わ}かる → {f|分|わ}かりやしない |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + やしない | {f|食|た}べる → {f|食|た}べやしない |
| Verb (する) | し + やしない | {f|気|き}にする → {f|気|き}にしやしない |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | **き** + やしない | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|き}やしない |
| ある | ありやしない → **ありゃしない** | ある → ありゃしない |
| Further contraction | ～りやしない → ～りゃしない | {f|知|し}る → {f|知|し}りゃしない |
| い-adjective | ～く + もありゃしない | {f|痛|いた}い → {f|痛|いた}くもありゃしない |
| Neutral source form | ます-stem + はしない | {f|分|わ}かる → {f|分|わ}かりはしない |
| Modern equivalent | plain ～ない／～なんか～ない | {f|分|わ}かる → {f|分|わ}からない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - there is **no polite form**. ❌<code>やしません</code> does not
exist, and neither does a past ❌<code>やしなかった</code> in normal use. The
expression only appears in plain, casual speech, and that is a fact about its
character rather than a gap in the paradigm.

</div>

## Examples

These are the kinds of line the form really occurs in - dialogue in a novel, a manga
panel, a dubbed film, a comic monologue.

<pre>
そんなこと、おれは{f|知|し}り<b>やしない</b>よ。
I don't know a thing about it.
</pre>

<pre>
あいつの{f|話|はなし}なんか、{f|誰|だれ}も{f|信|しん}じ<b>やしない</b>。
Nobody's going to believe a word he says.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|待|ま}っていたって、どうせ{f|誰|だれ}も{f|来|き}<b>やしない</b>。
You can wait all you like - nobody's coming.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|金|かね}なんか、これっぽっちも<b>ありゃしない</b>。
Money? I haven't got two coins to rub together.
</pre>

<pre>
こんな{f|給料|きゅうりょう}じゃ、やって<b>られやしない</b>。
On wages like these, you can't be expected to carry on.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

"Are you all right?" "Fine. A scratch like this doesn't hurt a bit."
</pre>

Tell young people today about the old hardships and they won't understand a word of it.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**Read it; do not say it.** <code>やしない</code> is the emphatic negative of blunt,
informal speech, and in the modern language it survives mainly on the page and the
screen: manga, novels, {f|落語|らくご}, period drama, and the dubbing of foreign
films where a translator wants a character to sound gruff. Its associations run
towards older speakers, working-class {f|下町|したまち} dialogue, and male speech -
tendencies, not rules, and brash characters of any age or gender get given it. Used
by a learner in a real conversation it does not read as fluent; it reads as an
impression of a character.

The modern equivalents are ordinary. For a plain denial, use
<code>～ない</code>. For emphasis, <code>～なんか～ない</code>
（{f|知|し}らないよ, そんなの{f|信|しん}じないよ, {f|誰|だれ}も{f|来|こ}ないよ）or
<code>{f|全然|ぜんぜん}～ない</code>. All of these carry the same content with none of
the costume.

Two corners of the pattern are more alive than the rest.
<code>ありゃしない</code> is common in the frame
～ったらありゃしない - 「うるさいったらありゃしない」, "the noise, honestly" - which
adults do still use half-jokingly. And やってられやしない, "I can't be expected to
put up with this", turns up in genuinely irritated speech. Even these sit at the
theatrical end of casual, and neither is something to reach for at work.

The contraction ladder is worth being able to hear.
<code>はしない</code> is the neutral ancestor and is still perfectly usable;
<code>やしない</code> is the casual descendant; and where the stem ends in り, it
contracts once more to <code>りゃしない</code>
（{f|知|し}りゃしない, {f|分|わ}かりゃしない, ありゃしない）. Each step down is a step
rougher.

## Contrast

### ～やしない vs plain ～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same denial. <code>ない</code> is **what you say**;
<code>やしない</code> adds emphasis and a strongly marked, dated speaking voice.

</div>

<pre>
そんな{f|話|はなし}は{f|信|しん}じ<b>ない</b>よ。
I don't believe that.
</pre>

<pre>
そんな{f|話|はなし}、{f|信|しん}じ<b>やしない</b>よ。
I don't believe a word of that.
</pre>

The content is identical. What changes is the speaker: the first could be anyone, the
second is a character. If you want the emphasis without the costume,
{f|信|しん}じるもんか or {f|全然|ぜんぜん}{f|信|しん}じない will do the job in modern
speech.

### ～やしない vs ～はしない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>はしない</code> is the **neutral** emphatic negative and is
usable in real life, including in writing. <code>やしない</code> is its **casual and
rough** descendant.

</div>

<pre>
{f|心配|しんぱい}する{f|必要|ひつよう}はない。この{f|数字|すうじ}は{f|変|か}わり<b>はしない</b>。
There is no need to worry. These figures are not going to change.
</pre>

<pre>
どうせ{f|何|なに}も{f|変|か}わり<b>やしない</b>さ。
Nothing's going to change anyway.
</pre>

<code>はしない</code> is contrastive - it says "that particular thing, no" and often
implies something else is true. It is at home in careful prose and measured speech.
<code>やしない</code> keeps the emphasis but loses the composure, which is why the
second sentence needs the さ at the end to sound natural and the first does not.

### ～やしない vs ～ものか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>やしない</code> denies a **fact**: it isn't so.
<code>ものか</code> refuses a **course of action**: I'm certainly not going to.

</div>

<pre>
あんな{f|店|みせ}、{f|二度|にど}と{f|行|い}く<b>もんか</b>。
I am never setting foot in that place again.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|運|はこ}べ<b>やしない</b>よ、こんな{f|重|おも}いもの。
There's no carrying this on your own - it's far too heavy.
</pre>

Both are emphatic, both are casual, and both are marked. The difference is what they
deny. <code>ものか</code> is a declaration about the speaker's own will and is
therefore first-person by nature; <code>やしない</code> makes a claim about how
things are and can be said of anybody.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|分|わ}かる<b>やしない</b> → ✅ {f|分|わ}かり<b>やしない</b>
  The base is the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|来|こ}<b>やしない</b> → ✅ {f|来|き}<b>やしない</b>
  {f|来|く}る is irregular. Its ます-stem is き, as in {f|来|き}ます; こ belongs to
  {f|来|こ}ない.

- ❌ そのようなことは{f|存|ぞん}じ<b>やしません</b>。 → ✅ そのようなことは{f|存|ぞん}じ<b>ません</b>。
  There is no polite form. Attaching ます to a deliberately rough colloquialism
  produces something no speaker would say.

- ❌ （in a report）{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|対策|たいさく}では{f|効果|こうか}が{f|出|で}やしない。 → ✅ {f|今回|こんかい}の{f|対策|たいさく}では{f|効果|こうか}は{f|期待|きたい}できない。
  Fiction dialogue in a document. Written argument uses ordinary negatives, or
  <code>はしない</code> at most.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to meet this line, and what sort of speaker says it?
「あんな{f|奴|やつ}の{f|言|い}うことなんか、{f|信|しん}じ<b>やしない</b>」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In fiction - a manga panel, a novel, a film subtitle - and from a blunt, informal
speaker, often an older or deliberately rough-talking character.

The markers are the pejorative {f|奴|やつ}, the dismissive なんか, and
<code>やしない</code> itself. In real conversation a modern speaker would just say
{f|信|しん}じないよ.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in ordinary modern Japanese: {f|誰|だれ}も{f|手伝|てつだ}ってくれやしない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|誰|だれ}も{f|手伝|てつだ}って<b>くれない</b>。（or {f|誰|だれ}も{f|手伝|てつだ}ってなんかくれない）

The plain negative carries the whole meaning. If the complaint needs emphasis,
なんか or {f|全然|ぜんぜん} supplies it without the theatrical voice.

</details>

**3.** Fill the blank with the contracted form: そんな{f|昔|むかし}のこと、もう{f|覚|おぼ}えて____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|覚|おぼ}えて<b>やしない</b>（or {f|覚|おぼ}えちゃいない）

<code>やしない</code> attaches happily to a て-form plus い-verb here, since
{f|覚|おぼ}えている gives the stem {f|覚|おぼ}えてい-. Note that the very similar
ちゃいない comes from ては→ちゃ and is a separate contraction.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|絶対|ぜったい}に{f|来|こ}やしませんよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|絶対|ぜったい}に<b>{f|来|き}ませんよ</b>。（casual: {f|来|き}やしないよ）

Two faults. The stem of {f|来|く}る here is き, not こ; and
<code>やしない</code> has no polite form, so ます cannot be bolted onto it. If the
sentence needs to be polite, the whole expression has to go.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in register:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|話|はなし}を{f|信|しん}じ<b>てはいない</b>。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|話|はなし}なんか{f|信|しん}じ<b>やしない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is measured and could appear in a report or in careful speech. The は marks a
contrast - the speaker doubts his account specifically, and may accept other things
he has said.

(b) is dialogue. The なんか is contemptuous, <code>やしない</code> adds heat, and the
whole line sounds like a character in a story. A learner who produces it in a
meeting will get a startled look rather than a fluent one.

</details>
