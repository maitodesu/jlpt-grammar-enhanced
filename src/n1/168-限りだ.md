# ～限りだ

**Meaning** · I could not be more ～; nothing could be more ～ than this - the speaker's own feeling, at its ceiling

{f|限|かぎ}り is "limit", so <code>{f|嬉|うれ}しい{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code> is "happy to
the limit". Two things make this pattern narrower than the English "extremely".
The adjective has to name **a feeling**, and the feeling has to be **the
speaker's own**. It is a report from inside, not a measurement of the world: a
price cannot be {f|高|たか}い{f|限|かぎ}りだ, and your colleague cannot be
{f|嬉|うれ}しい{f|限|かぎ}りだ.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective | plain form + {f|限|かぎ}りだ | {f|嬉|うれ}しい → {f|嬉|うれ}しい{f|限|かぎ}りだ |
| い-adjective (humble お) | お + adjective + {f|限|かぎ}りです | {f|恥|は}ずかしい → お{f|恥|は}ずかしい{f|限|かぎ}りです |
| な-adjective (rare) | ～な + {f|限|かぎ}りだ | {f|残念|ざんねん}な → {f|残念|ざんねん}な{f|限|かぎ}りだ |
| Polite | ～{f|限|かぎ}りです | {f|心強|こころづよ}い{f|限|かぎ}りです |
| Past | ～{f|限|かぎ}りだった | {f|情|なさ}けない{f|限|かぎ}りだった |
| Not a feeling | ❌{f|高|たか}い{f|限|かぎ}りだ | ✅{f|高|たか}すぎる |

<div class="note">

**Note - which adjectives really occur.** The list is short enough to learn whole:
{f|嬉|うれ}しい, {f|羨|うらや}ましい, {f|寂|さび}しい, {f|心細|こころぼそ}い,
{f|心強|こころづよ}い, {f|頼|たの}もしい, {f|恥|は}ずかしい, {f|悔|くや}しい,
{f|情|なさ}けない, ありがたい, {f|恐|おそ}ろしい. Grammar books also allow a
な-adjective with な in front of it, but in real text almost everything you meet is
an い-adjective; if you are unsure, use one.

</div>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

Spending your holidays abroad every single year - I could not be more envious.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

<pre>

It is only through everyone's support that we have kept going this long, and I am more grateful than I can say.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>～{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code> is **formal but usable**, which sets it apart from the
rest of this family. It belongs to written Japanese, work email, speeches and
carefully spoken conversation; with a friend over coffee you would simply say
<code>すごく{f|嬉|うれ}しい</code>. But in a thank-you message, a report or a
retirement speech, <code>{f|嬉|うれ}しい{f|限|かぎ}りです</code> is exactly right
and not at all stuffy.

Several of its uses have hardened into social formulas.
<code>お{f|恥|は}ずかしい{f|限|かぎ}りです</code> is the standard humble response to
praise or to having been caught out; <code>{f|心強|こころづよ}い{f|限|かぎ}りです</code>
is what you say when someone reliable joins the effort;
<code>ありがたい{f|限|かぎ}りです</code> closes a message of thanks.

<code>{f|羨|うらや}ましい{f|限|かぎ}りです</code> deserves a warning. Said warmly it
is a compliment, but the same words delivered flatly are a well-known way of
needling someone whose good fortune you think is undeserved - the English "must be
nice". Tone and context carry all of it, so use it only where the goodwill is
obvious.

Because the feeling must be the speaker's own, third-person sentences need
something else: {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|嬉|うれ}しそうだった,
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|悔|くや}しがっていた. A question to the listener is possible when
you are inviting them to voice their own feeling, but it is rare and reads as
literary.

## Contrast

### ～{f|限|かぎ}りだ vs ～{f|極|きわ}まりない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code> reports **how I feel**.
<code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code> passes a **verdict on something**, and it is
nearly always a hostile one.

</div>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}だけ{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|受|う}けていなかったとは、{f|不愉快|ふゆかい}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>。
To find that I alone was never briefed is thoroughly offensive.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事情|じじょう}も{f|知|し}らされないまま{f|待|ま}たされて、{f|情|なさ}けない<b>{f|限|かぎ}りだ</b>。
Left waiting with nothing explained to me, I felt utterly wretched.
</pre>

Both speakers are unhappy about the same treatment. The first is condemning the
handling; the second is describing what it did to them. That is the whole
difference, and English "extremely" hides it.

### ～{f|限|かぎ}りだ vs ～てたまらない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>てたまらない</code> is a feeling you cannot bear, often
physical, and it is ordinary spoken Japanese.
<code>{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code> is a feeling at its maximum, stated for the record, and
it is formal.

</div>

<pre>
エアコンが{f|壊|こわ}れていて、{f|暑|あつ}く<b>てたまらない</b>。
The air conditioning is broken and it is unbearably hot.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|地元|じもと}のチームが{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}し、{f|嬉|うれ}しい<b>{f|限|かぎ}りです</b>。
Our local team took the title, and I could not be more delighted.
</pre>

<code>てたまらない</code> covers heat, itching, sleepiness and craving, none of
which <code>{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code> can touch - ❌{f|眠|ねむ}い{f|限|かぎ}りだ.
Conversely <code>{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code> handles social feelings such as gratitude
and embarrassment that <code>てたまらない</code> would make sound like a physical
symptom.

### ～{f|限|かぎ}りだ vs the other {f|限|かぎ}り

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if {f|限|かぎ}り follows an **adjective of feeling** and ends
the sentence, it is this pattern. If it follows a **verb or a noun**, it is the
unrelated "as far as / as long as / unless" {f|限|かぎ}り.

</div>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}の{f|知|し}る<b>{f|限|かぎ}り</b>、{f|変更|へんこう}の{f|連絡|れんらく}は{f|来|き}ていません。
As far as I know, no notice of any change has come through.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}が{f|夢|ゆめ}を{f|諦|あきら}めずにいてくれるのは、{f|嬉|うれ}しい<b>{f|限|かぎ}りだ</b>。
That my son has not given up on his dream makes me happier than I can say.
</pre>

The same noun is doing quite different work: scope in the first
（{f|見|み}る{f|限|かぎ}り, できる{f|限|かぎ}り, {f|行|い}かない{f|限|かぎ}り）,
degree of feeling in the second. Only the second takes だ or です directly.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}い<b>{f|限|かぎ}りだ</b>。 → ✅ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}すぎる。
  The adjective must name an emotion. Size, price, speed and temperature are out.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|嬉|うれ}しい<b>{f|限|かぎ}りです</b>。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|嬉|うれ}しそうでした。
  The pattern states the speaker's own feeling. For someone else you need
  そうだ, がる or a reported form.

- ❌ {f|嬉|うれ}しい<b>の{f|限|かぎ}りです</b> → ✅ {f|嬉|うれ}しい<b>{f|限|かぎ}りです</b>
  No の. The adjective attaches directly, exactly as it would to こと.

- ❌ ご{f|心配|しんぱい}をおかけして、{f|恥|は}ずかしい{f|限|かぎ}り<b>に{f|思|おも}います</b>。 → ✅ ご{f|心配|しんぱい}をおかけして、お{f|恥|は}ずかしい<b>{f|限|かぎ}りです</b>。
  <code>{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code> is already the predicate and does not need a verb of
  feeling after it. In this apology the humble お is close to obligatory.

- ❌ {f|眠|ねむ}い<b>{f|限|かぎ}りだ</b>。 → ✅ {f|眠|ねむ}くてたまらない。
  Sleepiness is a bodily state rather than an emotion about a situation, so it
  takes てたまらない or てしかたがない.

## Practice

**1.** {f|新|あたら}しいチームに{f|信頼|しんらい}できる{f|先輩|せんぱい}がいてくれて、{f|心強|こころづよ}い____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|心強|こころづよ}い<b>{f|限|かぎ}りです</b>（or {f|限|かぎ}りだ）

{f|心強|こころづよ}い is one of the pattern's core adjectives, and the feeling is
the speaker's own. The polite です suits the workplace setting the sentence
implies.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|説明|せつめい}もなく{f|三時間|さんじかん}も{f|待|ま}たされるとは、{f|失礼|しつれい}（{f|限|かぎ}りだ・{f|極|きわ}まりない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|失礼|しつれい}<b>{f|極|きわ}まりない</b>。

The sentence condemns someone else's conduct, which is
<code>{f|極|きわ}まりない</code>. {f|失礼|しつれい} is also a な-adjective, and
<code>{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code> takes い-adjectives of feeling. If you wanted the
speaker's side of it: {f|情|なさ}けない{f|限|かぎ}りだ.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "To have been able to work with all of you for ten years is something I am deeply grateful for."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|十年|じゅうねん}もの{f|間|あいだ}{f|皆様|みなさま}とご{f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|仕事|しごと}ができたことは、ありがたい<b>{f|限|かぎ}りです</b>。

A retirement or farewell speech is prime territory for this pattern.
ありがたい{f|限|かぎ}りです carries the formality that
とても{f|感謝|かんしゃ}しています would leave out, without becoming ceremonial.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|同期|どうき}が{f|先|さき}に{f|昇進|しょうしん}して、{f|悔|くや}しい{f|限|かぎ}りに{f|感|かん}じました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|同期|どうき}が{f|先|さき}に{f|昇進|しょうしん}して、<b>{f|悔|くや}しい{f|限|かぎ}りです</b>。（or {f|悔|くや}しい{f|限|かぎ}りでした）
"A colleague who joined the same year as me was promoted first, and I could not be more frustrated."

<code>{f|限|かぎ}りだ</code> ends the sentence by itself. Adding
に{f|感|かん}じました stacks a second predicate on a phrase that already is one.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) お{f|子|こ}さんが{f|医者|いしゃ}になられたそうで、{f|羨|うらや}ましい<b>{f|限|かぎ}りです</b>。
(b) お{f|子|こ}さんが{f|医者|いしゃ}になられたそうで、すごいですね。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b) is a plain compliment and cannot be taken any other way.

(a) is more formal, and more double-edged. {f|羨|うらや}ましい puts the speaker's
own envy on the table, and <code>{f|限|かぎ}りです</code> maximises it. Said warmly
it is a generous piece of praise; said flatly, or by someone with reason to be
sour, it is the Japanese "must be nice for some". Use it where your goodwill is
already established, and prefer (b) when it is not.

</details>
