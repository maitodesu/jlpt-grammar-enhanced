# ～を皮切りに

**Meaning** · starting with X - X was the first of a run, and the run kept going and usually kept growing

<code>{f|皮切|かわき}り</code> is the noun "the first of a series". The pattern names
that first item and promises the rest: a tour that begins in one city and takes in
twenty, a shop that opens in one station and multiplies, a single remark that lets
loose a flood of them. The main clause almost always says how far the series then
spread.

## Formation

A noun goes in front with を; a verb clause is nominalised with の or こと first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event noun | Noun + を{f|皮切|かわき}りに | {f|大阪|おおさか}{f|公演|こうえん} → {f|大阪|おおさか}{f|公演|こうえん}を{f|皮切|かわき}りに |
| Place noun | Noun + を{f|皮切|かわき}りに | {f|札幌|さっぽろ} → {f|札幌|さっぽろ}を{f|皮切|かわき}りに |
| Emphatic | ～を{f|皮切|かわき}りにして | {f|受賞|じゅしょう}を{f|皮切|かわき}りにして |
| Formal written | ～を{f|皮切|かわき}りとして | {f|初日|しょにち}を{f|皮切|かわき}りとして |
| Verb clause | plain past + の／こと + を{f|皮切|かわき}りに | {f|発言|はつげん}したのを{f|皮切|かわき}りに |
| Typical main clause | {f|次々|つぎつぎ}と／{f|相次|あいつ}いで／{f|各地|かくち}で | {f|次々|つぎつぎ}と{f|開店|かいてん}した |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the reading is かわきり: {f|皮|かわ} plus {f|切|き}り. It is traditionally
explained as coming from the first burn in a course of moxa treatment
（{f|灸|きゅう}）, the one that stung as though it cut the skin - hence "the painful
first of many".

</div>

## Nuance & register

This belongs to **announcements and news copy**: tour schedules, product rollouts,
festival programmes, corporate histories, sports write-ups. It is not casual speech.
A friend telling you about their weekend says
<code>{f|最初|さいしょ}に{f|渋谷|しぶや}に{f|行|い}って</code>, not
<code>{f|渋谷|しぶや}を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</code>, and the formal version would sound
like a parody of a press release.

The pattern has one hard requirement: **a series must follow**. One first event and
one second event is not enough; the main clause has to show a run, an expansion or a
spread, which is why {f|次々|つぎつぎ}と, {f|相次|あいつ}いで, {f|各地|かくち}で and
{f|全国|ぜんこく}に are its constant companions. If nothing follows, the sentence
sounds as though it has been cut off.

The tendency is towards **momentum**, and most often that momentum is welcome - tours,
openings, awards. It is not restricted to good news, though: journalism uses it
readily for a spreading strike, a wave of protests, a run of closures. What it will
not do is describe a small routine first step. A first cup of coffee is not the
opening of a series in the sense this word means.

## Contrast

### ～を{f|皮切|かわき}りに vs ～をはじめ(として)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>をはじめ</code> picks a **representative** member of a group
that already exists. <code>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</code> picks the **first** of a series
that then unfolds in time.

</div>

<pre>
{f|社長|しゃちょう}<b>をはじめ</b>、{f|多|おお}くの{f|社員|しゃいん}が{f|式|しき}に{f|出席|しゅっせき}した。
Many staff attended the ceremony, the president among them.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|東京|とうきょう}{f|公演|こうえん}<b>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</b>、{f|三|さん}か{f|月|げつ}の{f|旅|たび}が{f|始|はじ}まる。
Starting with the Tokyo show, a three-month tour gets under way.
</pre>

The president is not the first attendee to arrive; he is the most important name on
the list. Swap the patterns and the first sentence claims the staff turned up in
sequence behind him, which is not what anyone means.

### ～を{f|皮切|かわき}りに vs ～を{f|機|き}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|機|き}に</code> leads to **one change**, usually in the
speaker's own life. <code>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</code> leads to **a series** of similar
events.

</div>

<pre>
{f|結婚|けっこん}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|郊外|こうがい}に{f|家|いえ}を{f|借|か}りた。
Getting married, we rented a place out in the suburbs.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}での{f|開店|かいてん}<b>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</b>、{f|店舗|てんぽ}は{f|三年|さんねん}で{f|全国|ぜんこく}に{f|広|ひろ}がった。
Beginning with the branch in Hokkaido, the chain spread nationwide within three years.
</pre>

The married couple rent one house, once. The chain keeps opening shops. If you cannot
name the second, third and tenth members of the series,
<code>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</code> is the wrong choice.

### ～を{f|皮切|かわき}りに vs ～から{f|始|はじ}まって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same idea, ordinary clothes.
<code>から{f|始|はじ}まって</code> is what you can say out loud without sounding like a
news bulletin.

</div>

<pre>
{f|自己|じこ}{f|紹介|しょうかい}から{f|始|はじ}まって、{f|最後|さいご}はみんなで{f|歌|うた}を{f|歌|うた}った。
It started with self-introductions and ended with everyone singing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|福岡|ふくおか}{f|大会|たいかい}<b>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</b>、{f|全国|ぜんこく}{f|六|ろっ}か{f|所|しょ}で{f|予選|よせん}が{f|行|おこな}われる。
Beginning with next month's Fukuoka meet, qualifiers will be held at six venues nationwide.
</pre>

The first sentence is a person describing an evening; the second is a schedule being
announced. Both name a first item, but only one of them is written to be printed.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|朝|あさ}のコーヒー<b>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</b>、{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|始|はじ}める。 → ✅ {f|朝|あさ}はコーヒーを{f|飲|の}んでから{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|始|はじ}める。
  A daily routine has no series in it and no expansion. The pattern needs a run of
  comparable events.

- ❌ {f|開会式|かいかいしき}<b>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</b>、{f|大会|たいかい}は{f|終|お}わった。 → ✅ {f|開会式|かいかいしき}を{f|皮切|かわき}りに、{f|熱|あつ}い{f|戦|たたか}いが{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}{f|続|つづ}いた。
  The main clause has to open outwards, not shut the door. Ending immediately
  contradicts the pattern.

- ❌ {f|発表|はっぴょう}する<b>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</b>、{f|問|と}い{f|合|あ}わせが{f|増|ふ}えた。 → ✅ {f|発表|はっぴょう}したの<b>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</b>、{f|問|と}い{f|合|あ}わせが{f|増|ふ}えた。
  <code>{f|皮切|かわき}り</code> is a noun; a verb clause needs の or こと in between.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}、パン{f|屋|や}<b>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</b>スーパーにも{f|行|い}った。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}はまずパン{f|屋|や}に{f|行|い}って、スーパーにも{f|寄|よ}った。
  Two errands are not a series, and an errand is not news. Both the meaning and the
  register are wrong here.

## Practice

**1.** {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|名古屋|なごや}{f|公演|こうえん}____、{f|全国|ぜんこく}{f|十五|じゅうご}{f|都市|とし}をまわる{f|予定|よてい}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|名古屋|なごや}{f|公演|こうえん}<b>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</b>

One named first date and fifteen cities to follow - the series is right there in the
sentence, which is exactly the condition the pattern imposes.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|田中|たなか}{f|選手|せんしゅ}（をはじめ・を{f|皮切|かわき}りに）、{f|多|おお}くの{f|選手|せんしゅ}が{f|大会|たいかい}に{f|参加|さんか}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|田中|たなか}{f|選手|せんしゅ}<b>をはじめ</b>

Tanaka is being named as the most notable of the competitors, not as the first of a
sequence that unfolded over time. <code>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</code> would imply the
others entered one after him, like cities on a tour.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Beginning with a small exhibition in her home town, her work was shown in city after city."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|故郷|こきょう}での{f|小|ちい}さな{f|展覧会|てんらんかい}<b>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</b>、{f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|作品|さくひん}は{f|各地|かくち}で{f|展示|てんじ}されていった。

The first exhibition is small and the series grows outwards - the shape the pattern
was made for. <code>{f|各地|かくち}で</code> and the ていった ending carry the
expansion.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|社長|しゃちょう}の{f|挨拶|あいさつ}を{f|皮切|かわき}りに、パーティーは{f|静|しず}かに{f|終|お}わった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|社長|しゃちょう}の{f|挨拶|あいさつ}<b>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</b>、{f|出席者|しゅっせきしゃ}の{f|挨拶|あいさつ}が{f|次々|つぎつぎ}と{f|続|つづ}いた。

The pattern promises a run of events after the first one, so a main clause that ends
the party at once contradicts it. Either supply the series, or write
<code>{f|社長|しゃちょう}の{f|挨拶|あいさつ}のあと</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|京都|きょうと}<b>を{f|皮切|かわき}りに</b>、{f|展示会|てんじかい}が{f|各地|かくち}で{f|開|ひら}かれる。
(b) {f|京都|きょうと}から{f|始|はじ}まって、{f|展示会|てんじかい}が{f|各地|かくち}で{f|開|ひら}かれる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is announcement Japanese. It reads as a printed schedule or a press release, and
it presents Kyoto as the formal opening of a planned series.

(b) is the same information in a voice a person could use in conversation. It is
neutral about whether the series was planned, and it would look plain on a poster
where (a) looks official.

</details>
