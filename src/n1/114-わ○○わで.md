# ～わ～わで

**Meaning** · what with A happening and B happening - one thing after another, and all of it bad

This is the shape a Japanese speaker uses to tell the story of a disastrous day.
Two things are named - it rained, the trains stopped - each closed with an
exclamatory `わ`, and the で at the end hands over to the conclusion: it was a
catastrophe, we gave up, I wanted to go home. The `わ` here is **not** the
sentence-final `わ` of 「そうだわ」. It is a listing particle, used by everybody, and
it exists to pile troubles up.

## Formation

Plain forms, and - this is the part that surprises people - normally **non-past**,
even when the story is about yesterday.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain non-past + わ | {f|降|ふ}る → {f|雨|あめ}は{f|降|ふ}るわ |
| い-adjective | plain non-past + わ | {f|暑|あつ}い → {f|部屋|へや}は{f|暑|あつ}いわ |
| Verb (negative) | ～ない + わ | {f|届|とど}かないわ |
| Noun predicate | use する instead | ❌{f|停電|ていでん}だわ → ✅{f|停電|ていでん}はするわ |
| Closing | ～わ～わ + で + consequence | …で、さんざんだった |
| Subject marking | each clause takes は | {f|子|こ}どもは{f|泣|な}くわ{f|犬|いぬ}は{f|吠|ほ}えるわ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the non-past forms are a narrative present, the same trick English uses
in "so I'm standing there and the phone rings". The tense of the whole story is
carried by the verb after で:
<code>…で、{f|大変|たいへん}だった</code>.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise it; tell your own bad-day stories with `し` instead.** This is not an
archaic pattern - you will hear it from friends, and read it in blogs, essays and
manga - but it is a strongly stylised one. It has a rhythm, a fixed emotional
colour and a set of things it can and cannot contain, and a learner who deploys it
slightly off - with past-tense verbs, with good news, or in a written report -
produces something that sounds like a bad impression of a comedian. The safe
equivalent is <code>～し、～しで</code>:
<code>{f|雨|あめ}は{f|降|ふ}るし{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|止|と}まるしで、さんざんだった</code>
says the same thing and costs you nothing.

Three things define it.

**It is spoken, and casual.** It belongs to complaining to a friend, to
storytelling, and to writing that imitates speech. It does not appear in a report,
a formal email or a news article.

**It is for troubles.** Two or more things went wrong, they piled up, and the
speaker was on the receiving end. A run of good luck can be told this way as a
deliberate joke - 「{f|給料|きゅうりょう}は{f|上|あ}がるわ{f|彼女|かのじょ}はできるわで」
- and the humour lies precisely in borrowing the complaint frame. Straight, it is
complaints.

**The `わ` is not the feminine one.** Learners meet 「そうだわ」「きれいだわ」 as a
softening sentence-final particle with a female tendency, and then assume this わ is
the same. It is not: it does not end the sentence, it does not soften anything, and
it is used by speakers of any gender. Confusing the two is the single most common
misreading of the pattern.

## Contrast

### ～わ～わで vs ～し～しで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same list of grievances. `し` is neutral and safe;
`わ` is theatrical, and turns the list into a performance.

</div>

<pre>
{f|道|みち}は{f|混|こ}む<b>し</b>{f|子|こ}どもは{f|騒|さわ}ぐ<b>しで</b>、{f|運転|うんてん}どころではなかった。
The roads were jammed and the kids were shrieking, so driving was no fun at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|道|みち}は{f|混|こ}む<b>わ</b>{f|子|こ}どもは{f|騒|さわ}ぐ<b>わ</b>で、{f|運転|うんてん}どころではなかった。
What with the traffic and the kids screaming, driving was out of the question.
</pre>

`し` simply gives reasons, and can be used for anything - good, bad or neutral -
in almost any register. `わ` announces that a story is being told and that the
teller has been badly used. Where `し` can stand alone at the end of a sentence
（「{f|安|やす}いし」）, `わ` cannot: it must come in a pair.

### ～わ～わで vs ～やら～やら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `やら…やら` lists **nouns** and reports bewilderment.
`わ…わ` lists **whole events** and reports a run of misfortune.

</div>

<pre>
{f|年末|ねんまつ}は{f|大掃除|おおそうじ}<b>やら</b>{f|年賀状|ねんがじょう}<b>やら</b>で{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}かない。
The end of the year is unsettled, what with the big clean and the new year cards.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|年末|ねんまつ}は{f|来客|らいきゃく}はある<b>わ</b>{f|風邪|かぜ}はひく<b>わ</b>で、{f|休|やす}んだ{f|気|き}がしなかった。
The end of the year brought visitors and then a cold, and it never felt like a break.
</pre>

The grammatical difference is what goes in the slots: `やら` takes nouns, `わ` takes
predicates. The feeling differs too. `やら` is a person overwhelmed by a heap of
tasks; `わ` is a person to whom things keep happening.

### ～わ～わで vs ～たり～たり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たり…たり` samples actions **neutrally**, and the speaker is
often the one doing them. `わ…わ` reports events that **happened to** the speaker,
with feeling.

</div>

<pre>
{f|連休|れんきゅう}は{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}<b>たり</b>{f|友|とも}だちに{f|会|あ}っ<b>たり</b>していた。
Over the long weekend I watched films, met friends, that sort of thing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|連休|れんきゅう}は{f|熱|ねつ}は{f|出|で}る<b>わ</b>{f|車|くるま}は{f|故障|こしょう}する<b>わ</b>で、ひどい{f|目|め}に{f|遭|あ}った。
Over the long weekend I came down with a fever and the car broke down - a thoroughly miserable time.
</pre>

Note the difference in agency. In the first the speaker chose everything; in the
second nothing was chosen. That is also why `たり` takes the past form
（{f|見|み}たり）and `わ` does not（{f|出|で}るわ）.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}は{f|降|ふ}った<b>わ</b>{f|風|かぜ}は{f|強|つよ}かった<b>わ</b>で、{f|大変|たいへん}だった。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}は{f|降|ふ}る<b>わ</b>{f|風|かぜ}は{f|強|つよ}い<b>わ</b>で、{f|大変|たいへん}だった。
  The listed clauses stay in the plain non-past however long ago the day was. The
  tense lives in the final verb.

- ❌ {f|停電|ていでん}だ<b>わ</b>{f|断水|だんすい}だ<b>わ</b>で、{f|何|なに}もできなかった。 → ✅ {f|停電|ていでん}はする<b>わ</b>{f|断水|だんすい}はする<b>わ</b>で、{f|何|なに}もできなかった。
  A noun plus `だ` does not take this `わ`. Turn the noun into an event with する
  and the pattern works.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|長|なが}い<b>わ</b>で、{f|疲|つか}れた。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|長|なが}い<b>わ</b>{f|資料|しりょう}は{f|足|た}りない<b>わ</b>で、{f|疲|つか}れた。
  One `わ` is not the pattern. Alone it will be read as the sentence-final particle
  and the sentence changes character completely.

- ❌ {f|本件|ほんけん}については、{f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}は{f|不在|ふざい}にする<b>わ</b>{f|資料|しりょう}は{f|届|とど}かない<b>わ</b>で、{f|対応|たいおう}が{f|遅|おく}れました。 → ✅ {f|本件|ほんけん}については、{f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}の{f|不在|ふざい}や{f|資料|しりょう}の{f|遅|おく}れが{f|重|かさ}なり、{f|対応|たいおう}が{f|遅|おく}れました。
  An apology to a client is the one place this pattern must never go. It sounds as
  though the writer were performing their own misfortune instead of apologising.

## Practice

**1.** Where would you expect to hear this, and who is speaking to whom?
「{f|朝|あさ}から{f|停電|ていでん}はするわ{f|水|みず}は{f|出|で}ないわで、もう{f|散々|さんざん}だったよ。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In conversation, to a friend or a family member - never to a customer or a
superior.

Everything about it is casual: the narrative present in the listed clauses, the
final よ, and the frank self-pity of {f|散々|さんざん}だった. The pattern is a
storytelling device, and stories of this kind are told to equals.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in neutral Japanese that would be safe in any spoken situation:
「{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|遠|とお}いわ{f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|混|こ}むわで、{f|開演|かいえん}に{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わなかった。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|遠|とお}い<b>し</b>{f|受付|うけつけ}も{f|混|こ}んでいた<b>ので</b>、{f|開演|かいえん}に{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}いませんでした。

`し` carries the same accumulation of reasons without the performance, and once the
theatrical frame is gone the sentence can take polite endings comfortably.

</details>

**3.** True or false: the `わ` in this pattern is the same feminine sentence-final
particle as in 「きれいだわ」. Explain.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

False.

The sentence-final `わ` softens an assertion and ends the sentence; it has a female
tendency in standard Japanese. The `わ` of this pattern is a listing particle in the
middle of a sentence, has no softening function, and is used by speakers of any
gender. They are homophones, nothing more.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|道|みち}に{f|迷|まよ}ったわ{f|傘|かさ}をなくしたわで、へとへとになった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|道|みち}に{f|迷|まよ}う<b>わ</b>{f|傘|かさ}をなくす<b>わ</b>で、へとへとになった。

Past forms in the listed clauses. Japanese switches to the plain non-past inside
this pattern and lets the closing verb - here になった - carry the tense.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|残業|ざんぎょう}やら{f|接待|せったい}やらで{f|遅|おそ}くなった。
(b) {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|残業|ざんぎょう}はある<b>わ</b>{f|接待|せったい}は{f|入|はい}る<b>わ</b>で{f|遅|おそ}くなった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) names two things as nouns and sounds tired but even-tempered. It would pass in a
fairly polite conversation.

(b) turns them into a sequence of blows and invites sympathy. It is more vivid,
more casual, and more clearly a complaint - the speaker is not just explaining why
they were late but performing how unfair the day was.

</details>
