# とはいえ

**Meaning** · that said; even so; granted, but - grants the point that has just been made, then reports that the thing it led you to expect did not happen

`とはいえ` is `と` + `は` + the old imperative-shaped form of {f|言|い}う, and it still
behaves like its literal sense: *although one says that*. Something has been
established - by you, by the other person, or by the world at large - and this word
concedes it in full before turning to the part of reality that failed to follow.

## Usage

`とはいえ` sits in three positions, and all three are common.

- **Straight after a noun**, with no `だ`. This is its most distinctive shape and
  the one that has no easy English equivalent:
  {f|子|こ}どもとはいえ、{f|約束|やくそく}は{f|約束|やくそく}だ。
- **After a plain clause**, verb or adjective:
  {f|値段|ねだん}が{f|下|さ}がったとはいえ、まだ{f|高|たか}い。
  い-adjectives take no `だ` before it - ❌{f|高|たか}いだとはいえ. な-adjectives and
  nouns usually drop the `だ` too, though {f|便利|べんり}だとはいえ also occurs.
- **Opening a new sentence**, after a full stop, followed by a comma:
  {f|作業|さぎょう}は{f|終|お}わった。とはいえ、{f|確認|かくにん}が{f|残|のこ}っている。

What follows `とはいえ` is a **statement of how things actually are**. The clause in
front of it must be something the listener is already prepared to accept; `とはいえ`
does not introduce new information, it disarms information already on the table.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|春|はる}<b>とはいえ</b>、{f|朝|あさ}はまだ{f|寒|さむ}い。
It may be spring, but the mornings are still cold.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|安|やす}くなった。<b>とはいえ</b>、{f|私|わたし}にはまだ{f|手|て}が{f|出|で}ない。
The price has come down. Even so, it's still beyond me.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}に{f|合格|ごうかく}した<b>とはいえ</b>、すぐに{f|仕事|しごと}が{f|見|み}つかるとは{f|限|かぎ}らない。
Passing the exam is one thing; it hardly guarantees you'll find work straight away.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|景気|けいき}は{f|回復|かいふく}{f|傾向|けいこう}にある<b>とはいえ</b>、{f|中小|ちゅうしょう}{f|企業|きぎょう}を{f|取|と}り{f|巻|ま}く{f|環境|かんきょう}は{f|依然|いぜん}として{f|厳|きび}しい。
The economy may be trending upwards, but conditions for smaller firms remain harsh.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`とはいえ` is **written Japanese first**. Editorials, columns, reports and business
email are its home, and in speech it belongs to the register where someone is
choosing their words: a meeting, a presentation, an interview. It is not stiff
enough to sound odd in careful conversation, but dropped into chat with friends it
sounds as though you are quoting yourself. The everyday versions are `でも`,
`そうはいっても` and `とはいっても`.

The tone is fair-minded rather than combative. By conceding first, the speaker
signals that they are not brushing the other side away - they have taken the point
and are adding the qualification anyway. That is why it is so common in the second
half of a written argument, and why a `とはいえ` sentence rarely sounds rude even
when it is disagreeing.

It is not marked for age or gender, and it has no contracted form. The one thing to
watch is that its noun-attaching use covers a lot of ground:
{f|新人|しんじん}とはいえ、{f|冗談|じょうだん}とはいえ、{f|一度|いちど}とはいえ - "new though
he is", "joke though it was", "even if only once". This is the shape you will meet
most often in print.

## Contrast

### とはいえ vs とはいうものの

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same move, at slightly different speeds. `とはいえ` is
compressed and can hang straight off a bare noun; `とはいうものの` spells the
concession out and reads a shade softer.

</div>

<pre>
{f|素人|しろうと}<b>とはいえ</b>、これはひどい。
Amateur or not, this is bad.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|素人|しろうと}だ<b>とはいうものの</b>、これはひどい。
He's an amateur, granted - but this is bad.
</pre>

The difference is genuinely thin, and in a long clause either will pass unnoticed.
Where they part company is at the extremes: a bare noun plus `とはいえ` is idiomatic
and tight, while `とはいうものの` prefers a full clause behind it and is the one you
would more readily say out loud.

### とはいえ vs にもかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `にもかかわらず` reports a **contradiction between two facts**.
`とはいえ` adds the speaker's **hand on the brake**: I accept that, now hold on.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大雨|おおあめ}<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|満員|まんいん}だった。
Despite the heavy rain, the venue was full.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|満員|まんいん}だった<b>とはいえ</b>、{f|売上|うりあげ}は{f|目標|もくひょう}に{f|届|とど}かなかった。
The venue was full, true - but takings still fell short of target.
</pre>

`にもかかわらず` is neutral and can be written by someone with no stake in the
matter; it simply notes that A did not prevent B. `とはいえ` always has a speaker
behind it, weighing something up. That is also why `にもかかわらず` sits comfortably
in a statistical report and `とはいえ` sits in the commentary next to it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|高|たか}いだ<b>とはいえ</b>、{f|質|しつ}はいい。 → ✅ {f|高|たか}い<b>とはいえ</b>、{f|質|しつ}はいい。
  The `と` is the quotative particle, so it takes a plain form. い-adjectives never
  carry `だ`.

- ❌ {f|京都|きょうと}<b>とはいえ</b>、{f|紅葉|こうよう}が{f|有名|ゆうめい}ですね。 → ✅ {f|京都|きょうと}<b>といえば</b>、{f|紅葉|こうよう}が{f|有名|ゆうめい}ですね。
  `といえば` is "speaking of"; `とはいえ` is "although one says that". The single
  `は` flips the meaning completely, and this is the most common mix-up.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|月曜日|げつようび}だ。<b>とはいえ</b>、{f|明日|あした}は{f|火曜日|かようび}だ。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|月曜日|げつようび}だ。だから、{f|明日|あした}は{f|火曜日|かようび}だ。
  Nothing has been conceded and nothing has gone against expectation. `とはいえ`
  needs a defeated expectation to work on.

- ❌ この{f|案|あん}はいい<b>とはいえ</b>、{f|採用|さいよう}しましょう。 → ✅ この{f|案|あん}はいい<b>とはいえ</b>、{f|問題|もんだい}も{f|残|のこ}っている。
  What comes after `とはいえ` is a report of the awkward reality, not a proposal
  that agrees with the first half.

## Practice

**1.** {f|新人|しんじん}____、{f|客|きゃく}の{f|前|まえ}であの{f|態度|たいど}は{f|許|ゆる}されない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|新人|しんじん}<b>とはいえ</b>、{f|客|きゃく}の{f|前|まえ}であの{f|態度|たいど}は{f|許|ゆる}されない。

Being new is granted as a genuine excuse, and then refused anyway - the classic
noun-plus-`とはいえ` shape. Note that no `だ` appears between the noun and `と`.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word:
{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}（といえば・とはいえ）、やはり{f|海|うみ}の{f|幸|さち}でしょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}<b>といえば</b>、やはり{f|海|うみ}の{f|幸|さち}でしょう。

The sentence picks a topic up and runs with it, which is `といえば`, "speaking of".
Nothing is being conceded, so `とはいえ` has nothing to work against.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|給料|きゅうりょう}は{f|上|あ}がった<b>が</b>、{f|生活|せいかつ}は{f|楽|らく}にならない。
(b) {f|給料|きゅうりょう}は{f|上|あ}がった<b>とはいえ</b>、{f|生活|せいかつ}は{f|楽|らく}にならない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply puts two facts side by side with a plain "but". It is neutral, spoken,
and could open a conversation from nothing.

(b) treats the pay rise as something already claimed - by the company, by the news,
by whoever the speaker is answering - and grants it before pushing back. It reads as
a written argument, and it implies there is an opposing view in the room.

</details>
