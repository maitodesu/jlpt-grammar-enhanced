# あらかじめ

**Meaning** · beforehand; in advance; ahead of time

`あらかじめ` marks an action as done **ahead of a known later point** - before the
meeting, before the deadline, before the customer arrives. It is the formal, written
member of a small family of words that all mean "in advance", and it is the one you
will meet on notices and in contracts rather than in conversation.

## Usage

An adverb, and only an adverb. It stands in front of the verb it modifies, usually at
the head of the clause, and nothing attaches to it - no `に`, no `の`, no `で`.

The verb has to be something **someone does on purpose**, in preparation. A thing
that merely happens early cannot be done `あらかじめ`.

Two collocations carry most of its real use, and both are worth learning whole.

- <code>あらかじめ ～ておく</code>. The adverb announces the preparation and
  <code>～ておく</code> supplies it: あらかじめ{f|調|しら}べておく,
  あらかじめ{f|伝|つた}えておく, あらかじめ{f|買|か}っておく. If you are unsure whether
  a sentence needs `ておく`, it almost always does.
- <code>あらかじめ ～られた/～ている</code> before a noun, meaning "pre-set,
  pre-arranged": あらかじめ{f|決|き}められた{f|時間|じかん},
  あらかじめ{f|用意|ようい}された{f|資料|しりょう},
  あらかじめ{f|定|さだ}められた{f|手順|てじゅん}. This shape is everywhere in written
  procedure.

One fixed phrase accounts for a large share of the sightings:
<code>あらかじめご{f|了承|りょうしょう}ください</code>, "we ask for your understanding
in advance", the standard closing of an announcement about something inconvenient.

The kanji spelling <code>{f|予|あらかじ}め</code> exists, but modern print and public
notices overwhelmingly use kana, and you should too.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|参加|さんか}する{f|人|ひと}の{f|数|かず}を<b>あらかじめ</b>{f|教|おし}えてください。
Please tell me the number of people attending in advance.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}の{f|場所|ばしょ}は<b>あらかじめ</b>{f|調|しら}べておきました。
I looked up where the venue is beforehand.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|当日|とうじつ}は{f|混雑|こんざつ}が{f|予想|よそう}されますので、<b>あらかじめ</b>ご{f|了承|りょうしょう}ください。
Heavy crowds are expected on the day, and we ask for your understanding in advance.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|申込書|もうしこみしょ}に{f|不備|ふび}があると{f|受|う}け{f|付|つ}けられませんので、{f|記入例|きにゅうれい}を<b>あらかじめ</b>ご{f|確認|かくにん}のうえ、ご{f|提出|ていしゅつ}ください。
Incomplete application forms cannot be accepted, so please check the sample entry beforehand and then submit yours.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a **written and formal** word. The places you will genuinely meet it are
notices in stations and shops, terms and conditions, user manuals, application
guidance, and business email. In those places it is completely ordinary and
extremely frequent.

In speech it is not wrong, but it is heavy. Between friends, "I'll tell you in
advance" is {f|先|さき}に{f|言|い}っておくね, not
あらかじめ{f|言|い}っておくね; the second sounds like you are quoting the terms of
service at someone. Where a colleague might use it out loud is in a slightly formal
setting - explaining a procedure at a meeting, briefing a client - and even there
{f|事前|じぜん}に is commoner.

It carries no politeness of its own. あらかじめ{f|言|い}っとけよ is perfectly
possible and perfectly rude; the politeness in
あらかじめご{f|了承|りょうしょう}ください comes from ご～ください, not from the adverb.

One quiet implication is worth knowing. Because `あらかじめ` frames an action as
prudent preparation, putting it in a request makes the request sound like a
procedural requirement rather than a favour. あらかじめご{f|連絡|れんらく}ください
reads as a rule of the establishment; {f|先|さき}にご{f|連絡|れんらく}いただけますか
reads as a person asking.

## Contrast

### あらかじめ vs {f|前|まえ}もって vs {f|事前|じぜん}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - all three mean "in advance". `あらかじめ` is the written one,
<code>{f|前|まえ}もって</code> is the spoken one, and
<code>{f|事前|じぜん}に</code> is the only one that is really a noun underneath, so
it is the only one that modifies a noun comfortably.

</div>

<pre>
{f|遅|おく}れそうなときは<b>{f|前|まえ}もって</b>{f|連絡|れんらく}してね。
If you think you'll be late, let me know ahead of time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工事|こうじ}の{f|日程|にってい}は<b>あらかじめ</b>{f|掲示|けいじ}いたします。
The construction schedule will be posted in advance.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|見学|けんがく}には<b>{f|事前|じぜん}の</b>{f|予約|よやく}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。
A prior booking is required in order to visit.
</pre>

Swap the adverbs in the first two sentences and nothing changes in the meaning -
only the temperature. The third sentence is the one that cannot be swapped:
{f|事前|じぜん} is a noun, so <code>{f|事前|じぜん}の{f|予約|よやく}</code>,
<code>{f|事前|じぜん}{f|審査|しんさ}</code> and
<code>{f|事前|じぜん}に</code> are all available, while
あらかじめの{f|予約|よやく} is at best stiff and usually simply avoided.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>あらかじめの</b>{f|連絡|れんらく}をお{f|願|ねが}いします。 → ✅ <b>{f|事前|じぜん}の</b>{f|連絡|れんらく}をお{f|願|ねが}いします。
  `あらかじめ` modifies verbs, not nouns. When you need a noun modifier, reach for
  <code>{f|事前|じぜん}の</code>.

- ❌ {f|電車|でんしゃ}が<b>あらかじめ</b>{f|着|つ}きました。 → ✅ {f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|予定|よてい}より{f|早|はや}く{f|着|つ}きました。
  The train did not arrive as an act of preparation; it simply arrived early. `あらかじめ`
  needs a deliberate act aimed at something later.

- ❌ ちょっと<b>あらかじめ</b>{f|言|い}っとくけど、{f|明日|あした}{f|遅|おく}れるわ。 → ✅ ちょっと<b>{f|先|さき}に</b>{f|言|い}っとくけど、{f|明日|あした}{f|遅|おく}れるわ。
  The clash is one of register, not grammar: ちょっと and {f|言|い}っとく are relaxed
  speech, and `あらかじめ` belongs to printed notices.

- ❌ <b>あらかじめ</b>{f|質問|しつもん}を{f|考|かんが}えます。 → ✅ <b>あらかじめ</b>{f|質問|しつもん}を{f|考|かんが}えておきます。
  Not ungrammatical, but incomplete-sounding. Without <code>～ておく</code> the
  sentence states an intention and leaves out the preparation the adverb has just
  promised.

## Practice

**1.** {f|台風|たいふう}に{f|備|そな}えて、{f|水|みず}と{f|食料|しょくりょう}を____{f|買|か}っておいた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>あらかじめ</b>{f|買|か}っておいた

The typhoon is the later point the shopping is aimed at, and
<code>～ておく</code> is already in the sentence waiting for it. Note that
{f|前|まえ}もって would also be correct here and would sound more conversational.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|工場|こうじょう}の{f|見学|けんがく}には（あらかじめ・{f|事前|じぜん}の）{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みが{f|必要|ひつよう}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|事前|じぜん}の</b>{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}み

{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}み is a noun, and only {f|事前|じぜん} can modify it, because
only {f|事前|じぜん} is itself a noun. To use `あらかじめ` you would have to rebuild
the sentence around a verb: あらかじめ{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}んでおく{f|必要|ひつよう}が
あります。

</details>

**3.** Where would you expect to read this sentence, and how would a friend say the same thing to you?
{f|一部|いちぶ}の{f|商品|しょうひん}は{f|品切|しなぎ}れとなる{f|場合|ばあい}がございます。<b>あらかじめ</b>ご{f|了承|りょうしょう}ください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

On a printed notice or a shop's website - a sign by the door, a line at the bottom of
a leaflet, the small print of an online order page. The combination of ございます,
ご{f|了承|りょうしょう}ください and `あらかじめ` is the standard formula for warning
customers about an inconvenience.

A friend would not use any of it. Spoken, it collapses to something like
「{f|売|う}り{f|切|き}れてるかもしれないから、{f|先|さき}に{f|言|い}っとくね」 - same
information, none of the notice-board register.

</details>
