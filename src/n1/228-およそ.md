# およそ

**Meaning** · roughly, approximately - and, in the N1 use, "not by any measure at
all", teamed with a negative

One word, two jobs, and the reader tells them apart by what comes next. A number
follows, and `およそ` means "about". A negative follows, and it means "utterly
not - this does not even belong in the category".

## Usage

**Sense 1 - approximately.** `およそ` goes in front of a quantity:
<code>およそ{f|十分|じゅっぷん}</code>, <code>およそ{f|二万人|にまんにん}</code>,
<code>およそ{f|半分|はんぶん}</code>. Neutral, and usable in speech and writing
alike, though written Japanese often prefers <code>{f|約|やく}</code> in front of
figures. Note that it can also mean "a rough idea of" in
<code>およその{f|見当|けんとう}</code> and <code>およその{f|数|かず}</code>.

**Sense 2 - the N1 one.** The frame is <code>およそ～ない</code>. The predicate is
negative, or a word whose content is negative:

- <code>およそ{f|現実的|げんじつてき}ではない</code>
- <code>およそ{f|考|かんが}えられない</code>
- <code>およそふさわしくない</code>
- <code>およそ{f|縁|えん}のない</code>

The force is not "very much not". It is "not by any standard one might apply" - a
verdict rather than a measurement. There is no affirmative version of this sense:
❌<code>およそ{f|現実的|げんじつてき}だ</code>.

**A third use** worth recognising: <code>およそ～というものは</code>, "any X at all,
X considered generally", as in
<code>およそ{f|研究|けんきゅう}というものは{f|時間|じかん}がかかる</code>. Essayistic
and formal.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}まで<b>およそ</b>{f|十分|じゅっぷん}かかります。
It takes about ten minutes to the station.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|計画|けいかく}は<b>およそ</b>{f|現実的|げんじつてき}ではない。
This plan is not remotely realistic.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|責任|せきにん}ある{f|立場|たちば}の{f|人間|にんげん}の{f|発言|はつげん}としては、<b>およそ</b>ふさわしくない{f|内容|ないよう}だった。
For someone in a position of responsibility, the remarks were wholly inappropriate.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|流行|りゅうこう}とは<b>およそ</b>{f|縁|えん}のない{f|服装|ふくそう}で、{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|平気|へいき}な{f|顔|かお}をして{f|街|まち}を{f|歩|ある}いていた。
Dressed in a way that had nothing whatever to do with fashion, she walked through town without a care.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Sense 1 is register-neutral and extremely common; nobody will notice you using it.
In documents and news copy <code>{f|約|やく}</code> is the house style in front of
figures, and <code>およそ</code> sounds a touch softer and more spoken by
comparison.

Sense 2 is the opposite: written, formal and judgemental. It belongs to editorials,
reviews, criticism and prepared speech. Said in conversation about a bowl of ramen
it is absurd - ❌<code>このラーメン、およそおいしくない</code> - where you want
<code>{f|全然|ぜんぜん}おいしくない</code>. Reserve it for the moment when you want
to say that something falls outside the bounds of what could be called reasonable,
realistic or appropriate.

The kanji <code>{f|凡|およ}そ</code> exists and turns up in older writing. The kana
spelling is standard.

Because the two senses share a word, the surrounding sentence has to disambiguate,
and in practice it always does: numbers on one side, `ない` on the other. Learners
who trip up are usually reading sense 1 into a sentence that has no number in it.

## Contrast

### およそ～ない vs まったく～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>まったく～ない</code> measures: none at all.
<code>およそ～ない</code> judges: not by any standard, not even close to counting as
that.

</div>

<pre>
その{f|説明|せつめい}は<b>まったく</b>{f|理解|りかい}できなかった。
I couldn't understand that explanation at all.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|説明|せつめい}は、{f|専門家|せんもんか}のものとしては<b>およそ</b>{f|説明|せつめい}になっていない。
As the words of a specialist, that does not begin to count as an explanation.
</pre>

The first reports the speaker's own failure to follow. The second delivers a
verdict on the explanation itself, and needs the standard - "as the words of a
specialist" - to measure against. That comparison clause is very often present when
`およそ` is used this way.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ その{f|案|あん}はおよそ{f|現実的|げんじつてき}だ。 → ✅ その{f|案|あん}はおよそ{f|現実的|げんじつてき}では<b>ない</b>。
  Sense 2 exists only in the negative. Without the `ない` the sentence says nothing
  the speaker intended.

- ❌ {f|準備|じゅんび}はおよそ{f|終|お}わりました。 → ✅ {f|準備|じゅんび}は<b>ほぼ</b>{f|終|お}わりました。
  "Roughly" in the sense of "nearly complete" is <code>ほぼ</code> or
  <code>だいたい</code>. `およそ` in sense 1 quantifies numbers, not degrees of
  completion.

- ❌ この{f|店|みせ}のコーヒーはおよそおいしくない。 → ✅ この{f|店|みせ}のコーヒーは<b>{f|全然|ぜんぜん}</b>おいしくない。
  Grammatical, and comically over-dressed. Everyday complaints take
  <code>{f|全然|ぜんぜん}</code>.

## Practice

**1.** Which sense is this, and how do you know?
{f|会場|かいじょう}にはおよそ{f|三百人|さんびゃくにん}が{f|集|あつ}まった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sense 1, "approximately". The giveaway is the number immediately after the adverb
and the entirely neutral, affirmative predicate. Sense 2 would require a negative
ending, and there is none here.

</details>

**2.** {f|子供|こども}を{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|留守番|るすばん}させるなど、{f|安全|あんぜん}という{f|点|てん}から{f|見|み}て____{f|感心|かんしん}できない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|安全|あんぜん}という{f|点|てん}から{f|見|み}て<b>およそ</b>{f|感心|かんしん}できない。

The negative predicate and the explicit standard - "from the point of view of
safety" - are the two signals for sense 2. The sentence is passing judgement, not
reporting a quantity.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|提案|ていあん}はまったく{f|新|あたら}しくない。
(b) この{f|提案|ていあん}はおよそ{f|新|あたら}しさに{f|欠|か}けている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (a) is a flat statement: there is nothing new in it, and the tone could be
spoken. Sentence (b) reads as written criticism - the adverb says the proposal does
not come near the standard of novelty at all, and the whole sentence sounds like a
line from a review or a referee's report.

</details>
