# ～ないではすまない / ～ずにはすまない

**Meaning** · you are not going to get out of doing it - the situation stays open until you do. Read <code>ないでは{f|済|す}まない</code> / <code>ずには{f|済|す}まない</code>

Literally "it will not be settled without doing it". The pressure comes from
**outside**: somebody is owed an apology, a payment or an explanation, and the
matter will simply not close until they get it. That is what separates this from an
ordinary statement of obligation - nothing in a rulebook forces the speaker, but
everybody around them is waiting.

## Formation

Both endings sit on the ない-stem. する has two shapes: the regular しないでは and
the older せずには.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ～ない stem + ないでは／ずには{f|済|す}まない | {f|払|はら}わない → {f|払|はら}わないでは{f|済|す}まない・{f|払|はら}わずには{f|済|す}まない |
| Verb (る-verb) | same | {f|届|とど}けない → {f|届|とど}けずには{f|済|す}まない |
| Verb (する) | **せずには{f|済|す}まない**／しないでは{f|済|す}まない | {f|説明|せつめい}する → {f|説明|せつめい}せずには{f|済|す}まない |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | <b>{f|来|こ}ずには{f|済|す}まない</b> | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ずには{f|済|す}まない |
| Past | ～ずには{f|済|す}まなかった | {f|謝|あやま}らずには{f|済|す}まなかった |
| Polite | ～ずには{f|済|す}みません | {f|報告|ほうこく}せずには{f|済|す}みません |
| Heavier, passive | ～ないでは{f|済|す}まされない | {f|謝|あやま}らないでは{f|済|す}まされない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ❌<code>{f|説明|せつめい}しずには{f|済|す}まない</code> is the
standard error. With ずに, する takes the old negative stem せ:
<code>せずには{f|済|す}まない</code>. If you want し, you need the other ending -
<code>しないでは{f|済|す}まない</code>.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Both endings are **formal**, and ずには is the stiffer, more written of the two.
ないでは is a shade lighter but still belongs to careful speech - a meeting, a
serious conversation with a client, a formal letter - not to chat. Among friends the
same thought comes out as <code>{f|謝|あやま}るしかない</code> or
<code>{f|謝|あやま}らないわけにはいかない</code>.

What the form always adds is the **social eye**. Somebody is watching, somebody is
owed something, and the speaker knows perfectly well how it would look if they did
nothing. That is why it collocates so heavily with {f|謝|あやま}る,
{f|説明|せつめい}する, {f|弁償|べんしょう}する, {f|報告|ほうこく}する,
{f|挨拶|あいさつ}する - the verbs of settling accounts with other people. It sits
badly on a purely private matter, because there is nobody there for the situation
to remain unsettled with.

The passive <code>ずには{f|済|す}まされない</code> exists and is heavier still: it
moves the pressure from the speaker's own judgement to society's. Reserve it for
writing.

## Contrast

### ～ずには{f|済|す}まない vs ～ずにはおかない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|済|す}まない</code>: **the world will not let me
off** until I do it. <code>おかない</code>: **it will not stop** until it has had
its effect on somebody else. The pressure runs in opposite directions.

</div>

<pre>
これだけ{f|騒|さわ}ぎになった{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|説明|せつめい}せ<b>ずには{f|済|す}まない</b>。
With a fuss on this scale, the company will not be allowed to say nothing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|発表|はっぴょう}は、{f|利用者|りようしゃ}の{f|反発|はんぱつ}を{f|招|まね}か<b>ずにはおかない</b>。
This announcement is bound to provoke a backlash from users.
</pre>

In the first the company is under pressure; in the second the announcement is the
thing applying pressure. A quick test: if the subject of your sentence is the one
being squeezed, use {f|済|す}まない.

### ～ずには{f|済|す}まない vs ～なければならない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>なければならない</code> is a **rule or a duty**, and it
would hold even if nobody were looking. <code>ずには{f|済|す}まない</code> is
**social**: it holds because people are looking.

</div>

<pre>
{f|税金|ぜいきん}は{f|期限|きげん}までに{f|払|はら}わ<b>なければなりません</b>。
Tax has to be paid by the deadline.
</pre>

<pre>
これだけ{f|世話|せわ}になったのだから、お{f|礼|れい}を{f|言|い}わ<b>ずには{f|済|す}まない</b>。
After all the help they gave me, I can't simply not thank them.
</pre>

The first is true of everyone and appears on a government leaflet. The second is
true of this speaker in this situation, and would be strange on a leaflet.

### ～ずには{f|済|す}まない vs ～ずにはいられない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|済|す}まない</code> = **circumstances** will not let
the matter rest. <code>いられない</code> = **my own feelings** will not let me sit
still. Outside versus inside.

</div>

<pre>
あの{f|知|し}らせを{f|聞|き}いて、{f|涙|なみだ}を{f|流|なが}さ<b>ずにはいられなかった</b>。
Hearing that news, I couldn't hold back the tears.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部下|ぶか}のミスである{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|私|わたし}が{f|頭|あたま}を{f|下|さ}げ<b>ずには{f|済|す}まなかった</b>。
Since the mistake was my subordinate's, there was no way I could avoid apologising myself.
</pre>

Swap them and both fail. Tears are not demanded by the situation, and a formal
apology is not an urge welling up from inside.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|説明|せつめい}<b>しずには{f|済|す}まない</b> → ✅ {f|説明|せつめい}<b>せずには{f|済|す}まない</b>（or {f|説明|せつめい}しないでは{f|済|す}まない）
  With ずに, する uses the older stem せ. This is by far the most common slip.

- ❌ {f|払|はら}う<b>ないでは{f|済|す}まない</b> → ✅ {f|払|はら}わ<b>ないでは{f|済|す}まない</b>
  The base is the ない-form stem, not the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|嬉|うれ}しくて{f|笑|わら}わ<b>ずには{f|済|す}まなかった</b> → ✅ {f|嬉|うれ}しくて{f|笑|わら}わ<b>ずにはいられなかった</b>
  Laughter is an inner reaction, and no situation was demanding it of the speaker.

- ❌ {f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|走|はし}ら<b>ずには{f|済|す}まない</b> → ✅ {f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|走|はし}らないと{f|気|き}が{f|済|す}まない
  A private habit has nobody on the other side of it. The idiom for "I'm not
  satisfied unless I do it" is {f|気|き}が{f|済|す}まない, with {f|気|き}.

- ❌ {f|来|き}<b>ずには{f|済|す}まない</b> → ✅ {f|来|こ}<b>ずには{f|済|す}まない</b>
  {f|来|く}る is irregular; its ない stem is こ, as in {f|来|こ}ない.

## Practice

**1.** これだけ{f|大|おお}きな{f|事故|じこ}を{f|起|お}こした{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|記者|きしゃ}{f|会見|かいけん}を{f|開|ひら}か____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|記者|きしゃ}{f|会見|かいけん}を{f|開|ひら}か<b>ないでは{f|済|す}まない</b>（or {f|開|ひら}かずには{f|済|す}まない）

The pressure is entirely social - the press and the public are waiting, and the
matter will not close until the company faces them. Note the ない stem
{f|開|ひら}か, not the dictionary form.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|久|ひさ}しぶりに{f|恩師|おんし}の{f|顔|かお}を{f|見|み}て、{f|昔|むかし}の{f|話|はなし}をし（ずには{f|済|す}まなかった・ずにはいられなかった）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昔|むかし}の{f|話|はなし}を<b>せずにはいられなかった</b>。

Reminiscing with an old teacher is an impulse, not an obligation, so いられない is
the fit. Note also that する becomes せ before ずに in either pattern.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Since we caused the customer this much trouble, we cannot get away without going to apologise in person."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お{f|客様|きゃくさま}にこれだけご{f|迷惑|めいわく}をおかけしたのだから、{f|直接|ちょくせつ}{f|謝罪|しゃざい}に{f|伺|うかが}わ<b>ないでは{f|済|す}みません</b>。

"Cannot get away without" is the pattern's core meaning, and a customer waiting on
the other side is exactly the social pressure it encodes. The polite
{f|済|す}みません suits a work context.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部品|ぶひん}を{f|壊|こわ}してしまったので、{f|弁償|べんしょう}しずには{f|済|す}まない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部品|ぶひん}を{f|壊|こわ}してしまったので、{f|弁償|べんしょう}<b>せずには{f|済|す}まない</b>。
（or {f|弁償|べんしょう}<b>しないでは{f|済|す}まない</b>。）

する has two negative stems in play. ずに demands the classical せ; the modern し is
only correct with the ないでは ending.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|明日|あした}までに{f|書類|しょるい}を{f|出|だ}さ<b>なければならない</b>。
(b) こうなった{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|明日|あした}までに{f|書類|しょるい}を{f|出|だ}さ<b>ずには{f|済|す}まない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain duty: the deadline exists, and the speaker is stating it. It says
nothing about who is watching or why the deadline matters today.

(b) implies a situation that has developed - a complaint, a delay, someone's
patience running out - and says that submitting the papers is now the only thing
that will close it. The register is noticeably more formal, and the listener will
expect to hear what こうなった refers to.

</details>
