# ことごとく

**Meaning** · every last one of them; all, without a single exception

`ことごとく` counts nothing and quantifies nothing. It says that a whole set went
the same way and not one member escaped - and, nine times out of ten, that the way
they went was bad.

## Usage

`ことごとく` is used adverbially in modern Japanese: it modifies the predicate, not
the noun. The subject or object is a **plural set** - proposals, predictions,
windows, plans - because a word meaning "without exception" needs exceptions to be
possible.

- ✅ <code>{f|提案|ていあん}は<b>ことごとく</b>{f|退|しりぞ}けられた</code>
- ❌ <code>ことごとくの{f|提案|ていあん}</code> - for that you need
  <code>すべての{f|提案|ていあん}</code>

Its habitual company is adversity, and very often the passive:
<code>{f|失敗|しっぱい}する</code>, <code>{f|外|はず}れる</code>,
<code>{f|無視|むし}される</code>, <code>{f|否定|ひてい}される</code>,
<code>{f|崩|くず}れる</code>, <code>{f|裏切|うらぎ}られる</code>. Favourable uses
exist - <code>{f|予想|よそう}がことごとく{f|的中|てきちゅう}した</code> is fine - but
the default colour is defeat, one item after another.

In kanji it is <code>{f|悉|ことごと}く</code>, which is outside the standard
list and appears mainly in literary writing. Write the kana.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|予想|よそう}は<b>ことごとく</b>{f|外|はず}れた。
Every one of my predictions turned out wrong.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|用意|ようい}していた{f|質問|しつもん}は<b>ことごとく</b>{f|無視|むし}された。
Every question I had prepared was ignored.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|委員会|いいんかい}に{f|出|だ}した{f|案|あん}は、<b>ことごとく</b>{f|反対|はんたい}にあって{f|流|なが}れた。
The proposals we put to the committee all ran into opposition and came to nothing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}で、{f|海岸|かいがん}{f|沿|ぞ}いの{f|建物|たてもの}は<b>ことごとく</b>{f|屋根|やね}を{f|飛|と}ばされた。
The typhoon tore the roofs off every one of the buildings along the coast.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Written, and rather grand. `ことごとく` belongs to journalism, history writing,
fiction and speeches; in ordinary conversation it sounds as though you have stepped
into a narration booth. Reporting that everything in the shop was cheap, you say
<code>{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|安|やす}かった</code>, not
<code>ことごとく{f|安|やす}かった</code>.

The spoken equivalents are <code>{f|全部|ぜんぶ}</code>, <code>すべて</code>,
<code>{f|何|なに}もかも</code> and, with a similar flavour of exhaustiveness,
<code>{f|一|ひと}つ{f|残|のこ}らず</code>. Of those,
<code>{f|一|ひと}つ{f|残|のこ}らず</code> is closest in feel, and it is safe in
speech.

Because the word is dramatic, it carries emotion. A writer choosing `ことごとく`
over <code>すべて</code> is usually registering dismay, or admiration for a
relentless run of something. Used of trivia it sounds comic - which is a real
effect writers use on purpose, but not one to stumble into.

## Contrast

### ことごとく vs すべて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>すべて</code> is a neutral quantifier and can be a noun
（<code>すべてが</code>, <code>すべての{f|人|ひと}</code>）. `ことごとく` is
adverb-only, written, and adds "not one of them got through".

</div>

<pre>
{f|書類|しょるい}は<b>すべて</b>{f|確認|かくにん}しました。
I've checked all of the documents.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|規則|きそく}は{f|現場|げんば}の{f|事情|じじょう}を<b>ことごとく</b>{f|無視|むし}している。
The new rules ignore every last one of the realities on the ground.
</pre>

The first sentence is a report of coverage; nothing is felt about it. The second is
a complaint. Swapping in <code>すべて</code> there would still be grammatical, but
the sting goes out of it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ことごとくの{f|窓|まど}が{f|割|わ}れた。 → ✅ {f|窓|まど}が<b>ことごとく</b>{f|割|わ}れた。
  It cannot modify a noun. Move it in front of the verb, or use
  <code>すべての{f|窓|まど}</code>.

- ❌ この{f|本|ほん}をことごとく{f|読|よ}んだ。 → ✅ この{f|本|ほん}を<b>{f|全部|ぜんぶ}</b>{f|読|よ}んだ。
  One book is not a set. `ことごとく` needs several things that could each have
  gone differently - <code>{f|棚|たな}の{f|本|ほん}をことごとく{f|読|よ}んだ</code>
  would be fine.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}の{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}、ことごとく{f|安|やす}かったよ。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}の{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}、<b>{f|全部|ぜんぶ}</b>{f|安|やす}かったよ。
  Casual chat about shopping is the wrong room for this word. The register clash is
  loud enough to sound like a joke.

## Practice

**1.** {f|事前|じぜん}に{f|立|た}てた{f|計画|けいかく}は、{f|現地|げんち}に{f|着|つ}いたとたん____{f|崩|くず}れた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|事前|じぜん}に{f|立|た}てた{f|計画|けいかく}は、{f|現地|げんち}に{f|着|つ}いたとたん<b>ことごとく</b>{f|崩|くず}れた。

The plural set - the plans made in advance - is already in the sentence, and the
outcome is a uniform collapse, which is exactly this adverb's territory. The
written narrative style of the sentence also fits it.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in natural spoken Japanese:
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|言|い}うことは、ことごとく{f|正|ただ}しかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}の{f|言|い}うこと、{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|当|あ}たってたよ。／{f|彼|かれ}の{f|言|い}ったことは{f|一|ひと}つ{f|残|のこ}らず{f|正|ただ}しかった。

Speech drops the literary adverb for <code>{f|全部|ぜんぶ}</code>, or keeps the
"without exception" feel with <code>{f|一|ひと}つ{f|残|のこ}らず</code>, which is at
home in conversation in a way that `ことごとく` is not.

</details>

**3.** Find and fix the error: {f|会議|かいぎ}でことごとくの{f|意見|いけん}が{f|出|で}た。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}で<b>さまざまな</b>{f|意見|いけん}が{f|出|で}た。

Two problems, and the second is the interesting one. `ことごとく` cannot take
`の` before a noun; and "various opinions came out" is not a claim about a set
being exhausted at all, so no repair using this adverb will work. Choose a
different word entirely.

</details>
