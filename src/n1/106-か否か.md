# ～か否か

**Meaning** · whether or not - the written, formal counterpart of かどうか (read か**いな**か)

{f|否|いな} is the classical "no", the word that answers a yes-or-no question in the
negative. <code>か{f|否|いな}か</code> therefore spells out both branches - "whether
it is so or it is not" - and hands the pair to a verb that decides, examines,
disputes or sets them aside. The meaning is exactly かどうか's. The difference is
that this one belongs to documents.

## Formation

A plain, non-polite form goes in front, and nouns and な-adjectives attach bare or
with `である`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + か{f|否|いな}か | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}くか{f|否|いな}か |
| Verb (past) | ～た + か{f|否|いな}か | {f|受|う}け{f|取|と}ったか{f|否|いな}か |
| い-adjective | plain form + か{f|否|いな}か | {f|正|ただ}しいか{f|否|いな}か |
| な-adjective | stem（+である）+ か{f|否|いな}か | {f|適切|てきせつ}か{f|否|いな}か |
| Noun | bare noun（+である）+ か{f|否|いな}か | {f|事実|じじつ}であるか{f|否|いな}か |
| Set frames | ～か{f|否|いな}かは{f|別|べつ}として／～か{f|否|いな}かにかかわらず | {f|勝|か}てるか{f|否|いな}かは{f|別|べつ}として |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - no `だ` in front of it.
❌<code>{f|必要|ひつよう}だか{f|否|いな}か</code> → ✅
<code>{f|必要|ひつよう}か{f|否|いな}か</code>, or the more formal
<code>{f|必要|ひつよう}であるか{f|否|いな}か</code>. This is the same restriction
かどうか has, and it is broken for the same reason: learners hear a copula where the
noun is in fact standing bare.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Formal and largely written: judgments and contracts, academic papers, newspaper
copy, committee minutes, the summing-up in a formal speech. Unlike some N1 forms it
is not archaic - a lawyer, a chair of a meeting or a television reporter will say
it out loud without a trace of self-consciousness. What it will not do is fit a
casual sentence. 「{f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}くか{f|否|いな}か、まだ{f|決|き}めてない」
is the kind of joke a Japanese speaker might make about their own indecision, and
straight-faced it sounds absurd. In speech, かどうか.

It attracts a particular set of predicates: {f|判断|はんだん}する,
{f|検討|けんとう}する, {f|問|と}う, {f|争|あらそ}う, {f|確|たし}かめる, and the
nominal frames ～が{f|焦点|しょうてん}だ, ～が{f|問題|もんだい}だ. That is because
its job is to name a **yes-or-no question as an object** - something to be settled
by a body that has the authority to settle it.

Two frames are worth memorising whole.
<code>～か{f|否|いな}かは{f|別|べつ}として</code> parks the question and moves on,
and <code>～か{f|否|いな}かにかかわらず</code> declares the answer irrelevant.
Both are standard moves in formal argument, and both are commoner than the bare
pattern.

## Contrast

### ～か{f|否|いな}か vs ～かどうか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different clothes. `かどうか` goes anywhere;
<code>か{f|否|いな}か</code> needs a formal sentence around it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}が{f|来|く}る<b>かどうか</b>、まだわからない。
I don't know yet whether he's coming.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|契約|けいやく}が{f|有効|ゆうこう}である<b>か{f|否|いな}か</b>を{f|慎重|しんちょう}に{f|判断|はんだん}する{f|必要|ひつよう}がある。
It will be necessary to determine carefully whether the contract is valid.
</pre>

Swap them and the first sentence turns pompous while the second turns chatty. Note
also what surrounds each: `かどうか` sits happily with わからない and まだ, while
<code>か{f|否|いな}か</code> pulls in {f|慎重|しんちょう}に,
{f|判断|はんだん}する and {f|必要|ひつよう}がある. Register is a property of the
whole sentence.

### ～か{f|否|いな}か vs ～か～ないか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>か{f|否|いな}か</code> **abbreviates** the negative
branch. `～か～ないか` **spells it out**, and is the ordinary spoken way of putting
the same fork.

</div>

<pre>
{f|行|い}く<b>か{f|行|い}かないか</b>、はっきりさせてほしい。
Make up your mind whether you're going or not.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|支援|しえん}を{f|続|つづ}ける<b>か{f|否|いな}か</b>、{f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|会議|かいぎ}で{f|決定|けってい}される。
Whether support is to continue will be decided at next month's meeting.
</pre>

Repeating the verb is emphatic and concrete; it makes the two branches feel like
two real futures, and it is what you say when you are pressing somebody. The
compressed form is impersonal, and no one is being pressed - a body will decide in
due course.

### ～か{f|否|いな}か vs ～や{f|否|いな}や

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same {f|否|いな} in two unrelated patterns.
<code>か{f|否|いな}か</code> = "whether or not"; <code>や{f|否|いな}や</code> = "the
moment that". Look at the particle in front, not the kanji.

</div>

<pre>
{f|辞|や}める<b>か{f|否|いな}か</b>で{f|何日|なんにち}も{f|迷|まよ}った。
I agonised for days over whether or not to quit.
</pre>

<pre>
ベルが{f|鳴|な}る<b>や{f|否|いな}や</b>、{f|生徒|せいと}たちは{f|教室|きょうしつ}を{f|飛|と}び{f|出|だ}した。
The instant the bell rang, the pupils shot out of the classroom.
</pre>

`か` marks a question, so <code>か{f|否|いな}か</code> is a question with its
negative half attached. `や` is doing something else entirely, and the phrase means
"no sooner than". Both are written forms and both are read with いな, which is why
the pair is worth fixing in memory now rather than in an exam.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|方法|ほうほう}が{f|安全|あんぜん}だ<b>か{f|否|いな}か</b>を{f|検討|けんとう}する。 → ✅ この{f|方法|ほうほう}が{f|安全|あんぜん}<b>か{f|否|いな}か</b>を{f|検討|けんとう}する。
  な-adjectives and nouns attach bare. If you want the extra weight, use
  <code>{f|安全|あんぜん}であるか{f|否|いな}か</code> - never `だ`.

- ❌ {f|参加|さんか}します<b>か{f|否|いな}か</b>を{f|教|おし}えてください。 → ✅ {f|参加|さんか}する<b>か{f|否|いな}か</b>をお{f|知|し}らせください。
  The embedded clause stays plain. Politeness is carried by the main verb at the
  end of the sentence, not inside the question.

- ❌ {f|何|なに}を{f|注文|ちゅうもん}する<b>か{f|否|いな}か</b>{f|決|き}めましょう。 → ✅ {f|何|なに}を{f|注文|ちゅうもん}する<b>か</b>{f|決|き}めましょう。
  {f|否|いな} answers a yes-or-no question. With a question word in the clause there
  is no negative branch to attach.

- ❌ {f|今夜|こんや}{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}く<b>か{f|否|いな}か</b>、{f|決|き}めた？ → ✅ {f|今夜|こんや}{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}く<b>かどうか</b>、{f|決|き}めた？
  Not wrong so much as ridiculous: a courtroom phrase in a message about drinks.

## Practice

**1.** {f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}の{f|内容|ないよう}が{f|事実|じじつ}____、{f|第三者|だいさんしゃ}に{f|調査|ちょうさ}を{f|依頼|いらい}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|事実|じじつ}<b>か{f|否|いな}か</b>（を{f|確|たし}かめるため）

A noun attaches bare, and the sentence is a formal one about commissioning an
investigation - the register the pattern needs.
<code>{f|事実|じじつ}であるか{f|否|いな}か</code> is also correct and slightly
heavier.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|明日|あした}の{f|天気|てんき}がどうなる（か・か{f|否|いな}か）、{f|朝|あさ}のニュースで{f|確|たし}かめよう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

どうなる<b>か</b>

どうなる is an open question, not a yes-or-no one, so there is no negative branch
for {f|否|いな} to stand for. The casual ending {f|確|たし}かめよう rules the formal pattern
out a second time.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Whether the new rule applies to part-time staff is still under discussion."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|新|あたら}しい{f|規則|きそく}がパート{f|社員|しゃいん}にも{f|適用|てきよう}される<b>か{f|否|いな}か</b>は、まだ{f|検討|けんとう}{f|中|ちゅう}である。

A yes-or-no question raised as the subject of a formal sentence, with a passive verb
and a written ending. Every part of the sentence agrees on register.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会長|かいちょう}が{f|辞任|じにん}するや{f|否|いな}やが、{f|株主|かぶぬし}の{f|関心|かんしん}を{f|集|あつ}めている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会長|かいちょう}が{f|辞任|じにん}する<b>か{f|否|いな}か</b>が、{f|株主|かぶぬし}の{f|関心|かんしん}を{f|集|あつ}めている。

<code>や{f|否|いな}や</code> means "the moment that" and cannot be the subject of a
sentence. The intended meaning - whether he resigns or not - needs `か` on both
sides.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|続|つづ}けるか{f|否|いな}かは{f|別|べつ}として、まず{f|現状|げんじょう}を{f|把握|はあく}したい。
(b) {f|続|つづ}けるか{f|否|いな}かにかかわらず、まず{f|現状|げんじょう}を{f|把握|はあく}したい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) postpones the question. It is still a live one, and the speaker intends to come
back to it once the facts are in.

(b) dismisses it. The answer will make no difference to the need for a survey, so
it is not worth waiting for. (a) sets the question down; (b) rules it out of the
argument.

</details>
