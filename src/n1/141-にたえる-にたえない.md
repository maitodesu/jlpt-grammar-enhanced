# ～にたえる / ～にたえない

**Meaning** · can stand up to it / cannot bear it - and, with a feeling noun, cannot contain it

One shape, two jobs. After a perception verb or a noun like {f|鑑賞|かんしょう},
<code>にたえる</code> says the thing **holds up** under looking, listening or use,
and <code>にたえない</code> says it is so poor or so painful that you cannot go
on. After a feeling noun - {f|感謝|かんしゃ}, {f|遺憾|いかん},
{f|同情|どうじょう} - <code>にたえない</code> means the opposite of poor: the
feeling is more than the speaker can hold, "I am deeply grateful", "I regret it
profoundly".

## Formation

The verb is {f|堪|た}える, usually written in kana in this pattern.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (perception) | dictionary form + にたえる | {f|読|よ}む → {f|読|よ}むにたえる |
| Verb (perception) | dictionary form + にたえない | {f|見|み}る → {f|見|み}るにたえない |
| Noun (appreciation, use) | noun + にたえる | {f|鑑賞|かんしょう} → {f|鑑賞|かんしょう}にたえる |
| Noun (feeling) | noun + にたえない | {f|感謝|かんしゃ} → {f|感謝|かんしゃ}にたえない |
| Modifying a noun | ～にたえる + noun | {f|使用|しよう}にたえる{f|部品|ぶひん} |
| Past | ～にたえなかった | {f|聞|き}くにたえなかった |
| Polite | ～にたえます ／ ～にたえません | {f|遺憾|いかん}にたえません |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the kanji is {f|堪|た}える. The similar-looking
<code>{f|耐|た}える</code> is the endurance verb of
<code>{f|暑|あつ}さに{f|耐|た}える</code>; convention keeps {f|耐|た} for putting
up with hardship and {f|堪|た} for this pattern. {f|堪|た}える is also the kanji
of a second verb read こらえる ("hold back tears"), which is why kana is the safe
spelling here.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
あの{f|映画|えいが}は{f|見|み}る<b>にたえなかった</b>。
That film was unwatchable.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|机|つくえ}はまだ{f|使用|しよう}<b>にたえる</b>。
This desk is still perfectly usable.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|悪口|わるぐち}ばかりで、{f|聞|き}く<b>にたえない</b>{f|話|はなし}だった。
It was nothing but insults - the kind of talk you cannot sit through.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|皆様|みなさま}の{f|温|あたた}かいご{f|支援|しえん}に、{f|感謝|かんしゃ}<b>にたえません</b>。
For all your warm support I am more grateful than I can say.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事故|じこ}の{f|現場|げんば}は{f|見|み}る<b>にたえない</b>{f|状態|じょうたい}だった。
The scene of the accident was in a state you could hardly bear to look at.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|素人|しろうと}の{f|翻訳|ほんやく}だが、{f|内容|ないよう}は{f|十分|じゅうぶん}{f|読|よ}む<b>にたえる</b>。
It's an amateur translation, but it reads well enough to be worth your time.
</pre>

<pre>
このような{f|結果|けっか}を{f|招|まね}いたことは、{f|誠|まこと}に{f|遺憾|いかん}<b>にたえません</b>。
That matters should have come to this is a source of the deepest regret.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三十年|さんじゅうねん}{f|前|まえ}の{f|記録|きろく}{f|映像|えいぞう}だが、{f|画質|がしつ}がよく、{f|今|いま}なお{f|鑑賞|かんしょう}<b>にたえる</b>。
It's documentary footage from thirty years ago, but the picture is good and it still stands up to viewing today.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Formal and written, in both senses, though {f|見|み}るにたえない and
{f|聞|き}くにたえない do surface in emphatic speech - they are strong words, close
to English "unwatchable" or "sickening", and they carry disgust or pity rather
than mild disapproval.

The verb slot in the perception sense is small: {f|見|み}る, {f|聞|き}く,
{f|読|よ}む and the nouns {f|鑑賞|かんしょう}, {f|観賞|かんしょう},
{f|使用|しよう}. It is not a general "can't stand it" - ❌この{f|暑|あつ}さは
{f|耐|た}えるにたえない is not a sentence.

The feeling-noun frame is a different animal: it belongs to speeches, condolence
letters, apologies and official statements, and it is almost always polite -
{f|感謝|かんしゃ}にたえません, {f|遺憾|いかん}にたえません,
{f|同情|どうじょう}にたえません, {f|喜|よろこ}びにたえません. Notice that
{f|遺憾|いかん}にたえない is the language of a public apology; hearing it, a
Japanese listener expects a bow and a formal setting, not a conversation.

That frame only exists in the negative. There is no ❌{f|感謝|かんしゃ}にたえる:
the whole point is that the feeling cannot be contained.

## Contrast

### ～にたえない vs ～に{f|足|た}りない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>にたえない</code> is too **painful or too bad** to
continue. <code>に{f|足|た}りない</code> is too **slight to bother** starting.

</div>

<pre>
{f|差別的|さべつてき}な{f|発言|はつげん}が{f|続|つづ}き、{f|聞|き}く<b>にたえなかった</b>。
The remarks turned discriminatory and became impossible to listen to.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|中身|なかみ}のない{f|批評|ひひょう}で、{f|読|よ}む<b>に{f|足|た}りない</b>。
The review has nothing in it and is not worth reading.
</pre>

Strong feeling versus cool dismissal. <code>にたえない</code> says the
experience assaulted you; <code>に{f|足|た}りない</code> says you did not think
it merited your attention in the first place.

### ～にたえる vs ～に{f|耐|た}える

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|鑑賞|かんしょう}にたえる</code> is about **quality**:
it survives being judged. <code>{f|寒|さむ}さに{f|耐|た}える</code> is about
**endurance**: a person or a thing puts up with hardship.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|素材|そざい}は{f|高温|こうおん}に<b>{f|耐|た}える</b>。
This material withstands high temperatures.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|初期|しょき}の{f|作品|さくひん}だが、{f|今|いま}{f|見|み}ても{f|鑑賞|かんしょう}<b>にたえる</b>。
It's early work, but it still repays viewing today.
</pre>

The subject is the giveaway. In the endurance sense something suffers a force -
heat, cold, pressure, grief. In the pattern taught here nothing suffers: an
audience is doing the judging, and the work is passing.

### {f|感謝|かんしゃ}にたえない vs {f|感謝|かんしゃ}している

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|感謝|かんしゃ}している</code> reports the feeling.
<code>{f|感謝|かんしゃ}にたえない</code> performs it, in public, in formal
Japanese.

</div>

<pre>
{f|手伝|てつだ}ってくれて、{f|本当|ほんとう}に<b>{f|感謝|かんしゃ}しているよ</b>。
Thanks for helping - I really do appreciate it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|関係者|かんけいしゃ}{f|各位|かくい}のご{f|尽力|じんりょく}に、{f|感謝|かんしゃ}<b>にたえない{f|次第|しだい}です</b>。
For the efforts of everyone concerned I am more grateful than words allow.
</pre>

Use the second with a friend and it sounds like a press release. The frame wants
an audience, a podium or a letter.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ご{f|支援|しえん}に{f|感謝|かんしゃ}<b>にたえます</b>。 → ✅ ご{f|支援|しえん}に{f|感謝|かんしゃ}<b>にたえません</b>。
  With a feeling noun the frame is negative only. The affirmative does not exist.

- ❌ {f|見|み}るにたえない<b>の</b>{f|光景|こうけい} → ✅ <b>{f|見|み}るにたえない</b>{f|光景|こうけい}
  たえない is a verb form and modifies the noun directly.

- ❌ この{f|料理|りょうり}は{f|食|た}べる<b>にたえない</b>。 → ✅ この{f|料理|りょうり}は<b>{f|食|た}べられたものではない</b>。
  The perception frame does not extend to eating. Japanese has its own idiom for
  inedible food.

- ❌ {f|遺憾|いかん}にたえない<b>です</b>。 → ✅ {f|遺憾|いかん}に<b>たえません</b>。
  たえない is a verb, so its polite form is たえません. ないです is far too
  casual for the ceremonial register this phrase lives in.

## Practice

**1.** {f|会場|かいじょう}の{f|野次|やじ}がひどく、{f|演説|えんぜつ}は{f|最後|さいご}まで{f|聞|き}く____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|聞|き}く<b>にたえなかった</b>

The heckling made listening unbearable, which is the perception sense; the past
form fits because the speech is over. {f|聞|き}く is one of the handful of verbs
this frame accepts.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|古|ふる}い{f|建物|たてもの}だが、{f|補強|ほきょう}したので{f|地震|じしん}（にたえる・に{f|耐|た}える）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|地震|じしん}<b>に{f|耐|た}える</b>。

An earthquake is a force acting on the building, so this is endurance, spelled
{f|耐|た}. <code>にたえる</code> in the sense taught here would claim the
building holds up as an object of appreciation, which is not what reinforcement
work is about.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "We received warm messages from so many people, and I am more grateful than I can express."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|多|おお}くの{f|方|かた}から{f|温|あたた}かいお{f|言葉|ことば}をいただき、{f|感謝|かんしゃ}<b>にたえません</b>。

"More grateful than I can express" is exactly the feeling-noun frame, and the
polite ません form is obligatory in the letter-or-podium setting where it
belongs.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|論文|ろんぶん}は{f|誤|ご}りが{f|多|おお}く、{f|読|よ}むにたえないの{f|出来|でき}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|論文|ろんぶん}は{f|誤|ご}りが{f|多|おお}く、<b>{f|読|よ}むにたえない</b>{f|出来|でき}です。
"The paper is riddled with errors and is in no state to be read."

たえない modifies {f|出来|でき} directly; the の between them comes from
treating a verb as if it were a noun. The rest of the sentence is fine, and the
register - a written judgement on a paper - suits the pattern well.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|作品|さくひん}は{f|鑑賞|かんしょう}<b>にたえない</b>。
(b) この{f|作品|さくひん}を{f|見|み}て、{f|感動|かんどう}<b>にたえない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the perception sense and is harsh: the work is so poor that looking at it
is not something a viewer can be asked to do. It is a review you would not want
written about you.

(b) is the feeling-noun sense and is the opposite - the speaker is overwhelmed
with emotion by the very same work. Nothing in the grammar distinguishes them
except what sits in front of に: an act of viewing in (a), a feeling in (b).

</details>
