# ～を控えて

**Meaning** · with something big coming up shortly - and the present already shaped by it

{f|控|ひか}える here means "to have waiting in the wings". The sentence names an
event that has not happened yet and then describes **how things are now because of
it**: the nerves, the preparations, the empty shelves, the quiet market. The event
casts a shadow backwards, and that shadow is what the sentence is actually about.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (event) | noun + を{f|控|ひか}えて | {f|試験|しけん} → {f|試験|しけん}を{f|控|ひか}えて |
| With an interval | noun + を + time + に{f|控|ひか}えて | {f|出発|しゅっぱつ} → {f|出発|しゅっぱつ}を{f|明日|あした}に{f|控|ひか}えて |
| Emphatic | noun + を{f|目前|もくぜん}に{f|控|ひか}えて | {f|大会|たいかい}を{f|目前|もくぜん}に{f|控|ひか}えて |
| Modifying a noun | noun + を{f|控|ひか}えた + noun | {f|試験|しけん}を{f|控|ひか}えた{f|受験生|じゅけんせい} |
| Written clause link | noun + を{f|控|ひか}え、… | {f|選挙|せんきょ}を{f|来月|らいげつ}に{f|控|ひか}え、… |
| Spatial sense | noun + を{f|控|ひか}えた + noun | {f|背後|はいご}に{f|山|やま}を{f|控|ひか}えた{f|村|むら} |
| Not possible | verb directly + を{f|控|ひか}えて | ❌{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}するを{f|控|ひか}えて |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|控|ひか}える</code> has two other everyday meanings that have
nothing to do with this pattern: "cut down on" ({f|塩分|えんぶん}を{f|控|ひか}える,
"go easy on salt") and "note down" ({f|番号|ばんごう}を{f|控|ひか}えておく). Context
separates them easily, but the first in particular catches learners out.

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is the phrasing of **newspaper leads and briefings**. Open a Japanese paper on
the morning of an election, a summit or a season opener and the first clause will
very often be <code>～を{f|控|ひか}え、</code>. It is equally at home in a report, a
speech or a company notice, and it works in careful conversation too - a colleague
saying <code>{f|決算|けっさん}を{f|控|ひか}えて{f|忙|いそが}しくて</code> sounds
entirely normal, just a little formal.

What the pattern supplies is **atmosphere**. The event is in the future, so nothing
has happened yet; the interest is in the tension, preparation or restraint visible
in the present. That is why the second clause is nearly always
<code>～ている</code>, a description of an ongoing condition, and why the pattern
suits nerves, deadlines, rehearsals and markets standing still.

Two requirements. The noun must name a **scheduled or firmly expected event** with a
place on the calendar: {f|試験|しけん}, {f|開幕|かいまく}, {f|出産|しゅっさん},
{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}し, {f|決算|けっさん}. A mere possibility does not qualify -
❌<code>{f|雨|あめ}を{f|控|ひか}えて</code> should be
<code>{f|雨|あめ}に{f|備|そな}えて</code>. And the event must lie **ahead** of the
situation described; once it has happened, the pattern is unusable.

The clause-final <code>{f|控|ひか}え、</code> without て is the newspaper's preferred
shape and is very slightly more formal. In speech, keep the て.

## Contrast

### ～を{f|控|ひか}えて vs ～を{f|前|まえ}に（して）

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for a scheduled event these are **near-identical**.
<code>を{f|前|まえ}に</code> is the wider word: it also covers standing physically in
front of something, and reacting emotionally to it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大自然|だいしぜん}<b>を{f|前|まえ}に</b>、{f|言葉|ことば}が{f|出|で}なかった。
Face to face with all that nature, I had no words.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本番|ほんばん}<b>を{f|控|ひか}えて</b>、{f|楽屋|がくや}は{f|静|しず}まり{f|返|かえ}っていた。
With the performance about to start, the dressing room had gone silent.
</pre>

The first is a scene, not a schedule, so <code>を{f|控|ひか}えて</code> is impossible.
In the second, <code>を{f|前|まえ}に</code> would pass without any change of meaning.
The one thing <code>を{f|控|ひか}えて</code> does better is take an interval:
<code>{f|三日|みっか}{f|後|ご}に{f|控|ひか}えて</code> is idiomatic in a way that
<code>{f|三日|みっか}{f|後|ご}に{f|前|まえ}に</code> is not even grammatical.

### ～を{f|控|ひか}えて vs ～に{f|向|む}けて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|向|む}けて</code> is **purposeful**: the action is aimed
at the coming event. <code>を{f|控|ひか}えて</code> is **circumstantial**: this is
simply how things are with the event looming.

</div>

<pre>
{f|開幕|かいまく}<b>に{f|向|む}けて</b>、クラブは{f|新|あたら}しい{f|選手|せんしゅ}を{f|三人|さんにん}{f|補強|ほきょう}した。
Ahead of the season opener, the club brought in three new players.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|開幕|かいまく}<b>を{f|控|ひか}えて</b>、スタジアムの{f|周|まわ}りは{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}かない{f|雰囲気|ふんいき}だ。
With the season about to open, there's a restless feeling around the stadium.
</pre>

<code>に{f|向|む}けて</code> answers "what are they doing about it?";
<code>を{f|控|ひか}えて</code> answers "what is it like right now?". A tense dressing
room is not aimed at anything, and buying players is not merely a symptom - which is
why each sentence resists the other form.

### ～を{f|控|ひか}えて vs ～を{f|境|さかい}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - opposite directions in time.
<code>を{f|控|ひか}えて</code> looks **forward** at an event that has not happened.
<code>を{f|境|さかい}に</code> looks **back** at one that has, and marks the change it
caused.

</div>

<pre>
{f|移転|いてん}<b>を{f|控|ひか}えて</b>、{f|事務所|じむしょ}は{f|段|だん}ボールだらけだ。
With the move coming up, the office is buried in cardboard boxes.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|移転|いてん}<b>を{f|境|さかい}に</b>、{f|来客|らいきゃく}の{f|数|かず}が{f|大|おお}きく{f|増|ふ}えた。
Since the move, visitor numbers have risen sharply.
</pre>

The first describes the week before; the second compares before with after. Both
attach to the same noun, and swapping them changes not the register but the whole
timeline of the sentence.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|出発|しゅっぱつ}する<b>を{f|控|ひか}えて</b>、{f|準備|じゅんび}をしている。 → ✅ {f|出発|しゅっぱつ}<b>を{f|控|ひか}えて</b>、{f|準備|じゅんび}をしている。
  を needs a noun. Drop the する, or nominalise the clause some other way.

- ❌ {f|試験|しけん}<b>を{f|控|ひか}えて</b>、{f|無事|ぶじ}{f|合格|ごうかく}した。 → ✅ {f|試験|しけん}が{f|終|お}わり、{f|無事|ぶじ}{f|合格|ごうかく}した。
  The event has to still be ahead. Once it is over, there is nothing left waiting in
  the wings.

- ❌ {f|台風|たいふう}<b>を{f|控|ひか}えて</b>、{f|窓|まど}に{f|板|いた}を{f|打|う}った。 → ✅ {f|台風|たいふう}に{f|備|そな}えて、{f|窓|まど}に{f|板|いた}を{f|打|う}った。
  A typhoon may or may not arrive. The pattern wants a scheduled event, not a risk;
  risks take <code>に{f|備|そな}えて</code>.

- ❌ {f|医者|いしゃ}に{f|塩分|えんぶん}<b>を{f|控|ひか}えて</b>と{f|言|い}われ、{f|三|さん}キロやせた。 → ✅ {f|医者|いしゃ}に{f|塩分|えんぶん}を{f|控|ひか}えるように{f|言|い}われ、{f|三|さん}キロやせた。
  Here {f|控|ひか}える is the ordinary verb "cut down on", not the pattern. Written
  as a bare て-form it reads as a garbled version of one or the other.

## Practice

**1.** {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}し____、{f|部屋|へや}は{f|荷物|にもつ}でいっぱいになっている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}し<b>を{f|控|ひか}えて</b>

A dated event still ahead, and a present condition caused by it - the two halves the
pattern needs. The ～ている ending is typical: what is being described is the state
of the room right now.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|地震|じしん}（を{f|控|ひか}えて・に{f|備|そな}えて）、{f|水|みず}と{f|食料|しょくりょう}を{f|用意|ようい}しておいた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|地震|じしん}<b>に{f|備|そな}えて</b>、{f|水|みず}と{f|食料|しょくりょう}を{f|用意|ようい}しておいた。

An earthquake is a risk with no date, so it cannot be "waiting in the wings".
<code>を{f|控|ひか}えて</code> requires an event that is actually scheduled to
happen.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, in news style: "With the summit three days away, security around the venue has been stepped up."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|首脳|しゅのう}{f|会議|かいぎ}<b>を{f|三日|みっか}{f|後|ご}に{f|控|ひか}え</b>、{f|会場|かいじょう}{f|周辺|しゅうへん}の{f|警備|けいび}が{f|強化|きょうか}されている。

The interval slot ({f|三日|みっか}{f|後|ご}に) and the clause-final
{f|控|ひか}え without て are both marks of newspaper style, and the passive
{f|強化|きょうか}されている keeps the second half in the same register.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|卒業|そつぎょう}{f|式|しき}を{f|控|ひか}えて、みんなで{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|撮|と}ってから{f|解散|かいさん}しました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|卒業|そつぎょう}{f|式|しき}のあと、みんなで{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|撮|と}ってから{f|解散|かいさん}しました。

Taking photographs and going home are things that happened after the ceremony, so
the ceremony is no longer ahead. <code>を{f|控|ひか}えて</code> can only describe the
period before an event.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|開幕|かいまく}<b>を{f|控|ひか}えて</b>、チームは{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|重|かさ}ねている。
(b) {f|開幕|かいまく}<b>に{f|向|む}けて</b>、チームは{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|重|かさ}ねている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) frames the training as part of the atmosphere of the weeks before the opening
day. The emphasis is on the situation - the season is nearly here, and this is what
things look like.

(b) frames the training as aimed at the opening day. The emphasis is on purpose: the
team is preparing *for* it, and every session is directed at that target. Both are
natural here, and the choice is one of angle rather than of fact.

</details>
