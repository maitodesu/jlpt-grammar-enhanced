# ～をよそに

**Meaning** · paying no heed to; unmoved by - other people are worried, angry or opposed, and the person in the sentence carries on as though none of it were happening

<code>よそ</code> is {f|余所|よそ}, "somewhere else, another place". `をよそに`
therefore files something away as *not my business*. What gets filed away is almost
always **somebody else's reaction** - their worry, their objection, their outrage -
and the sentence watches the subject walk straight through it.

## Formation

A noun goes in front. A clause has to be nominalised with `の` first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + をよそに | {f|心配|しんぱい} → {f|心配|しんぱい}をよそに |
| Noun (with a possessor) | ～の + Noun + をよそに | {f|親|おや}の{f|反対|はんたい}をよそに |
| Clause | plain form + のをよそに | {f|騒|さわ}いでいる → {f|騒|さわ}いでいるのをよそに |
| Longer variant | ～をよそにして | {f|批判|ひはん}をよそにして |
| Kanji spelling | ～を{f|余所|よそ}に | {f|忠告|ちゅうこく}を{f|余所|よそ}に |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the noun in the slot names a **reaction**, not a circumstance.
{f|心配|しんぱい}, {f|不安|ふあん}, {f|反対|はんたい}, {f|批判|ひはん},
{f|期待|きたい}, {f|混乱|こんらん}, {f|忠告|ちゅうこく} all work. ❌<code>{f|雨|あめ}をよそに</code>
does not: rain is nobody's opinion.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the reaction belongs to **other people**. A sentence about ignoring your
own feelings takes something else -
<code>{f|不安|ふあん}を{f|抑|おさ}えて</code>, <code>{f|迷|まよ}いを{f|振|ふ}り{f|切|き}って</code> -
and the kanji spelling {f|余所|よそ} is nowadays much rarer than the kana.

</div>

## Nuance & register

**Recognise this; reach for something else when you speak.** `をよそに` is not
archaic - it is alive and productive - but it lives almost entirely in **written
Japanese**, and above all in news reporting and narrative prose. It is the phrase a
journalist uses to frame a decision as high-handed, and the phrase a novelist uses
to show one character absorbed while the room boils around them. Said in
conversation it does not sound rude or wrong; it sounds like someone reading a
newspaper out loud. In speech, use
<code>～を{f|気|き}にせず</code>, <code>～を{f|無視|むし}して</code> or
<code>～にもかかわらず</code>.

The default colouring is **mild criticism**. Because what is disregarded is other
people's worry or opposition, the subject comes off as indifferent to them, and in
reporting this is often the whole point of choosing the phrase - it lets the writer
pass judgement without saying anything the subject could object to. That is a
tendency rather than a rule: with {f|心配|しんぱい} it can be admiring (nerves of
steel), and with {f|期待|きたい} it is simply the neutral "contrary to what
everyone expected".

Two restrictions worth holding on to. The subject is normally somebody being
observed - a third party, or the writer narrating their own past - rather than the
speaker in the room. And the disregarded thing belongs to **others**: there is no
natural ❌<code>{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|不安|ふあん}をよそに</code>.

## Contrast

### ～をよそに vs ～にもかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `にもかかわらず` is the neutral, register-safe way to say
"despite". `をよそに` adds a **person who is disregarding** something, and a hint
that they perhaps should not be.

</div>

<pre>
{f|反対|はんたい}の{f|声|こえ}が{f|強|つよ}かった<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|承認|しょうにん}された。
Despite strong opposition, the plan was approved.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|反対|はんたい}の{f|声|こえ}<b>をよそに</b>、{f|市|し}は{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|承認|しょうにん}した。
Brushing the objections aside, the city approved the plan.
</pre>

The first sentence could be written by anybody; it simply records that the
opposition did not stop the approval. The second has the city as an actor turning
its back, and the reader hears the writer's eyebrow go up. If you want to be
neutral, or if you are speaking rather than writing, take the first.

### ～をよそに vs ～をものともせず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `をものともせず` is for **obstacles overcome**, and it praises.
`をよそに` is for **other people's feelings ignored**, and it usually does not.

</div>

<pre>
{f|強風|きょうふう}<b>をものともせず</b>、{f|救助隊|きゅうじょたい}は{f|山|やま}を{f|登|のぼ}り{f|続|つづ}けた。
Undaunted by the gale, the rescue team kept climbing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|救助隊|きゅうじょたい}の{f|制止|せいし}<b>をよそに</b>、{f|男性|だんせい}は{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|山|やま}に{f|入|はい}っていった。
Paying no attention to the rescue team's attempts to stop him, the man walked into the mountains alone.
</pre>

Wind is a hardship, and beating it is admirable. Being told to stop is somebody
else's judgement, and walking past it is not. Swap the two and the sentences change
sides: ❌<code>{f|強風|きょうふう}をよそに</code> would oddly cast the wind as having
an opinion, and <code>{f|制止|せいし}をものともせず</code> would turn the man into a
hero.

### ～をよそに vs ～を{f|無視|むし}して

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|無視|むし}する</code> is an **act**: the person saw it
and deliberately ignored it. `をよそに` describes a **state of detachment** from the
outside, and the subject may simply be absorbed elsewhere.

</div>

<pre>
{f|注意|ちゅうい}を<b>{f|無視|むし}して</b>{f|撮影|さつえい}を{f|続|つづ}けた{f|客|きゃく}が{f|追|お}い{f|出|だ}された。
A visitor who ignored the warnings and carried on filming was thrown out.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|係員|かかりいん}の{f|注意|ちゅうい}<b>をよそに</b>、{f|客|きゃく}は{f|撮影|さつえい}を{f|続|つづ}けていた。
Heedless of the attendant's warnings, the visitor went on filming.
</pre>

<code>{f|無視|むし}して</code> is blunt, accusatory and perfectly ordinary in
speech - it is what you say to a colleague about a driver who ran a red light.
`をよそに` is the same event seen from the narrator's chair, and it is what the
report of the incident will say.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|大雨|おおあめ}<b>をよそに</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|続|つづ}けられた。 → ✅ {f|大雨|おおあめ}<b>をものともせず</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|続|つづ}けられた。
  Weather has no opinion. Only a reaction - worry, opposition, criticism, an
  instruction - fits in front of `をよそに`.

- ❌ {f|騒|さわ}いでいる<b>をよそに</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|眠|ねむ}っていた。 → ✅ {f|騒|さわ}いでいるの<b>をよそに</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|眠|ねむ}っていた。
  `を` needs a noun, so a clause must be nominalised with `の` first.

- ❌ {f|自分|じぶん}の{f|不安|ふあん}<b>をよそに</b>、{f|舞台|ぶたい}に{f|上|あ}がった。 → ✅ {f|不安|ふあん}を{f|抑|おさ}えて、{f|舞台|ぶたい}に{f|上|あ}がった。
  What is set aside belongs to other people. Your own feelings are suppressed, not
  filed elsewhere.

- ❌ （to a friend）{f|心配|しんぱい}<b>をよそに</b>{f|遊|あそ}んでてごめん。 → ✅ {f|心配|しんぱい}かけたのに{f|遊|あそ}んでてごめん。
  A register error rather than a grammar one. Applying newspaper narration to
  yourself, in an apology, to a friend, is three mismatches at once.

## Practice

**1.** {f|医師|いし}の{f|忠告|ちゅうこく}____、{f|祖父|そふ}は{f|毎晩|まいばん}{f|酒|さけ}を{f|飲|の}み{f|続|つづ}けた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|医師|いし}の{f|忠告|ちゅうこく}<b>をよそに</b>、{f|祖父|そふ}は{f|毎晩|まいばん}{f|酒|さけ}を{f|飲|の}み{f|続|つづ}けた。

A doctor's advice is somebody else's judgement, and the grandfather is presented as
walking past it - the standard shape. Note the faint disapproval the phrase carries
into the sentence.

</details>

**2.** Where would you expect to read this sentence, and how would you say it out loud instead?
{f|近隣|きんりん}の{f|苦情|くじょう}<b>をよそに</b>、{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|深夜|しんや}まで{f|続|つづ}いた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In a newspaper article, a local-news broadcast or a written complaint - anywhere a
writer is quietly criticising the contractor.

Spoken, you would say something like
「{f|近所|きんじょ}から{f|苦情|くじょう}が{f|出|で}ていたのに、{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|夜中|よなか}まで{f|続|つづ}いた」.
`のに` carries the same objection without the reporter's voice.

</details>

**3.** Choose: {f|吹雪|ふぶき}（をよそに・をものともせず）、{f|隊員|たいいん}たちは{f|前進|ぜんしん}を{f|続|つづ}けた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|吹雪|ふぶき}<b>をものともせず</b>、{f|隊員|たいいん}たちは{f|前進|ぜんしん}を{f|続|つづ}けた。

A blizzard is a physical hardship being overcome, which is `をものともせず` and its
note of admiration. `をよそに` would need somebody's worry or objection in the slot,
not the weather.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会場|かいじょう}の{f|混乱|こんらん}をよそにして、{f|司会者|しかいしゃ}は{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}いてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会場|かいじょう}の{f|混乱|こんらん}<b>をよそに</b>、{f|司会者|しかいしゃ}は{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}いていた。
"With the hall in uproar, the host stayed calm."

The pattern narrates what somebody did; it cannot be followed by a request. Nothing
after `をよそに` is addressed to a listener - the whole sentence is a description
seen from outside.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|部下|ぶか}の{f|反対|はんたい}を<b>{f|無視|むし}して</b>、{f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|進|すす}めた。
(b) {f|部下|ぶか}の{f|反対|はんたい}<b>をよそに</b>、{f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|進|すす}めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) accuses. <code>{f|無視|むし}する</code> says the manager heard the objections
and chose to override them, and the word is blunt enough that you would hesitate to
use it in front of him. It is ordinary spoken Japanese.

(b) describes. The objections are placed in the frame and the manager proceeds
outside them; the reader draws the conclusion themselves. It is the wording a
written account of the episode would use, and it is cooler on the surface while
being, if anything, more damning.

</details>
