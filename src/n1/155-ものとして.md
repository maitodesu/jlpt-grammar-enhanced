# ～ものとして

**Meaning** · on the assumption that ～; treating it as though ～ - fixes a premise so that the work can go ahead, whether or not the premise is true

<code>もの</code> here is the abstract "case, state of affairs", and
<code>として</code> is "as, in the capacity of". Together they say: *take it as being
so, and proceed*. The pattern is not interested in whether the premise is correct.
It is interested in what follows from adopting it - a plan, a calculation, a piece
of paperwork, a polite fiction.

## Formation

<code>もの</code> is a noun, so what comes in front is a **noun modifier**. Nouns and
な-adjectives reach it through である rather than `だ`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + ものとして | {f|降|ふ}る → {f|降|ふ}るものとして |
| Verb (past) | plain past + ものとして | {f|受|う}かった → {f|受|う}かったものとして |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + ものとして | {f|知|し}らない → {f|知|し}らないものとして |
| い-adjective | plain form + ものとして | ない → ないものとして |
| な-adjective | ～である + ものとして | {f|有効|ゆうこう} → {f|有効|ゆうこう}であるものとして |
| Noun | ～である + ものとして | {f|事実|じじつ} → {f|事実|じじつ}であるものとして |
| Predicate form | ～ものとする | {f|成立|せいりつ}した → {f|成立|せいりつ}したものとする |
| With a treating verb | ～ものとして{f|扱|あつか}う／{f|考|かんが}える | ない → ないものとして{f|扱|あつか}う |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ものとして</code> is **adverbial**: it sets up the premise
and then something has to happen on the strength of it. It cannot finish a sentence.
To end on the premise itself, switch to <code>ものとする</code>.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>ものとする</code> is the ordinary way a Japanese contract says
*shall*: <code>{f|費用|ひよう}は{f|甲|こう}が{f|負担|ふたん}するものとする</code>,
"the costs shall be borne by Party A". Its close relative
<code>ものとみなす</code> is *shall be deemed*. Neither is unusual language in a
document, and neither belongs anywhere else.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>ものとして</b>{f|準備|じゅんび}しよう。
Let's get ready on the assumption that it will rain tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}の{f|話|はなし}は{f|聞|き}かなかった<b>ものとして</b>ください。
Please treat what I just said as something you never heard.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|空気|くうき}の{f|抵抗|ていこう}はない<b>ものとして</b>{f|計算|けいさん}しなさい。
Calculate on the assumption that there is no air resistance.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|参加者|さんかしゃ}は{f|全員|ぜんいん}{f|初|はじ}めて{f|来|く}る<b>ものとして</b>、{f|案内|あんない}を{f|作|つく}り{f|直|なお}した。
Assuming that every participant would be coming for the first time, we rewrote the directions.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|当面|とうめん}この{f|条件|じょうけん}は{f|変|か}わらない<b>ものとして</b>、{f|見積|みつ}もりを{f|出|だ}します。
I'll draw up the estimate on the basis that these terms will hold for the time being.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|期日|きじつ}までにご{f|連絡|れんらく}がない{f|場合|ばあい}は、{f|参加|さんか}されない<b>ものとして</b>{f|扱|あつか}わせていただきます。
If we do not hear from you by the closing date, we will take it that you are not attending.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|失敗|しっぱい}を、はじめから{f|無|な}かった<b>ものとして</b>{f|話|はな}している。
He talks about his own failure as though it had never happened in the first place.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本|ほん}{f|契約|けいやく}は、{f|双方|そうほう}の{f|署名|しょめい}をもって{f|成立|せいりつ}した<b>ものとする</b>。
This agreement shall be deemed to have been concluded upon signature by both parties.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Formal, but not confined to writing. Four settings account for nearly every use:

| Setting | What the premise is for |
|---|---|
| Planning and estimating | working from a figure nobody has confirmed yet |
| Exam and textbook problems | throwing away the complications on purpose |
| Contracts and notices | fixing what will count as having happened |
| Social smoothing | agreeing to treat something as unsaid or unseen |

The exam use is worth recognising on sight. A physics or maths question that says
<code>{f|摩擦|まさつ}はないものとする</code> is not claiming there is no friction; it
is telling you to ignore it. Read it as "for the purposes of this question".

The social use is the one that carries feeling.
<code>{f|聞|き}かなかったものとしてください</code> asks the listener to co-operate in
a fiction, and it is a real request, made when something has been said that would be
awkward to have on the record. It is polite and slightly heavy - the sentence
acknowledges that it is asking a favour. Delivered to a superior it is fine;
delivered to a friend it sounds unexpectedly stiff, and 「{f|今|いま}のなし」 is what
you actually say.

Because the premise is adopted rather than believed, the pattern is neutral about
truth. <code>{f|無|な}かったものとして</code> can describe a reasonable simplification
or a piece of self-deception, and only the surrounding sentence tells you which.

## Contrast

### ものとして vs としたら・とすれば

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `としたら` **entertains** a supposition and wonders what would
follow. <code>ものとして</code> **adopts** one and gets on with the work.

</div>

<pre>
{f|宝|たから}くじが{f|当|あ}たった<b>としたら</b>、{f|何|なに}に{f|使|つか}う？
If you won the lottery, what would you spend it on?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|予算|よさん}は{f|去年|きょねん}と{f|同|おな}じ<b>であるものとして</b>、{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|立|た}てた。
We drew up the plan taking the budget to be the same as last year's.
</pre>

Swap them and both sentences go wrong. A daydream about a lottery win is not a
premise anybody is working from, so <code>ものとして</code> makes it sound like a
board meeting. The budget sentence with `としたら` stops being a plan and becomes
speculation. The test is whether real work happens in the second half.

### ものとして vs ことにして

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ことにする` is a **decision**, and with a past clause it means
*pretend*. <code>ものとして</code> is a **working premise**, and pretending is only
one of the things you can do with it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}のことは{f|見|み}なかった<b>ことにする</b>よ。
I'll pretend I didn't see anything today.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|在庫|ざいこ}はゼロである<b>ものとして</b>{f|発注|はっちゅう}をかけた。
We placed the order on the basis that stock was at zero.
</pre>

<code>{f|見|み}なかったことにする</code> is warm, personal and a little conspiratorial -
one person choosing to let something go. The stock sentence is a business decision
about how to treat a number. Where the two do overlap, in the social use,
<code>ものとして</code> is the more formal and the more distancing of the two.

### ものとして vs つもりで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `つもりで` sets an assumption about **yourself and your attitude**.
<code>ものとして</code> sets an assumption about **the facts**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新人|しんじん}になった<b>つもりで</b>、{f|一|いち}から{f|勉強|べんきょう}し{f|直|なお}すことにした。
I decided to start studying again from scratch, as though I were a new recruit.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|人手|ひとで}が{f|増|ふ}える<b>ものとして</b>、{f|作業|さぎょう}を{f|分|わ}けた。
We split up the work on the assumption that we'd have more people from next month.
</pre>

`つもりで` is about the frame of mind you adopt, and the person adopting it is always
the subject of the main clause. <code>ものとして</code> is about a state of the world
that anybody could adopt, and it is happy in an impersonal sentence where `つもりで`
would be strange.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|事実|じじつ}<b>ものとして</b>{f|扱|あつか}う。 → ✅ {f|事実|じじつ}である<b>ものとして</b>{f|扱|あつか}う。
  A bare noun cannot modify <code>もの</code>. Bridge it with である - or, in a
  contract, with とみなす.

- ❌ {f|来|こ}られない{f|方|かた}は{f|欠席|けっせき}する<b>ものとして</b>。 → ✅ {f|来|こ}られない{f|方|かた}は{f|欠席|けっせき}する<b>ものとします</b>。
  <code>ものとして</code> is adverbial and leaves the sentence unfinished. If nothing
  follows, you want <code>ものとする</code>.

- ❌ {f|宝|たから}くじが{f|当|あ}たった<b>ものとして</b>、{f|世界|せかい}{f|一周|いっしゅう}したいなあ。 → ✅ {f|宝|たから}くじが{f|当|あ}たった<b>としたら</b>、{f|世界|せかい}{f|一周|いっしゅう}したいなあ。
  Nothing is being worked out here, so there is no premise to adopt - only a
  daydream, which is `としたら` territory.

- ❌ {f|新人|しんじん}である<b>ものとして</b>、{f|一|いち}から{f|学|まな}び{f|直|なお}す。 → ✅ {f|新人|しんじん}になった<b>つもりで</b>、{f|一|いち}から{f|学|まな}び{f|直|なお}す。
  Adopting an attitude towards yourself is `つもりで`. The original reads as though
  somebody's employment status were being fixed for administrative purposes.

## Practice

**1.** {f|台風|たいふう}で{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まる____、{f|前|まえ}の{f|日|ひ}に{f|移動|いどう}しておこう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|止|と}まる<b>ものとして</b>、{f|前|まえ}の{f|日|ひ}に{f|移動|いどう}しておこう。

Nobody knows whether the trains will stop; the speaker adopts it as a premise and
then acts on it, which is precisely the shape this pattern exists for. The plain
form goes straight in front of <code>もの</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|明日|あした}{f|休|やす}みが{f|取|と}れた（ものとして・としたら）、どこへ{f|行|い}きたい？

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|休|やす}みが{f|取|と}れた<b>としたら</b>、どこへ{f|行|い}きたい？

The second half asks about a wish, not about work to be done. <code>ものとして</code>
would announce that the premise had been settled and a plan was now being executed,
which does not fit a question beginning "where would you like".

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, in the register of a written notice: "Applications received after the deadline will be treated as not having been submitted."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}り{f|後|ご}に{f|届|とど}いた{f|申込書|もうしこみしょ}は、{f|提出|ていしゅつ}されなかった<b>ものとして{f|扱|あつか}います</b>。

"Will be treated as" maps almost word for word onto
<code>ものとして{f|扱|あつか}う</code>, and the negative goes inside the premise, not
on the treating verb. <code>ものとします</code> would also be correct and slightly
more legal in tone.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|数字|すうじ}は{f|正確|せいかく}ものとして、{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}をまとめました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|数字|すうじ}は{f|正確|せいかく}である<b>ものとして</b>、{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}をまとめました。
"I compiled the report taking these figures to be accurate."

<code>{f|正確|せいかく}</code> is a な-adjective and cannot sit bare in front of a noun.
It needs である - or `な`, though
<code>{f|正確|せいかく}なものとして</code> pulls the reading towards "as an accurate
item", describing the figures themselves rather than setting up a premise. What it
can never take is `だ`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) さっきの{f|話|はなし}は{f|聞|き}かなかった<b>ことにします</b>。
(b) さっきの{f|話|はなし}は{f|聞|き}かなかった<b>ものとして{f|扱|あつか}います</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a personal decision, and a kind one: the speaker is choosing to let the
matter drop, and the sentence has a shrug in it. Between colleagues it is almost a
favour.

(b) is procedural. It says the remark will not be entered in the record, minuted or
acted on - a statement about how the information will be handled, not about how the
speaker feels. In a meeting (b) reassures; between friends it sounds alarmingly like
a ruling.

</details>
