# ～た瞬間に

**Meaning** · at the very instant that something happened - a pinpoint moment, with nothing on either side of it

{f|瞬間|しゅんかん} is an ordinary noun meaning "instant, split second"
({f|瞬|またた}く{f|間|ま}, "the space of a blink"). Used as a connective it puts a pin
in the timeline: not "soon after", not "when", but *at that exact point*. Because
it is a real noun it is far more flexible than the connectives it competes with -
it can be pointed at, counted, made the subject of a sentence, or stood on its own
as <code>その{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</code>.

## Formation

The に is optional and is dropped freely, especially in speech and in headings.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (two past events) | た-form + {f|瞬間|しゅんかん}（に） | {f|触|さわ}る → {f|触|さわ}った{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}に |
| Verb (the moment itself) | dictionary form + {f|瞬間|しゅんかん} | {f|押|お}す → シャッターを{f|押|お}す{f|瞬間|しゅんかん} |
| Verb (instruction, future) | た-form + {f|瞬間|しゅんかん}に | {f|鳴|な}る → {f|鳴|な}った{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}に{f|押|お}してください |
| Noun | noun + の + {f|瞬間|しゅんかん} | {f|勝利|しょうり} → {f|勝利|しょうり}の{f|瞬間|しゅんかん} |
| Standing alone | その{f|瞬間|しゅんかん} | その{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}、{f|時|とき}が{f|止|と}まった |
| As a plain noun | {f|瞬間|しゅんかん}が／を／は | {f|決|き}まる{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}を{f|撮|と}った |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the two verb forms are not interchangeable. The た-form links **two
events** ({f|見|み}た{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}に{f|逃|に}げた, "the instant I saw it I ran").
The dictionary form names **the moment itself** as a thing you can watch or
photograph ({f|飛|と}び{f|込|こ}む{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}, "the instant of diving in").

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is **register-neutral and thoroughly modern**. It is at home in conversation,
in email, in essays, in a job interview and in sports commentary, and it changes
nothing about the politeness of the sentence around it. Of the "the moment that"
patterns collected at this level, it is the one you should feel free to use without
thinking about style.

Its characteristic colour is **drama**. {f|瞬間|しゅんかん} is a slightly heightened
word - the instant a record falls, the instant you first see someone, the instant
a diagnosis is delivered - and it invites the listener to slow down and look at
that one point in time. For a mundane sequence it is faintly overblown:
<code>コンビニに{f|入|はい}った{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}、パンを{f|買|か}った</code> reads as
though buying bread were a turning point in a life. Ordinary sequences want
<code>{f|入|はい}ってすぐ</code>.

Because it is a plain noun, two things are possible that are impossible with
<code>とたん</code>. You can put an **instruction or a plan** after it -
<code>ランプが{f|消|き}えた{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}にボタンを{f|押|お}してください</code> - and
you can use it about the **future**. Those two freedoms are the practical reason to
keep it in your active vocabulary.

The に is genuinely optional. Dropping it is very slightly more literary and very
common in narration and in captions; keeping it is neutral. Neither choice is
marked.

## Contrast

### ～た{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}（に） vs ～たとたん（に）

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>とたん</code> is locked to the **past** and to something
**unexpected**. <code>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</code> is a free noun: it takes commands,
plans and futures as well.

</div>

<pre>
{f|席|せき}に{f|座|すわ}っ<b>たとたん</b>、{f|電話|でんわ}が{f|鳴|な}り{f|出|だ}した。
The instant I sat down, the phone started ringing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|席|せき}に{f|座|すわ}った<b>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}に</b>、ボタンを{f|押|お}してください。
Press the button the moment you sit down.
</pre>

The second sentence cannot be rewritten with <code>とたん</code>: ❌<code>{f|座|すわ}ったとたん{f|押|お}してください</code>
is ungrammatical. Going the other way, <code>とたん</code> can nearly always be
replaced by <code>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</code>, losing only the sense of an unwelcome
surprise.

### ～た{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}（に） vs ～た{f|時|とき}（に）

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|時|とき}</code> is a **stretch** of time.
<code>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</code> is a **point** with no width.

</div>

<pre>
{f|京都|きょうと}に{f|行|い}っ<b>た{f|時|とき}</b>、{f|古|ふる}い{f|寺|てら}をいくつも{f|回|まわ}った。
When I went to Kyoto I visited a good many old temples.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|門|もん}をくぐっ<b>た{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</b>、{f|空気|くうき}が{f|変|か}わった。
The moment I passed through the gate, the air changed.
</pre>

The first sentence is impossible with {f|瞬間|しゅんかん}: you cannot fit a
temple tour into a split second. The test is whether the second clause could
plausibly take {f|一瞬|いっしゅん}, "for an instant". If it needs minutes, use
{f|時|とき}.

### ～た{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}（に） vs ～た{f|拍子|ひょうし}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|拍子|ひょうし}</code> needs a **physical jolt** that
**accidentally** causes the second event. <code>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</code> just marks
the timing and says nothing about cause.

</div>

<pre>
{f|振|ふ}り{f|向|む}いた<b>{f|拍子|ひょうし}に</b>、{f|棚|たな}のコップを{f|落|お}としてしまった。
I turned round and knocked a glass off the shelf as I did so.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|振|ふ}り{f|向|む}いた<b>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</b>、{f|見|み}なれた{f|顔|かお}が{f|目|め}に{f|入|はい}った。
The moment I turned round, a familiar face came into view.
</pre>

Only the first is about cause and mishap. Replace {f|拍子|ひょうし} with
{f|瞬間|しゅんかん} there and the sentence still works but stops blaming the turn for
the broken glass; replace {f|瞬間|しゅんかん} with {f|拍子|ひょうし} in the second and
it fails, because turning round did not knock the face into existence.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|出|で}た<b>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}に</b>、ずっと{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}んでいた。 → ✅ {f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|出|で}てから、ずっと{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}んでいた。
  A point in time cannot host a long activity. ずっと and {f|瞬間|しゅんかん}
  contradict each other.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}を{f|見|み}る<b>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</b>、{f|驚|おどろ}いた。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}を{f|見|み}た<b>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</b>、{f|驚|おどろ}いた。
  Linking two events that happened needs the た-form. The dictionary form would
  make it "the instant of looking at him" as a thing being described.

- ❌ {f|昼|ひる}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べた<b>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</b>、{f|散歩|さんぽ}に{f|出|で}た。 → ✅ {f|昼|ひる}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べてすぐ、{f|散歩|さんぽ}に{f|出|で}た。
  Nothing dramatic is happening, and eating lunch is not instantaneous. The word
  oversells an ordinary sequence.

- ❌ {f|優勝|ゆうしょう}した<b>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}の{f|時|とき}</b>は{f|泣|な}いた。 → ✅ {f|優勝|ゆうしょう}した<b>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</b>は{f|泣|な}いた。
  {f|瞬間|しゅんかん} is already a time noun; adding {f|時|とき} doubles it.

## Practice

**1.** カーテンを{f|開|あ}けた____、{f|朝日|あさひ}がまぶしく{f|差|さ}し{f|込|こ}んできた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|開|あ}けた<b>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</b>（に）

Two events meeting at one point in time, with a vivid, sudden second event - the
core use. The た-form is required because both things actually happened; the に can
be left in or out.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|信号|しんごう}が{f|青|あお}に{f|変|か}わった（とたん・{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}に）{f|渡|わた}り{f|始|はじ}めてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|変|か}わった<b>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}に</b>{f|渡|わた}り{f|始|はじ}めてください。

The sentence is an instruction about the future. <code>とたん</code> can only report
an unexpected past event, so it cannot carry てください;
{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}, an ordinary noun, can.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The moment I heard her voice, I knew something had happened."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|声|こえ}を{f|聞|き}いた<b>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</b>、{f|何|なに}かあったと{f|分|わ}かった。

A single pinpoint moment plus a flash of realisation is exactly what
{f|瞬間|しゅんかん} is for. Note that "something had happened" is naturally
{f|何|なに}かあった - no need to build a past perfect.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|試合|しあい}が{f|終|お}わった{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}に、{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}ほど{f|拍手|はくしゅ}が{f|続|つづ}いた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|試合|しあい}が{f|終|お}わったあと、{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}ほど{f|拍手|はくしゅ}が{f|続|つづ}いた。（or {f|終|お}わった{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}に{f|拍手|はくしゅ}がわき{f|起|お}こった）

Half an hour of applause cannot be squeezed into an instant. Either widen the time
word, or keep {f|瞬間|しゅんかん} and make the second clause punctual.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|指輪|ゆびわ}を{f|見|み}た<b>{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}</b>、{f|母|はは}のことを{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}した。
(b) {f|指輪|ゆびわ}を{f|見|み}た<b>{f|時|とき}</b>、{f|母|はは}のことを{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) collapses the two events into a single point: the recognition was instant and
involuntary, and the sentence asks the reader to feel that jolt. It is the more
emotionally loaded of the pair.

(b) simply gives the occasion. Looking at the ring is the setting in which the
memory arrived, and it may have surfaced after a few seconds of turning it over. It
is flatter, and perfectly natural in a plain account.

</details>
