# ～折に

**Meaning** · when; on the occasion of - the courteous word for a chance that comes round, used above all in letters and polite speech

<code>{f|折|おり}</code> (read おり) is an old noun meaning "moment, occasion, season".
As a connective it does the job of <code>とき</code>, but it is the version you use
when you are being careful with someone: inviting them, thanking them for a past
kindness, or promising to do something the next time an opportunity arises. It leans
towards occasions that **may or may not come**, which is why
<code>{f|折|おり}があれば</code> and <code>{f|折|おり}を{f|見|み}て</code> are so common.

## Formation

<code>{f|折|おり}</code> is a noun, so what comes in front is in noun-modifying shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (dictionary) | dictionary + {f|折|おり}に | {f|上京|じょうきょう}する → {f|上京|じょうきょう}する{f|折|おり}に |
| Verb (た-form) | た-form + {f|折|おり}に | お{f|会|あ}いした → お{f|会|あ}いした{f|折|おり}に |
| Noun | Noun + の + {f|折|おり}に | ご{f|旅行|りょこう} → ご{f|旅行|りょこう}の{f|折|おり}に |
| い-adjective (letter idiom) | ～い + {f|折|おり} | お{f|忙|いそが}しい{f|折|おり}に |
| Topic-marked, invitations | ～{f|折|おり}には | お{f|近|ちか}くへお{f|越|こ}しの{f|折|おり}には |
| Looking back | その{f|折|おり}は／その{f|折|おり}には | その{f|折|おり}はお{f|世話|せわ}になりました |
| Waiting for a chance | {f|折|おり}を{f|見|み}て／{f|折|おり}があれば | {f|折|おり}を{f|見|み}て{f|話|はな}します |
| に dropped, written style | ～{f|折|おり}、… | ご{f|来社|らいしゃ}の{f|折|おり}、… |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the reading is おり, not せつ or とき. The same kanji gives the verbs
<code>{f|折|お}る</code> "to fold, to break" and <code>{f|折|お}れる</code>, where the
reading is お with り as okurigana. The occasion noun is
<code>{f|折|おり}</code> with no okurigana at all.

</div>

## Nuance & register

<code>{f|折|おり}</code> is **polite and warm**, with a slightly old-fashioned grain to
it. It belongs to letters, greeting cards, formal invitations and the kind of spoken
Japanese that comes with keigo around it - a customer visit, a speech at a farewell
party, a message to a former teacher. Said between friends it would sound as if you
were reading from a card, and in that setting <code>とき</code> is the natural word.

Two idioms are worth learning whole. <code>その{f|折|おり}は</code> looks back at a
shared past occasion and is the standard opening for thanking someone for it. And
<code>{f|寒|さむ}さの{f|厳|きび}しい{f|折|おり}</code>, along with its seasonal cousins,
is a letter formula: here <code>{f|折|おり}</code> means "season, time of year" and the
phrase leads into a wish for the reader's health. A stiffer written variant keeps the
old attributive ending - {f|寒|さむ}さ{f|厳|きび}しき{f|折|おり} - which you should
recognise on a New Year card but need never write.

There is a tendency worth knowing: <code>{f|折|おり}</code> prefers occasions that are
neutral or pleasant, and sits badly on disasters and emergencies. Safety notices say
<code>{f|地震|じしん}の{f|際|さい}には</code>, not
❌<code>{f|地震|じしん}の{f|折|おり}には</code>. Think of
<code>{f|折|おり}</code> as an opportunity rather than a contingency.

## Contrast

### ～{f|折|おり}に vs ～{f|際|さい}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are formal "when". <code>{f|際|さい}</code> is
**institutional**: manuals, notices, contracts, emergencies.
<code>{f|折|おり}</code> is **personal**: letters, invitations, thanks.

</div>

<pre>
ご{f|入場|にゅうじょう}の<b>{f|際|さい}は</b>、チケットをご{f|用意|ようい}ください。
Please have your ticket ready when entering.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|京都|きょうと}へお{f|越|こ}しの<b>{f|折|おり}は</b>、ご{f|連絡|れんらく}ください。
Do get in touch when you come to Kyoto.
</pre>

A second, quieter difference: <code>{f|際|さい}</code> insists on an event and refuses
adjectives - ❌<code>お{f|忙|いそが}しい{f|際|さい}に</code> - while
<code>{f|折|おり}</code> takes a state quite happily, which is why
<code>お{f|忙|いそが}しい{f|折|おり}に{f|恐縮|きょうしゅく}ですが</code> is a stock
business opening.

### ～{f|折|おり}に vs ～とき(に)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>とき</code> covers every "when" there is.
<code>{f|折|おり}</code> covers the polite ones, and only occasions - never states of
being.

</div>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもの<b>とき</b>、この{f|川|かわ}でよく{f|泳|およ}いだ。
When I was a child I often swam in this river.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先日|せんじつ}お{f|会|あ}いした<b>{f|折|おり}に</b>、その{f|話|はなし}を{f|伺|うかが}いました。
I heard about that when we met the other day.
</pre>

❌<code>{f|子|こ}どもの{f|折|おり}</code> fails because childhood is a stretch of life,
not an occasion. And in the second sentence <code>とき</code> would be perfectly
grammatical - just less deferential.

### ～{f|折|おり}に vs {f|機会|きかい}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same idea, different clothes. <code>{f|機会|きかい}</code> is the
plain modern word for an opportunity; <code>{f|折|おり}</code> is its courteous,
literary counterpart.

</div>

<pre>
{f|機会|きかい}があれば、また{f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}きましょう。
If we get the chance, let's go for a drink again.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|折|おり}を{f|見|み}て、{f|部長|ぶちょう}にお{f|伝|つた}えしておきます。
I will find a suitable moment and pass it on to the department head.
</pre>

<code>{f|機会|きかい}</code> is neutral about timing; <code>{f|折|おり}</code> hints that
the moment has to be a **fitting** one, which is exactly the work
<code>{f|折|おり}を{f|見|み}て</code> does - waiting until the mood is right.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|火事|かじ}の<b>{f|折|おり}</b>は、エレベーターを{f|使|つか}わないでください。 → ✅ {f|火事|かじ}の<b>{f|際|さい}</b>は、エレベーターを{f|使|つか}わないでください。
  Emergency instructions take <code>{f|際|さい}</code>.
  <code>{f|折|おり}</code> frames a fire as a nice opportunity.

- ❌ {f|旅行|りょこう}<b>{f|折|おり}に</b>{f|買|か}いました。 → ✅ {f|旅行|りょこう}<b>の{f|折|おり}に</b>{f|買|か}いました。
  <code>{f|折|おり}</code> is a noun, so another noun links to it with の.

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}の<b>{f|折|おり}</b>、アルバイトばかりしていた。 → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}の<b>とき</b>、アルバイトばかりしていた。
  A period of life is not an occasion. <code>{f|折|おり}</code> needs a bounded moment.

- ❌ その{f|折|おり}は<b>ありがとう</b>。 → ✅ その{f|折|おり}は<b>お{f|世話|せわ}になりました</b>。
  Not a grammar error but a register clash. If you have reached for
  <code>{f|折|おり}</code>, the rest of the sentence has to be polite too.

## Practice

**1.** {f|来月|らいげつ}そちらへ{f|参|まい}ります____には、{f|一度|いちど}ご{f|挨拶|あいさつ}に{f|伺|うかが}わせてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|来月|らいげつ}そちらへ{f|参|まい}る<b>{f|折|おり}には</b>

The verb goes into its plain dictionary form to modify the noun
<code>{f|折|おり}</code>, so <code>{f|参|まい}ります</code> becomes
<code>{f|参|まい}る</code>. The politeness is carried by
<code>{f|伺|うかが}わせてください</code> at the end, not by keeping ます inside the
clause.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|停電|ていでん}の（{f|折|おり}・{f|際|さい}）は、この{f|懐中|かいちゅう}{f|電灯|でんとう}をお{f|使|つか}いください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|停電|ていでん}の<b>{f|際|さい}</b>は

A power cut is a contingency, and instructions for contingencies take
<code>{f|際|さい}</code>. <code>{f|折|おり}</code> is reserved for occasions you would
welcome, or at least not dread.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Please give her my regards when you see her next."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|次|つぎ}にお{f|会|あ}いになる<b>{f|折|おり}に</b>、よろしくお{f|伝|つた}えください。

An occasion that will come round some time, wrapped in polite speech - the natural
home of <code>{f|折|おり}に</code>. <code>とき</code> would be understood but would
drop the sentence a notch in courtesy.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: お{f|忙|いそが}しい{f|際|さい}に{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありませんが、{f|少|すこ}しお{f|時間|じかん}をいただけますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ お{f|忙|いそが}しい<b>{f|折|おり}に</b>{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありませんが、{f|少|すこ}しお{f|時間|じかん}をいただけますか。

<code>{f|際|さい}</code> wants an event, and "being busy" is a state, so an
い-adjective in front of it is ungrammatical. <code>{f|折|おり}</code> accepts the
adjective, and <code>お{f|忙|いそが}しい{f|折|おり}に</code> is a set business opening.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|機会|きかい}があれば、ぜひ{f|工場|こうじょう}を{f|見|み}に{f|来|き}てください。
(b) {f|折|おり}があれば、ぜひ{f|工場|こうじょう}を{f|見|み}に{f|来|き}てください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is ordinary polite Japanese and could be said to a colleague or a customer alike.
It simply means "if you get the chance".

(b) is more formal and a little old-fashioned, the sort of thing said in a prepared
greeting or written in a letter. It also carries a shade of "if a suitable moment
arises", leaving the timing to the other person's convenience.

</details>
