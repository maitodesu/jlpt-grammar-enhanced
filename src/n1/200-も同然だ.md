# ～も同然だ

**Meaning** · as good as ~; practically ~ - strictly speaking it is not, but for every purpose that matters it may as well be

Read <code>も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</code>. <code>{f|同然|どうぜん}</code> is a noun
meaning "the same thing", and the `も` is the one that says "you might as well call
it that too". The sentence is always a **judgement**: the speaker has decided that
the remaining difference between the situation and the label is not worth counting.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | ～も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ | {f|新品|しんぴん} → {f|新品|しんぴん}も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ |
| Verb (plain past) | ～たも{f|同然|どうぜん}だ | {f|終|お}わる → {f|終|お}わったも{f|同然|どうぜん}だ |
| Verb (negative) | ～ないも{f|同然|どうぜん}だ | ある → あってもないも{f|同然|どうぜん}だ |
| Modifying a noun | ～も{f|同然|どうぜん}の + {f|名詞|めいし} | ただ{f|同然|どうぜん}の{f|値段|ねだん} |
| Polite | ～も{f|同然|どうぜん}です | {f|決|き}まったも{f|同然|どうぜん}です |
| Past | ～も{f|同然|どうぜん}だった | {f|他人|たにん}{f|同然|どうぜん}だった |
| With `も` dropped (set compounds) | {f|名詞|めいし} + {f|同然|どうぜん} | {f|家族|かぞく}{f|同然|どうぜん}, ただ{f|同然|どうぜん}, {f|他人|たにん}{f|同然|どうぜん} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - a **past-tense verb** in front is the norm, because the pattern says the
result is effectively already in place:
<code>{f|勝|か}ったも{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</code>, "we've as good as won", said while
the match is still going. ❌<code>{f|勝|か}つも{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</code> is not
Japanese. When it modifies a following noun, use `の`:
<code>{f|家族|かぞく}{f|同然|どうぜん}の{f|付|つ}き{f|合|あ}い</code>, not
❌<code>{f|同然|どうぜん}な</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}は{f|新品|しんぴん}<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</b>。
This bicycle is as good as new.
</pre>

<pre>
あと{f|一問|いちもん}だから、もう{f|終|お}わった<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</b>。
There's only one question left, so I'm as good as finished.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|十年|じゅうねん}{f|連絡|れんらく}を{f|取|と}っていないので、いとことはもう{f|他人|たにん}<b>{f|同然|どうぜん}</b>です。
We haven't been in touch for ten years, so my cousin and I are practically strangers now.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|机|つくえ}は{f|閉店|へいてん}{f|前|まえ}の{f|店|みせ}でただ<b>{f|同然|どうぜん}</b>で{f|手|て}に{f|入|い}れた。
I got that desk for next to nothing from a shop that was closing down.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}がドイツ{f|語|ご}だけでは、{f|私|わたし}には{f|無|な}い<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</b>。
With the manual only in German, for me it might as well not exist.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|留学中|りゅうがくちゅう}、ホストファミリーには{f|家族|かぞく}<b>{f|同然|どうぜん}の</b>{f|扱|あつか}いをしてもらった。
While I was studying abroad my host family treated me as one of their own.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|決勝|けっしょう}{f|進出|しんしゅつ}は{f|決|き}まった<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</b>と{f|言|い}われていたが、{f|最終|さいしゅう}{f|戦|せん}で{f|敗|やぶ}れた。
A place in the final was said to be as good as settled, but they lost the last match.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|給料|きゅうりょう}の{f|半分|はんぶん}が{f|家賃|やちん}に{f|消|き}えるのだから、{f|貯金|ちょきん}は{f|無|な}い<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}</b>です。
Half my salary goes on rent, so my savings are as good as nonexistent.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and thoroughly usable. <code>も{f|同然|どうぜん}です</code> is fine
in a meeting, <code>も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</code> is fine over dinner, and it appears
in newspapers without looking formal.

What it always adds is the speaker's **willingness to round off**. Nothing in the
world is measuring the difference for you: the bicycle really has been ridden, the
exam really is not over, and the cousin really is family. The sentence says those
remainders do not matter. That makes it a natural fit for reassurance
(<code>もう{f|終|お}わったも{f|同然|どうぜん}だよ</code>), for a bargain
(<code>ただ{f|同然|どうぜん}</code>), and for warmth
(<code>{f|家族|かぞく}{f|同然|どうぜん}</code>) - and equally for dismissal, which is
where it can bite.

Be careful in that last direction. <code>{f|死|し}んだも{f|同然|どうぜん}</code>,
<code>クビも{f|同然|どうぜん}</code>, <code>{f|他人|たにん}{f|同然|どうぜん}</code>
are heavy sentences, and applied to a person's work -
<code>やっていないも{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</code> - the pattern says their effort
counts for nothing. Because the rounding-off is the speaker's own choice, the
listener hears a verdict rather than a fact, and a mild-sounding
<code>です</code> at the end does not soften it.

Two idioms are worth learning whole. <code>ただ{f|同然|どうぜん}</code> means "for
practically nothing" and is the standard way to describe a bargain price.
<code>{f|家族|かぞく}{f|同然|どうぜん}</code> means "like family" and is warm; note
that both drop the `も` - after a familiar noun the particle very often disappears,
though putting it back is never wrong.

## Contrast

### ～も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ vs ～ようなものだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</code> says the situation has
**effectively reached** that state. <code>ようなものだ</code> offers a
**comparison or explanation** - "it's basically like X".

</div>

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|残業|ざんぎょう}で、{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|住|す}んでいる<b>ようなものだ</b>。
I work late every day - it's as if I lived at the office.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|残|のこ}りは{f|署名|しょめい}だけなので、{f|契約|けいやく}は{f|成立|せいりつ}した<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}です</b>。
All that's left is the signature, so the contract is as good as concluded.
</pre>

The first is a figure of speech: nobody thinks the speaker lives at the office, and
the sentence is describing what it feels like. The second is a claim about the actual
state of the contract, and you could act on it. Where a metaphor is intended,
<code>も{f|同然|どうぜん}</code> sounds oddly literal; where a real state is being
reported, <code>ようなものだ</code> sounds evasive.

### ～も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ vs ～に{f|等|ひと}しい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same idea in two registers.
<code>に{f|等|ひと}しい</code> is **written and often severe**, common in argument and
criticism; <code>も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</code> is **everyday**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|証拠|しょうこ}もなくそう{f|断|だん}じるのは、{f|中傷|ちゅうしょう}<b>に{f|等|ひと}しい</b>。
To assert that without evidence amounts to defamation.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}、{f|物置|ものおき}<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}</b>だね。
This room is practically a storeroom, isn't it.
</pre>

<code>に{f|等|ひと}しい</code> is at home with abstractions - {f|不可能|ふかのう},
{f|犯罪|はんざい}, ゼロ - and in editorial or legal prose, where it delivers a
judgement in impersonal-sounding terms.
<code>も{f|同然|どうぜん}</code> handles concrete situations and everyday
exaggeration. Put <code>に{f|等|ひと}しい</code> on the storeroom and you sound as
though you are drafting a complaint.

### ～も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ vs ～と{f|同|おな}じだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>と{f|同|おな}じだ</code> claims things **really are** the
same. <code>も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</code> concedes they are **not**, and says the
difference does not matter.

</div>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}のモデルと{f|中身|なかみ}は<b>{f|同|おな}じ</b>です。
The internals are the same as last year's model.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}のモデル<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}</b>です。
It might as well be last year's model.
</pre>

The first is a statement of fact that an engineer could confirm. The second is an
opinion - the changes are so slight that the speaker refuses to count them - and it
is faintly disparaging, which the first is not. Whenever a sentence could be checked
against reality, <code>{f|同|おな}じ</code> is the honest word;
<code>{f|同然|どうぜん}</code> announces that someone has made a judgement call.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|試合|しあい}は{f|勝|か}つ<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</b>。 → ✅ {f|試合|しあい}は{f|勝|か}った<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</b>。
  The verb goes into the plain past, because the point is that the result is already
  in place for practical purposes.

- ❌ この{f|二|ふた}つの{f|数字|すうじ}は<b>{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</b>。 → ✅ この{f|二|ふた}つの{f|数字|すうじ}は<b>{f|同|おな}じだ</b>。
  For literal identity use <code>{f|同|おな}じ</code>.
  <code>{f|同然|どうぜん}</code> exists precisely for cases where the two things are
  *not* identical.

- ❌ {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|姉|あね}<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}な{f|人|ひと}</b>だ。 → ✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|姉|あね}<b>{f|同然|どうぜん}の{f|人|ひと}</b>だ。
  <code>{f|同然|どうぜん}</code> modifies a following noun with `の`, not `な`.

- ❌ {f|先輩|せんぱい}の{f|資料|しりょう}は{f|役|やく}に{f|立|た}たない<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}ですね</b>。 → ✅ {f|先輩|せんぱい}の{f|資料|しりょう}は{f|少|すこ}し{f|情報|じょうほう}が{f|足|た}りないようですね。
  The polite ending does not blunt the content. Saying someone's work may as well not
  exist is a dismissal however you conjugate it.

## Practice

**1.** {f|荷物|にもつ}はあと{f|一箱|ひとはこ}だけだから、{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}しの{f|準備|じゅんび}は{f|終|お}わった____だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|終|お}わった<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}</b>だ

One box is not zero boxes, and that is exactly the gap the pattern waves away. Note
the past tense <code>{f|終|お}わった</code>: the state is being treated as already
reached.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|毎日|まいにち}こんなに{f|残業|ざんぎょう}していたら、{f|会社|かいしゃ}で{f|暮|く}らしている（も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ・ようなものだ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会社|かいしゃ}で{f|暮|く}らしている<b>ようなものだ</b>。

This is a figure of speech about how the days feel, not a report on where the speaker
sleeps, so <code>ようなものだ</code> is the fit.
<code>も{f|同然|どうぜん}</code> would push it towards a literal claim about the
speaker's living arrangements.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The shop sold me the display model for next to nothing."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|店|みせ}は{f|展示品|てんじひん}をただ<b>{f|同然|どうぜん}</b>で{f|売|う}ってくれた。

<code>ただ{f|同然|どうぜん}で</code> is the set phrase for "for next to nothing" -
note that `も` drops out of this one, and that
<code>{f|売|う}ってくれた</code> marks the sale as a favour to the speaker.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}とは{f|十年|じゅうねん}{f|会|あ}っていないので、もう{f|他人|たにん}も{f|同然|どうぜん}な{f|関係|かんけい}になってしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}とは{f|十年|じゅうねん}{f|会|あ}っていないので、もう{f|他人|たにん}<b>{f|同然|どうぜん}の{f|関係|かんけい}</b>になってしまった。
"I haven't seen him in ten years, so we've ended up practically strangers."

Before a noun the form is <code>{f|同然|どうぜん}の</code>, never
❌<code>{f|同然|どうぜん}な</code>. With a familiar noun like
<code>{f|他人|たにん}</code> the `も` is usually dropped as well.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|地区|ちく}のバスは{f|一日|いちにち}{f|二本|にほん}しかない。
(b) この{f|地区|ちく}のバスは{f|無|な}い<b>も{f|同然|どうぜん}だ</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports the timetable. Two buses a day is a fact, and a listener can draw their
own conclusions from it.

(b) draws the conclusion for them: for anyone trying to get about, a service that
thin does not count as a service. It is stronger, it is clearly the speaker's
judgement rather than the timetable's, and it invites agreement - which is why it
belongs in a complaint, a letter to the council or a conversation about moving away.

</details>
