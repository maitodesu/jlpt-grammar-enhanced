# ひとつ

**Meaning** · go on, give it a try; do me this one favour - an adverbial ひとつ that has nothing to do with counting

Dropped in front of a proposal or a request, `ひとつ` does not mean "one". It warms
the sentence up: ひとつやってみるか is "right, let's have a go at it", and
ひとつよろしくお{f|願|ねが}いします is "I'd be grateful for your help". Think of it as
the Japanese of a genial "go on then".

## Usage

An adverb. Nothing attaches to it - no `を`, no `の`, no `が` - and in writing it is
usually followed by a comma. Kana is the safer spelling, because
<code>{f|一|ひと}つ</code> invites the reader to look for something being counted.

It cannot stand on its own. `ひとつ` needs a **proposal, an attempt or a request**
after it, and the shape of that clause is where the meaning lives.

- <code>ひとつ～てみる / ～てみよう / ～てみるか</code> - trying something, either as a
  suggestion to a group or as a resolution to yourself:
  ひとつ{f|試|ため}してみよう, ひとつ{f|作|つく}ってみるか.
- <code>ここはひとつ～</code> - by far the strongest collocation. It means "in this
  particular situation, let us…", and it introduces a compromise, an appeal or a plea:
  ここはひとつ{f|穏便|おんびん}に, ここはひとつ{f|私|わたし}に{f|任|まか}せてください.
- <code>ひとつよろしくお{f|願|ねが}いします</code> and its relatives -
  ひとつご{f|検討|けんとう}ください, ひとつお{f|力|ちから}をお{f|貸|か}しください. Here
  it is pure lubrication on a request.
- <code>ひとつ～てくれないか / ～ていただけませんか</code> - a favour asked as a
  favour, not as a right.

Put it in front of a plain statement and it reverts to the numeral. ひとつ{f|食|た}べた
is "I ate one", not "I went ahead and ate".

## Examples

<pre>
せっかくだから、<b>ひとつ</b>{f|挑戦|ちょうせん}してみるか。

</pre>

We'll be in the same section from next month. I look forward to working with you.
</pre>

<pre>

Look, do me a favour and let this one go, would you?
</pre>

<pre>

I've brought along a new proposal, and I'd be most grateful if you would consider it.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is an idiomatic, slightly **old-fashioned and hearty** word. It tends to belong
to middle-aged speakers and, as a tendency rather than a rule, to men - a section
head raising a glass, a shopkeeper coaxing a customer, someone talking a dispute down.
Younger speakers use it, but often with a faint sense of putting on a voice.

The parts of it are not equally marked, and it is worth separating them.

<code>ひとつよろしくお{f|願|ねが}いします</code> is ordinary, current business
Japanese. Anyone can say it on being introduced, at the start of a project, or when
handing work over. It is a shade more familiar and more personal than a bare
よろしくお{f|願|ねが}いします - it says "I'm asking you as a person, not filing a
request" - and that is exactly why it is useful.

<code>ここはひとつ</code> is the marked one. It is the language of smoothing things
over, and it carries an audible appeal to the listener's generosity. It is common in
negotiation and in fiction, especially where someone is asking to be let off, and used
by a learner in a neutral situation it will sound theatrical.

`ひとつ` adds **no politeness of its own**. It supplies warmth and ingratiation, and
it takes its politeness from the ending: ひとつやってみるか is blokey, and
ひとつご{f|検討|けんとう}ください is deferential. What it will not do is soften an
order. In front of an imperative the friendliness and the command work against each
other and the result sounds sarcastic.

One practical warning: ひとつ{f|食|た}べてみてください is genuinely ambiguous between
"try one" and "go on, try it". Japanese speakers resolve it by context and intonation.
If you need to be sure, count with {f|一|ひと}つだけ or use どうぞ instead.

## Contrast

### ひとつ vs ちょっと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both soften a request. <code>ちょっと</code> shrinks it ("it's only
a small thing"). `ひとつ` does not shrink anything - it appeals to the listener's
goodwill and asks them to grant a favour.

</div>

<pre>
すみません、<b>ちょっと</b>{f|手伝|てつだ}ってもらえますか。
Sorry - could you give me a hand for a second?
</pre>

<pre>
ここは<b>ひとつ</b>、{f|力|ちから}を{f|貸|か}してもらえないだろうか。
Come on - couldn't you help me out here?
</pre>

The first is an everyday, low-stakes ask, and `ちょっと` is available to any speaker in
any register. The second concedes that the favour is a real one and leans on the
relationship to get it. Swap them and both sentences go wrong:
ひとつ{f|手伝|てつだ}ってもらえますか makes a two-minute job sound like a matter of
honour, and ちょっと{f|力|ちから}を{f|貸|か}してもらえないだろうか trivialises
something the speaker has just made a plea about.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}、<b>ひとつ</b>{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}ました。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}、{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}ました。
  In front of a plain past statement, `ひとつ` can only be the numeral - and films are
  not usually counted with つ. The adverbial use needs a proposal or a request after
  it.

- ❌ <b>ひとつ</b>{f|窓|まど}を{f|閉|し}めろ。 → ✅ ここは<b>ひとつ</b>、{f|窓|まど}を{f|閉|し}めてもらえないか。
  The word asks for goodwill; an imperative does not. Together they sound sarcastic.

- ❌ <b>ひとつの</b>お{f|願|ねが}いがあります。 → ✅ <b>ひとつ</b>お{f|願|ねが}いできませんか。
  With `の` it becomes the numeral again, and the sentence turns into "I have one
  request". As an adverb it takes no particle at all.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、<b>ひとつ</b>{f|説明|せつめい}しろとおっしゃいましたよね。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|説明|せつめい}するようにとおっしゃいましたよね。
  `ひとつ` belongs to the person doing the asking, not to a report of someone else's
  instruction. It cannot be inserted into a quotation of an order.

## Practice

**1.** {f|久|ひさ}しぶりに{f|集|あつ}まったんだから、____みんなで{f|写真|しゃしん}でも{f|撮|と}ってみようか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>ひとつ</b>みんなで{f|写真|しゃしん}でも{f|撮|と}ってみようか

The sentence already has the shape `ひとつ` needs: a suggestion in
<code>～てみようか</code>. The adverb adds the genial "go on then" that makes it an
invitation rather than a plan, and it fits the warm, reunion tone of the first clause.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: {f|課長|かちょう}、ひとつの{f|相談|そうだん}がありますので、{f|後|あと}でお{f|時間|じかん}をください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|課長|かちょう}、<b>ひとつ</b>ご{f|相談|そうだん}したいことがありますので、{f|後|あと}でお{f|時間|じかん}をいただけませんか。

Two things. The `の` turns the word back into the numeral, and the adverbial `ひとつ`
takes no particle. And since `ひとつ` is asking a favour, the request itself should be
phrased as one - ください is a shade blunt to a superior here.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) よろしくお{f|願|ねが}いします。
(b) <b>ひとつ</b>よろしくお{f|願|ねが}いします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the standard formula. It is correct everywhere, and it is what you write at the
end of an email or say on being introduced.

(b) is warmer and more personal. The `ひとつ` frames the request as something the
speaker is asking of *you* specifically, with a slight tilt of the head - "do look
after me, won't you". It is common when someone is about to depend on the listener,
and it has a faintly hearty, older-generation flavour, so it suits a greeting between
adults at work better than an email to a customer you have never met.

</details>
