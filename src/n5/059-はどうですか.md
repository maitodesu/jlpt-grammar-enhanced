# ～はどうですか

**Meaning** · how about ~? / how is ~? - putting an option on the table, or asking for an impression

`～はどうですか` does two related jobs, and Japanese does not distinguish them
grammatically:

- **Suggesting** - you offer something and ask what the listener makes of it.
  {f|土曜日|どようび}はどうですか。「How about Saturday?」
- **Asking for an evaluation** - you ask how something is, or was.
  {f|新|あたら}しい{f|仕事|しごと}はどうですか。「How's the new job?」

Underneath, both are the same question: *what is your assessment of this?* Tense and
context decide which one the listener hears.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + はどうですか | コーヒー → コーヒーはどうですか |
| Verb (proposing an action) | Dictionary + のは + どうですか | {f|歩|ある}く → {f|歩|ある}いて{f|行|い}くのはどうですか |
| Verb (advising the listener) | た-form + らどうですか | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}んだらどうですか |
| Past (a finished event) | ～はどうでしたか | {f|旅行|りょこう} → {f|旅行|りょこう}はどうでしたか |
| Politer | ～はいかがですか | お{f|茶|ちゃ}はいかがですか |
| Softer, more tentative | ～はどうでしょうか | {f|水曜日|すいようび}はどうでしょうか |
| Casual | ～はどう？ | これはどう？ |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `どう` asks *how / in what state*. It is not `どんな`, which asks
*what kind of* and must be followed by a noun.
✅{f|映画|えいが}はどうでしたか。 ✅どんな{f|映画|えいが}でしたか。
❌{f|映画|えいが}はどんなでしたか。

</div>

## Nuance & register

`どうですか` is polite and completely ordinary, but it is *plain* polite - the same
level as `です／ます` and no higher. Two situations expose that.

The first is **offering something to a superior or a customer**.
{f|部長|ぶちょう}、コーヒーはどうですか is not rude, exactly, but it is the register
you would use with a colleague. お{f|茶|ちゃ}はいかがですか is what waiters, hosts and
careful employees say, and it costs nothing to learn.

The second is **asking someone senior how something is going**.
{f|論文|ろんぶん}はどうですか to your supervisor can sound like you are checking up on
them. いかがですか, or {f|進|すす}んでいますか, avoids the edge.

For proposals, `どうですか` is genuinely soft, because it asks for an opinion rather
than a commitment. It is the standard way to float a time, a place or an option in a
conversation, and {f|水曜日|すいようび}はどうでしょうか is softer still - `でしょうか`
adds a layer of tentativeness that is very common in business email.

Watch the tense. A finished event takes `どうでしたか`; using `どうですか` about last
night's party is a real mistake, not a stylistic one.

Casually the copula drops: これ、どう？, {f|味|あじ}はどう？ Among friends this is the
default, and it can be blunt in the sense of demanding a verdict - asking どう？
about someone's cooking while they watch you eat is as pointed in Japanese as in
English.

## Contrast

### ～はどうですか vs ～はいかがですか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different altitude. `いかがですか` is the polite
form of `どうですか`, and it is the default when offering something to a guest,
customer or superior.

</div>

<pre>
デザート<b>はどうですか</b>。
How about dessert?
</pre>

<pre>
デザート<b>はいかがですか</b>。
Would you care for a dessert?
</pre>

`いかが` is not a different word class or a different construction - everything you
can do with `どう`, you can do with `いかが`: いかがでしたか, いかがでしょうか. What
changes is who you are speaking to.

The one place `どうですか` is preferred is when you genuinely want a *judgement*
between equals: この{f|案|あん}はどうですか to a colleague asks for their honest read.
`いかがですか` in the same slot can sound as though you are serving them something.

### ～はどうですか vs ～ませんか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `はどうですか` proposes an **option** and asks what you think of
it; `ませんか` invites the **person** to do something with you.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昼|ひる}ごはんは、そばとカレーとどちらがいいですか。ここ<b>はどうですか</b>。
Soba or curry for lunch? How about here?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを<b>{f|食|た}べませんか</b>。
Would you like to have lunch together?
</pre>

The second commits you both to an activity; the first only puts a candidate forward
inside a plan you are already making. That is why `はどうですか` is so useful in
arranging things - dates, venues, options - while `ませんか` is what opens the
arrangement in the first place.

They stack naturally in one conversation: {f|食|た}べに{f|行|い}きませんか opens it,
{f|金曜日|きんようび}はどうですか settles the detail.

### ～はどうですか vs ～たらどうですか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `はどうですか` offers an option; `たらどうですか` tells the
listener what *they* should do. The second is advice, and it can sting.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|薬|くすり}<b>はどうですか</b>。
How about this medicine?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|少|すこ}し<b>{f|休|やす}んだらどうですか</b>。
Why don't you take a bit of a rest?
</pre>

`～たらどうですか` is built on the conditional た-form and means "why don't you…".
Said warmly it is caring; said flatly it implies the listener has failed to think of
something obvious, which is why it carries a faint edge of impatience. Upward it is
best avoided altogether - ～てはいかがでしょうか is the safe version.

Casual speech shortens it to `～たら？` - {f|医者|いしゃ}に{f|行|い}ったら？ - and the
trailing, unfinished shape is exactly what makes it sound offhand.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}のパーティー<b>はどうですか</b>。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}のパーティー<b>はどうでしたか</b>。
  A finished event takes the past form.

- ❌ {f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}く<b>はどうですか</b>。 → ✅ {f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}く<b>のはどうですか</b>。
  A verb needs the nominaliser `の` before `は`.

- ❌ {f|新|あたら}しい{f|先生|せんせい}は<b>どんなですか</b>。 → ✅ {f|新|あたら}しい{f|先生|せんせい}は<b>どうですか</b>。／<b>どんな{f|人|ひと}ですか</b>。
  `どんな` cannot stand alone; it must attach to a noun.

- ❌ {f|社長|しゃちょう}、お{f|飲|の}み{f|物|もの}は<b>どうですか</b>。 → ✅ {f|社長|しゃちょう}、お{f|飲|の}み{f|物|もの}は<b>いかがですか</b>。
  Offering to someone senior calls for `いかが`.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、もっと{f|早|はや}く<b>{f|帰|かえ}ったらどうですか</b>。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、もっと{f|早|はや}く<b>お{f|帰|かえ}りになってはいかがでしょうか</b>。
  Advice aimed upward needs a much softer frame.

## Practice

**1.** つかれた{f|顔|かお}をしていますね。{f|少|すこ}し{f|休|やす}む____どうですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|少|すこ}し{f|休|やす}む<b>のは</b>どうですか。

A verb cannot be followed directly by `は`; the nominaliser `の` turns
<code>{f|休|やす}む</code> into something the topic particle can mark. You could also say
<code>{f|休|やす}んだらどうですか</code>, which is more directly advice.

</details>

**2.** お{f|客|きゃく}さんに：「お{f|茶|ちゃ}は（どうですか・いかがですか）。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お{f|茶|ちゃ}は<b>いかがですか</b>。

`いかが` is the polite counterpart of `どう`, and offering refreshment to a guest or
customer is exactly where it belongs. <code>どうですか</code> is not wrong, just level
with a colleague rather than with a customer.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "How was the exam yesterday?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|試験|しけん}<b>はどうでしたか</b>。

The exam is over, so the question has to be in the past: <code>どうでしたか</code>.
<code>どうですか</code> would be asking about an exam that is still going on.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|新|あたら}しいアパートはどんなですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|新|あたら}しいアパートは<b>どうですか</b>。

`どんな` is an attributive - it has to sit in front of a noun, as in
<code>どんなアパートですか</code>. To ask how something is, on its own, the word is `どう`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|明日|あした}<b>はどうですか</b>。
(b) {f|明日|あした}{f|会|あ}い<b>ませんか</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) puts a date forward as a candidate. It assumes you are already arranging to meet
and asks the other person to evaluate the option - it is about the *day*, not about
whether to meet at all.

(b) is the invitation itself. It asks whether the listener wants to meet you, and it
is the sentence that opens the arrangement rather than filling in its details.

</details>
