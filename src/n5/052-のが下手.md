# ～のが下手

**Meaning** · to be bad at something - a lack of skill, plainly stated

<code>{f|下手|へた}</code> is a **な-adjective**, the mirror image of <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code>. What you
are bad at is marked with `が`, and an *action* has to be nominalised with `の`
first. Because it is a blunt verdict, it is comfortable about yourself - where it
reads as modesty - and uncomfortable about anyone else.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (dictionary form) | Dictionary + のが + {f|下手|へた} | {f|踊|おど}る → {f|踊|おど}るのが{f|下手|へた} |
| Noun | Noun + が + {f|下手|へた} | テニス → テニスが{f|下手|へた} |
| Person + thing | Person + は + Thing + が + {f|下手|へた} | {f|私|わたし}は{f|絵|え}が{f|下手|へた}です |
| Adverbially (modifying a verb) | {f|下手|へた}に + Verb | {f|下手|へた}に{f|動|うご}く |

| な-adjective form | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Plain | {f|下手|へた}だ | {f|踊|おど}るのが{f|下手|へた}だ |
| Polite | {f|下手|へた}です | {f|踊|おど}るのが{f|下手|へた}です |
| Negative | {f|下手|へた}じゃない | {f|下手|へた}じゃないです |
| Past | {f|下手|へた}だった／{f|下手|へた}でした | {f|下手|へた}でした |
| Before a noun | {f|下手|へた}な + Noun | {f|下手|へた}な{f|絵|え} |
| Softened | あまり{f|上手|じょうず}じゃない | あまり{f|上手|じょうず}じゃないです |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|下手|へた}</code> ends in `た`, not `い`, so nothing about it is an
い-adjective: ❌<code>{f|下手|へた}くない</code>, ❌<code>{f|下手|へた}かった</code>. It is
<code>{f|下手|へた}じゃない</code> and <code>{f|下手|へた}だった</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}は<b>{f|泳|およ}ぐのが{f|下手|へた}</b>です。
I'm a bad swimmer.
</pre>

<pre>
うちの{f|父|ちち}は<b>{f|運転|うんてん}が{f|下手|へた}</b>だ。
My dad is a bad driver.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}を<b>{f|片付|かたづ}けるのが{f|下手|へた}</b>です。
I'm bad at tidying my room.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昔|むかし}は{f|人|ひと}の{f|前|まえ}で<b>{f|話|はな}すのが{f|下手|へた}</b>でした。
I used to be bad at speaking in front of people.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}はまだはしを<b>{f|使|つか}うのが{f|下手|へた}</b>です。
My little brother still isn't good with chopsticks.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|兄|あに}はサッカーは{f|上手|じょうず}ですが、テニスは<b>{f|下手|へた}</b>です。
My older brother is good at football but bad at tennis.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}は<b>{f|断|ことわ}るのが{f|下手|へた}</b>なので、いつも{f|仕事|しごと}が{f|多|おお}すぎます。
I'm bad at saying no, so I always end up with too much work.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|字|じ}が<b>{f|下手|へた}</b>ですから、メールで{f|送|おく}ってもいいですか。
My handwriting is terrible, so would it be all right if I sent it by email?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Pointed at yourself, <code>{f|下手|へた}</code> is safe and even likeable. Japanese conversation
runs on modesty, and {f|料理|りょうり}が{f|下手|へた}なんです is a normal, friendly thing
to say about your own cooking. There is no need to soften it.

Pointed at anyone else it is a judgement, and a harsh one. Telling a colleague
{f|英語|えいご}が{f|下手|へた}ですね is close to an insult in English terms. The two
standard repairs are あまり{f|上手|じょうず}じゃない (a hedged negative) and
{f|苦手|にがて} (a weakness rather than a failing) - and about a superior you simply do
not go there at all.

<code>{f|下手|へた}くそ</code> exists and is much cruder; it is what one friend shouts at
another over a video game, not something to try on strangers. Note also that unlike
<code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code>, <code>{f|下手|へた}</code> has no polite お-form in ordinary use - there is
nothing to be honorific about.

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|下手|へた}に</code> before a verb has drifted away from "badly". It means
*carelessly, without thinking, rashly*:
{f|下手|へた}に{f|動|うご}くと{f|危|あぶ}ないです「Move carelessly and you'll get hurt」.
The literal "does it badly" sense is usually carried by
{f|下手|へた}な{f|絵|え} or あまり{f|上手|じょうず}じゃない instead.

</div>

## Contrast

### {f|下手|へた} vs {f|苦手|にがて}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|下手|へた}</code> is about **skill**; <code>{f|苦手|にがて}</code> is about how
you **feel** about it. <code>{f|苦手|にがて}</code> covers things you have no skill in at all - 
food, insects, particular people.

</div>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}は{f|歌|うた}を<b>{f|歌|うた}うのが{f|下手|へた}</b>です。
I'm a bad singer.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}はカラオケが<b>{f|苦手|にがて}</b>です。
I'm not keen on karaoke.
</pre>

The first is a claim about your voice. The second says karaoke makes you
uncomfortable - perhaps because you cannot sing, perhaps because you hate being
looked at. That extra emotional layer is why <code>{f|苦手|にがて}</code> reaches places
<code>{f|下手|へた}</code> cannot: なっとうが{f|苦手|にがて}です and
あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|少|すこ}し{f|苦手|にがて}です are both natural, while
❌なっとうが{f|下手|へた}です is nonsense - you do not perform natto.

<code>{f|苦手|にがて}</code> is also the kinder word about other people, because "he finds it
hard" is gentler than "he is bad at it": {f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|数字|すうじ}が{f|苦手|にがて}みたいです.

### {f|下手|へた} vs あまり{f|上手|じょうず}じゃない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - about **yourself**, <code>{f|下手|へた}</code>. About **other people**, the
hedged negative あまり{f|上手|じょうず}じゃない.

</div>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}は<b>{f|下手|へた}</b>ですが、やってみます。
I'm bad at this, but I'll give it a go.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|料理|りょうり}が<b>あまり{f|上手|じょうず}じゃない</b>です。
He isn't a very good cook.
</pre>

The hedge does two things at once. あまり plus a negative is the standard Japanese
way of saying "not very", and phrasing the criticism as a *shortfall of skill*
rather than *presence of badness* takes the sting out. Add みたいです or
ようです and it softens again, because you are now reporting an impression rather
than passing sentence.

The asymmetry is the same one that governs <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code>, run in reverse: you
praise other people and criticise yourself, never the other way round.

### Noun が{f|下手|へた} vs Verb のが{f|下手|へた}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if a single noun names the skill, use it bare. Reach for `のが`
when the skill only exists as an action.

</div>

<pre>
{f|姉|あね}はピアノが<b>{f|下手|へた}</b>です。
My older sister is bad at the piano.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|姉|あね}はピアノを<b>{f|人|ひと}の{f|前|まえ}で{f|弾|ひ}くのが{f|下手|へた}</b>です。
My older sister is bad at playing the piano in front of an audience.
</pre>

Nouns that already name an activity - ピアノ, テニス, {f|料理|りょうり},
{f|運転|うんてん} - go straight in with `が`. Adding <code>を{f|弾|ひ}くのが</code> is not wrong,
it just spells the activity out, which is what you want as soon as the description
gets more specific than the noun can carry.

And some skills have no noun at all. There is no single word for "remembering
people's names" or "saying no", so the verb clause is the only route:
{f|人|ひと}の{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|覚|おぼ}えるのが{f|下手|へた}です. What you cannot do is
drop the `の` and hand the raw verb to the adjective.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ なっとうが<b>{f|下手|へた}</b>です。 → ✅ なっとうが<b>{f|苦手|にがて}</b>です。
  Food is not a skill. <code>{f|苦手|にがて}</code> is the word for things you would rather avoid.

- ❌ {f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|英語|えいご}が<b>{f|下手|へた}</b>ですね。 → ✅ {f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|英語|えいご}が<b>あまり{f|上手|じょうず}じゃない</b>ですね。
  Direct <code>{f|下手|へた}</code> about another person is rude. Hedge it, or use
  <code>{f|苦手|にがて}</code>.

- ❌ {f|踊|おど}る<b>が</b>{f|下手|へた}です。 → ✅ {f|踊|おど}る<b>のが</b>{f|下手|へた}です。
  The verb has to be nominalised by `の` before it can take `が`.

- ❌ {f|下手|へた}<b>くない</b>です。 → ✅ {f|下手|へた}<b>じゃない</b>です。
  <code>{f|下手|へた}</code> is a な-adjective. The final `た` is part of the reading, not an
  adjective ending.

- ❌ {f|下手|へた}<b>の</b>{f|絵|え} → ✅ {f|下手|へた}<b>な</b>{f|絵|え}
  な-adjectives take `な` before a noun.

## Practice

**1.** {f|私|わたし}は{f|人|ひと}に{f|道|みち}を{f|説明|せつめい}する____{f|下手|へた}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|道|みち}を{f|説明|せつめい}する<b>のが</b>{f|下手|へた}です。

<code>{f|説明|せつめい}する</code> is a verb, so `の` turns it into something `が` can mark. You
could also say <code>{f|説明|せつめい}が{f|下手|へた}です</code>, using the noun on its own.

</details>

**2.** {f|課長|かちょう}に：「{f|部長|ぶちょう}はゴルフが（{f|下手|へた}です・あまり{f|上手|じょうず}じゃないです）ね。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|部長|ぶちょう}はゴルフがあまり<b>{f|上手|じょうず}じゃないです</b>ね。

Even this is risky about a superior, but of the two it is the survivable one. A
flat <code>{f|下手|へた}</code> about someone above you is a straight insult; the hedged
negative reads as an observation rather than a verdict.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'm bad at getting up early."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|私|わたし}は{f|朝|あさ}{f|早|はや}く<b>{f|起|お}きるのが{f|下手|へた}</b>です。

About yourself, <code>{f|下手|へた}</code> is the modest, natural choice. Many speakers would
also say <code>{f|苦手|にがて}です</code> here, which adds "and I dislike it" to the meaning.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|私|わたし}は{f|歌|うた}が{f|下手|へた}くなかったです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|歌|うた}が<b>{f|下手|へた}じゃなかった</b>です。

<code>{f|下手|へた}</code> only looks like an い-adjective. It is a な-adjective, so the
negative past is <code>じゃなかった</code>, exactly as with <code>{f|静|しず}かじゃなかった</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|私|わたし}は{f|漢字|かんじ}を{f|書|か}くのが<b>{f|下手|へた}</b>です。
(b) {f|私|わたし}は{f|漢字|かんじ}が<b>{f|苦手|にがて}</b>です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a statement about the output: your kanji come out badly formed. It says
nothing about how you feel while writing them.

(b) is about your relationship with kanji - they are your weak point, you avoid
them, tests involving them make you nervous. It may or may not imply that your
handwriting is poor, and it is the sentence a learner is usually reaching for when
they want to say "kanji are my nemesis".

</details>
