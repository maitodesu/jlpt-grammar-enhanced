# ～前に

**Meaning** · before doing X - X hasn't happened yet at the moment Y takes place

<code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> puts one event in front of another on the timeline. The whole
sentence can be past, present or future; the clause in front of
<code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> is always in the **dictionary form**, because from the
viewpoint of the main clause that event still lies ahead.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | **dictionary form** + {f|前|まえ}に | {f|寝|ね}る → {f|寝|ね}る{f|前|まえ}に |
| Verb (in a past sentence) | still **dictionary form** | {f|来|く}る{f|前|まえ}に{f|習|なら}い**ました** |
| Verb (in a future sentence) | still **dictionary form** | {f|来|く}る{f|前|まえ}に{f|習|なら}い**ます** |
| Noun | Noun + **の** + {f|前|まえ}に | {f|食事|しょくじ} → {f|食事|しょくじ}**の**{f|前|まえ}に |
| Duration ("ago") | Quantity + {f|前|まえ}に - **no** の | {f|三日|みっか}{f|前|まえ}に、{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}{f|前|まえ}に |
| Politeness | on the **final** verb only | {f|寝|ね}る{f|前|まえ}に{f|歯|は}をみがき**ます** |
| ❌ Negative | ❌ない{f|前|まえ}に does not exist - use ～ないうちに | ❌{f|忘|わす}れない{f|前|まえ}に |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> takes the **dictionary form**, and its
mirror image <code>～たあとで</code> takes the **past form**. That asymmetry is not a
quirk of memorisation: "before eating" is a moment when the eating has not
happened yet, "after eating" is a moment when it has. Japanese marks that
literally. ❌{f|食|た}べた{f|前|まえ}に and ❌{f|食|た}べるあとで are both wrong, and for
the same reason.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the `に` can drop when the phrase is doing adverbial duty at the head
of a sentence: {f|寝|ね}る{f|前|まえ}、{f|少|すこ}し{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}みます。 Keep the `に` while
you are learning; it is never wrong.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|寝|ね}る<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、{f|歯|は}をみがきます。
I brush my teeth before going to bed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|出|で}かける<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、かぎをかけてください。
Please lock up before you go out.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|映画|えいが}が{f|始|はじ}まる<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、{f|飲|の}み{f|物|もの}を{f|買|か}っておきましょう。
Let's buy drinks before the film starts.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}の<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、トイレに{f|行|い}っておいたほうがいいですよ。
You'd be wise to go to the toilet before the exam.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日本|にほん}に{f|来|く}る<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、２{f|年|ねん}ほど{f|日本語|にほんご}を{f|習|なら}いました。
I studied Japanese for about two years before coming to Japan.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|暗|くら}くなる<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、{f|家|いえ}に{f|帰|かえ}りなさい。
Get home before it gets dark.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|始|はじ}まる<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、コピーを{f|人数|にんずう}{f|分|ぶん}{f|用意|ようい}しておきました。
I got enough copies for everyone ready before the meeting started.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}す<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、{f|市役所|しやくしょ}で{f|手続|てつづ}きをしなければなりません。
You have to do the paperwork at the city office before you move.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> is register-neutral and has no contracted form. It appears in
signs, in manuals and in ordinary chat without changing its shape.

The one thing to internalise is that **the dictionary form here carries no
tense**. Look at the fifth example again: the studying is finished and gone, the
main verb is <code>{f|習|なら}いました</code>, and yet the clause still reads {f|来|く}る{f|前|まえ}に. A
learner who translates "before I came" word for word produces
❌{f|来|き}た{f|前|まえ}に every time. The dictionary form is not "present tense" - it
is simply the unmarked form the construction demands.

Two related patterns worth recognising when you meet them:

- <b>A bare {f|前|まえ} after a quantity means "ago".</b> {f|三日|みっか}{f|前|まえ}に{f|電話|でんわ}しました - "I called three days ago." No `の`, and the sentence is
  about the past, not about an ordering.
- <b>{f|前|まえ} also means "in front of" in space</b>, and then it takes `で` or `に`
  according to the usual place rules: {f|駅|えき}の{f|前|まえ}で{f|待|ま}っています。 Same
  kanji, entirely different job.

In more formal writing you will meet <code>{f|以前|いぜん}に</code> and
<code>～に{f|先立|さきだ}ち</code> doing similar work. Neither is expected at N5.

## Contrast

### ～{f|前|まえ}に vs ～たあとで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> demands the **dictionary form**;
<code>あとで</code> demands the **た-form**. Neither one ever changes to match the
tense of the sentence.

</div>

This is the single most useful table on the page, so here it is as a pair:

<pre>
ごはんを{f|食|た}べる<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}みました。
I took the medicine before eating.
</pre>

<pre>
ごはんを{f|食|た}べ<b>たあとで</b>、{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}みました。
I took the medicine after eating.
</pre>

Both main clauses are past. Only the first clause differs, and it differs in
form, not in tense: <code>{f|食|た}べる{f|前|まえ}に</code> versus <code>{f|食|た}べたあとで</code>. Learners routinely write
❌<code>{f|食|た}べた{f|前|まえ}に</code> by copying the English past tense across. Japanese does not.

### {f|前|まえ}に vs {f|前|まえ}で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> is **time**; <code>{f|前|まえ}で</code> is
**place**, and it means physically in front of something.

</div>

<pre>
{f|授業|じゅぎょう}の<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>{f|質問|しつもん}しました。
I asked a question before the lesson.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|教室|きょうしつ}の<b>{f|前|まえ}で</b>{f|質問|しつもん}しました。
I asked a question at the front of the classroom.
</pre>

The two are told apart by the noun as much as by the particle: a *time* noun
({f|授業|じゅぎょう}、{f|食事|しょくじ}、{f|試合|しあい}) makes it temporal, a *place* noun
({f|教室|きょうしつ}、{f|駅|えき}、{f|店|みせ}) makes it spatial. With a place noun and a
verb of existence you get <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> again, but locationally:
{f|駅|えき}の{f|前|まえ}に{f|銀行|ぎんこう}があります。

### ～{f|前|まえ}に vs ～までに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> orders two events;
<code>までに</code> sets a **deadline**. If English would say "by", you want
`までに`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|始|はじ}まる<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|配|くば}ります。
I'll hand out the documents before the meeting starts.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|金曜日|きんようび}<b>までに</b>、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|出|だ}してください。
Please submit the documents by Friday.
</pre>

The difference bites with a fixed time. {f|五時|ごじ}の{f|前|まえ}に{f|帰|かえ}ります
sounds like you are arranging your afternoon around five o'clock;
{f|五時|ごじ}までに{f|帰|かえ}ります is a promise to be back no later than five. For
appointments and obligations, `までに`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|寝|ね}**た**{f|前|まえ}に、{f|電気|でんき}を{f|消|け}しました。 → ✅ {f|寝|ね}**る**{f|前|まえ}に、{f|電気|でんき}を{f|消|け}しました。
  Always the dictionary form, no matter how far in the past the sentence is. The
  English "before I went to bed" is what leads you astray.

- ❌ {f|三日|みっか}**の**{f|前|まえ}に{f|着|つ}きました。 → ✅ {f|三日|みっか}{f|前|まえ}に{f|着|つ}きました。
  A duration takes no `の`. The `の` appears only when
  <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> follows an ordinary noun: {f|食事|しょくじ}の{f|前|まえ}に.

- ❌ {f|食事|しょくじ}{f|前|まえ}に{f|手|て}を{f|洗|あら}います。 → ✅ {f|食事|しょくじ}**の**{f|前|まえ}に{f|手|て}を{f|洗|あら}います。
  The mirror error: an ordinary noun does need `の`. (Some fixed compounds such as
  {f|食前|しょくぜん} exist, but they are separate words with their own readings.)

- ❌ {f|駅|えき}の{f|前|まえ}**に**{f|待|ま}っています。 → ✅ {f|駅|えき}の{f|前|まえ}**で**{f|待|ま}っています。
  Waiting is an action, and actions happen `で`. `に` in front of
  <code>{f|前|まえ}</code> marks location only with existence verbs.

- ❌ {f|忘|わす}れない{f|前|まえ}に、メモしておきます。 → ✅ {f|忘|わす}れない**うちに**、メモしておきます。
  There is no negative <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code>. "While it hasn't yet happened" is
  `～ないうちに`.

## Practice

**1.** {f|旅行|りょこう}に____{f|前|まえ}に、パスポートを{f|確認|かくにん}してください。（{f|行|い}く）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}く<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、パスポートを{f|確認|かくにん}してください。

Dictionary form, unchanged. Even though the trip is in the future and the check
comes first, the verb in front of <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> is never conjugated - 
neither for tense nor for politeness.

</details>

**2.** {f|日本|にほん}に{f|来|く}る{f|前|まえ}に、{f|中国|ちゅうごく}に（{f|住|す}む・{f|住|す}んでいました）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|日本|にほん}に{f|来|く}る{f|前|まえ}に、{f|中国|ちゅうごく}に<b>{f|住|す}んでいました</b>。

The dictionary form belongs to the <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> clause and nowhere
else. All the tense in the sentence is carried by the **main** verb at the end,
which here has to be past - hence <code>{f|住|す}んでいました</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Wash the vegetables before you cut them."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|野菜|やさい}は{f|切|き}る<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>{f|洗|あら}ってください。

<code>{f|切|き}る</code> stays in the dictionary form; the instruction ("please wash")
sits at the end. Note the Japanese order is the reverse of the English one - the
<code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> clause comes first, and the main verb last.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|試験|しけん}の{f|前|まえ}で、よく{f|勉強|べんきょう}しました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|試験|しけん}の{f|前|まえ}**に**、よく{f|勉強|べんきょう}しました。

<code>{f|試験|しけん}</code> is an event in time, not a place, so this is the temporal
<code>{f|前|まえ}に</code>. <code>{f|前|まえ}で</code> would have to mean "physically in
front of the exam", which is not a thing.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|六時|ろくじ}の{f|前|まえ}に{f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}きます。
(b) {f|六時|ろくじ}までに{f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}きます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply places your arrival earlier on the clock than six - it describes the
order of things and sounds a little odd on its own, because a bare clock time is
not much of an event to be "before".

(b) treats six o'clock as a deadline and commits you to arriving at some point
between now and then. That is what you say when someone is waiting for you or a
train is leaving. With fixed times and obligations, Japanese overwhelmingly
prefers `までに`.

</details>
