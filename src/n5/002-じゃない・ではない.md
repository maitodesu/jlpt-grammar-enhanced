# じゃない・ではない

**Meaning** · the negative copula - "X is not Y"

`じゃない` and `ではない` are the negative of `だ` / `です`. They deny that one thing
is the other. `ではない` is the full form; `じゃない` is its everyday contraction,
and it is what you will hear almost all the time.

## Formation

`では` contracts to `じゃ` in speech, which is the whole story behind the two shapes.
Everything below comes in pairs for that reason.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (plain) | Noun + じゃない / ではない | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}じゃない |
| Noun (polite) | Noun + じゃないです / じゃありません | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}じゃありません |
| Noun (formal) | Noun + ではありません | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}ではありません |
| な-adjective | stem (no な) + じゃない | {f|静|しず}かな → {f|静|しず}かじゃない |
| い-adjective | ～い → ～くない (never じゃない) | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}くない |
| Verb | ～ない form (never じゃない) | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}かない |
| Past | じゃなかった / じゃありませんでした | {f|学生|がくせい}じゃなかった |
| Joining on | じゃなくて (て-form) | Aじゃなくて、B |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the politeness ladder runs
`じゃない` → `じゃないです` → `じゃありません` → `ではありません` → `ではございません`,
from most casual to most formal. The middle three are all "polite"; they differ only
in how starched they sound.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
これは{f|私|わたし}のかさ<b>じゃない</b>。
This isn't my umbrella.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}は{f|木曜日|もくようび}<b>じゃありません</b>。
Today isn't Thursday.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|話|はなし}は{f|本当|ほんとう}<b>じゃないです</b>よ。
That story isn't true.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}<b>じゃなくて</b>、{f|再来週|さらいしゅう}です。
The exam isn't next week - it's the week after.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|冗談|じょうだん}<b>じゃない</b>よ。{f|本当|ほんとう}の{f|話|はなし}だ。
I'm not joking. It really happened.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大学|だいがく}のとき、わたしは{f|真面目|まじめ}な{f|学生|がくせい}<b>じゃなかった</b>。
I wasn't a serious student back in university.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|辺|へん}は{f|夜|よる}あまり{f|静|しず}か<b>じゃない</b>ので、{f|窓|まど}を{f|閉|し}めて{f|寝|ね}ています。
It isn't very quiet around here at night, so I sleep with the window shut.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありませんが、これは{f|私|わたし}の{f|担当|たんとう}<b>ではありません</b>ので、{f|係|かかり}の{f|者|もの}を{f|呼|よ}びます。
I'm very sorry, but this isn't my area - I'll fetch the person in charge.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The split between `じゃ` and `では` is one of pronunciation, not of meaning.
`ではない` is what you write; `じゃない` is what you say. Reading `ではありません`
aloud in a friendly conversation sounds like reading from a form.

Within the polite forms there is a further, smaller split. `じゃありません` is the
textbook polite negative and is perfectly normal in speech. `じゃないです` is a shade
more conversational - it is built by taking the plain `じゃない` and adding `です`,
the same trick い-adjectives use - and it is extremely common in modern spoken
Japanese. `ではありません` belongs to announcements, written notices and stiff
business speech, where nobody is trying to sound warm.

One more thing about `じゃない` catches learners badly. With **falling** intonation
it negates: 「{f|山田|やまだ}さんじゃない。」 = "That's not Yamada." With **rising**
intonation it does the opposite - it becomes a tag question asserting that something
*is* so: 「{f|山田|やまだ}さんじゃない?」 = "Isn't that Yamada?" This second use can
attach to things the negative copula normally cannot, including い-adjectives and
verbs (「{f|高|たか}いじゃない!」 = "That's expensive!"). If you meet `じゃない`
attached to an い-adjective, it is this tag-question use, not negation.

In casual speech `じゃない` shortens further to `じゃん`: 「いいじゃん」 = "that's
fine, isn't it". It is friendly, young-sounding, and out of place at work.

## Contrast

### じゃない vs ～くない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `じゃない` negates **nouns and な-adjectives**. い-adjectives
negate themselves by changing `～い` to `～くない`.

</div>

The two look interchangeable in English ("is not X") but they are not.

<pre>
この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|簡単|かんたん}<b>じゃない</b>。
This problem isn't easy.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|難|むずか}し<b>くない</b>。
This problem isn't hard.
</pre>

<code>{f|簡単|かんたん}</code> is a な-adjective, so it needs the copula and takes `じゃない`.
<code>{f|難|むずか}しい</code> is an い-adjective, so it conjugates on its own. The same test as with
`だ`: if the word needs the copula to stand up, it negates with `じゃない`.

`いい` is the irregular one worth learning now: it negates as `よくない`, never
❌`いくない`.

### じゃありません vs じゃないです vs ではありません

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - all three are polite. Pick by how formal the room is, not by
what you mean.

</div>

<pre>
それは{f|私|わたし}のかばん<b>じゃないです</b>。
That's not my bag.
</pre>

<pre>
それは{f|私|わたし}のかばん<b>じゃありません</b>。
That's not my bag.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|当店|とうてん}は{f|本日|ほんじつ}{f|営業|えいぎょう}<b>ではありません</b>。
Our shop is not open for business today.
</pre>

The first is what you say to a coworker; the second is what a textbook or a careful
speaker says; the third is what a sign says. A learner who only ever produces
`じゃありません` will never sound wrong, which makes it the safe default.

### じゃない vs {f|違|ちが}う

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `じゃない` denies an equation ("A is not B"); <code>{f|違|ちが}う</code> is a verb
meaning "to differ / to be mistaken", and it is what you say to correct someone.

</div>

<pre>
これは{f|私|わたし}の{f|傘|かさ}<b>じゃない</b>です。
This isn't my umbrella.
</pre>

<pre>
「{f|田中|たなか}さんですか。」「いいえ、<b>{f|違|ちが}います</b>。」
"Are you Tanaka-san?" "No, I'm not."
</pre>

Both are heard as "no", but they work differently. <code>{f|違|ちが}います</code> is a complete
answer on its own and needs no noun in front of it, which is exactly why it is the
standard way to say "that's not right". Notice that answering a question with a bare
「{f|田中|たなか}じゃありません」 sounds blunter than 「{f|違|ちが}います」 - the
verb is the softer, more idiomatic correction.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|本|ほん}は{f|高|たか}いじゃない。（meaning "this book isn't expensive"） → ✅ この{f|本|ほん}は{f|高|たか}くない。
  い-adjectives negate with `～くない`. The string <code>{f|高|たか}いじゃない</code> does exist, but only as
  the rising tag question "it's expensive, isn't it!" - the opposite of what you meant.

- ❌ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|静|しず}かだじゃない。 → ✅ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|静|しず}かじゃない。
  `じゃない` already contains the copula. Never leave `だ` in front of it.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|雨|あめ}じゃないでした。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|雨|あめ}じゃありませんでした。
  The polite negative past is built on `ありません`, not on `です`. In plain form:
  {f|雨|あめ}じゃなかった。

- ❌ わたしはコーヒーを{f|飲|の}むじゃない。 → ✅ わたしはコーヒーを{f|飲|の}まない。
  Verbs have their own negative. `じゃない` never attaches to a verb to negate it.

## Practice

**1.** あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|先生|せんせい}____。（plain, negative）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|先生|せんせい}<b>じゃない</b>。

<code>{f|先生|せんせい}</code> is a noun, so it needs the copula, and the plain negative copula is
`じゃない`. The fuller `ではない` would be the written equivalent.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|店|みせ}は（{f|安|やす}くない・{f|安|やす}いじゃない）。 - "This shop isn't cheap."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|店|みせ}は<b>{f|安|やす}くない</b>。

<code>{f|安|やす}い</code> is an い-adjective, so it negates by changing `い` to `くない`. The other
option would be heard as a tag question meaning "it's cheap, isn't it!", which is
the opposite claim.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, politely: "It isn't Monday today - it's Tuesday."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|今日|きょう}は{f|月曜日|げつようび}<b>じゃなくて</b>、{f|火曜日|かようび}です。

The て-form `じゃなくて` links the correction to what follows, so you get one smooth
sentence instead of two. 「{f|月曜日|げつようび}じゃありません。{f|火曜日|かようび}です。」
also works and is a little more emphatic.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|去年|きょねん}のアパートは{f|便利|べんり}だじゃありませんでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|去年|きょねん}のアパートは{f|便利|べんり}じゃありませんでした。

<code>{f|便利|べんり}</code> is a な-adjective, and the negative form already carries the copula, so
the extra `だ` has to go. Only the stem is left in front of `じゃ`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|山田|やまだ}さんじゃない。
(b) {f|山田|やまだ}さんじゃない?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a), said with falling intonation, is straightforward negation: "that isn't Yamada."

(b), with a rising tail, flips into a tag question and actually claims the opposite:
"that's Yamada, isn't it?" The speaker is fairly sure and is inviting the listener to
confirm. The written form is identical, so intonation carries the whole meaning here.

</details>
