# だ / です

**Meaning** · the copula - "X is Y"

`だ` and `です` are the word that glues a noun to what it *is*. They are the same
word at two politeness levels: `です` is polite, `だ` is plain. Japanese has no verb
"to be" in the English sense - this is what stands in for it.

## Formation

`だ` / `です` come at the very end of the sentence, after the noun or な-adjective
they attach to. Nothing changes inside the noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + だ / です | {f|先生|せんせい} → {f|先生|せんせい}だ / {f|先生|せんせい}です |
| な-adjective | stem (no な) + だ / です | {f|静|しず}かな → {f|静|しず}かだ / {f|静|しず}かです |
| い-adjective | plain adjective + です (never だ) | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}いです |
| の-phrase (possession) | Noun + の + だ / です | {f|兄|あに}の → {f|兄|あに}のです |
| Question (polite) | Noun + ですか | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}ですか |
| Question (plain) | Noun + ? - the だ drops | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}? |
| Verb | does **not** take です - use ます | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}きます |

<div class="note">

**Note** - `です` after an い-adjective is doing a different job. It is not linking
anything; the adjective already is the predicate, and `です` is bolted on purely to
make the sentence polite. That is why <code>{f|高|たか}いです</code> is fine but
❌<code>{f|高|たか}いだ</code> is not: `だ` has no politeness to add.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|先生|せんせい}<b>です</b>。
Yamada-san is a teacher.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}は{f|休|やす}み<b>だ</b>。
Today's a day off.
</pre>

<pre>
わたしの{f|部屋|へや}は{f|静|しず}か<b>です</b>。
My room is quiet.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}は{f|兄|あに}の<b>です</b>。
That bicycle is my older brother's.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}<b>ですか</b>。
Is it going to rain tomorrow?
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|新|あたら}しい<b>です</b>。
This dictionary is new.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|待|ま}ち{f|合|あ}わせは{f|六時|ろくじ}に{f|駅|えき}の{f|前|まえ}<b>です</b>。
We're meeting at six in front of the station.
</pre>

<pre>
「ご{f|出身|しゅっしん}はどちら<b>ですか</b>。」「{f|大阪|おおさか}<b>です</b>。」
"Where are you from?" "Osaka."
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`です` is the default polite ending and it is safe everywhere: with strangers, at
work, in class, in a shop. If you are unsure which form to use, use `です`. It is
neither stiff nor warm - it is simply neutral-polite, and nobody will read anything
into it.

`だ` is the plain form, used with friends, family, and in your own head. It is also
the form used in writing that has no listener - notes, diaries, newspapers, essays
 - where politeness would be meaningless. What makes `だ` tricky is that in actual
speech it is very often **dropped**:

- 「これ、だれのかさ?」「わたしの。」 - the `だ` is simply not said.
- 「あの{f|人|ひと}、{f|学生|がくせい}?」 - a plain question does not keep `だ`.

Spoken `だ` that is *kept* sounds assertive and a little blunt, and it is more
common in men's casual speech. Women's casual speech drops it more often, or
softens it with `よ` / `ね` (「そうよ」「そうだね」). A learner who ends every casual
sentence with a hard `だ` sounds like a cartoon character; leaving it off is safer.

Two further forms you will meet but do not need to produce yet. `である` is the
copula of formal written argument - reports, papers, legal text; it never appears in
ordinary conversation. `でございます` is the humble-polite copula of shop staff and
announcements: 「こちらが{f|新|あたら}しいメニューでございます」. Recognising them is
enough for now.

## Contrast

### だ vs です

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same word, two registers. `です` for anyone you are being polite
to; `だ` for people you are close to, and for writing with no reader in mind.

</div>

The choice says nothing about meaning and everything about the relationship.

<pre>
こちらが{f|私|わたし}の{f|机|つくえ}<b>です</b>。
This is my desk.
</pre>

<pre>
そこ、ぼくの{f|席|せき}<b>だ</b>よ。
Hey, that's my seat.
</pre>

The second sentence is not ruder in content - it is simply aimed at a friend. Said
to a stranger, it would land as abrupt.

Politeness in Japanese is decided at the **end** of the sentence, so this one word
sets the tone for everything that came before it. Do not mix levels inside a single
sentence without a reason.

### い-adjective + です vs な-adjective + だ / です

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - い-adjectives are already complete predicates: add `です` for
politeness, and never `だ`. な-adjectives and nouns need the copula to stand up at
all.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|重|おも}い<b>です</b>。
This luggage is heavy.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|静|しず}か<b>です</b>。
This room is quiet.
</pre>

Both end in `です`, but for different reasons - and the difference surfaces the
moment you go plain. <code>{f|重|おも}い</code> stands alone as a whole sentence;
<code>{f|静|しず}か</code> cannot, and needs `だ`:

<pre>
この{f|荷物|にもつ}、<b>{f|重|おも}い</b>。
This luggage is heavy.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}、{f|静|しず}か<b>だ</b>。
This room is quiet.
</pre>

So the test is: strip the politeness away. If what is left needs propping up, it
takes `だ`.

### です vs ます

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `です` makes a **noun or adjective** sentence polite; `ます`
makes a **verb** sentence polite. You never need both.

</div>

English hides "is" inside every tense, so learners reach for `です` when a verb is
already doing the work. Japanese verbs carry their own politeness.

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|医者|いしゃ}<b>です</b>。
Tanaka-san is a doctor.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|走|はし}り<b>ます</b>。
Tanaka-san runs every day.
</pre>

❌<code>{f|走|はし}るです</code> and ❌<code>{f|走|はし}りますです</code> are both broken. The one place a verb
does meet `です` is through a nominaliser - <code>{f|走|はし}るのが{f|好|す}きです</code> - and even there
`です` is attached to <code>{f|好|す}き</code>, a な-adjective, not to the verb.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|本|ほん}は{f|高|たか}いだ。 → ✅ この{f|本|ほん}は{f|高|たか}い。
  An い-adjective is already a predicate. It takes `です` for politeness, but never `だ`.

- ❌ {f|静|しず}かだです。 → ✅ {f|静|しず}かです。
  `だ` and `です` are one word at two levels. You choose one; you never stack them.

- ❌ {f|毎朝|まいあさ}コーヒーを{f|飲|の}むです。 → ✅ {f|毎朝|まいあさ}コーヒーを{f|飲|の}みます。
  Verbs are made polite with `ます`, not with `です`.

- ❌ あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|学生|がくせい}だですか。 → ✅ あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|学生|がくせい}ですか。
  In a polite question the whole copula is `です`, and `か` follows it directly.

- ❌ これは{f|静|しず}かなです。 → ✅ これは{f|静|しず}かです。
  A な-adjective keeps its `な` only in front of a noun (<code>{f|静|しず}かな{f|部屋|へや}</code>).
  Before the copula the `な` drops.

## Practice

**1.** あの{f|建物|たてもの}は{f|図書館|としょかん}____。（polite）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

あの{f|建物|たてもの}は{f|図書館|としょかん}<b>です</b>。

A noun predicate needs the copula, and the polite copula is `です`. Nothing changes
inside the noun itself.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|難|むずか}しい（だ・です・nothing）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|難|むずか}しい<b>です</b> (polite), or {f|難|むずか}しい on its own (plain).

<code>{f|難|むずか}しい</code> is an い-adjective, so it is already a complete sentence by itself.
Add `です` if you want to be polite, but ❌<code>{f|難|むずか}しいだ</code> is never possible.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, politely: "That umbrella is my mother's."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

そのかさは{f|母|はは}の<b>です</b>。

The `の` carries the possession, and the noun it stands for ("umbrella") is simply
left out, so `です` attaches straight onto `の`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|明日|あした}{f|来|く}るですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|明日|あした}{f|来|き}ますか。

The predicate is a verb, and verbs take `ます` for politeness - `です` has no work to
do here. The question particle `か` then follows `ます`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in feel:
(a) {f|明日|あした}は{f|休|やす}みです。
(b) {f|明日|あした}は{f|休|やす}みだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The information is identical: tomorrow is a day off. Only the relationship differs.

(a) is aimed at someone you are being polite to - a colleague, a customer, a
teacher. (b) is aimed at a friend, or at nobody at all: it is also exactly how the
sentence would be written in a diary or a memo. Said out loud to a stranger, (b)
sounds abrupt.

</details>
