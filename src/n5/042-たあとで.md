# ～たあとで

**Meaning** · after X - plain chronological order, with no claim that X caused Y

<code>～たあとで</code> places one event later than another on the timeline and says
nothing else about them. It is the neutral "after": useful precisely because it
does not imply that the first thing had to happen for the second to be possible.

## Formation

The verb in front of `あとで` is in the **た-form**, and it stays in the た-form
whether the sentence is about the past, the present or the future.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | **た-form** + あとで | {f|食|た}べる → {f|食|た}べたあとで |
| Verb (voiced た-form) | だ-form + あとで | {f|飲|の}む → {f|飲|の}ん**だ**あとで |
| Verb (in a future sentence) | still **た-form** | {f|終|お}わったあとで{f|行|い}き**ます** |
| Noun | Noun + **の** + あとで | {f|仕事|しごと} → {f|仕事|しごと}**の**あとで |
| Without で | た-form + あと | {f|食|た}べたあと、コーヒーを{f|飲|の}む |
| In kanji | あと = {f|後|あと} | {f|食|た}べた{f|後|あと}で |
| Politeness | on the **final** verb only | {f|食|た}べたあとで{f|飲|の}み**ます** |
| ❌ Negative | ❌なかったあとで does not exist | ❌{f|見|み}なかったあとで |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>あとで</code> takes the **た-form**; its mirror image
<code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> takes the **dictionary form**. ❌{f|食|た}べるあとで and
❌{f|食|た}べた{f|前|まえ}に are the two halves of the same mistake. The logic: after
eating, the eating is done (た); before eating, it is not (dictionary).

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the `で` is optional and drops freely, especially in writing.
{f|授業|じゅぎょう}が{f|終|お}わったあと、{f|質問|しつもん}しました is completely natural. There is
also <code>あとに</code>, but at N5 treat it as a different word: it is about a thing
being left *in the place of* another, not about time.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
ごはんを{f|食|た}べ<b>たあとで</b>、コーヒーを{f|飲|の}みます。
I have coffee after eating.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|仕事|しごと}<b>のあとで</b>、{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}きます。
I'm going shopping after work.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}<b>たあとで</b>、みんなで{f|感想|かんそう}を{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いました。
After the film we all talked about what we thought of it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|授業|じゅぎょう}が{f|終|お}わっ<b>たあと</b>、{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|質問|しつもん}しました。
I asked the teacher a question after the lesson finished.
</pre>

<pre>
シャワーを{f|浴|あ}び<b>たあとで</b>、すぐに{f|寝|ね}てしまった。
I fell straight asleep after taking a shower.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}がやん<b>だあとで</b>、{f|空|そら}に{f|虹|にじ}が{f|出|で}ました。
A rainbow appeared in the sky after the rain stopped.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|夕|ゆう}ごはんを{f|作|つく}っ<b>たあとで</b>、{f|台所|だいどころ}をきれいに{f|片付|かたづ}けた。
After making dinner I tidied the kitchen up properly.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わっ<b>たあとで</b>、{f|少|すこ}しお{f|時間|じかん}をいただけませんか。
Could I have a few minutes of your time once the meeting is over?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>たあとで</code> is register-neutral and does not contract. Whether the sentence
is polite is settled entirely by the final verb, exactly as with
<code>{f|前|まえ}に</code>.

Its defining quality is **neutrality**. It reports a sequence and stops there. If
you want to say that the first event made the second possible, or that you were
waiting for it, <code>たあとで</code> is too flat and you want `てから` instead. This
is why <code>たあとで</code> feels slightly odd in instructions: a sign that read
{f|手|て}を{f|洗|あら}ったあとで{f|食|た}べてください is grammatical but sounds like it is
describing your habits rather than telling you what to do.

Two smaller habits:

- <b>{f|後|あと} written in kanji is common in adult writing</b>, and the reading is
  あと, not うしろ. Same character, two words: {f|後|あと}で = "later, afterwards";
  {f|後|うし}ろ = "behind". A learner who reads them the wrong way round produces
  sentences about standing behind a meeting.
- **<code>あとで</code> also stands alone as an adverb meaning "later"**, with no
  clause in front of it at all: あとで{f|電話|でんわ}するね。 That usage is extremely
  common in speech and is not the same grammar point, though it is obviously the
  same word.

## Contrast

### ～たあとで vs ～てから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>たあとで</code> reports the order; `てから` claims the
first event was a **precondition** for the second. If you could insert "and only
then" in English, use `てから`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|宿題|しゅくだい}をし<b>たあとで</b>、{f|友|とも}だちに{f|電話|でんわ}した。
I phoned my friend after doing my homework. (What I did, in order.)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|宿題|しゅくだい}をし<b>てから</b>、{f|友|とも}だちに{f|電話|でんわ}した。
I did my homework first, and then phoned my friend. (The homework had to come first.)
</pre>

Three practical splits:

- Rules, instructions and permissions want `てから`, because the whole point is
  the gate: お{f|金|かね}を{f|払|はら}ってから{f|入|はい}ってください。
- Elapsed time takes `てから` only: {f|結婚|けっこん}してから{f|十年|じゅうねん}になります。
  ❌{f|結婚|けっこん}したあとで{f|十年|じゅうねん}になります is wrong.
- A noun can only take <code>のあとで</code>. There is no ❌{f|仕事|しごと}のてから.

And do not miss the form difference: `てから` needs the **て-form**,
<code>たあとで</code> the **た-form**.

### ～たあとで vs ～{f|前|まえ}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - mirror images, and the mirror includes the verb form.
<code>たあとで</code> = **た-form**; <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> = **dictionary form**.

</div>

Put the two side by side on the same pair of events and the pattern is obvious.

<pre>
{f|会|かい}が{f|終|お}わっ<b>たあとで</b>、{f|写真|しゃしん}をとりました。
We took photos after the party ended.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会|かい}が{f|終|お}わる<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、{f|写真|しゃしん}をとりました。
We took photos before the party ended.
</pre>

Both main clauses are past, and neither first clause is marked for tense at all - 
<code>{f|終|お}わった</code> and <code>{f|終|お}わる</code> are chosen by the *construction*, not by when things
happened. English speakers reliably write ❌{f|終|お}わった{f|前|まえ}に because English
says "before it ended". Japanese never does.

There is one more asymmetry worth noticing: a noun takes `の` with both
(<code>{f|会|かい}のあとで</code>, <code>{f|会|かい}の{f|前|まえ}に</code>), so the noun
pattern gives you no clue about which verb form to use. Learn the verb forms as a
pair.

### <code>あとで</code> on its own vs ～たあとで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with a clause in front, <code>あとで</code> means "after
[that event]". With nothing in front, it means simply "later on".

</div>

<pre>
{f|電話|でんわ}が{f|終|お}わっ<b>たあとで</b>、{f|返事|へんじ}します。
I'll reply after I finish the phone call.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>あとで</b>{f|返事|へんじ}します。
I'll reply later.
</pre>

The second sentence names no reference point at all - it just pushes the action
into the unspecified future, which is why it is a favourite for putting things
off. Note that {f|後|あと}でね。 on its own ("see you later", "in a bit") is a
complete utterance in casual speech.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ごはんを{f|食|た}べ**る**あとで、{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}みます。 → ✅ ごはんを{f|食|た}べ**た**あとで、{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}みます。
  <code>あとで</code> always takes the た-form, even when the whole sentence is about
  the future. Nothing here is being said about *when* - only about order.

- ❌ {f|授業|じゅぎょう}あとで{f|質問|しつもん}します。 → ✅ {f|授業|じゅぎょう}**の**あとで{f|質問|しつもん}します。
  A noun needs `の` before <code>あとで</code>. Only a verb can attach directly, and
  only in the た-form.

- ❌ {f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|後|うし}ろで{f|話|はな}しましょう。 → ✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|後|あと}で{f|話|はな}しましょう。
  <code>{f|後|うし}ろ</code> is a place word meaning "behind". The time word is
  <code>{f|後|あと}</code>, and it is written without the ろ.

- ❌ {f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}んだあとで、{f|運転|うんてん}してもいいです。 → ✅ {f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}ん**でから**、{f|運転|うんてん}してもいいです。
  Not a grammar error, a nuance error. Permission depends on the first event, so
  the sentence wants the precondition marker `てから`.

- ❌ {f|日本|にほん}に{f|来|き}たあとで、３{f|年|ねん}になります。 → ✅ {f|日本|にほん}に{f|来|き}**てから**、３{f|年|ねん}になります。
  Only `てから` can measure elapsed time from a starting point.
  <code>たあとで</code> needs a second *event*, not a duration.

## Practice

**1.** {f|昼|ひる}ごはんを____あとで、{f|散歩|さんぽ}しませんか。（{f|食|た}べる）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べ**た**あとで、{f|散歩|さんぽ}しませんか。

The た-form is required even though the lunch has not happened yet - the
invitation is about the future. <code>あとで</code> fixes the form of the verb in
front of it and leaves all the tense to the final verb.

</details>

**2.** {f|大学|だいがく}を{f|出|で}（たあとで・てから）、もう{f|十年|じゅうねん}になります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|大学|だいがく}を{f|出|で}**てから**、もう{f|十年|じゅうねん}になります。

Counting years from a starting point is a job `てから` does and
<code>たあとで</code> cannot. <code>たあとで</code> needs a second event to place on
the timeline, and "ten years have become" is a duration, not an event.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'll do the dishes after the guests go home."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お{f|客|きゃく}さんが{f|帰|かえ}っ**たあとで**、{f|皿|さら}を{f|洗|あら}います。

The washing-up is not conditional on the guests leaving in any interesting way - 
it is just the next thing that happens - so the neutral <code>たあとで</code> fits
better than `てから`. Note again the た-form on a future event.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|仕事|しごと}たあとで、{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}きましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|仕事|しごと}**の**あとで、{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}きましょう。

<code>{f|仕事|しごと}</code> is a noun, and a noun links to <code>あとで</code> with `の`.
The `た` in <code>たあとで</code> is not part of the phrase - it is the past ending
of a verb, and there is no verb here.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|手|て}を{f|洗|あら}ったあとで、パンを{f|食|た}べた。
(b) {f|手|て}を{f|洗|あら}ってから、パンを{f|食|た}べた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a report: two things happened, in that order. It would be a fine sentence
in a diary, and it carries no suggestion that the hand-washing mattered.

(b) says the washing came first *because it should* - the bread was not going to
be touched until the hands were clean. Parents, food-safety notices and anyone
explaining a rule use (b). Same events, but (b) adds a reason for the order that
(a) leaves out entirely.

</details>
