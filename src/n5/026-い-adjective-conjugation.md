# い-adjective conjugation

**Meaning** · how い-adjectives change for negative, past, and linking - 
くない, かった, くなかった, くて

An い-adjective is a word like {f|高|たか}い, {f|新|あたら}しい or
{f|楽|たの}しい: it ends in い in its dictionary form, and - this is the point - 
it **conjugates by itself**. It does not lean on the copula the way a noun does.
{f|高|たか}い carries its own past tense, {f|高|たか}かった, with no `でした`
anywhere in sight. Learners who treat an い-adjective like a noun produce
❌{f|高|たか}いでした, and it is the single most recognisable beginner error in
the language.

## Formation

Chop the final い off and replace it. Everything below is one operation on that
stem.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective (plain non-past) | ～い | {f|安|やす}い |
| い-adjective (polite non-past) | ～い + です | {f|安|やす}いです |
| い-adjective (negative) | い → くない | {f|安|やす}くない |
| い-adjective (past) | い → かった | {f|安|やす}かった |
| い-adjective (past negative) | い → くなかった | {f|安|やす}くなかった |
| い-adjective (て-form) | い → くて | {f|安|やす}くて |
| い-adjective (adverb) | い → く | {f|安|やす}く{f|買|か}う |
| い-adjective (before a noun) | ～い + Noun | {f|安|やす}い{f|店|みせ} |

Politeness is added afterwards, by putting `です` on the finished form - it never
changes the form itself:

| Plain | Polite (everyday) | Polite (formal) |
|---|---|---|
| {f|寒|さむ}い | {f|寒|さむ}いです | {f|寒|さむ}いです |
| {f|寒|さむ}くない | {f|寒|さむ}くないです | {f|寒|さむ}くありません |
| {f|寒|さむ}かった | {f|寒|さむ}かったです | {f|寒|さむ}かったです |
| {f|寒|さむ}くなかった | {f|寒|さむ}くなかったです | {f|寒|さむ}くありませんでした |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the past tense lives **inside the adjective**, never on
`です`. {f|寒|さむ}かったです is correct; ❌{f|寒|さむ}いでした is not. `です`
here is a politeness marker only, and it does not inflect.

</div>

**いい is irregular.** It is the one い-adjective that does not conjugate on its
own stem: every form except the dictionary form is built on its older shape
**よい**.

| Form | いい / よい |
|---|---|
| Plain non-past | いい（よい） |
| Negative | よくない |
| Past | よかった |
| Past negative | よくなかった |
| て-form | よくて |
| Adverb | よく |
| Polite past | よかったです |

There is no ❌いくない and no ❌いかった. The irregularity spreads to every
compound built on いい: かっこいい → かっこよかった, ちょうどいい →
ちょうどよくない, {f|都合|つごう}がいい → {f|都合|つごう}がよかった.

<div class="note">

**Note** - a handful of words end in い but are **not** い-adjectives. きれい,
{f|有名|ゆうめい}, {f|嫌|きら}い are な-adjectives; the negative of きれい is
きれいじゃない, not ❌きれくない. One partial test: a genuine い-adjective never
ends in **えい**, so きれい and {f|有名|ゆうめい} are ruled out on sight.
{f|嫌|きら}い ends in あい and passes the test, so that one simply has to be
memorised. Going the other way, {f|大|おお}きい and {f|小|ちい}さい really are
い-adjectives, though they also have the special prenominal forms
{f|大|おお}きな and {f|小|ちい}さな.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
このスープは<b>{f|熱|あつ}くない</b>。
This soup isn't hot.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|映画|えいが}は<b>{f|面白|おもしろ}かった</b>。
Yesterday's film was good.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}のパンは<b>{f|安|やす}くて</b>おいしいです。
The bread at this shop is cheap and tasty.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}より{f|野菜|やさい}が<b>{f|高|たか}く</b>なりました。
Vegetables have got more expensive than last year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|天気|てんき}が<b>よくなかった</b>ので、{f|山|やま}に{f|登|のぼ}りませんでした。
The weather wasn't good, so we didn't climb the mountain.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}は{f|朝|あさ}から<b>{f|忙|いそが}しくて</b>、{f|昼|ひる}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べる{f|時間|じかん}もなかった。
I've been busy since the morning and didn't even have time for lunch.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}は{f|思|おも}っていたほど<b>{f|難|むずか}しくなかった</b>です。
The exam wasn't as hard as I'd been expecting.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どものころは{f|夏休|なつやす}みが<b>{f|長|なが}かった</b>のに、{f|今|いま}は1{f|週間|しゅうかん}しか{f|休|やす}めない。
The summer holidays were so long when I was a child, and now I only get a week off.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

い-adjectives are register-neutral in themselves; the register is set by what
you put after them. Plain {f|寒|さむ}い to a friend, {f|寒|さむ}いです to
anyone else.

The negative has a genuine register split that learners are rarely told about.
`～くないです` and `～くありません` mean the same thing, but `～くありません`
sounds noticeably more formal and a little stiff - it belongs to announcements,
written notices, and careful speech. In ordinary conversation
{f|高|たか}くないです is what people actually say. The past is more lopsided
still: {f|高|たか}くなかったです is normal speech, while
{f|高|たか}くありませんでした sounds like a customer-service script.

A bare い-adjective is a complete sentence, and Japanese uses them that way
constantly: {f|寒|さむ}い! {f|痛|いた}い! おいしい! Adding `です` to
these exclamations makes them oddly formal - nobody yelps ❌{f|痛|いた}いです
when they stub a toe.

Two things to be careful of:

- `です` after an い-adjective is **not** the copula doing its usual work; it is
  purely a politeness ending. That is why it cannot inflect for tense, and why
  ❌{f|高|たか}いでした fails.
- Adjectives describing another person's inner state - {f|寂|さび}しい,
  {f|欲|ほ}しい, {f|痛|いた}い - cannot be asserted flatly about a third person.
  You need そう, ようだ, or `～がる`: {f|弟|おとうと}は{f|寂|さび}しがっている.

Finally, `～く` is the adverb, and it does far more work than English learners
expect: {f|早|はや}く{f|起|お}きる "get up early", {f|大|おお}きく{f|書|か}く
"write it big", よく{f|寝|ね}た "slept well".

## Contrast

### い-adjective vs な-adjective

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - an い-adjective inflects **itself**; a な-adjective leaves
the work to the copula. So the tense marker moves: {f|高|たか}かった against
{f|静|しず}かだった.

</div>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}のアパートは<b>{f|狭|せま}かった</b>。
Last year's flat was cramped.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}のアパートは<b>{f|静|しず}かだった</b>。
Last year's flat was quiet.
</pre>

Both sentences are past tense; the machinery differs completely. In the first,
い became かった inside the word. In the second, the adjective did not change at
all and `だ` became `だった`. This is why the negatives are also
{f|狭|せま}くない against {f|静|しず}かじゃない - and why mixing them up gives
❌{f|狭|せま}いじゃない or ❌{f|静|しず}かくない.

### ～く vs ～に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both make an adverb, but the ending follows the adjective's
class: い-adjectives take **く**, な-adjectives take **に**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|字|じ}をもっと<b>{f|大|おお}きく</b>{f|書|か}いてください。
Please write the characters bigger.
</pre>

<pre>
もう{f|少|すこ}し<b>{f|静|しず}かに</b>{f|話|はな}してください。
Please speak a bit more quietly.
</pre>

The same split covers "becoming": {f|暖|あたた}かくなる against
{f|元気|げんき}になる, and "making": {f|短|みじか}くする against きれいにする.
Get into the habit of storing every new adjective together with its adverb
form - it removes the guesswork permanently.

### ～くないです vs ～くありません

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning. `くないです` is conversational,
`くありません` is formal and a bit starchy. Neither is wrong; the mismatch is
with the situation, not the grammar.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|問題|もんだい}はそんなに<b>{f|難|むずか}しくないです</b>。
This question isn't all that hard. (talking to a classmate)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|当店|とうてん}のサービスは{f|決|けっ}して<b>{f|高|たか}くありません</b>。
Our service is by no means expensive. (a shop's notice)
</pre>

The rule extends to the past: `くなかったです` in speech,
`くありませんでした` in formal contexts. If you only want to remember one
column, remember the `です` one - it is never wrong, only occasionally casual.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|寒|さむ}いでした → ✅ <b>{f|寒|さむ}かったです</b>
  The past tense goes inside the adjective. `です` never inflects after an
  い-adjective.

- ❌ {f|寒|さむ}いじゃない → ✅ <b>{f|寒|さむ}くない</b>
  `じゃない` is the negative for nouns and な-adjectives. い-adjectives swap い
  for くない.

- ❌ いくない／いかった → ✅ <b>よくない</b>／<b>よかった</b>
  いい is irregular: everything except the dictionary form is built on よ. The
  same applies to かっこいい → かっこよかった.

- ❌ {f|新|あたら}しいの{f|車|くるま} → ✅ <b>{f|新|あたら}しい{f|車|くるま}</b>
  An い-adjective attaches straight to the noun. No `の`, and no `な` either.

- ❌ {f|高|たか}いくない → ✅ <b>{f|高|たか}くない</b>
  Remove the い before adding くない. Keeping both is the same slip as
  ❌{f|食|た}べるない with verbs.

## Practice

**1.** {f|昨日|きのう}のパーティーはとても{f|楽|たの}し____です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|楽|たの}<b>しかった</b>です

{f|昨日|きのう} puts the sentence in the past, and an い-adjective carries its
own past tense: い → かった. `です` is then added unchanged, purely for
politeness.

</details>

**2.** この{f|部屋|へや}は（きれくない・きれいじゃない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|部屋|へや}は<b>きれいじゃない</b>。

きれい ends in い but is a な-adjective, so it negates with `じゃない`. The
giveaway is that it ends in **えい**, a shape no genuine い-adjective has.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The film wasn't interesting, but the music was good."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|映画|えいが}は<b>{f|面白|おもしろ}くなかった</b>けど、{f|音楽|おんがく}は<b>よかった</b>です。

Two い-adjectives, both carrying their own tense. Note that いい becomes
よかった - never ❌いかった - and that only the last clause takes `です`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|町|まち}は{f|昔|むかし}、あまり{f|大|おお}きいじゃなかったです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|町|まち}は{f|昔|むかし}、あまり<b>{f|大|おお}きくなかった</b>です。

{f|大|おお}きい is an い-adjective despite looking like it might be a
な-adjective, so the past negative is built by stacking い-adjective endings:
い → くない → くなかった.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|辺|へん}は{f|夜|よる}、あまり{f|明|あか}るくないです。
(b) この{f|辺|へん}は{f|夜|よる}、あまり{f|明|あか}るくありません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The meaning is the same: neither area is well lit. What differs is how formal
the speaker is being. (a) is the everyday polite negative - what you would say
to a neighbour or a colleague.

(b) is the older, more formal construction. It sounds appropriate in a written
notice, a news report, or an official explanation, and slightly starchy in
casual conversation.

</details>
