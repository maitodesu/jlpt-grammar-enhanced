# ～つもり - plan to; intend to

**Meaning** · I intend to; I'm planning to - a decision already made in the
speaker's head

`つもり` is a noun meaning something like "intention", so a sentence with it
literally says *my intention is to do X*. That is why it feels more settled than
"I think I'll" and less fixed than a diary entry: the plan exists, it belongs to
the speaker, and it could still change.

## Formation

`つもり` is a noun, so it needs `だ` or `です` after it, and things attach to it the
way they attach to any noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + つもりだ | {f|買|か}う → {f|買|か}うつもりだ |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + つもりだ | {f|買|か}う → {f|買|か}わないつもりだ |
| Polite | つもりだ → つもりです | {f|買|か}うつもりです |
| Denying the intention | つもりはない | {f|買|か}うつもりはない |
| Intention that changed | つもりだった | {f|買|か}うつもりだった |
| Question | つもりですか | {f|買|か}うつもりですか |
| Noun | noun + の + つもり | {f|冗談|じょうだん} → {f|冗談|じょうだん}のつもり |
| Verb (た-form) | た-form + つもり = "under the impression" | {f|言|い}った → {f|言|い}ったつもり |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the verb before `つもり` stays **plain**.
❌<code>{f|行|い}きますつもりです</code> is one of the most recognisable learner errors in
the language.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}は{f|早|はや}く<b>{f|寝|ね}るつもりです</b>。
I'm planning to go to bed early tonight.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来年|らいねん}、{f|日本|にほん}へ<b>{f|行|い}くつもりです</b>。
I intend to go to Japan next year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今年|ことし}こそたばこを<b>やめるつもりだ</b>。
This year I really am going to quit smoking.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|夏休|なつやす}みはどう<b>するつもり</b>ですか。
What are you thinking of doing over the summer?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|甘|あま}いものは、しばらく<b>{f|食|た}べないつもりです</b>。
I'm planning to stay off sweet things for a while.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しいパソコンを<b>{f|買|か}うつもりだった</b>が、お{f|金|かね}が{f|足|た}りなかった。
I'd meant to buy a new computer, but I didn't have enough money.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大学|だいがく}を{f|出|で}たら{f|国|くに}に{f|帰|かえ}って、{f|日本語|にほんご}を<b>{f|教|おし}えるつもりです</b>。
After I finish university I intend to go home and teach Japanese.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}にはもう<b>{f|会|あ}うつもりはありません</b>。{f|連絡|れんらく}もしないでください。
I have no intention of seeing him again. Please don't contact me either.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`つもり` is **volitional and personal**. It reports a decision the speaker has
taken about their own behaviour, which puts two hard limits on it.

**No intending what you cannot control.** ❌<code>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るつもりです</code> is
impossible - the rain has no plans. The same rule blocks `つもり` with verbs like
<code>できる</code>, <code>{f|分|わ}かる</code> or <code>ある</code>. For predictions, `でしょう` or
<code>と{f|思|おも}います</code> does the job.

**Careful with other people's intentions.** <code>{f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|来|く}るつもりです</code>
claims direct access to Tanaka's mind. Add evidence:
<code>{f|来|く}るつもりらしいです</code>, <code>{f|来|く}るつもりだと{f|言|い}っていました</code>.

**`どうするつもりですか` can be an accusation.** Asked flatly, "what do you intend to
do?" carries the tone of a parent facing a broken window. Aimed at a superior it
is worse - <code>{f|部長|ぶちょう}はいつ{f|帰|かえ}るつもりですか</code> sounds like a
challenge. Upward, ask about their <code>ご{f|予定|よてい}</code> instead:
<code>{f|部長|ぶちょう}のご{f|予定|よてい}はいかがでしょうか</code>.

**`つもりはない` is a door slamming.** It denies that the intention exists at all,
and in an argument it is very cold. `～ないつもりです` is the mild version.

**`つもりだった` almost always implies the plan failed or changed.** Nobody says
<code>{f|行|い}くつもりだった</code> about a trip they took; it sets up a "but".

**A second life as a plain noun.** <code>{f|冗談|じょうだん}のつもりだった</code> ("I meant
it as a joke"), <code>{f|言|い}ったつもりだったんだけど</code> ("I was sure I'd told you").
Here `つもり` is about what you *believed*, not what you planned, and the belief is
usually wrong. Register-wise `つもり` itself is neutral; `つもりです` is fine in a job
interview, and `つもり` bare is fine with friends.

## Contrast

### ～つもりです vs ～{f|予定|よてい}です

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `つもり` is what you have decided. <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> is what
is on the calendar. Intention lives in your head; a schedule exists outside it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|引|ひ}っ<b>{f|越|こ}すつもりです</b>。
I'm planning to move next month. (I've decided; nothing is booked yet)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}す{f|予定|よてい}です。
I'm due to move next month. (it's arranged - date set, movers booked)
</pre>

Because <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> points at an arrangement rather than a state of mind,
it is also the polite way to ask about someone senior, where `つもり` would be
intrusive.

### ～つもりです vs ～(よ)うと{f|思|おも}います

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `つもり` reports a decision already taken.
<code>～(よ)うと{f|思|おも}います</code> presents the thought as forming now, which makes it
softer.

</div>

<pre>
{f|週末|しゅうまつ}は{f|海|うみ}へ<b>{f|行|い}くつもりです</b>。
I'm planning to go to the sea at the weekend.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|週末|しゅうまつ}は{f|海|うみ}へ{f|行|い}こうと{f|思|おも}います。
I think I'll head to the sea at the weekend.
</pre>

The second is the safer answer to a casual question, because it does not commit
you. The first can sound slightly firm, even stubborn, if the listener was hoping
to change your mind.

### ～ないつもりです vs ～つもりはありません

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～ないつもり` = I plan not to. `～つもりはない` = I have no such
plan, and I resent the suggestion.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}はお{f|酒|さけ}を<b>{f|飲|の}まないつもりです</b>。
I'm planning not to drink today.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|酒|さけ}を<b>{f|飲|の}むつもりはありません</b>。
I have no intention of drinking.
</pre>

The first describes a resolution you have made for yourself. The second rejects
the whole idea, and depending on the tone it can be a refusal, a defence, or a
rebuke. It is a useful sentence to understand and a risky one to use.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}、{f|行|い}き**ます**つもりです。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}、{f|行|い}**く**つもりです。
  `つもり` follows a plain form. The politeness goes on `です` at the end of the
  sentence, once.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るつもりです。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るでしょう。
  Only a person with a choice can have an intention. Weather takes `でしょう`.

- ❌ {f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|来|こ}ないつもりです。 → ✅ {f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|来|こ}ないつもりらしいです。
  Asserting someone else's intentions flatly claims to read their mind. Mark the
  evidence.

- ❌ これは{f|冗談|じょうだん}つもりでした。 → ✅ これは{f|冗談|じょうだん}のつもりでした。
  `つもり` is a noun, so another noun joins it with `の`.

- ❌ {f|社長|しゃちょう}、いつ{f|海外|かいがい}へ{f|行|い}くつもりですか。 → ✅ {f|社長|しゃちょう}、{f|海外|かいがい}へはいつ{f|行|い}かれるご{f|予定|よてい}ですか。
  The grammar is sound; asking a superior about their `つもり` sounds like an
  interrogation.

## Practice

**1.** {f|来週|らいしゅう}から{f|毎日|まいにち}３０{f|分|ぷん}{f|走|はし}____つもりです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|毎日|まいにち}３０{f|分|ぷん}{f|走|はし}<b>る</b>つもりです。

`つもり` takes the plain dictionary form, so <code>{f|走|はし}る</code> stays as it is. The
politeness is carried by `です` at the end - never by the verb inside.

</details>

**2.** {f|来年|らいねん}、{f|結婚|けっこん}する（つもり・{f|予定|よてい}）です。{f|式場|しきじょう}も、もう{f|予約|よやく}しました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|来年|らいねん}、{f|結婚|けっこん}する<b>{f|予定|よてい}</b>です。

The venue is already booked, so this is an arrangement in the world, not a plan in
the speaker's head. <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code> is what fits. `つもり` would oddly suggest
nothing had been settled yet.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I was going to call you yesterday, but I forgot."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

きのう{f|電話|でんわ}を<b>するつもりだった</b>んですが、{f|忘|わす}れてしまいました。

`つもりだった` is the form for an intention that did not survive contact with
reality, and it practically demands a `が` or `けど` clause explaining what went
wrong.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the problem: {f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|明日|あした}の{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|出|で}るつもりです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|明日|あした}の{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|出|で}られるご{f|予定|よてい}です。
（or, if you were told so: …{f|出|で}るつもりだとおっしゃっていました。）

Two problems at once: you cannot assert another person's intention as fact, and
`つもり` is too familiar to use of a superior. Switch to
<code>ご{f|予定|よてい}</code>, or quote your source.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会議|かいぎ}には{f|出|で}ないつもりです。
(b) {f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|出|で}るつもりはありません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plan: the speaker has decided to skip the meeting, and the sentence
invites a follow-up reason without any heat in it.

(b) denies that the intention exists at all. It is flat and final, and in a
workplace it would read as a refusal - the kind of thing said when someone has
just pressed you to attend. Same facts, very different temperature.

</details>
