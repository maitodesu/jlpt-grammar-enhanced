# ～ましょうか

**Meaning** · shall I? / shall we? - offering to do something, or proposing tentatively

`～ましょうか` is `～ましょう` with a question mark built in, and that small `か`
does two different jobs.

- **Shall I?** - you offer to do something *for* the listener.
  {f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けましょうか。
- **Shall we?** - you propose something for both of you, but check first instead of
  announcing. そろそろ{f|帰|かえ}りましょうか。

Which one a listener hears is decided by the situation, not the grammar: if the
action benefits them and only you can do it, it is an offer; if it is something you
would do together, it is a proposal.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + ましょうか | {f|持|も}ちます → {f|持|も}ちましょうか |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + ましょうか | {f|開|あ}けます → {f|開|あ}けましょうか |
| Verb (する) | し + ましょうか | します → しましょうか |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | き + ましょうか | {f|来|き}ます → {f|来|き}ましょうか |
| Humble offer (service Japanese) | お + ます-stem + しましょうか | お{f|持|も}ちしましょうか |
| Casual | plain volitional + か | {f|開|あ}けようか／{f|手伝|てつだ}おうか |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - mark the doer and the ambiguity disappears.
{f|私|わたし}が{f|運|はこ}びましょうか is unmistakably an offer;
{f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|運|はこ}びましょうか is unmistakably a proposal.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`ましょうか` is the workhorse of everyday helpfulness. It is what you say when you
notice someone struggling with a door, a suitcase, a printer. It is polite without
being stiff, it commits you to nothing until they answer, and - crucially - it does
not put the listener under obligation, because you are volunteering rather than
asking.

Learn the replies as a set, because you will hear them constantly:

- <b>お{f|願|ねが}いします</b> - yes, please.
- <b>すみません、ありがとうございます</b> - yes, with thanks and apology at once.
- <b>あ、{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}です</b> - no, I'm fine. The everyday polite refusal.
- <b>いえ、{f|結構|けっこう}です</b> - no thank you. Firmer, and cooler.

Upward, `ましょうか` is acceptable but plain. In service Japanese and towards
superiors the humble frame is standard: お{f|持|も}ちしましょうか,
お{f|調|しら}べしましょうか, ご{f|案内|あんない}しましょうか. These raise the listener by
lowering the action, and they are what hotel staff and careful employees use.

Casually the whole thing shortens to the plain volitional plus `か`:
{f|開|あ}けようか, {f|手伝|てつだ}おうか, {f|買|か}ってこようか. Note that in casual
speech the `か` is often dropped entirely and carried by intonation - {f|手伝|てつだ}おう？

One warning about intonation and reading: `ましょうか` is a question, and it takes a
rising tone. Written down, it is often followed by `。` rather than `？` in Japanese
prose, which does not make it any less of a question.

## Contrast

### ～ましょうか vs ～ましょう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ましょう` **announces**; `ましょうか` **checks**. The `か` hands
the decision back to the listener.

</div>

<pre>
{f|電気|でんき}を<b>つけましょう</b>。
Let's put the light on.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電気|でんき}を<b>つけましょうか</b>。
Shall I put the light on?
</pre>

The first version assumes; the second asks. That is why the `か` version doubles as
an offer and the bare version does not: an offer only makes sense if the other
person can decline it.

Between the two, `ましょうか` is almost always the safer choice with people you do
not know well. Use bare `ましょう` when the plan is already agreed and you are simply
moving it along - {f|行|い}きましょう as you both stand up.

### ～ましょうか vs ～ませんか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both ask, but `ませんか` **invites** the listener to join you;
`ましょうか` proposes a next step in something already underway, or offers to act on
their behalf.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今度|こんど}の{f|土曜日|どようび}、{f|山|やま}に<b>{f|登|のぼ}りませんか</b>。
How about climbing a mountain this Saturday?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ってきましたね。{f|下|お}り<b>ましょうか</b>。
It's started raining. Shall we head back down?
</pre>

`ませんか` opens something new and can be refused without awkwardness - it is the
form for asking someone out. `ましょうか` takes the shared activity as given and asks
about the *next move* within it.

There is also a distribution difference that catches learners. For an **offer**,
`ませんか` does not work: ❌{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|持|も}ちませんか sounds like you are
inviting the other person to carry luggage, not offering to carry theirs. Offers are
`ましょうか` territory.

### ～ましょうか vs ～てあげましょうか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - plain `ましょうか` for offers.
`～てあげましょうか` announces that you are doing the listener a kindness, and that
is not yours to announce.

</div>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}まで{f|車|くるま}で<b>{f|送|おく}りましょうか</b>。
Shall I drive you to the station?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}まで{f|車|くるま}で<b>{f|送|おく}ってあげましょうか</b>。
Shall I do you the favour of driving you to the station?
</pre>

The second is grammatical and is used - among close friends, or lightly to a child - 
but `あげる` explicitly frames the act as a benefit conferred, and offering that to
an equal or a superior sounds self-congratulatory. The neutral offer leaves the
gratitude to the other person.

The polite move goes the other way, towards humility: お{f|送|おく}りしましょうか
lowers your own action, and is the form used in service and business. `～てあげる` and
`お～する` are the two ends of the same axis, and the safe default at N5 is plain
`ましょうか` in the middle.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|開|あ}ける<b>ましょうか</b> → ✅ {f|開|あ}け<b>ましょうか</b>
  Built on the ます-stem: {f|開|あ}けます → {f|開|あ}け + ましょうか.

- ❌ {f|荷物|にもつ}を<b>{f|持|も}ちませんか</b>。（as an offer） → ✅ {f|荷物|にもつ}を<b>{f|持|も}ちましょうか</b>。
  `ませんか` invites the listener to do it. To offer your own help, use `ましょうか`.

- ❌ {f|窓|まど}を<b>{f|開|あ}けましょうか</b>。（meaning "please open the window"） → ✅ {f|窓|まど}を<b>{f|開|あ}けてください</b>。
  `ましょうか` puts the action on *you*, not on the listener. It can never be a
  request.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、コピーして<b>あげましょうか</b>。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、コピー<b>しましょうか</b>。／お{f|手伝|てつだ}い<b>しましょうか</b>。
  `てあげる` advertises your own kindness. Upward, drop it or go humble.

- ❌ {f|行|い}き<b>ましょうか、ですか</b>。 → ✅ {f|行|い}き<b>ましょうか</b>。
  `ましょうか` is already a complete question.

## Practice

**1.** {f|重|おも}そうですね。{f|私|わたし}が{f|持|も}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|私|わたし}が{f|持|も}<b>ちましょうか</b>。

<code>{f|持|も}つ</code> → ます-stem <code>{f|持|も}ち</code> → + ましょうか. The <code>{f|私|わたし}が</code>
makes it unambiguously an offer rather than a proposal that you both carry it.

</details>

**2.** {f|友|とも}だちが{f|寒|さむ}そうです。（エアコンを{f|消|け}しましょうか・エアコンを{f|消|け}しませんか）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

エアコンを<b>{f|消|け}しましょうか</b>。

You are offering to do something for your friend's benefit, and that is exactly what
`ましょうか` is for. <code>{f|消|け}しませんか</code> would be inviting *them* to switch it
off, which is not what you mean.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Shall we decide the date now?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|今|いま}、{f|日|ひ}にちを<b>{f|決|き}めましょうか</b>。

A joint action within a conversation already under way - the "shall we?" sense.
Plain <code>{f|決|き}めましょう</code> would work too, but it announces the decision instead
of checking that the other person is ready.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: すみません、この{f|字|じ}が{f|読|よ}めません。{f|読|よ}みましょうか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ すみません、この{f|字|じ}が{f|読|よ}めません。<b>{f|読|よ}んでいただけませんか</b>。

The speaker wants *the listener* to read it, but `ましょうか` offers the speaker's own
action - as written, it says "shall I read it?", which makes no sense after "I
can't read it". A request needs the `ください` or `いただけませんか` family.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) お{f|茶|ちゃ}を<b>{f|入|い}れましょうか</b>。
(b) お{f|茶|ちゃ}を<b>{f|入|い}れてあげましょうか</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain, warm offer to make tea. It is the version that works with a guest, a
colleague or a boss, because it says nothing about who is doing whom a favour.

(b) adds `あげる`, which explicitly marks the tea as a kindness you are bestowing.
Between close friends it can be playful; to anyone senior it sounds like you are
inviting thanks in advance. The humble お{f|入|い}れしましょうか goes the opposite
way and is the safest of the three in a workplace.

</details>
