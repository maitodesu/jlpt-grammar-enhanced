# だろう・でしょう - probably; … right?

**Meaning** · probably - or, said with a rising tone, "…don't you think?"

`だろう` and `でしょう` are the same word in two politeness levels, and that word
does one job: it stops a sentence being a flat assertion. Whether it comes out as
a guess or as an appeal for agreement is decided almost entirely by **intonation**
 - falling for "probably", rising for "right?". Learners are usually taught only
the first half.

## Formation

`でしょう` attaches to **plain forms**, and the `だ` of nouns and な-adjectives
disappears in front of it.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + でしょう | {f|降|ふ}る → {f|降|ふ}るでしょう |
| Verb (past) | た-form + でしょう | {f|降|ふ}った → {f|降|ふ}ったでしょう |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + でしょう | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ないでしょう |
| い-adjective | plain form + でしょう | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}いでしょう |
| な-adjective | drop だ + でしょう | {f|便利|べんり}だ → {f|便利|べんり}でしょう |
| Noun | noun + でしょう | {f|雨|あめ} → {f|雨|あめ}でしょう |
| Plain / written style | でしょう → だろう | {f|降|ふ}るだろう |
| Casual spoken | でしょう → でしょ／だろ | {f|降|ふ}るでしょ |
| Common partners | たぶん, きっと, もしかしたら | たぶん{f|降|ふ}るでしょう |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - no `だ` and no `ます` in front. ❌<code>{f|学生|がくせい}だでしょう</code>,
❌<code>{f|行|い}きますでしょう</code>. The politeness of the sentence is already carried by
`でしょう` itself.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}<b>でしょう</b>。
It will probably rain tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
たぶん{f|田中|たなか}さんは<b>{f|来|こ}ないでしょう</b>。
Tanaka probably won't come.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|問題|もんだい}、<b>{f|難|むずか}しいでしょう</b>？
This question's hard, isn't it?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今夜|こんや}はかなり<b>{f|冷|ひ}えるだろう</b>。
It'll get pretty cold tonight.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}はもう{f|空港|くうこう}に<b>{f|着|つ}いているでしょう</b>。
He's probably already arrived at the airport.
</pre>

<pre>
ほら、{f|言|い}った<b>でしょう</b>？だから{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っていけばよかったのに。
See? I told you. You should have taken an umbrella.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|午後|ごご}から{f|風|かぜ}が<b>{f|強|つよ}くなるでしょう</b>。おでかけの{f|方|かた}はご{f|注意|ちゅうい}ください。
The wind will strengthen from the afternoon. Those going out should take care.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|映画|えいが}、もう<b>{f|見|み}たでしょう</b>？どうだった？
You've already seen that film, haven't you? What did you think?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

**The two senses are one form.** Falling intonation gives conjecture: the speaker
is guessing and knows they are guessing. Rising intonation turns the sentence
outward and asks the listener to agree. In writing there is no intonation, so a
`？` marks the second sense - and a `でしょう` at the end of a written forecast is
always the first.

**`だろう` is plain, and in speech it leans male.** In writing - essays, novels,
news commentary - `だろう` is simply the plain-style equivalent of `でしょう` and
carries no gender at all. In conversation, bare <code>{f|来|く}るだろう</code> and the
clipped `だろ？` sound distinctly masculine and rather rough; women's casual speech
prefers `でしょ？` or `～よね？`. `でしょ` is the everyday spoken shortening for
everyone.

**Weather forecasts live on `でしょう`.** Japanese meteorologists do not assert;
they say <code>{f|晴|は}れるでしょう</code>, <code>{f|雪|ゆき}になるでしょう</code>. Hearing that
register helps fix the falling, conjectural sense in your ear.

**The rising `でしょう？` has an edge.** It presses the listener toward your view.
<code>だから{f|言|い}ったでしょう</code> is a genuine "I told you so", and used upward it
is bad news: <code>{f|部長|ぶちょう}、これでいいでしょう？</code> is telling your boss what to
think. The upward version is a question - <code>これでよろしいでしょうか</code> - where
`でしょう` has become deference rather than pressure.

**Do not guess about your own choices.** ❌<code>{f|私|わたし}は{f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}くでしょう</code>
is strange for the same reason "I will probably go, I suppose" is strange about a
decision that is yours to make. Use <code>{f|行|い}きます</code> or
<code>{f|行|い}くつもりです</code>. `でしょう` about yourself is only natural for things
outside your control: <code>{f|私|わたし}も{f|太|ふと}るでしょうね</code>.

## Contrast

### だろう vs でしょう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different level. `でしょう` is the ます／です-style
form; `だろう` is the plain-style form and, in speech, noticeably blunt.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}には{f|終|お}わる<b>でしょう</b>。
It'll probably be finished by next week.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}には{f|終|お}わる<b>だろう</b>。
It'll probably be finished by next week.
</pre>

The English is identical; the Japanese is not interchangeable. Use `でしょう` with
anyone you would use `です` with. Use `だろう` in writing, in a diary, or with close
friends - and be aware that a man and a woman saying `だろう` out loud will be heard
differently, fairly or not.

### Falling でしょう vs rising でしょう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - falling = *I think so, but I'm not sure*. Rising = *you know
this too, don't you?* One looks inward at the speaker's uncertainty; the other
reaches out to the listener.

</div>

<pre>
{f|山田|やまだ}さんはこのニュースを<b>{f|知|し}らないでしょう</b>。
Yamada probably doesn't know about this news. (falling - a guess)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|山田|やまだ}さんもこのニュース、<b>{f|知|し}っているでしょう</b>？
Yamada knows about this news too, right? (rising - checking with you)
</pre>

The rising version assumes the listener is in a position to confirm, so it is used
about things they can be expected to know - shared experience, obvious facts, or
something you told them earlier. Using it about something they could not possibly
know sounds like an interrogation.

### でしょう vs かもしれません

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a rough scale of confidence:
`かもしれません` (maybe, 50/50) → `でしょう` (probably) → `はずです` (it should be, by
reasoning) → <code>に{f|違|ちが}いありません</code> (it must be).

</div>

<pre>
{f|午後|ごご}から{f|雪|ゆき}になる<b>でしょう</b>。
It'll probably turn to snow this afternoon.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|午後|ごご}から{f|雪|ゆき}になるかもしれません。
It might turn to snow this afternoon.
</pre>

<code>と{f|思|おも}います</code> sits alongside these but does something different: it
labels the statement as the speaker's personal opinion rather than measuring
likelihood. <code>{f|来|こ}ないでしょう</code> is a forecast about the world;
<code>{f|来|こ}ないと{f|思|おも}います</code> is what *I* think - which is why the second is
often the politer choice when you might be wrong.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|学生|がくせい}**だ**でしょう。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|学生|がくせい}でしょう。
  Nouns and な-adjectives lose their `だ` before `でしょう`.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}、{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}り**ます**でしょう。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}、{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るでしょう。
  `でしょう` follows plain forms. Doubling the politeness sounds like a bad
  announcement voice.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|来年|らいねん}{f|大学|だいがく}に{f|入|はい}るでしょう。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|来年|らいねん}{f|大学|だいがく}に{f|入|はい}るつもりです。
  You do not speculate about your own decisions.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、この{f|案|あん}でいいでしょう？ → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、この{f|案|あん}でよろしいでしょうか。
  Rising `でしょう？` pushes the listener to agree. Upward, ask.

- ❌ {f|静|しず}か**だろう**でしょう。 → ✅ {f|静|しず}かでしょう。
  `だろう` and `でしょう` are the same word; they never stack.

## Practice

**1.** {f|天気|てんき}{f|予報|よほう}によると、{f|明日|あした}は{f|寒|さむ}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|明日|あした}は{f|寒|さむ}<b>いでしょう</b>。

い-adjectives keep their `い` and simply take `でしょう`. A forecast is the textbook
home of falling-tone `でしょう`: the speaker is predicting, not asserting.

</details>

**2.** {f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|習|なら}ったんだから、この{f|漢字|かんじ}は{f|読|よ}める（でしょう？・かもしれません）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|漢字|かんじ}は{f|読|よ}める<b>でしょう？</b>

You studied it together, so you are appealing to shared knowledge and expecting
agreement - that is rising `でしょう？`. `かもしれません` would mean you have no idea
whether they can read it, which contradicts the first half of the sentence.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He's probably still at work."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}はまだ{f|会社|かいしゃ}に<b>いるでしょう</b>。
（plain: {f|彼|かれ}はまだ{f|会社|かいしゃ}にいるだろう。）

`でしょう` attaches straight to the plain <code>いる</code>. Choose `だろう` only if the
surrounding sentences are also plain style.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the problem: あの{f|人|ひと}はたぶん{f|先生|せんせい}だでしょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ あの{f|人|ひと}はたぶん{f|先生|せんせい}でしょう。

The `だ` has to go. `でしょう` is itself the conjectural form of `だ`／`です`, so
keeping both is like saying "is probably is". Note how naturally `たぶん` pairs with
the falling sense.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|店|みせ}、{f|高|たか}いでしょう。
(b) この{f|店|みせ}、{f|高|たか}いでしょう？

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a), said with a falling tone, is a guess about a shop the speaker does not know
first-hand: "this place is probably expensive." It might be a reason for not going
in.

(b), rising, assumes the listener already knows - they have been there, or the
prices are on the board in front of you - and asks them to agree: "pricey, isn't
it?" Same eight characters; the tone decides who is supposed to know the answer.

</details>
