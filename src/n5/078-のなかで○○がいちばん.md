# ～のなかで～がいちばん

**Meaning** · of all ～, ～ is the most ～ - naming the top of a stated group

This is the frame that makes a superlative explicit. The first slot names the
field of comparison, the second names the winner. Japanese marks that winner with
`が`, never `は`, because the sentence exists precisely to single one item out.

## Formation

Two halves that go together: <code>～の{f|中|なか}で</code> sets the field,
<code>～がいちばん + adjective</code> announces the winner.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Group noun | Noun + の{f|中|なか}で | クラス → クラスの{f|中|なか}で |
| Category noun | Noun + の{f|中|なか}で | スポーツ → スポーツの{f|中|なか}で |
| Listed items | A と B と C + の{f|中|なか}で | {f|赤|あか}と{f|青|あお}と{f|黒|くろ}の{f|中|なか}で |
| Place or period | Noun + で（no {f|中|なか}） | {f|世界|せかい}で／{f|一年|いちねん}で |
| Winner | Noun + が + いちばん + adjective | {f|夏|なつ}がいちばん{f|暑|あつ}いです |
| Question (people) | だれが いちばん …か | だれがいちばん{f|強|つよ}いですか |
| Question (closed set) | どれが いちばん …か | どれがいちばん{f|安|やす}いですか |
| Question (place / time) | どこが／いつが いちばん …か | どこがいちばん{f|寒|さむ}いですか |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the winner takes `が`. Using `は` there
（❌{f|夏|なつ}はいちばん{f|暑|あつ}いです）turns the sentence into a topic
statement and loses the "out of all of them" force. `が` selects; `は` merely
comments.

</div>

The question word matches the field: <b>だれ</b> for people, <b>どれ</b> for a closed
set of listed things, <b>なに</b> for an open category, <b>どこ</b> for places, <b>いつ</b>
for times.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|果物|くだもの}<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、りんご<b>がいちばん</b>{f|好|す}きです。
Of all fruit, I like apples best.
</pre>

<pre>
クラス<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、{f|林|はやし}さん<b>がいちばん</b>{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}いです。
Hayashi is the tallest in the class.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|家族|かぞく}<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、だれ<b>がいちばん</b>{f|早|はや}く{f|起|お}きますか。
Who gets up earliest in your family?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一年|いちねん}<b>で</b>、{f|八月|はちがつ}<b>がいちばん</b>{f|暑|あつ}いです。
August is the hottest month of the year.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|三|みっ}つ<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、どれ<b>がいちばん</b>{f|軽|かる}いですか。
Which of these three is the lightest?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|兄弟|きょうだい}<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、{f|妹|いもうと}<b>がいちばん</b>ピアノが{f|上手|じょうず}です。
Of us siblings, my little sister is the best at the piano.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}のメニュー<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、この{f|定食|ていしょく}<b>がいちばん</b>{f|人気|にんき}があります。
Of everything on this restaurant's menu, this set meal is the most popular.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}まで{f|見|み}た{f|映画|えいが}<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、あの{f|作品|さくひん}<b>がいちばん</b>おもしろかったです。
Of all the films I've seen, that one was the most interesting.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral. The whole frame works unchanged in plain speech - just drop
`です` - and it is one of the first things a learner is asked in conversation
practice: {f|日本|にほん}の{f|食|た}べ{f|物|もの}の{f|中|なか}で、{f|何|なに}がいちばん{f|好|す}きですか。

The <code>{f|中|なか}</code> is often left out when the field is a **place or a period of
time**, because those are already containers:
{f|世界|せかい}でいちばん{f|高|たか}い{f|山|やま}, {f|一年|いちねん}でいちばん{f|寒|さむ}い{f|日|ひ},
クラスでいちばん{f|元気|げんき}な{f|人|ひと}. Saying
{f|世界|せかい}の{f|中|なか}で is not wrong, but it is heavier than a native
speaker would usually bother with. With a category noun - fruit, sports, films - 
the <code>の{f|中|なか}で</code> is the normal choice.

In writing you will see <code>{f|最|もっと}も</code> in place of `いちばん`, and
<code>～のうちで</code> in place of <code>～の{f|中|なか}で</code>. Both are stiffer. Neither
changes the grammar.

Answering such a question, you normally repeat the frame only if it is not
already obvious. Asked
{f|季節|きせつ}の{f|中|なか}でいつがいちばん{f|好|す}きですか, you answer simply
{f|秋|あき}がいちばん{f|好|す}きです - or just {f|秋|あき}です.

## Contrast

### ～の{f|中|なか}で～がいちばん vs ～より～のほうが

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - count the candidates. Two → `より`. Three or more → `いちばん`.

</div>

<pre>
スポーツ<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、サッカー<b>がいちばん</b>{f|好|す}きです。
Of all sports, I like football best.
</pre>

<pre>
サッカーより{f|野球|やきゅう}の{f|方|ほう}が{f|好|す}きです。
I like baseball more than football.
</pre>

Mixing the two produces sentences that are simply broken:
❌ サッカーよりいちばん{f|好|す}きです. If a `より` phrase is present, the
comparison is between two named things and there is no top of any ranking to
point at.

### が vs は in the winner slot

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - this pattern answers "which one?", and answers to "which
one?" always take `が`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|四人|よにん}<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>、{f|田中|たなか}さん<b>がいちばん</b>{f|若|わか}いです。
Tanaka is the youngest of the four.
</pre>

Replace `が` with `は` and the sentence stops selecting: it would be read as "as
for Tanaka, he is the youngest", which is a comment about Tanaka rather than the
result of comparing four people. The same logic governs the question - the
question word `だれ` cannot take `は` at all.

`は` does appear one step earlier in the sentence, on the field, when the field is
being contrasted: {f|日本|にほん}の{f|山|やま}の{f|中|なか}では…, "in Japan at
least, …".

### の{f|中|なか}で vs で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with a **group**, keep <code>の{f|中|なか}で</code>. With a **place
or a stretch of time**, the plain `で` is enough.

</div>

<pre>
{f|日本|にほん}<b>で</b>{f|富士山|ふじさん}<b>がいちばん</b>{f|高|たか}いです。
Mt. Fuji is the highest mountain in Japan.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日本|にほん}の{f|山|やま}<b>の{f|中|なか}で</b>{f|富士山|ふじさん}<b>がいちばん</b>{f|高|たか}いです。
Of all the mountains in Japan, Mt. Fuji is the highest.
</pre>

Both are correct. The first treats Japan as a location; the second explicitly
assembles the set of Japanese mountains and ranks it. What you must not do is drop
the particle altogether: ❌{f|日本|にほん}{f|富士山|ふじさん}がいちばん{f|高|たか}いです.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|果物|くだもの}の{f|中|なか}で、みかん<b>は</b>いちばん{f|安|やす}いです。 → ✅ {f|果物|くだもの}の{f|中|なか}で、みかん<b>が</b>いちばん{f|安|やす}いです。
  The item being crowned takes `が`. This is the error learners make most often
  with the pattern, because English "oranges are the cheapest" looks like a topic
  sentence.

- ❌ スポーツの{f|中|なか}<b>に</b>、テニスがいちばん{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ スポーツの{f|中|なか}<b>で</b>、テニスがいちばん{f|好|す}きです。
  `で` marks the domain within which the comparison is carried out. `に` would
  mark a location of existence, which is not what is going on.

- ❌ {f|世界|せかい}の{f|中|なか}でいちばん{f|大|おお}きい{f|国|くに} → ✅ {f|世界|せかい}でいちばん{f|大|おお}きい{f|国|くに}
  Not ungrammatical, but a place name plus <code>の{f|中|なか}で</code> is clumsy.
  Places and time spans take the bare `で`.

- ❌ この{f|三|みっ}つの{f|中|なか}で、<b>{f|何|なに}</b>がいちばんいいですか。 → ✅ この{f|三|みっ}つの{f|中|なか}で、<b>どれ</b>がいちばんいいですか。
  When the candidates have been listed, the question word is `どれ`. `なに` asks
  about an open field with no stated members.

- ❌ {f|兄弟|きょうだい}の{f|中|なか}で、{f|弟|おとうと}がいちばん{f|元気|げんき}<b>な</b>です。 → ✅ {f|兄弟|きょうだい}の{f|中|なか}で、{f|弟|おとうと}がいちばん{f|元気|げんき}です。
  `いちばん` does not change how the adjective ends. A な-adjective keeps its `な`
  only in front of a noun.

## Practice

**1.** {f|飲|の}み{f|物|もの}の{f|中|なか}で、お{f|茶|ちゃ}____いちばん{f|好|す}きです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|飲|の}み{f|物|もの}の{f|中|なか}で、お{f|茶|ちゃ}<b>が</b>いちばん{f|好|す}きです。

`が` marks the item selected out of the group. It is also the particle
<code>{f|好|す}き</code> normally takes for the thing liked, so the two requirements
agree here.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|春|はる}と{f|秋|あき}と（どちら・どれ）がいちばん{f|好|す}きですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|春|はる}と{f|秋|あき}と<b>どちら</b>が{f|好|す}きですか。

With exactly two candidates you use `どちら` - and then `いちばん` has to go, since
there is no ranking of three or more to head. `どれ` needs at least three listed
options.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Which season do you like best?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|季節|きせつ}の{f|中|なか}で、<b>いつがいちばん</b>{f|好|す}きですか。
（also natural: どの{f|季節|きせつ}がいちばん{f|好|す}きですか。）

Seasons are times, so `いつ` fits; <code>どの + {f|季節|きせつ}</code> is the other
standard way to ask. Either way the question word carries `が`, never `は`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|日本|にほん}の{f|町|まち}の{f|中|なか}に、{f|京都|きょうと}はいちばんきれいだと{f|思|おも}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|日本|にほん}の{f|町|まち}の{f|中|なか}<b>で</b>、{f|京都|きょうと}<b>が</b>いちばんきれいだと{f|思|おも}います。

Two particles are wrong. The field of comparison takes `で`, not `に`, and the
selected item takes `が`, not `は`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|店|みせ}のパンはおいしいです。
(b) この{f|辺|へん}の{f|店|みせ}の{f|中|なか}で、この{f|店|みせ}のパンがいちばんおいしいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is an unranked judgement - the bread here is good, and nothing is said about
anywhere else.

(b) sets up a field (the shops around here), compares them, and awards first
place. The listener now knows the speaker has tried the others. That explicit
field is exactly what the pattern adds over a bare adjective.

</details>
