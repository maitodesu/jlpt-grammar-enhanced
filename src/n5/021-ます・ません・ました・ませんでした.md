# ～ます・～ません・～ました・～ませんでした

**Meaning** · the polite verb endings - non-past and past, affirmative and negative

These four are one system, not four separate words. `ます` is a **politeness**
marker that happens to carry tense as well: it tells your listener you are
speaking to them carefully. Every Japanese verb has a plain form and a `ます`
form, and the choice between them is a choice about the relationship, not about
the meaning.

<div class="note">

**Note** - Japanese verbs fall into three classes, and every textbook names them
differently. **う-verbs** are also called {f|五段|ごだん} (godan) verbs, or Group 1;
**る-verbs** are also called {f|一段|いちだん} (ichidan) verbs, or Group 2; する and
{f|来|く}る are the two irregulars, sometimes called Group 3. This page says
**う-verb** and **る-verb** throughout.

</div>

## Formation

All four endings attach to the same place - the **ます-stem**. Get the stem
right and the rest is just picking one of four endings.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | final -u → -i, + ます | {f|書|か}く → {f|書|か}きます |
| Verb (る-verb) | drop る, + ます | {f|食|た}べる → {f|食|た}べます |
| Verb (する) | し + ます | する → します |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | き + ます | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|き}ます |

Then the four endings, which never change shape:

| Tense and polarity | Ending | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Non-past, affirmative | ます | {f|飲|の}みます |
| Non-past, negative | ません | {f|飲|の}みません |
| Past, affirmative | ました | {f|飲|の}みました |
| Past, negative | ませんでした | {f|飲|の}みませんでした |

For う-verbs, the final vowel moves from the う row to the い row. Every ending
behaves the same way:

| Dictionary ending | ます-stem ending | Example |
|---|---|---|
| う | い | {f|買|か}う → {f|買|か}います |
| つ | ち | {f|待|ま}つ → {f|待|ま}ちます |
| る | り | {f|帰|かえ}る → {f|帰|かえ}ります |
| む | み | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}みます |
| ぶ | び | {f|遊|あそ}ぶ → {f|遊|あそ}びます |
| ぬ | に | {f|死|し}ぬ → {f|死|し}にます |
| く | き | {f|聞|き}く → {f|聞|き}きます |
| ぐ | ぎ | {f|急|いそ}ぐ → {f|急|いそ}ぎます |
| す | し | {f|話|はな}す → {f|話|はな}します |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a verb ending in る is only a る-verb if the vowel before the
る is **i** or **e**. But plenty of verbs that pass that test are still う-verbs:
{f|帰|かえ}る, {f|入|はい}る, {f|走|はし}る, {f|切|き}る, {f|知|し}る,
{f|要|い}る. They give {f|帰|かえ}ります, {f|入|はい}ります, {f|走|はし}ります,
{f|切|き}ります, {f|知|し}ります, {f|要|い}ります - never ❌{f|帰|かえ}ます.
{f|着|き}る "wear" is a genuine る-verb ({f|着|き}ます); {f|切|き}る "cut" is not
({f|切|き}ります). Same sound, different class.

</div>

The ます-stem is not only for `ます`. It is the hub the rest of the language
hangs off: {f|飲|の}み**たい**, {f|飲|の}み**ながら**, {f|飲|の}み**ましょう**,
{f|飲|の}み**やすい**, {f|飲|の}み**に{f|行|い}く**. Learning to find it once pays
off many times.

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>
わたしはお{f|酒|さけ}を<b>{f|飲|の}みません</b>。
I don't drink alcohol.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|一日中|いちにちじゅう}{f|雨|あめ}が<b>{f|降|ふ}りました</b>。
It rained all day yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
すみません、この{f|漢字|かんじ}の{f|読|よ}み{f|方|かた}が<b>わかりません</b>。

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

Tomorrow's meeting starts at nine, so please be at the office by eight thirty.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ます` is {f|丁寧語|ていねいご} - the polite, or "distal", style. It is the default with anyone
you are not close to: shop staff, colleagues, teachers, strangers, and anyone
you have just met. The plain forms ({f|飲|の}む, {f|飲|の}んだ,
{f|飲|の}まない) are the "close" style, used with family, friends, and people
clearly junior to you.

Three things learners get wrong about that split:

- **`ます` is not honorific.** It says nothing about respecting the *subject* of
  the sentence. Honorific and humble language ({f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がります,
  いたします) is a separate layer stacked on top.
- **Plain is not sloppy.** Newspapers, essays, academic writing and most novels
  are written in plain form throughout. `ます` in an essay looks like a letter to
  the reader.
- **Plain form is grammatically required inside sentences.** Before と{f|思|おも}う,
  つもり, とき, ので, から and inside a clause modifying a noun, the verb goes
  plain regardless of how polite the sentence ends up:
  {f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}くと{f|思|おも}います, not ❌{f|行|い}きますと{f|思|おも}います.
  `ます` lives at the **end** of the sentence.

The non-past `ます` covers habits and the future alike - 
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|走|はし}ります "I run every day" and
{f|明日|あした}{f|走|はし}ります "I'll run tomorrow" - but never an action
happening at this moment; that needs `～ています`.

Within a single conversation you normally hold one register. Dropping one plain
sentence into polite speech is a real and natural move - it reads as thinking
aloud, or as a flash of warmth - but a whole reply in plain form to a stranger
is rude. Going the other way, switching from plain back to `ます` with a friend
signals distance, and Japanese speakers use it deliberately when they are
annoyed.

In speech, `ます` is very often followed by softeners: `～ますね`, `～ますよ`,
`～ますけど`. Bare `～ます` sentence after sentence sounds like a public
announcement.

## Contrast

### ～ます vs the dictionary form

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the ます-form is about **who you are talking to**. The plain
form is what the **grammar around the verb** demands. They answer different
questions, so they are not really alternatives.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来年|らいねん}、{f|国|くに}に<b>{f|帰|かえ}ります</b>。
I'm going home to my country next year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来年|らいねん}、{f|国|くに}に<b>{f|帰|かえ}る</b>と{f|思|おも}います。
I think I'll go home to my country next year.
</pre>

The second sentence is exactly as polite as the first - the politeness is
carried by {f|思|おも}います at the end. The embedded verb has to be plain.

### ～ません vs ～ないです

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the meaning is identical. `～ません` is the tidier, more
formal negative; `～ないです` is the plain negative with a polite `です` bolted
on, and belongs to relaxed speech.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}は{f|時間|じかん}が<b>ありません</b>。
I don't have time today. (careful, businesslike)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}はちょっと{f|時間|じかん}が<b>ないです</b>。
I haven't really got time today. (softer, conversational)
</pre>

The same split runs through the past: `～ませんでした` is the formal past
negative, `～なかったです` the colloquial one. In writing, an email to a client,
or any situation with a script, use the `ません` column. With a colleague you
get on with, `ないです` is friendlier.

### ～ませんか vs ～ましょう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～ませんか` **asks** whether you'd like to; `～ましょう`
**assumes** you would. Both are built on the same ます-stem.

</div>

<pre>
{f|一緒|いっしょ}にお{f|昼|ひる}を<b>{f|食|た}べませんか</b>。
Would you like to have lunch together?
</pre>

<pre>
じゃ、そろそろ<b>{f|行|い}きましょう</b>。
Right, let's get going then.
</pre>

`～ませんか` leaves the other person free to say no, so it is the polite way to
open an invitation. `～ましょう` is for when the plan is already agreed, or when
you are the one leading - a tour guide, a teacher, the person organising the
evening. Using `～ましょう` to make the first invitation to someone senior is
pushy.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|食|た}べるます → ✅ <b>{f|食|た}べます</b>
  `ます` attaches to the ます-stem, never to the dictionary form. Drop the る
  first.

- ❌ {f|飲|の}みませんかった → ✅ <b>{f|飲|の}みませんでした</b>
  `ません` has no past form of its own. You put the past tense on the copula
  behind it: ません + でした.

- ❌ {f|帰|かえ}ます → ✅ <b>{f|帰|かえ}ります</b>
  {f|帰|かえ}る ends in る but is an う-verb, so the stem is {f|帰|かえ}り, not
  {f|帰|かえ}.

- ❌ この{f|本|ほん}は{f|高|たか}いますか。 → ✅ この{f|本|ほん}は<b>{f|高|たか}いですか</b>。
  `ます` is for verbs only. Adjectives and nouns take `です`.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}きますと{f|思|おも}います。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}<b>{f|行|い}く</b>と{f|思|おも}います。
  Inside a clause the verb is plain. `ます` only appears at the very end of the
  sentence.

## Practice

**1.** {f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|電車|でんしゃ}で{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|行|い}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|行|い}<b>きます</b>

{f|行|い}く is an う-verb, so the final -ku becomes -ki before `ます`:
{f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}き + ます. The non-past covers habits, which is what
{f|毎日|まいにち} calls for.

</details>

**2.** {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|雪|ゆき}が（{f|降|ふ}りません・{f|降|ふ}りませんでした）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|雪|ゆき}が<b>{f|降|ふ}りませんでした</b>。

{f|昨日|きのう} fixes the sentence in the past, so the negative has to be past
too. Japanese marks tense on the verb even when the time word already says it - 
you cannot leave the ending non-past the way English can.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I didn't watch television last night."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|夜|よる}はテレビを<b>{f|見|み}ませんでした</b>。

{f|見|み}る is a る-verb, so the stem is just {f|見|み}: drop る, add
ませんでした. The plain-form answer {f|見|み}なかった is equally correct if you
are talking to a friend.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|今朝|けさ}は{f|朝|あさ}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べるませんでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|今朝|けさ}は{f|朝|あさ}ご{f|飯|はん}を<b>{f|食|た}べませんでした</b>。

The dictionary form and the polite ending cannot both be there. {f|食|た}べる is
a る-verb: remove る to get the stem {f|食|た}べ, then attach ませんでした.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|今|いま}、{f|時間|じかん}がありますか。
(b) {f|今|いま}、{f|時間|じかん}ある？

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

They ask exactly the same question; only the relationship differs. (a) is the
polite style - what you say to a colleague, a teacher, or anyone you are not
close to. (b) is plain form with the question mark carried by rising
intonation alone, which is what you use with a friend or a family member.

Say (b) to your manager and it sounds startlingly familiar; say (a) to a close
friend and it sounds like you are about to deliver bad news.

</details>
