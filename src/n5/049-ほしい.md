# ほしい

**Meaning** · want a thing - the speaker's own desire for an object

`ほしい` (kanji {f|欲|ほ}しい, but usually written in kana at this level) is an
**い-adjective**, not a verb. It does not take an object with `を`; the thing you
want is marked with `が`. And like `～たい`, it reports a want felt from the inside,
so plain `ほしい` covers **your own** wants and questions about the **listener's** - 
never a flat statement about what a third person wants.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (the thing wanted) | Noun + が + ほしい | {f|車|くるま} → {f|車|くるま}がほしい |
| Verb (an action you want *from someone*) | て-form + ほしい | {f|手伝|てつだ}う → {f|手伝|てつだ}ってほしい |
| Verb (an action you want *to do*) | not possible - use `～たい` | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}みたい |
| Third person's want | Noun + を + ほしがる | {f|犬|いぬ} → {f|犬|いぬ}をほしがっている |

Once `ほしい` is in place it conjugates exactly like {f|高|たか}い or {f|安|やす}い:

| い-adjective form | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Polite | + です | ほしいです |
| Negative | ほしい → ほしくない | ほしくない |
| Polite negative | + です／ありません | ほしくないです／ほしくありません |
| Past | ほしい → ほしかった | ほしかった |
| Negative past | ほしい → ほしくなかった | ほしくなかった |
| て-form | ほしい → ほしくて | ほしくて |
| Adverbial | ほしい → ほしく | ほしくなる |
| Before a noun | ほしい + Noun | ほしいもの |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ほしい` is an い-adjective. So it is `ほしくない`, never
❌`ほしいじゃない`, and `ほしかった`, never ❌`ほしいでした`. And it never conjugates
like a verb: there is no ❌`ほしみます`.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
お{f|金|かね}が<b>ほしい</b>。
I want money.
</pre>

<pre>
もっと{f|時間|じかん}が<b>ほしい</b>です。
I want more time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|誕生日|たんじょうび}に{f|何|なに}が<b>ほしい</b>ですか。
What do you want for your birthday?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}は{f|何|なに}も<b>ほしくない</b>。
I don't want anything right now.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どものころ、{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|部屋|へや}が<b>ほしかった</b>。
When I was little I wanted a room of my own.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|前|まえ}から{f|犬|いぬ}を<b>ほしがっています</b>。
My little brother has wanted a dog for ages.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}は{f|急|いそ}ぎませんが、{f|一度|いちど}ゆっくり{f|話|はな}す{f|機会|きかい}が<b>ほしい</b>です。
There's no rush on an answer, but I'd like a chance to talk it through properly.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|書類|しょるい}、{f|明日|あした}の{f|朝|あさ}までに{f|目|め}を{f|通|とお}して<b>ほしい</b>んですが、お{f|願|ねが}いできますか。
I'd like you to look these documents over by tomorrow morning - could I ask you to?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ほしいです` is polite in *form* only. The content is still a bare statement of
appetite, and pointing it at someone above you lands like a child asking for a
present: {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|新|あたら}しいパソコンがほしいです is grammatical and
socially awkward. Upward, you reframe the want as a request or a hedge:

- <b>～がほしいのですが／～がほしいんですが</b> - trails off and lets the listener
  offer. {f|資料|しりょう}のコピーがほしいんですが…
- <b>～をいただけますか／お{f|願|ねが}いできますか</b> - asks instead of wanting.
- <b>～があると{f|助|たす}かります</b> - "it would help if I had…", the standard
  workplace softener.

The same asymmetry runs upward in questions. {f|社長|しゃちょう}、{f|何|なに}がほしいですか
is far too direct; offering with いかがですか is what you actually say.

In a shop or a café, `ほしい` is simply the wrong tool. You do not say
これがほしいです to a waiter - you say これをください or これをお{f|願|ねが}いします.
`ほしい` describes your inner state; `ください` performs the request.

Casually, `ほしい` is very common and takes all the usual endings:
ほしいなあ (wistful), ほしい！, ほしいんだけど…. Men and women use it alike. In
writing the kanji {f|欲|ほ}しい appears. What `ほしい` does **not** have is a
humble register of its own - there is no polite conjugation that makes it suitable
upward, which is why formal Japanese switches word entirely, to
<code>{f|頂|いただ}きたい</code> or <code>お{f|願|ねが}いしたい</code>.

`ほしがる` is an ordinary う-verb ({f|欲|ほ}しがらない, ほしがります, ほしがって).
It describes desire as something you can *see* in someone - the reaching, the
asking, the looking. That outside-observer flavour makes it faintly belittling, so
it is not used about superiors: ❌{f|課長|かちょう}は{f|新|あたら}しい{f|車|くるま}をほしがっています.

## Contrast

### ほしい vs ～たい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ほしい` wants a **noun**; `～たい` wants an **action**. They
divide the work; they never stack.

</div>

<pre>
アイスクリーム<b>がほしい</b>。
I want ice cream.
</pre>

<pre>
アイスクリームが<b>{f|食|た}べたい</b>。
I want to eat ice cream.
</pre>

The two are otherwise twins: both are い-adjectives, both mark their target with
`が`, both are barred from flat third-person statements, and both have a
`～がる` form for other people. What `ほしい` cannot do is attach to a verb - 
❌{f|休|やす}むがほしい is not a sentence. If the thing you want is an action you
perform yourself, `～たい` is the only route.

### ほしい vs ～てほしい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `Noun + がほしい` wants a **thing**; `Verb-て + ほしい` wants
**someone else to do something**. The person you want it from takes `に`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}が<b>ほしい</b>。
I want the instruction manual.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}を<b>{f|教|おし}えてほしい</b>。
I want you to show me how to use it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|母|はは}に{f|来|き}て<b>ほしくない</b>。
I don't want my mother to come.
</pre>

`～てほしい` is a wish, not a command, so it is gentler than a direct request - but
it is also more personal, and to a superior it presumes. The polite ladder runs
{f|待|ま}ってほしい → {f|待|ま}っていただきたい → {f|待|ま}っていただけないでしょうか.
The negative is `～ないでほしい`: {f|勝手|かって}に{f|決|き}めないでほしい.

### ほしい vs ほしがる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ほしい` for me and you; `ほしがる` for him, her and them. The
particle flips too: `が` with `ほしい`, `を` with `ほしがる`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|娘|むすめ}はずっとピアノを<b>ほしがっています</b>。
My daughter has been wanting a piano for a long time.
</pre>

Note the `～ている`. A desire someone is feeling *now* is `ほしがっている`; bare
`ほしがる` reads as a habit - あの{f|子|こ}は{f|友|とも}だちのものを{f|何|なん}でもほしがる.

`ほしがる` is not the only escape. These are often smoother, and safer upward:

- {f|妹|いもうと}は{f|新|あたら}しい{f|辞書|じしょ}がほしいそうです。 (I hear that…)
- {f|妹|いもうと}は{f|新|あたら}しい{f|辞書|じしょ}がほしいと{f|言|い}っています。 (she says…)
- {f|妹|いもうと}は{f|新|あたら}しい{f|辞書|じしょ}がほしいようです。 (it seems…)

<div class="note">

**Note** - the ban is on **plain main-clause statements** only. Inside a modifying
clause, plain `ほしい` about other people is perfectly normal:
{f|資料|しりょう}がほしい{f|人|ひと}は、{f|前|まえ}の{f|机|つくえ}から{f|取|と}ってください。
「Anyone who wants a copy, take one from the desk at the front.」

</div>

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|車|くるま}<b>を</b>ほしいです。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|車|くるま}<b>が</b>ほしいです。
  `ほしい` is an adjective, so it has no direct object. `を` appears only with the
  verb `ほしがる`.

- ❌ {f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|新|あたら}しい{f|車|くるま}がほしいです。 → ✅ {f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|新|あたら}しい{f|車|くるま}を<b>ほしがっています</b>。
  A flat statement about a third person's want needs `ほしがっている`, or a hedge
  like `ほしいそうです`.

- ❌ <b>ほしいじゃない</b>／<b>ほしいでした</b> → ✅ <b>ほしくない</b>／<b>ほしかった</b>
  い-adjective endings, not な-adjective ones.

- ❌ {f|休|やす}む<b>がほしい</b>。 → ✅ {f|休|やす}み<b>たい</b>。
  `ほしい` cannot take a verb. Wanting to perform an action is `～たい` territory.

- ❌ （カフェで）これ<b>がほしいです</b>。 → ✅ これを<b>ください</b>。
  Naming your inner state at a server is odd. Ordering is done with `ください` or
  <code>お{f|願|ねが}いします</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|新|あたら}しいカメラ____ほしいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|新|あたら}しいカメラ<b>が</b>ほしいです。

`ほしい` is an い-adjective, and what it wants is marked with `が`, not `を`. The
English "want a camera" has an object, but the Japanese does not.

</details>

**2.** {f|兄|あに}はバイクを（ほしいです・ほしがっています）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|兄|あに}はバイクを<b>ほしがっています</b>。

<code>{f|兄|あに}</code> is a third person, so a plain `ほしい` statement would be claiming direct
access to someone else's inner state. `ほしがっている` reports the desire as
something you can observe - and note that it takes `を`, which is why the sentence
was written with `を` to begin with.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "When I was a student, I wanted a motorbike."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|学生|がくせい}のとき、バイク<b>がほしかった</b>（です）。

The past of an い-adjective: ほしい → ほしかった. There is no ❌`ほしいでした`; adding
`です` after `ほしかった` is what makes it polite.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|辞書|じしょ}をほしいんですが、いい{f|店|みせ}を{f|知|し}りませんか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|辞書|じしょ}<b>が</b>ほしいんですが、いい{f|店|みせ}を{f|知|し}りませんか。

The `を` is the interference: English treats "want" as a transitive verb, Japanese
treats `ほしい` as an adjective whose target takes `が`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|静|しず}かにして<b>ほしい</b>。
(b) {f|静|しず}かにして<b>ください</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b) is a direct request aimed at the listener - it tells them to do something, and
in the wrong context it can sound like an instruction. (a) reports the speaker's
wish instead of issuing the request, so it is indirect, and among friends it comes
across as softer, almost a complaint said out loud.

Indirectness is not the same as politeness upward, though. To a superior neither
is ideal: {f|静|しず}かにしていただけませんか is the form that actually works.

</details>
