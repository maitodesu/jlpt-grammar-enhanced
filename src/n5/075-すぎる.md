# ～すぎる

**Meaning** · too much; excessively - past the point where it is still all right

`～すぎる` is **not** an intensifier. {f|高|たか}すぎる does not mean "very
expensive"; it means the price has crossed a line and become a problem. Whenever
you attach it, you are passing judgement: this is more than it should be.

## Formation

Cut the word back to its stem, then add `すぎる`. `すぎる` itself is a regular
る-verb: すぎます, すぎない, すぎた, すぎて.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + すぎる | {f|飲|の}む → {f|飲|の}み → {f|飲|の}みすぎる |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + すぎる | {f|食|た}べる → {f|食|た}べ → {f|食|た}べすぎる |
| Verb (する／{f|来|く}る) | し／{f|来|き} + すぎる | {f|心配|しんぱい}する → {f|心配|しんぱい}しすぎる |
| Verb (negative) | ない → なさ + すぎる | {f|食|た}べない → {f|食|た}べなさすぎる |
| い-adjective | drop い + すぎる | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}すぎる |
| な-adjective | stem + すぎる | {f|静|しず}かだ → {f|静|しず}かすぎる |
| いい／よい | よ + すぎる（irregular） | いい → よすぎる |
| ない | な<b>さ</b> + すぎる | ない → なさすぎる |
| Noun form | すぎ | {f|飲|の}みすぎ, {f|働|はたら}きすぎ |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - whatever goes in front of `すぎる` is the same shape you would
use in front of `ます` (for verbs) or in front of `～くない` (for adjectives, minus
the く). If you can still see an い or a だ, you have cut too little.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
このかばんは<b>{f|高|たか}すぎます</b>。
This bag is too expensive.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}のパーティーで<b>{f|飲|の}みすぎました</b>。
I drank too much at the party yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
このスープは{f|私|わたし}には<b>からすぎる</b>。
This soup is too spicy for me.
</pre>

<pre>
ごはんを<b>{f|食|た}べすぎて</b>、おなかが{f|痛|いた}いです。
I ate too much and now my stomach hurts.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}は<b>{f|静|しず}かすぎて</b>、なんだかおちつきません。
This room is too quiet - it makes me uneasy somehow.
</pre>

<pre>
このシャツは{f|息子|むすこ}には{f|少|すこ}し<b>{f|大|おお}きすぎました</b>。
This shirt turned out a bit too big for my son.
</pre>

<pre>
テレビを<b>{f|見|み}すぎる</b>と、{f|目|め}が{f|悪|わる}くなりますよ。
If you watch too much television, your eyes will suffer.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|一年生|いちねんせい}には<b>{f|難|むずか}しすぎる</b>ので、もっとやさしいものに{f|変|か}えましょう。
This question is too hard for first-years, so let's swap it for an easier one.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral in itself - <code>{f|高|たか}すぎます</code> is as polite as the `ます` on
the end makes it - but the *content* is always a criticism, so it can land harder
than you intend. Softening it with `ちょっと` or <code>{f|少|すこ}し</code> is extremely common:
ちょっと{f|高|たか}すぎますね sounds like a negotiation, while
{f|高|たか}すぎます sounds like a verdict.

The `すぎ` noun form appears constantly in everyday health talk:
{f|飲|の}みすぎ, {f|食|た}べすぎ, {f|働|はたら}きすぎ, {f|寝|ね}すぎ - often on its own
as a diagnosis. {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|飲|の}みすぎだ。

One modern exception to the "always negative" rule: in casual speech, especially
among younger speakers, `すぎる` is used as pure hyperbole for praise - 
かわいすぎる, おいしすぎる, すごすぎる. This is slangy, it is written with a lot of
exclamation, and it does not belong in a polite conversation or in writing. Learn
to recognise it; do not build sentences on it yet.

Note also that `すぎる` is a real verb in its own right meaning "to pass by"
（{f|時間|じかん}が{f|過|す}ぎる, {f|駅|えき}を{f|通|とお}り{f|過|す}ぎる）. Compounds are
usually written in kana, the standalone verb in kanji.

## Contrast

### ～すぎる vs とても／すごく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `とても` says the quality is high. `すぎる` says the quality has
gone wrong. Praise never uses `すぎる`.

</div>

<pre>
このコーヒーはとても{f|熱|あつ}いです。
This coffee is very hot. (nice and hot)
</pre>

<pre>
このコーヒーは<b>{f|熱|あつ}すぎます</b>。
This coffee is too hot. (I can't drink it)
</pre>

So a compliment must not take `すぎる` in careful speech: if you tell a cook
❌ この{f|料理|りょうり}はおいしすぎます in a formal setting, the literal reading is
that the dish is unreasonably rich. Say とてもおいしいです.

### ～すぎる vs ～すぎ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `すぎる` is a verb and conjugates; `すぎ` is a noun and takes
`だ／です`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}は<b>{f|働|はたら}きすぎました</b>。
I worked too much yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|働|はたら}きすぎですよ。{f|少|すこ}し{f|休|やす}んでください。
You're overworking. Take a break.
</pre>

The noun form is what you use to label a situation rather than narrate an action,
and it is the form that appears in set warnings like
{f|食|た}べすぎに{f|注意|ちゅうい} - "don't overeat".

### ～すぎる vs the standalone verb すぎる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - attached to a stem, it means "too much". Standing alone with
its own particle, the same verb means "to go past".

</div>

<pre>
{f|三時|さんじ}を{f|過|す}ぎました。
It's gone three o'clock.
</pre>

<pre>
おかしを<b>{f|食|た}べすぎました</b>。
I ate too many sweets.
</pre>

In the first sentence `を` marks the point being passed and the verb is the main
verb of the sentence. In the second, `すぎる` has no object of its own - it is
riding on {f|食|た}べ. If you can find a `を` in front of it, it is the "pass by"
verb.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|食|た}べるすぎる → ✅ <b>{f|食|た}べすぎる</b>
  Verbs attach at the ます-stem, never at the dictionary form. Ask yourself what
  goes before `ます`: {f|食|た}べます → {f|食|た}べ.

- ❌ {f|高|たか}いすぎる → ✅ <b>{f|高|たか}すぎる</b>
  い-adjectives drop the final い first. The same cut you make for
  <code>{f|高|たか}くない</code> or <code>{f|高|たか}かった</code>.

- ❌ {f|静|しず}かなすぎる → ✅ <b>{f|静|しず}かすぎる</b>
  な-adjectives use the bare stem. No `な`, and no `だ`.

- ❌ いいすぎる（meaning "too good"）→ ✅ <b>よすぎる</b>
  `いい` becomes `よ-` in every conjugation, and this one is no exception. Careful:
  {f|言|い}いすぎる is a real and different word - "to say too much, to go too far".

- ❌ {f|野菜|やさい}を{f|食|た}べないすぎる → ✅ {f|野菜|やさい}を<b>{f|食|た}べなさすぎる</b>
  Negatives insert `さ` before `すぎる`, because they behave like adjectives - 
  the same `さ` you see in なさすぎる.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|靴|くつ}は{f|小|ちい}____すぎます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|靴|くつ}は{f|小|ちい}<b>さ</b>すぎます。

<code>{f|小|ちい}さい</code> is an い-adjective, so you remove only the final い and keep
the さ: {f|小|ちい}さい → {f|小|ちい}さ → {f|小|ちい}さすぎます. Learners often cut
back to {f|小|ちい} by mistake.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|昨日|きのう}の{f|映画|えいが}は（とてもおもしろかった・おもしろすぎた）です。 You loved it and want to recommend it.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|映画|えいが}は<b>とてもおもしろかった</b>です。

`すぎる` is a complaint. おもしろすぎた would suggest the film was somehow
excessive - overwhelming, more than you wanted. To recommend something, use
`とても` or `すごく`.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I got up too late this morning."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|今朝|けさ}は<b>{f|起|お}きるのが{f|遅|おそ}すぎました</b>。
（or simply: {f|今朝|けさ}は<b>{f|寝|ね}すぎました</b>。）

Either the adjective takes `すぎる`（{f|遅|おそ}い → {f|遅|おそ}すぎる）or you put it
on the verb that caused the problem. <code>{f|寝|ね}すぎる</code> - "to oversleep" - is what
a Japanese speaker would actually say.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: このお{f|茶|ちゃ}はあついいすぎます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ このお{f|茶|ちゃ}は<b>あつすぎます</b>。

The writer has attached `すぎる` without cutting the adjective, and doubled the
い along the way. あつい → あつ → あつすぎます. Only the final い goes.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|町|まち}はとてもしずかです。
(b) この{f|町|まち}はしずかすぎます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a compliment, or at worst a neutral description. Quiet is being presented
as a feature of the town.

(b) says the quiet has become a problem - nothing is open, nobody is about, the
speaker is unsettled by it. The `すぎる` is what converts a pleasant quality into
a complaint, and that conversion happens whatever the adjective is.

</details>
