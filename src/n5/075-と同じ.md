# ～と同じ

**Meaning** · the same as ～ - identical to something else, not merely similar

<code>{f|同|おな}じ</code> is the word for sameness, and `と` is the particle that names
what the sameness is measured against. English says "the same **as**"; Japanese
says "<b>と</b> the same". Get that particle wrong and the sentence falls apart.

## Formation

<code>{f|同|おな}じ</code> is a strange word: it behaves like a な-adjective everywhere
*except* directly in front of a noun, where the `な` disappears.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (predicate) | Noun + と{f|同|おな}じ + です／だ | {f|私|わたし}と{f|同|おな}じです |
| Noun (modifying) | Noun + と{f|同|おな}じ + Noun | {f|私|わたし}と{f|同|おな}じクラス |
| Negative | と{f|同|おな}じ + じゃない／ではありません | {f|同|おな}じじゃありません |
| Past | と{f|同|おな}じ + でした／だった | {f|同|おな}じでした |
| Degree | Noun + と{f|同|おな}じくらい + adjective | {f|私|わたし}と{f|同|おな}じくらい{f|高|たか}い |
| Manner | Noun + と{f|同|おな}じように + verb | {f|前|まえ}と{f|同|おな}じように{f|作|つく}る |
| Two things listed | AとBは{f|同|おな}じ | このかばんとあのかばんは{f|同|おな}じです |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - no `な` before a noun. ✅{f|同|おな}じ{f|学校|がっこう} ／
❌{f|同|おな}じな{f|学校|がっこう}. This one word is the exception that every
learner trips over, because {f|静|しず}かな{f|学校|がっこう} and
{f|有名|ゆうめい}な{f|学校|がっこう} behave normally.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>

</pre>

</pre>

</pre>

<pre>

My younger brother goes to the same school as my older brother.

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

I'll write down the same thing I explained yesterday, one more time.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and just as common in speech as in writing.

The `と` may be dropped when the two items are listed together as a pair:
このペンとそのペンは{f|同|おな}じです。 Here the `と` you can see is the "and"
particle joining the two pens, and <code>{f|同|おな}じ</code> stands alone as the
predicate. Both patterns are correct - what is *not* correct is having neither.

Watch the `の` when you compare possessions. <code>{f|私|わたし}と{f|同|おな}じ</code>
compares the other thing to **me**, the person. <code>{f|私|わたし}のと{f|同|おな}じ</code>
compares it to **mine**, the object. English hides this distinction; Japanese does
not, and getting it wrong produces sentences like "this bag is the same as me."

Two fixed extensions are worth learning as units:

- <code>{f|同|おな}じくらい</code> - "about the same amount / to the same degree". This is
  how you say "as … as" for adjectives.
- <code>{f|同|おな}じように</code> - "in the same way". It modifies a verb, not a noun.

In writing you may also meet <code>{f|同様|どうよう}に</code> and <code>{f|同|おな}じく</code>, both
stiffer equivalents. Neither belongs in conversation at this level.

## Contrast

### {f|同|おな}じ + noun vs {f|同|おな}じ<b>な</b> + noun

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|同|おな}じ</code> is the one な-adjective that drops its `な`
in front of a noun.

</div>

<pre>
{f|山田|やまだ}さんと<b>{f|同|おな}じ</b>{f|意見|いけん}です。
I'm of the same opinion as Yamada.
</pre>

❌ {f|同|おな}じな{f|意見|いけん} is simply wrong. But `な` **does** come back before
`ので` and `のに`: {f|同|おな}じなので, {f|同|おな}じなのに. So the rule is narrower
than "no `な` ever" - it is "no `な` before a noun".

### ～と{f|同|おな}じ vs ～に{f|似|に}ている

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|同|おな}じ</code> means identical. <code>{f|似|に}ている</code> means
resembling. And note the particle flips: `と` for same, `に` for similar.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|時計|とけい}は{f|兄|あに}の<b>と{f|同|おな}じ</b>です。
This watch is the same one my brother has.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|時計|とけい}は{f|兄|あに}のに{f|似|に}ています。
This watch looks like my brother's.
</pre>

The first says you could swap them and nobody would notice. The second says they
merely look alike. For faces, <code>{f|似|に}ている</code> is the only option - 
{f|母|はは}に{f|似|に}ています, never {f|母|はは}と{f|同|おな}じです unless you mean
something very odd.

### ～と{f|同|おな}じ vs ～と{f|違|ちが}う

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same frame, negated. Both take `と`, and both put the
comparison target in front of it.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|漢字|かんじ}はあの{f|漢字|かんじ}<b>と{f|同|おな}じ</b>{f|読|よ}み{f|方|かた}です。
This kanji has the same reading as that one.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|漢字|かんじ}はあの{f|漢字|かんじ}と{f|読|よ}み{f|方|かた}が{f|違|ちが}います。
This kanji is read differently from that one.
</pre>

Note that <code>{f|違|ちが}う</code> is a **verb**, so it takes ます／ました and never
です: ❌{f|違|ちが}うです → ✅{f|違|ちが}います. <code>{f|同|おな}じ</code>, being adjectival,
does the opposite and needs です.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}<b>に</b>{f|同|おな}じです。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>と</b>{f|同|おな}じです。
  The comparison target is marked with `と`. `に` belongs to
  <code>{f|似|に}ている</code>, not here.

- ❌ {f|山田|やまだ}さんと{f|同|おな}じ<b>な</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}です。 → ✅ {f|山田|やまだ}さんと{f|同|おな}じ{f|会社|かいしゃ}です。
  No `な` in front of a noun. This is the single most frequent error with the word.

- ❌ このかばんは{f|私|わたし}と{f|同|おな}じです。（meaning "the same as mine"）→ ✅ このかばんは{f|私|わたし}<b>の</b>と{f|同|おな}じです。
  Without `の` you are comparing a bag to a person. `の` turns
  <code>{f|私|わたし}</code> into "mine".

- ❌ {f|去年|きょねん}と{f|同|おな}じ<b>に</b>{f|作|つく}りました。 → ✅ {f|去年|きょねん}と{f|同|おな}じ<b>ように</b>{f|作|つく}りました。
  To modify a verb you need <code>ように</code>. Bare
  <code>{f|同|おな}じに</code> is not idiomatic here.

- ❌ {f|二人|ふたり}の{f|背|せ}は{f|同|おな}じ<b>くらいと</b>{f|高|たか}いです。 → ✅ {f|二人|ふたり}は{f|同|おな}じくらい{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}いです。
  <code>{f|同|おな}じくらい</code> sits directly in front of the adjective, with no
  particle between them.

## Practice

**1.** {f|林|はやし}さんは{f|私|わたし}____{f|同|おな}じアパートに{f|住|す}んでいます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|林|はやし}さんは{f|私|わたし}<b>と</b>{f|同|おな}じアパートに{f|住|す}んでいます。

The thing being matched always takes `と`. Note also that
<code>{f|同|おな}じ</code> attaches straight to アパート with no `な` in between.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|写真|しゃしん}の{f|人|ひと}は{f|母|はは}（と{f|同|おな}じ・に{f|似|に}ている）です。 You mean the person resembles your mother.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|写真|しゃしん}の{f|人|ひと}は{f|母|はは}<b>に{f|似|に}ています</b>。

Resemblance takes <code>に{f|似|に}ている</code>, with `に`.
{f|母|はは}と{f|同|おな}じです would claim the two are the same person - or the
same *thing*, which is stranger still. Note the answer ends in ています, not です,
because <code>{f|似|に}る</code> is a verb.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I ordered the same thing as you."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

あなた<b>と{f|同|おな}じ</b>ものを{f|注文|ちゅうもん}しました。

<code>{f|同|おな}じ</code> modifies もの directly - no `な`. In real conversation the
`あなた` would usually be replaced by the person's name, or dropped entirely:
{f|同|おな}じものをください。

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|傘|かさ}はあなたと{f|同|おな}じなです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|傘|かさ}はあなた<b>の</b>と{f|同|おな}じです。

Two problems. `な` cannot stand before `です` - <code>{f|同|おな}じです</code> is the
predicate form. And the umbrella should be compared with *yours*, not with *you*,
which is what the added `の` does.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|去年|きょねん}と{f|同|おな}じ{f|店|みせ}です。
(b) {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|店|みせ}に{f|似|に}ている{f|店|みせ}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) claims identity: it is literally that shop, the one you went to last year,
still in the same place under the same name.

(b) claims resemblance only: a different shop that reminds you of the old one - 
similar food, similar atmosphere. Japanese keeps these two claims strictly apart,
where English "like last year's place" can be read either way.

</details>
