# な-adjective conjugation

**Meaning** · how な-adjectives work - the な before a noun, and じゃない,
だった, で

A な-adjective is a word like {f|静|しず}か, {f|便利|べんり}, {f|好|す}き or
きれい. The name comes from the one place it differs visibly from a noun: put it
in front of a noun and it needs a な: <b>{f|静|しず}かな{f|町|まち}</b> "a
quiet town".

The important structural fact is what な-adjectives *don't* do: they do not
conjugate. The word itself never changes. All the tense and negation happens on
the copula behind it - {f|静|しず}かだ → {f|静|しず}かだった →
{f|静|しず}かじゃない. If you can handle {f|学生|がくせい}だった, you can
already handle {f|静|しず}かだった, because they are the same machinery.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| な-adjective (before a noun) | ～な + Noun | {f|便利|べんり}な{f|店|みせ} |
| な-adjective (plain non-past) | ～だ | {f|便利|べんり}だ |
| な-adjective (polite non-past) | ～です | {f|便利|べんり}です |
| な-adjective (negative) | ～じゃない／ではない | {f|便利|べんり}じゃない |
| な-adjective (past) | ～だった | {f|便利|べんり}だった |
| な-adjective (past negative) | ～じゃなかった | {f|便利|べんり}じゃなかった |
| な-adjective (て-form) | ～で | {f|便利|べんり}で |
| な-adjective (adverb) | ～に | {f|便利|べんり}になる |

Politeness, as with nouns, is a separate layer:

| Plain | Polite (everyday) | Polite (formal) |
|---|---|---|
| {f|元気|げんき}だ | {f|元気|げんき}です | {f|元気|げんき}です |
| {f|元気|げんき}じゃない | {f|元気|げんき}じゃないです | {f|元気|げんき}ではありません |
| {f|元気|げんき}だった | {f|元気|げんき}でした | {f|元気|げんき}でした |
| {f|元気|げんき}じゃなかった | {f|元気|げんき}じゃなかったです | {f|元気|げんき}ではありませんでした |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `な` appears **only** directly before a noun. Everywhere else
it is not there. So {f|静|しず}かな{f|部屋|へや} but {f|部屋|へや}は
{f|静|しず}かです - never ❌{f|静|しず}かなです.

</div>

Set the two adjective classes side by side and the whole system falls out of one
question: does the word inflect, or does the copula do it for you?

| | い-adjective ({f|高|たか}い) | な-adjective ({f|静|しず}か) |
|---|---|---|
| Before a noun | {f|高|たか}い{f|山|やま} | {f|静|しず}かな{f|山|やま} |
| Plain non-past | {f|高|たか}い | {f|静|しず}かだ |
| Negative | {f|高|たか}くない | {f|静|しず}かじゃない |
| Past | {f|高|たか}かった | {f|静|しず}かだった |
| Past negative | {f|高|たか}くなかった | {f|静|しず}かじゃなかった |
| て-form | {f|高|たか}くて | {f|静|しず}かで |
| Adverb | {f|高|たか}く | {f|静|しず}かに |

<div class="note">

**Note** - な-adjectives behave almost exactly like nouns, and historically that
is what many of them are. {f|元気|げんき}, {f|親切|しんせつ} and
{f|健康|けんこう} can all appear as nouns in their own right. The **only**
systematic difference is what goes in front of a noun: a な-adjective takes
`な`, a noun takes `の`. {f|病気|びょうき}の{f|人|ひと} "a sick person" (noun),
{f|元気|げんき}な{f|人|ひと} "a lively person" (な-adjective). Everything else - 
だ, だった, じゃない, で, でした - is shared.

</div>

Words that catch people out:

- Ending in い but な-adjectives: きれい, {f|嫌|きら}い, {f|有名|ゆうめい},
  {f|嫌|いや}, {f|得意|とくい}. きれいじゃない, not ❌きれくない.
- {f|同|おな}じ is a な-adjective that drops the な before a noun:
  {f|同|おな}じ{f|人|ひと}, never ❌{f|同|おな}じな{f|人|ひと}. But the な comes
  back before ので and のに: {f|同|おな}じなので.
- {f|好|す}き, {f|嫌|きら}い, {f|上手|じょうず}, {f|下手|へた} are adjectives in
  Japanese although they translate as English verbs, and their object takes
  `が`: {f|魚|さかな}が{f|好|す}きです.

</pre>

## Nuance & register

The word itself is register-neutral; the copula after it is where register
lives. `だ` is plain and slightly assertive, `です` is polite, and in casual
speech `だ` is very often dropped altogether:
この{f|店|みせ}、{f|静|しず}か - a complete, natural sentence. Women's casual
speech in particular tends to omit `だ`; keeping it can sound blunt or
declamatory.

The negative comes in three shapes, and they form a clear ladder:

- **じゃない** - casual, spoken. A contraction of ではない.
- **じゃないです／じゃありません** - polite, everyday.
- **ではありません／ではございません** - formal, written, and service Japanese.

`ではない` in speech sounds like a lecture or a debate; `じゃない` in a written
report looks careless. That mismatch, rather than any grammatical error, is what
makes learners' Japanese sound off.

One more thing worth knowing early: `じゃない` with rising intonation is a
tag question, not a negative - きれいじゃない？ means "isn't it pretty?", the
opposite of what the words look like. Context and intonation carry it.

Some な-adjectives are almost never used about oneself. Saying
わたしは{f|上手|じょうず}です about your own Japanese is boastful; the modest
answer is まだまだです. And {f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ} has drifted in modern
speech into a soft refusal - offered a plastic bag, {f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}です
means "no thanks", not "that's fine".

## Contrast

### な-adjective vs い-adjective - telling them apart

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ignore the last letter and look at the **negative**. If it
takes じゃない, it is a な-adjective. If it takes くない, it is an い-adjective.
The dictionary form lies; the negative never does.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}は<b>きれいじゃない</b>。
This room isn't clean.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}は<b>{f|汚|きたな}くない</b>。
This room isn't dirty.
</pre>

きれい and {f|汚|きたな}い both end in い, and they belong to different classes.
The reliable signal is the syllable before the final い: an い-adjective never
ends in **えい**, so きれい (kirei) and {f|有名|ゆうめい} (yuumei) give
themselves away. {f|嫌|きら}い does not, and just has to be learned. When you
meet a new adjective, learn it with its な attached - きれいな, {f|好|す}きな - 
and the problem disappears.

### な vs の before a noun

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - な-adjectives take `な`; nouns take `の`. Everything after
the noun is identical, so this one particle is the whole visible difference
between the two word classes.

</div>

<pre>
{f|親切|しんせつ}な<b>{f|店員|てんいん}</b>さんに{f|道|みち}を{f|教|おし}えてもらった。
A kind shop assistant gave me directions.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日本語|にほんご}の<b>{f|先生|せんせい}</b>に{f|手紙|てがみ}を{f|書|か}きました。
I wrote a letter to my Japanese teacher.
</pre>

A few words sit in both camps and change meaning accordingly.
{f|病気|びょうき}の{f|人|ひと} is "a person with an illness" (noun +の);
{f|元気|げんき}な{f|人|ひと} is "a lively person" (な-adjective). If you are
unsure which class a word is in, `の` is the safer guess for anything that
names a thing, and `な` for anything that describes a quality.

### ～な vs ～に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `な` modifies a **noun**; `に` modifies a **verb**. Same
adjective, two different jobs.

</div>

<pre>
<b>{f|静|しず}かな</b>{f|人|ひと}が{f|好|す}きです。
I like quiet people.
</pre>

<pre>
ドアを<b>{f|静|しず}かに</b>{f|閉|し}めてください。
Please close the door quietly.
</pre>

`に` is also the form used with なる and する:
{f|上手|じょうず}になる "get good at something", きれいにする "make something
clean". This `に` is the な-adjective's equivalent of the
い-adjective's `く`, and the two never cross over:
❌{f|静|しず}かくする, ❌{f|安|やす}にする.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|静|しず}かの{f|部屋|へや} → ✅ <b>{f|静|しず}かな{f|部屋|へや}</b>
  {f|静|しず}か is a な-adjective, so it takes `な` before a noun. `の` is for
  nouns modifying nouns.

- ❌ きれいくない → ✅ <b>きれいじゃない</b>
  きれい ends in い but is a な-adjective. It negates with `じゃない`, like a
  noun.

- ❌ {f|元気|げんき}なです → ✅ <b>{f|元気|げんき}です</b>
  The `な` only appears immediately before a noun. Before `です` there is
  nothing.

- ❌ {f|有名|ゆうめい}かった → ✅ <b>{f|有名|ゆうめい}でした</b>／<b>{f|有名|ゆうめい}だった</b>
  な-adjectives have no past tense of their own; the copula supplies it.
  `かった` belongs to い-adjectives.

- ❌ {f|上手|じょうず}に{f|人|ひと} → ✅ <b>{f|上手|じょうず}な{f|人|ひと}</b>
  `に` goes with verbs, `な` with nouns. {f|上手|じょうず}に{f|話|はな}す is
  fine; {f|上手|じょうず}な{f|人|ひと} is the noun version.

## Practice

**1.** {f|田中|たなか}さんは____{f|人|ひと}です。（しんせつ）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|田中|たなか}さんは<b>{f|親切|しんせつ}な</b>{f|人|ひと}です。

{f|親切|しんせつ} is a な-adjective, and it is standing directly in front of the
noun {f|人|ひと}, which is exactly the environment that requires `な`.

</details>

**2.** この{f|問題|もんだい}は（{f|簡単|かんたん}くない・{f|簡単|かんたん}じゃない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|問題|もんだい}は<b>{f|簡単|かんたん}じゃない</b>。

{f|簡単|かんたん} is a な-adjective, so its negative is made with the copula:
じゃない (or the more formal ではありません). `くない` is reserved for
い-adjectives.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The hotel was clean and quiet, but it wasn't cheap."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ホテルは<b>きれいで</b>{f|静|しず}かでしたが、{f|安|やす}くなかったです。

Two な-adjectives linked by the て-form, which for a な-adjective is simply `で`.
Only the last one carries tense. The final adjective {f|安|やす}い is an
い-adjective, so it makes its own past negative.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|昨日|きのう}のコンサートはとてもすてきかったです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|昨日|きのう}のコンサートはとても<b>すてきでした</b>。

すてき is a な-adjective, and な-adjectives never take `かった`. The past is
carried by the copula: だった in plain style, でした in polite style.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}はギターが{f|上手|じょうず}です。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}はギターを{f|上手|じょうず}に{f|弾|ひ}きます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) uses {f|上手|じょうず} as an adjective describing him, so the guitar is
marked with `が` - it is a statement about what kind of player he is.

(b) uses the adverbial `に` form to describe how he performs the action of
playing, so the guitar returns to being an ordinary object with `を`. (a) is a
judgement about ability; (b) is a description of a performance.

</details>
