# た-form / plain past

**Meaning** · the plain past - "did", "was" - and the base that a whole family of
patterns attaches to

The た-form is the plain-style counterpart of `～ました`. It says an event
happened, or that something is now finished. It is also structural: `～たら`,
`～たり`, `～たことがある`, `～たあとで` and `～たほうがいい` are all built on it,
and none of them will make sense until this form is automatic.

<div class="note">

**Note** - verbs come in three classes: **う-verbs** ({f|五段|ごだん} / godan / Group 1),
**る-verbs** ({f|一段|いちだん} / ichidan / Group 2), and the irregulars する and {f|来|く}る
(Group 3). This page says **う-verb** and **る-verb**.

</div>

## Formation

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - **the た-form is the て-form with た instead of て.** Every
sound change is identical; only the last letter differs, and で becomes だ.
{f|飲|の}んで → {f|飲|の}んだ. {f|書|か}いて → {f|書|か}いた. If you know one,
you already know the other.

</div>

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb, う・つ・る) | → った | {f|買|か}う → {f|買|か}った |
| Verb (う-verb, む・ぶ・ぬ) | → んだ | {f|読|よ}む → {f|読|よ}んだ |
| Verb (う-verb, く) | → いた | {f|書|か}く → {f|書|か}いた |
| Verb (う-verb, ぐ) | → いだ | {f|泳|およ}ぐ → {f|泳|およ}いだ |
| Verb (う-verb, す) | → した | {f|話|はな}す → {f|話|はな}した |
| Verb ({f|行|い}く, exception) | → った | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}った |
| Verb (る-verb) | drop る, + た | {f|寝|ね}る → {f|寝|ね}た |
| Verb (する) | した | {f|運動|うんどう}する → {f|運動|うんどう}した |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | きた | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|き}た |
| Verb (negative) | ない → なかった | {f|行|い}かない → {f|行|い}かなかった |
| い-adjective | い → かった | {f|寒|さむ}い → {f|寒|さむ}かった |
| な-adjective | + だった | {f|元気|げんき} → {f|元気|げんき}だった |
| Noun | + だった | {f|休|やす}み → {f|休|やす}みだった |

The う-verbs again, one ending at a time:

| Dictionary ending | た-form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| う | った | {f|習|なら}う → {f|習|なら}った |
| つ | った | {f|勝|か}つ → {f|勝|か}った |
| る | った | {f|取|と}る → {f|取|と}った |
| む | んだ | {f|住|す}む → {f|住|す}んだ |
| ぶ | んだ | {f|呼|よ}ぶ → {f|呼|よ}んだ |
| ぬ | んだ | {f|死|し}ぬ → {f|死|し}んだ |
| く | いた | {f|着|つ}く → {f|着|つ}いた |
| ぐ | いだ | {f|急|いそ}ぐ → {f|急|いそ}いだ |
| す | した | {f|返|かえ}す → {f|返|かえ}した |

Patterns that need the た-form to exist at all:

- `～たら` - if / when. {f|着|つ}いたら{f|電話|でんわ}して。
- `～たり～たりする` - doing this and that. {f|寝|ね}たり{f|起|お}きたり。
- `～たことがある` - have ever done. {f|登|のぼ}ったことがある。
- `～たあとで` - after. {f|食|た}べたあとで。
- `～たほうがいい` - had better. {f|休|やす}んだほうがいい。

## Nuance & register

The た-form is plain style. Ending a sentence with it to a stranger or a superior
is too familiar - {f|食|た}べた to your boss should be {f|食|た}べました. Inside
a sentence, though, plain is compulsory regardless of politeness: the verb
before とき, ので, から, あとで, and before any noun it modifies, goes into the
た-form even when the sentence ends politely.
{f|買|か}った{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}んでいます is correct;
❌{f|買|か}いました{f|本|ほん} is not.

The た-form does more than past tense. Three uses that surprise learners:

- **Completion, not past time.** バスが{f|来|き}た! - the bus is arriving right
  now; you are reporting that the awaited event has landed. Likewise
  あ、あった "there it is", said while still looking at the thing.
- **A sudden recollection.** {f|明日|あした}、{f|休|やす}みだったね - "oh, tomorrow's
  a day off, isn't it." Nothing is past; the `た` marks the fact newly clicking
  into place.
- **A sharp command in casual speech.** {f|帰|かえ}った、{f|帰|かえ}った! - 
  "go on, off you go!", typical of a shopkeeper closing up. Rough, and worth
  recognising rather than using.

In writing, plain past is the default: novels, newspapers and reports are
written in `た`, not `ました`. So the た-form is not "informal Japanese", it is
*plain-style* Japanese - a different axis from politeness.

In casual speech the た-form very often turns up inside the contraction
`～ちゃった` (from `～てしまった`), and `～じゃった` after ん-forms:
{f|忘|わす}れちゃった "oh no, I forgot", {f|飲|の}んじゃった "I went and drank
it." Learners hear these long before they are taught them.

## Contrast

### ～た vs ～ました

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same tense, different register. `ました` is polite style;
`た` is plain style, and is also the only option inside a sentence.

</div>

<pre>
{f|先週|せんしゅう}、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}を<b>{f|買|か}いました</b>。
I bought a new bicycle last week.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先週|せんしゅう}<b>{f|買|か}った</b>{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}が、もう{f|壊|こわ}れました。
The bicycle I bought last week has already broken.
</pre>

In the second sentence the politeness sits on {f|壊|こわ}れました at the end. The
relative clause modifying {f|自転車|じてんしゃ} has to be plain - this is a hard
grammatical rule, not a stylistic preference.

### ～た vs ～ていた

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～た` reports an event as a single point; `～ていた` looks
inside it and describes it as ongoing, repeated, or a state that held for a
while.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}、{f|本|ほん}を<b>{f|読|よ}んだ</b>。
I read a book yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|夜|よる}はずっと{f|本|ほん}を<b>{f|読|よ}んでいた</b>。
I was reading all last night.
</pre>

The trap is verbs of change. {f|結婚|けっこん}した means the wedding happened;
{f|結婚|けっこん}している means the person is married *now*. {f|知|し}る behaves
the same way: "I know" is {f|知|し}っています, never a plain
{f|知|し}ります, while the negative flips back - 
{f|知|し}りません "I don't know", {f|知|し}りませんでした "I didn't know."

### ～た vs ～たことがある

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～た` reports a **particular occasion**; `～たことがある`
reports that the thing is somewhere in your **experience**, with no occasion
attached.

</div>

<pre>
2019{f|年|ねん}に{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}へ<b>{f|行|い}った</b>。
I went to Hokkaido in 2019.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}へ<b>{f|行|い}ったことがある</b>。
I've been to Hokkaido before.
</pre>

Because `たことがある` is about experience rather than occasion, it clashes with
a specific time word: ❌{f|昨日|きのう}{f|行|い}ったことがあります. It also
implies the experience is unusual enough to be worth mentioning, so it sounds
odd about everyday things - {f|今日|きょう}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べたことが
あります is grammatical and absurd.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|寒|さむ}いでした → ✅ <b>{f|寒|さむ}かったです</b>
  い-adjectives carry their own past tense. The い becomes かった, and `です` is
  added afterwards for politeness without changing anything.

- ❌ {f|行|い}いた → ✅ <b>{f|行|い}った</b>
  {f|行|い}く breaks the く → いた rule. Compare {f|聞|き}いた, {f|着|つ}いた,
  which are regular.

- ❌ {f|遊|あそ}んた → ✅ <b>{f|遊|あそ}んだ</b>
  む・ぶ・ぬ verbs take the voiced んだ. So do ぐ verbs: {f|泳|およ}いだ,
  never ❌{f|泳|およ}いた.

- ❌ {f|食|た}べるた → ✅ <b>{f|食|た}べた</b>
  For a る-verb you replace る with た; you never keep both.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}、{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}ます。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}、{f|映画|えいが}を<b>{f|見|み}ました</b>。
  A past time word does not conjugate the verb for you. Japanese marks tense on
  the verb every time.

## Practice

**1.** {f|先週|せんしゅう}、{f|友達|ともだち}と{f|山|やま}に{f|登|のぼ}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|登|のぼ}<b>った</b>（or {f|登|のぼ}りました）

{f|登|のぼ}る is an う-verb ending in る, so it takes った - the same sound change
as the て-form {f|登|のぼ}って. Note that る-looking う-verbs are common;
{f|登|のぼ}る is one of them.

</details>

**2.** {f|富士山|ふじさん}に（{f|登|のぼ}った・{f|登|のぼ}ったことがある）？ - うん、2{f|回|かい}あるよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|富士山|ふじさん}に<b>{f|登|のぼ}ったことがある</b>？

The reply counts occasions, so the question is about experience in general, not
about one particular climb. Plain {f|登|のぼ}った would be asking whether you
climbed it on some specific occasion both speakers already have in mind.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The film I watched last night wasn't very interesting."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|夜|よる}<b>{f|見|み}た</b>{f|映画|えいが}は、あまり{f|面白|おもしろ}くなかった。

The verb inside the relative clause modifying {f|映画|えいが} must be plain
past: {f|見|み}た, not ❌{f|見|み}ました. The final adjective carries its own
past negative, {f|面白|おもしろ}くなかった.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|冬|ふゆ}はあまり{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}りませんだった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|去年|きょねん}の{f|冬|ふゆ}はあまり{f|雪|ゆき}が<b>{f|降|ふ}りませんでした</b>。

`ません` takes `でした`, not ❌`んだった`. The plain-form equivalent is
{f|降|ふ}らなかった - note that the plain past negative is built on `ない`, which
inflects like an い-adjective.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|財布|さいふ}を{f|落|お}とした。
(b) {f|財布|さいふ}を{f|落|お}としてしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a neutral report: the wallet got dropped, and that is the fact. (b) adds
`～てしまう`, which colours the same event with regret or with the sense that it
is now irreversible - closer to "I've gone and dropped my wallet."

Both are plain past. The tense is identical; what changes is how the speaker
feels about it, which is a job Japanese hands to an auxiliary rather than to
intonation.

</details>
