# まで

**Meaning** · until, up to, as far as - and, in one special use, "even"

`まで` marks the **far end**. Something continues, and `まで` says where it stops: an hour
on the clock, a station, the moment a condition changes. Whatever it attaches to is
*included* - {f|五時|ごじ}まで covers five o'clock itself. Its partner is `から`, which
marks the near end.

## Formation

`まで` attaches directly to a noun or to a plain non-past verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun - place | Place + まで | {f|駅|えき} → {f|駅|えき}まで{f|歩|ある}く |
| Noun - time | Time + まで | {f|五時|ごじ} → {f|五時|ごじ}まで{f|働|はたら}く |
| Verb - plain non-past | dictionary form + まで | {f|終|お}わる → {f|終|お}わるまで{f|待|ま}つ |
| Paired with から | A から B まで | {f|月曜日|げつようび} → {f|月曜日|げつようび}から{f|金曜日|きんようび}まで |
| Deadline | Noun／Verb + までに | {f|金曜日|きんようび} → {f|金曜日|きんようび}までに{f|出|だ}す |
| Noun - "even" | Noun + まで | {f|子|こ}ども → {f|子|こ}どもまで{f|知|し}っている |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the verb before `まで` is always **plain and non-past**, no matter how the
sentence ends. {f|終|お}わるまで{f|待|ま}ちました is correct;
❌<code>{f|終|お}わったまで</code> and ❌<code>{f|終|お}わりますまで</code> are not. The final verb
carries all the tense and all the politeness.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}<b>まで</b>{f|歩|ある}きます。
I'll walk as far as the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|五時|ごじ}<b>まで</b>{f|働|はたら}きます。
I work until five.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}がやむ<b>まで</b>ここで{f|待|ま}ちましょう。
Let's wait here until the rain stops.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|美術館|びじゅつかん}は{f|月曜日|げつようび}<b>まで</b>{f|休|やす}みです。
This museum is closed until Monday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|東京|とうきょう}から{f|大阪|おおさか}<b>まで</b>{f|新幹線|しんかんせん}で{f|行|い}きました。
I took the shinkansen from Tokyo to Osaka.
</pre>

<pre>
そんなことは{f|子|こ}ども<b>まで</b>{f|知|し}っていますよ。
Even children know that.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試合|しあい}が{f|終|お}わる<b>まで</b>だれも{f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}たなかった。
Nobody left their seat until the match was over.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|仕事|しごと}が{f|終|お}わらなくて、{f|夜中|よなか}の{f|二時|にじ}<b>まで</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|残|のこ}っていました。
I couldn't get finished yesterday and stayed at the office until two in the morning.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`まで` is register-neutral. It is identical in plain and polite speech and in writing,
and it is never dropped.

Three things about how it feels in use:

- **It is inclusive.** {f|三日|みっか}まで means the third is still covered. If you need
  "before, not including", Japanese uses {f|前|まえ}に rather than `まで`.
- **The "even" use is emphatic and slightly loaded.** {f|先生|せんせい}まで{f|笑|わら}った - 
  "even the teacher laughed" - presents the teacher as the least likely member of the
  set. It always carries a note of surprise, and it can sound accusatory if the sentence
  is a complaint: {f|君|きみ}まで{f|反対|はんたい}するのか。 Use it deliberately.
- **までも and までは.** {f|明日|あした}までには{f|終|お}わります adds "by tomorrow at the
  latest"; そこまでしなくてもいい means "you don't have to go that far". These build on the
  same core idea of a limit and are worth recognising early.

`まで` also survives on its own as a sentence-final marker in signs and notices:
{f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|五時|ごじ}まで。 Nothing is missing - the reader supplies です.

## Contrast

### まで vs までに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `まで` = "**until**", for something that continues. `までに` = "**by**",
for something that happens once, at any point before the deadline.

</div>

This is the highest-value distinction on the page, and it is tested at every level.

<pre>
{f|五時|ごじ}<b>まで</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}にいます。
I'll be at the office until five.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|五時|ごじ}<b>までに</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|戻|もど}ります。
I'll be back at the office by five.
</pre>

The first stretches across the whole period; the second is a single event that lands
somewhere inside it. So the test is the verb. **Durative** verbs - いる, {f|待|ま}つ,
{f|働|はたら}く, {f|続|つづ}ける, {f|寝|ね}ている - take `まで`. **Punctual** verbs - 
{f|着|つ}く, {f|出|だ}す, {f|帰|かえ}る, {f|終|お}わらせる - take `までに`.

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}の{f|朝|あさ}<b>までに</b>この{f|書類|しょるい}を{f|出|だ}してください。
Please hand this document in by tomorrow morning.
</pre>

Say まで there and you are asking someone to keep submitting the document all night.

### まで vs から

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `から` opens the span, `まで` closes it. Either can stand alone; when
both appear, `から` always comes first.

</div>

<pre>
{f|夏休|なつやす}みは{f|七月|しちがつ}から{f|八月|はちがつ}<b>まで</b>です。
The summer holiday runs from July to August.
</pre>

Alone, each simply leaves the other end open. {f|来週|らいしゅう}から{f|忙|いそが}しいです
says nothing about when the busy period ends; {f|水曜日|すいようび}まで{f|忙|いそが}しいです
says nothing about when it started. The pairing is so strong that a `から` early in a
sentence sets up an expectation of `まで` - leaving it unfilled is a stylistic choice, not
an error.

### まで vs に and へ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `に` and `へ` give the goal of a movement. `まで` measures the stretch
covered and says where it ended.

</div>

<pre>
{f|空港|くうこう}<b>まで</b>{f|送|おく}ります。
I'll take you as far as the airport.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|空港|くうこう}<b>に</b>{f|着|つ}きました。
I've arrived at the airport.
</pre>

The first is about the journey - the whole distance is covered and the airport is where
your involvement stops; it quietly implies you are not going further. The second is about
the endpoint alone. This is why {f|送|おく}る, {f|歩|ある}く and {f|走|はし}る take `まで` so
naturally, and why {f|着|つ}く cannot: arriving has no stretch to measure.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|金曜日|きんようび}<b>まで</b>レポートを{f|出|だ}してください。 → ✅ {f|金曜日|きんようび}<b>までに</b>レポートを{f|出|だ}してください。
  Handing something in happens once, so it needs the deadline form. The まで version
  asks for continuous submission.

- ❌ {f|六時|ろくじ}<b>までに</b>{f|勉強|べんきょう}しました。 → ✅ {f|六時|ろくじ}<b>まで</b>{f|勉強|べんきょう}しました。
  Studying is durative - it filled the period - so it takes plain `まで`.

- ❌ {f|映画|えいが}が{f|終|お}わり<b>ますまで</b>{f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}たないでください。 → ✅ {f|映画|えいが}が{f|終|お}わる<b>まで</b>{f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}たないでください。
  The verb in front of `まで` is always plain non-past. Politeness lives at the end of
  the sentence.

- ❌ {f|駅|えき}<b>までに</b>{f|送|おく}ります。 → ✅ {f|駅|えき}<b>まで</b>{f|送|おく}ります。
  With a place, `までに` would mean "by the time we reach the station". If you mean "as
  far as", it is plain `まで`.

- ❌ {f|三日|みっか}までに{f|出|だ}してください（meaning "before the 3rd"） → ✅ {f|二日|ふつか}までに{f|出|だ}してください
  `まで` and `までに` **include** the point they name, so the first sentence allows the
  3rd itself. To exclude it, name the day before, or say {f|三日|みっか}の{f|前|まえ}に.

## Practice

**1.** {f|宿題|しゅくだい}は{f|水曜日|すいようび}____{f|出|だ}してください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|宿題|しゅくだい}は{f|水曜日|すいようび}<b>までに</b>{f|出|だ}してください。

Handing in homework is a one-off act, so Wednesday is a deadline, not a stretch. Plain
`まで` would ask the student to keep handing it in continuously until Wednesday.

</details>

**2.** {f|父|ちち}は{f|毎晩|まいばん}{f|遅|おそ}く（まで・までに）{f|仕事|しごと}をしています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|父|ちち}は{f|毎晩|まいばん}{f|遅|おそ}く<b>まで</b>{f|仕事|しごと}をしています。

{f|仕事|しごと}をする here is durative - it fills the evening - so it takes `まで`. `までに`
needs a verb that happens at a single moment.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'll wait here until you get back."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|帰|かえ}ってくる<b>まで</b>ここで{f|待|ま}っています。

{f|待|ま}つ is the classic durative verb, so `まで`. The verb in front of `まで` stays in
the plain non-past - {f|帰|かえ}ってくる, not {f|帰|かえ}ってきた - even though the return is
in the future.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|始|はじ}まりますまで、ロビーでお{f|待|ま}ちください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|始|はじ}まる<b>まで</b>、ロビーでお{f|待|ま}ちください。
"Please wait in the lobby until the meeting begins."

`まで` takes the plain dictionary form. The sentence is still perfectly polite:
お{f|待|ま}ちください at the end carries that, and it is the only place politeness is marked.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|五時|ごじ}<b>までに</b>レポートを{f|書|か}きます。
(b) {f|五時|ごじ}<b>まで</b>レポートを{f|書|か}きます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a promise about a deadline: the report will be finished at some point before five.
Nothing is said about when you actually work on it - you might write it in the last ten
minutes.

(b) is a description of how you will spend the time: you will be writing the report right
up to five o'clock. It says nothing about whether it gets finished. Native speakers hear
these as completely different commitments, which is why the extra `に` matters so much.

</details>
