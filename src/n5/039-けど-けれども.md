# けど / けれども

**Meaning** · but; although - and, very often, just a soft way to start talking

`けど` joins two clauses where the second is not what the first led you to
expect. It is also, in everyday Japanese, the ordinary way to preface a request
or a piece of news without landing on it too hard.

## Formation

`けど` clips onto the **end of a finished clause**, plain or polite. Nouns and
な-adjectives need `だ` (plain) or `です` (polite) first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain) | plain form + けど | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}くけど |
| Verb (polite) | ます-form + けど | {f|行|い}きます → {f|行|い}きますけど |
| Verb (past) | た-form + けど | {f|行|い}った → {f|行|い}ったけど |
| い-adjective | plain form + けど | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}いけど |
| な-adjective | ～**だ** + けど | {f|便利|べんり}だ → {f|便利|べんり}**だ**けど／{f|便利|べんり}**です**けど |
| Noun | Noun + **だ** + けど | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}**だ**けど／{f|学生|がくせい}**です**けど |
| Negative | ない-form + けど | {f|知|し}らないけど |

The four spellings are one word at four levels of formality:

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Casual speech | けど | {f|安|やす}いけど |
| Slightly softer | けども | {f|安|やす}いですけども |
| Neutral / written | けれど | {f|安|やす}いけれど |
| Formal / written | けれども | {f|安|やす}いけれども |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - longer means more formal. `けど` in conversation,
`けれども` in a written report. `が` is one step more formal again.

</div>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

I love the sea in summer, but the crowds are a bit much.
</pre>

I'm very sorry, but I'll have to miss tomorrow's meeting.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`けど` is one of the most-used words in spoken Japanese, and most of the time it
is not really contrastive at all.

**The trailing `けど`.** A sentence that stops on `けど` is a complete, polite
utterance. {f|少|すこ}し{f|伺|うかが}いたいことがあるんですけど…。
Nothing has been left out by accident; the `けど` hands the floor to the listener
instead of stating your business flatly. Requests, complaints, refusals and
first approaches to a stranger nearly all arrive this way, and a learner who
never uses trailing `けど` sounds blunt without knowing why.

**The preface `けど`.** {f|映画|えいが}の{f|話|はなし}なんですけど、{f|今週|こんしゅう}{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}きませんか。 Here `けど` marks the first clause as background - 
"about the film" - with no contrast whatsoever. Translating it as "but" gives
nonsense; it is closer to English "so,".

**Register.** `けど` is casual but not rude, and it is fine after `です`／`ます`:
{f|高|たか}いですけど、いいですか。 is perfectly polite. `けれども` in speech sounds
careful and slightly formal; in writing it is unremarkable. `けども` is a spoken
middle ground you will hear a lot from shop staff.

`けど` is not gender- or age-marked. What *is* marked is stacking: two `けど`
clauses in one sentence sounds rambling, and three sounds like you have lost the
thread.

## Contrast

### けど vs が

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same grammar, different formality. `が` is the written and
formal member of the pair; `けど` is the spoken one.

</div>

They are interchangeable in structure - both attach to a finished clause, both
can be contrastive or merely prefatory, both can trail off at the end.

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}は{f|高|たか}いです<b>が</b>、{f|品質|ひんしつ}はたしかです。
It is expensive, but the quality is dependable. (a written product description)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}は{f|高|たか}い<b>けど</b>、ものはいいよ。
Pricey, but it's good stuff. (the same claim, said to a friend)
</pre>

Two practical notes. `が` after a plain form ({f|高|たか}いが) reads as literary,
not casual - plain form plus `が` belongs to essays, not chat. And in a speech or
a formal email, `が` is the safe default.

### けど vs でも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `けど` goes at the **end** of the first clause. `でも` goes at
the **start** of the second sentence. They are never interchangeable in position.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}い<b>けど</b>、{f|明|あか}るいです。
This room is small, but it's bright. (one sentence)
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}いです。<b>でも</b>、{f|明|あか}るいです。
This room is small. But it's bright. (two sentences)
</pre>

This is the mistake English speakers make most, because English "but" sits
between the two halves and so *looks* like `でも`. It is not: ❌{f|狭|せま}いでも
is ungrammatical. If the word comes after the first clause, it must be `けど`.

### けど vs のに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `のに` is `けど` plus a grievance. Use `のに` only when the
outcome genuinely disappoints or surprises the speaker.

</div>

<pre>
{f|三時間|さんじかん}{f|待|ま}った<b>けど</b>、{f|来|こ}なかった。
I waited three hours, but he didn't come.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三時間|さんじかん}も{f|待|ま}った<b>のに</b>、{f|来|こ}なかった。
I waited three whole hours and he still didn't come! (and I'm annoyed)
</pre>

Because `のに` carries emotion, it is wrong where you are simply setting two facts
side by side: ❌{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|背|せ}が{f|低|ひく}いのに、{f|兄|あに}は{f|高|たか}いです。
Nobody's expectation has been thwarted by how tall someone's brother is, so this
needs `けど`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}けど、{f|働|はたら}いています。 → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}**だ**けど、{f|働|はたら}いています。
  A noun cannot sit bare in front of `けど`. Insert `だ` (plain) or `です`
  (polite). Same for な-adjectives: ❌{f|静|しず}かけど → ✅{f|静|しず}か**だ**けど.

- ❌ {f|高|たか}いでも、{f|買|か}います。 → ✅ {f|高|たか}い**けど**、{f|買|か}います。
  `でも` cannot follow a clause. It opens a new sentence:
  {f|高|たか}いです。**でも**、{f|買|か}います。

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っているけど、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っているけど、ぬれました。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っていて{f|傘|かさ}も{f|持|も}っていたけど、ぬれました。
  One `けど` per sentence. Chaining them reads as unfinished thinking.

- ❌ {f|安|やす}いけれども。 as a full written sentence → ✅ {f|安|やす}いけれども、{f|買|か}いませんでした。
  Trailing off is a *spoken* move. In writing, finish the sentence - a dangling
  `けれども` on the page just looks truncated.

- ❌ けど、{f|明日|あした}は{f|行|い}けません。 (at the start of a sentence) → ✅ でも、{f|明日|あした}は{f|行|い}けません。
  `けど` is a clause-final connective. To begin a sentence with "but", use `でも`,
  `しかし`, or `ですが`.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|便利|べんり}____、{f|重|おも}いです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|便利|べんり}**だけど**（polite: **ですけど**）、{f|重|おも}いです。

<code>{f|便利|べんり}</code> is a な-adjective, so it cannot touch `けど` directly - you need
`だ` or `です` between them. ❌<code>{f|便利|べんり}けど</code> and ❌<code>{f|便利|べんり}なけど</code> are both wrong.

</details>

**2.** {f|時間|じかん}がなかったです。（けど・でも）、{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|終|お}わりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|時間|じかん}がなかったです。**でも**、{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|終|お}わりました。

The first clause has already been closed with `です` and a full stop, so the
connector is starting a new sentence - that is `でも`'s job. `けど` would have had
to attach inside the first sentence: {f|時間|じかん}がなかったけど、{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|終|お}わりました。

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I've read the book, but I don't really understand it."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

その{f|本|ほん}は{f|読|よ}みました**けど**、あまりわかりません。

Polite `ました` plus `けど` is a completely normal combination - `けど` does not
drag the register down. In casual speech: {f|読|よ}んだ**けど**、あまりわからない。

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: すみません、{f|道|みち}を{f|聞|き}きたいでも、いいですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ すみません、{f|道|みち}を{f|聞|き}きたいん**ですけど**、いいですか。

`でも` cannot follow a clause, and here the speaker is not contrasting anything
anyway - this is the prefatory `けど` that softens an approach to a stranger. The
`んですけど` shape is the standard opening for asking a favour.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}したけど、{f|試合|しあい}に{f|負|ま}けました。
(b) {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}したのに、{f|試合|しあい}に{f|負|ま}けました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) states two facts and marks the second as unexpected. It is neutral - a
reasonable thing to write in a diary or say to a coach without making a scene.

(b) adds the speaker's feeling: after all that practice, losing is unfair or
maddening. `のに` is never emotionally blank. Use it when you want the listener to
hear the disappointment, and `けど` when you just want the facts joined.

</details>
