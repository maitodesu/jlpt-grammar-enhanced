# ～にいく

**Meaning** · to go in order to ～ - movement with a purpose attached

This is how Japanese says "go and do something". The verb naming the purpose is
cut back to its ます-stem, `に` marks it as the goal of the trip, and a verb of
movement finishes the sentence. The same `に` that points at a destination is
here pointing at an errand.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + に + {f|行|い}く | {f|買|か}う → {f|買|か}い → {f|買|か}いに{f|行|い}く |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + に + {f|行|い}く | {f|見|み}る → {f|見|み} → {f|見|み}に{f|行|い}く |
| する-verb (as noun) | Noun + に + {f|行|い}く | {f|買|か}い{f|物|もの} → {f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}く |
| する-verb (as verb) | し + に + {f|行|い}く | {f|勉強|べんきょう}する → {f|勉強|べんきょう}しに{f|行|い}く |
| Other movement verbs | stem + に + {f|来|く}る／{f|帰|かえ}る／{f|出|で}かける／{f|戻|もど}る | {f|習|なら}いに{f|来|く}る |
| With a destination | Place へ／に … stem + に{f|行|い}く | {f|駅|えき}へ{f|迎|むか}えに{f|行|い}く |
| Polite | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}きます | {f|買|か}いに{f|行|い}きます |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the purpose verb takes the shape it has in front of `ます`.
{f|買|か}います → {f|買|か}い. Never the dictionary form:
❌{f|買|か}うに{f|行|い}く.

</div>

The object of the purpose verb keeps its own `を`, and the destination keeps its
own `へ` or `に`, so a full sentence can carry three particles at once:
{f|本屋|ほんや}へ{f|雑誌|ざっし}を{f|買|か}いに{f|行|い}きます。

## Examples

<pre>
{f|映画|えいが}を<b>{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}きます</b>。
I'm going to see a film.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|友|とも}だちに<b>{f|会|あ}いに{f|行|い}きました</b>。
I went to see a friend.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}まで{f|母|はは}を<b>{f|迎|むか}えに{f|行|い}きます</b>。
I'm going to the station to pick up my mother.
</pre>

<pre>
コンビニへパンを<b>{f|買|か}いに{f|行|い}って</b>きます。
I'm just popping to the convenience store for some bread.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを<b>{f|食|た}べに{f|出|で}かけました</b>。
He went out to get lunch.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日曜日|にちようび}に{f|公園|こうえん}へ{f|写真|しゃしん}を<b>{f|撮|と}りに{f|行|い}きませんか</b>。
Shall we go to the park on Sunday to take some photos?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}、いとこが{f|日本語|にほんご}を<b>{f|習|なら}いに{f|来|き}ます</b>。
My cousin is coming to learn Japanese next week.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|夏休|なつやす}みには{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}へ{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}に<b>{f|乗|の}りに{f|行|い}きたい</b>です。
Over the summer holidays I want to go to Hokkaido to ride my bike.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral. Everything about politeness is decided by the final movement
verb: {f|行|い}きます, {f|行|い}った, {f|行|い}きましょう.

The pattern only works with **verbs of movement**. {f|行|い}く, {f|来|く}る,
{f|帰|かえ}る, {f|出|で}かける, {f|戻|もど}る, {f|入|はい}る are the usual set. You
cannot append it to a verb like {f|待|ま}つ or {f|作|つく}る; for purposes attached
to a non-movement verb Japanese uses <code>ために</code> instead.

Several very common expressions are the noun version of the pattern and are best
learnt whole: {f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}く (go shopping),
{f|散歩|さんぽ}に{f|行|い}く (go for a walk), {f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}く (go
travelling), {f|遊|あそ}びに{f|行|い}く (go round to see someone, go out and
enjoy yourself). That last one has no tidy English equivalent and is used
constantly - {f|今度|こんど}{f|遊|あそ}びに{f|来|き}てください is the standard "come
and visit sometime".

Adding `てくる` - {f|買|か}いに{f|行|い}ってきます - signals that you will be back,
and it is what you say on your way out of the house.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|行|い}く vs ～ために{f|行|い}く

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|行|い}く</code> is the everyday form for going to do
something. <code>ために</code> is heavier and states a purpose formally.

</div>

<pre>
{f|銀行|ぎんこう}へお{f|金|かね}を<b>{f|下|お}ろしに{f|行|い}きます</b>。
I'm going to the bank to take out some money.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|留学|りゅうがく}のために{f|日本|にほん}へ{f|行|い}きます。
I'm going to Japan in order to study abroad.
</pre>

<code>ために</code> takes a <b>dictionary-form verb or a noun + の</b>, not a stem, and it
suits large purposes - study, work, treatment. Using it for a trip to the shop
（{f|買|か}うために{f|行|い}きます）is not wrong but sounds oddly weighty, like
saying "I am proceeding to the shop for the purpose of purchasing bread."

### ～に{f|行|い}く vs ～て{f|行|い}く

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - stem + <code>に{f|行|い}く</code> means "go *in order to* do it".
て-form + <code>{f|行|い}く</code> means "do it *and then* go".

</div>

<pre>
お{f|弁当|べんとう}を<b>{f|買|か}いに{f|行|い}きます</b>。
I'm going out to buy a packed lunch.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|弁当|べんとう}を{f|買|か}って{f|行|い}きます。
I'll buy a packed lunch and take it with me.
</pre>

In the first, the buying happens at the destination - that is the whole reason for
going. In the second the buying happens first, on the way. One て-form instead of
a stem reverses the order of events.

### Noun + に{f|行|い}く vs stem + に{f|行|い}く

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a する-noun goes in directly. Only a real verb gets cut back
to its stem.

</div>

<pre>
{f|土曜日|どようび}に<b>{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}きます</b>。
I'm going shopping on Saturday.
</pre>

<pre>
デパートへくつを<b>{f|買|か}いに{f|行|い}きます</b>。
I'm going to the department store to buy some shoes.
</pre>

Both are built on {f|買|か}う, but the first uses the noun
{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの} and names a general activity, while the second uses the
verb stem and names a specific thing you intend to buy. For する-verbs both routes
exist - {f|勉強|べんきょう}に{f|行|い}く and
{f|勉強|べんきょう}しに{f|行|い}く are both said, the second slightly more
concrete.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ パンを<b>{f|買|か}うに</b>{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ パンを<b>{f|買|か}いに</b>{f|行|い}きます。
  The dictionary form cannot carry this `に`. Cut the verb back to the shape it
  has before `ます`. This is by far the most common error with the pattern, because
  English "go to buy" tempts you toward the infinitive.

- ❌ {f|図書館|としょかん}へ{f|勉強|べんきょう}<b>するに</b>{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ {f|図書館|としょかん}へ{f|勉強|べんきょう}<b>しに</b>{f|行|い}きます。
  Same rule for する-verbs: する → し. Or drop to the bare noun,
  {f|勉強|べんきょう}に{f|行|い}きます.

- ❌ {f|映画|えいが}<b>に</b>{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ {f|映画|えいが}<b>を</b>{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}きます。
  The object of the purpose verb keeps `を`. Only the purpose itself takes `に`,
  and one sentence does not need two.

- ❌ プールへ<b>{f|泳|およ}ぎ</b>{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ プールへ<b>{f|泳|およ}ぎに</b>{f|行|い}きます。
  The `に` is not optional. Without it the two verbs simply sit next to each other
  with nothing linking them.

- ❌ {f|友|とも}だちを{f|待|ま}ちに{f|駅|えき}に<b>{f|立|た}っています</b>。 → ✅ {f|友|とも}だちを{f|待|ま}つために{f|駅|えき}に{f|立|た}っています。
  <code>に + verb</code> for purpose only works when the second verb is one of
  movement. {f|立|た}つ is not, so the purpose must be expressed with
  <code>ために</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|郵便局|ゆうびんきょく}へ{f|切手|きって}を{f|買|か}____{f|行|い}きます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|郵便局|ゆうびんきょく}へ{f|切手|きって}を{f|買|か}<b>いに</b>{f|行|い}きます。

<code>{f|買|か}う</code> becomes {f|買|か}い before `に` - the same form as in
{f|買|か}います. Note that {f|切手|きって} keeps its `を` and the post office keeps
its `へ`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: おかしを（{f|買|か}いに・{f|買|か}って）{f|行|い}きます。 You mean you'll buy them here first and bring them along.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

おかしを<b>{f|買|か}って</b>{f|行|い}きます。

The て-form puts the buying **before** the going.
{f|買|か}いに{f|行|い}きます would mean you are setting out in order to buy them,
with the buying still ahead of you.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I went to the library to borrow a book."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|図書館|としょかん}へ{f|本|ほん}を<b>{f|借|か}りに{f|行|い}きました</b>。

<code>{f|借|か}りる</code> is a る-verb, so the stem is {f|借|か}り. The destination
takes `へ` (or `に`), and the thing borrowed keeps `を`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|来週|らいしゅう}、{f|友|とも}だちが{f|遊|あそ}ぶに{f|来|き}ます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|来週|らいしゅう}、{f|友|とも}だちが<b>{f|遊|あそ}びに{f|来|き}ます</b>。

The dictionary form {f|遊|あそ}ぶ has to become the stem {f|遊|あそ}び. Note that
the pattern works just as well with <code>{f|来|く}る</code> as with
<code>{f|行|い}く</code> - the direction changes, the grammar does not.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べに{f|帰|かえ}ります。
(b) {f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べて{f|帰|かえ}ります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says you are going home *in order to* eat lunch - the meal is waiting at home
and is the reason for the trip.

(b) says you will eat lunch first, somewhere else, and go home afterwards. The
choice between a stem and a て-form decides whether the eating happens at the
destination or before you set off.

</details>
