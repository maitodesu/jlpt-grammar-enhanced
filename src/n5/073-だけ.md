# だけ

**Meaning** · only; just - this much and nothing more

`だけ` draws a line around something and says that everything outside the line is
excluded. It is a neutral limiter: it reports the boundary without complaining
about it and without apologising for it.

## Formation

`だけ` is a particle-like suffix. It goes **after** the word it limits, and the
word keeps its own plain form.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + だけ | {f|水|みず} → {f|水|みず}だけ |
| Number / counter | Counter + だけ | {f|三人|さんにん} → {f|三人|さんにん}だけ |
| Verb | plain form + だけ | {f|見|み}る → {f|見|み}るだけ |
| い-adjective | plain form + だけ | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}いだけ |
| な-adjective | stem + な + だけ | {f|静|しず}かだ → {f|静|しず}かなだけ |
| After a particle | に／で／と + だけ | {f|友|とも}だちにだけ／{f|友|とも}だちだけに |
| Sentence-final | だけ + です／だ | {f|見|み}るだけです |

`が` and `を` are **dropped** after だけ, or moved behind it
（{f|水|みず}だけ{f|飲|の}む／{f|水|みず}だけを{f|飲|の}む）. `に`, `で`, `と`, `へ`
are kept, on either side of だけ.

## Examples

<pre>
コーヒーを{f|一杯|いっぱい}<b>だけ</b>{f|飲|の}みました。
I had just one cup of coffee.
</pre>

<pre>
うちには{f|猫|ねこ}が{f|一匹|いっぴき}<b>だけ</b>います。
We have only one cat.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|土曜日|どようび}<b>だけ</b>アルバイトをしています。
I only work my part-time job on Saturdays.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|少|すこ}し<b>だけ</b>{f|待|ま}ってください。
Please wait just a moment.
</pre>

<pre>
これは{f|見|み}る<b>だけ</b>で、{f|買|か}いません。
I'm only looking - I'm not going to buy anything.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|名前|なまえ}をここに{f|書|か}く<b>だけ</b>でいいですよ。
All you have to do is write your name here.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|店|みせ}は{f|高|たか}い<b>だけ</b>で、あまりおいしくありませんでした。
That place was just expensive - it wasn't even very good.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}に{f|残|のこ}っていたのは{f|私|わたし}<b>だけ</b>でしたから、{f|電気|でんき}を{f|消|け}して{f|帰|かえ}りました。
I was the only one left in the room, so I turned off the lights and went home.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral. `だけです` in polite speech, `だけだ` or a bare `だけ` in plain
speech, and nothing about it is marked for age or gender.

The important feeling is that `だけ` is **factual, not emotional**. It states the
limit and stops. If you want the limit to sound *insufficient* - "only three
people came, unfortunately" - Japanese reaches for <code>しか～ない</code> instead, which
is the same fact wrapped in disappointment.

Two very common frames are worth memorising whole:

- <code>～だけで</code> - "just with / merely by". {f|名前|なまえ}を{f|書|か}くだけでいいです
  means the writing is all that is required.
- <code>これだけ</code>／<code>それだけ</code> - depending on the sentence these mean either
  "only this" or, with rising emphasis, "*this* much". これだけ{f|勉強|べんきょう}したのに is
  "after studying *this* much…". Context decides.

In speech `だけ` often carries a small dismissive shrug when it follows an
adjective: {f|高|たか}いだけ, {f|大|おお}きいだけ - "expensive and that's all it has
going for it".

## Contrast

### だけ vs しか～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same fact, opposite framing. `だけ` is neutral; <code>しか～ない</code>
says the amount is *less than you'd want*, and forces the verb negative.

</div>

<pre>
{f|千円|せんえん}<b>だけ</b>{f|持|も}っています。
I have a thousand yen. (that's the amount, stated flatly)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|千円|せんえん}しか{f|持|も}っていません。
I've only got a thousand yen. (and it isn't going to be enough)
</pre>

Note the verb: `だけ` leaves it positive, `しか` requires the negative. Mixing them
up - ❌{f|千円|せんえん}しか{f|持|も}っています - is the single most common error with
this pair.

### だけ vs ばかり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だけ` counts; `ばかり` complains. `ばかり` means "nothing but",
and usually implies the speaker thinks that is too much of one thing.

</div>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}はゲーム<b>だけ</b>{f|好|す}きです。
Games are the one thing my little brother likes.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}はゲームばかりしています。
My little brother does nothing but play games.
</pre>

`ばかり` (an N4 pattern) does not really restrict the set - other things may
happen - it says one thing dominates, and it usually sounds critical.

### だけ vs のみ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register. `のみ` is the written,
formal twin of `だけ` and sounds stiff in conversation.

</div>

<pre>
このドアは{f|関係者|かんけいしゃ}<b>だけ</b>{f|使|つか}えます。
Only staff can use this door.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|関係者|かんけいしゃ}のみ{f|入場|にゅうじょう}できます。
Staff only. (as printed on a sign)
</pre>

You will meet `のみ` on notices, in contracts and in announcements. Say `だけ` when
you are speaking to a person.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ りんごを<b>だけ</b>{f|買|か}いました。 → ✅ りんご<b>だけ</b>{f|買|か}いました。／りんご<b>だけを</b>{f|買|か}いました。
  `を` cannot sit in front of `だけ`. Either drop it or move it behind. The same
  goes for `が`.

- ❌ {f|水|みず}<b>だけ</b>{f|飲|の}みません。（meaning "I only drink water"）→ ✅ {f|水|みず}<b>だけ</b>{f|飲|の}みます。／{f|水|みず}しか{f|飲|の}みません。
  `だけ` plus a negative negates the limit itself: "it isn't only water that I
  drink." If you want the restriction to stand, keep the verb positive or switch
  to `しか`.

- ❌ {f|静|しず}かだ<b>だけ</b>の{f|町|まち} → ✅ {f|静|しず}か<b>な</b>だけの{f|町|まち}
  な-adjectives take `な` before `だけ`, exactly as they do before a noun.

- ❌ テレビを{f|見|み}ます<b>だけ</b>です。 → ✅ テレビを{f|見|み}る<b>だけ</b>です。
  Politeness is carried by the final `です`. The verb in front of `だけ` stays in
  its plain dictionary form.

## Practice

**1.** ケーキを{f|一|ひと}つ____{f|食|た}べました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ケーキを{f|一|ひと}つ<b>だけ</b>{f|食|た}べました。

`だけ` follows the counter directly, and the `を` on ケーキ is unaffected because
it belongs to a different word. "I ate just one piece of cake."

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|時間|じかん}が{f|十分|じゅっぷん}（だけ・しか）ありません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|時間|じかん}が{f|十分|じゅっぷん}<b>しか</b>ありません。

The verb is already negative (`ありません`), and only `しか` licenses that. With
`だけ` you would have to write {f|十分|じゅっぷん}だけあります - same ten minutes,
but reported as a plain fact rather than as "only ten minutes left".

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I only studied for thirty minutes yesterday."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}<b>だけ</b>{f|勉強|べんきょう}しました。

The limiter goes on the amount of time, not on the verb. If you want it to sound
like an admission - *only* thirty minutes, which isn't enough - 
{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}しか{f|勉強|べんきょう}しませんでした does that job.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|私|わたし}は{f|野菜|やさい}をだけ{f|食|た}べます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|野菜|やさい}<b>だけ</b>{f|食|た}べます。

`を` may not precede `だけ`. The natural fix is to delete it;
{f|野菜|やさい}だけを{f|食|た}べます is also grammatical and puts a little extra
weight on the restriction.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|三人|さんにん}だけ{f|来|き}ました。
(b) {f|三人|さんにん}しか{f|来|き}ませんでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply reports the number: three people, no more. A host counting chairs
would say it. There is no attitude attached.

(b) says three was disappointing. The speaker had expected more, and the negative
verb carries that shortfall. Same three people, but (b) tells you how the speaker
feels about them.

</details>
