# ～より～のほうが～

**Meaning** · ～ is more ～ than ～ - comparing exactly two things

Japanese adjectives have no comparative form: {f|高|たか}い covers both "tall" and
"taller". The comparison is carried entirely by the particles. `より` marks the
loser, <code>の{f|方|ほう}が</code> marks the winner, and the adjective stays exactly as
it is.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (winner) | Noun + の{f|方|ほう}が | {f|猫|ねこ} → {f|猫|ねこ}の{f|方|ほう}が |
| Noun (loser) | Noun + より | {f|犬|いぬ} → {f|犬|いぬ}より |
| Verb | plain form + {f|方|ほう}が（no の） | {f|歩|ある}く → {f|歩|ある}く{f|方|ほう}が |
| い-adjective | plain form + {f|方|ほう}が | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}い{f|方|ほう}が |
| な-adjective | stem + な + {f|方|ほう}が | {f|静|しず}かだ → {f|静|しず}かな{f|方|ほう}が |
| Question | AとBと、どちらが + adjective + か | どちらが{f|安|やす}いですか |
| Answer | Bの{f|方|ほう}が + adjective + です | Bの{f|方|ほう}が{f|安|やす}いです |
| Short form | Aより + adjective | {f|去年|きょねん}より{f|寒|さむ}いです |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the order is free. AよりBの{f|方|ほう}が{f|高|たか}いです and
Bの{f|方|ほう}がAより{f|高|たか}いです say the same thing. What is fixed is which
particle goes on which noun: `より` on the one that loses, <code>の{f|方|ほう}が</code>
on the one that wins. Swap the particles and you reverse the sentence.

</div>

Either half can also stand alone. <code>{f|去年|きょねん}より{f|寒|さむ}いです</code>
("colder than last year") and <code>こちらの{f|方|ほう}が{f|安|やす}いです</code> ("this
one is cheaper") are both complete, ordinary sentences.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|犬|いぬ}<b>より</b>{f|猫|ねこ}<b>の{f|方|ほう}が</b>{f|好|す}きです。
I like cats more than dogs.
</pre>

<pre>
バス<b>より</b>{f|電車|でんしゃ}<b>の{f|方|ほう}が</b>{f|速|はや}いです。
The train is faster than the bus.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}は{f|昨日|きのう}<b>より</b>{f|暖|あたた}かいですね。
It's warmer today than yesterday, isn't it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|東京|とうきょう}と{f|大阪|おおさか}と、どちら<b>の{f|方|ほう}が</b>{f|人|ひと}が{f|多|おお}いですか。
Which has more people, Tokyo or Osaka?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|肉|にく}<b>より</b>{f|魚|さかな}<b>の{f|方|ほう}を</b>よく{f|食|た}べます。
I eat fish more often than meat.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}<b>の{f|方|ほう}が</b>{f|姉|あね}<b>より</b>{f|少|すこ}し{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}いです。
I'm slightly taller than my older sister.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|車|くるま}で{f|行|い}く<b>より</b>、{f|歩|ある}く<b>{f|方|ほう}が</b>{f|早|はや}いですよ。
It's quicker to walk than to go by car.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|冬|ふゆ}の{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}は、{f|思|おも}っていた<b>より</b>ずっと{f|寒|さむ}かったです。
Hokkaido in winter was far colder than I had expected.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral. In casual speech <code>の{f|方|ほう}が</code> is often reduced to
<code>の{f|方|ほう}</code> with the `が` dropped - こっちの{f|方|ほう}{f|安|やす}いよ - and
the whole `より` half is regularly left out when it is obvious what the comparison
is against.

Degree adverbs slot in before the adjective and are very common:
`ずっと` (far, by a long way), `もっと` (more), <code>{f|少|すこ}し</code> and `ちょっと` (a
little). {f|去年|きょねん}よりずっと{f|忙|いそが}しいです.

The question form is <code>AとBと、どちらが…</code>. Note three things about it: `どちら`
not `どれ` (two candidates, not three); `が` not `は`; and the polite
<code>どちら</code> has a casual counterpart `どっち` that you will hear constantly in
conversation - {f|紅茶|こうちゃ}とコーヒー、どっちがいい?

<code>の{f|方|ほう}</code> literally means "side, direction", and that image is worth
keeping: you are pointing at one side of a pair. It is why the pattern feels wrong
with three or more items - there is no "other side" to point away from.

Finally, `より` has a second life in formal writing and announcements where it
means "from": {f|九時|くじ}より{f|開|ひら}きます, "opening from nine". Same
character, unrelated job.

## Contrast

### ～より～の{f|方|ほう}が vs ～の{f|中|なか}で～がいちばん

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - two candidates take `より`; three or more take `いちばん`. They
never appear in the same clause.

</div>

<pre>
コーヒー<b>より</b>{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}<b>の{f|方|ほう}が</b>{f|好|す}きです。
I prefer tea to coffee.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|飲|の}み{f|物|もの}の{f|中|なか}で{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}がいちばん{f|好|す}きです。
Of all drinks, tea is my favourite.
</pre>

The first ranks two named things and says nothing about anything else - the
speaker may dislike both. The second crowns a winner over a whole field.

### comparative `より` vs "from" `より`

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if `より` is followed by an adjective, it is comparing. If it
is followed by a verb of starting, opening or beginning, it means "from" and is a
formal replacement for `から`.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|映画|えいが}は{f|前|まえ}の{f|作品|さくひん}<b>より</b>{f|長|なが}いです。
This film is longer than the previous one.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|十時|じゅうじ}より{f|始|はじ}まります。
Reception opens from ten o'clock.
</pre>

The second belongs to notices and announcements. In speech you would say
{f|十時|じゅうじ}から.

### ～の{f|方|ほう}が vs ～{f|方|ほう}がいい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same word <code>{f|方|ほう}</code>, two different jobs. With an
adjective it compares; with `いい` and a verb it advises.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しいパソコン<b>の{f|方|ほう}が</b>{f|軽|かる}いです。
The new laptop is lighter.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しいパソコンを{f|買|か}った{f|方|ほう}がいいですよ。
You'd better buy a new laptop.
</pre>

The advice pattern normally takes the た-form of the verb and ends in `いい`. If
you find yourself saying <code>{f|方|ほう}がいい</code> when you only meant "more", you
have accidentally told someone what to do.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|猫|ねこ}<b>の{f|方|ほう}が</b>{f|犬|いぬ}<b>が</b>{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ {f|犬|いぬ}<b>より</b>{f|猫|ねこ}<b>の{f|方|ほう}が</b>{f|好|す}きです。
  The losing item needs `より`. Without it there is nothing to compare against and
  the sentence has two competing `が`.

- ❌ {f|歩|ある}く<b>の</b>{f|方|ほう}が{f|早|はや}いです。 → ✅ {f|歩|ある}く{f|方|ほう}が{f|早|はや}いです。
  Nouns take `の` before <code>{f|方|ほう}</code>; verbs and い-adjectives attach
  directly. Only な-adjectives insert anything, and what they insert is `な`.

- ❌ {f|猫|ねこ}{f|方|ほう}が{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ {f|猫|ねこ}<b>の</b>{f|方|ほう}が{f|好|す}きです。
  The mirror-image error. After a noun the `の` is compulsory.

- ❌ コーヒーと{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}と、どちらが<b>いちばん</b>{f|好|す}きですか。 → ✅ コーヒーと{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}と、どちらが{f|好|す}きですか。
  `いちばん` needs three or more candidates. With two, `どちら` alone already asks
  the question.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|昨日|きのう}より<b>もっと{f|暖|あたた}かいの{f|方|ほう}が</b>です。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|昨日|きのう}より{f|暖|あたた}かいです。
  The adjective itself never changes shape, and the two halves of the pattern do
  not both attach to it. Comparison lives in the particles, not in the adjective.

## Practice

**1.** {f|飛行機|ひこうき}は{f|新幹線|しんかんせん}____{f|速|はや}いです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|飛行機|ひこうき}は{f|新幹線|しんかんせん}<b>より</b>{f|速|はや}いです。

`より` attaches to the thing being beaten. The winner here is already the topic,
marked with `は`, so <code>の{f|方|ほう}が</code> is not needed - though
{f|新幹線|しんかんせん}より{f|飛行機|ひこうき}の{f|方|ほう}が{f|速|はや}いです is
equally correct.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|春|はる}と{f|夏|なつ}と、（どちら・どれ）が{f|好|す}きですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|春|はる}と{f|夏|なつ}と、<b>どちら</b>が{f|好|す}きですか。

Two options means `どちら` (casually `どっち`). `どれ` requires a set of at least
three, and choosing it here would leave the listener looking for a third season.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "This room is quieter than that one."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

あの{f|部屋|へや}<b>より</b>この{f|部屋|へや}<b>の{f|方|ほう}が</b>{f|静|しず}かです。

Both halves are nouns, so both take their own particle, and
<code>{f|静|しず}かです</code> stays in its ordinary form - there is no comparative
ending to add. Reversing the two halves is also fine.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|私|わたし}は{f|兄|あに}の{f|方|ほう}が{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}いです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>より</b>{f|兄|あに}の{f|方|ほう}が{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}いです。

As written, `は` and <code>の{f|方|ほう}が</code> are fighting over which noun is the
subject. Marking the speaker with `より` makes it clear who is being compared with
whom - and, incidentally, who is taller.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|辞書|じしょ}の{f|方|ほう}が{f|便利|べんり}です。
(b) この{f|辞書|じしょ}を{f|使|つか}った{f|方|ほう}がいいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a comparison. Some other dictionary is in play, and this one is the more
useful of the two. The speaker is describing, not instructing.

(b) is advice: you should use this dictionary. The た-form plus
<code>{f|方|ほう}がいい</code> is a recommendation, and depending on tone it can sound
quite firm. Same noun <code>{f|方|ほう}</code>, entirely different social act.

</details>
