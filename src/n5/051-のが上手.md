# ～のが上手

**Meaning** · to be good at something - skill you can see in the result

<code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> is a **な-adjective** describing an ability that has visibly been
trained. What someone is good at is marked with `が`, and to say they are good at
an *action* you nominalise the verb with `の` first. The important thing to learn
alongside the grammar is who you may say it about: <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> is a judgement
handed down, so it is used about other people, and almost never about yourself.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (dictionary form) | Dictionary + のが + {f|上手|じょうず} | {f|話|はな}す → {f|話|はな}すのが{f|上手|じょうず} |
| Noun | Noun + が + {f|上手|じょうず} | ピアノ → ピアノが{f|上手|じょうず} |
| Person + thing | Person + は + Thing + が + {f|上手|じょうず} | {f|姉|あね}は{f|絵|え}が{f|上手|じょうず}です |
| Adverbially (modifying a verb) | {f|上手|じょうず}に + Verb | {f|上手|じょうず}に{f|使|つか}う |

The な-adjective forms behave as expected:

| な-adjective form | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Plain | {f|上手|じょうず}だ | {f|歌|うた}うのが{f|上手|じょうず}だ |
| Polite | {f|上手|じょうず}です | {f|歌|うた}うのが{f|上手|じょうず}です |
| Negative | {f|上手|じょうず}じゃない | {f|上手|じょうず}じゃないです |
| Past | {f|上手|じょうず}だった／{f|上手|じょうず}でした | {f|上手|じょうず}でした |
| Before a noun | {f|上手|じょうず}な + Noun | {f|上手|じょうず}な{f|人|ひと} |
| Honorific | お{f|上手|じょうず} | お{f|上手|じょうず}ですね |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> before a **noun** takes `な`; before a **verb** it
takes `に`. <code>{f|上手|じょうず}な{f|字|じ}</code> "neat handwriting" versus
<code>{f|上手|じょうず}に{f|書|か}く</code> "to write neatly".

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|母|はは}は<b>{f|料理|りょうり}を{f|作|つく}るのが{f|上手|じょうず}</b>です。
My mother is good at cooking.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|字|じ}を{f|書|か}くのが{f|上手|じょうず}</b>ですね。
He has lovely handwriting, doesn't he.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|兄|あに}は{f|人|ひと}の{f|名前|なまえ}を<b>{f|覚|おぼ}えるのが{f|上手|じょうず}</b>じゃないです。
My older brother isn't good at remembering people's names.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|人|ひと}の{f|話|はなし}を<b>{f|聞|き}くのが{f|上手|じょうず}</b>です。
Tanaka is a good listener.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}はまだ{f|五|ご}さいですが、{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}に<b>{f|乗|の}るのが{f|上手|じょうず}</b>です。
My son is only five, but he's good at riding a bicycle.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|娘|むすめ}は{f|小|ちい}さいころから{f|絵|え}を<b>{f|上手|じょうず}に</b>かきました。
My daughter drew well from the time she was small.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|会議|かいぎ}で{f|話|はなし}を<b>まとめるのが{f|上手|じょうず}</b>なので、いつも{f|司会|しかい}をお{f|願|ねが}いしています。
Yamada is good at pulling a discussion together, so we always ask her to chair.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|祖母|そぼ}は{f|昔|むかし}から{f|着物|きもの}を<b>{f|着|き}るのが{f|上手|じょうず}</b>で、{f|近所|きんじょ}の{f|人|ひと}にもよく{f|教|おし}えています。
My grandmother has always been good at putting on a kimono, and she often teaches the neighbours too.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> is a **verdict**. You are standing outside someone's performance and
rating it, and that is exactly why the word behaves the way it does socially.

First, you do not normally use it about yourself. ❌{f|私|わたし}は{f|英語|えいご}が{f|上手|じょうず}です
is grammatical and sounds boastful; the natural equivalents are
{f|英語|えいご}が{f|得意|とくい}です, {f|英語|えいご}が{f|少|すこ}し{f|話|はな}せます, or the
modest classic まだまだです.

Second - and this catches learners going the other way - praising a **superior**
with <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> is also awkward, because rating someone above you is not your
place. {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|英語|えいご}がお{f|上手|じょうず}ですね is softened by the
honorific `お`, and is heard, but さすがですね or
{f|勉強|べんきょう}になります is safer. Between equals and downward it is completely
normal.

Third, when someone says
{f|日本語|にほんご}がお{f|上手|じょうず}ですね to you, the expected reply is not ありがとうございます
but a deflection: いいえ、まだまだです or そんなことないです. Accepting the compliment
flatly is not wrong, just noticeably un-Japanese.

<code>お{f|上手|じょうず}ですね</code> said with the wrong timing means "you flatterer" - 
お{f|上手|じょうず}を{f|言|い}って is a fixed phrase for exactly that. Context carries it,
but be aware the word has that second life.

## Contrast

### ～のが{f|上手|じょうず} vs ～のが{f|得意|とくい}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> rates someone **else**; <code>{f|得意|とくい}</code> is how
you talk about **yourself**. <code>{f|得意|とくい}</code> also carries confidence, not just skill.

</div>

<pre>
{f|林|はやし}さんはギターを<b>{f|弾|ひ}くのが{f|上手|じょうず}</b>です。
Hayashi is good at playing the guitar.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}は{f|人|ひと}の{f|前|まえ}で<b>{f|話|はな}すのが{f|得意|とくい}</b>です。
I'm good at speaking in front of people.
</pre>

The difference is where the judgement comes from. <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> is an outside
assessment of quality, so turning it on yourself is self-congratulation.
<code>{f|得意|とくい}</code> describes your own sense of what you are strong at and can be
asserted without immodesty - it is what you write on a job application.

Note that <code>{f|得意|とくい}</code> is not a full escape either: said too readily it still
sounds confident, so many speakers reach for
{f|少|すこ}しできます or a plain potential form instead.

<code>{f|得意|とくい}</code> about a third person is fine too, but it then reports *their*
confidence: {f|弟|おとうと}は{f|数学|すうがく}が{f|得意|とくい}です suggests he would say so
himself, whereas {f|弟|おとうと}は{f|数学|すうがく}が{f|上手|じょうず}です is your opinion of
him - and for school subjects it sounds odd, because <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> wants a
performed skill.

### {f|上手|じょうず} vs できる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> is *how well*; できる is *whether at all*.

</div>

<pre>
{f|妹|いもうと}はスペイン{f|語|ご}が<b>できます</b>。
My little sister can speak Spanish.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|妹|いもうと}はスペイン{f|語|ご}を{f|話|はな}すのが<b>{f|上手|じょうず}</b>です。
My little sister speaks Spanish well.
</pre>

The first says the ability exists; the second grades it. This is why できる and the
potential form are the safe way to describe **your own** abilities - 
{f|少|すこ}し{f|泳|およ}げます reports a fact, while
{f|泳|およ}ぐのが{f|上手|じょうず}です awards yourself a prize.

They also differ in what they accept. できる takes nouns and する-nouns
({f|運転|うんてん}ができる) and, with `ことが`, verbs. <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> takes nouns
and `のが` clauses, and refuses anything that is not a trainable skill:
❌{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}いのが{f|上手|じょうず}です is nonsense.

### {f|上手|じょうず} vs うまい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, lower register. うまい is casual, slightly
male-coded, and ambiguous with "tasty".

</div>

<pre>
あいつ、{f|歌|うた}<b>うまい</b>な。
Wow, he's a good singer.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}はテニスが<b>お{f|上手|じょうず}</b>ですね。
You're good at tennis, aren't you, sensei.
</pre>

うまい is an い-adjective ({f|上手|うま}い in kanji, though usually kana), so it takes
うまくない, うまかった - a different paradigm from <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> entirely. It
belongs to friends, sport and the internet, never to a business email.

Its other meaning is "delicious", which is where the ambiguity bites: この{f|店|みせ}、うまい
in a restaurant is about the food, not the chef's technique. And the negative
{f|下手|へた} has no equivalent slot here - the casual opposite of うまい is
へた or へたくそ, which is blunter still.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|日本語|にほんご}が<b>{f|上手|じょうず}です</b>。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|日本語|にほんご}が<b>{f|得意|とくい}です</b>。／{f|少|すこ}し{f|話|はな}せます。
  Grammatical but boastful. <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> is a verdict on someone else.

- ❌ ギター<b>を</b>{f|上手|じょうず}です。 → ✅ ギター<b>が</b>{f|上手|じょうず}です。
  <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> is an adjective and has no direct object.

- ❌ {f|泳|およ}ぐ<b>が</b>{f|上手|じょうず}です。 → ✅ {f|泳|およ}ぐ<b>のが</b>{f|上手|じょうず}です。
  A verb needs the nominaliser `の` before `が`.

- ❌ {f|上手|じょうず}<b>に</b>{f|人|ひと} → ✅ {f|上手|じょうず}<b>な</b>{f|人|ひと}
  `に` modifies verbs, `な` modifies nouns.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|上手|じょうず}<b>します</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|上手|じょうず}<b>です</b>。／{f|上手|じょうず}に<b>します</b>。
  <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> is not a verb; it needs the copula, or `に` if it is describing how
  something is done.

## Practice

**1.** {f|姉|あね}はケーキを{f|作|つく}る____{f|上手|じょうず}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ケーキを{f|作|つく}る<b>のが</b>{f|上手|じょうず}です。

The verb <code>{f|作|つく}る</code> must be nominalised by `の` before it can take `が` and be
graded by <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code>.

</details>

**2.** {f|面接|めんせつ}で：「{f|私|わたし}はコンピューターが（{f|上手|じょうず}です・{f|得意|とくい}です）。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|私|わたし}はコンピューターが<b>{f|得意|とくい}です</b>。

Talking about your own strengths calls for <code>{f|得意|とくい}</code>. <code>{f|上手|じょうず}</code> is an
evaluation you hand to someone else, so applying it to yourself in an interview
would sound like bragging rather than reporting.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "My grandfather is good at growing vegetables."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|祖父|そふ}は{f|野菜|やさい}を<b>{f|育|そだ}てるのが{f|上手|じょうず}</b>です。

A third person, a trainable skill, and a verb that needs `のが` - the textbook shape
for this pattern. <code>{f|野菜|やさい}が{f|上手|じょうず}です</code> on its own would not work,
because it is the growing, not the vegetables, that is skilled.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼女|かのじょ}はピアノを{f|上手|じょうず}に{f|人|ひと}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}はピアノ<b>が</b>{f|上手|じょうず}<b>な</b>{f|人|ひと}です。

Two errors. The thing you are good at takes `が`, not `を`; and before the noun
<code>{f|人|ひと}</code> the な-adjective must be <code>{f|上手|じょうず}な</code>, since `に` is the form
that attaches to verbs.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|英語|えいご}が<b>{f|上手|じょうず}</b>です。
(b) {f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|英語|えいご}が<b>できます</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b) reports a plain fact: Yamada has English at her disposal. It says nothing about
how good she is, and it is the neutral thing to write in an email about who can
handle a foreign client.

(a) grades her, and grading is a social act. It is warm and complimentary between
equals or from above, but if Yamada outranks you it can read as presumptuous - 
さすがですね avoids the problem.

</details>
