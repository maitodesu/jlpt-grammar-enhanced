# 何も・誰も・どこも

**Meaning** · nothing / no one / nowhere - a question word plus `も`, finished off with a **negative** verb

Japanese builds "nothing" out of two halves. <code>{f|何|なに}も</code> supplies the
"any", and the negative ending on the verb supplies the "not". Neither half works
alone: <code>{f|何|なに}も{f|食|た}べません</code> is "I don't eat anything", while
❌<code>{f|何|なに}も{f|食|た}べます</code> is not a sentence. English puts the negation
in the pronoun; Japanese puts it in the verb, every time.

## Formation

The negative sits on the predicate at the end of the clause, however far away that
is.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| {f|何|なに} | {f|何|なに} + も + **negative** - nothing | {f|何|なに}も{f|見|み}えない |
| {f|誰|だれ} | {f|誰|だれ} + も + **negative** - no one | {f|誰|だれ}も{f|知|し}らない |
| どこ | どこ + も + **negative** - nowhere | どこも{f|開|あ}いていない |
| どれ | どれ + も + **negative** - none of them | どれも{f|気|き}に{f|入|い}らない |
| Particles が・を・は | **dropped** | ❌{f|何|なに}もを → ✅{f|何|なに}も |
| Particles に・で・へ・と | **inserted before the も** | {f|誰|だれ}<b>に</b>も／どこ<b>へ</b>も／{f|誰|だれ}<b>と</b>も |
| Emphatic | {f|何|なに}も → {f|何|なん}にも（なんにも） | {f|何|なん}にも{f|残|のこ}っていない |
| Positive predicate | どこも／どれも + affirmative = **every** | どこも{f|混|こ}んでいる |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the particle goes **inside**, not outside. It is
<code>{f|誰|だれ}にも{f|言|い}わない</code>, not ❌<code>{f|誰|だれ}もに{f|言|い}わない</code>.
Think of it as `question word + particle + も`, with `が`, `を` and `は` simply
deleted at that first slot.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|朝|あさ}から<b>{f|何|なに}も</b>{f|食|た}べていません。
I haven't eaten anything since this morning.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|教室|きょうしつ}には<b>{f|誰|だれ}も</b>いません。
There is no one in the classroom.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}は<b>どこへも</b>{f|行|い}きませんでした。
I didn't go anywhere yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|店|みせ}に{f|入|はい}ったが、<b>{f|何|なに}も</b>{f|買|か}わなかった。
I went into the shop but didn't buy anything.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|話|はなし}は<b>{f|誰|だれ}にも</b>{f|言|い}わないでください。
Please don't tell anyone about this.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三|さん}{f|着|ちゃく}{f|試|ため}してみたが、<b>どれも</b>サイズが{f|合|あ}わなかった。
I tried three of them on, but none of them fitted.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|誰|だれ}も</b>{f|手|て}を{f|上|あ}げなかったので、{f|先生|せんせい}は{f|困|こま}った{f|顔|かお}をしていた。
Nobody put their hand up, so the teacher looked stuck.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|連休|れんきゅう}{f|中|ちゅう}は<b>どこも</b>{f|人|ひと}でいっぱいだから、{f|家|いえ}でゆっくりしようと{f|思|おも}う。
Everywhere is packed over the long weekend, so I think I'll take it easy at home.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}してきたばかりで、この{f|町|まち}には{f|知|し}り{f|合|あ}いが<b>{f|誰|だれ}も</b>いない。
I've only just moved here, so I don't know a soul in this town.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Neutral in register; what varies is force.

**`なんにも` is the emphatic version.** Written
<code>{f|何|なん}にも</code>, it is <code>{f|何|なに}も</code> with feeling - 
<code>{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}に{f|何|なん}にもない</code> means the fridge is *completely*
empty. Spoken, slightly whiny, very common. Its counterpart for people is
<code>{f|誰|だれ}{f|一人|ひとり}…ない</code>, which is the *formal* intensifier:
<code>{f|誰|だれ}{f|一人|ひとり}{f|反対|はんたい}しなかった</code>, "not a single person
objected".

**Not every も-word is negative.** This is the trap. `いつも` means "always" and
takes a positive verb. `どこも` and `どれも` swing both ways: with a negative they
mean "nowhere" and "none", with a positive they mean "everywhere" and "every one of
them" - <code>どれもおいしかった</code>, "they were all delicious". Only
<code>{f|何|なに}も</code> is reliably negative-only.
<code>{f|誰|だれ}も</code> is negative in ordinary sentences, but
<code>{f|誰|だれ}もが</code>, with `が` added, flips to "everyone" and belongs to
formal writing: <code>{f|誰|だれ}もが{f|知|し}っている{f|話|はなし}</code>.

**It is the standard answer to a `か`-question.** 「{f|何|なに}か{f|買|か}った?」
「ううん、{f|何|なに}も」 - the negative verb can even be left off entirely when the
context supplies it, and trailing off after <code>{f|何|なに}も</code> is completely natural in
speech.

**Word order is flexible but the negative is not.** You can move
<code>{f|誰|だれ}にも</code> around the sentence freely; what you cannot do is let the
verb come out positive.

## Contrast

### {f|何|なに}も・{f|誰|だれ}も・どこも vs {f|何|なに}か・{f|誰|だれ}か・どこか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `か` is the positive indefinite, `も` the negative one. `か`
sentences end in a positive verb; `も` sentences end in a negative one.

</div>

They are the same nine question words wearing opposite hats, and a `か`-question is
normally answered with a `も`-sentence.

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}に{f|誰|だれ}かいますか。
Is there anybody in the room?
</pre>

<pre>
いいえ、<b>{f|誰|だれ}も</b>いません。
No, there's no one.
</pre>

The two cannot be crossed. ❌<code>{f|誰|だれ}かいません</code> does not mean "there is
nobody" - it is at best a strange way of denying that someone is present. And
❌<code>{f|誰|だれ}もいます</code> is simply ungrammatical.

### {f|何|なに}も vs {f|何|なん}でも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `も` with a negative = "not any". `でも` with a positive =
"any at all, whichever". One closes the set, the other throws it open.

</div>

English "anything" covers both, so the two get swapped constantly.

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}は<b>{f|何|なに}も</b>しませんでした。
I did nothing yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}なら{f|何|なん}でもできます。
Tomorrow I can do anything you like.
</pre>

The same split runs through the whole set: <code>{f|誰|だれ}も{f|来|こ}ない</code>
("nobody comes") versus <code>{f|誰|だれ}でも{f|来|こ}られる</code> ("anybody can
come"); <code>どこにも{f|売|う}っていない</code> ("it isn't sold anywhere") versus
<code>どこでも{f|売|う}っている</code> ("it's sold everywhere").

### どこも with a negative vs どこも with a positive

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `どこも` and `どれも` take their meaning from the verb.
Negative verb → "nowhere / none". Positive verb → "everywhere / all of them".

</div>

This is the one place where the "も means negative" shortcut misleads you, and the
resulting misreadings are total reversals of meaning.

<pre>
{f|正月|しょうがつ}は<b>どこも</b>{f|開|あ}いていなかった。
Nothing was open over New Year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|正月|しょうがつ}は<b>どこも</b>{f|静|しず}かだった。
Everywhere was quiet over New Year.
</pre>

<code>{f|何|なに}も</code> does not behave this way - it stays negative - and
<code>{f|誰|だれ}も</code> only turns positive in the fixed written pattern
<code>{f|誰|だれ}もが</code>. So the swing is a property of `どこも` and `どれも`
specifically. Read to the end of the sentence before deciding what a も-word means.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は<b>{f|何|なに}も</b>{f|食|た}べました。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は<b>{f|何|なに}も</b>{f|食|た}べませんでした。
  <code>{f|何|なに}も</code> carries no negation of its own. The verb has to supply it.

- ❌ パーティーで<b>{f|誰|だれ}もと</b>{f|話|はな}さなかった。 → ✅ パーティーで<b>{f|誰|だれ}とも</b>{f|話|はな}さなかった。
  The particle goes between the question word and `も`, not after it. Likewise
  <code>{f|誰|だれ}にも</code>, <code>どこへも</code>, <code>どこでも</code>.

- ❌ {f|夏休|なつやす}みは<b>どこも</b>{f|行|い}きませんでした。 → ✅ {f|夏休|なつやす}みは<b>どこへも</b>（or <b>どこにも</b>）{f|行|い}きませんでした。
  With a verb of motion the destination particle is required. Bare `どこも` before
  <code>{f|行|い}く</code> is heard casually but reads as sloppy, and it collides with
  the "everywhere" sense.

- ❌ お{f|皿|さら}に<b>{f|何|なに}もを</b>{f|残|のこ}さないでください。 → ✅ お{f|皿|さら}に<b>{f|何|なに}も</b>{f|残|のこ}さないでください。
  `を`, `が` and `は` are all deleted after a も-word. Only `に`, `で`, `へ` and `と`
  survive, and they go inside.

- ❌ reading <code>{f|何|なに}も</code> as **なんも** → ✅ **なにも**
  `も` is not in the た／だ／な rows, so <code>{f|何|なに}</code> keeps its なに
  reading. `なんも` exists in some regional speech but is not standard.

## Practice

**1.** {f|財布|さいふ}を{f|開|あ}けたが、{f|中|なか}には____{f|入|はい}っていなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|中|なか}には<b>{f|何|なに}も</b>{f|入|はい}っていなかった。
"I opened my wallet, but there was nothing in it."

The verb is already negative, so the slot needs the も-series, not the か-series.
<code>{f|何|なに}か</code> here would produce a strange denial rather than "nothing".

</details>

**2.** すみません、この{f|質問|しつもん}について（{f|誰|だれ}か・{f|誰|だれ}も）{f|知|し}っている{f|人|ひと}はいませんか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|誰|だれ}か</b>{f|知|し}っている{f|人|ひと}はいませんか。

Although the sentence ends in a negative, that negative belongs to
<code>いませんか</code>, which is a polite *invitation* - "isn't there anyone who…?"
You are hoping the answer is yes, so the positive indefinite `か` is correct.
<code>{f|誰|だれ}も</code> would flatly assert that no such person exists.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Don't say anything to anyone."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|誰|だれ}にも{f|何|なに}も</b>{f|言|い}わないでください。

Two も-words can share one negative verb without any conflict - Japanese has no
double-negative rule. `に` sits inside <code>{f|誰|だれ}にも</code> because it marks
the person spoken to, while <code>{f|何|なに}も</code> drops its `を`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|連休|れんきゅう}に{f|温泉|おんせん}へ{f|行|い}ったが、どれもホテルが{f|空|あ}いていました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|連休|れんきゅう}に{f|温泉|おんせん}へ{f|行|い}ったが、<b>どのホテルも</b>{f|空|あ}いていませんでした。
"I went to a hot spring over the long weekend, but none of the hotels had rooms."

Two problems. `どれも` cannot be followed by the noun it refers to - attach `も` to
the noun instead: <code>どのホテルも</code>. And the intended meaning is "none", so
the verb must be negative; as written, the sentence claimed every hotel was free.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|店|みせ}は<b>どこも</b>{f|込|こ}んでいた。
(b) {f|店|みせ}は<b>どこも</b>{f|込|こ}んでいなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) has a positive verb, so `どこも` reads as "every one of them": every shop was
crowded. (b) has a negative verb, so the same `どこも` reads as "not any of them":
no shop was crowded anywhere.

The two sentences are identical up to the ending, which is why you must read a
も-word all the way to the verb before deciding what it means. With
<code>{f|何|なに}も</code> this ambiguity never arises - it is negative-only.

</details>
