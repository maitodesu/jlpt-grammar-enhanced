# ～ませんか

**Meaning** · won't you…? how about it? - a genuine, open invitation

`～ませんか` invites. It is built as a **negative question**, and that is the whole
point: a negative question presupposes nothing, so it leaves the listener free to
decline without embarrassment. Where `～ましょう` announces a plan, `～ませんか` asks
whether you would like one - which is why it is the polite way to ask someone to do
something with you.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + ませんか | {f|行|い}きます → {f|行|い}きませんか |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + ませんか | {f|食|た}べます → {f|食|た}べませんか |
| Verb (する) | し + ませんか | します → しませんか |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | き + ませんか | {f|来|き}ます → {f|来|き}ませんか |
| Casual | ない-form, rising tone | {f|行|い}かない？／{f|食|た}べない？ |
| Politer | ～ませんでしょうか | いらっしゃいませんでしょうか |
| Accepting | ええ、～ましょう | ええ、{f|行|い}きましょう |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ませんか` is just the polite negative `ません` plus the question
particle `か`, so it is built on the ます-stem like everything else in that family:
{f|話|はな}し<b>ます</b> → {f|話|はな}し<b>ません</b> → {f|話|はな}し<b>ませんか</b>.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`ませんか` is the polite invitation, full stop. It works upward, sideways and to
strangers, and it is the form to reach for whenever the answer could genuinely be
no. The politeness comes from the shape of the question: by asking in the negative,
you build the refusal into the sentence and remove any pressure to accept.

Because it is a real question, it comes with a real conversational script.
**Accepting** is almost never a bare はい:

- ええ、{f|行|い}きましょう。 - yes, let's.
- いいですね。ぜひ。 - sounds good, I'd love to.

**Declining** almost never uses いいえ either. The standard refusal trails off and
is never completed:

- すみません、{f|明日|あした}はちょっと… - sorry, tomorrow is a bit…
- ありがとうございます。でも、その{f|日|ひ}は{f|用事|ようじ}があって… - thank you, but I have something on that day.

The unfinished ちょっと is doing real work. Saying the refusal out loud would be
blunt; leaving it hanging lets both sides treat it as understood.

Casually, `ませんか` becomes the plain negative with a rising tone: {f|行|い}かない？,
{f|食|た}べない？, {f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}かない？ These are extremely common among
friends and carry exactly the same softness.

One caution: `～ませんか` is also, literally, an ordinary negative question. Out of
context, {f|食|た}べませんか could mean "aren't you eating?" Intonation and situation
sort it out, and the invitation reading dominates when the sentence names a shared
activity or includes {f|一緒|いっしょ}に.

Upward, `ませんか` is already good; いかがですか is the softer noun-based version, and
in formal invitations you will meet ～ませんでしょうか and
ご{f|一緒|いっしょ}しませんか.

## Contrast

### ～ませんか vs ～ましょう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ませんか` **asks**, `ましょう` **assumes**. Invite with the
first; accept with the second.

</div>

<pre>
{f|週末|しゅうまつ}、テニスを<b>しませんか</b>。
Would you like to play tennis at the weekend?
</pre>

<pre>
いいですね、<b>しましょう</b>。
Sounds good - let's.
</pre>

That two-turn exchange is the natural home of both patterns, and it shows why they
are not interchangeable. Opening with `ましょう` treats the other person's weekend as
already spent; replying with `ませんか` would be asking them a question they have
just asked you.

`ましょう` is fine as an opener only when agreement is safe - when you are already
out together, or when you have the standing to direct the group.

### ～ませんか vs ～ましょうか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ませんか` invites the listener **to join you**; `ましょうか`
offers **your own** action, or checks the next step of something already agreed.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}、{f|美術館|びじゅつかん}に<b>{f|行|い}きませんか</b>。
How about going to the art museum tomorrow?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|切符|きっぷ}は{f|私|わたし}が<b>{f|買|か}っておきましょうか</b>。
Shall I get the tickets in advance?
</pre>

The first opens a plan; the second handles a detail inside a plan that already
exists. And for offers of help the division is strict:
{f|手伝|てつだ}いましょうか offers your hands, while
❌{f|手伝|てつだ}いませんか asks the listener to come and help with something - 
possibly the opposite of what you meant.

### ～ませんか vs ～ますか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ますか` **asks a question** about what the listener will do;
`ませんか` **invites**. The negative is what turns information-gathering into an
invitation.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}のパーティーに<b>{f|行|い}きますか</b>。
Are you going to the party tomorrow?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}のパーティーに<b>{f|行|い}きませんか</b>。
Would you like to come to the party tomorrow?
</pre>

The first is neutral enquiry - you want to know her plans, and you may not be going
yourself. The second includes you, and asks her to come along.

Using `ますか` where you meant to invite is a common learner slip. It is not rude, it
simply does not invite: {f|食|た}べますか offers information-gathering where
{f|食|た}べませんか offers company. The affirmative question can also feel a touch
demanding, because it presupposes that going is on the table.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|行|い}く<b>ませんか</b> → ✅ {f|行|い}き<b>ませんか</b>
  Built on the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|行|い}き<b>ませんですか</b> → ✅ {f|行|い}き<b>ませんか</b>
  `ません` is already polite. Do not add another `です`.

- ❌ {f|荷物|にもつ}を<b>{f|持|も}ちませんか</b>。（offering to carry it） → ✅ {f|荷物|にもつ}を<b>{f|持|も}ちましょうか</b>。
  `ませんか` invites the other person to do the action. For your own offer, use
  `ましょうか`.

- ❌ 「コーヒーを{f|飲|の}みませんか。」「はい、{f|飲|の}みません。」 → ✅ 「ええ、いただきます。」／「ありがとうございます。」
  Do not answer the negative literally. Accepting an invitation is ええ plus a
  positive verb.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べに<b>{f|行|い}きましょう</b>。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べに<b>{f|行|い}きませんか</b>。
  Upward, invite rather than announce.

## Practice

**1.** {f|来週|らいしゅう}、{f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|花見|はなみ}に{f|行|い}____か。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|花見|はなみ}に{f|行|い}<b>きません</b>か。

<code>{f|行|い}く</code> → ます-stem <code>{f|行|い}き</code> → ません + か. The negative question
is what makes this an invitation rather than an announcement.

</details>

**2.** {f|重|おも}い{f|箱|はこ}を{f|運|はこ}んでいる{f|同僚|どうりょう}に：「（{f|手伝|てつだ}いませんか・{f|手伝|てつだ}いましょうか）。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|手伝|てつだ}いましょうか</b>。

You are offering your own help, and `ましょうか` is the form for that.
<code>{f|手伝|てつだ}いませんか</code> would be inviting your colleague to come and help
someone else - the wrong direction entirely.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Would you like to go to the cinema with me on Friday?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|金曜日|きんようび}、{f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|映画館|えいがかん}に<b>{f|行|い}きませんか</b>。

An open invitation to someone who must be able to refuse - the core use of the
pattern. Adding よかったら at the front softens it further.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|明日|あした}、{f|山|やま}に{f|登|のぼ}るませんですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|明日|あした}、{f|山|やま}に<b>{f|登|のぼ}りませんか</b>。

Two errors stacked. `ません` attaches to the ます-stem <code>{f|登|のぼ}り</code>, not the
dictionary form; and `ません` is already the polite negative, so the extra `です` has
nothing to do.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|今晩|こんばん}、{f|飲|の}みに<b>{f|行|い}きますか</b>。
(b) {f|今晩|こんばん}、{f|飲|の}みに<b>{f|行|い}きませんか</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) asks about the listener's plans. It is a perfectly normal question - you might
be checking whether she is joining a group that is already going - but it does not
in itself invite her, and it can sound as though you expect an answer either way.

(b) invites. The negative form builds in the possibility of "no", so it puts no
weight on the listener, and it implies that you are going too. For asking a
colleague out for a drink, (b) is the sentence you want.

</details>
