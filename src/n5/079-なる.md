# ～なる

**Meaning** · to become; to get; to turn into - a change that simply happens

`なる` describes a change of state with **nobody responsible for it**. Water gets
cold, a child grows up, a town gets quiet at night. If a person deliberately
brings the change about, Japanese uses `する` instead - that difference runs
through the whole language.

## Formation

The word class decides the connector: い-adjectives take `く`, everything else
takes `に`, and verbs need `ように` first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective | drop い + <b>く</b> + なる | {f|寒|さむ}い → {f|寒|さむ}くなる |
| いい／よい | よ + くなる（irregular） | いい → よくなる |
| ない | な + くなる | ない → なくなる |
| な-adjective | stem + <b>に</b> + なる | {f|静|しず}かだ → {f|静|しず}かになる |
| Noun | Noun + <b>に</b> + なる | {f|先生|せんせい} → {f|先生|せんせい}になる |
| Verb | plain form + <b>ように</b> + なる | {f|使|つか}う → {f|使|つか}うようになる |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form → な<b>く</b>なる | {f|行|い}かない → {f|行|い}かなくなる |
| Polite | なる → なります | {f|寒|さむ}くなります |
| Past | なる → なった／なりました | {f|寒|さむ}くなりました |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `く` for い-adjectives, `に` for nouns and な-adjectives. It is
the same split you already use for adverbs（{f|早|はや}く / {f|静|しず}かに）, and
learning it once serves you for `なる`, `する` and every other pattern that needs
a word turned into a modifier.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|夜|よる}は<b>{f|寒|さむ}くなります</b>。
It gets cold at night.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|四月|しがつ}から<b>{f|大学生|だいがくせい}になります</b>。
I'll be a university student from April.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}が<b>きれいになりました</b>ね。
The room's got tidy, hasn't it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}んだので、{f|気分|きぶん}が<b>よくなりました</b>。
I took some medicine, so I feel better now.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|野菜|やさい}を<b>{f|食|た}べるようになりました</b>。
My son has come to eat vegetables.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}から{f|甘|あま}い{f|物|もの}を<b>{f|食|た}べなくなりました</b>。
I stopped eating sweet things last year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|冬|ふゆ}になると、この{f|道|みち}は{f|人|ひと}が<b>{f|少|すく}なくなります</b>。
When winter comes, this street gets much emptier.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}したので、ピアノが<b>{f|上手|じょうず}になりました</b>。
I practised every day, and I've got good at the piano.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来年|らいねん}、{f|姉|あね}は{f|東京|とうきょう}の{f|病院|びょういん}で<b>{f|看護師|かんごし}になります</b>。
Next year my older sister is going to be a nurse at a hospital in Tokyo.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and one of the most-used verbs in the language.

The core feeling is **spontaneity**. Even when a person clearly did the work - 
{f|上手|じょうず}になりました after months of practice - `なる` presents the result
as something that came about, not as something achieved. Japanese prefers this
framing far more often than English does, which is why
「{f|日本語|にほんご}が{f|上手|じょうず}になりましたね」 is a compliment and not a
comment on your effort.

<code>～ようになる</code> is the verb version, and it always means a **change in what
is habitual or possible** - you did not use to do this, and now you do.
{f|朝|あさ}ごはんを{f|食|た}べるようになりました. The negative counterpart is
<code>～なくなる</code>: {f|朝|あさ}ごはんを{f|食|た}べなくなりました.

Two things that look like `なる` but are set phrases:

- お{f|世話|せわ}になります - "thank you for looking after me", said constantly in
  business and on meeting someone's family. Never analysed as a change of state.
- Shop staff say 「こちらメニューになります」 for "here's the menu". This is
  much-criticised service-industry Japanese; it is very common, and you should not
  imitate it.

`なります` also does duty for scheduling and totals:
{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|三時|さんじ}になりました, and {f|全部|ぜんぶ}で{f|千円|せんえん}になります.

## Contrast

### ～くなる vs ～になる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the connector follows the word class, not the meaning.
い-adjective → `く`. な-adjective or noun → `に`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|外|そと}が<b>{f|暗|くら}くなりました</b>。
It's got dark outside.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|町|まち}は<b>{f|有名|ゆうめい}になりました</b>。
This town has become famous.
</pre>

<code>{f|暗|くら}い</code> ends in い and is an い-adjective, so it takes `く`.
<code>{f|有名|ゆうめい}</code> is a な-adjective and takes `に`. Beware the words that
end in い but are な-adjectives all the same: きれい, {f|嫌|きら}い and
{f|元気|げんき} give きれいになる, {f|嫌|きら}いになる, {f|元気|げんき}になる - 
never ❌きれくなる.

### ～なる vs ～する

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なる` = it changed. `する` = somebody changed it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}が<b>{f|暖|あたた}かくなりました</b>。
The room has got warm.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}を{f|暖|あたた}かくしました。
I warmed the room up.
</pre>

Notice the particle. `なる` takes `が` on the thing that changes - it is
intransitive, and nothing is being acted on. `する` takes `を`, because there is
now an object and an agent. This `が`／`を` pairing is your quickest test for which
verb a sentence needs.

### ～ようになる vs plain ～なる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - nouns and adjectives attach straight to `なる`. Verbs cannot;
they need `ように` in between.

</div>

<pre>
{f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|走|はし}る<b>ようになりました</b>。
I've started running every morning.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|朝|あさ}が{f|早|はや}く<b>なりました</b>。
The mornings have got earlier.
</pre>

The first describes a new habit, the second a change in a quality. ❌
{f|走|はし}るなりました is simply not a possible shape - a plain verb cannot sit
directly in front of `なる`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|寒|さむ}い<b>なります</b>。 → ✅ <b>{f|寒|さむ}くなります</b>。
  い-adjectives must lose their い and take `く`. Leaving the い in place is the
  most common error with this pattern.

- ❌ {f|静|しず}か<b>く</b>なる → ✅ {f|静|しず}か<b>に</b>なる
  な-adjectives take `に`. The `く` belongs to い-adjectives only.

- ❌ {f|医者|いしゃ}<b>を</b>なりたいです。 → ✅ {f|医者|いしゃ}<b>に</b>なりたいです。
  `なる` is intransitive and never takes `を`. The result of the change is marked
  with `に`, whatever that result is.

- ❌ <b>いくなりました</b>。 → ✅ <b>よくなりました</b>。
  `いい` switches to `よ-` in every form it takes - よくない, よかった, よくなる.

- ❌ {f|日本語|にほんご}を{f|話|はな}す<b>なりました</b>。 → ✅ {f|日本語|にほんご}を{f|話|はな}す<b>ようになりました</b>。
  A verb needs `ように` before `なる`. Without it the sentence has two verbs
  colliding.

## Practice

**1.** {f|春|はる}になって、{f|花|はな}が{f|多|おお}____なりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|春|はる}になって、{f|花|はな}が{f|多|おお}<b>く</b>なりました。

<code>{f|多|おお}い</code> is an い-adjective, so it drops い and takes `く`. Note that
{f|春|はる} at the start of the sentence, being a noun, took `に` instead - both
connectors in one sentence.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|髪|かみ}を{f|短|みじか}く（なりました・しました）。 You had it cut.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|髪|かみ}を{f|短|みじか}く<b>しました</b>。

You did it on purpose, and the `を` on {f|髪|かみ} already tells you a transitive
verb is needed. {f|髪|かみ}が{f|短|みじか}くなりました would describe hair that
somehow became short by itself.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "My little brother has become able to swim." (that is, he now swims, which he did not before)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|泳|およ}げる<b>ようになりました</b>。

Verbs take <code>ようになる</code>, and the verb here is the potential form
<code>{f|泳|およ}げる</code> - "can swim". The pattern is the standard way to say that an
ability or a habit is newly acquired.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|店|みせ}は{f|有名|ゆうめい}くなりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|店|みせ}は<b>{f|有名|ゆうめい}になりました</b>。

<code>{f|有名|ゆうめい}</code> is a な-adjective - you can tell because it needs `な` in
front of a noun（{f|有名|ゆうめい}な{f|店|みせ}） - so the connector is `に`. The
final い of <code>{f|有名|ゆうめい}</code> is part of the reading, not an adjective
ending.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|子|こ}どもが{f|静|しず}かになりました。
(b) {f|子|こ}どもを{f|静|しず}かにしました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports what happened: the children went quiet. Maybe they fell asleep, maybe
they got bored. The speaker takes no credit and assigns no cause.

(b) says somebody quietened them - the speaker made it happen, and the `を` marks
the children as the thing acted upon. Japanese chooses between these two verbs
every time a change is described, so the choice is never optional.

</details>
