# まだ - still; not yet

**Meaning** · still (it hasn't changed) - or, with a negative, not yet (it hasn't
happened)

`まだ` marks a situation as **unchanged**. If the sentence is positive, what has
not changed is a state that continues: *still*. If the sentence is negative, what
has not changed is an event everyone is waiting for: *not yet*. One word, one
idea, two English translations.

## Usage

`まだ` is an adverb. It does not conjugate and it attaches to nothing - you simply
put it before the part of the sentence it applies to, usually near the front.

With an **affirmative** predicate it means *still*:
<code>まだ{f|降|ふ}っています</code> (still raining),
<code>まだ{f|子|こ}どもです</code> (still a child),
<code>まだ{f|新|あたら}しい</code> (still new). Verbs, い-adjectives, な-adjectives and nouns
all take it equally.

With a **negative** predicate it means *not yet*:
<code>まだ{f|決|き}まっていません</code> (not decided yet),
<code>まだ{f|来|き}ません</code> (hasn't come yet). The most important pattern of this
kind is <code>まだ～ていません</code>, which has a page of its own.

`まだ` also stands alone as a complete answer. To
<code>もう{f|食|た}べましたか</code> you can reply simply `まだです`, or casually `まだ`.
And doubled, `まだまだ` means "nowhere near yet":
<code>{f|日本語|にほんご}はまだまだです</code>.

One extra use worth knowing: before a quantity or a time, `まだ` means *only* - 
<code>まだ{f|三時|さんじ}ですよ</code> ("it's only three"), <code>まだ{f|半分|はんぶん}{f|残|のこ}っている</code>
("there's still half left"). The nuance is that there is more of it than the
listener assumed.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>まだ</b>{f|時間|じかん}がありますよ。
There's still time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|父|ちち}は<b>まだ</b>{f|寝|ね}ています。
My father is still asleep.
</pre>

<pre>
バスが<b>まだ</b>{f|来|き}ません。
The bus still hasn't come.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅前|えきまえ}のパン{f|屋|や}、<b>まだ</b>やっているかな。もう{f|九時|くじ}だけど。
I wonder if the bakery by the station is still open - it's gone nine, though.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`まだ` is register-neutral. It sits comfortably in a business email and in a text
message to a friend, and it carries no politeness of its own - the ending of the
sentence does that work.

What it does carry is a faint sense of **expectation not yet met**, and that is
where tone creeps in. A bare <code>まだ？</code> to someone who is running late is
impatient in any language. The polite answer `まだです` is neutral, but as a
*question* - <code>まだですか</code> - it presses. Aimed upward, replace it with
something that does not ask the person to account for the delay:
<code>{f|恐|おそ}れ{f|入|い}りますが、いかがでしょうか</code>.

In the other direction, `まだまだ` is a standard humility formula. Told that your
Japanese is good, <code>いえいえ、まだまだです</code> is the expected reply - literally
"not by a long way yet", and understood as modesty rather than self-criticism.

## Contrast

### まだ vs もう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `まだ` says nothing has changed; `もう` says the change has
already happened. They divide the timeline at the moment of change, and they are
each other's natural opposite in both positive and negative sentences.

</div>

<pre>
{f|外|そと}は<b>まだ</b>{f|明|あか}るいですね。
It's still light outside.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|外|そと}はもう{f|暗|くら}いですね。
It's already dark outside.
</pre>

The negative pairing is the one to watch, because the English flips too:

<pre>
{f|兄|あに}は<b>まだ</b>{f|働|はたら}いていません。
My brother isn't working yet. (he will - he's still studying)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|兄|あに}はもう{f|働|はたら}いていません。
My brother isn't working any more. (he has retired or quit)
</pre>

`まだ` + negative looks forward to something expected; `もう` + negative looks back
at something finished. Choosing the wrong one does not produce a slightly odd
sentence - it produces the opposite fact.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>まだ</b>{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べませんでした。 → ✅ <b>まだ</b>{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べていません。
  `ませんでした` closes the door on the event: it did not happen and that is that.
  `まだ` means it is still coming, so it needs the `ていません` form.

- ❌ レポートは<b>まだ</b>{f|終|お}わりました。 → ✅ レポートは<b>もう</b>{f|終|お}わりました。
  `まだ` cannot introduce a completed event. Once something is done, the adverb you
  want is `もう`.

- ❌ もう<b>まだ</b>{f|着|つ}いていません。 → ✅ <b>まだ</b>{f|着|つ}いていません。
  `もう` and `まだ` are opposites and do not stack. Pick one.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、あの{f|件|けん}、<b>まだ</b>ですか。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、あの{f|件|けん}はいかがでしょうか。
  Grammatical, but it asks a superior why they have not finished. Ask about the
  status instead of the delay.

## Practice

**1.** 「もう{f|帰|かえ}りますか。」「いいえ、____{f|仕事|しごと}が{f|残|のこ}っているので、もう{f|少|すこ}しいます。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

いいえ、<b>まだ</b>{f|仕事|しごと}が{f|残|のこ}っているので…

The work is unfinished and the situation is unchanged, which is exactly `まだ` with
an affirmative verb: *still* there is work left. `もう` would claim the opposite.

</details>

**2.** {f|子|こ}どもたちは（まだ・もう）{f|起|お}きています。{f|寝|ね}かせないと。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|子|こ}どもたちは<b>まだ</b>{f|起|お}きています。

The second sentence - "I need to get them to bed" - tells you the children ought
to be asleep already and are not. That is a continuing state contrary to
expectation, so `まだ`. <code>もう{f|起|お}きています</code> would mean they are already
awake, which fits a morning, not a bedtime.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) まだ{f|三時|さんじ}ですよ。
(b) もう{f|三時|さんじ}ですよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both state the same time and disagree entirely about what it means.

(a) presents three o'clock as early: there is more of the day left than the
listener thought - sit down, finish your coffee. (b) presents it as late: time has
run away and something should have happened by now. `まだ` measures what remains;
`もう` measures what has already gone.

</details>
