# ～のが好き

**Meaning** · like doing something - a standing preference, not a present urge

<code>{f|好|す}き</code> is a **な-adjective** meaning "pleasing, to my taste", and what pleases you
is marked with `が`. English makes "like" a verb with an object; Japanese makes it
an adjective with a subject. To like an *action*, you first turn the verb into a
noun with the nominaliser `の`, then feed it to <code>{f|好|す}き</code>.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (dictionary form) | Dictionary + のが + {f|好|す}き | {f|泳|およ}ぐ → {f|泳|およ}ぐのが{f|好|す}き |
| Verb (alternative nominaliser) | Dictionary + ことが + {f|好|す}き | {f|読|よ}む → {f|読|よ}むことが{f|好|す}き |
| Noun | Noun + が + {f|好|す}き | {f|音楽|おんがく} → {f|音楽|おんがく}が{f|好|す}き |
| Verb (する-noun) | Noun + が + {f|好|す}き | {f|料理|りょうり}する → {f|料理|りょうり}が{f|好|す}き |

<code>{f|好|す}き</code> then behaves like {f|静|しず}か or {f|元気|げんき} - a な-adjective in every respect:

| な-adjective form | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Plain | {f|好|す}きだ | {f|泳|およ}ぐのが{f|好|す}きだ |
| Polite | {f|好|す}きです | {f|泳|およ}ぐのが{f|好|す}きです |
| Negative | {f|好|す}きじゃない | {f|泳|およ}ぐのが{f|好|す}きじゃない |
| Polite negative | {f|好|す}きじゃないです／ではありません | {f|好|す}きじゃありません |
| Past | {f|好|す}きだった／{f|好|す}きでした | {f|好|す}きでした |
| Negative past | {f|好|す}きじゃなかった | {f|好|す}きじゃなかったです |
| Before a noun | {f|好|す}きな + Noun | {f|好|す}きな{f|歌|うた} |
| Intensified | {f|大好|だいす}き | {f|歌|うた}うのが{f|大好|だいす}き |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|好|す}き</code> is an adjective, so there is no ❌<code>{f|好|す}きます</code>,
no ❌<code>{f|好|す}きません</code> and no ❌<code>{f|好|す}きくない</code>. And the verb needs `の`
to become a noun first: ❌<code>{f|読|よ}むが{f|好|す}き</code> → ✅<code>{f|読|よ}むのが{f|好|す}き</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|歌|うた}を<b>{f|歌|うた}うのが{f|好|す}き</b>です。
I like singing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}は<b>{f|走|はし}るのが{f|好|す}き</b>じゃない。
I don't like running.
</pre>

<pre>
どんな{f|音楽|おんがく}を<b>{f|聞|き}くのが{f|好|す}き</b>ですか。
What kind of music do you like listening to?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|父|ちち}は{f|朝|あさ}{f|早|はや}く<b>{f|起|お}きるのが{f|好|す}き</b>です。
My father likes getting up early.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どものころは{f|絵|え}を<b>かくのが{f|大好|だいす}き</b>でした。
As a child I absolutely loved drawing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}の{f|日|ひ}に{f|家|いえ}で{f|映画|えいが}を<b>{f|見|み}るのが{f|好|す}き</b>です。
I like watching films at home on a rainy day.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|妻|つま}は{f|週末|しゅうまつ}に{f|市場|いちば}まで{f|歩|ある}いて{f|野菜|やさい}を<b>{f|選|えら}ぶのが{f|好|す}き</b>です。
My wife likes walking to the market at the weekend and picking out the vegetables.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|地図|ちず}を{f|見|み}ないで{f|知|し}らない{f|町|まち}を<b>{f|歩|ある}き{f|回|まわ}るのが{f|好|す}き</b>なんです。
The thing is, I love wandering around a town I don't know without looking at a map.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral. <code>{f|好|す}きです</code> is the everyday polite form,
<code>{f|好|す}きだ</code> the plain one, and in casual speech the copula usually vanishes
altogether - <code>{f|甘|あま}いものが{f|好|す}き</code>, with a rising tone for a question.
Upward, お{f|好|す}きですか is the polite version: {f|部長|ぶちょう}、コーヒーはお{f|好|す}きですか.

One important difference from `～たい`: <code>{f|好|す}き</code> may be used flatly about a third
person. A preference is a settled, observable trait, not a private impulse, so
{f|姉|あね}は{f|山|やま}に{f|登|のぼ}るのが{f|好|す}きです needs no `がる` and no hedge. This is
one of the few places where describing someone else is *easier* than describing
yourself.

Be careful with people as the object. <code>{f|田中|たなか}さんが{f|好|す}きです</code> is a
confession of romantic feeling, not "I get on with Tanaka". If you mean the latter,
say {f|田中|たなか}さんは、いい{f|人|ひと}ですね or
{f|田中|たなか}さんと{f|話|はな}すのが{f|好|す}きです - attaching the liking to an
activity defuses it.

Finally, negation carries weight. Japanese hedges dislike far more than English
does: あまり{f|好|す}きじゃないです is the polite way to decline food, music or a
suggestion, and it is what a learner should reach for by default.

## Contrast

### ～のが{f|好|す}き vs ～ことが{f|好|す}き

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `の` for concrete, lived, experienced activity; `こと` for the
activity in the abstract. With <code>{f|好|す}き</code> both are usually fine, and `の` is the
more conversational of the two.

</div>

<pre>
{f|海|うみ}で<b>{f|泳|およ}ぐのが{f|好|す}き</b>です。
I like swimming in the sea.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人|ひと}に<b>{f|教|おし}えることが{f|好|す}き</b>です。
I like teaching people.
</pre>

The first is the feel of the water; the second is teaching as a category of thing.
Push the difference and you can hear it, but for a plain statement of taste nobody
will correct you either way.

Where the choice stops being free is when the nominalised clause is not the object
of an adjective but a noun in its own right - a `は…です` sentence, for instance.
There only `こと` works:

<pre>
{f|趣味|しゅみ}は{f|古|ふる}い{f|本|ほん}を<b>{f|集|あつ}めること</b>です。
My hobby is collecting old books.
</pre>

❌<code>{f|集|あつ}めるのです</code> would be read as the explanatory `のです`, which means
something else entirely.

### ～のが{f|好|す}き vs ～たい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|好|す}き</code> is what you like **in general**; `～たい` is what you
want **now**. Liking something does not mean you want it this minute.

</div>

<pre>
コーヒーを<b>{f|飲|の}むのが{f|好|す}き</b>です。
I like drinking coffee. (a habit, a taste)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}、コーヒーが<b>{f|飲|の}みたい</b>です。
I want a coffee right now. (an urge)
</pre>

The two also behave differently across persons. `～たい` cannot be stated flatly
about someone else, but <code>{f|好|す}き</code> can - which is why a description of another
person's tastes almost always comes out in <code>{f|好|す}き</code>:
{f|夫|おっと}は{f|料理|りょうり}するのが{f|好|す}きです.

### {f|好|す}き vs {f|嫌|きら}い

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|嫌|きら}い</code> is much stronger than English "don't like". The
everyday negative is あまり{f|好|す}きじゃない.

</div>

<pre>
{f|運動|うんどう}するのは<b>あまり{f|好|す}きじゃない</b>です。
I'm not that keen on exercising.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|虫|むし}が<b>{f|嫌|きら}い</b>です。
I hate insects.
</pre>

Two traps sit here. First, <code>{f|嫌|きら}い</code> ends in `い` but is a **な-adjective**:
<code>{f|嫌|きら}いな{f|食|た}べ{f|物|もの}</code>, <code>{f|嫌|きら}いじゃない</code>, never
❌<code>{f|嫌|きら}くない</code>. Second, notice the `は` in the first sentence. Swapping the
`が` for `は` when you negate is standard, and it softens further - you are marking
this one thing as the exception rather than making a sweeping declaration.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}む<b>が</b>{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ {f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}む<b>のが</b>{f|好|す}きです。
  A verb cannot be the subject of an adjective on its own. `の` nominalises it.

- ❌ {f|音楽|おんがく}<b>を</b>{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ {f|音楽|おんがく}<b>が</b>{f|好|す}きです。
  Interference from English. <code>{f|好|す}き</code> is not a verb and has no direct object.

- ❌ ピアノを<b>{f|好|す}きます</b>。 → ✅ ピアノが<b>{f|好|す}きです</b>。
  <code>{f|好|す}き</code> takes adjective endings. A verb <code>{f|好|す}く</code> does exist, but in
  everyday Japanese it survives mainly in the passive (<code>{f|人|ひと}に{f|好|す}かれる</code>).

- ❌ {f|泳|およ}ぐのが<b>{f|好|す}きくない</b>です。 → ✅ {f|泳|およ}ぐのが<b>{f|好|す}きじゃない</b>です。
  な-adjective negation, despite the final `き` looking harmless.

- ❌ {f|好|す}き<b>の</b>{f|食|た}べ{f|物|もの} → ✅ {f|好|す}き<b>な</b>{f|食|た}べ{f|物|もの}
  Before a noun, a な-adjective takes `な`, not `の`.

## Practice

**1.** {f|私|わたし}は{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}に{f|乗|の}る____{f|好|す}きです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}に{f|乗|の}る<b>のが</b>{f|好|す}きです。

The verb <code>{f|乗|の}る</code> has to become a noun before it can be the subject of
<code>{f|好|す}き</code>, and that is what `の` does. The particle after it is `が`, never `を`.

</details>

**2.** {f|弟|おとうと}は{f|甘|あま}いものを{f|食|た}べるのが（{f|好|す}きです・{f|食|た}べたいです）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|甘|あま}いものを{f|食|た}べるのが<b>{f|好|す}きです</b>。

Two reasons. The sentence describes a standing taste rather than a present urge,
and it is about a third person, where a flat `～たい` is not allowed anyway. The
second option would also leave the nominalised clause stranded.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "My grandmother likes taking photographs of flowers."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|祖母|そぼ}は{f|花|はな}の{f|写真|しゃしん}を<b>{f|撮|と}るのが{f|好|す}き</b>です。

Note that no hedge is needed even though the subject is a third person: unlike
`～たい`, a preference can be asserted about other people directly.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|兄|あに}はサッカーを{f|好|す}きの{f|人|ひと}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|兄|あに}はサッカーが<b>{f|好|す}きな{f|人|ひと}</b>です。

Two problems in one short sentence. The object of <code>{f|好|す}き</code> takes `が`, not
`を`; and before a noun a な-adjective uses `な`, not `の`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) パクチーは<b>あまり{f|好|す}きじゃない</b>です。
(b) パクチーが<b>{f|嫌|きら}い</b>です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b) is a flat declaration of dislike and sounds surprisingly strong in Japanese - 
closer to "I can't stand coriander" than to "I'm not a fan". (a) is the polite
default: the softener あまり plus a negative, with `は` marking coriander as the one
exception rather than condemning it outright.

If a host offers you something you would rather not eat, (a) is the sentence you
want. (b) risks sounding like a complaint about the food.

</details>
