# まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet

**Meaning** · I haven't done it yet - and it is still on the list

This is the standard answer to <code>もう～ましたか</code>. The two halves each do a
job: `まだ` says the situation is unchanged, and `～ていません` says the action has
not reached completion. Together they describe something that has not happened
**and is still expected to** - which is why "yet" is such a good translation and
"didn't" is such a bad one.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | まだ + て-form + いません | {f|食|た}べる → まだ{f|食|た}べていません |
| Plain style | まだ + て-form + いない | {f|食|た}べる → まだ{f|食|た}べていない |
| Casual contraction | ていない → てない | {f|食|た}べる → まだ{f|食|た}べてない |
| する verbs | まだ + していません | {f|予約|よやく}する → まだ{f|予約|よやく}していません |
| {f|来|く}る | まだ + きていません | {f|来|く}る → まだ{f|来|き}ていません |
| Short answer | まだです | 「もう{f|読|よ}みましたか。」「いいえ、まだです。」 |
| The question it answers | もう～ましたか | もう{f|読|よ}みましたか |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the negative goes on `いる`, not on the main verb. It is
<code>{f|食|た}べて + いません</code>, never ❌<code>{f|食|た}べない + います</code> and never
❌<code>{f|食|た}べませんでした</code>.

</div>

<pre>
いいえ、<b>まだ{f|読|よ}んでいません</b>。

</pre>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

**It promises the action is coming.** <code>まだ{f|払|はら}っていません</code> means the
bill is unpaid *and* that paying is still on the table. That is why it makes such a
good excuse and such a good apology: it admits the gap and implies you will close
it. If the thing genuinely will never happen, this pattern is the wrong choice.

**It is softer than it looks.** Compared with a flat `いいえ`, answering
<code>まだです</code> or <code>まだ{f|決|き}めていません</code> leaves the door open and buys
time. In business Japanese this is a standard hedge:
<code>{f|社内|しゃない}でまだ{f|検討|けんとう}しております</code> - "we are still considering it
internally" - says no without saying no.

**Register is carried by the ending, not by `まだ`.** `まだ～ていません` polite,
`まだ～ていない` plain, `まだ～てない` casual and extremely common in speech.
<code>まだ？</code> on its own is blunt and impatient; <code>まだですか</code> presses; and
neither should be aimed at a superior.

**Watch the stative verbs.** A handful of verbs describe states already and do not
take `ている` in the negative: it is <code>まだ{f|分|わ}かりません</code> ("I don't know
yet"), <code>まだ{f|要|い}りません</code> ("I don't need it yet"), and
<code>まだありません</code>. The most notorious is <code>{f|知|し}る</code> - "I don't know yet" is
<code>まだ{f|知|し}りません</code>, never ❌<code>まだ{f|知|し}っていません</code>.

## Contrast

### まだ～ていません vs ～ませんでした

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～ませんでした` closes the book: it didn't happen, end of
story. `まだ～ていません` leaves it open: it hasn't happened *so far*.

</div>

<pre>
お{f|昼|ひる}は<b>まだ{f|食|た}べていません</b>。
I haven't had lunch yet. (and I intend to)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}はお{f|昼|ひる}を{f|食|た}べませんでした。
I didn't have lunch yesterday. (I skipped it - that day is over)
</pre>

English uses "haven't" and "didn't" for this distinction and Japanese uses the
aspect of the verb, so the mapping is clean once you see it. If you can add "yet"
to the English, you need `まだ～ていません`.

### まだ～ていません vs もう～ました

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - these two are the answer pair to the same question. `もう～ました`
= done. `まだ～ていません` = not done. Nothing else belongs in that slot.

</div>

<pre>
「もう{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}みましたか。」「はい、もう{f|飲|の}みました。」
"Have you taken your medicine?" "Yes, I've taken it."
</pre>

<pre>
「もう{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}みましたか。」「いいえ、<b>まだ{f|飲|の}んでいません</b>。」
"Have you taken your medicine?" "No, not yet."
</pre>

Note the asymmetry that trips everyone up: the affirmative answer uses the plain
past, but the negative answer cannot. ❌<code>いいえ、{f|飲|の}みませんでした</code> would
mean you skipped it altogether - a different, and in a hospital a dangerous,
answer.

### まだ～ていません vs ～たことがありません

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - "not yet" implies you expect to. "Never" makes no such
promise. `まだ～ていません` is about a pending task;
<code>～たことがありません</code> is about an empty life-experience column.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|資料|しりょう}は、<b>まだ{f|作|つく}っていません</b>。
I haven't made the materials for next week's meeting yet.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}へは{f|行|い}ったことがありません。
I've never been to Hokkaido.
</pre>

Saying <code>{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}へはまだ{f|行|い}っていません</code> is not wrong, but it
means something narrower: a trip is planned, or expected of you, and it has not
happened *yet*. Use it when there is a plan; use
<code>～たことがありません</code> when there is only an absence.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ まだレポートを{f|出|だ}し**ませんでした**。 → ✅ まだレポートを{f|出|だ}し**ていません**。
  `まだ` cannot sit with a plain past negative. The two contradict each other: one
  says "still pending", the other says "over and done with".

- ❌ **もう**{f|荷物|にもつ}が{f|届|とど}いていません。 → ✅ **まだ**{f|荷物|にもつ}が{f|届|とど}いていません。
  `もう～ていません` means "not any more". With a parcel that never came, you want
  `まだ`.

- ❌ まだ{f|知|し}っていません。 → ✅ まだ{f|知|し}りません。
  <code>{f|知|し}る</code> has no negative `ている` form. This exception survives inside
  the `まだ` pattern too.

- ❌ まだ{f|食|た}べ**ないです**。 → ✅ まだ{f|食|た}べ**ていません**。
  <code>{f|食|た}べない</code> means "won't eat" - a refusal, not a delay. The negative
  belongs on `いる`.

- ❌ お{f|寿司|すし}はまだ{f|食|た}べていません。（meaning "I've never eaten sushi"）→ ✅ お{f|寿司|すし}は{f|食|た}べたことがありません。
  `まだ` promises that it is going to happen. For a plain absence of experience,
  use <code>～たことがありません</code>.

## Practice

**1.** 「もうチケットを{f|買|か}いましたか。」「いいえ、まだ{f|買|か}____。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

いいえ、まだ<b>{f|買|か}っていません</b>。

The negative attaches to `いる`, giving <code>{f|買|か}って + いません</code>. The answer
also implies you still mean to buy them, which is exactly what the questioner
wants to know.

</details>

**2.** {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|忙|いそが}しくて、{f|晩|ばん}ごはんを（{f|食|た}べませんでした・まだ{f|食|た}べていません）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|忙|いそが}しくて、{f|晩|ばん}ごはんを<b>{f|食|た}べませんでした</b>。

Yesterday is finished, so the meal was skipped rather than postponed.
`まだ～ていません` would claim that yesterday's dinner is still coming, which time
no longer allows.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I haven't decided yet."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>まだ{f|決|き}めていません</b>。
（casual: まだ{f|決|き}めてない。）

<code>{f|決|き}める</code> → <code>{f|決|き}めて</code> → <code>{f|決|き}めていません</code>. This is
one of the most useful sentences in the language, because it declines to answer
without refusing to answer.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the problem: メールをまだ{f|送|おく}りませんでした。すみません、これから{f|送|おく}ります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ メールをまだ{f|送|おく}っていません。すみません、これから{f|送|おく}ります。

The second sentence says you are about to send it, so the first cannot use
`ませんでした` - that would mean the sending is off the table. <code>まだ{f|送|おく}っていません</code>
keeps the action pending, which matches the apology.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) その{f|本|ほん}はまだ{f|読|よ}んでいません。
(b) その{f|本|ほん}は{f|読|よ}んだことがありません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the book is on your list. It is sitting on the shelf, someone recommended
it, perhaps it was assigned - and you fully expect to get to it.

(b) simply reports that reading it has never been part of your life. There is no
implication of a plan, and none of guilt. If your professor asks whether you have
read the set text, (a) is a promise and (b) is a confession.

</details>
