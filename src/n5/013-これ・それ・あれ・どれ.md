# これ・それ・あれ・どれ

**Meaning** · this one / that one / that one over there / which one - the four words that stand in for a *thing* without naming it

These four are **pronouns**. Each one *is* a noun: it takes particles, it sits
where a noun sits, and it can be the entire answer to a question. Which one you
pick depends on whose territory the thing is in - yours (`これ`), your listener's
(`それ`), or neither of yours (`あれ`) - and `どれ` asks the listener to choose one
out of several.

## Formation

Nothing attaches to these words; they *are* the noun. The table shows what each
one picks out and how it slots into a sentence.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing - これ is itself a noun | これ = a thing in the speaker's reach | これ、おいしいですね |
| Nothing | それ = a thing in the listener's reach, or one just mentioned | それを{f|三|みっ}つください |
| Nothing | あれ = a thing away from both of you | あれは{f|何|なん}ですか |
| Nothing | どれ = which one, out of **three or more** | どれが{f|安|やす}いですか |
| Particles | これ／それ／あれ／どれ + は・が・を・に・で・と | それと{f|同|おな}じものをください |
| A following noun | ❌ これ + Noun → use この + Noun | ❌ これかばん → ✅ このかばん |
| Two choices only | どれ → どちら（casual どっち） | コーヒーとお{f|茶|ちゃ}、どちらにしますか |
| Polite equivalent | これ／それ／あれ／どれ → こちら／そちら／あちら／どちら | こちらが{f|新|あたら}しいメニューです |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if a noun follows, you need `この・その・あの・どの`. If nothing
follows, you need `これ・それ・あれ・どれ`. A word in this series is never sitting
directly in front of the thing it points at.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
<b>これ</b>は{f|姉|あね}のかさです。
This is my older sister's umbrella.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>それ</b>、ちょっと{f|見|み}せてください。
Could you let me see that for a second?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>あれ</b>は{f|駅|えき}のビルです。
That building over there is the station.
</pre>

<pre>
メニューの{f|中|なか}で<b>どれ</b>が{f|一番|いちばん}{f|安|やす}いですか。
Which is the cheapest thing on the menu?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>それ</b>はいい{f|考|かんが}えですね。
That's a good idea.
</pre>

<pre>
すみません、<b>これ</b>と<b>それ</b>をください。
Excuse me - I'll take this one and that one.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}{f|京都|きょうと}で{f|買|か}ったカップ、<b>あれ</b>はどこにしまいましたか。
That cup we bought in Kyoto last year - where did you put it away?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}は{f|分|わ}かりましたが、<b>それ</b>が{f|本当|ほんとう}かどうかは{f|別|べつ}の{f|話|はなし}です。
I follow the explanation, but whether it is true is another matter.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The series itself is register-neutral. <code>これは…です</code> is the first full
sentence most learners ever build, and it stays usable for life.

Three things are worth knowing before you aim one of these words at a person.

**Never point at a human with これ／それ／あれ.** They are for objects. For people,
use <code>この{f|人|ひと}</code> at the blunt end and `こちら` when you are being polite - 
<code>こちらは{f|田中|たなか}さんです</code> is the standard way to introduce someone.
`こいつ`／`そいつ`／`あいつ` also exist for people and they are rough: tolerable
between close friends about an absent third party, insulting anywhere else.

**こちら・そちら・あちら・どちら are the polite upgrade**, and they double as the
two-item version of `どれ`. In a shop you will hear `こちら` where a friend would
say `これ` - <code>こちらでよろしいでしょうか</code>, "will this one be all right?"

**`あれ` on its own is also an interjection** meaning "huh?" - the noise you make
when something is not where you left it: <code>あれ、かぎがない</code>. Same word on
the page, completely different job, and context makes it obvious.

In speech the particle is routinely dropped: `これ、いくら?` rather than
<code>これはいくらですか</code>. Keep the particle in writing and in ます-speech.

## Contrast

### これ・それ・あれ・どれ vs この・その・あの・どの

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the れ-series replaces the noun; the の-series introduces it.
`これ` = "this one". `この` = "this ___", and the blank is compulsory.

</div>

This is the commonest error in the whole topic, because English uses one word - 
"this" - for both jobs.

<pre>
<b>これ</b>は{f|重|おも}いです。
This one is heavy.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>この</b>はこは{f|重|おも}いです。
This box is heavy.
</pre>

You cannot mix them: ❌<code>これはこ</code> and ❌<code>このは{f|重|おも}いです</code> are
both broken. And unlike the place words, the れ-series does not take `の` in front
of a noun either - ❌<code>これの{f|本|ほん}</code> is not how you say "this book";
<code>この{f|本|ほん}</code> is.

### それ vs あれ - pointing back at the conversation

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for something *said* rather than seen: `それ` picks up what one
of you has just mentioned; `あれ` picks up something you both already know.

</div>

Once nothing is physically in the room, distance stops being about metres. `それ`
handles information that has only just entered the conversation, from one side or
the other.

<pre>
「{f|来週|らいしゅう}、{f|試験|しけん}があるよ。」「えっ、<b>それ</b>、{f|本当|ほんとう}?」
"There's an exam next week." "Wait - is that true?"
</pre>

`あれ` reaches instead for shared memory: something you have both experienced,
which is why it can turn up with no antecedent in the sentence at all.

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}{f|二人|ふたり}で{f|見|み}た{f|映画|えいが}、<b>あれ</b>はよかったね。
That film the two of us saw last year - that was a good one, wasn't it.
</pre>

Use `あれ` when the listener does *not* share the memory and you will sound as if
you are talking to yourself.

### どれ vs どちら・どっち

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `どれ` needs **three or more** candidates. With exactly two,
Japanese switches to `どちら`, or `どっち` in casual speech.

</div>

English "which" does not care how many there are, so this has to be learned on
purpose.

<pre>
この{f|三|みっ}つのうち、<b>どれ</b>にしますか。
Which of these three will you have?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|赤|あか}と{f|青|あお}、<b>どちら</b>がいいですか。
Red or blue - which would you prefer?
</pre>

`どちら` is also simply the polite form of `どれ`, so a shop assistant may use it
even facing a whole shelf of options. `どっち` is casual and extremely frequent in
speech; keep it out of email.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>これ</b>かばんは{f|私|わたし}のです。 → ✅ <b>この</b>かばんは{f|私|わたし}のです。
  A noun follows, so the の-series is required. `これ` can only stand alone.

- ❌ <b>これの</b>{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}みました。 → ✅ <b>この</b>{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}みました。
  `の` does not rescue it. Unlike <code>ここの{f|店|みせ}</code>, which is perfectly
  normal, <code>これの{f|本|ほん}</code> is not a thing.

- ❌ {f|紅茶|こうちゃ}とコーヒー、<b>どれ</b>がいいですか。 → ✅ {f|紅茶|こうちゃ}とコーヒー、<b>どちら</b>がいいですか。
  Two candidates means `どちら`／`どっち`. `どれ` starts at three.

- ❌ <b>あれ</b>は{f|山田|やまだ}さんです。 → ✅ <b>あちら</b>は{f|山田|やまだ}さんです。
  `あれ` is for objects. Aimed at a person it is genuinely rude; `あちら`
 - or <code>あの{f|人|ひと}</code> among friends - is what you want.

- ❌ 「これは{f|何|なん}ですか。」「はい、<b>これ</b>はペンです。」 → ✅ 「…」「<b>それ</b>はペンです。」
  Once the object is in your *listener's* hands it has moved into their sphere, so
  the answer flips to `それ`. Replying with `これ` claims you are the one holding it.

## Practice

**1.** すみません、____をください。（you are pointing at a cake inside the display case, out of reach of both of you）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

すみません、<b>あれ</b>をください。

The cake is behind glass, away from both you and the shop assistant, so it sits in
neither person's sphere - which is exactly what `あれ` is for. <code>こちら</code>
would be the staff's word, not yours.

</details>

**2.** {f|靴|くつ}が{f|四|よん}{f|足|そく}{f|並|なら}んでいます。（どれ・どちら）が{f|好|す}きですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>どれ</b>が{f|好|す}きですか。

Four pairs means three or more candidates, so `どれ`. `どちら` would tell the
listener you are offering a choice between exactly two - or that you are being
markedly polite.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Is that yours? - No, this one is mine."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>それ</b>はあなたのですか。 - いいえ、<b>これ</b>が{f|私|わたし}のです。

The thing asked about is near the listener, hence `それ`; the speaker then points
at the one in their own reach, hence `これ`. The answer uses `が` because "this
one" is being singled out as the correct one among several.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: これのシャツは{f|小|ちい}さいですから、{f|大|おお}きいのをください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ <b>この</b>シャツは{f|小|ちい}さいですから、{f|大|おお}きいのをください。
"This shirt is too small, so please give me a bigger one."

<code>これの</code> is not a possible modifier. When a noun follows, switch to the
の-series. The `の` later in the sentence is a different `の` - the pronoun "one",
standing in for シャツ.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|昨日|きのう}の{f|話|はなし}、<b>それ</b>はもう{f|終|お}わりました。
(b) {f|昨日|きのう}の{f|話|はなし}、<b>あれ</b>はもう{f|終|お}わりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) treats yesterday's matter as something that has just been raised in this
conversation - typically the listener brought it up and you are answering their
words. It is the neutral, businesslike choice.

(b) treats it as a file you both already have open: shared history you do not need
to re-explain. It sounds warmer and more familiar, and it would be strange if the
listener had no idea what you were referring to.

</details>
