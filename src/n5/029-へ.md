# へ - direction, toward

**Meaning** · points movement in a direction - toward, off in the direction of

`へ` orients. It says which way something is heading, and unlike `に` it says nothing
about arriving. That is why `へ` works for compass directions and for the address on an
envelope, and why it is useless for times, for existence, and for people you talk to.

<div class="note">

**Note - `へ` is read *e*.** When it is a particle, the hiragana へ is pronounced **え**,
not *he*. {f|東京|とうきょう}へ is *Tōkyō e*. It is written へ for historical reasons only,
exactly like the topic particle は being read *wa*.

</div>

## Formation

`へ` is a particle and attaches directly to a noun. The noun does not change.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun - place you head for | Place + へ + motion verb | {f|京都|きょうと} → {f|京都|きょうと}へ{f|行|い}く |
| Noun - compass or relative direction | Direction + へ | {f|右|みぎ} → {f|右|みぎ}へ{f|曲|ま}がる |
| Noun - addressee of a letter or gift | Person + へ | {f|母|はは} → {f|母|はは}へ |
| Noun modifying another noun | Noun + への + Noun | {f|東京|とうきょう} → {f|東京|とうきょう}への{f|電車|でんしゃ} |
| Fixed greeting | ようこそ + Place + へ | {f|日本|にほん} → ようこそ{f|日本|にほん}へ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the fourth row is the one grammatical thing `へ` can do that `に` cannot.
`へ` takes `の` and modifies a following noun; `に` does not.
{f|友達|ともだち}へのプレゼント is natural, ❌<code>{f|友達|ともだち}にのプレゼント</code> is
not Japanese.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}<b>へ</b>{f|行|い}きます。
I'm going to the station.
</pre>

<pre>
どこ<b>へ</b>{f|行|い}きますか。
Where are you going?
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|角|かど}を{f|右|みぎ}<b>へ</b>{f|曲|ま}がってください。
Please turn right at that corner.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|国|くに}<b>へ</b>{f|帰|かえ}ります。
I'm going back to my home country next month.
</pre>

<pre>
ようこそ{f|日本|にほん}<b>へ</b>。
Welcome to Japan.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|祖母|そぼ}<b>へ</b>{f|手紙|てがみ}を{f|書|か}きました。
I wrote a letter to my grandmother.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|東京|とうきょう}<b>への</b>{f|電車|でんしゃ}はもう{f|出|で}てしまいました。

</pre>

<pre>

The typhoon is moving north, so tomorrow's flight may not take off.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`へ` is neutral in politeness - it is not more formal than `に` in the way learners are
sometimes told. What is true is that `へ` is **more common in writing** and in slightly
composed speech, and `に` dominates ordinary conversation. Announcements, timetables,
travel writing and envelopes lean `へ`.

Two places where `へ` is essentially fixed and swapping in `に` would sound wrong:

- **Envelopes and dedications.** {f|田中|たなか}{f|様|さま}へ on the front of a card,
  {f|両親|りょうしん}へ at the top of a letter. The `へ` here is "to, in the direction of
  you" rather than "handed to you".
- **ようこそ～へ.** {f|大阪|おおさか}へようこそ, ようこそわが{f|社|しゃ}へ. A set frame.

In fast casual speech `へ` is dropped just as `に` is - どこ{f|行|い}くの? - so the choice
disappears entirely at the most informal level.

One last register point: because `へ` emphasises the heading rather than the arrival, it
carries a faint flavour of a journey being undertaken. {f|海|うみ}へ is a touch more
evocative than {f|海|うみ}に. Song titles and book titles exploit this constantly. You do
not need to reproduce the effect, but recognise it when you meet it.

## Contrast

### へ vs に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `へ` is the **heading**; `に` is the **landing point**. For plain
{f|行|い}く／{f|来|く}る／{f|帰|かえ}る they overlap almost completely, so pick either.
Outside destinations, only `に` exists.

</div>

<pre>
{f|山|やま}の{f|方|ほう}<b>へ</b>{f|歩|ある}いていきました。
He walked off in the direction of the mountains.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|山|やま}の{f|上|うえ}<b>に</b>{f|着|つ}きました。
We reached the top of the mountain.
</pre>

The first says nothing about whether he got anywhere; `へ` only gives the bearing. The
second is entirely about arrival, and that is a point, so `に`.

Because of this, verbs that focus on the endpoint prefer `に`: {f|着|つ}く,
{f|入|はい}る, {f|乗|の}る, {f|届|とど}く. Verbs of open-ended motion happily take `へ`:
{f|向|む}かう, {f|進|すす}む, {f|飛|と}ぶ, {f|歩|ある}く.

And the hard boundary is worth restating. `へ` cannot mark a **time**
(❌<code>{f|九時|くじ}へ</code>), cannot mark **existence**
(❌<code>{f|部屋|へや}へ{f|犬|いぬ}がいます</code>), and cannot mark the **person you tell or
ask** (❌<code>{f|先生|せんせい}へ{f|質問|しつもん}しました</code>). Those are `に` jobs, all
three.

### へ vs まで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `へ` gives the direction of travel; `まで` gives the far end of the
stretch you cover.

</div>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}<b>へ</b>{f|走|はし}りました。
I ran to the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}<b>まで</b>{f|走|はし}りました。
I ran as far as the station.
</pre>

The first is about where you were headed. The second measures the running: the station
is the point where it stopped, and the sentence implies the whole distance was covered
on foot. That is why `まで` pairs with `から` and `へ` does not:
{f|家|いえ}から{f|駅|えき}まで - a span with two ends.

### へ vs を

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `へ` marks the place you are heading for; `を` marks the space you
move **through**.

</div>

English "go along the river" and "go to the river" both look like one preposition slot,
so learners over-use `へ`. When the noun is the route rather than the goal, Japanese
uses `を` - with {f|通|とお}る, {f|渡|わた}る, {f|歩|ある}く, {f|散歩|さんぽ}する, {f|曲|ま}がる.

<pre>
この{f|橋|はし}<b>を</b>{f|渡|わた}ると、{f|郵便局|ゆうびんきょく}があります。
Cross this bridge and there's a post office.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|橋|はし}<b>へ</b>{f|行|い}きましょう。
Let's go over to that bridge.
</pre>

Note that {f|曲|ま}がる takes both, in different slots: {f|角|かど}<b>を</b>{f|右|みぎ}<b>へ</b>{f|曲|ま}がる - you turn *at* the corner, *toward* the right.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ pronouncing {f|学校|がっこう}へ as "gakkō **he**" → ✅ "gakkō **e**"
  The particle へ is read え. Learners who first meet it in writing often carry the
  wrong pronunciation for years.

- ❌ {f|七時|しちじ}<b>へ</b>{f|駅|えき}へ{f|来|き}てください。 → ✅ {f|七時|しちじ}<b>に</b>{f|駅|えき}へ{f|来|き}てください。
  `へ` cannot mark a time. Only the destination slot is available to it.

- ❌ {f|教室|きょうしつ}<b>へ</b>{f|先生|せんせい}がいます。 → ✅ {f|教室|きょうしつ}<b>に</b>{f|先生|せんせい}がいます。
  Existence with ある／いる is always `に`. `へ` needs movement.

- ❌ {f|東京|とうきょう}<b>にの</b>{f|荷物|にもつ} → ✅ {f|東京|とうきょう}<b>への</b>{f|荷物|にもつ}
  Only `へ` combines with `の` to modify a following noun.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}<b>へ</b>{f|聞|き}きました。 → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}<b>に</b>{f|聞|き}きました。
  A person you question is a recipient, not a direction. `へ` marks a person only when
  the thing sent is physical - a letter, a parcel, a present.

## Practice

**1.** {f|海外|かいがい}____の{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|三階|さんがい}で{f|受|う}け{f|付|つ}けています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|海外|かいがい}<b>へ</b>の{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|三階|さんがい}で{f|受|う}け{f|付|つ}けています。
"Parcels going overseas are handled on the third floor."

Only `へ` can take `の` and modify a noun. ❌<code>{f|海外|かいがい}にの</code> is impossible,
so this blank has exactly one answer.

</details>

**2.** {f|机|つくえ}の{f|上|うえ}（へ・に）{f|花|はな}があります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|机|つくえ}の{f|上|うえ}<b>に</b>{f|花|はな}があります。

The verb is ある - nothing is moving, so there is no direction for `へ` to point in.
Location of existence is `に` without exception.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I want to go to Hokkaido next year."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|来年|らいねん}、{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}<b>へ</b>{f|行|い}きたいです。

A plain destination with {f|行|い}く, so `へ` and `に` are both correct here; `へ` suits the
"setting off somewhere" feel of a trip. {f|来年|らいねん} takes no particle at all.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わってから、{f|部長|ぶちょう}へ{f|報告|ほうこく}します。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わってから、{f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>に</b>{f|報告|ほうこく}します。
"I'll report to the manager after the meeting."

Reporting, telling, asking and teaching all mark the person with `に`. `へ` would only
work if something physical were being sent to him.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|南|みなみ}<b>へ</b>{f|向|む}かっています。
(b) {f|南|みなみ}の{f|島|しま}<b>に</b>{f|着|つ}きました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports a bearing. {f|向|む}かう is open-ended motion, and `へ` gives the direction
without committing to a destination - you are somewhere on the way, pointing south.

(b) reports an arrival. {f|着|つ}く is entirely about the endpoint, which is a point, so
`に` is required; ❌<code>{f|島|しま}へ{f|着|つ}きました</code> sounds wrong to a native ear.

</details>
