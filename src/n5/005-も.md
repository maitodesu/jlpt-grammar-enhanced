# も

**Meaning** · also; too; as well

`も` adds its noun to something already on the table. Whatever was just said about A
is now said about B as well. In negative sentences the same particle gives you "not
… either", and with a number it turns into an expression of surprise: "as many as".

## Formation

`も` sits exactly where `は` would sit, and follows the same replacement rule.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + も | {f|私|わたし} → {f|私|わたし}も |
| A subject marked が | が is **replaced** | {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る → {f|雨|あめ}も{f|降|ふ}る |
| An object marked を | を is **replaced** | ケーキを{f|食|た}べる → ケーキも{f|食|た}べる |
| A topic marked は | は is **replaced** | {f|私|わたし}は → {f|私|わたし}も |
| に・で・と・から・まで | も is **added after** them | {f|京都|きょうと}に → {f|京都|きょうと}にも |
| A も B も | both A and B (or, with a negative, neither) | {f|英語|えいご}も{f|中国語|ちゅうごくご}も |
| Question word + も | + negative = not any | {f|何|なに}も／だれも／どこも |
| Number + counter + も | as many as … | {f|三杯|さんばい}も |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the replacement rule matters more here than anywhere else, because
English lets you tack "too" onto the end of a finished sentence. Japanese does not:
`も` goes on the noun that is being added, and it pushes `が` / `を` / `は` out of
the way.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
わたし<b>も</b>{f|行|い}きます。
I'll go too.
</pre>

<pre>
これ<b>も</b>ください。
I'll take this one as well, please.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|姉|あね}は{f|英語|えいご}<b>も</b>{f|中国語|ちゅうごくご}<b>も</b>{f|話|はな}します。
My older sister speaks both English and Chinese.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|何|なに}<b>も</b>{f|買|か}いませんでした。
I didn't buy anything yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
「わたし、{f|甘|あま}いものが{f|苦手|にがて}なんだ。」「え、わたし<b>も</b>。」
"I'm not big on sweet things." "Oh - me neither."
</pre>

<pre>
きのうは{f|水|みず}を{f|二|に}リットル<b>も</b>{f|飲|の}んだ。
I drank a whole two litres of water yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さん<b>も</b>{f|同|おな}じ{f|電車|でんしゃ}に{f|乗|の}っていて、{f|駅|えき}でびっくりしました。
Tanaka was on the same train as well, and I got a surprise at the station.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|店|みせ}は{f|値段|ねだん}が{f|安|やす}くて、{f|店員|てんいん}さん<b>も</b>{f|親切|しんせつ}なので、よく{f|行|い}きます。
That shop is cheap and the staff are friendly too, so I go there often.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`も` is register-neutral: identical in plain and polite speech, in writing, and in
anyone's mouth. Nothing about it is casual or formal.

What it *is* sensitive to is context. `も` only works if the listener can supply the
"as well as what". 「わたしも」 as a one-word reply - "me too" - is perfectly natural
precisely because the other half was just said out loud. Dropped into a conversation
with nothing to attach to, `も` sounds like the beginning of a sentence you forgot to
finish.

Beyond plain addition, two extensions are worth having now.

With a **number**, `も` stops meaning "also" and starts meaning "as many as" - it
marks the amount as more than expected: 「{f|三時間|さんじかん}も{f|待|ま}った」 = "I
waited a full three hours." With a *small* number plus a negative it flips to "not
even": 「{f|一度|いちど}も{f|行|い}ったことがない」 = "I've never been, not even once."

With a **question word**, `も` plus a negative sweeps the whole category away:
だれも{f|来|こ}ない = "nobody came", {f|何|なに}もない = "there's nothing",
どこにも{f|行|い}かない = "I'm not going anywhere". This construction is fixed - the
negative is not optional, and this is where beginners most often build a sentence
that means the opposite of what they wanted.

`も` also has a softening, generous feel when offering things: 「コーヒーもいかがですか」
sounds like an addition rather than a demand, and shopkeepers use it constantly.

## Contrast

### も vs は

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `は` sets its noun **apart** from the others; `も` puts it
**alongside** them.

</div>

They fight for the same slot in the sentence, so the choice is always meaningful.

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}<b>は</b>ピアノを{f|習|なら}っています。
My little brother takes piano lessons.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}<b>も</b>ピアノを{f|習|なら}っています。
My little brother takes piano lessons too.
</pre>

The second sentence only stands up if somebody else was already named as a piano
student. The first quietly does the reverse: it hints that the brother is the
exception among the people under discussion.

This distinction is sharpest in refusals and admissions. 「{f|今日|きょう}は
{f|無理|むり}です」 leaves other days open; 「{f|今日|きょう}も{f|無理|むり}です」
admits today is one more in a run of no's.

### も vs と

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `と` glues nouns into one phrase ("A and B"); `も` marks each
noun separately as an addition ("A as well, B as well").

</div>

English "and" covers both, which is why this goes wrong so often.

<pre>
パン<b>と</b>たまごを{f|買|か}いました。
I bought bread and eggs.
</pre>

<pre>
パン<b>も</b>たまご<b>も</b>{f|買|か}いました。
I bought bread *and* eggs - both of them.
</pre>

The `と` version is a neutral shopping list. The `も…も` version stresses the
completeness of it, and it is the only one that can turn into "neither" simply by
negating the verb: 「パンもたまごも{f|買|か}いませんでした」 = "I bought neither."

Note also that `と` joins **nouns only**. To link two whole sentences, `と` is not
available at all.

### だれも / {f|何|なに}も vs だれか / {f|何|なに}か

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - question word + `も` needs a **negative** and means "none";
question word + `か` is **positive** and means "some".

</div>

The two look like near-twins and mean opposite things.

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}にだれ<b>も</b>いません。
There's nobody in the room.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}にだれ<b>か</b>いますか。
Is somebody in the room?
</pre>

Mixing them up produces sentences that are not just unnatural but ungrammatical:
❌<code>だれも{f|来|き}ました</code> cannot mean "somebody came" - `だれも` demands a negative verb,
so the sentence simply breaks. Use <code>だれか{f|来|き}ました</code> for "somebody came."

## Common mistakes

- ❌ わたし**はも**{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ わたし**も**{f|行|い}きます。
  `も` replaces `は`, `が` and `を`. It never stacks with them.

- ❌ ケーキ**をも**{f|食|た}べました。 → ✅ ケーキ**も**{f|食|た}べました。
  Same rule for the object marker: `を` disappears under `も`.

- ❌ {f|友|とも}だち**もと**{f|行|い}きました。 → ✅ {f|友|とも}だち**とも**{f|行|い}きました。
  With the particles that survive (`に`, `で`, `と`, `から`), the order is always
  particle first, `も` second.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|何|なに}**も**{f|食|た}べました。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|何|なに}**か**{f|食|た}べました。
  <code>{f|何|なに}も</code> can only be followed by a negative. For "I ate something", use <code>{f|何|なに}か</code>.

## Practice

**1.** 「{f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|来|き}ますか。」「はい。{f|田中|たなか}さん____{f|来|き}ますよ。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|田中|たなか}さん<b>も</b>{f|来|き}ますよ。

Yamada has already been established as coming, so Tanaka is being added to that
group. `も` replaces the `が` that would otherwise mark the subject.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}に{f|何|なに}（も・か）ありませんでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}に{f|何|なに}<b>も</b>ありませんでした。

The verb is negative, and question word + `も` + negative is the fixed way to say
"nothing at all". <code>{f|何|なに}か</code> would need a positive verb: <code>{f|何|なに}かありました</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "My brother can't swim, and I can't either."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|兄|あに}は{f|泳|およ}げません。わたし<b>も</b>{f|泳|およ}げません。

The `も` does the work of "either" - Japanese uses the same particle for "too" and
"either", and the negative verb supplies the difference. Note that `も` replaces the
`は` that marked the first speaker's topic.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|去年|きょねん}、{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}もへ{f|行|い}きました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|去年|きょねん}、{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}<b>へも</b>{f|行|い}きました。

`へ` shows the direction and cannot be dropped, so `も` goes behind it, not in front.
The rule is that `も` follows every particle except `が`, `を` and `は`, which it
replaces.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ケーキを{f|三|みっ}つ{f|食|た}べました。
(b) ケーキを{f|三|みっ}つ<b>も</b>{f|食|た}べました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain count: three cakes were eaten, no comment attached.

(b) adds `も` to the number, which turns it into an evaluation - "I ate *three whole
cakes*." The speaker is flagging the amount as more than expected, usually with a
touch of guilt or amazement. Nothing about the quantity itself has changed; only the
attitude toward it has.

</details>
