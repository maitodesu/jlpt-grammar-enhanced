# ない-form

**Meaning** · the plain negative - "doesn't", "won't", "isn't" - and the base a
whole set of later patterns attaches to

The ない-form is the plain-style negative, the partner of `～ません`. It is also
the foundation of `～ないでください`, `～なくてもいい`, `～なければならない`,
`～ないほうがいい` and `～ないで`. Get the stem wrong here and every one of those
patterns comes out wrong, which is why it repays care.

<div class="note">

**Note** - verbs come in three classes: **う-verbs** ({f|五段|ごだん} / godan / Group 1),
**る-verbs** ({f|一段|いちだん} / ichidan / Group 2), and the irregulars する and {f|来|く}る
(Group 3). This page says **う-verb** and **る-verb**.

</div>

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | final -u → -a, + ない | {f|書|か}く → {f|書|か}かない |
| Verb (う-verb ending in う) | う → **わ**, + ない | {f|買|か}う → {f|買|か}わない |
| Verb (る-verb) | drop る, + ない | {f|見|み}る → {f|見|み}ない |
| Verb (する) | しない | {f|練習|れんしゅう}する → {f|練習|れんしゅう}しない |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | こない | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ない |
| Verb (ある, irregular) | ない | ある → ない |
| い-adjective | い → くない | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}くない |
| な-adjective | + じゃない／ではない | {f|暇|ひま} → {f|暇|ひま}じゃない |
| Noun | + じゃない／ではない | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}じゃない |
| Past negative (all of the above) | ない → なかった | {f|書|か}かない → {f|書|か}かなかった |

For う-verbs the final vowel drops to the あ row:

| Dictionary ending | ない-form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| う | わない | {f|使|つか}う → {f|使|つか}わない |
| つ | たない | {f|待|ま}つ → {f|待|ま}たない |
| る | らない | {f|分|わ}かる → {f|分|わ}からない |
| む | まない | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}まない |
| ぶ | ばない | {f|呼|よ}ぶ → {f|呼|よ}ばない |
| ぬ | なない | {f|死|し}ぬ → {f|死|し}なない |
| く | かない | {f|聞|き}く → {f|聞|き}かない |
| ぐ | がない | {f|脱|ぬ}ぐ → {f|脱|ぬ}がない |
| す | さない | {f|出|だ}す → {f|出|だ}さない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - verbs ending in う take **わない**, not ❌あない.
{f|買|か}う → {f|買|か}わない, {f|言|い}う → {f|言|い}わない,
{f|会|あ}う → {f|会|あ}わない, {f|歌|うた}う → {f|歌|うた}わない. The reason is
historical - these verbs used to end in a -wu sound, and the w only survives
before あ - but for learning purposes it is simply the rule.

</div>

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - **ある has no ない-form**. Its negative is the word `ない`
itself. There is no ❌あらない. Polite: ありません.
{f|時間|じかん}がない "there's no time", {f|時間|じかん}がなかった "there was no
time". This one irregularity catches almost everybody once.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - {f|来|く}る is read three different ways depending on the form:
{f|来|く}る, {f|来|き}ます／{f|来|き}た／{f|来|き}て, and {f|来|こ}ない. The
kanji does not change, so the negative is read **こない**, never ❌きない.

</div>

Once `ない` is attached, the whole thing behaves as an **い-adjective**, and
inflects like one:

| Form | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Plain negative | ない | {f|行|い}かない |
| Past negative | ない → なかった | {f|行|い}かなかった |
| て-form (cause) | ない → なくて | {f|行|い}かなくて |
| Manner / request | ない + で | {f|行|い}かないで |
| Before a noun | ない + Noun | {f|行|い}かない{f|人|ひと} |
| Polite | ない + です | {f|行|い}かないです |

## Examples

<pre>
わたしは{f|肉|にく}を<b>{f|食|た}べない</b>。
I don't eat meat.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}は{f|授業|じゅぎょう}が<b>ない</b>。
There's no class today.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さん、まだ<b>{f|来|こ}ない</b>ね。
Tanaka still isn't here, is he.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|金|かね}が<b>{f|足|た}りない</b>から、また{f|今度|こんど}にします。
I haven't got enough money, so I'll leave it for another time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}を<b>{f|持|も}って{f|行|い}かない</b>と、ぬれるよ。
If you don't take an umbrella you'll get soaked.
</pre>

<pre>
ここでは{f|写真|しゃしん}を<b>{f|撮|と}らないで</b>ください。
Please don't take photographs here.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}が{f|来週|らいしゅう}なのに、{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|全然|ぜんぜん}<b>{f|勉強|べんきょう}しない</b>。
The exam is next week and my brother isn't studying at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|給料|きゅうりょう}が{f|何年|なんねん}も<b>{f|上|あ}がらない</b>ので、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|探|さが}している。
My pay hasn't gone up in years, so I'm looking for a new job.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ない` is plain style, so as a sentence ending it belongs with friends and
family. To a customer or a superior you want `～ません`. The middle option,
`～ないです`, is polite but relaxed, and is what colleagues say to each other all
day.

Bare `ない` at the end of a question - {f|行|い}かない？ - is a casual
invitation, not a plain question: "want to come?" It is the plain-style twin of
`～ませんか`, and it is extremely common. {f|今晩|こんばん}ご{f|飯|はん}
{f|食|た}べない？

The negative also softens requests, exactly as it does in English. Compare
{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けてくれますか with
{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けてくれませんか - the negative version is more polite,
because a negative question makes it easier to refuse.

Two things to keep separate. `ない` is the plain **negative** of a verb; the
adjective `ない` meaning "there isn't" is a different word that happens to look
the same. And in speech, ん is a very common contraction of ない in the Kansai
area and in men's rough speech ({f|分|わ}からん, {f|知|し}らん) - recognise it,
but stick to `ない` yourself until your speech is firmly anchored somewhere.

Written formal Japanese prefers `ず` and `ぬ` in fixed phrases - 
{f|知|し}らずに, やむを{f|得|え}ず - and uses `ではない` where speech uses
`じゃない`. `じゃない` is a contraction of `ではない`, and it is casual: never
write it in a report.

## Contrast

### ～ない vs ～ません vs ～ないです

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one meaning, three registers. `ない` plain, `ないです`
polite-casual, `ません` polite-formal.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|学校|がっこう}に<b>{f|行|い}かない</b>。
I'm not going to school tomorrow. (to a friend)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|学校|がっこう}に<b>{f|行|い}きません</b>。
I will not be going to school tomorrow. (to a teacher)
</pre>

Note the different bases: `ません` is built on the **ます-stem**
({f|行|い}き), while `ない` is built on the **あ-row stem** ({f|行|い}か). They
are not two endings on the same stem, which is why you cannot convert one to the
other by ear.

### The verb ない vs the adjective ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - three different negatives all end in `ない`, and each is
formed from a different base: verbs take あ-row + ない, い-adjectives swap い for
くない, and nouns and な-adjectives take じゃない.

</div>

<pre>
このカメラは<b>{f|動|うご}かない</b>。
This camera doesn't work.
</pre>

<pre>
このカメラは<b>{f|新|あたら}しくない</b>。
This camera isn't new.
</pre>

<pre>
このカメラは<b>{f|防水|ぼうすい}じゃない</b>。
This camera isn't waterproof.
</pre>

All three then inflect identically, because `ない` is an い-adjective:
{f|動|うご}かなかった, {f|新|あたら}しくなかった,
{f|防水|ぼうすい}じゃなかった. That shared tail is why
❌{f|動|うご}かないでした and ❌{f|新|あたら}しくないでした are wrong - an
い-adjective never takes `でした`.

### ある → ない vs いる → いない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both mean "there isn't", but they split the world the same
way their positives do: `ある`/`ない` for things, `いる`/`いない` for people and
animals.

</div>

<pre>
{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}に{f|何|なに}も<b>ない</b>。
There's nothing in the fridge.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|教室|きょうしつ}にはだれも<b>いない</b>。
There's nobody in the classroom.
</pre>

`いる` is a perfectly regular る-verb, so its negative is formed the ordinary
way: drop る, add ない. `ある` is the odd one out - it simply becomes `ない`.
The polite forms are ありません and いません respectively, and both keep the
thing-versus-creature split.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|買|か}あない → ✅ <b>{f|買|か}わない</b>
  う-verbs ending in う insert わ, not あ. Same for {f|言|い}わない and
  {f|会|あ}わない.

- ❌ お{f|金|かね}があらない → ✅ お{f|金|かね}が<b>ない</b>
  `ある` has no regular negative. Its negative is the word `ない`, and the polite
  form is ありません.

- ❌ {f|食|た}べるない → ✅ <b>{f|食|た}べない</b>
  For a る-verb you replace る with ない. Keeping both is the commonest
  beginner slip of all.

- ❌ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|広|ひろ}いない → ✅ この{f|部屋|へや}は<b>{f|広|ひろ}くない</b>
  い-adjectives do not take the verb negative. Swap the final い for くない.

- ❌ {f|来|く}る → {f|来|き}ない → ✅ {f|来|く}る → <b>{f|来|こ}ない</b>
  The kanji stays the same but the reading changes to こ in the negative. Say it
  aloud when you practise, or the mistake will be invisible on paper.

## Practice

**1.** {f|明日|あした}は{f|日曜日|にちようび}だから、{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|行|い}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|行|い}<b>かない</b>

{f|行|い}く is an う-verb ending in く, so the final -ku drops to -ka before
`ない`. The polite version is {f|行|い}きません, built on a different stem
altogether.

</details>

**2.** {f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}に{f|牛乳|ぎゅうにゅう}が（ない・いない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}に{f|牛乳|ぎゅうにゅう}が<b>ない</b>。

Milk is not alive, so the existence verb is `ある`, whose negative is the
irregular `ない`. `いない` is the negative of `いる`, reserved for people and
animals.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I don't drink coffee at night, so I sleep well."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|夜|よる}はコーヒーを<b>{f|飲|の}まない</b>ので、よく{f|寝|ね}られます。

{f|飲|の}む is an う-verb ending in む, so the negative stem is {f|飲|の}ま.
`ので` requires the plain form in front of it even when the sentence ends
politely.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|友達|ともだち}に{f|会|あ}あなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|友達|ともだち}に<b>{f|会|あ}わなかった</b>。

{f|会|あ}う ends in う, so the negative stem is {f|会|あ}わ, not
❌{f|会|あ}あ. The past negative is then formed the い-adjective way:
ない → なかった.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|朝|あさ}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べないで{f|出|で}かけた。
(b) {f|朝|あさ}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べなくて、おなかがすいた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) uses `ないで`, which describes the **manner** of the second action: he went
out, and the not-eating is how he did it - "without eating breakfast."

(b) uses `なくて`, which gives the **reason** for the second clause: because he
didn't eat, he got hungry. Substituting one for the other produces something
that a native speaker will hear as broken, even though English uses the same
word "not" in both.

</details>
