# に

**Meaning** · marks a point - where something ends up, where it sits, when it happens, or who receives it

`に` names a **point**, never a stretch or an area. The place a movement finishes, the
spot a thing occupies, the moment on the clock, the person a thing lands with: all of
those are single points, and `に` is how Japanese pins them down. Nearly every use
below is a version of that one idea.

## Formation

`に` is a particle. It attaches straight onto a noun, a time word, or a ます-stem, and
nothing changes shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun - destination | Noun + に + motion verb | {f|学校|がっこう} → {f|学校|がっこう}に{f|行|い}く |
| Noun - where something exists | Noun + に + ある／いる | {f|台所|だいどころ} → {f|台所|だいどころ}にある |
| Noun - person who receives | Person + に + giving or telling verb | {f|母|はは} → {f|母|はは}に{f|話|はな}す |
| Time word with a number or a name | Time + に | {f|六時|ろくじ} → {f|六時|ろくじ}に{f|起|お}きる |
| Verb ます-stem - purpose | stem + に + {f|行|い}く／{f|来|く}る／{f|帰|かえ}る | {f|買|か}います → {f|買|か}いに{f|行|い}く |
| Noun - thing entered, boarded, sat on | Noun + に + {f|入|はい}る／{f|乗|の}る／{f|座|すわ}る | いす → いすに{f|座|すわ}る |
| Period - rate or frequency | Period + に + number | {f|週|しゅう} → {f|週|しゅう}に{f|三回|さんかい} |
| Noun or な-adjective - result of a change | Noun + に + なる | {f|先生|せんせい} → {f|先生|せんせい}になる |

<div class="note">

**Note - which time words take `に`?** If the time word is a number or a fixed name on
the calendar, it takes `に`: {f|七時|しちじ}に, {f|三月|さんがつ}に, {f|月曜日|げつようび}に,
{f|二十日|はつか}に, {f|誕生日|たんじょうび}に. If it is positioned relative to *now*, or
if it is a frequency word, it takes **nothing at all**: {f|今日|きょう}, {f|明日|あした},
{f|昨日|きのう}, {f|今朝|けさ}, {f|今晩|こんばん}, {f|先週|せんしゅう}, {f|来年|らいねん},
{f|毎日|まいにち}, いつ. Adding `に` to those is one of the most persistent N5 errors.
{f|朝|あさ}, {f|夜|よる} and {f|夏|なつ} sit in between - both forms are heard, and the
bare one is more common in speech.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|七時|しちじ}<b>に</b>{f|起|お}きます。
I get up at seven.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|机|つくえ}の{f|下|した}<b>に</b>{f|猫|ねこ}がいます。
There's a cat under the desk.
</pre>

<pre>
ゆうべ{f|母|はは}<b>に</b>{f|電話|でんわ}しました。
I phoned my mother last night.
</pre>

<pre>
ちょっとパンを{f|買|か}い<b>に</b>{f|行|い}ってきます。
I'm just popping out to buy some bread.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|水曜日|すいようび}<b>に</b>{f|大阪|おおさか}で{f|会議|かいぎ}があります。
There's a meeting in Osaka next Wednesday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|週|しゅう}<b>に</b>{f|三回|さんかい}ジムで{f|泳|およ}いでいます。
I swim at the gym three times a week.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どものころ、この{f|公園|こうえん}<b>に</b>よく{f|遊|あそ}び<b>に</b>{f|来|き}ました。
When I was a kid I often came to this park to play.
</pre>

<pre>
すみません、この{f|書類|しょるい}を{f|田中|たなか}さん<b>に</b>{f|渡|わた}してもらえますか。
Sorry - could you pass this document to Tanaka?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`に` is completely register-neutral. It is the same in plain speech, in ます-form, in
business email and in newspapers. Choosing `に` never makes you sound stiff or casual.

What varies is whether you say it. In quick casual speech the **destination** `に` is
routinely dropped:

- どこ{f|行|い}くの? - "Where are you off to?"
- ちょっとコンビニ{f|行|い}ってくる。 - "Just nipping to the convenience store."

That dropping is limited to destinations. The `に` of **existence**, the `に` of a
**recipient**, and the `に` of a **clock time** are not dropped - remove them and the
sentence stops parsing rather than sounding casual.

`に` is phonologically weightless. It never carries stress, and it attaches so tightly
to the preceding noun that Japanese speakers hear <code>{f|六時|ろくじ}に</code> as one
word. Do not pause before it.

One more use worth knowing early, because it turns up constantly: `に` marks the
**result of a change**, with なる and する. {f|姉|あね}は{f|医者|いしゃ}になりました。
{f|部屋|へや}をきれい<b>に</b>してください。 It is the same "landing point" idea - the
thing ends up *at* that state.

## Contrast

### に vs で

This is the single most common particle error at N5, and it is worth over-learning.

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `に` marks where something **is**; `で` marks where something
**happens**.

</div>

If the verb is ある, いる, {f|住|す}む, {f|座|すわ}る, {f|置|お}く, {f|泊|と}まる - verbs about
a thing occupying a spot - use `に`. If the verb is an activity that could happen
anywhere, use `で`.

<pre>
{f|公園|こうえん}<b>に</b>ベンチがあります。
There's a bench in the park.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|公園|こうえん}<b>で</b>お{f|弁当|べんとう}を{f|食|た}べました。
We ate our packed lunch in the park.
</pre>

Same park; different job. The bench *is located* there, so `に`. The eating *took
place* there, so `で`.

The trap that catches nearly everyone is {f|住|す}む. In English "live in Tokyo" feels
like an activity, but Japanese treats it as being located somewhere:

<pre>
{f|兄|あに}は{f|東京|とうきょう}<b>に</b>{f|住|す}んでいます。
My older brother lives in Tokyo.
</pre>

❌<code>{f|東京|とうきょう}で{f|住|す}んでいます</code> is wrong. Learn <code>{f|住|す}む</code> as
a に-verb the way you learn a verb's conjugation.

### に vs へ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for a destination they are interchangeable. Everywhere else, only
`に` works.

</div>

<pre>
{f|空港|くうこう}<b>に</b>{f|着|つ}きました。
I've arrived at the airport.
</pre>

Swap that `に` for `へ` and the sentence is odd, because {f|着|つ}く is about the arrival
*point*, which is `に` territory. Where the verb is plain movement - {f|行|い}く,
{f|来|く}る, {f|帰|かえ}る - either particle is fine and the difference is a shade of
emphasis: `に` on the endpoint, `へ` on the heading.

The clean division is what `へ` **cannot** do. It cannot mark a time
(❌<code>{f|六時|ろくじ}へ</code>), it cannot mark existence
(❌<code>{f|部屋|へや}へいます</code>), and it cannot mark the person you tell or ask
something (❌<code>{f|先生|せんせい}へ{f|聞|き}きました</code>). All three are `に`.

### に vs を with verbs of movement

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - go **in**, get **on**, sit **down**: `に`. Go **out**, get **off**,
pass **through**: `を`.

</div>

English uses a preposition for both directions, so learners reach for one particle for
all of them. Japanese splits them. Entering, boarding and attaching take `に`, because
they end at a point. Leaving and traversing take `を`, because the noun is the thing
being *departed* or *crossed*.

<pre>
{f|次|つぎ}の{f|駅|えき}で{f|急行|きゅうこう}<b>に</b>{f|乗|の}ります。
I'll get on the express at the next station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|次|つぎ}の{f|駅|えき}で{f|電車|でんしゃ}<b>を</b>{f|降|お}ります。
I'll get off the train at the next station.
</pre>

The same pairing runs through {f|入|はい}る／{f|出|で}る and
{f|大学|だいがく}に{f|入|はい}る／{f|大学|だいがく}を{f|出|で}る.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}<b>に</b>{f|東京|とうきょう}へ{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}{f|東京|とうきょう}へ{f|行|い}きます。
  Time words that float relative to now - {f|今日|きょう}, {f|明日|あした}, {f|毎日|まいにち},
  {f|来週|らいしゅう} - take no particle at all.

- ❌ {f|公園|こうえん}<b>で</b>ベンチがあります。 → ✅ {f|公園|こうえん}<b>に</b>ベンチがあります。
  ある and いる report where a thing *exists*, and existence is always `に`.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}<b>を</b>{f|聞|き}きました。 → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}<b>に</b>{f|聞|き}きました。
  The person you ask is a recipient, not an object. <code>{f|聞|き}く</code> takes `を` for
  the *question* and `に` for the *person*.

- ❌ パンを{f|買|か}う<b>に</b>{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ パンを{f|買|か}い<b>に</b>{f|行|い}きます。
  Purpose `に` attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form. With a する-noun you
  can drop the verb entirely: {f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}く.

- ❌ {f|東京|とうきょう}<b>で</b>{f|住|す}んでいます。 → ✅ {f|東京|とうきょう}<b>に</b>{f|住|す}んでいます。
  <code>{f|住|す}む</code> is a location-of-existence verb in Japanese, however active
  "live" feels in English.

## Practice

**1.** {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|金曜日|きんようび}____{f|友達|ともだち}に{f|会|あ}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|金曜日|きんようび}<b>に</b>{f|友達|ともだち}に{f|会|あ}います。

{f|金曜日|きんようび} is a named day on the calendar, so it takes `に`. Note that
{f|来週|らいしゅう} on its own would take nothing - it is the {f|金曜日|きんようび} inside the
phrase that licenses the particle.

</details>

**2.** {f|机|つくえ}の{f|上|うえ}（に・で）{f|辞書|じしょ}があります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|机|つくえ}の{f|上|うえ}<b>に</b>{f|辞書|じしょ}があります。

The verb is ある, which reports where a thing exists, and existence takes `に`. `で`
would need an action happening on the desk, which a dictionary is not doing.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I go to the gym twice a week."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|週|しゅう}<b>に</b>{f|二回|にかい}ジム<b>に</b>{f|行|い}きます。

Two different jobs for the same particle. The first `に` is rate - a number of times
per period. The second is destination. Saying {f|週|しゅう}{f|二回|にかい} without `に` is
possible in casual speech but the full form is safer.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|毎朝|まいあさ}に{f|六時|ろくじ}{f|起|お}きて、{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|行|い}きます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|六時|ろくじ}<b>に</b>{f|起|お}きて、{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|行|い}きます。
"I get up at six every morning and go to work."

The two particles are swapped. {f|毎朝|まいあさ} is a frequency word and takes nothing;
{f|六時|ろくじ} is a clock time and needs `に`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) パンを{f|買|か}い<b>に</b>{f|行|い}きます。
(b) パンを{f|買|か}って{f|行|い}きます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is purpose: buying bread is the *reason* for going. You set off, and the errand is
what the trip is for. Nothing is said about where you go afterwards.

(b) is sequence: you buy bread and then continue on your way. The going is already
planned, and the bread is picked up en route - this is what you would say about
bringing something to a friend's house.

</details>
