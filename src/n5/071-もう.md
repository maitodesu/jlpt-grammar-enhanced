# もう - already; anymore

**Meaning** · the change has happened - already, by now, or (with a negative) not
any more

`もう` marks a threshold as crossed. Whatever the situation used to be, it is not
that any longer: the meal is eaten, the shop is shut, the pain has stopped. Point
it at a positive sentence and you get *already*; point it at a negative one and
you get *not any more*.

## Usage

`もう` is an adverb and never changes shape. It normally comes early in the
sentence, though it can sit right before the word it modifies.

With a **past or completed** predicate it means *already*:
<code>もう{f|終|お}わりました</code>, <code>もう{f|出|で}かけた</code>,
<code>もう{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}です</code>. In a question it is the standard way to ask
whether something is done: <code>もう{f|見|み}ましたか</code>.

With a **negative** predicate it means *no longer*:
<code>もう{f|吸|す}いません</code> (I don't smoke any more),
<code>もう{f|子|こ}どもじゃない</code> (not a child any more). The change has already
happened and there is no going back.

Before a **number or quantity** it means *another*, *more*:
<code>もう{f|一杯|いっぱい}</code> (one more glass), <code>もう{f|一度|いちど}</code> (once
more), <code>もう{f|少|すこ}し</code> (a bit more), <code>もうちょっと</code>. This use has
nothing to do with time, and learners often meet it before they meet the other
two.

Before a **time expression** it means the moment has arrived:
<code>もうすぐ{f|着|つ}きます</code> (arriving shortly),
<code>もう{f|帰|かえ}る{f|時間|じかん}だ</code> (time to go home). And on its own, `もう` is
an interjection of exasperation - see below.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>もう</b>{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べましたか。
Have you had lunch already?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>もう</b>お{f|酒|さけ}は{f|飲|の}みません。
I don't drink any more.
</pre>

<pre>
コーヒーを<b>もう</b>{f|一杯|いっぱい}いかがですか。
Would you like another cup of coffee?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>もう</b>こんな{f|時間|じかん}。そろそろ{f|失礼|しつれい}します。
Look at the time already. I ought to be going.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`もう` itself is neutral - it belongs equally to a text message and to a meeting.
What varies is what surrounds it, and `もう` has a few spoken lives worth knowing.

**`もう？` is surprise.** Said with a rising tone in reply to news, it means "that
soon?" - <code>え、もう{f|終|お}わったの？</code>. It is friendly, and it is one of the
easiest natural-sounding reactions to learn.

**`もう！` is exhaustion.** Alone, drawn out, it expresses irritation or fond
exasperation: <code>もう、{f|遅|おそ}いよ！</code>. This interjection leans female and very
casual, and it is the `もう` you will hear most often in drama.

**`もういい` needs care.** It means "that's enough" and lands anywhere between
"I'm full, thank you" and "forget it, I'm done with this," depending entirely on
tone. <code>もういいです</code> said flatly to a shop assistant sounds like giving up on
them; <code>{f|結構|けっこう}です</code> is the safe refusal.

**Formal writing prefers `すでに`.** Reports and notices use
<code>すでに{f|完了|かんりょう}しております</code> rather than `もう`. `もう` is not rude, just
conversational.

## Contrast

### もう vs まだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `もう` is on the far side of the change; `まだ` is on the near
side. Every `もう` sentence has a `まだ` mirror image, and the mirror flips in the
negative as well as the positive.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}は<b>もう</b>{f|始|はじ}まりました。
The meeting has already started.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}はまだ{f|始|はじ}まっていません。
The meeting hasn't started yet.
</pre>

Now the negatives, which is where English gives you two completely different
words:

<pre>
{f|足|あし}は<b>もう</b>{f|痛|いた}くありません。
My leg doesn't hurt any more. (it did; it stopped)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|足|あし}はまだ{f|痛|いた}いです。
My leg still hurts. (nothing has changed)
</pre>

Keep hold of the direction rather than the English: `もう` + negative looks back at
something that has ended, `まだ` + negative looks forward to something that has not
begun. <code>もう{f|働|はたら}いていません</code> is a retirement;
<code>まだ{f|働|はたら}いていません</code> is a student.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|宿題|しゅくだい}は<b>もう</b>しています。（intending "I've already done it"）→ ✅ {f|宿題|しゅくだい}は<b>もう</b>しました。
  <code>もう～ている</code> says you are already in the middle of it. Completion in the
  past takes the plain past: `もう～ました`.

- ❌ バスが<b>もう</b>{f|来|き}ていません。（intending "the bus hasn't come yet"）→ ✅ バスが<b>まだ</b>{f|来|き}ていません。
  With a negative, `もう` means "not any more" - you would be saying the bus service
  has stopped running.

- ❌ コーヒーを<b>もう</b>{f|一|いち}ください。 → ✅ コーヒーを<b>もう</b>{f|一杯|いっぱい}ください。
  `もう` needs a full counter phrase after it: <code>{f|一杯|いっぱい}</code>,
  <code>{f|一|ひと}つ</code>, <code>{f|一度|いちど}</code>, <code>{f|少|すこ}し</code>.

- ❌ （in a written report）{f|作業|さぎょう}は<b>もう</b>{f|終|お}わりました。 → ✅ {f|作業|さぎょう}はすでに{f|完了|かんりょう}しております。
  Not ungrammatical, just conversational. Formal writing reaches for `すでに`.

## Practice

**1.** 「{f|映画|えいが}のチケット、____{f|買|か}いましたか。」「はい、{f|昨日|きのう}{f|買|か}いました。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

「{f|映画|えいが}のチケット、<b>もう</b>{f|買|か}いましたか。」

Asking whether something is done by now is the core use of `もう` with the past
tense. It is also the question that `まだ～ていません` exists to answer in the
negative.

</details>

**2.** {f|昔|むかし}はたばこを{f|吸|す}っていましたが、（もう・まだ）{f|吸|す}っていません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昔|むかし}はたばこを{f|吸|す}っていましたが、<b>もう</b>{f|吸|す}っていません。

The first half establishes that the speaker used to smoke, so the habit has ended:
"not any more." `まだ` would mean they had never started, which contradicts
<code>{f|昔|むかし}は{f|吸|す}っていました</code>.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) もう{f|少|すこ}し{f|待|ま}ってください。
(b) もう{f|待|ま}ちません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) uses the quantity sense: <code>もう{f|少|すこ}し</code> is "a little more", so the speaker is
asking for extra time. It is polite and cooperative.

(b) uses the negative sense: the waiting is over and will not continue. Depending
on the tone it is either a decision to give up or an outright threat. The same
adverb, doing two of its jobs, in sentences that could not be further apart.

</details>
