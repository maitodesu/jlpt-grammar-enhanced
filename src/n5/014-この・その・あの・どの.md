# この・その・あの・どの

**Meaning** · this ~ / that ~ / that ~ over there / which ~ - pointing words that modify a noun instead of replacing it

`この` and its siblings are **adnominals**: they do nothing on their own and must be
followed immediately by a noun. <code>この{f|本|ほん}</code> is "this book", never "this". The
こ／そ／あ／ど split is the same one the rest of the series uses - the speaker's
side, the listener's side, away from both, and the question.

## Formation

Nothing attaches *to* these words. They attach to the noun on their right, with
nothing allowed in between except an adjective.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | この + Noun - in the speaker's territory | この{f|傘|かさ} |
| Noun | その + Noun - in the listener's territory, or just mentioned | その{f|話|はなし} |
| Noun | あの + Noun - away from both, or shared memory | あの{f|店|みせ} |
| Noun | どの + Noun - which ~, out of **three or more** | どの{f|電車|でんしゃ} |
| Adjective + Noun | この + Adj + Noun | この{f|赤|あか}いくつ |
| The particle | この + Noun **+ particle** - never この + particle | この{f|傘|かさ}は{f|私|わたし}のです |
| Nothing | ❌ この on its own → use これ | ❌ このは{f|大|おお}きい → ✅ これは{f|大|おお}きい |
| Two choices only | どの → どちらの（casual どっちの） | どちらの{f|席|せき}がいいですか |
| Polite equivalent | この／その／あの → こちらの／そちらの／あちらの | そちらの{f|方|かた}にお{f|聞|き}きください |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `この` is a hook with nothing on it. Something must hang from it,
and that something is a noun. If you have no noun to supply, you wanted
`これ・それ・あれ・どれ` instead.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
<b>この</b>ペンは{f|父|ちち}のです。
This pen is my father's.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>その</b>いすに{f|座|すわ}ってください。
Please sit on that chair.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>あの</b>{f|白|しろ}い{f|建物|たてもの}は{f|図書館|としょかん}です。
That white building over there is the library.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>どの</b>バスが{f|空港|くうこう}へ{f|行|い}きますか。
Which bus goes to the airport?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>その</b>{f|話|はなし}はもう{f|聞|き}きました。
I have already heard that story.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>あの</b>とき、もっと{f|早|はや}く{f|出|で}ればよかった。
I should have left earlier that time.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>この</b>{f|辺|へん}は{f|夜|よる}になると{f|静|しず}かですから、{f|勉強|べんきょう}するのにいいですよ。
This area goes quiet after dark, so it's a good place to study.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|注文|ちゅうもん}は<b>あの</b>{f|背|せ}の{f|高|たか}い{f|店員|てんいん}さんにお{f|願|ねが}いしました。
I gave my order to that tall member of staff over there.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Neutral in itself, and unavoidable - you cannot get through a paragraph of
Japanese without it.

**`あの` is also the standard hesitation noise**, usually stretched to `あのう`. It
opens a request to a stranger the way "excuse me…" does in English:
<code>あのう、すみません、ちょっとよろしいですか</code>. Beginners hear it constantly
and assume it is the demonstrative; it is not, and it points at nothing.

**`その` is the workhorse of written and business Japanese.** In an email,
<code>その{f|件|けん}について</code> ("regarding that matter") refers back to whatever
the previous sentence or the previous email established. Anaphoric `その` is far
more common in writing than the physical "near you" sense you learn first.

**Politeness rises through `こちらの`.** <code>あの{f|人|ひと}</code> is fine among
friends but too blunt about a customer or a superior; <code>あちらの{f|方|かた}</code>
is the safe formal version. `あいつ` and friends are the rough end of the same
scale and take a `の` too: `あいつの` - avoid in polite company.

**Casual speech shortens `どの` to `どっちの` when there are two**, and drops the
noun altogether when it can be guessed: 「どっち?」rather than
<code>どちらの{f|席|せき}がいいですか</code>.

## Contrast

### この・その・あの・どの vs これ・それ・あれ・どれ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `この` modifies, `これ` replaces. `この` must be followed by a
noun; `これ` must **not** be.

</div>

They are the same four distances packaged for two different grammatical slots, and
mixing them up is the single most frequent mistake in this topic.

<pre>
<b>その</b>{f|時計|とけい}はいくらですか。
How much is that watch?
</pre>

<pre>
それはいくらですか。
How much is that one?
</pre>

Both are correct; the first names the object, the second assumes you can see which
object is meant. What you may never write is ❌<code>それ{f|時計|とけい}</code> or
❌<code>この、いくらですか</code>.

There is one asymmetry worth memorising: the place words *do* take `の` before a
noun (<code>ここの{f|店|みせ}</code>, "the shop here"), but the thing pronouns do not.
❌<code>これの{f|店|みせ}</code> is wrong; <code>この{f|店|みせ}</code> is right.

### その vs あの

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `その` = it is on your listener's side, or it entered the
conversation a moment ago. `あの` = it is on nobody's side, or we both already
know it.

</div>

Physically the split is easy. In conversation, where nothing is visible, it is
about who owns the information.

<pre>
「{f|新|あたら}しいカフェができたよ。」「へえ、<b>その</b>カフェ、どこ?」
"A new café has opened." "Oh - where is that café?"
</pre>

The speaker has never seen it. It exists for them only because the listener just
said so, so `その`.

<pre>
{f|学生|がくせい}のとき、よく{f|二人|ふたり}で{f|行|い}ったよね。<b>あの</b>カフェ、まだあるかな。
We used to go together as students, didn't we. I wonder if that café is still there.
</pre>

Here the café is in both memories, so `あの`. Using `あの` for something the
listener has never heard of sounds as though you are muttering to yourself - which
is precisely why writers use it for that effect.

### どの vs どんな

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `どの` picks one out of a known set. `どんな` asks what kind of
thing it is, with no set in front of you.

</div>

Both sit in front of a noun and both come out as "what/which" in English, so they
collapse together unless you separate them deliberately.

<pre>
{f|棚|たな}の{f|本|ほん}のうち、<b>どの</b>{f|本|ほん}が{f|一番|いちばん}{f|役|やく}に{f|立|た}ちますか。
Of the books on the shelf, which book is the most useful?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>どんな</b>{f|本|ほん}をよく{f|読|よ}みますか。
What kind of books do you usually read?
</pre>

Answer the first with a book you can point at; answer the second with a category - 
{f|歴史|れきし}の{f|本|ほん}, {f|小説|しょうせつ}. A quick test: if the English is
"which of these", it is `どの`; if it is "what sort of", it is `どんな`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>この</b>は{f|私|わたし}のかばんです。 → ✅ <b>これ</b>は{f|私|わたし}のかばんです。
  `この` cannot stand alone. Either supply a noun (<code>このかばんは{f|私|わたし}のです</code>)
  or switch to `これ`.

- ❌ <b>それ</b>{f|本|ほん}を{f|取|と}ってください。 → ✅ <b>その</b>{f|本|ほん}を{f|取|と}ってください。
  A noun follows, so the の-series is required. `それ` never sits in front of a noun.

- ❌ <b>あのの</b>{f|人|ひと}は{f|先生|せんせい}です。 → ✅ <b>あの</b>{f|人|ひと}は{f|先生|せんせい}です。
  `この・その・あの・どの` already end in the `の` they need. Adding a second one is
  doubling up.

- ❌ {f|肉|にく}と{f|魚|さかな}、<b>どの</b>{f|料理|りょうり}にしますか。 → ✅ {f|肉|にく}と{f|魚|さかな}、<b>どちらの</b>{f|料理|りょうり}にしますか。
  Two options means `どちらの`. `どの` needs three or more to choose between.

- ❌ <b>どの</b>{f|音楽|おんがく}が{f|好|す}きですか。（meaning "what kind of music do you like?"）→ ✅ <b>どんな</b>{f|音楽|おんがく}が{f|好|す}きですか。
  `どの` would be asking the listener to point at one specific piece of music from a
  list in front of them. For a genre, you want `どんな`.

## Practice

**1.** ____{f|建物|たてもの}は{f|百年|ひゃくねん}{f|前|まえ}に{f|建|た}てられました。（the building is a long way off, visible from where you both stand）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>あの</b>{f|建物|たてもの}は{f|百年|ひゃくねん}{f|前|まえ}に{f|建|た}てられました。

It is away from both of you, so `あの`. A noun follows, so it must be the の-series
and not `あれ`.

</details>

**2.** {f|会議室|かいぎしつ}が{f|五|いつ}つあります。（どの・どんな）{f|部屋|へや}を{f|使|つか}いますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>どの</b>{f|部屋|へや}を{f|使|つか}いますか。

There is a defined set of five rooms and you are asking the listener to pick one
out of it. <code>どんな{f|部屋|へや}</code> would be asking what sort of room they want - big,
quiet, with a projector - which is a different question.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I don't know that word. Could you write it on the board?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>その</b>{f|言葉|ことば}は{f|知|し}りません。ホワイトボードに{f|書|か}いていただけますか。

The word came from the listener's mouth, so it belongs to their side of the
conversation: `その`. `あの` would wrongly imply you both already know it, which is
the opposite of what you are saying.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: すみません、これシャツはいくらですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ すみません、<b>この</b>シャツはいくらですか。
"Excuse me, how much is this shirt?"

シャツ is a noun and it follows immediately, so the modifying form `この` is
required. The alternative repair is to drop the noun: <code>これはいくらですか</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) <b>その</b>{f|映画|えいが}は{f|面白|おもしろ}かったですか。
(b) <b>あの</b>{f|映画|えいが}は{f|面白|おもしろ}かったですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) refers to a film the listener has just brought up, and which the speaker knows
nothing about beyond that mention. It is the natural reply to "I went to the
cinema yesterday."

(b) presupposes that both of you already have a particular film in mind - one you
discussed before, or one everybody in the room has heard of. Said out of nowhere it
would leave the listener asking 「どの{f|映画|えいが}?」.

</details>
