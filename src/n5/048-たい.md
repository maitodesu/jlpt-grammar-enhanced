# ～たい

**Meaning** · want to do something - the speaker's own desire

`～たい` attaches to a verb and reports a wish felt from the inside. That "from the
inside" is not decoration: it is a grammatical restriction. Plain `～たい` is for
**your own** desires, and for asking about the **listener's**. You cannot use it
to state flatly what a third person wants.

## Formation

Attach `たい` to the ます-stem, then conjugate it exactly like an い-adjective.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + たい | {f|飲|の}みます → {f|飲|の}みたい |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + たい | {f|食|た}べます → {f|食|た}べたい |
| Verb (する) | し + たい | する → したい／{f|勉強|べんきょう}したい |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | き + たい | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|き}たい |

Once `たい` is attached, <code>{f|飲|の}みたい</code> behaves like <code>{f|高|たか}い</code> in every
respect:

| い-adjective form | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Polite | + です | {f|飲|の}みたいです |
| Negative | たい → たくない | {f|飲|の}みたくない |
| Past | たい → たかった | {f|飲|の}みたかった |
| Negative past | たい → たくなかった | {f|飲|の}みたくなかった |
| て-form | たい → たくて | {f|飲|の}みたくて |
| Adverbial | たい → たく | {f|飲|の}みたくなる |
| Before a noun | たい + Noun | {f|飲|の}みたいもの |
| Third person | ます-stem + たがる | {f|飲|の}みたがる／{f|飲|の}みたがっている |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たい` is an い-adjective, not a verb. So it is
<code>{f|行|い}きたくない</code>, never ❌<code>{f|行|い}きたいじゃない</code>, and <code>{f|行|い}きたかった</code>,
never ❌<code>{f|行|い}きたいでした</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|水|みず}が<b>{f|飲|の}みたい</b>。
I want some water.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日本|にほん}へ<b>{f|行|い}きたい</b>です。
I want to go to Japan.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|何|なに}が<b>{f|食|た}べたい</b>ですか。
What do you want to eat?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}はどこにも<b>{f|行|い}きたくない</b>。
I don't want to go anywhere today.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どものとき、パイロットに<b>なりたかった</b>。
When I was a kid, I wanted to be a pilot.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|新|あたら}しいゲームを<b>{f|買|か}いたがっている</b>。
My little brother wants to buy the new game.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|疲|つか}れたので{f|今日|きょう}は{f|早|はや}く<b>{f|寝|ね}たい</b>んですが、まだ{f|仕事|しごと}が{f|終|お}わりません。
I'm exhausted and I want an early night, but I still haven't finished work.
</pre>

<pre>

Next year I'm hoping to go to Okinawa and swim in the sea.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`～たい` itself is neutral; `～たいです` is polite in *form*. The trouble is that
politeness of form does not fix bluntness of content. Stating a raw want at
someone senior - {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}りたいです - is
grammatical and still lands badly, because you are pushing your preference at a
superior. Softeners exist for exactly this:

- **～たいと{f|思|おも}います** - "I'm thinking I'd like to…". The standard business
  hedge. {f|来週|らいしゅう}お{f|休|やす}みを{f|取|と}りたいと{f|思|おも}います。
- **～たいんですが** - sets up a request and leaves the ask unsaid.
  すみません、これを{f|返|かえ}したいんですが…
- **～たいのですが／～たく{f|存|ぞん}じます** - written and formal registers.

The same asymmetry runs the other way. Asking a superior
{f|何|なに}が{f|食|た}べたいですか is a bit bald; {f|何|なん}になさいますか or
{f|何|なに}がよろしいですか is what you actually say. Among friends and to
subordinates, plain `～たい?` is perfectly normal.

In casual speech the negative `～たくない` is the everyday form; `～たくありません`
is correct but stiff, and mostly turns up in writing or careful speech.

`～たがる` is a real verb (a う-verb: `たがらない`, `たがります`, `たがって`) and it
carries a slight sense of *watching someone from outside* - you are describing
observable behaviour, not reading a mind. That distance can shade into criticism,
so `たがる` about a superior is rude:
❌{f|社長|しゃちょう}は{f|帰|かえ}りたがっています.

## Contrast

### ～たい vs ～たがる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～たい` for me and for you; `～たがる` for him, her and them.

</div>

A flat statement about a third person's desire in plain `～たい` sounds wrong to
native ears, because you are claiming direct access to someone else's inner state.

<pre>
{f|妹|いもうと}はケーキを<b>{f|食|た}べたがっています</b>。
My little sister wants cake.
</pre>

Note the particle: `たがる` is an ordinary verb, so its object takes `を`, never
the `が` that `たい` allows. Note the `～ている` too. For a desire someone is feeling *right now*,
`たがっている` is the normal form; bare `たがる` reads as a habit - 
{f|弟|おとうと}はいつも{f|新|あたら}しいものを{f|買|か}いたがる - "my brother always
wants to buy the newest thing."

`たがる` is not the only way out. These are often more natural, and politer:

- {f|妹|いもうと}はケーキが{f|食|た}べたいそうです。 (I hear that…)
- {f|妹|いもうと}はケーキが{f|食|た}べたいと{f|言|い}っています。 (she says…)
- {f|妹|いもうと}はケーキが{f|食|た}べたいようです／みたいです。 (it seems…)

<div class="note">

**Note** - the third-person ban only applies to a **plain main-clause statement**.
Inside a subordinate clause, plain `たい` about someone else is fine:
{f|日本|にほん}に{f|行|い}きたい{f|人|ひと}は{f|手|て}を{f|挙|あ}げてください。
「Anyone who wants to go to Japan, raise your hand.」 It is also fine under
<code>と{f|思|おも}っている</code>: {f|彼|かれ}は{f|行|い}きたいと{f|思|おも}っています。

</div>

### が vs を with ～たい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are correct. `が` spotlights the *thing* you crave; `を`
keeps the focus on the *action*. When in doubt at N5, `が` is the safer answer.

</div>

Because `たい` turns the verb into an adjective-like word, the old object can take
`が` - and that option is genuinely alive in modern Japanese.

<pre>
ビール<b>が</b>{f|飲|の}みたい。
I want a beer. (it's beer specifically that I'm after)
</pre>

<pre>
ビール<b>を</b>{f|飲|の}みたい。
I want to drink a beer. (the drinking is what I'm picturing)
</pre>

Three places where `を` is the only option:

1. **The `を` is not a direct object.** Path and departure `を` never becomes `が`:
   {f|空|そら}**を**{f|飛|と}びたい, {f|家|いえ}**を**{f|出|で}たい.
2. **The clause already has a `が`.** Two `が` in one clause collide:
   ❌{f|私|わたし}がビールが{f|飲|の}みたい.
3. **The object is far from the verb.** Once material intervenes, `が` degrades:
   ビール**を**{f|冷|ひ}やして、あとでゆっくり{f|飲|の}みたい.

`を` is also the safer default in writing and in longer sentences; `が` is at its
most natural in short, felt statements - the ones you say when you actually want
the thing.

### ～たい vs ほしい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ほしい` wants a **noun**; `～たい` wants an **action**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しいパソコン<b>がほしい</b>です。
I want a new computer.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しいパソコンが<b>{f|買|か}いたい</b>です。
I want to buy a new computer.
</pre>

`ほしい` is also an い-adjective, and it carries the same first-person restriction
as `たい` - third person needs `ほしがっている`. Its verb-taking cousin,
`～てほしい`, means you want *someone else* to do something:
{f|手伝|てつだ}ってほしい - "I want you to help."

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|日本|にほん}に{f|行|い}きたいです。 → ✅ {f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|日本|にほん}に{f|行|い}き**たがっています**。
  A flat statement about a third person's desire needs `たがる`, or a hedge like
  `たいそうです`／<code>たいと{f|言|い}っています</code>.

- ❌ {f|行|い}きたい**じゃない**／{f|行|い}きたい**でした** → ✅ {f|行|い}き**たくない**／{f|行|い}き**たかった**
  `たい` is an い-adjective. It never takes `じゃない` or `でした`.

- ❌ {f|食|た}べる**たい** → ✅ {f|食|た}べ**たい**
  `たい` attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.
  {f|食|た}べます → {f|食|た}べ + たい.

- ❌ コーヒーが**ほしいたい**。 → ✅ コーヒーが<b>{f|飲|の}みたい</b>。／コーヒーが**ほしい**。
  `ほしい` and `たい` do the same job for different word classes. Never stack them.

- ❌ {f|課長|かちょう}、{f|何|なに}が{f|食|た}べたいですか。 → ✅ {f|課長|かちょう}、{f|何|なに}がよろしいですか。
  Grammatical, but too direct upward. Asking a superior about their wants calls
  for `よろしいですか` or `なさいますか`.

## Practice

**1.** のどがかわきました。{f|水|みず}が{f|飲|の}____です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|水|みず}が{f|飲|の}**みたい**です。

`たい` attaches to the ます-stem: {f|飲|の}みます → {f|飲|の}み + たい. The polite
form is just `です` on the end, because <code>{f|飲|の}みたい</code> is an い-adjective.

</details>

**2.** {f|妹|いもうと}は{f|新|あたら}しい{f|靴|くつ}を（{f|買|か}いたいです・{f|買|か}いたがっています）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|妹|いもうと}は{f|新|あたら}しい{f|靴|くつ}を<b>{f|買|か}いたがっています</b>。

<code>{f|妹|いもうと}</code> is a third person, so plain `たい` in a main clause is out - you cannot assert
someone else's inner state directly. `たがっている` describes the desire as
something you can observe from outside.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I didn't want to go to school yesterday."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

きのうは{f|学校|がっこう}に<b>{f|行|い}きたくなかった</b>（です）。

Past negative of an い-adjective: たい → たく → たくない → たくなかった. There is no
❌<code>{f|行|い}きたいじゃなかった</code>. Adding `です` makes it polite without changing the
stem.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|今日|きょう}は{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}きたいじゃないです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}き**たくない**です。

`じゃない` is the negative for nouns and な-adjectives. `～たい` is an い-adjective,
so the negative is built by swapping the final `い` for `くない`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ラーメンが{f|食|た}べたい。
(b) ラーメンを{f|食|た}べたい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are correct and a speaker would accept either. (a) puts the spotlight on
*ramen* as the object of the craving - "it's ramen I want", the thing itself. (b)
keeps the sentence built around the action of eating, so it reads a touch more
neutral and descriptive, and it's what you'd reach for in a longer or written
sentence.

The choice narrows once the sentence grows: with material between the object and
the verb, or with a `が`-marked subject already present, only `を` works.

</details>
