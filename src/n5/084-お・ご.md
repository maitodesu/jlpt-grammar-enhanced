# お・ご

**Meaning** · the politeness prefix - attached to a noun to make it respectful,
humble, or simply more refined

`お` and `ご` are the same prefix wearing two coats. Which one a word takes is
decided by the word's **origin**, not by how polite you are feeling: native
Japanese vocabulary takes `お`, vocabulary borrowed from Chinese takes `ご`. The
choice is fixed per word and has to be learnt with the word.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Native noun (kun reading) | <b>お</b> + noun | {f|名前|なまえ} → お{f|名前|なまえ} |
| Native noun (kun reading) | <b>お</b> + noun | {f|金|かね} → お{f|金|かね} |
| Sino-Japanese noun (on reading) | <b>ご</b> + noun | {f|家族|かぞく} → ご{f|家族|かぞく} |
| Sino-Japanese noun (on reading) | <b>ご</b> + noun | {f|住所|じゅうしょ} → ご{f|住所|じゅうしょ} |
| Sino-Japanese exceptions | <b>お</b> + noun | {f|電話|でんわ} → お{f|電話|でんわ} |
| な-adjective | お／ご + adjective | {f|元気|げんき} → お{f|元気|げんき}／{f|親切|しんせつ} → ご{f|親切|しんせつ} |
| Verb, honorific | お + ます-stem + になる | {f|読|よ}む → お{f|読|よ}みになる |
| Verb, humble | お／ご + stem + する | {f|電話|でんわ}する → お{f|電話|でんわ}します |
| Fossilised | prefix is part of the word | ご{f|飯|はん}、お{f|茶|ちゃ}、おなか |
| Loanwords | normally nothing | コーヒー → ❌おコーヒー |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - read the word aloud. If it sounds like plain old Japanese
（{f|名前|なまえ}, {f|金|かね}, {f|手紙|てがみ}）, use `お`. If it is a compact
two-character compound read with Chinese-derived sounds
（{f|住所|じゅうしょ}, {f|家族|かぞく}, {f|注文|ちゅうもん}）, use `ご`. Then learn
the handful of exceptions by heart.

</div>

The common Sino-Japanese words that take `お` anyway are worth memorising as a
list: お{f|電話|でんわ}, お{f|返事|へんじ}, お{f|食事|しょくじ},
お{f|時間|じかん}, お{f|料理|りょうり}, お{f|約束|やくそく}, お{f|礼|れい},
お{f|茶|ちゃ}, お{f|客|きゃく}さん. These have been in everyday Japanese so long
that they stopped feeling foreign.

## Nuance & register

The prefix is doing one of three quite different jobs, and telling them apart is
the whole skill.

**Respect.** Attached to something belonging to the listener - お{f|名前|なまえ},
ご{f|家族|かぞく}, お{f|仕事|しごと} - it raises *them*. This is the use that must
never be turned on yourself: ❌{f|私|わたし}のお{f|名前|なまえ}は… is the classic
beginner's error, and it sounds like bowing to your own reflection.

**Humility.** In お + stem + する（お{f|電話|でんわ}します,
お{f|待|ま}ちしています）the prefix belongs to *your* action, lowering you rather
than raising anyone. The `お` is attached to a verb here, not a noun.

**Beautification** - {f|美化語|びかご}. お{f|茶|ちゃ}, ご{f|飯|はん},
お{f|金|かね}, お{f|菓子|かし}, お{f|水|みず} have nothing to do with respect at
all. Everyone says them about everyone's tea and everyone's money, and dropping
the prefix from some of these（{f|茶|ちゃ}, {f|金|かね}）sounds coarse. A few have
fused completely: おなか and おかし are simply the words, and no one hears a prefix
in them any more.

Register notes worth having:

- Heavy use of {f|美化語|びかご} is traditionally associated with women's speech and
  with a refined register. Layering it everywhere - おビール, おズボン - sounds
  affected, though shops and restaurants do exactly that.
- Katakana loanwords normally take nothing. The one you will genuinely hear is
  おトイレ, which has become ordinary polite speech.
- A small set of い-adjectives takes `お` in fixed courtesies:
  お{f|忙|いそが}しいところすみません, お{f|若|わか}いですね.
- Some words take either prefix - お{f|返事|へんじ} and ご{f|返事|へんじ} are both
  used, with お the more common today.

You cannot attach the prefix wherever you like. There is no ❌お{f|机|つくえ}, no
❌おテレビ, no ❌ご{f|勉強|べんきょう}{f|部屋|べや}. The set of words that take it
is closed, and it is learnt word by word.

## Contrast

### お vs ご

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `お` for {f|和語|わご} (native words, kun readings), `ご` for
{f|漢語|かんご} (Chinese-derived words, on readings).

</div>

<pre>
<b>お{f|手紙|てがみ}</b>をありがとうございました。
Thank you for your letter.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>ご{f|意見|いけん}</b>をお{f|聞|き}かせください。
Please let us hear your opinion.
</pre>

<code>{f|手紙|てがみ}</code> is read with native Japanese sounds, so `お`.
<code>{f|意見|いけん}</code> is a two-character on-reading compound, so `ご`. The test
is not infallible - {f|返事|へんじ} is an on-reading compound and still prefers
`お` - but it is right far more often than it is wrong.

### Respectful お vs beautifying お

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if removing the prefix would be rude to a *person*, it is
respect. If it would just sound rough, it is beautification.

</div>

<pre>
<b>お{f|仕事|しごと}</b>は{f|何|なん}ですか。
What do you do for a living?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>お{f|茶|ちゃ}</b>でも{f|飲|の}みませんか。
Shall we have some tea?
</pre>

The first `お` honours the listener, and you would drop it when talking about your
own job: {f|私|わたし}の{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|教師|きょうし}です. The second belongs
to the tea and stays put whoever the tea is for - you say お{f|茶|ちゃ} about your
own cup without a second thought.

### お + stem + になる vs お + stem + する

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>になる</code> raises the other person; <code>する</code> lowers
you. Both are polite; they point in opposite directions.

</div>

<pre>
{f|社長|しゃちょう}はもう<b>お{f|帰|かえ}りになりました</b>。
The company president has already gone home.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}、こちらから<b>お{f|届|とど}けします</b>。
We'll deliver it to you tomorrow.
</pre>

Use the first about somebody else's action, the second about your own. Applying
the honorific form to yourself - ❌{f|私|わたし}がお{f|帰|かえ}りになります - is a
serious error, and the mirror-image mistake of using
<code>{f|私|わたし}のお{f|名前|なまえ}</code>. Both are covered properly at N4; what
matters now is knowing that the prefix alone does not make a phrase polite in the
right direction.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}の<b>お</b>{f|名前|なまえ}は{f|田中|たなか}です。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}の{f|名前|なまえ}は{f|田中|たなか}です。
  The respectful prefix never goes on your own possessions. Ask the listener for
  お{f|名前|なまえ}, then answer with a bare {f|名前|なまえ}.

- ❌ <b>ご</b>{f|電話|でんわ}ありがとうございます。 → ✅ <b>お</b>{f|電話|でんわ}ありがとうございます。
  <code>{f|電話|でんわ}</code> looks like a `ご` word - two characters, on readings - 
  but it is one of the fixed exceptions that take `お`.

- ❌ <b>お</b>{f|住所|じゅうしょ}を{f|書|か}いてください。 → ✅ <b>ご</b>{f|住所|じゅうしょ}を{f|書|か}いてください。
  The mirror error. <code>{f|住所|じゅうしょ}</code> is straightforwardly
  {f|漢語|かんご} and takes `ご`, as do ご{f|職業|しょくぎょう} and
  ご{f|連絡|れんらく} on the same form.

- ❌ <b>お</b>コーヒーをどうぞ。 → ✅ コーヒーをどうぞ。
  Loanwords do not take the prefix. Shop staff sometimes say it anyway; a learner
  saying it sounds like a mistake rather than like service language.

- ❌ {f|朝|あさ}、{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べました。 → ✅ {f|朝|あさ}ごはんを{f|食|た}べました。
  In ご{f|飯|はん} the prefix has fused into the word. Removing it does not make
  the sentence casual - it makes it wrong.

## Practice

**1.** すみません、____{f|名前|なまえ}は{f|何|なん}とおっしゃいますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

すみません、<b>お</b>{f|名前|なまえ}は{f|何|なん}とおっしゃいますか。

<code>{f|名前|なまえ}</code> is a native Japanese word, so the prefix is `お`. It is
required here because you are asking about the listener's name, and a bare
{f|名前|なまえ} in a question to a stranger sounds abrupt.

</details>

**2.** Choose: （お・ご）{f|家族|かぞく}によろしくお{f|伝|つた}えください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>ご</b>{f|家族|かぞく}によろしくお{f|伝|つた}えください。

<code>{f|家族|かぞく}</code> is a two-character on-reading compound - 
{f|漢語|かんご} - so it takes `ご`. Note that
お{f|伝|つた}えください in the same sentence takes `お`, because
<code>{f|伝|つた}える</code> is a native verb.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Thank you for your reply."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>お{f|返事|へんじ}</b>ありがとうございます。

<code>{f|返事|へんじ}</code> is one of the on-reading words that nevertheless prefers
`お`. You will also see ご{f|返事|へんじ}, especially in older writing, but
お{f|返事|へんじ} is the safer choice today.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|私|わたし}のお{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|先生|せんせい}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|私|わたし}の{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|教師|きょうし}です。

Two things. The honorific `お` cannot describe your own job, so it must go. And
<code>{f|先生|せんせい}</code> is itself a respectful word - use
<code>{f|教師|きょうし}</code> about yourself.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|時間|じかん}がありますか。
(b) お{f|時間|じかん}、よろしいですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain question about availability. Between friends it is perfectly
normal; addressed to a customer or a superior it sounds blunt.

(b) treats the listener's time as something belonging to them and worth
honouring, which is why the `お` appears. Combined with よろしいですか it becomes
the standard way to ask a busy person for a moment - the prefix is carrying the
politeness that the grammar alone does not.

</details>
