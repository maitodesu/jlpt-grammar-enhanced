# くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately

**Meaning** · about, roughly - stuck onto an amount to say you are not being exact

`くらい` takes a number and loosens it. {f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}ぐらい is "about half
an hour", {f|千円|せんえん}ぐらい is "a thousand yen or so", これくらい is "about this
much". Whatever it attaches to has to be a **quantity or an extent** - a length of
time, a price, a headcount, a size - never a point on the clock or the calendar.

## Formation

`くらい` and `ぐらい` are the same word, and nothing about what comes in front
decides which one you say. It is a suffix: it goes straight onto the amount, with
nothing in between.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Number + counter | Amount + くらい／ぐらい | {f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん} → {f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}ぐらい |
| People | Number + {f|人|にん} + ぐらい | {f|十五人|じゅうごにん} → {f|十五人|じゅうごにん}ぐらい |
| Money | Price + ぐらい | {f|千円|せんえん} → {f|千円|せんえん}ぐらい |
| Amount word | Noun + くらい | {f|半分|はんぶん} → {f|半分|はんぶん}くらい |
| Demonstrative | これ／それ／あれ + くらい | これ → これくらい |
| Modifying a noun | ～くらいの + Noun | これ → これぐらいの{f|大|おお}きさ |
| Asking an amount | どのくらい／どれくらい | どのくらいかかりますか |
| Followed by a particle | ～くらい + で／から／まで | {f|五分|ごふん} → {f|五分|ごふん}ぐらいで |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `くらい` answers **how much? how long? how many?** If the phrase
answers **when?**, you need `ごろ` instead: <code>{f|三時間|さんじかん}ぐらい</code>
("about three hours") but <code>{f|三時|さんじ}ごろ</code> ("around three o'clock").

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - a quantity with `くらい` on it usually needs no particle at all. You say
<code>ビールを{f|三本|さんぼん}ぐらい{f|飲|の}みました</code>, not
❌<code>ビールを{f|三本|さんぼん}ぐらいを{f|飲|の}みました</code> - the amount sits in
front of the verb as an adverb, and `を` is already doing its work on ビール.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
コーヒーを{f|三杯|さんばい}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|飲|の}みました。
I drank about three cups of coffee.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|本|ほん}は{f|千円|せんえん}<b>ぐらい</b>です。
This book is about a thousand yen.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|教室|きょうしつ}に{f|学生|がくせい}が{f|十五人|じゅうごにん}<b>ぐらい</b>います。
There are about fifteen students in the classroom.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|空港|くうこう}まで、どの<b>くらい</b>かかりますか。
How long does it take to get to the airport?
</pre>

<pre>
これ<b>ぐらい</b>の{f|大|おお}きさの{f|箱|はこ}はありますか。
Do you have a box about this big?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|姉|あね}は{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|二時間|にじかん}<b>ぐらい</b>ピアノを{f|練習|れんしゅう}します。
My older sister practises the piano for about two hours a day.
</pre>

<pre>
すみません、あと{f|十分|じゅっぷん}<b>ぐらい</b>で{f|着|つ}きますので、{f|先|さき}に{f|注文|ちゅうもん}しておいてください。
Sorry - I'll be there in about ten minutes, so go ahead and order without me.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}のラーメンは、{f|昼|ひる}に{f|行|い}くと{f|二十分|にじゅっぷん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|並|なら}ばなければなりません。
If you go to this ramen place at lunchtime you have to queue for twenty minutes or so.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`くらい` is completely register-neutral. It is the same in casual speech and in a
business email, it is not marked as male or female, and adding it to a number never
makes you sound less serious - if anything the opposite, because Japanese
conversation prefers a hedged number to a bare one. Answering
<code>どのくらいかかりますか</code> with a flat
<code>{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}です</code> claims a precision you probably do not
have; <code>{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}ぐらいです</code> is what a native speaker says.

**`くらい` and `ぐらい` are the same word.** They mean the same thing, both are
correct everywhere, and no listener will notice which one you chose. The variation
is distributional rather than semantic: `ぐらい` is somewhat more common in speech
and especially after the demonstratives - これぐらい, どれぐらい - while `くらい`
turns up a little more often in careful writing. Those are tendencies, not rules.
Pick whichever comes out of your mouth, and stay consistent inside a sentence.

In writing, and in announcements read aloud, you will also meet
<code>{f|約|やく}</code>, which does the same job from the other side of the number.
It is treated in the Contrast section below.

One thing to expect later: the same word has a second job, measuring a **degree**
rather than an amount - <code>{f|泣|な}きたいくらいうれしい</code>, "so happy I could
cry" - along with a dismissive use that belittles a small task. That sense is a
separate grammar point at N3. If you meet a `くらい` that is not sitting on a number,
it is that other job, not this one.

## Contrast

### くらい／ぐらい vs ごろ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ごろ` blurs a **point in time**; `くらい` blurs an **amount**.
One character in the counter decides it: <code>{f|六時|ろくじ}</code> is a when,
<code>{f|六時間|ろくじかん}</code> is a how-long.

</div>

<pre>
{f|夕方|ゆうがた}{f|六時|ろくじ}<b>ごろ</b>{f|帰|かえ}ります。
I'll be home around six in the evening.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|六時間|ろくじかん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|寝|ね}ました。
I slept about six hours last night.
</pre>

Dates and days of the week are points in time too, so they take `ごろ`:
<code>{f|来週|らいしゅう}ごろ</code>, <code>{f|三月|さんがつ}ごろ</code>. Anything that
is counted rather than located - money, distance, weight, people, pages - can only
take `くらい`: ❌<code>{f|五人|ごにん}ごろ</code> is not Japanese.

### くらい vs {f|約|やく}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, opposite position, different register.
<code>{f|約|やく}</code> is a prefix and belongs to **written** Japanese; `ぐらい` is
a suffix and belongs to **speech**. Do not put both on one number.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}まで<b>{f|約|やく}</b>{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}かかります。
It is approximately thirty minutes to the venue.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}まで{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}<b>ぐらい</b>かかります。
It takes about half an hour to get to the venue.
</pre>

The first belongs on a printed leaflet or in a station announcement; the second is
what you say to a friend on the phone. <code>{f|約|やく}</code> only ever attaches to
a number, so there is no ❌<code>{f|約|やく}これ</code> to match これぐらい, and
stacking the two - ❌<code>{f|約|やく}{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}ぐらい</code> - hedges
the same number twice.

### Amount くらい vs degree くらい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if `くらい` sits on a **number or an amount**, it means "about".
If it sits on a **verb or an adjective**, it is measuring a degree, and "about" is
the wrong translation.

</div>

<pre>
{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|待|ま}ちました。
I waited about thirty minutes.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|立|た}てない<b>ぐらい</b>{f|疲|つか}れました。
I got so tired I couldn't stand up.
</pre>

The second sentence has no number in it at all: the clause
<code>{f|立|た}てない</code> is being used as a yardstick for how tired the speaker
was. That degree use, together with a dismissive "it's only ～" reading, is a
separate grammar point at N3. Here you only need to recognise the shape and not
translate it as "approximately".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|二週間|にしゅうかん}<b>ごろ</b>{f|休|やす}みます。 → ✅ {f|二週間|にしゅうかん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|休|やす}みます。
  <code>{f|二週間|にしゅうかん}</code> is a length of time, not a date. Only a point
  in time takes `ごろ`.

- ❌ <b>ぐらい</b>{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}かかります。 → ✅ {f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}<b>ぐらい</b>かかります。
  English puts "about" in front of the number; Japanese puts `ぐらい` behind it.
  `くらい` is a suffix and can never lead.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|三時間|さんじかん}ぐらい<b>に</b>{f|勉強|べんきょう}しました。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|三時間|さんじかん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|勉強|べんきょう}しました。
  A duration goes in front of the verb with no particle. The `に` of
  <code>{f|三時|さんじ}に</code> marks a point in time, and a span has no point to
  mark.

- ❌ {f|約|やく}{f|五千円|ごせんえん}<b>ぐらい</b>でした。 → ✅ {f|約|やく}{f|五千円|ごせんえん}でした。／{f|五千円|ごせんえん}<b>ぐらい</b>でした。
  Both words already mean "about". Use one of them, and let the register decide
  which.

## Practice

**1.** 「{f|駅|えき}から{f|会社|かいしゃ}まで、どの____かかりますか。」「{f|歩|ある}いて{f|十五分|じゅうごふん}____です。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

「どの<b>くらい</b>かかりますか。」「{f|十五分|じゅうごふん}<b>ぐらい</b>です。」

Both blanks are about a **length** of time, so both take `くらい`／`ぐらい`. Note how
the hedged answer is the natural one: a bare
<code>{f|十五分|じゅうごふん}です</code> would sound as though you had timed it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|明日|あした}は{f|九時|くじ}（ごろ・ぐらい）{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|着|つ}いて、{f|一時間|いちじかん}（ごろ・ぐらい）{f|準備|じゅんび}をします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|九時|くじ}<b>ごろ</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|着|つ}いて、{f|一時間|いちじかん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|準備|じゅんび}をします。

<code>{f|九時|くじ}</code> is a clock time - a point - so it takes `ごろ`.
<code>{f|一時間|いちじかん}</code> is a span of time, so it takes `ぐらい`. The two
counters differ by one character and choose different words.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "About twenty people came to the party."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

パーティーに{f|二十人|にじゅうにん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|来|き}ました。

The amount goes in front of the verb with `ぐらい` attached and no particle after
it. <code>{f|二十人|にじゅうにん}くらい</code> is equally correct - the two forms are
the same word.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|土曜日|どようび}ぐらいに{f|電話|でんわ}をします。{f|話|はなし}は{f|十分|じゅっぷん}ごろで{f|終|お}わると{f|思|おも}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|土曜日|どようび}<b>ごろ</b>{f|電話|でんわ}をします。{f|話|はなし}は{f|十分|じゅっぷん}<b>ぐらい</b>で{f|終|お}わると{f|思|おも}います。

The two words have swapped places. A day of the week is a point in time and needs
`ごろ`; ten minutes is a span and needs `ぐらい`. Note that `に` also drops away once
`ごろ` is there.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference:
(a) この{f|町|まち}に{f|十年|じゅうねん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|住|す}んでいます。
(b) この{f|町|まち}に{f|十年前|じゅうねんまえ}<b>ごろ</b>{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}してきました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) measures **how long** the speaker has lived here: roughly ten years, and they
are still here. <code>{f|十年|じゅうねん}</code> is a duration, so `ぐらい` is the
only choice.

(b) locates **when** they arrived: at some point around ten years ago, as a single
completed event. <code>{f|十年前|じゅうねんまえ}</code> is a moment on the calendar,
which is `ごろ` territory. The same number, doing two different jobs.

</details>
