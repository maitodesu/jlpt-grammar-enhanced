# を通じて / を通して

**Meaning** · by way of - through a go-between, a channel or an experience; or right through a stretch of time

Read <code>を{f|通|つう}じて</code> and <code>を{f|通|とお}して</code>. Same kanji, two readings, and
 - this is the honest part - very nearly the same meaning. Both put something in
the middle of you and your goal: a person who passes the message on, a medium
that carries it, an experience you came out the other side of. Both also mark a
period of time that a state holds right across.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (go-between) | Noun + を{f|通|つう}じて／を{f|通|とお}して | {f|友人|ゆうじん} → {f|友人|ゆうじん}を{f|通|つう}じて{f|知|し}り{f|合|あ}う |
| Noun (channel, medium) | as above | テレビ → テレビを{f|通|とお}して{f|知|し}る |
| Noun (experience) | as above | {f|経験|けいけん} → {f|経験|けいけん}を{f|通|とお}して{f|学|まな}ぶ |
| Noun (period, range) - throughout | as above | {f|一年|いちねん} → {f|一年|いちねん}を{f|通|つう}じて{f|暖|あたた}かい |
| Before a noun | Noun + を{f|通|つう}じての／を{f|通|とお}しての + Noun | ネット → ネットを{f|通|つう}じての{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}み |
| Physical passage (only {f|通|とお}して) | Noun + を{f|通|とお}して | カーテン → カーテンを{f|通|とお}して{f|光|ひかり}が{f|入|はい}る |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the last row is the one place the two really do split.
<code>を{f|通|とお}して</code> keeps the literal sense of the verb <code>{f|通|とお}す</code>, "to
pass something through", so light, sound and air can travel through things with
it. <code>を{f|通|つう}じて</code> has lost that literal meaning entirely.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|友人|ゆうじん}を<b>{f|通|つう}じて</b>{f|彼|かれ}に{f|連絡|れんらく}しました。
I got in touch with him through a friend.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|島|しま}は{f|一年|いちねん}を<b>{f|通|つう}じて</b>{f|暖|あたた}かいです。
This island is warm all year round.
</pre>

<pre>
インターネットを<b>{f|通|とお}して</b>、{f|世界中|せかいじゅう}の{f|人|ひと}と{f|話|はな}せる{f|時代|じだい}になった。
We've reached an age where you can talk to people all over the world via the internet.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|失敗|しっぱい}を<b>{f|通|とお}して</b>{f|学|まな}んだことのほうが、あとまで{f|残|のこ}る。
What you learn through failure is what stays with you.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|市|し}の{f|窓口|まどぐち}を<b>{f|通|つう}じて</b>{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}めば、{f|費用|ひよう}が{f|半分|はんぶん}で{f|済|す}みます。
If you apply via the city office counter, it costs only half as much.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|留学|りゅうがく}の{f|一年間|いちねんかん}を<b>{f|通|とお}して</b>、{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|考|かんが}え{f|方|かた}がずいぶん{f|変|か}わった。
Over the whole year I spent studying abroad, my way of thinking changed a good deal.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|通訳|つうやく}を<b>{f|通|とお}して</b>{f|話|はな}すと、どうしても{f|細|こま}かい{f|気持|きも}ちまでは{f|伝|つた}わりにくい。
When you speak through an interpreter, the finer feelings inevitably fail to carry.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|事件|じけん}のことは、{f|新聞|しんぶん}やテレビの{f|報道|ほうどう}を<b>{f|通|つう}じて</b>{f|知|し}った{f|人|ひと}がほとんどだろう。
Most people will have learned of that incident through newspaper and television coverage.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Both forms are a step above casual speech. When the "channel" is an ordinary
everyday tool, Japanese uses plain `で`: 「メールで{f|送|おく}ります」, not
「メールを{f|通|つう}じて{f|送|おく}ります」. Reach for these patterns when the point is that
something had to **pass through** an intermediary, or when you are writing.

The difference between the two is real but slight, and it is a matter of
tendency rather than rule. Where speakers do feel a difference:

- <code>を{f|通|とお}して</code> leans towards the subject **actively using** the medium,
  and towards experience: <code>{f|経験|けいけん}を{f|通|とお}して</code>,
  <code>{f|活動|かつどう}を{f|通|とお}して</code>, <code>{f|三年間|さんねんかん}を{f|通|とお}して</code>. It is a
  little more at home in speech.
- <code>を{f|通|つう}じて</code> leans towards **routing and connection**, and towards
  written and journalistic style: <code>マスコミを{f|通|つう}じて</code>,
  <code>{f|代理店|だいりてん}を{f|通|つう}じて</code>, <code>{f|一年|いちねん}を{f|通|つう}じて</code>.

Say plainly what is true: in the great majority of sentences either is
acceptable, native speakers disagree about which sounds better, and no examiner
can build a fair question on the choice alone. The one place you can be
confident is the literal physical sense - light through a window, a voice through
a wall - which belongs to <code>を{f|通|とお}して</code> only.

Before a noun, both need `の`: <code>{f|友人|ゆうじん}を{f|通|つう}じての{f|紹介|しょうかい}</code>.
This form is stiff and mostly appears in notices and forms.

## Contrast

### を{f|通|つう}じて vs を{f|通|とお}して

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - in the go-between, channel and time-span senses, treat them
as interchangeable. Prefer <code>を{f|通|とお}して</code> when the subject deliberately
works *through* something, and when the passage is physical.

</div>

<pre>
{f|部活|ぶかつ}を<b>{f|通|とお}して</b>、{f|人|ひと}に{f|頼|たよ}ることを{f|覚|おぼ}えた。
Through club activities I learned how to rely on other people.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大使館|たいしかん}を<b>{f|通|つう}じて</b>{f|正式|せいしき}に{f|申|もう}し{f|入|い}れがあった。
A formal approach was made through the embassy.
</pre>

Each sentence would survive the swap. The first has a person putting themselves
through an experience, which suits <code>{f|通|とお}して</code>; the second is a routed,
official message, which suits <code>{f|通|つう}じて</code>. Those are leanings, not laws.

The genuine split:

<pre>
すりガラスを<b>{f|通|とお}して</b>、{f|人影|ひとかげ}がぼんやりと{f|見|み}えた。
A figure showed dimly through the frosted glass.
</pre>

❌<code>すりガラスを{f|通|つう}じて</code> does not work - nothing is being routed, something
is physically passing through.

### を{f|通|とお}して vs で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `で` names the tool you used. These patterns name a
**path something took**, and they imply that the path mattered.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|件|けん}はメール<b>で</b>{f|知|し}らせておきました。
I let them know about yesterday's business by email.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|社長|しゃちょう}の{f|考|かんが}えは、{f|秘書|ひしょ}<b>を{f|通|とお}して</b>{f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|伝|つた}えられた。
The president's thinking was conveyed to the manager through his secretary.
</pre>

Replace `で` in the first sentence and it starts to sound as though the email
were a diplomatic channel. Replace <code>を{f|通|とお}して</code> in the second with
`で` and the secretary becomes an instrument rather than a person passing a
message on.

### を{f|通|つう}じて (a period) vs にわたって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with a stretch of time, <code>を{f|通|つう}じて</code> says a state held
at **every point inside** it. `にわたって` says an **event spanned** it from one
end to the other.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|地方|ちほう}は{f|冬|ふゆ}を<b>{f|通|つう}じて</b>{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|少|すく}ない。
This region gets little snow right through the winter.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いは{f|三日間|みっかかん}<b>にわたって</b>{f|続|つづ}けられた。
The talks went on over three days.
</pre>

The first describes a condition you could check on any given day; the second
measures how far a single continuous event reached. A one-off event with
<code>を{f|通|つう}じて</code> - ❌「{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|三日|みっか}を{f|通|つう}じて{f|開|ひら}かれた」 - sounds
wrong for exactly that reason.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}、{f|電話|でんわ}を<b>{f|通|つう}じて</b>{f|母|はは}と{f|話|はな}しました。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}、{f|電話|でんわ}<b>で</b>{f|母|はは}と{f|話|はな}しました。
  An ordinary tool takes `で`. The pattern would suggest the phone was an
  intermediary you had to go through to reach her.

- ❌ カーテンを<b>{f|通|つう}じて</b>{f|朝|あさ}の{f|光|ひかり}が{f|入|はい}ってきた。 → ✅ カーテンを<b>{f|通|とお}して</b>{f|朝|あさ}の{f|光|ひかり}が{f|入|はい}ってきた。
  Physical passage is <code>{f|通|とお}して</code> only.

- ❌ ネットを{f|通|つう}じて{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みは{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|開始|かいし}です。 → ✅ ネットを{f|通|つう}じて<b>の</b>{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みは{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|開始|かいし}です。
  Directly in front of a noun, both forms need `の`.

- ❌ {f|試験|しけん}は{f|二日|ふつか}を{f|通|つう}じて{f|行|おこな}われた。 → ✅ {f|試験|しけん}は{f|二日間|ふつかかん}<b>にわたって</b>{f|行|おこな}われた。
  A bounded event spanning a period wants `にわたって`. Save
  <code>を{f|通|つう}じて</code> for a state that holds all the way through.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|会社|かいしゃ}とは、{f|知|し}り{f|合|あ}いの{f|弁護士|べんごし}____{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いを{f|進|すす}めている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|知|し}り{f|合|あ}いの{f|弁護士|べんごし}<b>を{f|通|つう}じて</b>（or <b>を{f|通|とお}して</b>）{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いを{f|進|すす}めている。

The lawyer is a go-between: the two sides are not talking directly. Either form
is fine, with <code>{f|通|つう}じて</code> perhaps a shade more natural for a formal,
routed negotiation.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|障子|しょうじ}（を{f|通|つう}じて・を{f|通|とお}して）、{f|外|そと}の{f|明|あか}るさが{f|感|かん}じられる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|障子|しょうじ}<b>を{f|通|とお}して</b>、{f|外|そと}の{f|明|あか}るさが{f|感|かん}じられる。

Light physically passing through paper screens is the literal sense of
<code>{f|通|とお}す</code>, and <code>を{f|通|つう}じて</code> has no literal sense left. This is one of
the few places where the choice is genuinely determined.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I learned a lot about myself through the volunteer work."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ボランティア{f|活動|かつどう}<b>を{f|通|とお}して</b>、{f|自分|じぶん}について{f|多|おお}くのことを{f|学|まな}んだ。

Learning *through an experience* is the collocation <code>{f|通|とお}して</code> is most at
home in. <code>を{f|通|つう}じて</code> is not wrong, but essays and self-introductions
overwhelmingly use <code>{f|通|とお}して</code> in this frame.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|工事|こうじ}は{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}を{f|通|つう}じて{f|行|おこな}われました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|工事|こうじ}は{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}<b>にわたって</b>{f|行|おこな}われました。

The construction is a single event measured from one end of the week to the
other, which is what `にわたって` does. <code>を{f|通|つう}じて</code> would need a
continuing *state* - 「{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}を{f|通|つう}じて{f|天気|てんき}がよかった」 is fine.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) テレビ<b>で</b>その{f|歌手|かしゅ}を{f|知|し}った。
(b) テレビ<b>を{f|通|とお}して</b>その{f|歌手|かしゅ}を{f|知|し}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the ordinary way to say it: the television was where you happened to
come across the singer. Plain, spoken, no comment on the process.

(b) makes the television a **medium** and quietly draws attention to it - you
know the singer only as television presented them, not in person. It is the
version you would write in an essay about how media shapes what we know, and in
casual conversation it sounds a little heavy.

</details>
