# にかわって / にかわり

**Meaning** · in place of; standing in for; taking over from

Read <code>にかわって</code>, and written ～に{f|代|か}わって when kanji is used. Two
situations, one pattern: a **person** doing something that somebody else was
supposed to do, and a **thing** taking over the role another thing used to fill.

## Formation

The pattern attaches only to nouns. `にかわり` is the written form of exactly the
same thing.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (person) - filling in | Noun + にかわって | {f|父|ちち} → {f|父|ちち}にかわって{f|出席|しゅっせき}する |
| Noun (thing) - superseding | Noun + にかわって | ろうそく → ろうそくにかわって{f|電気|でんき}が{f|使|つか}われる |
| Written notices, reports | Noun + にかわり | {f|担当者|たんとうしゃ} → {f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}にかわりご{f|案内|あんない}します |
| Formal spoken, ceremonies | Noun + にかわりまして | {f|社長|しゃちょう} → {f|社長|しゃちょう}にかわりましてお{f|礼|れい}{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます |
| Before a noun | Noun + にかわる + Noun | {f|石油|せきゆ} → {f|石油|せきゆ}にかわるエネルギー |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the kanji is <code>代</code> (substitute), not <code>変</code> (change). <code>{f|変|か}わって</code>
is a different verb altogether and produces a different sentence; an IME will
happily offer you the wrong one. If you are unsure, kana is safe.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|父|ちち}<b>にかわって</b>{f|私|わたし}が{f|会|かい}に{f|出|で}ます。
I'll attend the meeting in my father's place.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さん<b>にかわって</b>{f|山口|やまぐち}さんが{f|司会|しかい}をします。
Yamaguchi will chair the session in place of Tanaka.
</pre>

<pre>
けがをした{f|選手|せんしゅ}<b>にかわって</b>、{f|一年生|いちねんせい}がコートに{f|入|はい}った。
A first-year went onto the court in place of the injured player.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|留守|るす}の{f|母|はは}<b>にかわり</b>、{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|受|う}け{f|取|と}っておきました。
I took delivery of the parcel on behalf of my mother, who was out.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|紙|かみ}の{f|辞書|じしょ}<b>にかわって</b>、スマホのアプリを{f|使|つか}う{f|学生|がくせい}が{f|増|ふ}えている。
Students are increasingly using phone apps in place of paper dictionaries.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|町|まち}では、{f|昔|むかし}からの{f|市場|いちば}<b>にかわって</b>{f|大|おお}きなスーパーが{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}の{f|中心|ちゅうしん}になった。
In this town a big supermarket has taken over from the old market as the centre of shopping.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本日|ほんじつ}は{f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>にかわりまして</b>、{f|私|わたし}{f|中村|なかむら}がご{f|説明|せつめい}いたします。
Today I, Nakamura, will give the presentation in the department manager's place.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|石油|せきゆ}<b>にかわる</b>エネルギーを{f|探|さが}す{f|研究|けんきゅう}が、{f|世界中|せかいじゅう}で{f|進|すす}められている。
Research into an energy source to replace oil is under way all over the world.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is **formal, mostly written** Japanese. Between friends it sounds like a
press release: to ask someone to watch your bag while you step out, you want
<code>{f|代|か}わりに</code>, not `にかわって`.

`にかわり` narrows the register further - notices, minutes, business email. And
`にかわりまして` is the ceremonial form you hear at weddings, funerals, opening
speeches and company presentations, where somebody speaks for an absent superior
or for a family. 「{f|家族|かぞく}にかわりまして、ひとことごあいさつ{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます」 is
about as fixed as a phrase gets.

The "thing supersedes thing" use belongs to news and essay writing, and it
almost always ends in a verb of change: {f|増|ふ}える, {f|広|ひろ}がる,
{f|使|つか}われるようになる, {f|中心|ちゅうしん}になる. A static predicate after it sounds
unfinished.

Note also the order. In <code>Aにかわって B</code>, **A is the one being replaced** and
B is the newcomer. Learners reverse this constantly, and because both halves are
plausible nouns the mistake produces a perfectly grammatical sentence that means
the opposite of what you intended.

## Contrast

### にかわって vs {f|代|か}わりに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same substitution meaning, but `にかわって` is formal and
attaches only to nouns, while <code>{f|代|か}わりに</code> is everyday, attaches to verbs
and adjectives too, and has a second sense - "in exchange for" - that
`にかわって` does not have at all.

</div>

<pre>
ちょっと{f|席|せき}を{f|外|はず}すので、{f|私|わたし}の<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>{f|電話|でんわ}に{f|出|で}てもらえますか。
I'm stepping out for a moment - could you take any calls for me?
</pre>

An office favour asked with 「{f|私|わたし}にかわって{f|電話|でんわ}に{f|出|で}てください」 is not
wrong, but it lands with the weight of an official handover.

The exchange sense is where the two genuinely part company:

<pre>
{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}しを{f|手伝|てつだ}う<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>、ごはんをおごってね。
I'll help you move - you're buying dinner in return.
</pre>

Nothing here is standing in for anything, so `にかわって` is simply unavailable.

### にかわって vs として

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `にかわって` says the person who should be doing this is
absent. `として` says you hold the role in your own right.

</div>

<pre>
{f|課長|かちょう}<b>にかわって</b>{f|報告|ほうこく}いたします。
I'll deliver the report in the section chief's place.
</pre>

<pre>
チームの{f|代表|だいひょう}<b>として</b>{f|報告|ほうこく}いたします。
I'll deliver the report as the team's representative.
</pre>

The first implies an empty chair; the second implies a title. Combining them is
possible and common in speeches - 「{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|代表|だいひょう}し、{f|社長|しゃちょう}にかわりましてごあいさつ{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます」 - precisely because they say different
things.

### AにかわってB vs AがBに{f|変|か}わる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the two patterns put opposite things in front of `に`. In
`にかわって`, the noun before `に` is what gets **replaced**. In
<code>～に{f|変|か}わる</code>, the noun before `に` is what something **turns into**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|現金|げんきん}<b>にかわって</b>カードで{f|払|はら}う{f|人|ひと}が{f|多|おお}くなった。
More and more people pay by card rather than cash.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|信号|しんごう}が{f|青|あお}<b>に{f|変|か}わった</b>ので、{f|車|くるま}が{f|動|うご}き{f|出|だ}した。
The light turned green, so the cars started moving.
</pre>

In the first, cash is on the way out. In the second, green is what has arrived.
Reading the second pattern into the first is the single most productive source of
back-to-front sentences with `にかわって`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|石炭|せきたん}が{f|石油|せきゆ}<b>にかわって</b>{f|使|つか}われるようになった。（meaning: oil replaced coal） → ✅ {f|石炭|せきたん}<b>にかわって</b>{f|石油|せきゆ}が{f|使|つか}われるようになった。
  The noun before `に` is the one being pushed out. As written, the first
  sentence says coal replaced oil.

- ❌ {f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}く<b>にかわって</b>、ネットで{f|注文|ちゅうもん}した。 → ✅ {f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}く<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>、ネットで{f|注文|ちゅうもん}した。
  `にかわって` takes a noun and nothing else. A verb needs
  <code>{f|代|か}わりに</code>.

- ❌ {f|皿|さら}を{f|洗|あら}ってあげる<b>にかわって</b>、アイスを{f|買|か}って。 → ✅ {f|皿|さら}を{f|洗|あら}ってあげる<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>、アイスを{f|買|か}って。
  There is no "in exchange for" sense in `にかわって`, even with a noun in front
  of it.

- ❌ ねえ、{f|私|わたし}<b>にかわって</b>ちょっと{f|見|み}ててくれない? → ✅ ねえ、{f|私|わたし}の<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>ちょっと{f|見|み}ててくれない?
  The grammar is fine but the register is not. Casual speech does not reach for
  `にかわって`, and note that <code>{f|代|か}わりに</code> needs `の` after a noun.

## Practice

**1.** {f|病気|びょうき}の{f|先生|せんせい}____、{f|校長|こうちょう}{f|先生|せんせい}が{f|話|はなし}をしてくださった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|病気|びょうき}の{f|先生|せんせい}<b>にかわって</b>、{f|校長|こうちょう}{f|先生|せんせい}が{f|話|はなし}をしてくださった。

The sick teacher was the one who should have spoken; the principal filled the
gap. Because the sentence is describing a school event in a slightly formal way,
`にかわって` fits better than <code>の{f|代|か}わりに</code> would.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|説明会|せつめいかい}は、{f|部長|ぶちょう}（にかわりまして・の{f|代|か}わりに）{f|私|わたし}がお{f|話|はな}しいたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>にかわりまして</b>{f|私|わたし}がお{f|話|はな}しいたします。

The rest of the sentence is in humble form (<code>お{f|話|はな}しいたします</code>), so it
is being said to an audience, not to a colleague. `にかわりまして` matches that
level; <code>の{f|代|か}わりに</code> would drop the register mid-sentence.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Buses have taken over from trams in this city."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|市|し}では、{f|路面|ろめん}{f|電車|でんしゃ}<b>にかわって</b>バスが{f|走|はし}るようになった。

Trams are the thing being replaced, so they go before `に`. The "thing
supersedes thing" use wants a verb of change at the end - here
<code>{f|走|はし}るようになった</code>; <code>{f|増|ふ}えた</code> would work too.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|兄|あに}にかわりに{f|私|わたし}が{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|取|と}りに{f|行|い}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|兄|あに}<b>にかわって</b>{f|私|わたし}が{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|取|と}りに{f|行|い}った。
✅ {f|兄|あに}の<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>{f|私|わたし}が{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|取|と}りに{f|行|い}った。

Two patterns have been blended into one. `にかわって` carries its own `に` and
takes nothing else; <code>{f|代|か}わりに</code> needs `の` after a noun. Since this is a
plain report about a brother, the second version is the one you would actually
say.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|母|はは}<b>にかわって</b>{f|私|わたし}が{f|挨拶|あいさつ}をしました。
(b) {f|母|はは}の<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>{f|私|わたし}が{f|挨拶|あいさつ}をしました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both say the mother did not speak and the child did, and both are correct
Japanese. The difference is entirely one of register and framing.

(a) sounds like a formal occasion - a ceremony, a funeral, a wedding - where
speaking for an absent family member is a recognised role. (b) is the plain
everyday report of what happened, the version you would give a friend
afterwards. In writing, (a); in conversation, (b).

</details>
