# ～ということだ / ～とのことだ

**Meaning** · I hear that ～; word is that ～ - relays information you received from somewhere else, with the wording kept at arm's length

Both endings hand a piece of information on. `ということだ` is the general relay:
whatever you were told, read or heard, repackaged as a report. `とのことだ` is the
message-carrier's version - it fits a phone call, an email or a note you are
passing to someone else, and it is the standard shape in business Japanese.

## Formation

Both attach to a **plain-form clause**. Nothing changes shape except the noun and
な-adjective slots.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + ということだ | {f|戻|もど}る → {f|戻|もど}るということだ |
| Verb (past) | た-form + ということだ | {f|届|とど}いた → {f|届|とど}いたということです |
| い-adjective | plain form + ということだ | {f|忙|いそが}しい → {f|忙|いそが}しいということだ |
| な-adjective | ～だ + ということだ | {f|不便|ふべん}だ → {f|不便|ふべん}だということだ |
| Noun | Noun + だ + ということだ | {f|中止|ちゅうし} → {f|中止|ちゅうし}だということです |
| Noun, with とのことだ | Noun + とのことだ | {f|中止|ちゅうし} → {f|中止|ちゅうし}とのことです |
| Polite past | … + ということでした / とのことでした | {f|遅|おく}れる → {f|遅|おく}れるとのことでした |
| Casual speech | … + ってことだ | {f|無理|むり}だ → {f|無理|むり}だってことだ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - before `とのことだ`, the `だ` after a noun is normally left out:
<code>{f|休|やす}みとのことです</code> rather than
<code>{f|休|やす}みだとのことです</code>. Before `ということだ` the `だ` is usually
there. This is a tendency in how the two are written, not a hard rule of grammar.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|社長|しゃちょう}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|戻|もど}る<b>ということです</b>。
I'm told the president is back next week.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}は5{f|時|じ}まで<b>とのことです</b>。
Reception is open until five, apparently.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}のお{f|話|はなし}では、{f|試験|しけん}の{f|範囲|はんい}は{f|変|か}わらない<b>ということだ</b>。
According to the teacher, the scope of the exam isn't changing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|林|はやし}さんから{f|連絡|れんらく}があって、{f|渋滞|じゅうたい}で{f|少|すこ}し{f|遅|おく}れる<b>とのことでした</b>。
Hayashi got in touch - he says he'll be a little late because of the traffic.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|歓迎会|かんげいかい}は{f|来月|らいげつ}に{f|延期|えんき}になった<b>ということなので</b>、みんなに{f|伝|つた}えておきます。
The welcome party has been put off until next month, I gather, so I'll let everyone know.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|客様|きゃくさま}は{f|明日|あした}の{f|午前中|ごぜんちゅう}にもう{f|一度|いちど}{f|電話|でんわ}をくださる<b>とのことです</b>。
The customer says she'll ring again tomorrow morning.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}に{f|確認|かくにん}したところ、{f|書類|しょるい}は{f|金曜日|きんようび}までに{f|出|だ}せば{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}う<b>とのことでした</b>。
I checked with the person in charge, and she said we'll be in time if we submit the papers by Friday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工事|こうじ}の{f|音|おと}がうるさいという{f|苦情|くじょう}が{f|何件|なんけん}か{f|来|き}ている<b>ということです</b>ので、{f|作業|さぎょう}の{f|時間|じかん}を{f|見直|みなお}しましょう。
Word is there have been several complaints about the noise from the building work, so let's reconsider the working hours.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Both are more formal than the everyday hearsay `そうだ`. `ということだ` is the
ending a news bulletin closes on and a report uses to attribute a fact; in speech
it sounds careful and slightly detached, which is exactly why it is useful when you
do not want to be held responsible for the content. `とのことだ` is narrower and
more human: somebody told you, and you are passing it along. In an office you will
hear <code>{f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|本日|ほんじつ}{f|直帰|ちょっき}とのことです</code> many
times a week.

In plain form, `ということだ` and `とのことだ` both read as written or announcement
Japanese. Casual speech contracts the first to `ってことだ` - 「あの{f|店|みせ}、
{f|今日|きょう}{f|休|やす}みだってことだよ」 - and often drops the frame altogether in
favour of a bare `って`: 「{f|休|やす}みだって」.

`ということだ` has a **second job** that shares the same form: drawing a conclusion,
"which means", "so what it amounts to is". It usually arrives with つまり or
{f|要|よう}するに and restates something already said rather than reporting news.

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}がないということは、{f|興味|きょうみ}がない<b>ということだ</b>。
No reply means they aren't interested.
</pre>

Context tells the two apart without much effort: a named source, or a fresh fact,
points to hearsay; a premise sitting right in front of it points to the conclusion
reading.

## Contrast

### ということだ vs そうだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same information, different formality - and one grammatical
difference: hearsay `そうだ` does not conjugate, while `ということだ` does.

</div>

Hearsay `そうだ` has exactly one shape. There is no ❌`そうでした`, no ❌`そうだった`
and no ❌`そうではない` in the hearsay sense; those forms all belong to the
*looks-like* `そうだ`. So when you need a past or a negative on the reporting frame
itself, `ということだ` is the pattern that has one.

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}{f|問|と}い{f|合|あ}わせたときは、{f|在庫|ざいこ}がまだある<b>ということでした</b>。
When I enquired yesterday, they said there was still stock.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|在庫|ざいこ}がまだある<b>そうです</b>。
Apparently there's still stock.
</pre>

The second is what you say to a colleague in passing. The first pins the report to
a moment in time, and only `ということだ` can do it.

### とのことだ vs ということだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `とのことだ` relays a **message from an identifiable person**,
usually one you received directly. `ということだ` covers that too, but also general
report, rumour and conclusion.

</div>

<pre>
{f|山|やま}の{f|上|うえ}の{f|旅館|りょかん}は{f|来年|らいねん}{f|閉|し}めるらしい、<b>ということだ</b>。
Word going round is that the inn up the mountain is closing next year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|予約|よやく}の{f|変更|へんこう}はメールでお{f|願|ねが}いしたい<b>とのことです</b>。
They'd like changes to bookings to be made by email, they said.
</pre>

Swapping them is rarely *ungrammatical*, but `とのことだ` on an anonymous rumour
sounds oddly official, and `ということだ` on a message you took two minutes ago
sounds needlessly distant.

### ということだ ("I hear") vs ということだ ("which means")

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if it introduces **new** information from a source, it is
hearsay. If it restates something **already** on the table, it is a conclusion.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|支店|してん}は{f|駅前|えきまえ}にできる<b>ということです</b>。
I hear the new branch is going up in front of the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅前|えきまえ}の{f|土地|とち}を{f|買|か}ったのなら、そこに{f|支店|してん}を{f|作|つく}る<b>ということですね</b>。
If they've bought the land by the station, that means they're going to build a branch there.
</pre>

The second one has its premise attached with `なら`, and it adds no facts - it just
spells out what follows. That is the conclusion use, and it is the one that turns up
in <code>ということは</code> at the head of a sentence.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}だそうでした。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}だということでした。
  Hearsay `そうだ` has no past form. When the reporting itself happened in the past,
  use `ということでした` or `とのことでした`.

- ❌ {f|部屋|へや}が{f|狭|せま}いだということです。 → ✅ {f|部屋|へや}が{f|狭|せま}いということです。
  い-adjectives never take `だ`. Only nouns and な-adjectives do, and only before
  `ということだ`.

- ❌ {f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|休|やす}むそうということです。 → ✅ {f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|休|やす}むということです。
  One hearsay marker per sentence. Stacking `そうだ` and `ということだ` reports the
  same second-hand status twice.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}はこの{f|案|あん}に{f|賛成|さんせい}だということです。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}はこの{f|案|あん}に{f|賛成|さんせい}です。
  The frame exists to put distance between you and the content. Your own position
  needs no distance.

## Practice

**1.** {f|受付|うけつけ}から{f|電話|でんわ}があって、お{f|客様|きゃくさま}がロビーで{f|待|ま}っている____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

…{f|待|ま}っている<b>とのことです</b>（or ということです）。

A specific person rang you with a specific message, so `とのことです` is the natural
relay. `ということです` is also correct, just a shade more impersonal.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|昨日|きのう}{f|聞|き}いたときは、{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|三月|さんがつ}に{f|終|お}わる（そうでした・ということでした）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨日|きのう}{f|聞|き}いたときは、{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|三月|さんがつ}に{f|終|お}わる<b>ということでした</b>。

The sentence puts the reporting in the past, and hearsay `そうだ` has no past form - 
❌`そうでした` is not available in this meaning. `ということでした` is.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The manager said she'd like the report by Thursday."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|木曜日|もくようび}までに{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}がほしい<b>とのことです</b>。

You are relaying a named person's message, which is what `とのことです` is for. In a
more distant report, <code>ほしいということです</code> works equally well.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|田中|たなか}さんによると、{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|静|しず}かだそうということです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|田中|たなか}さんによると、{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|静|しず}か<b>だということです</b>。

`そう` and `ということ` are both hearsay markers, and one is enough. Keep the `だ`
after the な-adjective and drop the `そう`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった<b>そうです</b>。
(b) {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった<b>とのことです</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is everyday hearsay: you heard it somewhere and you are mentioning it. It fits
conversation with a colleague on the stairs.

(b) reads as a message being passed on in an official capacity - somebody informed
you, and you are relaying the notification. It is what you would write in an email
to the team, and it quietly implies that the information is reliable and came
through a proper channel.

</details>
