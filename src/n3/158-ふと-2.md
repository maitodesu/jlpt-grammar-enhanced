# ふと - without meaning to

**Meaning** · unintentionally; by chance - of an action done with no purpose behind
it, and of what you happen to notice as a result

This is the same word as the `ふと` of thoughts arriving unbidden, pointed
outward. Here it modifies something you **did** - glancing up, stopping, saying a
thing - and its job is to strip the action of any intention.

## Usage

`ふと` stands directly in front of the verb. In this sense it selects small,
momentary, involuntary actions:

- {f|見|み}る・{f|見|み}{f|上|あ}げる - ふと{f|空|そら}を{f|見|み}{f|上|あ}げた。
- {f|目|め}が{f|合|あ}う - ふと{f|目|め}が{f|合|あ}って、{f|気|き}まずかった。
- {f|立|た}ち{f|止|ど}まる - ふと{f|立|た}ち{f|止|ど}まって{f|振|ふ}り{f|返|かえ}った。
- {f|口|くち}にする, {f|手|て}に{f|取|と}る, {f|足|あし}を{f|止|と}める

It also appears before a noun as `ふとした`, an adnominal form that means "chance,
trifling":

- ふとしたことから{f|知|し}り{f|合|あ}った。 - "we met through some small chance"
- ふとした{f|瞬間|しゅんかん}に{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}す

Two limits are worth stating plainly. First, the action has to be **small and
momentary**: you can ふと glance at a shop window, but not ふと buy the shop.
❌ふと{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|辞|や}めた is wrong, because quitting a job takes
decisions. Second, `ふと` implies no carelessness and no damage. It is neutral - 
for an unintended action with a bad outcome, Japanese uses うっかり.

Like the other sense, `ふと` is almost always narrated in the past.

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral in politeness, but stylistically marked: `ふと` belongs to
narration. It is common in novels, essays and anecdotes, and rarer in brisk
practical speech, where people simply say なんとなく ("for no particular reason")
or たまたま ("as it happened").

The word is quiet. It gives an action a drifting, unhurried feel, which is why
writers use it to open a scene - ふと{f|窓|まど}の{f|外|そと}を{f|見|み}ると… - and
why it sits oddly in a hurried context.

Note that `ふと` says nothing about the consequences. In the fourth example above
the remark caused trouble, but the trouble is carried by the rest of the sentence,
not by `ふと`. If you want the listener to hear that you were careless and are
sorry, you need うっかり.

`ふと` is written in kana, has no contraction, and is not marked for age or gender.

## Contrast

### ふと vs うっかり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both mean "without intending to", but うっかり adds
carelessness and a bad result. `ふと` is neutral and blames nobody.

</div>

<pre>
<b>うっかり</b>{f|傘|かさ}を{f|電車|でんしゃ}に{f|忘|わす}れてしまった。
I carelessly left my umbrella on the train.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>ふと</b>{f|窓|まど}の{f|外|そと}に{f|目|め}をやった。
I let my eyes drift to the window.
</pre>

うっかり goes with mistakes - forgetting, mixing things up, pressing the wrong
button - and typically appears with `てしまう`, which is itself the marker of
regret. `ふと` goes with harmless small actions and does not take `てしまう` for
that reason. Swapping them is immediately audible:
❌うっかり{f|空|そら}を{f|見|み}{f|上|あ}げた makes looking at the sky sound like an
error.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ふとパスワードを{f|間違|まちが}えた。 → ✅ <b>うっかり</b>パスワードを{f|間違|まちが}えた。
  A mistake with a consequence calls for the careless-action adverb. `ふと` says
  only that there was no intention, which is not what you want to convey when
  apologising.

- ❌ ふと{f|三時間|さんじかん}{f|散歩|さんぽ}した。 → ✅ <b>なんとなく</b>{f|三時間|さんじかん}{f|散歩|さんぽ}してしまった。
  `ふと` covers an instant, not a stretch of time. It marks the moment an action
  begins, so a three-hour activity is outside its range.

- ❌ ふとの{f|一言|ひとこと}が{f|問題|もんだい}になった。 → ✅ <b>ふとした</b>{f|一言|ひとこと}が{f|問題|もんだい}になった。
  Before a noun the form is `ふとした`, not ❌ふとの. As an adverb it modifies verbs
  only.

## Practice

**1.** {f|駅|えき}の{f|前|まえ}で____{f|立|た}ち{f|止|ど}まって、{f|来|き}た{f|道|みち}を{f|振|ふ}り{f|返|かえ}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|駅|えき}の{f|前|まえ}で<b>ふと</b>{f|立|た}ち{f|止|ど}まって、{f|来|き}た{f|道|みち}を{f|振|ふ}り{f|返|かえ}った。

Stopping for no reason the speaker could name is exactly the unintended, momentary
action this adverb marks, and the past-tense narration is typical of it.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: （ふと・うっかり）{f|大事|だいじ}なファイルを{f|消|け}してしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>うっかり</b>{f|大事|だいじ}なファイルを{f|消|け}してしまった。

Deleting an important file is a mistake with a cost, and `てしまう` at the end is
already signalling regret. `ふと` would strangely report the deletion as an idle
gesture with no consequence attached.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ふとした{f|質問|しつもん}から{f|話|はなし}が{f|広|ひろ}がった。
(b) ある{f|質問|しつもん}から{f|話|はなし}が{f|広|ひろ}がった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (b) simply says a question started the conversation. Sentence (a) adds
that the question was a trivial one, asked with no plan behind it - and the
implication is that the interesting turn the conversation took was pure accident.
`ふとした` is doing that work in one word.

</details>
