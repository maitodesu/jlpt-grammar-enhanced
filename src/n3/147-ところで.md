# ところで

**Meaning** · by the way - putting the current topic down and picking up a new one

`ところで` is a signal, not a link. It tells the listener that what follows has
nothing to do with what came before, and that the speaker is doing it on purpose.
Nine times out of ten, what follows is a question the speaker has been sitting on.

## Usage

`ところで` goes at the **head of a sentence**, followed by a comma or a pause. It
does not attach to a word, does not conjugate, and never appears in the middle of
a clause.

- ところで、{f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|旅行|りょこう}はどうなりましたか。
- ところで、あの{f|話|はなし}、{f|覚|おぼ}えてる?

Two things it is *not*. It is not "however" - it contradicts nothing, so
❌ところで、{f|私|わたし}は{f|反対|はんたい}です is wrong where `しかし` was wanted.
And it is not the `ところ` family of grammar points: the <code>～たところで</code> of
{f|急|いそ}いだところで{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わない ("even if you hurry…") and the
<code>～ているところ</code> of {f|今|いま}{f|食|た}べているところです ("in the middle of
eating") are separate constructions that merely look alike. The `ところで` of this
page always begins a sentence and never follows a verb.

Because the whole point of the word is to close one subject and open another, it
usually appears once in a conversation, at a natural seam - after a topic has run
its course, not on top of someone else who is still talking.

<pre>
<b>ところで</b>、{f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|元気|げんき}?
By the way, how's Yamada doing?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>ところで</b>、{f|昼|ひる}ごはんはもう{f|食|た}べましたか。
By the way, have you had lunch yet?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|資料|しりょう}は{f|以上|いじょう}です。<b>ところで</b>、{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|何時|なんじ}からでしたか。
That's everything in the handouts. By the way, what time was next week's meeting again?
</pre>

<pre>

Thanks for the photos - they came out really well. By the way, roughly how much was that camera?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ところで` is register-neutral in itself: it works in ます-form speech and in plain
speech, and it appears in writing wherever a writer changes tack. What is not
neutral is the act it performs. Announcing a change of subject is a small claim on
the floor, so it lands most comfortably when you have the standing to make it - 
among equals, or from whoever is hosting the conversation.

Speaking to a customer or someone well above you, the same move is usually padded:
{f|話|はなし}は{f|変|か}わりますが、… or それはそうと、… or simply
{f|一|ひと}つお{f|伺|うかが}いしてもよろしいでしょうか. `ところで` itself is not
rude - it is used in business meetings constantly - but it is brisk, and dropped
straight after someone has said something emotionally weighty it can sound as
though you were not listening.

There is no casual contraction of it, and it is not marked for age or gender.

## Contrast

### ところで vs ちなみに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ところで` drops the current topic. `ちなみに` stays on it and
adds a footnote.

</div>

<pre>
このカフェ、{f|安|やす}くておいしいよ。<b>ちなみに</b>、{f|夜|よる}は{f|十時|じゅうじ}までやってる。
This café is cheap and good. Incidentally, it's open until ten at night.
</pre>

<pre>
このカフェ、{f|安|やす}くておいしいよ。<b>ところで</b>、{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}しの{f|準備|じゅんび}は{f|進|すす}んでる?
This café is cheap and good. Anyway - how's the packing for your move coming along?
</pre>

Swapping them breaks both sentences: the opening hours are plainly still about the
café, and the packing plainly is not. A quick test - if the new sentence would
also work with そういえば in front of it, `ところで` is the right choice.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ところで、この{f|案|あん}には{f|賛成|さんせい}できません。 → ✅ <b>しかし</b>、この{f|案|あん}には{f|賛成|さんせい}できません。
  `ところで` changes the subject; it does not disagree. Learners reach for it
  because it looks like a conjunction, but it carries no adversative meaning at all.

- ❌ {f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}たところで、{f|晩|ばん}ごはんは{f|何|なに}にする?（meaning "by the way"）→ ✅ {f|映画|えいが}、{f|面白|おもしろ}かったね。<b>ところで</b>、{f|晩|ばん}ごはんは{f|何|なに}にする?
  Placed after a verb, the same syllables become the pattern meaning "even if", and
  the sentence turns into "even if I watch the film…". Keep it at the front.

- ❌ ところでですが、… → ✅ ところで、…
  `ところで` already contains everything it needs, and `ですが` does not politen it.
  If you want a politer version, replace the whole word with
  {f|話|はなし}は{f|変|か}わりますが.

## Practice

**1.** ____、{f|田中|たなか}さんの{f|連絡先|れんらくさき}を{f|知|し}っていますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>ところで</b>、{f|田中|たなか}さんの{f|連絡先|れんらくさき}を{f|知|し}っていますか。

A question with no connection to the previous topic needs a marker warning the
listener to switch tracks, and that is exactly the job this word does.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural connector:
{f|明日|あした}の{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|市民|しみん}ホールです。（ところで・ちなみに）、{f|駐車場|ちゅうしゃじょう}は{f|有料|ゆうりょう}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>ちなみに</b>、{f|駐車場|ちゅうしゃじょう}は{f|有料|ゆうりょう}です。

The parking charge is a further detail about the same venue, so the topic has not
changed at all. `ところで` would promise the listener something unrelated and then
fail to deliver it.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ところで、{f|部長|ぶちょう}、そろそろ{f|失礼|しつれい}してもよろしいでしょうか。
(b) {f|話|はなし}は{f|変|か}わりますが、{f|部長|ぶちょう}、そろそろ{f|失礼|しつれい}してもよろしいでしょうか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both change the subject and both are grammatical. Sentence (a) is brisk: the
speaker announces the switch in one word and moves on, which is fine between
colleagues but a little bald when addressing a superior. Sentence (b) spends a few
extra syllables acknowledging that a switch is being made, and that small detour
is what makes it sound considerate.

</details>
