# ～きれない

**Meaning** · there is too much of it to get through; more than one can manage

`～きれない` is the negative potential of {f|切|き}る in its "do it all" sense.
It does not say you are incapable of the action - it says the **amount defeats
you**. You can eat; there is simply more food than you can eat. You can count; there
are simply more stars than you can count.

## Formation

`きれない` attaches to the **ます-stem**. It is already a negative potential, so it
never takes a second potential ending.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + きれない | {f|食|た}べます → {f|食|た}べきれない |
| する-verb | Noun + し + きれない | {f|説明|せつめい}します → {f|説明|せつめい}しきれない |
| Polite | ～きれません | {f|覚|おぼ}えきれません |
| Past | ～きれなかった | {f|読|よ}みきれなかった |
| Before a noun (with ほど/くらい) | ～きれない + ほど / くらい + の | {f|数|かぞ}えきれないほどの{f|星|ほし} |
| Affirmative counterpart | ～きれる | {f|一人|ひとり}で{f|運|はこ}びきれる |
| Literary negative て-form | ～きれず(に) | {f|待|ま}ちきれずに{f|出|で}かけた |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the affirmative <code>きれる</code> exists but is far less common than
the negative. When you mean "I managed to finish it", the ordinary
<code>{f|食|た}べきった</code> is what people actually say;
<code>{f|食|た}べきれた</code> is reserved for contexts where the *capacity* is the
point（{f|一人|ひとり}でも{f|食|た}べきれる{f|量|りょう}です）. The pattern is
lopsided, and it is worth learning it as a negative first.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|料理|りょうり}が{f|多|おお}すぎて、<b>{f|食|た}べきれない</b>。
There's too much food - I can't finish it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|漢字|かんじ}が{f|多|おお}くて、{f|一日|いちにち}では<b>{f|覚|おぼ}えきれない</b>。
There are so many kanji that I can't learn them all in one day.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|空|そら}には<b>{f|数|かぞ}えきれない</b>ほどの{f|星|ほし}が{f|光|ひか}っていた。
There were countless stars shining in the sky.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|旅行|りょこう}が{f|楽|たの}しみで、<b>{f|待|ま}ちきれない</b>。
I'm so looking forward to next week's trip that I can hardly wait.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|荷物|にもつ}が{f|多|おお}くて{f|一度|いちど}では<b>{f|運|はこ}びきれない</b>ので、{f|二回|にかい}に{f|分|わ}けます。
There's too much to carry in one trip, so I'll split it into two.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|世話|せわ}になった{f|方々|かたがた}への{f|感謝|かんしゃ}は、{f|言葉|ことば}では<b>{f|伝|つた}えきれません</b>。
I can't put into words how grateful I am to everyone who looked after me.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|資料|しりょう}が{f|多|おお}すぎて{f|会議|かいぎ}までに<b>{f|読|よ}みきれなかった</b>ので、{f|要点|ようてん}だけまとめてもらった。
There was too much material to get through before the meeting, so I had someone summarise the key points.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}では<b>{f|抱|かか}えきれない</b>{f|問題|もんだい}を、{f|何年|なんねん}も{f|誰|だれ}にも{f|言|い}わずにいたそうだ。
Apparently he kept a problem too big for one person to handle to himself for years, without telling anyone.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The core meaning is **capacity overrun**, and it is the amount that overruns, not
your skill. That distinction does real work in a sentence:
{f|読|よ}みきれない means the book was too long, while
{f|読|よ}めない would mean you cannot read it at all - bad eyesight, wrong language,
handwriting you cannot decipher. Learners who use `きれない` for lack of ability end
up claiming that a two-line note is too long for them.

`きれない` is register-neutral and it turns up constantly in one particular shape:
<code>～きれないほど</code>, "so many/much that you couldn't ～".
{f|数|かぞ}えきれないほど is the standard Japanese for "countless", and
{f|食|た}べきれないほどの{f|料理|りょうり} is what you say about a table that groans.

Two idiomatic uses do not follow the "too much" template, and both are worth
knowing. {f|待|ま}ちきれない is eager anticipation - the waiting exceeds what you can
bear - and it is a warm, positive expression.
{f|信|しん}じきれない and {f|割|わ}りきれない (in its emotional sense) describe a
feeling you cannot fully settle into; they are more literary.

In writing you will also meet the て-form <code>きれず(に)</code>:
{f|我慢|がまん}しきれずに{f|笑|わら}ってしまった. That is standard written Japanese
and sounds slightly formal in speech.

## Contrast

### ～きれない vs the plain potential negative

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>られない</code> denies the **ability**. `きれない` grants
the ability and denies that it is **enough for the quantity**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|辛|から}い{f|物|もの}が{f|苦手|にがて}なので、このカレーは<b>{f|食|た}べられない</b>。
I'm no good with spicy food, so I can't eat this curry.
</pre>

<pre>
おいしいけれど{f|量|りょう}が{f|多|おお}くて、このカレーは<b>{f|食|た}べきれない</b>。
It's delicious, but there's so much of it that I can't finish this curry.
</pre>

In the first sentence the speaker cannot eat one spoonful. In the second they have
been eating happily and have simply run out of room. Notice that `きれない`
presupposes the action is under way or possible - which is why it fits so naturally
with a half-empty plate.

### ～きれない vs ～すぎる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>すぎる</code> describes **the thing**: there is too much of
it. `きれない` describes **the person**: they cannot get to the end of it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}の{f|宿題|しゅくだい}は<b>{f|多|おお}すぎる</b>。
There's far too much homework today.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}の{f|宿題|しゅくだい}は{f|夜|よる}までに<b>やりきれない</b>。
I won't get through today's homework by tonight.
</pre>

They are two views of the same problem and they combine constantly - the commonest
frame in the whole pattern is <code>～すぎて～きれない</code>:
{f|多|おお}すぎて{f|食|た}べきれない. Using both is not redundant; the first names
the cause and the second the consequence.

### ～きれない vs ～きった

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - these are the two halves of one pattern.
<code>きった</code> = got through the whole lot. `きれない` = could not.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大|おお}きなピザだったが、{f|三人|さんにん}で<b>{f|食|た}べきった</b>。
It was a big pizza, but the three of us polished it off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大|おお}きなピザで、{f|二人|ふたり}では<b>{f|食|た}べきれなかった</b>。
It was a big pizza, and the two of us couldn't finish it.
</pre>

The asymmetry to remember is that the affirmative side normally uses the **plain**
form <code>{f|食|た}べきる / {f|食|た}べきった</code>, not the potential
<code>{f|食|た}べきれる</code>, while the negative side almost always uses the
**potential** <code>{f|食|た}べきれない</code> rather than the plain
<code>{f|食|た}べきらない</code>. Both plain negatives exist, but they sound
deliberate - "doesn't finish it" rather than "can't finish it".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|食|た}べる<b>きれない</b> → ✅ {f|食|た}べ<b>きれない</b>
  The suffix goes on the ます-stem.

- ❌ {f|食|た}べ<b>きれられない</b> → ✅ {f|食|た}べ<b>きれない</b>
  <code>きれない</code> is already the potential negative. Adding another potential
  ending doubles it.

- ❌ この{f|字|じ}は{f|小|ちい}さくて<b>{f|読|よ}みきれない</b>。 → ✅ この{f|字|じ}は{f|小|ちい}さくて<b>{f|読|よ}めない</b>。
  Small print is a problem of ability, not of quantity. `きれない` needs an amount to
  fall short of.

- ❌ {f|数|かぞ}えきれない<b>の</b>{f|星|ほし} → ✅ {f|数|かぞ}えきれない<b>ほどの</b>{f|星|ほし}
  <code>きれない</code> is a negative い-adjective form, so it modifies a noun
  directly（{f|数|かぞ}えきれない{f|星|ほし}）or through
  <code>ほどの</code>. It never takes a bare `の`.

- ❌ {f|来週|らいしゅう}のライブが<b>{f|待|ま}ちきれる</b>! → ✅ {f|来週|らいしゅう}のライブが<b>{f|待|ま}ちきれない</b>!
  The excited "I can't wait!" is the negative. The affirmative would mean you are
  perfectly capable of waiting, which rather kills the mood.

## Practice

**1.** パーティーの{f|料理|りょうり}が{f|多|おお}すぎて、{f|全部|ぜんぶ}は{f|食|た}べ____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|全部|ぜんぶ}は{f|食|た}べ<b>きれなかった</b>。

<code>{f|多|おお}すぎて</code> names the cause and <code>きれない</code> names the
consequence - the standard pairing. The past
<code>きれなかった</code> is needed because the party is over.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|目|め}が{f|悪|わる}くて、{f|遠|とお}くの{f|字|じ}が（{f|読|よ}めない・{f|読|よ}みきれない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|目|め}が{f|悪|わる}くて、{f|遠|とお}くの{f|字|じ}が<b>{f|読|よ}めない</b>。

Poor eyesight removes the ability altogether, which is the plain potential negative.
`きれない` would claim that the text is too long to finish, and it says nothing about
eyes.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I got so many messages that I couldn't reply to them all."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

メッセージが{f|多|おお}すぎて、{f|全部|ぜんぶ}には<b>{f|返事|へんじ}しきれなかった</b>。

<code>{f|返事|へんじ}する</code> is a する-verb, so the stem is
<code>{f|返事|へんじ}し</code>. The sentence is about volume defeating you rather
than about not knowing how to reply, which is precisely `きれない`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: おみやげが{f|多|おお}くて、{f|一|いっ}{f|回|かい}では{f|持|も}ちきれられませんでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ おみやげが{f|多|おお}くて、{f|一|いっ}{f|回|かい}では{f|持|も}ち<b>きれませんでした</b>。

<code>きれる</code> already carries the potential meaning, so
<code>きれられる</code> stacks two potentials. The polite negative past is simply
<code>きれませんでした</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|本|ほん}は{f|一日|いちにち}では{f|読|よ}め<b>ない</b>。
(b) この{f|本|ほん}は{f|一日|いちにち}では{f|読|よ}み<b>きれない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says reading it in a day is beyond you - which could be because it is long, but
could equally be because it is in a language you do not have, or too difficult. The
sentence does not say which.

(b) narrows it to volume. You can read the book perfectly well; there are just more
pages than fit into a day. It implicitly grants that you would get through it given
longer, which (a) does not.

</details>
