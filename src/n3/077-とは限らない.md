# ～とは限らない

**Meaning** · not necessarily ～; ～ isn't always the case - denies that a general rule holds every time

Read as *とは かぎらない*. The verb {f|限|かぎ}る means "to be limited to", so
<code>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> literally says "things are not limited to that
outcome". You use it when somebody - the listener, common sense, or the speaker's
own earlier sentence - is treating something as a rule, and you want to point out
the exceptions without denying the rule's usual force.

## Formation

It attaches to a whole **plain-form clause**, because what it denies is a whole
proposition, not a word.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + とは{f|限|かぎ}らない | {f|勝|か}つ → {f|勝|か}つとは{f|限|かぎ}らない |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + とは{f|限|かぎ}らない | {f|来|こ}ない → {f|来|こ}ないとは{f|限|かぎ}らない |
| い-adjective | plain form + とは{f|限|かぎ}らない | {f|正|ただ}しい → {f|正|ただ}しいとは{f|限|かぎ}らない |
| な-adjective | ～だ + とは{f|限|かぎ}らない | {f|安全|あんぜん}だ → {f|安全|あんぜん}だとは{f|限|かぎ}らない |
| Noun | Noun (+ だ) + とは{f|限|かぎ}らない | {f|本当|ほんとう} → {f|本当|ほんとう}だとは{f|限|かぎ}らない |
| Polite | … + とは{f|限|かぎ}りません | {f|届|とど}く → {f|届|とど}くとは{f|限|かぎ}りません |
| With the usual partner adverb | {f|必|かなら}ずしも + … + とは{f|限|かぎ}らない | {f|安|やす}い → {f|必|かなら}ずしも{f|安|やす}いとは{f|限|かぎ}らない |
| Double-negative variant | ～ない + とも{f|限|かぎ}らない | {f|起|お}こらない → {f|起|お}こらないとも{f|限|かぎ}らない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - {f|必|かなら}ずしも is not required, but it is the adverb the pattern
expects, and it makes the "not always" reading unmistakable. In written argument the
pair <code>{f|必|かなら}ずしも～とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> is close to a fixed phrase.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral, with a slight lean towards careful speech and writing. It is the
standard way to introduce a caveat in an argument, so it fills newspaper columns,
textbooks and advice of every kind, and it is equally at home in conversation when
you want to warn someone off an assumption.

Socially it is gentle. Because it denies only universality, not the claim itself, it
lets you push back without contradicting anyone flatly: "true as a rule, not true
every time." <code>{f|必|かなら}ずしもそうとは{f|限|かぎ}りません</code> is a stock polite
disagreement in a meeting, and it is much softer than
<code>そんなわけがありません</code>.

The variant <code>～ないとも{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> deserves attention, because it is a
double negative and learners routinely read it backwards. Literally "it is not
limited to not happening", it means "it might well happen - you never know", and it
almost always introduces a **risk** worth preparing for:
<code>{f|地震|じしん}が{f|起|お}きないとも{f|限|かぎ}らない</code>. If a sentence with
`とも限らない` seems to be arguing for complacency, you have the polarity the wrong
way round.

One boundary: the pattern needs something general to push against - a rule, a
tendency, a "just because" clause, an *everyone* or an *always*. Applied to a plain
one-off prediction it sounds oddly stiff, and <code>かもしれません</code> or
<code>とは{f|思|おも}えません</code> is what a speaker would actually use.

## Contrast

### とは{f|限|かぎ}らない vs わけではない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> denies that something is **always**
so. `わけではない` denies that a conclusion **follows in this case**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|人気|にんき}のある{f|店|みせ}がいつも{f|安|やす}い<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。
Popular shops aren't always cheap.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|店|みせ}が{f|安|やす}い<b>わけではない</b>。ただ{f|近|ちか}いから{f|通|かよ}っているだけだ。
It's not that the shop is cheap - I go there simply because it's close.
</pre>

The first is about shops in general and could open a paragraph of consumer advice.
The second is about one shop and one listener's mistaken assumption. Where a
sentence is about a general rule, the two patterns often both fit, and
<code>{f|必|かなら}ずしも</code> can appear with either.

### とは{f|限|かぎ}らない vs ～ないとも{f|限|かぎ}らない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>Xとは{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> = "X is not always so".
<code>Xないとも{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> = "it's not impossible that X happens" - a
warning.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}{f|晴|は}れる<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。
It won't necessarily be fine tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|急|きゅう}に{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}らない<b>とも{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>から、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っていきなさい。
It's not out of the question that it'll suddenly rain, so take an umbrella.
</pre>

The first removes a guarantee. The second raises a possibility you had dismissed,
and it points forward to a precaution. Note the different attitudes: `とは限らない`
dampens optimism, `とも限らない` disturbs complacency.

### とは{f|限|かぎ}らない vs かもしれない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `かもしれない` floats **one possibility**.
<code>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> corrects **someone's generalisation**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}の{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になる<b>かもしれない</b>。
Tomorrow's match might be called off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ったら{f|必|かなら}ず{f|中止|ちゅうし}になる<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。
Rain doesn't automatically mean the match is off.
</pre>

The first adds a new thought to the conversation. The second takes an existing
belief - rain means cancellation - and puts a limit on it. If nobody in the
conversation holds the belief, `とは限らない` has nothing to work on.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|道|みち}が{f|安全|あんぜん}な<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。 → ✅ この{f|道|みち}が{f|安全|あんぜん}<b>だとは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。
  What comes before `と` is a full sentence, not a noun modifier, so a な-adjective
  keeps its `だ` rather than turning into `な`.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}く<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}りません</b>。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}くかどうか、まだ{f|分|わ}かりません。
  Your own undecided plans are not a general rule with exceptions. The pattern is
  for correcting what is taken to be always true.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|来|こ}ない<b>とも{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>から、{f|準備|じゅんび}しなくてもいい。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|来|こ}ない<b>とも{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>から、{f|準備|じゅんび}しておこう。
  The double negative means "he may well turn up after all". That is a reason to get
  ready, not a reason to relax.

- ❌ {f|必|かなら}ずしも{f|安|やす}い<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らないではない</b>。 → ✅ {f|必|かなら}ずしも{f|安|やす}い<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。
  <code>{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> is already the negative of {f|限|かぎ}る. Adding a
  second negative on the end leaves the sentence saying the opposite of your point.

## Practice

**1.** {f|有名|ゆうめい}な{f|大学|だいがく}を{f|出|で}た{f|人|ひと}が{f|仕事|しごと}ができる____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|仕事|しごと}ができる<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。

The sentence takes a widely held generalisation - good university, capable worker - 
and denies that it holds every time. Adding {f|必|かなら}ずしも in front would make
the reading even clearer.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|夜道|よみち}は{f|危|あぶ}ないので、{f|何|なに}も{f|起|お}こらない（とは{f|限|かぎ}らない・とも{f|限|かぎ}らない）から、{f|気|き}をつけて。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|何|なに}も{f|起|お}こらない<b>とも{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>から、{f|気|き}をつけて。

The speaker is raising a risk - something could happen - and that is the
<code>ないとも{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> shape. <code>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> would say
"nothing happening isn't always the case", which is a clumsy way to state the same
thing and does not sit well with the warning.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Just because a book sells well doesn't mean it's well written."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

よく{f|売|う}れている{f|本|ほん}が、いい{f|文章|ぶんしょう}で{f|書|か}かれている<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}りません</b>。

A generalisation is being limited, so <code>とは{f|限|かぎ}りません</code>. You could
open with <code>{f|売|う}れているからといって</code> instead, and the ending would stay
the same.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|上手|じょうず}な{f|人|ひと}が{f|教|おし}えるのが{f|得意|とくい}なとは{f|限|かぎ}らない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|上手|じょうず}な{f|人|ひと}が{f|教|おし}えるのが{f|得意|とくい}<b>だとは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。

<code>{f|得意|とくい}</code> is a な-adjective, but here it ends a clause rather than
modifying a noun, so it keeps `だ` before `と`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|説明書|せつめいしょ}の{f|通|とお}りにやれば{f|直|なお}る<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。
(b) {f|説明書|せつめいしょ}の{f|通|とお}りにやっても{f|直|なお}らない<b>かもしれない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is aimed at a belief: you think following the manual guarantees a fix, and the
speaker is removing the guarantee while granting that the manual usually works. It
argues.

(b) simply raises a possibility about this attempt. Nobody's assumption is being
corrected, and the sentence would be equally natural said to yourself. In practice
(a) is what a senior colleague says to set expectations; (b) is what you mutter with
the screwdriver in your hand.

</details>
