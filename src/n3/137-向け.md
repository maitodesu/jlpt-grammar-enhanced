# ～向け

**Meaning** · intended for; aimed at - made, written, priced or organised with that audience or market in mind（むけ）

<code>{f|向|む}け</code> comes from <code>{f|向|む}ける</code>, "to point something at".
Somebody has aimed this thing at somebody: a programme at children, a car at the
export market, a seminar at second-year students. The word carries **intention**,
and that is its whole meaning.

It says nothing about whether the aim succeeded. A textbook can be
<code>{f|初心者|しょしんしゃ}{f|向|む}け</code> and still be far too hard for a
beginner - the publisher aimed, and missed. Judging the fit is the job of
<code>{f|向|む}き</code>.

## Formation

Attaches to a bare noun naming an audience, a market, a region or a customer type.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (audience), as predicate | Noun + {f|向|む}けだ | {f|海外|かいがい} → この{f|商品|しょうひん}は{f|海外|かいがい}{f|向|む}けだ |
| Noun, modifying a noun | Noun + {f|向|む}けの + Noun | {f|子|こ}ども → {f|子|こ}ども{f|向|む}けの{f|番組|ばんぐみ} |
| Noun, modifying a verb | Noun + {f|向|む}けに + verb | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}{f|向|む}けに{f|作|つく}る |
| Market or destination | {f|輸出|ゆしゅつ}・{f|国内|こくない} + {f|向|む}け | {f|輸出|ゆしゅつ} → {f|輸出|ゆしゅつ}{f|向|む}けの{f|車|くるま} |
| Contrasted pair | A{f|向|む}けとB{f|向|む}け | {f|法人|ほうじん} → {f|法人|ほうじん}{f|向|む}けと{f|個人|こじん}{f|向|む}け |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|向|む}けに</code> before a verb,
<code>{f|向|む}けの</code> before a noun, <code>{f|向|む}けだ</code> at the end of the
sentence. There is no <code>{f|向|む}けな</code>.

</div>

{f|会社|かいしゃ}が{f|学生|がくせい}<b>{f|向|む}け</b>の{f|説明会|せつめいかい}を{f|開|ひら}いた。

</pre>

</pre>

For the domestic market and for overseas, the manual that comes with the same product is a completely different thickness.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The word itself is register-neutral, but its **habitat** is not. You will meet it
in product descriptions, publishers' catalogues, course listings, TV schedules,
press releases and company presentations, far more often than in casual chat. That
is not because it sounds formal - it is because talking about target audiences is
mostly a business activity.

In conversation it turns up whenever the topic is products or content:
<code>これ、{f|大人|おとな}{f|向|む}けだよね</code> is perfectly ordinary spoken
Japanese. What you would not do is use it about your own private arrangements - 
see the contrast with <code>のために</code> below.

A few collocations are worth having ready-made, because they are how the pattern
actually appears in the wild: <code>{f|海外|かいがい}{f|向|む}け</code>,
<code>{f|国内|こくない}{f|向|む}け</code>, <code>{f|輸出|ゆしゅつ}{f|向|む}け</code>,
<code>{f|法人|ほうじん}{f|向|む}け</code> ("for businesses"),
<code>{f|個人|こじん}{f|向|む}け</code> ("for individuals"),
<code>{f|初心者|しょしんしゃ}{f|向|む}け</code>,
<code>{f|女性|じょせい}{f|向|む}け</code>. The last of these has become slightly
loaded in recent years - marketing categories drawn along gender lines attract
comment - so it is worth noticing that it describes a **marketing decision** and not
a claim about who may buy the thing.

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|向|む}け</code> wants a **group or market**, not a named
individual. <code>{f|部長|ぶちょう}{f|向|む}けの{f|資料|しりょう}</code> is odd unless
you mean managers as a category. For one person, use
<code>{f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|見|み}せる{f|資料|しりょう}</code> or
<code>{f|部長|ぶちょう}のための{f|資料|しりょう}</code>.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～{f|向|む}け vs ～{f|向|む}き

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|向|む}け</code> = somebody **aimed** it there.
<code>{f|向|む}き</code> = it **turns out to suit** them. Intention versus fit.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|問題集|もんだいしゅう}は{f|受験生|じゅけんせい}<b>{f|向|む}け</b>に{f|作|つく}られた。
This question book was produced for exam candidates.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|問題集|もんだいしゅう}は{f|説明|せつめい}が{f|丁寧|ていねい}なので{f|独学|どくがく}の{f|人|ひと}<b>{f|向|む}き</b>だ。
The explanations are thorough, so this question book is good for people studying alone.
</pre>

The first reports a decision made by the publisher. The second is the speaker's
verdict, and it could easily be about an audience the publisher never considered.
This is why a shop sign says <code>{f|子|こ}ども{f|向|む}けコーナー</code> - the shop
has designated it - while a review says
<code>{f|子|こ}ども{f|向|む}きだと{f|思|おも}う</code>.

Only <code>{f|向|む}け</code> combines comfortably with `に` plus a verb of making,
writing, opening or selling, precisely because those verbs describe the act of
aiming. And only <code>{f|向|む}き</code> can be preceded by a compass point:
<code>{f|南|みなみ}{f|向|む}き</code> is a direction, and
❌<code>{f|南|みなみ}{f|向|む}け</code> is nothing at all.

### ～{f|向|む}け vs ～{f|用|よう}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|向|む}け</code> names the **audience** something was
aimed at; <code>{f|用|よう}</code> names the **use** something is for. Who versus
what-for.

</div>

<pre>
{f|自宅|じたく}<b>{f|用|よう}</b>ですか、{f|贈|おく}り{f|物|もの}<b>{f|用|よう}</b>ですか。
Is it for yourself, or is it a gift?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会社|かいしゃ}<b>{f|向|む}け</b>のサービスも{f|始|はじ}めました。
We've launched a service for businesses as well.
</pre>

The shop assistant's question is about the **purpose** the item will serve, so
<code>{f|用|よう}</code>. The second sentence names the **customer segment**, so
<code>{f|向|む}け</code>. They can even stack in the same phrase without redundancy:
<code>{f|学生|がくせい}{f|向|む}けの{f|試験|しけん}{f|用|よう}アプリ</code> - an
exam-taking app aimed at students.

Note that <code>{f|用|よう}</code> attaches to activities and occasions as readily as
to people (<code>{f|旅行用|りょこうよう}</code>,
<code>{f|非常用|ひじょうよう}</code>), whereas <code>{f|向|む}け</code> is essentially
restricted to audiences and markets.

### ～{f|向|む}けに vs ～のために

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>のために</code> is **for someone's sake**, personal and
warm. <code>{f|向|む}けに</code> is **targeted at a market**, impersonal and
commercial.

</div>

<pre>
{f|母|はは}<b>のために</b>{f|大|おお}きい{f|字|じ}の{f|本|ほん}を{f|買|か}った。
I bought a large-print book for my mother.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|目|め}が{f|悪|わる}い{f|人|ひと}<b>{f|向|む}けに</b>{f|大|おお}きい{f|字|じ}の{f|本|ほん}が{f|出|だ}されている。
Large-print books are published for people with poor eyesight.
</pre>

Swapping them is a tonal mistake rather than a grammatical one, and it is a
memorable one. ❌<code>{f|母|はは}{f|向|む}けに{f|本|ほん}を{f|買|か}った</code> makes
your mother sound like a demographic segment. Conversely, a publisher's catalogue
would not write <code>{f|目|め}が{f|悪|わる}い{f|人|ひと}のために</code> unless it
wanted to sound charitable rather than commercial - which, occasionally, it does.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|南|みなみ}{f|向|む}けのベランダがある{f|部屋|へや}を{f|探|さが}しています。 → ✅ {f|南|みなみ}<b>{f|向|む}き</b>のベランダがある{f|部屋|へや}を{f|探|さが}しています。
  Compass points take <code>{f|向|む}き</code>. A balcony faces south; nobody aimed
  it at south as a market.

- ❌ {f|初心者|しょしんしゃ}{f|向|む}けな{f|教科書|きょうかしょ}を{f|使|つか}っています。 → ✅ {f|初心者|しょしんしゃ}<b>{f|向|む}けの</b>{f|教科書|きょうかしょ}を{f|使|つか}っています。
  <code>{f|向|む}け</code> is a noun and joins the next noun with `の`. There is no
  な-adjective form.

- ❌ {f|今夜|こんや}は{f|家族|かぞく}{f|向|む}けにカレーを{f|作|つく}りました。 → ✅ {f|今夜|こんや}は{f|家族|かぞく}<b>のために</b>カレーを{f|作|つく}りました。
  Cooking dinner at home is done for people's sake, not aimed at a market segment.
  <code>{f|向|む}けに</code> here sounds as though you are running a canteen.

- ❌ この{f|靴|くつ}は{f|私|わたし}{f|向|む}けです。 → ✅ この{f|靴|くつ}は{f|私|わたし}に<b>{f|合|あ}っています</b>。
  <code>{f|向|む}け</code> takes a group or a market, never a single named person.
  For an individual fit, use <code>{f|合|あ}う</code>, or
  <code>{f|向|む}いている</code> for an aptitude.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|数年前|すうねんまえ}から{f|高齢者|こうれいしゃ}____のスマートフォンを{f|作|つく}っている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|高齢者|こうれいしゃ}<b>{f|向|む}け</b>のスマートフォンを{f|作|つく}っている。

The company deliberately designs the phones for that market, so the sentence is
about **intention**. <code>{f|高齢者|こうれいしゃ}{f|向|む}き</code> would be a
reviewer's verdict on the result, which is not what a manufacturer's own activity
is described with.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|東|ひがし}（{f|向|む}き・{f|向|む}け）なので、{f|朝|あさ}は{f|明|あか}るいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|東|ひがし}<b>{f|向|む}き</b>なので、{f|朝|あさ}は{f|明|あか}るいです。

A compass direction is pure orientation, which only <code>{f|向|む}き</code>
expresses. <code>{f|向|む}け</code> requires an audience or a market, and "east" is
neither.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The museum has started an English-language guided tour for foreign visitors."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|美術館|びじゅつかん}が{f|外国人|がいこくじん}{f|観光客|かんこうきゃく}<b>{f|向|む}け</b>の{f|英語|えいご}のツアーを{f|始|はじ}めました。

The museum has aimed a service at a defined group of visitors - a planning
decision, so <code>{f|向|む}け</code>. You could equally write
<code>{f|外国人|がいこくじん}{f|観光客|かんこうきゃく}{f|向|む}けに{f|英語|えいご}のツアーを{f|始|はじ}めました</code>,
using the `に` form before the verb.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|小学生|しょうがくせい}{f|向|む}けに{f|作|つく}られているが、{f|説明|せつめい}が{f|短|みじか}くて{f|分|わ}かりやすいので、{f|大人|おとな}{f|向|む}けにもいい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ …{f|説明|せつめい}が{f|短|みじか}くて{f|分|わ}かりやすいので、{f|大人|おとな}<b>{f|向|む}き</b>でもある。

The first <code>{f|向|む}け</code> is right - the publisher aimed it at primary
school pupils. The second is not, because nobody aimed it at adults; the speaker is
judging that it **happens to suit** them. That is exactly the
<code>{f|向|む}き</code> job.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|子|こ}ども<b>{f|向|む}け</b>に{f|書|か}かれた{f|説明|せつめい}
(b) {f|子|こ}ども<b>のために</b>{f|書|か}かれた{f|説明|せつめい}

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a statement about the **target readership**: the writer pitched the level,
vocabulary and length at children. It is neutral and professional, the kind of thing
said about published material, and it implies nothing about the writer's feelings.

(b) is about **benefit**. Somebody wrote it so that children would be helped, and
the phrase carries warmth - it would suit a hospital leaflet or a note written by a
teacher for their own class. In practice (b) also allows the reader and the
beneficiary to differ: an explanation written *for the sake of* children might in
fact be addressed to their parents, which (a) rules out.

</details>
