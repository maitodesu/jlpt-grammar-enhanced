# ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる

**Meaning** · four suffixes that bolt a direction, a partner, a second attempt or a
habit onto a verb

Japanese builds a great deal of its vocabulary by welding a second verb onto the
ます-stem of a first. These four are the ones an N3 learner meets constantly:
<code>～{f|込|こ}む</code> sends the action **inward or deep**, <code>～{f|合|あ}う</code> makes it
**mutual**, <code>～{f|直|なお}す</code> makes it a **second go**, and
<code>～{f|慣|な}れる</code> says you have **done it often enough to be comfortable**.

## Formation

All four attach to the **ます-stem** and produce an ordinary verb.
{f|込|こ}む, {f|合|あ}う and {f|直|なお}す are **う-verbs**; {f|慣|な}れる is a
**る-verb**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) - inward | ます-stem + {f|込|こ}む | {f|飛|と}びます → {f|飛|と}び{f|込|こ}む |
| Verb (ます-stem) - deeply, at length | ます-stem + {f|込|こ}む | {f|考|かんが}えます → {f|考|かんが}え{f|込|こ}む |
| Verb (ます-stem) - mutually | ます-stem + {f|合|あ}う | {f|話|はな}します → {f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}う |
| Verb (ます-stem) - do over | ます-stem + {f|直|なお}す | {f|書|か}きます → {f|書|か}き{f|直|なお}す |
| Verb (ます-stem) - get used to doing | ます-stem + {f|慣|な}れる | {f|使|つか}います → {f|使|つか}い{f|慣|な}れる |
| Usual shape of the last one | ～{f|慣|な}れて + いる | {f|使|つか}い{f|慣|な}れている |
| Noun instead of a stem | Noun + に + {f|慣|な}れる | {f|生活|せいかつ}に{f|慣|な}れる |

<div class="note">

**Note** - these are **word-formation**, not free grammar. Each combination is a
separate item of vocabulary, and only some combinations exist:
{f|考|かんが}え{f|込|こ}む is a word, ❌<code>{f|走|はし}り{f|込|こ}む</code> in the
sense "run inward" mostly is not. {f|込|こ}む is the least predictable of the four - 
{f|使|つか}い{f|込|こ}む means both "use for years" and "embezzle" - so treat new
combinations as vocabulary to look up rather than forms to generate.
{f|直|なお}す and {f|合|あ}う are the two you can most safely build yourself.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|漢字|かんじ}は{f|間違|まちが}えたので、<b>{f|書|か}き{f|直|なお}します</b>。
I got this kanji wrong, so I'll write it again.
</pre>

<pre>
クラスのみんなで<b>{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}った</b>。
The whole class talked it over together.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもが{f|水|みず}たまりに<b>{f|飛|と}び{f|込|こ}んだ</b>。
The child jumped into the puddle.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|道具|どうぐ}は{f|十年|じゅうねん}{f|使|つか}っているので、すっかり<b>{f|使|つか}い{f|慣|な}れて</b>いる。
I've been using this tool for ten years, so it's completely second nature by now.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|質問|しつもん}を{f|聞|き}いて、しばらく<b>{f|考|かんが}え{f|込|こ}んで</b>いた。
The manager heard the question and sat there thinking hard for a while.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|留学|りゅうがく}をやめるかどうか、もう{f|一度|いちど}<b>{f|考|かんが}え{f|直|なお}して</b>みることにした。
I decided to give the question of quitting the exchange programme another think.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|困|こま}ったときに<b>{f|助|たす}け{f|合|あ}える</b>{f|友|とも}だちが{f|近|ちか}くにいるのは、ありがたいことだ。
It's a blessing to have friends nearby who can help each other out when things go wrong.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|東京|とうきょう}の{f|満員|まんいん}{f|電車|でんしゃ}にもだんだん<b>{f|乗|の}り{f|慣|な}れて</b>きて、{f|今|いま}では{f|本|ほん}も{f|読|よ}める。
I've gradually got used to Tokyo's packed trains, and these days I can even read on them.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}で{f|昔|むかし}の{f|友人|ゆうじん}に{f|会|あ}い、{f|喫茶店|きっさてん}で{f|二|に}{f|時間|じかん}も<b>{f|話|はな}し{f|込|こ}んで</b>しまった。
I ran into an old friend at the station and ended up talking for two solid hours in a coffee shop.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

All four are ordinary, register-neutral vocabulary. Nothing about them is formal or
casual; what varies is how freely you can invent new combinations.

<code>～{f|直|なお}す</code> is the most productive and the most useful for a learner. Almost
any deliberate action can be redone: {f|書|か}き{f|直|なお}す,
{f|読|よ}み{f|直|なお}す, {f|考|かんが}え{f|直|なお}す,
{f|作|つく}り{f|直|なお}す, やり{f|直|なお}す. It always implies the first
attempt was unsatisfactory, which is why it does not work for pleasant repetition - 
you do not {f|聞|き}き{f|直|なお}す a song you love, you listen again.

<code>～{f|合|あ}う</code> requires **two or more parties acting on each other**. That is a
real constraint: {f|助|たす}け{f|合|あ}う needs both sides helping, and if only one
person helps, it is plain {f|助|たす}ける. This use produces a warm, cooperative
tone, which is why {f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}い is the word for a proper discussion
rather than an argument.

<code>～{f|込|こ}む</code> splits into a physical direction（{f|飛|と}び{f|込|こ}む,
{f|乗|の}り{f|込|こ}む, {f|流|なが}れ{f|込|こ}む）and an intensity or duration
sense（{f|考|かんが}え{f|込|こ}む, {f|話|はな}し{f|込|こ}む,
{f|黙|だま}り{f|込|こ}む, {f|教|おし}え{f|込|こ}む）. The second group tends to
suggest being absorbed to the point of losing track of the outside world.

<code>～{f|慣|な}れる</code> almost always appears as **～{f|慣|な}れている** or
**～{f|慣|な}れてくる**, because it names a state you have arrived at rather than an
event. And keep it apart from its noun-taking twin:
{f|日本|にほん}の{f|生活|せいかつ}に{f|慣|な}れる takes a noun and `に`;
{f|日本語|にほんご}で{f|話|はな}し{f|慣|な}れる takes a stem.

## Contrast

### ～{f|直|なお}す vs もう{f|一度|いちど}～する

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>もう{f|一度|いちど}</code> is neutral repetition.
<code>～{f|直|なお}す</code> says the first attempt was **wrong or inadequate** and this is the
corrected version.

</div>

<pre>
{f|好|す}きな{f|映画|えいが}なので、<b>もう{f|一度|いちど}</b>{f|見|み}たい。
It's a film I love, so I'd like to watch it again.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|名前|なまえ}が{f|抜|ぬ}けていたので、{f|申込書|もうしこみしょ}を<b>{f|書|か}き{f|直|なお}した</b>。
The name was missing, so I filled the application form in again.
</pre>

Swap them and the meanings shift audibly.
{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}{f|直|なお}す would mean watching it again to check
something, or - in another sense - coming to think better of it.
もう{f|一度|いちど}{f|書|か}く is simply writing a second copy, with no implication
that the first was faulty.

### ～{f|合|あ}う vs {f|一緒|いっしょ}に～する

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～{f|合|あ}う</code> means the action goes **between** the
participants. <code>{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</code> means they do it **side by side**,
usually to something or someone else.

</div>

<pre>
{f|三人|さんにん}で{f|荷物|にもつ}を<b>{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</b>{f|運|はこ}んだ。
The three of us carried the luggage together.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|困|こま}っている{f|人|ひと}を、みんなで<b>{f|助|たす}け{f|合|あ}おう</b>。
Let's all help each other out when we're in trouble.
</pre>

The luggage is a third party; nobody is carrying anybody. That is why
❌<code>{f|運|はこ}び{f|合|あ}う</code> is odd. With {f|助|たす}ける, the sentence
only takes {f|合|あ}う if the helping flows in both directions - 
{f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|助|たす}ける would mean two people helping a third.

### ～{f|込|こ}む vs ～{f|出|だ}す

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - as directional suffixes they are opposites: <code>{f|込|こ}む</code> points
**in**, <code>{f|出|だ}す</code> points **out**. Note that
<code>{f|出|だ}す</code> also has the unrelated "suddenly begin" sense you met at N4.

</div>

<pre>
かばんから{f|財布|さいふ}を<b>{f|取|と}り{f|出|だ}した</b>。
I took my wallet out of my bag.
</pre>

<pre>
ポケットに{f|切符|きっぷ}を<b>{f|押|お}し{f|込|こ}んだ</b>。
I shoved the ticket into my pocket.
</pre>

The same verb takes both and the pair is worth memorising:
{f|飛|と}び{f|出|だ}す ("dash out, leap out") against
{f|飛|と}び{f|込|こ}む ("dive in").

The trap is that <code>{f|出|だ}す</code> has a second job - marking the sudden
start of an action, as in {f|泣|な}き{f|出|だ}す - and that job has nothing to do
with direction. Context tells them apart:
{f|走|はし}り{f|出|だ}す is "break into a run", not "run outwards".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|書|か}く<b>{f|直|なお}す</b> → ✅ {f|書|か}き<b>{f|直|なお}す</b>
  All four attach to the ます-stem.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}に{f|相談|そうだん}し<b>{f|合|あ}った</b>。 → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}に{f|相談|そうだん}した。
  <code>～{f|合|あ}う</code> needs the action to run both ways. Consulting a teacher goes one
  way, so the plain verb is correct.

- ❌ {f|日本|にほん}の{f|生活|せいかつ}を{f|住|す}み<b>{f|慣|な}れた</b>。 → ✅ {f|日本|にほん}の{f|生活|せいかつ}に{f|慣|な}れた。
  With a **noun**, use <code>に{f|慣|な}れる</code>. The stem form needs a verb in
  front of it: <code>この{f|町|まち}に{f|住|す}み{f|慣|な}れた</code>.

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}をもう{f|一度|いちど}<b>{f|考|かんが}え{f|込|こ}もう</b>。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}をもう{f|一度|いちど}<b>{f|考|かんが}え{f|直|なお}そう</b>。
  <code>{f|考|かんが}え{f|込|こ}む</code> is to brood, absorbed and silent. To
  rethink something is <code>{f|考|かんが}え{f|直|なお}す</code>.

- ❌ このパソコンは<b>{f|使|つか}い{f|慣|な}れる</b>。 → ✅ このパソコンは<b>{f|使|つか}い{f|慣|な}れている</b>。
  The suffix names a state you have reached, so the ている form is what you almost
  always want.

## Practice

**1.** {f|漢字|かんじ}を{f|一|いち}{f|文字|もじ}{f|間違|まちが}えたので、はじめから{f|書|か}き____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

はじめから{f|書|か}き<b>{f|直|なお}した</b>。

The first version was faulty, which is exactly what <code>～{f|直|なお}す</code> marks.
<code>{f|直|なお}す</code> is an う-verb, so the past is
<code>{f|直|なお}した</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|会議|かいぎ}では{f|全員|ぜんいん}で{f|意見|いけん}を（{f|言|い}い{f|合|あ}った・{f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|言|い}った）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|全員|ぜんいん}で{f|意見|いけん}を<b>{f|言|い}い{f|合|あ}った</b>。

Opinions were exchanged between the participants, which is the mutual sense.
<code>{f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|言|い}った</code> would mean everyone said the same thing
in unison, which is not what a meeting is.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I've got used to cooking for myself, so it doesn't bother me any more."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|料理|りょうり}を<b>{f|作|つく}り{f|慣|な}れた</b>ので、もう{f|苦|く}にならない。

<code>{f|作|つく}り{f|慣|な}れる</code> takes the ます-stem because the thing you are
used to is an **action**. Had the sentence been about being used to *life alone*, it
would need the noun pattern <code>{f|一人|ひとり}{f|暮|ぐ}らしに{f|慣|な}れる</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|来|き}たので、いそいで{f|乗|の}り{f|出|だ}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|来|き}たので、いそいで{f|乗|の}り<b>{f|込|こ}んだ</b>。

Getting onto a train is movement **inward**, so the suffix is
<code>{f|込|こ}む</code>. <code>{f|乗|の}り{f|出|だ}す</code> exists but means
"lean out" or "launch into a venture" - not boarding.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|黙|だま}って{f|下|した}を{f|見|み}ていた。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|黙|だま}り{f|込|こ}んで</b>しまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply describes what he was doing: not speaking, eyes down. It is a neutral
piece of stage direction and says nothing about why.

(b) says he shut down into silence and stayed there. The <code>{f|込|こ}む</code> supplies both
depth and duration - he has gone somewhere inward - and <code>しまった</code> adds
that this was not what anyone wanted. It is a much heavier silence than (a).

</details>
