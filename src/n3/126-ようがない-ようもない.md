# ～ようがない / ～ようもない

**Meaning** · there is no way of doing it; no method exists

`よう` here is {f|様|よう}, "manner, way of doing". `～ようがない` therefore says
that the **means are missing**: not that you lack the skill, not that you are
unwilling, but that there is nothing available to do it with. No address, so no way
to send it. No pieces left, so no way to mend it.

## Formation

`ようがない` attaches to the **ます-stem** and the result is an **い-adjective**,
because it ends in `ない`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + ようがない | {f|直|なお}します → {f|直|なお}しようがない |
| する-verb | Noun + し + ようがない | {f|連絡|れんらく}します → {f|連絡|れんらく}しようがない |
| Emphatic variant | ます-stem + ようもない | {f|想像|そうぞう}しようもない |
| Polite | ～ようがありません | {f|直|なお}しようがありません |
| Past | ～ようがなかった | {f|助|たす}けようがなかった |
| て-form / joining | ～ようがなく(て) | {f|書|か}きようがなくて{f|困|こま}った |
| Before a noun | ～ようがない + Noun | {f|直|なお}しようがない{f|時計|とけい} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>ようもない</code> is the same pattern with `も` in place of `が`,
and the `も` adds "not even". It is a shade stronger and a shade more literary;
everything else is identical. Do not confuse either with the **volitional**
<code>{f|行|い}こう</code>, <code>{f|食|た}べよう</code> - those come from a
different base and cannot take this ending.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|住所|じゅうしょ}が{f|分|わ}からないので、{f|送|おく}り<b>ようがない</b>。
I don't know the address, so there's no way to send it.
</pre>

<pre>
こんなに{f|壊|こわ}れていては、{f|直|なお}し<b>ようがない</b>。
Broken like this, there's no fixing it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電話|でんわ}{f|番号|ばんごう}も{f|知|し}らないから、{f|連絡|れんらく}し<b>ようがない</b>。
I don't even know his phone number, so there's no way to get in touch.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|名前|なまえ}が{f|大|おお}きく{f|書|か}いてあるので、{f|間違|まちが}え<b>ようがない</b>。
The name is written in big letters, so there's no way you can get it wrong.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本人|ほんにん}が{f|話|はな}してくれない{f|以上|いじょう}、こちらからは{f|助|たす}け<b>ようがない</b>。
As long as he won't talk to us, there's nothing we can do to help.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|材料|ざいりょう}が{f|何|なに}もないのだから、{f|作|つく}り<b>ようがない</b>じゃないか。
There aren't any ingredients, so how exactly am I supposed to make anything?
</pre>

<pre>
あのときの{f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|気持|きも}ちは、{f|想像|そうぞう}し<b>ようもない</b>。
I cannot even begin to imagine how she felt at the time.
</pre>

<pre>
データがすべて{f|消|き}えてしまったので、{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}を{f|書|か}き<b>ようがなく</b>、{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りを{f|延|の}ばしてもらった。
All the data was wiped, so there was simply no way to write the report and I had the deadline pushed back.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The feeling is **resignation without excuse-making**. Because the sentence claims
that no method exists, it removes blame from everybody: {f|直|なお}しようがない is
not "I failed to fix it" but "it was not fixable". That makes the pattern useful for
declining responsibility gracefully, and it also makes it a strong claim - if a
method *did* exist, the sentence is simply untrue.

There is a mirror-image use that surprises learners: `ようがない` on a verb of
error or failure produces a **guarantee**. {f|間違|まちが}えようがない means "you
couldn't get it wrong if you tried"; {f|負|ま}けようがない, "there's no way we lose".
The logic is the same - no method available - but the effect is confident rather than
defeated.

Register is neutral, leaning slightly written for `ようもない`. The one thing that
is *not* neutral is the contraction. {f|仕方|しかた}がない has a spoken cousin
しょうがない, and しょうがない is a contraction of しようがない - so the two patterns
share a form. In practice, しょうがない has drifted away into a fixed phrase meaning
"oh well, nothing to be done", and it is quite casual; if you mean specifically
"there is no way to do it", say しようがない with the full vowel.

Note also that しょうがない{f|人|ひと} means "hopeless case, incorrigible" - an
affectionate insult, not a comment on methods.

## Contrast

### ～ようがない vs the potential negative

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>られない</code> says **I** can't. `ようがない` says
**nobody** can, because there is nothing to do it with.

</div>

<pre>
{f|道具|どうぐ}がないので、{f|私|わたし}には<b>{f|直|なお}せない</b>。
I haven't got the tools, so I can't fix it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部品|ぶひん}がもう{f|作|つく}られていないので、<b>{f|直|なお}しようがない</b>。
The parts aren't made any more, so it simply can't be repaired.
</pre>

In the first sentence, a repair shop could still do it. In the second, nobody can.
This is why `ようがない` is the right choice when you want to close off the
possibility entirely - and the wrong choice when the obstacle is personal.
❌{f|忙|いそが}しくて{f|行|い}きようがない is a common learner error; being busy is
your circumstance, not an absence of method, so it should be
{f|忙|いそが}しくて{f|行|い}けない.

### ～ようがない vs ～がたい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `がたい` is **hard**; `ようがない` is **impossible**. `がたい`
concedes that the thing could be done and says you can hardly bring yourself to;
`ようがない` says the question does not arise.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|説明|せつめい}は<b>{f|理解|りかい}しがたい</b>が、{f|一応|いちおう}{f|聞|き}いておこう。
His explanation is hard to make sense of, but let's hear him out anyway.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|資料|しりょう}が{f|一|いち}{f|枚|まい}もなくては、<b>{f|理解|りかい}しようがない</b>。
Without a single document to go on, there's no way to understand it.
</pre>

The give-away is what can follow. `がたい` sits comfortably before `が` or けれど,
because there is another side. `ようがない` normally ends the matter.

### ～ようがない vs {f|仕方|しかた}がない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|仕方|しかた}がない</code> is about **accepting a
situation**: it can't be helped. `ようがない` is about **an action having no
method**: there's no way to do it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まってしまったのだから、<b>{f|仕方|しかた}がない</b>。
The trains have stopped, so there's nothing for it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|地図|ちず}も{f|電波|でんぱ}もないので、{f|道|みち}を{f|調|しら}べ<b>ようがない</b>。
With no map and no signal, there's no way to look up the route.
</pre>

<code>{f|仕方|しかた}がない</code> stands alone as a comment on the whole state of
affairs and attaches to nothing. `ようがない` must attach to a verb stem and names a
specific action that cannot be carried out. They often appear in the same breath:
{f|調|しら}べようがないのだから、{f|仕方|しかた}がない.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|直|なお}す<b>ようがない</b> → ✅ {f|直|なお}し<b>ようがない</b>
  The suffix takes the ます-stem.

- ❌ {f|行|い}こう<b>がない</b> → ✅ {f|行|い}き<b>ようがない</b>
  The volitional <code>{f|行|い}こう</code> is a different form entirely. `よう` here
  is the noun {f|様|よう}, and it follows the stem.

- ❌ {f|連絡|れんらく}する<b>ようがない</b> → ✅ {f|連絡|れんらく}し<b>ようがない</b>
  With a する-verb, the stem is `し`.

- ❌ {f|直|なお}しようがない<b>だ</b> → ✅ {f|直|なお}しようがない
  It ends in `ない`, so it is an い-adjective and takes no `だ`. Polite is
  <code>{f|直|なお}しようがありません</code>.

- ❌ {f|眠|ねむ}くて{f|勉強|べんきょう}し<b>ようがない</b>。 → ✅ {f|眠|ねむ}くて{f|勉強|べんきょう}<b>できない</b>。
  Being sleepy is a personal state, not a missing method. `ようがない` needs the
  means themselves to be absent.

## Practice

**1.** メールアドレスも{f|電話|でんわ}{f|番号|ばんごう}も{f|分|わ}からないので、{f|知|し}らせ____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|知|し}らせ<b>ようがない</b>。

Every possible channel is missing, so there is literally no method - which is what
`ようがない` claims. Note the ます-stem <code>{f|知|し}らせ</code>, and that the
whole thing behaves as an い-adjective.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|説明|せつめい}が{f|丁寧|ていねい}すぎるくらいなので、{f|作|つく}り{f|方|かた}を（{f|間違|まちが}えようがない・{f|間違|まちが}えがたい）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|作|つく}り{f|方|かた}を<b>{f|間違|まちが}えようがない</b>。

This is the confident use: the instructions are so thorough that no route to a
mistake exists. <code>{f|間違|まちが}えがたい</code> is not a combination Japanese
uses, and `がたい` would in any case only mean "hard to", not "impossible".

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The shop has closed down, so there's no way to complain about it now."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|店|みせ}がなくなってしまったので、{f|今|いま}となっては{f|文句|もんく}の{f|言|い}い<b>ようがない</b>。

The shop's disappearance removes the means, not the speaker's ability - exactly the
distinction the pattern draws. <code>{f|言|い}いようがない</code> sits on the
ます-stem of <code>{f|言|い}う</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|時間|じかん}がなくて、{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|返事|へんじ}しようがなかったです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|時間|じかん}がなくて、{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|返事|へんじ}できませんでした。

Lack of time is a personal circumstance, so the potential negative is what is wanted.
`ようがない` would claim that no channel for replying existed at all - which would be
true if the phone were dead, but not merely because you were busy.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|時計|とけい}は<b>{f|直|なお}せない</b>。
(b) この{f|時計|とけい}は<b>{f|直|なお}しようがない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the speaker cannot repair it. Somebody with more skill, better tools or the
right parts might well be able to, and the sentence quietly invites you to take it
somewhere else.

(b) closes that door. There is no method - the parts do not exist, or the damage is
past any repair - so taking it elsewhere would be pointless. It is a statement about
the watch rather than about the speaker.

</details>
