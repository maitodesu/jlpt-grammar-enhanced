# ～させてもらう・～ていただく

**Meaning** · do something with your kind permission; have something done for me - 
humble framing built out of the receiving verbs

Two related frames live on this page. <code>～ていただく</code> is the humble
<code>～てもらう</code>: **you** do something, and I receive the favour.
<code>～させていただく</code> adds the causative in front of it: **I** do something,
and what I receive is your permission to do it. Getting them the right way round is
the whole lesson.

## Formation

Build the causative, put it in the て-form, then attach もらう (plain) or いただく
(humble). Every causative verb is a る-verb, so the て-form step never varies.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | あ-stem + せていただく | {f|使|つか}う → {f|使|つか}わせていただきます |
| Verb (る-verb) | drop る + させていただく | {f|始|はじ}める → {f|始|はじ}めさせていただきます |
| Verb (する) | させていただく | {f|説明|せつめい}する → {f|説明|せつめい}させていただきます |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | {f|来|こ}させていただく | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}させていただきます |
| Casual equivalent | causative て-form + もらう | {f|帰|かえ}る → {f|帰|かえ}らせてもらうね |
| Someone else's action | て-form + いただく | {f|教|おし}える → {f|教|おし}えていただきました |
| Asking permission | causative て-form + いただけませんか | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}ませていただけませんか |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - count the actors. <code>させていただく</code>: I act, you permit.
<code>ていただく</code>: you act, I benefit. If the verb describes something you are
doing, the causative has to be in there.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - う-verbs take `せる`, not `させる`. ❌{f|読|よ}まさせていただきます is the
error known as さ{f|入|い}れ{f|言葉|ことば}, and it is common enough among native
speakers to be a running complaint in style guides. The correct form is
{f|読|よ}ませていただきます. る-verbs and する genuinely do take `させる`, which is
where the confusion comes from.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
コピーを<b>{f|取|と}らせていただきます</b>。
I'll take a copy, if I may.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|漢字|かんじ}の{f|由来|ゆらい}を<b>{f|教|おし}えていただきました</b>。
The teacher was kind enough to explain where the character comes from.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本日|ほんじつ}{f|司会|しかい}を<b>{f|務|つと}めさせていただきます</b>、{f|田中|たなか}と{f|申|もう}します。
I'm Tanaka, and I'll be chairing today's session.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

</pre>

I'm sorry for the short notice, but I'll be absent from next week's meeting.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>させていただく</code> is business Japanese's workhorse. It appears in
announcements, presentations, shop signs and apologetic emails, and its effect is
to present your own action as something granted rather than simply done. That is
why it so often accompanies bad news: {f|閉店|へいてん}させていただきます on a shop
door softens a closure by implying the customers' permission.

Note that a sentence ending in させていただきます is usually **not** a request. It
announces. The genuine request forms are させていただけませんか,
させていただけますでしょうか and させてください, and the difference matters: if you
need an answer, use one of those.

The plain <code>させてもらう</code> is the same grammar without the humility, and it is
ordinary casual speech: {f|先|さき}に{f|帰|かえ}らせてもらうね, "I'm going to head
off, if that's all right." It can also carry a faint note of asserting your right,
which is why it sometimes sounds slightly assertive between equals.

Overuse is a real phenomenon. When nobody has granted anything - 
❌{f|担当|たんとう}させていただいております for a job you were simply assigned - the
form is criticised as inflation, and plain いたします or しております is better
Japanese. The test is whether a permission-giver exists in the situation. In a
presentation the audience has given you the floor, so
{f|説明|せつめい}させていただきます is well founded; in a self-introduction, nobody
gave you permission to have your own name.

## Contrast

### ～させていただく vs ～させてください

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - させてください **asks**. させていただきます **announces**, on
the assumption that permission is already there.

</div>

<pre>
{f|少|すこ}し{f|考|かんが}え<b>させてください</b>。
Please let me think about it a little.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|結果|けっか}は{f|後日|ごじつ}ご{f|連絡|れんらく}<b>させていただきます</b>。
We'll be in touch with the result at a later date.
</pre>

Both are polite. させてください is a direct request and needs a yes; it can be a
touch too direct toward a customer, which is why business speech leans on
させていただきます and on the interrogative させていただけますでしょうか.

### ～させていただく vs ～ていただく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - whose hands are moving? Mine: させていただく. Yours:
ていただく.

</div>

<pre>
{f|資料|しりょう}を<b>{f|送|おく}らせていただきます</b>。
I'll send you the documents.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|資料|しりょう}を<b>{f|送|おく}っていただけますか</b>。
Could you send me the documents?
</pre>

One causative apart, and the two sentences point in opposite directions. Mixing
them up is the most consequential mistake on this page: you can easily promise to
do something when you meant to ask for it.

### ～させていただく vs ～いたします

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>いたします</code> is plain humility. Add
させていただく only when there is somebody whose permission is genuinely in play.

</div>

<pre>
ご{f|案内|あんない}<b>いたします</b>。
Allow me to show you the way.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本日|ほんじつ}は{f|代|か}わりに{f|私|わたし}が{f|担当|たんとう}<b>させていただきます</b>。
I'll be standing in and handling this today.
</pre>

The second sentence works because the listener is being asked to accept a
substitute. Where no such acceptance is involved, いたします is shorter, older and
better regarded.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|読|よ}まさせていただきます。 → ✅ <b>{f|読|よ}ませていただきます</b>。
  {f|読|よ}む is an う-verb, so the causative is {f|読|よ}ませる with `せ` alone.
  The extra `さ` is the さ{f|入|い}れ{f|言葉|ことば} error.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}に{f|説明|せつめい}させていただきました。（meaning the teacher explained）→ ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}に<b>{f|説明|せつめい}していただきました</b>。
  With the causative in place, the sentence says *you* did the explaining after the
  teacher allowed it. Drop the causative when the other person is the one acting.

- ❌ お{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|書|か}かせていただかせていただきます。 → ✅ お{f|名前|なまえ}を<b>{f|書|か}かせていただきます</b>。
  One layer of humility per verb. Stacking the frame on itself is
  {f|二重|にじゅう}{f|敬語|けいご}, not extra politeness.

- ❌ {f|山田|やまだ}と{f|申|もう}させていただきます。 → ✅ {f|山田|やまだ}と<b>{f|申|もう}します</b>。
  Nobody grants you permission to be called by your own name, so there is nothing
  for the frame to receive. This is the textbook case of overuse.

## Practice

**1.** {f|体調|たいちょう}が{f|悪|わる}いので、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}____ませんか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|今日|きょう}は{f|早|はや}く<b>{f|帰|かえ}らせていただけ</b>ませんか。

{f|帰|かえ}る is an う-verb, so the causative is {f|帰|かえ}らせる, and the
potential of いただく in the negative question form is what turns the sentence into
a polite request rather than an announcement.

</details>

**2.** Choose the correct form: {f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|推薦状|すいせんじょう}を（{f|書|か}かせていただきました・{f|書|か}いていただきました）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|推薦状|すいせんじょう}を<b>{f|書|か}いていただきました</b>。

The manager is the one who wrote the letter, so the plain て-form plus いただく is
correct. The causative version would mean the speaker wrote it after being given
permission.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "May I use this room?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|部屋|へや}を<b>{f|使|つか}わせていただけますか</b>。
（or {f|使|つか}わせていただいてもよろしいでしょうか。）

{f|使|つか}う is an う-verb, giving {f|使|つか}わせる - not ❌{f|使|つか}わさせる, which
is the さ{f|入|い}れ{f|言葉|ことば} error - and the potential form of いただく turns
the humble frame into an actual question.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|明日|あした}、{f|休|やす}まさせていただきます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|明日|あした}、<b>{f|休|やす}ませていただきます</b>。

{f|休|やす}む is an う-verb and takes `せる`, not `させる`. Only る-verbs, する and
{f|来|く}る take the `させ` form; the mistaken extra `さ` here is
さ{f|入|い}れ{f|言葉|ことば}.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|明日|あした}{f|休|やす}ませていただきます。
(b) {f|明日|あした}{f|休|やす}ませていただけませんか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (a) is an announcement dressed in humble clothing: the speaker has decided
to take the day off and is informing you courteously. Sentence (b) actually asks,
and leaves the decision with the listener. Say (a) to a manager who has already
approved the leave; say (b) when you still need approval, or you will sound as
though you are helping yourself to it.

</details>
