# たとえば

**Meaning** · for example; say - pulling one concrete case out of a general
statement

`たとえば` warns the listener that what follows is an illustration, not a new
claim. The example is offered as one of several possibilities, which is why it so
often arrives with `とか` or `など` trailing behind it.

## Usage

`たとえば` normally stands at the head of the sentence, but it can also sit
directly in front of the noun phrase it is introducing:

- {f|果物|くだもの}が{f|好|す}きです。たとえば、みかんとかぶどうとか。
- {f|近|ちか}くの{f|店|みせ}、たとえばコンビニでも{f|買|か}えます。

Three shapes carry most of its use:

- **たとえば～とか／など** - a list left deliberately open.
  たとえば{f|漢字|かんじ}のアプリなど、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|使|つか}えるものがいいです。
- **たとえば～のような／みたいな + noun** - the example modifies a noun.
  たとえば{f|京都|きょうと}のような{f|古|ふる}い{f|町|まち}
- **たとえば～なら** - set up a hypothetical case and then say what happens in it.
  たとえば{f|雨|あめ}なら、{f|中|なか}でやりましょう。

In kanji it is {f|例|たと}えば, and both spellings are completely normal: printed
prose and official documents tend to use the kanji, casual writing the kana. The
reading and the meaning are identical either way.

Do not confuse it with `たとえ`, which is a different word entirely - see the
contrast below.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|甘|あま}いものが{f|好|す}きです。<b>たとえば</b>、チョコレートやアイスです。
I like sweet things - chocolate and ice cream, for example.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>たとえば</b>、この{f|漢字|かんじ}は{f|読|よ}み{f|方|かた}が{f|三|みっ}つあります。
This kanji, for instance, has three different readings.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|休|やす}みの{f|日|ひ}にできること、<b>たとえば</b>{f|散歩|さんぽ}とか{f|料理|りょうり}とかを{f|増|ふ}やしたい。
I want more things I can do on a day off - walks, cooking, that sort of thing.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>たとえば</b>{f|地震|じしん}が{f|起|お}きたら、まず{f|机|つくえ}の{f|下|した}に{f|入|はい}ってください。
Say an earthquake happens - the first thing to do is get under a desk.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral. Nothing about `たとえば` marks formality, age or gender, and it
appears unchanged in academic writing and in chat with friends.

Two conversational uses are worth memorising whole.
「たとえばの{f|話|はなし}だけど」 prefaces a hypothetical you are not committing to - 
"just supposing, though". And 「たとえば?」 said alone is a request for one: "such
as?" That one-word question is enormously useful and very common.

In writing, a `たとえば` sentence is usually short and ends with `など`, `といった`
or a plain noun phrase, and the paragraph then returns to the general point. If
you find yourself giving three sentences of example, consider {f|具体|ぐたい}{f|的|てき}には
("specifically") instead, which promises a fuller account.

`たとえば` presents one member of a set. When you want to say that your example is
the *representative* one - the first of many, the flagship - Japanese has
<code>をはじめ</code> for that, and it is not interchangeable.

## Contrast

### たとえば vs たとえ○○ても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たとえば` introduces an example. `たとえ` introduces a
concession, and it always has a `ても` waiting for it later in the sentence.

</div>

<pre>
<b>たとえば</b>{f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ったら、どうしますか。
If it rains tomorrow, say, what will you do?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>たとえ</b>{f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っ<b>ても</b>、{f|試合|しあい}はやります。
Even if it rains tomorrow, the match is going ahead.
</pre>

The first sentence asks about one possible case among many; the second brushes the
case aside. The `ば` is the whole difference in writing, and in speech the giveaway
is the `ても` at the far end: if you hear one, the word at the front was `たとえ`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ たとえ{f|日本料理|にほんりょうり}が{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ <b>たとえば</b>{f|日本料理|にほんりょうり}が{f|好|す}きです。
  Without the `ば` the word means "even if", and the sentence is left dangling with
  nothing for it to concede to.

- ❌ たとえば、{f|日本|にほん}の{f|人口|じんこう}は{f|約|やく}{f|一億|いちおく}{f|二千万人|にせんまんにん}です。 → ✅ {f|日本|にほん}の{f|人口|じんこう}は{f|約|やく}{f|一億|いちおく}{f|二千万人|にせんまんにん}です。
  A single hard fact is not an example of anything. `たとえば` needs a general
  statement in front of it for the example to illustrate.

- ❌ {f|野菜|やさい}、たとえばにんじんをはじめ{f|買|か}いました。 → ✅ {f|野菜|やさい}、<b>たとえば</b>にんじんなどを{f|買|か}いました。
  `たとえば` pairs with `など`, `とか` and `といった`. <code>をはじめ</code> is a
  different pattern meaning "starting with", and the two cannot be stacked.

## Practice

**1.** {f|運動|うんどう}{f|不足|ぶそく}なので、____{f|階段|かいだん}を{f|使|つか}うようにしています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>たとえば</b>{f|階段|かいだん}を{f|使|つか}うようにしています。

The stairs are one concrete instance of the general problem stated first, which is
what this word is for. Note that the example still needs the general statement in
front of it to lean on.

</details>

**2.** Choose the right word: （たとえ・たとえば）{f|失敗|しっぱい}しても、やってみる{f|価値|かち}はある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>たとえ</b>{f|失敗|しっぱい}しても、やってみる{f|価値|かち}はある。

The `ても` later in the sentence is the tell: this is a concession, "even if I
fail", not an illustration. `たとえば` would leave the `ても` with no partner.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|静|しず}かな{f|町|まち}、たとえば{f|奈良|なら}のような{f|町|まち}に{f|住|す}みたい。
(b) {f|静|しず}かな{f|町|まち}、{f|奈良|なら}に{f|住|す}みたい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (a) uses Nara as an illustration of the kind of town the speaker wants;
other quiet towns would do just as well, and nobody would be surprised if they
ended up somewhere else. Sentence (b) names Nara as the actual destination and
describes it as quiet along the way. The example frame is what keeps the choice
open.

</details>
