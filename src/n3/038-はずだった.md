# ～はずだった

**Meaning** · it was supposed to ~, and it didn't - a well-grounded expectation that the world declined to meet

At N4, `はずだ` was an expectation you could justify if asked: a timetable, a promise, a
rule, a fact somebody told you. `はずだった` keeps the grounds and loses the outcome. It
says *this is what should have followed from what I knew* - and the fact that you are
saying it in the past tense tells the listener that it did not.

Where `つもりだった` reports a plan of your own that came to nothing, `はずだった` reports
the world failing to behave as the evidence promised. Nobody's will is involved, which
is why it works for parcels, trains and weather forecasts.

## Formation

`はず` is a noun, so the clause in front takes noun-modifying shapes: `な` for
な-adjectives, `の` for nouns.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain non-past + はずだった | {f|届|とど}く → {f|届|とど}くはずだった |
| Verb (past) | た-form + はずだった | {f|入|い}れた → {f|入|い}れたはずだった |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + はずだった | {f|来|こ}ない → {f|来|こ}ないはずだった |
| い-adjective | plain form + はずだった | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}いはずだった |
| な-adjective | stem + **な** + はずだった | {f|簡単|かんたん} → {f|簡単|かんたん}なはずだった |
| Noun | Noun + **の** + はずだった | {f|休|やす}み → {f|休|やす}みのはずだった |
| Polite | はずでした | {f|届|とど}くはずでした |
| With the "but" spelled out | はずだったのに／はずだったが | {f|届|とど}くはずだったのに |
| Set phrase | こんなはずではなかった | |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - say `はずだった` and you have as good as said the rest. If the
expectation *was* met, you do not use this pattern at all - you report the fact, or say
{f|予定|よてい}どおり. Using `はずだった` about something that worked out puzzles the
listener, who is still waiting for the "but".

</div>

## Nuance & register

`はずだった` is register-neutral and travels everywhere, which makes it unusually useful
at work. The polite `はずでした` plus a trailing `が` is the standard way of raising a
problem without accusing anybody: {f|本日|ほんじつ}{f|午前中|ごぜんちゅう}に{f|届|とど}く
はずでしたが、まだ{f|確認|かくにん}できておりません。 It is firm - you are pointing out
that a commitment was not kept - while attributing the failure to the situation rather
than to the person you are speaking to. That combination is hard to achieve in English
and easy here.

Two flavours are worth separating. With a **non-past** clause in front, the pattern is
about a scheduled or predicted event that failed to occur. With a **past** clause
(<code>{f|渡|わた}したはずだった</code>) it is about your own confident memory being
wrong - "I was sure I had." That second one shades into `～たつもりだった`, and the
difference is where the confidence came from: `つもり` from your intention, `はず` from
evidence you thought you had.

The negative deserves its own line. <code>こんなはずではなかった</code> - "this isn't how
it was supposed to go" - is a fixed phrase, slightly dramatic, and extremely common in
novels, films and genuine bad days. Its casual form is
<code>こんなはずじゃなかった</code>.

One honest qualification. In a narrative, `はずだった` can simply report what was
expected at a point in the story, and the next sentence may confirm it. But in
conversation, standing alone, it is heard as a defeated expectation almost without
exception - so treat the failure reading as the default and do not rely on the neutral
one.

## Contrast

### ～はずだった vs ～つもりだった

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `つもり` is what somebody **decided**; `はず` is what the **evidence
implied**. Only a being with a will can have a `つもり`; anything at all can have a
`はず`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}{f|休|やす}みを{f|取|と}る<b>つもりだった</b>んですが、{f|仕事|しごと}が{f|入|はい}りました。
I'd been meaning to take tomorrow off, but work came up.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|会社|かいしゃ}が{f|休|やす}みの<b>はずだった</b>んですが、{f|出勤|しゅっきん}になりました。
Tomorrow was supposed to be a company holiday, but now we're working.
</pre>

The first is about your own decision; the second about the calendar. Note what happens
when you get it backwards: ❌<code>{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|遅|おく}れるつもりだった</code>
attributes an intention to a train, which is not merely unusual but funny.

### ～はずだった vs ～{f|予定|よてい}だった

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|予定|よてい}だった</code> reports a **schedule** and says
nothing about whether it held. `はずだった` reports an **expectation** and implies it
did not.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|大阪|おおさか}に{f|出張|しゅっちょう}する<b>{f|予定|よてい}でした</b>が、{f|中止|ちゅうし}になりました。
I was scheduled to go to Osaka next week, but it's been called off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|飛行機|ひこうき}は{f|十時|じゅうじ}に{f|着|つ}く<b>はずでした</b>。
The plane was supposed to land at ten.
</pre>

<code>{f|予定|よてい}だった</code> can be followed by "…and that's what happened", which
is why it is the neutral word for an itinerary. `はずでした` said on its own already
tells the listener the plane is late. If the flight landed on time, you say
{f|予定|よてい}どおり{f|十時|じゅうじ}に{f|着|つ}きました and the `はず` never appears.

### ～はずだった vs ～ことになっていた

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ことになっていた</code> reports an **arrangement** somebody
made - a rule, an agreement, a rota. `はずだった` reports your **inference** from
whatever grounds you had, which may include such an arrangement.

</div>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|私|わたし}が{f|担当|たんとう}する<b>ことになっていた</b>のに、{f|誰|だれ}も{f|教|おし}えてくれなかった。
I was down to cover reception, and nobody told me.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}には{f|誰|だれ}かいる<b>はずだった</b>のに、{f|席|せき}が{f|空|あ}いていた。
There was supposed to be somebody on reception, but the desk was empty.
</pre>

The first points at a decision that was taken; the second at a conclusion you drew.
`ことになっていた` is the better choice whenever you can name who agreed to what, and it
is the safer one in a workplace because it appeals to a shared arrangement rather than
to your own reasoning.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|簡単|かんたん}はずだったのに。 → ✅ {f|簡単|かんたん}<b>な</b>はずだったのに。
  `はず` is a noun, so a な-adjective needs `な` and a noun needs `の`
  ({f|休|やす}みのはず). Compare `かもしれない`, which takes both bare.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|七時|しちじ}に{f|起|お}きるはずだった。（meaning "I meant to get up at seven"） → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|七時|しちじ}に{f|起|お}きる<b>つもりだった</b>。
  Your own resolve is `つもり`. `はず` would need external grounds - an alarm somebody
  else set, a schedule you were given.

- ❌ {f|予定|よてい}どおり{f|三時|さんじ}に{f|着|つ}くはずでした。（meaning "and we did"） → ✅ {f|予定|よてい}どおり{f|三時|さんじ}に<b>{f|着|つ}きました</b>。
  `はずでした` announces that the expectation was not met. If it was, simply report what
  happened.

- ❌ こんなはずじゃなかったです。 → ✅ こんなはず<b>ではありませんでした</b>。
  The casual contraction under a polite `です` clashes. In careful speech, expand it - 
  or keep the whole sentence casual.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|各駅|かくえき}に{f|止|と}まらない____のに、{f|次|つぎ}の{f|駅|えき}で{f|止|と}まった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|止|と}まらない<b>はずだった</b>のに

The expectation came from what kind of train it is, not from anyone's intention, so
`はず` is the right noun. `のに` then supplies the contradiction that the second half
spells out.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|今年|ことし}こそ{f|海外|かいがい}{f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}く（つもりだった・はずだった）のに、{f|結局|けっきょく}{f|行|い}けなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|行|い}く<b>つもりだった</b>のに

<code>{f|今年|ことし}こそ</code> marks this as the speaker's own resolve, and resolve is
`つもり` territory. `はずだった` would need something external - a booking already made,
a trip somebody else had arranged for you.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The package was supposed to arrive on Friday, but it still hasn't come."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|金曜日|きんようび}に{f|届|とど}く<b>はずでした</b>が、まだ{f|届|とど}いていません。

The expectation rests on a delivery date, so `はず` rather than `つもり`. The polite
`はずでした` plus `が` is the standard shape for raising this kind of problem without
blaming the person you are speaking to.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|休|やす}みはずでしたが、{f|急|きゅう}に{f|行|おこな}われました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}は<b>{f|中止|ちゅうし}のはず</b>でしたが、{f|急|きゅう}に{f|行|おこな}われました。

Two things: a noun before `はず` needs `の`, and <code>{f|休|やす}み</code> is odd for a
meeting - a cancelled meeting is <code>{f|中止|ちゅうし}</code>. The `はずでした` frame
itself is exactly right.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|部屋|へや}の{f|鍵|かぎ}は{f|閉|し}めた<b>はずだった</b>。
(b) この{f|部屋|へや}の{f|鍵|かぎ}は{f|閉|し}めた<b>つもりだった</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) rests on evidence: the speaker remembers the click, or knows it is on their checklist
every evening. The surprise is that the facts they were relying on have let them down.

(b) rests on intention: the speaker meant to lock it and believed they had followed
through. It admits more personal responsibility, which is why (b) is the better sentence
in an apology and (a) the better one when you are genuinely baffled.

</details>
