# ～つもりだった

**Meaning** · I had meant to (but didn't); I thought I had (but I hadn't) - an intention or a belief that reality declined to confirm

`つもり` is what is in your head. Put it in the past and you announce that what was in
your head and what happened in the world came apart. Nothing in the words says the plan
failed - but the past tense sets up a "but" so reliably that a Japanese listener hears
it before you finish the sentence.

The pattern has **two** readings, and which one you get depends entirely on the tense of
the verb in front:

- <code>{f|出|だ}すつもりだった</code> - I meant to send it. (I didn't.)
- <code>{f|出|だ}したつもりだった</code> - I thought I had sent it. (I hadn't.)

## Formation

`つもり` is a noun, so what precedes it takes the shape a noun-modifying clause takes:
な-adjectives get `な`, nouns get `の`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb - intention | plain non-past + つもりだった | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}くつもりだった |
| Verb - intention (negative) | ない-form + つもりだった | {f|言|い}わない → {f|言|い}わないつもりだった |
| Verb - mistaken belief | plain past + つもりだった | かけた → かけたつもりだった |
| Verb - belief about a state | ている + つもりだった | やっている → やっているつもりだった |
| い-adjective | plain form + つもりだった | {f|優|やさ}しい → {f|優|やさ}しいつもりだった |
| な-adjective | stem + **な** + つもりだった | {f|親切|しんせつ} → {f|親切|しんせつ}なつもりだった |
| Noun | Noun + **の** + つもりだった | {f|冗談|じょうだん} → {f|冗談|じょうだん}のつもりだった |
| Polite | つもりでした | {f|行|い}くつもりでした |
| With the "but" spelled out | つもりだったのに／つもりだったが | {f|行|い}くつもりだったのに |
| Denying an intention | ～つもりはなかった | {f|傷|きず}つける → {f|傷|きず}つけるつもりはなかった |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `つもり` needs a **will** behind it. Only something that can decide
things can have one. A train, a parcel or the weather cannot: for those the pattern is
`はずだった`.

</div>

## Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral - `つもりでした` sits comfortably in a business email - 
but it is socially loaded, because most of the time you are using it to explain
yourself.

The intention reading is the everyday softener of excuses. The full apology shape is
`～つもりだったんですが` with a trailing clause: {f|連絡|れんらく}するつもりだったんです
が、{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|長|なが}びいてしまって。 The unfinished ending is deliberate. It
lets the listener supply the conclusion, which is more polite than announcing it.

The mistaken-belief reading (`～たつもりだった`) is the Japanese for "I could have sworn
I…". It is honest and unglamorous, and it is what you say when you are the one who
dropped something. Its slightly sharper cousin is `～ているつもりだった` - "I thought I
was doing that" - which comes up when somebody tells you your effort has not shown:
{f|気|き}をつけているつもりだったんですが。

There is one construction here you should learn as a unit: <code>～つもりはなかった</code>,
"I didn't mean to." {f|傷|きず}つけるつもりはありませんでした is the standard way to
apologise for an accidental hurt. Note the `は`: it is contrastive, and it does the work
of "whatever else I may have done, that was not my intention." The casual version is
`つもりじゃなかった`.

<div class="note">

**Note** - do not confuse this with `～たつもりで`, a separate idiom meaning "as if you
had": {f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}ったつもりで{f|貯金|ちょきん}する - "save the money as
though you'd taken the trip." The `で` form is a way of pretending on purpose; the
`だった` form is finding out you were wrong.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～つもりだった vs ～はずだった

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `つもり` is what **you decided**. `はず` is what the **facts led you
to expect**. If somebody asked どうして？ and your answer would be "because I'd made up
my mind to", it is `つもり`; if it would be "because that's what the schedule said", it
is `はず`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|七時|しちじ}の{f|電車|でんしゃ}に{f|乗|の}る<b>つもりだった</b>んだけど、{f|寝坊|ねぼう}した。
I'd meant to catch the seven o'clock train, but I overslept.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|七時|しちじ}の{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|八時|はちじ}に{f|着|つ}く<b>はずだった</b>のに、まだ{f|動|うご}かない。
The seven o'clock was supposed to arrive at eight, and it still hasn't moved.
</pre>

Only a person can hold an intention, which is why the second sentence cannot take
`つもり`. Trains have timetables, not plans.

### ～るつもりだった vs ～たつもりだった

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - non-past in front means the action was never performed. Past in
front means you believe it was performed, and you were wrong.

</div>

<pre>
{f|窓|まど}を<b>{f|閉|し}めるつもりだった</b>。
I meant to close the window.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|窓|まど}を<b>{f|閉|し}めたつもりだった</b>。
I thought I'd closed the window.
</pre>

In the first, the window was never touched. In the second, you did something to it and
it wasn't enough. That is a real difference in what you are confessing to, and getting
it backwards in an apology gives entirely the wrong impression of how careful you were.

### ～つもりだった vs ～(よ)うと{f|思|おも}っていた

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `つもりだった` is a **decision** you were carrying around.
<code>～(よ)うと{f|思|おも}っていた</code> is a **thought** you were entertaining. The
second is vaguer, and therefore softer.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}す<b>つもりだった</b>んですが、{f|事情|じじょう}が{f|変|か}わりました。
I'd been planning to move next month, but circumstances changed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}そうと{f|思|おも}っていたんですが、まだ{f|決|き}めていません。
I'd been thinking of moving next month, but I haven't decided yet.
</pre>

Because `つもり` reports something settled, it can sound firm when you are asked about
your plans by somebody with authority over them. If you want to leave yourself room,
<code>{f|思|おも}っていた</code> is the more diplomatic choice.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|行|い}きますつもりでした。 → ✅ {f|行|い}<b>く</b>つもりでした。
  `つもり` is a noun and takes a plain noun-modifying clause. The politeness goes on the
  end of the sentence, never inside it.

- ❌ {f|冗談|じょうだん}つもりだったんです。 → ✅ {f|冗談|じょうだん}<b>の</b>つもりだったんです。
  A noun in front of `つもり` needs `の`; a な-adjective needs `な`
  ({f|親切|しんせつ}なつもり).

- ❌ {f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|三時|さんじ}に{f|着|つ}くつもりだった。 → ✅ {f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|三時|さんじ}に{f|着|つ}く<b>はずだった</b>。
  `つもり` requires somebody with a will. Timetables, deliveries and the weather take
  `はずだった`.

- ❌ {f|怒|おこ}らせるつもりじゃなかったです。 → ✅ {f|怒|おこ}らせるつもり<b>はありませんでした</b>。
  The meaning is right but the registers clash: casual `じゃなかった` under a polite
  `です`. In an apology, use the full form.

## Practice

**1.** {f|昨日|きのう}{f|返事|へんじ}を____んですが、{f|忙|いそが}しくてできませんでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|返事|へんじ}を<b>{f|書|か}くつもりだった</b>んですが

The reply was never written, so the verb in front stays non-past. `んですが` with a
trailing explanation is the standard apology frame for an intention that fell through.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|昨日|きのう}{f|届|とど}く（つもりだった・はずだった）のに、まだ{f|来|き}ません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨日|きのう}{f|届|とど}く<b>はずだった</b>のに

A parcel has no intentions. The expectation came from a delivery date, which is exactly
what `はずだった` reports.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I thought I'd turned the air conditioning off, but it was still running."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

エアコンを<b>{f|消|け}したつもりだった</b>のに、まだついていた。

The past-tense verb in front is what gives the "I thought I had" reading. With
<code>{f|消|け}すつもりだった</code> the sentence would mean you never touched it, which
contradicts the second half.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: すみません、{f|失礼|しつれい}なことを{f|言|い}うつもりでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|失礼|しつれい}なことを{f|言|い}うつもり<b>はありませんでした</b>。

As written, the sentence apologises by announcing that being rude was the plan. To deny
an intention you need <code>つもりはない</code> in the past, with the contrastive `は`
carrying "that, at least, was not what I meant."

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|部屋|へや}を<b>{f|片付|かたづ}けるつもりだった</b>。
(b) {f|部屋|へや}を<b>{f|片付|かたづ}けたつもりだった</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the tidying never began. The speaker had it on their list, the day got away
from them, and the room is exactly as it was.

(b) says the speaker did tidy - and then somebody looked at the room and disagreed. It
is a confession of a **judgement** that was wrong rather than a plan that was dropped,
and it carries a faint note of being caught out.

</details>
