# 決して～ない

**Meaning** · never; not under any circumstances - a categorical denial or an
absolute commitment

{f|決|けっ}して does not make a sentence negative; the verb does that. What
{f|決|けっ}して adds is *finality*. It closes the door on exceptions, which is why
it belongs to promises, warnings, rules and firm denials rather than to ordinary
reporting.

## Formation

{f|決|けっ}して is an adverb placed before the predicate, and the predicate must be
negative. Everything else about the sentence is unchanged.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain) | {f|決|けっ}して + ない-form | {f|忘|わす}れる → {f|決|けっ}して{f|忘|わす}れない |
| Verb (polite) | {f|決|けっ}して + ません | {f|言|い}う → {f|決|けっ}して{f|言|い}いません |
| Verb (past) | {f|決|けっ}して + なかった／ませんでした | {f|許|ゆる}す → {f|決|けっ}して{f|許|ゆる}さなかった |
| Verb (potential) | {f|決|けっ}して + ～られない | {f|忘|わす}れる → {f|決|けっ}して{f|忘|わす}れられない |
| い-adjective | {f|決|けっ}して + くない | {f|安|やす}い → {f|決|けっ}して{f|安|やす}くない |
| な-adjective・Noun | {f|決|けっ}して + ではない／じゃない | {f|無駄|むだ} → {f|決|けっ}して{f|無駄|むだ}ではない |
| Prohibition | {f|決|けっ}して + ないでください／てはいけない | {f|触|さわ}る → {f|決|けっ}して{f|触|さわ}らないでください |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - no negative, no {f|決|けっ}して. There is no affirmative use of
this word in modern Japanese, so ❌{f|決|けっ}して{f|行|い}きます is not a weaker
version of anything - it is simply broken.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the negative does not have to sit immediately next to
{f|決|けっ}して, but it does have to be in the same clause.
{f|決|けっ}して{f|人|ひと}の{f|悪口|わるぐち}を{f|言|い}わない is fine: the adverb
is at the front, the negation at the end, and everything between them is inside
the scope.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|約束|やくそく}は<b>{f|決|けっ}して{f|忘|わす}れません</b>。
I will never forget this promise.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|決|けっ}して</b>{f|弱音|よわね}を{f|吐|は}か<b>ない</b>。
He never lets on that he's struggling.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}は<b>{f|決|けっ}して</b>{f|安|やす}く<b>ない</b>が、{f|作|つく}りはしっかりしている。
It is by no means cheap, but it's solidly made.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|中|なか}の{f|水|みず}は<b>{f|決|けっ}して</b>{f|飲|の}ま<b>ないでください</b>。
Under no circumstances drink the water inside.
</pre>

<pre>
あのときいただいたご{f|恩|おん}を、{f|私|わたし}は<b>{f|決|けっ}して</b>{f|忘|わす}れ<b>ません</b>。
I will never forget the kindness you showed me then.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|失敗|しっぱい}したからといって、それが<b>{f|決|けっ}して</b>{f|無駄|むだ}だった<b>わけではありません</b>。
Just because it failed doesn't mean it was wasted effort.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|安全|あんぜん}のため、{f|走行中|そうこうちゅう}は<b>{f|決|けっ}して</b>{f|席|せき}をお{f|立|た}ちに<b>ならないでください</b>。
For your safety, please do not leave your seat at any time while the vehicle is moving.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

{f|決|けっ}して is emphatic and leans formal. It is the natural word in written
rules, safety notices, apologies, contracts and public statements, and it is what
you reach for in speech when you want to be heard as making a commitment rather
than a remark. Among friends, the everyday word for the same force is
{f|絶対|ぜったい}に.

Because it is absolute, it is also a favourite of denial. A politician saying
{f|決|けっ}してそのような{f|事実|じじつ}はございません is not saying "probably
not" - and the very strength of the word is why it can sound defensive when
overused.

Two smaller points. The kana spelling けっして is common in casual writing, and the
kanji {f|決|けっ}して in print; the reading is the same. And in formal writing you
will meet {f|決|けっ}して～まい, an old-fashioned negative-volitional pairing
meaning "I shall never": {f|決|けっ}して{f|忘|わす}れまい. Recognise it; you do not
need to produce it.

## Contrast

### {f|決|けっ}して vs {f|絶対|ぜったい}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - {f|絶対|ぜったい}に works with both affirmatives and negatives.
{f|決|けっ}して works only with negatives, and sounds more formal doing it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|絶対|ぜったい}に<b>{f|勝|か}ちます</b>。
We are absolutely going to win.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|決|けっ}して</b>あきらめ<b>ません</b>。
I will never give up.
</pre>

Note that ❌{f|決|けっ}して{f|勝|か}ちます is impossible while
{f|絶対|ぜったい}に{f|負|ま}けません is perfectly natural - so
{f|絶対|ぜったい}に is the more flexible word, and the one you will hear far more
often in conversation. In writing, {f|決|けっ}して is the more dignified of the two.

### {f|決|けっ}して vs {f|全然|ぜんぜん}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - {f|全然|ぜんぜん} measures **degree**: not at all, right now.
{f|決|けっ}して makes a claim about **every occasion**: not ever, no exceptions.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|問題|もんだい}は<b>{f|全然|ぜんぜん}</b>{f|分|わ}かり<b>ません</b>。
I don't understand this question at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|嘘|うそ}は<b>{f|決|けっ}して</b>つき<b>ません</b>。
I never tell lies.
</pre>

Putting {f|全然|ぜんぜん} in the second sentence would say your lies are
unconvincing rather than absent. And {f|決|けっ}して in the first would turn a
statement about today's homework into a lifelong vow of incomprehension.

### {f|決|けっ}して vs めったに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both take a negative, but めったに leaves room for exceptions
and {f|決|けっ}して does not.

</div>

<pre>
{f|父|ちち}は<b>めったに</b>{f|怒|おこ}ら<b>ない</b>。
My father hardly ever gets angry.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|父|ちち}は<b>{f|決|けっ}して</b>{f|怒|おこ}ら<b>ない</b>。
My father never gets angry - not once.
</pre>

めったに is a frequency report; {f|決|けっ}して is a claim about principle or
character. If you can imagine a counter-example, you want めったに.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|決|けっ}して{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ <b>{f|絶対|ぜったい}に</b>{f|行|い}きます。
  The word cannot appear with an affirmative predicate at all. For an emphatic
  positive promise, {f|絶対|ぜったい}に is the word.

- ❌ {f|決|けっ}して{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ {f|決|けっ}して{f|嫌|きら}い<b>ではありません</b>。
  The same trap with an adjective. Note how the natural fix flips the sentence into
  a denial, which is the shape this adverb always wants.

- ❌ {f|週|しゅう}に{f|一回|いっかい}ぐらいしか{f|決|けっ}して{f|行|い}きません。 → ✅ {f|週|しゅう}に{f|一回|いっかい}ぐらいしか{f|行|い}きません。
  A stated frequency contradicts an absolute denial. If exceptions exist, use
  めったに or あまり instead.

- ❌ {f|決|けっ}して{f|忘|わす}れないでください、{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}です。 → ✅ {f|忘|わす}れないでくださいね。
  In a light, friendly reminder {f|決|けっ}して is far too heavy - it sounds as
  though a serious consequence is waiting. Keep it for real warnings.

## Practice

**1.** {f|安全|あんぜん}のため、{f|工事中|こうじちゅう}はこの{f|線|せん}より{f|中|なか}に____{f|入|はい}ら____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|線|せん}より{f|中|なか}に<b>{f|決|けっ}して</b>{f|入|はい}ら<b>ないでください</b>。

The adverb and the negative are one frame: the first blank supplies the absolute
force and the second supplies the negation it needs. A safety notice is precisely
the register this word belongs to.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: {f|母|はは}は（{f|決|けっ}して・めったに）{f|映画館|えいがかん}に{f|行|い}きません。{f|年|ねん}に{f|一度|いちど}ぐらいでしょうか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|母|はは}は<b>めったに</b>{f|映画館|えいがかん}に{f|行|い}きません。

The second sentence admits about one visit a year, so the denial cannot be
absolute. {f|決|けっ}して would contradict the very next sentence.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I will never say anything like that again."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|決|けっ}して</b>{f|二度|にど}とそんなことは{f|言|い}い<b>ません</b>。
（or simply {f|決|けっ}してそんなことは{f|言|い}いません。）

The English "never … again" is a promise about all future occasions, which is what
this adverb is for. {f|二度|にど}と reinforces it, and the sentence still needs the
negative ending to be grammatical.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}の{f|作品|さくひん}は{f|決|けっ}して{f|素晴|すば}らしいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}の{f|作品|さくひん}は{f|決|けっ}して{f|悪|わる}く<b>ありません</b>。
（or, if praise was intended: {f|彼|かれ}の{f|作品|さくひん}は{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|素晴|すば}らしいです。）

The original pairs an absolute-denial adverb with a positive adjective, which is
ungrammatical. Either make the predicate negative, or drop the adverb and use an
ordinary intensifier.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|方法|ほうほう}は{f|決|けっ}して{f|難|むずか}しくありません。
(b) この{f|方法|ほうほう}は{f|全然|ぜんぜん}{f|難|むずか}しくありません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both deny that the method is hard. Sentence (b) is a plain, casual measurement of
difficulty - there is none. Sentence (a) sounds like a rebuttal: someone has
suggested the method is difficult, and the speaker is closing that idea down, in
the slightly formal register of a manual or a presentation.

</details>
