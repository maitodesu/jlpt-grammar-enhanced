# その結果

**Meaning** · as a result - what came out of the process just described

<code>その{f|結果|けっか}</code> is a connector between sentences. The sentence before it
describes something that ran its course - an effort, an investigation, a
negotiation, a habit kept up for years - and <code>その{f|結果|けっか}</code> announces
what that produced. It is neutral about whether the outcome was welcome; it reports
outcomes, it does not evaluate them.

## Formation

<code>その{f|結果|けっか}</code> is a fixed phrase, not a suffix, so nothing conjugates
in front of it. What the table below lists is the **position** it can occupy.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A completed sentence | ～。その{f|結果|けっか}、～。 | {f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}った。その{f|結果|けっか}、{f|方針|ほうしん}が{f|決|き}まった。 |
| A clause, mid-sentence | ～し／～て、その{f|結果|けっか}、～ | {f|値上|ねあ}げをして、その{f|結果|けっか}、{f|客|きゃく}が{f|減|へ}った |
| Formal written variant | その{f|結果|けっか}として | その{f|結果|けっか}として{f|見直|みなお}しが{f|必要|ひつよう}になった |
| Bare connective (speech) | {f|結果|けっか}、～ | {f|結果|けっか}、{f|何|なに}も{f|変|か}わらなかった |
| As an ordinary noun | ～のは、その{f|結果|けっか}だ | {f|今|いま}の{f|人手|ひとで}{f|不足|ぶそく}はその{f|結果|けっか}です |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the comma after <code>その{f|結果|けっか}</code> is standard and worth
writing. There is no ❌<code>その{f|結果|けっか}で</code> and no
❌<code>その{f|結果|けっか}に</code> in this connective use; the phrase stands on its own.
Bare <code>{f|結果|けっか}、</code> without `その` is common in speech and journalism and
sounds a little more clipped and modern.

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is a **written and formal-spoken** connector: reports, minutes, news, a
presentation, the explanatory half of an essay. In relaxed conversation it sounds
like somebody quoting a document, and the natural spoken equivalents are `それで`
and `で`. That said, it is not stiff in the way keigo is stiff - a colleague
summarising what a meeting decided will use it happily.

Two things govern whether it fits. First, the sentence in front has to describe a
**process**, not a single flat fact. Years of effort, repeated attempts,
negotiations, a policy left running: something with duration or intention behind it.
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った。その{f|結果|けっか}、… is odd for exactly this reason - rain
is an event, not a process anybody conducted.

Second, it is **evaluatively neutral**. Unlike `おかげで` and `せいで`, it takes no
view: the outcome that follows can be a triumph, a disaster or a set of numbers.
That neutrality is what makes it the natural connector for a report on something
that went badly, where naming a culprit would be out of place.

One habit worth copying from native writing: <code>その{f|結果|けっか}</code> very often
introduces a clause ending in <code>～ことになった</code>,
<code>～ようになった</code> or <code>～という{f|結果|けっか}になった</code>. The pattern likes
outcomes stated as settled states of affairs rather than as momentary actions.

## Contrast

### その{f|結果|けっか} vs そのため

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `そのため` treats the previous sentence as a **cause** and can be
followed by anything, including a plan or an instruction.
<code>その{f|結果|けっか}</code> treats it as a **process** and can only be followed by
its outcome.

</div>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}が{f|近|ちか}づいています。<b>そのため</b>、{f|明日|あした}の{f|便|びん}は{f|欠航|けっこう}となります。
A typhoon is approaching. Accordingly, tomorrow's flights are cancelled.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|欠航|けっこう}が{f|三日|みっか}{f|続|つづ}きました。<b>その{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|空港|くうこう}に{f|一万人|いちまんにん}{f|近|ちか}い{f|客|きゃく}が{f|足止|あしど}めされました。
Flights were cancelled for three days, and as a result nearly ten thousand passengers were stranded at the airport.
</pre>

Swap them and you feel the difference immediately. ❌An approaching typhoon is not a
process whose *result* is a cancellation - the cancellation is a decision taken
because of it. Three days of cancellations, on the other hand, is a situation that
ran on and produced something.

`そのため` is also the one you can put in front of a measure to be taken:
そのため、{f|窓口|まどぐち}を{f|増|ふ}やすことにいたしました。

### その{f|結果|けっか} vs それで・だから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `それで` and `だから` are conversation; <code>その{f|結果|けっか}</code>
is report. `だから` also carries the speaker's own reasoning in a way the other two
do not.

</div>

<pre>
{f|財布|さいふ}を{f|忘|わす}れちゃって。<b>それで</b>、{f|一度|いちど}{f|家|いえ}に{f|戻|もど}ったんだ。
I forgot my wallet - so I went back home for it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|調査|ちょうさ}を{f|三年|さんねん}{f|続|つづ}けた。<b>その{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|同|おな}じ{f|傾向|けいこう}が{f|各地|かくち}で{f|確認|かくにん}された。
The survey ran for three years, and as a result the same trend was confirmed across the country.
</pre>

Reading the first sentence with <code>その{f|結果|けっか}</code> in it is comic: it
promotes forgetting your wallet to the status of a research programme. Conversely,
`だから` in a report reads as though the writer were arguing with the reader rather
than presenting findings.

### その{f|結果|けっか} vs {f|結局|けっきょく}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>その{f|結果|けっか}</code> says "and this is what it produced".
<code>{f|結局|けっきょく}</code> says "and in the end, after all that". The second carries
a shrug: the effort was long, and often it did not get you where you hoped.

</div>

<pre>
いろいろ{f|試|ため}してみたが、<b>{f|結局|けっきょく}</b>{f|元|もと}のやり{f|方|かた}に{f|戻|もど}した。
We tried all sorts of things, but in the end we went back to the old way of doing it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|手順|てじゅん}を{f|見直|みなお}した。<b>その{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|作業|さぎょう}{f|時間|じかん}が{f|三割|さんわり}{f|短|みじか}くなった。
We reviewed the procedure, and as a result the job now takes thirty per cent less time.
</pre>

<code>{f|結局|けっきょく}</code> is also an adverb rather than a connector, so it sits
inside its clause and needs no comma after it, and it is perfectly at home in casual
speech. If your English is "in the end" or "it turned out that after all", it is
<code>{f|結局|けっきょく}</code>; if it is "as a result", it is
<code>その{f|結果|けっか}</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った。その{f|結果|けっか}、{f|試合|しあい}が{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}の<b>ため</b>、{f|試合|しあい}が{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。
  A shower is an event, not a process with an outcome. Use a cause word.

- ❌ その{f|結果|けっか}で、{f|方針|ほうしん}が{f|変|か}わりました。 → ✅ その{f|結果|けっか}、{f|方針|ほうしん}が{f|変|か}わりました。
  As a connector the phrase takes no particle after it - just a comma.

- ❌ その{f|結果|けっか}、{f|明日|あした}{f|九時|くじ}に{f|集|あつ}まってください。 → ✅ <b>そのため</b>、{f|明日|あした}{f|九時|くじ}に{f|集|あつ}まってください。
  An instruction is not an outcome. `そのため` accepts one; this phrase does not.

- ❌ お{f|腹|なか}がすいた。その{f|結果|けっか}、コンビニでパンを{f|買|か}った。 → ✅ お{f|腹|なか}がすいた。<b>それで</b>、コンビニでパンを{f|買|か}った。
  Both the register and the missing process are wrong. This is `それで` territory.

## Practice

**1.** {f|二年|にねん}かけて{f|海外|かいがい}の{f|取引先|とりひきさき}を{f|開拓|かいたく}した。____、{f|輸出|ゆしゅつ}が{f|売上|うりあげ}の{f|四割|よんわり}を{f|占|し}めるようになった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>その{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|輸出|ゆしゅつ}が{f|売上|うりあげ}の{f|四割|よんわり}を{f|占|し}めるようになった。

Two years of deliberate work is exactly the kind of extended process this connector
wants, and the second clause states the settled outcome with
<code>ようになった</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|工事|こうじ}が{f|長引|ながび}いています。（その{f|結果|けっか}・そのため）、{f|来週|らいしゅう}から{f|入口|いりぐち}を{f|変更|へんこう}いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|工事|こうじ}が{f|長引|ながび}いています。<b>そのため</b>、{f|来週|らいしゅう}から{f|入口|いりぐち}を{f|変更|へんこう}いたします。

What follows is a **measure being taken**, not something that came out of a process,
so the cause connector is the right one. <code>その{f|結果|けっか}</code> would suggest
the change of entrance simply emerged from the delay by itself.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "We repeated the experiment ten times. As a result, we got the same figure every time."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|実験|じっけん}を{f|十回|じゅっかい}{f|繰|く}り{f|返|かえ}しました。<b>その{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|毎回|まいかい}{f|同|おな}じ{f|数字|すうじ}が{f|出|で}ました。

A repeated experiment is a process, the register is that of a report, and the second
sentence states the finding - all three point to this connector. Note the counter
reading <code>{f|十回|じゅっかい}</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|何度|なんど}も{f|練習|れんしゅう}した。その{f|結果|けっか}で、{f|本番|ほんばん}では{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}いて{f|話|はな}せた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|何度|なんど}も{f|練習|れんしゅう}した。<b>その{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|本番|ほんばん}では{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}いて{f|話|はな}せた。

The `で` is the error. As a connector the phrase is followed directly by a comma;
<code>{f|結果|けっか}</code> only takes particles when it is being used as an ordinary
noun, as in <code>{f|検査|けんさ}の{f|結果|けっか}を{f|見|み}る</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|三回|さんかい}{f|面接|めんせつ}を{f|受|う}けた。<b>その{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|採用|さいよう}が{f|決|き}まった。
(b) {f|三回|さんかい}{f|面接|めんせつ}を{f|受|う}けた。<b>{f|結局|けっきょく}</b>{f|採用|さいよう}は{f|見送|みおく}られた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) presents the three interviews as a process and the offer as what came out of it.
The tone is factual and slightly formal, as in a written account.

(b) uses <code>{f|結局|けっきょく}</code> to close the story off with a shrug: after all
that, nothing came of it. <code>{f|結局|けっきょく}</code> is an adverb, sits inside its
clause without a comma, and characteristically marks an ending that fell short of
what the effort deserved. You could say
<code>その{f|結果|けっか}、{f|採用|さいよう}は{f|見送|みおく}られた</code> as well; it would
report the same fact with none of the disappointment.

</details>
