# だけど

**Meaning** · but; however - the casual sentence-opening "but"

`だけど` opens a **new sentence** that pushes back on the one just said. It is the
everyday spoken "however": friends use it constantly, and it is one of the few
words you should deliberately leave outside a job interview.

## Usage

`だけど` stands at the head of a sentence, with a comma after it in writing, and
the objection follows. It does not conjugate, take particles or attach to
anything - it is a fixed one-word opener.

- 「{f|明日|あした}、{f|海|うみ}に{f|行|い}こうよ。」「だけど、{f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|く}るらしいよ。」

Historically it is the copula `だ` plus the clause-final `けど` you met at N5, and
that history explains the one place learners reliably go wrong. After a **noun or
な-adjective** the same string turns up *inside* a sentence, because the `だ`
belongs to the first clause: {f|学生|がくせい}だけど、もう{f|働|はたら}いている.
After a **verb or い-adjective** there is no `だ` available, so it is plain `けど`:
{f|安|やす}いけど{f|使|つか}いにくい - never ❌{f|安|やす}いだけど.

Sentence-initially, then, `だけど` is always available. Mid-sentence it is only
available after a noun or な-adjective, and there it is really two words wearing
one coat.

One more very common shape: <code>～んだけど</code>, which prefaces a request or a piece
of news - ちょっと{f|聞|き}きたいことがあるんだけど… - where the trailing `だけど`
softens the approach rather than contradicting anything.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}は{f|寒|さむ}い。<b>だけど</b>、{f|雪|ゆき}は{f|降|ふ}らないと{f|思|おも}う。
It's cold today. But I don't think it'll snow.
</pre>

<pre>
「もう{f|一杯|いっぱい}どう?」「<b>だけど</b>、{f|明日|あした}{f|早|はや}いんだよね。」
"Another drink?" "But I've got an early start tomorrow."
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三年|さんねん}{f|習|なら}った。<b>だけど</b>、まだ{f|電話|でんわ}で{f|話|はな}すのはこわい。
I studied it for three years. But phone calls still terrify me.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}いし、{f|駅|えき}からも{f|遠|とお}い。<b>だけど</b>、この{f|家賃|やちん}なら{f|文句|もんく}は{f|言|い}えない。
The flat is cramped and far from the station. But at this rent I can't complain.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Casual. `だけど` belongs to conversation with friends and family, to messages, and
to relaxed writing such as blog posts. In a business email, an essay or anything
said to a customer, use `ですが` or `しかし` instead. Roughly, the ladder runs
`しかし` (formal, written) - `ですが` (polite) - `でも` (neutral casual) - `だけど`
(casual) - `けど` (very casual, often trailing off).

Against `でも`, `だけど` tends to be the firmer of the two. `でも` can register a
mild reservation; `だけど` picks up the previous statement and argues with it, so
opening several sentences in a row with it comes across as combative.

`だけれど` and `だけれども` are the unshortened forms: a shade more careful and
slightly bookish, and they lean older in speech. `だけどさ`, `だけどね` do the
opposite, softening the pushback with a final particle. None of these are marked
for gender.

Note also that `だけど` is not a subordinating conjunction. It cannot join two
clauses into one sentence the way `が` or `けど` do; it needs a full stop in front
of it, or at least the pause that a full stop would represent.

## Contrast

### だけど vs でも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same slot, same register, different force. `でも` raises a
qualification; `だけど` contradicts.

</div>

<pre>
「これ、おいしいね。」「<b>でも</b>、ちょっと{f|甘|あま}すぎない?」
"This is good." "Though - isn't it a bit too sweet?"
</pre>

<pre>
「これ、おいしいね。」「<b>だけど</b>、{f|値段|ねだん}を{f|見|み}た?」
"This is good." "But did you see the price?"
</pre>

Both are natural; the second sounds more like the start of an argument. When you
are not sure which you want, `でも` is the safer default.

### だけど vs けど

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だけど` begins a sentence; `けど` ends a clause. They are not
interchangeable positions.

</div>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}はある<b>けど</b>、お{f|金|かね}がない。
I have the time, but not the money.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}はある。<b>だけど</b>、お{f|金|かね}がない。
I have the time. But I don't have the money.
</pre>

The content is identical; the second version breaks it into two beats and lands
harder. Sentence-initial `けど` alone does exist in very casual speech - 
「けど、{f|無理|むり}でしょ」 - but `だけど` is the standard choice there.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|時間|じかん}がないだけど、{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ {f|時間|じかん}がない<b>けど</b>、{f|行|い}きます。
  A verb or い-adjective cannot be followed by `だ`. Only nouns and な-adjectives
  can carry the `だ` that makes `だけど`.

- ❌ {f|静|しず}かですだけど → ✅ {f|静|しず}かです<b>けど</b>
  `です` is already the copula; adding `だ` on top of it doubles up. In polite
  speech the clause-final connector is `ですけど` or `ですが`.

- ❌（{f|取引先|とりひきさき}へのメールで）だけど、{f|納期|のうき}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}になります。 → ✅ <b>しかし</b>、{f|納期|のうき}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}になります。
  `だけど` in business writing reads as though you forgot who you were talking to.
  `しかし` or `ですが` carries the same meaning at the right register.

## Practice

**1.** 「{f|明日|あした}のパーティー、{f|来|く}る?」「{f|行|い}きたい。____、バイトなんだ。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

「{f|行|い}きたい。<b>だけど</b>、バイトなんだ。」

The speaker has finished one sentence and starts another that contradicts it, so
the connector has to be the sentence-initial kind. `でも` would also fit here and
would sound a little softer.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: {f|高|たか}いだけど、けっきょく{f|買|か}うことにした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|高|たか}い<b>けど</b>、けっきょく{f|買|か}うことにした。

<code>{f|高|たか}い</code> is an い-adjective, and い-adjectives are never followed by `だ`.
Splitting it into two sentences - {f|高|たか}い。だけど、{f|買|か}うことにした。 - 
works too.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) <b>だけど</b>、{f|私|わたし}はそうは{f|思|おも}わない。
(b) <b>ですが</b>、{f|私|わたし}はそうは{f|思|おも}いません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The grammar is the same and the meaning is the same: both reject what was just
said. The difference is entirely register. Sentence (a) is what you say to a
friend over coffee, while (b) is what you say in a meeting or write in an essay.
Using (a) in a formal discussion would sound abrupt and a little rude, and using
(b) with a close friend would sound stiff and distant.

</details>
