# もしかしたら

**Meaning** · maybe; there's a chance that - raising a possibility the speaker has
just thought of

`もしかしたら` opens the door to something that has not been considered yet, often
something slightly unexpected or unwelcome. It is a low-confidence word: the
speaker is not predicting, only pointing at a possibility.

## Usage

`もしかしたら` sits at the head of the sentence. Like every adverb of conjecture,
its real lesson is the **partner at the other end of the sentence**:

- **もしかしたら～かもしれない／かも** - the default pairing, by a wide margin.
  もしかしたら{f|明日|あした}は{f|休|やす}みかもしれない。
- **もしかしたら～のではないか** - the same idea in careful or written style.
  もしかしたら{f|何|なに}か{f|誤解|ごかい}があるのではないでしょうか。
- **もしかしたら～かな／かしら** - thinking aloud.
  もしかしたら{f|道|みち}に{f|迷|まよ}ったのかな。

Leaving the sentence to end flatly - ❌もしかしたら{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ります - is
the single most common error. The adverb only sets up the possibility; something at
the end has to keep it open.

Three variants mean essentially the same thing and are interchangeable in most
sentences: もしかすると (a shade more formal), ひょっとしたら and ひょっとすると
(more colloquial, slightly more surprised). `もしかして` looks like a fourth but
behaves differently - see the contrast below.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>もしかしたら</b>{f|明日|あした}は{f|雪|ゆき}かもしれない。
There's a chance it'll snow tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>もしかしたら</b>、{f|鍵|かぎ}を{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|忘|わす}れてきたかも。
I might have left my keys at the office.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|何|なん}の{f|連絡|れんらく}もないので、<b>もしかしたら</b>{f|日|ひ}にちを{f|間違|まちが}えているのではないでしょうか。
There's been no word at all, so I wonder if she hasn't got the date wrong.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>もしかしたら</b>{f|来年|らいねん}、{f|大阪|おおさか}に{f|転勤|てんきん}になるかもしれないと{f|上司|じょうし}に{f|言|い}われた。
My boss told me there's a chance I'll be transferred to Osaka next year.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and usable in ます-form or plain form without changing shape. In
careful business speech it usually comes with the fuller ending
のではないでしょうか rather than `かも`, which is where the register is actually
carried.

The word has a distinctly tentative colour. Because it presents an idea as one that
has only just occurred to the speaker, it is a soft way to raise something
uncomfortable: もしかしたら{f|私|わたし}の{f|勘違|かんちが}いかもしれませんが…
lets you question someone's claim while taking the blame for the doubt yourself.
That politeness trick is worth stealing.

Contracted `もしかしたら～かも` is very common in speech and messages, and the whole
sentence is often left trailing: 「もしかしたら{f|無理|むり}かも…」 The trailing-off
is part of the effect - you have floated the bad news without stating it.

On the confidence ladder, `もしかしたら` sits at the bottom: below たぶん and well
below おそらく. If you are more than about half sure, it undersells you.

## Contrast

### もしかしたら vs もしかして

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `もしかしたら` floats a possibility to the world and ends in
`かもしれない`. `もしかして` aims a guess at the listener and ends in a question.

</div>

<pre>
<b>もしかして</b>、{f|田中|たなか}さんですか。
Excuse me - are you Tanaka, by any chance?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>もしかしたら</b>{f|田中|たなか}さんも{f|来|く}るかもしれません。
There's a chance Tanaka will come too.
</pre>

`もしかして` is what you say when you suspect something about the person in front
of you - that they are the one you were told to meet, that they are angry, that
they have not eaten. It expects an answer. Using `もしかしたら` there
（❌もしかしたら{f|田中|たなか}さんですか）sounds like you are guessing at a stranger
rather than asking one.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ もしかしたら{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|来|き}ます。 → ✅ もしかしたら{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|来|こ}ない<b>かもしれません</b>。
  A bare ます-ending asserts, which cancels the doubt the adverb just raised. The
  possibility has to stay open at the end of the sentence.

- ❌ もしかしたら{f|明日|あした}{f|会議|かいぎ}がありますか。 → ✅ <b>もしかして</b>{f|明日|あした}{f|会議|かいぎ}がありますか。
  A direct question to the listener takes `もしかして`. `もしかしたら` is for
  possibilities you are laying out, not for questions you are putting.

- ❌ もしかしたら{f|絶対|ぜったい}{f|遅|おく}れる。 → ✅ <b>きっと</b>{f|遅|おく}れる。
  You cannot hedge and insist in the same breath. If you are sure, drop the adverb
  of possibility entirely.

## Practice

**1.** {f|電気|でんき}がついているから、____{f|誰|だれ}か{f|残|のこ}っている____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|電気|でんき}がついているから、<b>もしかしたら</b>{f|誰|だれ}か{f|残|のこ}っている<b>かもしれない</b>。

Both blanks belong to one frame. The adverb opens the possibility and
<code>かもしれない</code> keeps it open; filling only one of them leaves the sentence
lopsided.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: （もしかして・もしかしたら）{f|今|いま}、お{f|時間|じかん}ありませんか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>もしかして</b>{f|今|いま}、お{f|時間|じかん}ありませんか。

The speaker is putting a guess directly to the person in front of them and wants an
answer, which is the question-shaped use. `もしかしたら` would be floating an idea
into the air with nobody to answer it.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) もしかしたら{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わないかもしれません。
(b) たぶん{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わないと{f|思|おも}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (a) raises a risk: the speaker still expects to make it, but wants the
listener warned in case. Sentence (b) is an actual expectation - the speaker
believes they will miss it. If you say (a) and then arrive late, nobody is
surprised; if you say (b), the listener will already have made other plans.

</details>
