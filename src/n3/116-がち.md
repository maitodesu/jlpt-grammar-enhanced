# ～がち

**Meaning** · has a habit of ～ing; keeps ending up that way

`～がち` says that something happens **often, and shouldn't**. It is not a
measurement of degree and not a statement about one occasion - it is a complaint
about a pattern, whether the pattern belongs to a person, a machine or the weather.

## Formation

The result is a **な-adjective**: `がちだ` as a predicate, `がちな` before a noun,
`がちに` before a verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + がち | {f|忘|わす}れます → {f|忘|わす}れがち |
| Verb (ます-stem of a する-verb) | Noun + し + がち | {f|無理|むり}します → {f|無理|むり}しがち |
| Noun (short fixed list) | Noun + がち | {f|病気|びょうき} → {f|病気|びょうき}がち |
| Predicate | ～がち + だ / です | {f|遅|おく}れがちです |
| Before a noun | ～がち + な + Noun | {f|見落|みお}としがちな{f|点|てん} |
| Before a verb | ～がち + に + Verb | {f|遠慮|えんりょ}がちに{f|話|はな}す |
| Common set phrase | ～がち + になる | {f|休|やす}みがちになる |

<div class="note">

**Note** - with verbs, `がち` is broadly productive. With **nouns** it is not: the
usable list is essentially {f|病気|びょうき}がち, {f|曇|くも}りがち,
{f|留守|るす}がち, {f|遠慮|えんりょ}がち, {f|伏|ふ}し{f|目|め}がち, and the
lexicalised ありがち ("all too common"). Inventing new noun combinations - ❌
<code>{f|元気|げんき}がち</code>, ❌<code>{f|雨|あめ}がち</code> - does not work.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|冬|ふゆ}は{f|運動不足|うんどうぶそく}に<b>なりがち</b>だ。
In winter you end up short of exercise.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|小|ちい}さいころ<b>{f|病気|びょうき}がち</b>でした。
My little brother was ill a lot when he was small.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}は<b>{f|曇|くも}りがち</b>で、{f|洗|あら}ったシーツがなかなか{f|乾|かわ}かない。
It's cloudy on and off today, and the sheets I washed just won't dry.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|忙|いそが}しいと、{f|朝|あさ}ごはんを<b>{f|抜|ぬ}きがち</b>になる。
When you're busy you get into the habit of skipping breakfast.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|遠慮|えんりょ}がちに</b>{f|手|て}を{f|挙|あ}げて、{f|小|ちい}さな{f|声|こえ}で{f|質問|しつもん}した。
He raised his hand hesitantly and asked his question in a small voice.
</pre>

<pre>
メールの{f|返事|へんじ}を{f|後回|あとまわ}しにすると、そのまま<b>{f|忘|わす}れがち</b>だ。
If you put off replying to an email, you tend to forget about it altogether.
</pre>

<pre>

The small print in the manual is the sort of thing people miss, so read it aloud.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}{f|暮|ぐ}らしを{f|始|はじ}めてから、{f|野菜|やさい}が{f|足|た}りなく<b>なりがち</b>で、{f|自分|じぶん}でも{f|反省|はんせい}している。
Since I started living alone I keep ending up short on vegetables, and I'm not proud of it.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The judgement built into `がち` is the thing to remember. Every ordinary use points
at something the speaker would rather did not keep happening: illness, absence,
oversight, delay, cloud. Turn it on a good outcome and the sentence collapses - 
❌<code>{f|成功|せいこう}しがち</code>, ❌<code>{f|元気|げんき}になりがち</code> - not
because the grammar breaks but because the word carries a frown that has nowhere to
land.

The frown can be aimed at yourself, and often is: {f|私|わたし}も
{f|無理|むり}しがちなので{f|気|き}をつけます is a standard, slightly rueful thing to
say. It can also be aimed at people in general, which is where the pattern earns its
keep in writing - ～と{f|考|かんが}えがちだが, "we tend to think that, but", is a
staple opening move in essays and newspaper columns.

Register is neutral. `がち` appears in conversation, in business email and in print
without shifting tone. Two combinations have drifted into fixed vocabulary and no
longer feel like grammar at all: {f|遠慮|えんりょ}がち ("diffident, hanging back")
and ありがち ("the sort of thing that happens all the time" - usually said of a
predictable plot or a familiar mistake).

Finally, `がち` needs **repetition**. It cannot describe one event. Reporting a
single late train as {f|遅|おく}れがちだった is wrong in the same way that saying
"the train tends to be late" about one specific morning is wrong in English.

## Contrast

### ～がち vs ～やすい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `やすい` is about **how easily** something can happen, and can
be good or bad. `がち` is about **how often** it does happen, and is always bad.

</div>

N4 taught `やすい` as "easy to do". Its second sense, "liable to", is what overlaps
here - and the two still split cleanly:

<pre>
この{f|皿|さら}は{f|薄|うす}いので<b>{f|割|わ}れやすい</b>。
These plates are thin, so they break easily.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}にまかせると、{f|皿|さら}を<b>{f|割|わ}りがち</b>だ。
If I leave it to my son, he's forever breaking plates.
</pre>

The first is a property of the plates that would hold even if nobody ever broke one.
The second counts actual breakages and blames somebody for them. This is also why
`やすい` is happy in praise - {f|使|つか}いやすい, {f|分|わ}かりやすい - and `がち`
never is.

They also build different words: `やすい` makes an い-adjective
（{f|割|わ}れやすかった, {f|割|わ}れやすくない）, `がち` a な-adjective
（{f|割|わ}りがちだった, {f|割|わ}りがちな{f|人|ひと}）.

### ～がち vs ～ぎみ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `がち` counts occasions; `ぎみ` measures amount. If you could
answer with "three times a week", it is `がち`. If you could answer with "just a
little", it is `ぎみ`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|去年|きょねん}から{f|会社|かいしゃ}を<b>{f|休|やす}みがち</b>だ。
He's been off work a lot since last year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}は{f|少|すこ}し<b>{f|疲|つか}れぎみ</b>なので、{f|残業|ざんぎょう}はやめておく。
I'm a bit worn out today, so I'll skip the overtime.
</pre>

Some verbs take both, and then the difference is unmistakable.
{f|太|ふと}りぎみ means I am currently a couple of kilos heavier than I should be;
{f|太|ふと}りがち means I am the kind of person who puts weight on again and again.
Only one of those is a comment about a body shape, and only one is a comment about a
life.

### ～がち vs ことが{f|多|おお}い

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ことが{f|多|おお}い</code> reports frequency as a neutral
fact. `がち` reports the same frequency and disapproves of it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|冬|ふゆ}は{f|山|やま}に{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>ことが{f|多|おお}い</b>。
It often snows in the mountains in winter.
</pre>

That sentence is a piece of information. Nothing is wrong with the snow. Swap in
`がち` and you have implied that the snow is a nuisance - fine if you are a driver,
odd in a geography textbook.

The neutral phrase is therefore the safe default for objective writing, and it takes
a whole clause rather than a stem, so it works with verbs `がち` cannot reach:
<code>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ることが{f|多|おお}い</code> is fine, while
❌<code>{f|雨|あめ}がちだ</code> is not Japanese.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|遅|おく}れる<b>がち</b> → ✅ {f|遅|おく}れ<b>がち</b>
  `がち` takes the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|病気|びょうき}がち<b>{f|子|こ}ども</b> → ✅ {f|病気|びょうき}がち<b>な</b>{f|子|こ}ども
  The result is a な-adjective, so it needs `な` before a noun.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}の{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|遅|おく}れ<b>がち</b>だった。（about one morning） → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}の{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|遅|おく}れていた。
  `がち` requires a repeated pattern. One late train is just a late train.

- ❌ {f|最近|さいきん}よく{f|眠|ねむ}れて、{f|元気|げんき}になり<b>がち</b>です。 → ✅ {f|最近|さいきん}よく{f|眠|ねむ}れて、{f|元気|げんき}になってきました。
  `がち` only fits tendencies the speaker regrets. Nobody regrets getting healthier.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}<b>がち</b>な{f|天気|てんき} → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}り<b>がち</b>な{f|天気|てんき}
  Only a handful of nouns take `がち` directly. {f|曇|くも}り is on the list;
  {f|雨|あめ} is not, so go through the verb.

## Practice

**1.** {f|夜遅|よるおそ}くまでスマホを{f|見|み}ていると、{f|朝|あさ}{f|寝坊|ねぼう}____になる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|寝坊|ねぼう}し<b>がち</b>になる。

<code>{f|寝坊|ねぼう}する</code> is a する-verb, so the stem is
<code>{f|寝坊|ねぼう}し</code>. The sentence describes a habit that forms and that
the speaker sees as a problem - exactly what `がち` is for.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|祖母|そぼ}は{f|去年|きょねん}から（{f|病気|びょうき}がち・{f|病気|びょうき}ぎみ）で、{f|入院|にゅういん}も{f|二回|にかい}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|祖母|そぼ}は{f|去年|きょねん}から<b>{f|病気|びょうき}がち</b>で…

<code>{f|入院|にゅういん}も{f|二回|にかい}</code> tells you this is about repeated
episodes over a year, which is frequency - `がち`. `ぎみ` would claim she is
*slightly* ill at this moment, which does not fit a sentence counting hospital
stays. <code>{f|病気|びょうき}ぎみ</code> is in any case not a natural combination.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Beginners tend to focus only on grammar and forget about pronunciation."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|初心者|しょしんしゃ}は{f|文法|ぶんぽう}ばかりに{f|気|き}を{f|取|と}られて、{f|発音|はつおん}を<b>{f|忘|わす}れがち</b>だ。

A statement about what learners repeatedly do wrong is the classic `がち` sentence,
and <code>{f|忘|わす}れがち</code> is one of its commonest partners. Note that this
generalising use - "people tend to…" - is very common in written Japanese.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}は{f|遠慮|えんりょ}がちで{f|意見|いけん}を{f|言|い}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|遠慮|えんりょ}がち<b>に</b>{f|意見|いけん}を{f|言|い}った。

`がち` is a な-adjective, and a な-adjective modifying a **verb** takes `に`, not
`で`. <code>{f|遠慮|えんりょ}がちで</code> would be the て-form used to join clauses,
which leaves <code>{f|意見|いけん}を{f|言|い}った</code> dangling.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|父|ちち}は{f|夜|よる}{f|遅|おそ}く<b>{f|帰|かえ}りがち</b>だ。
(b) {f|父|ちち}は{f|夜|よる}{f|遅|おそ}く{f|帰|かえ}ることが{f|多|おお}い。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) counts the late nights and colours them: the speaker is not happy about it, and
you can hear a family that would like him home earlier. `がち` almost always leaves
that trace of complaint.

(b) reports the same frequency flatly, as a fact about his schedule. It is what you
would write on a form or say to a stranger who asked what hours he works - no
attitude attached, and no invitation to sympathise.

</details>
