# と共に

**Meaning** · together with; at the same moment as; as one thing goes, so goes the other

Read <code>と{f|共|とも}に</code>, and just as often written in kana as とともに. It
starts as a formal replacement for <code>と{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</code>, but it stretches much
further than that: it can join two events at a point in time, track two things
that move in step, and pin two properties onto one subject at once.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (person, group) - accompaniment | Noun + と{f|共|とも}に | {f|家族|かぞく} → {f|家族|かぞく}と{f|共|とも}に{f|暮|く}らす |
| Noun (event, point in time) - simultaneity | Noun + と{f|共|とも}に | {f|卒業|そつぎょう} → {f|卒業|そつぎょう}と{f|共|とも}に{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}る |
| Noun (something that rises or falls) - in step | Noun + と{f|共|とも}に | {f|年齢|ねんれい} → {f|年齢|ねんれい}と{f|共|とも}に{f|増|ふ}える |
| Verb (dictionary form) | Verb + と{f|共|とも}に | {f|進|すす}む → {f|研究|けんきゅう}が{f|進|すす}むと{f|共|とも}に |
| Noun / な-adj + である - "and also" | N + である + と{f|共|とも}に | {f|医者|いしゃ} → {f|医者|いしゃ}であると{f|共|とも}に{f|作家|さっか}でもある |

<div class="note">

**Note** - there is no い-adjective row, because い-adjectives do not attach here.
For "busy and also…" use <code>{f|忙|いそが}しいうえに</code> or simply the て-form,
<code>{f|忙|いそが}しくて</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|家族|かぞく}と<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>{f|新|あたら}しい{f|町|まち}へ{f|移|うつ}りました。
I moved to a new town together with my family.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|年|とし}をとると<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>、{f|朝|あさ}{f|起|お}きるのが{f|早|はや}くなった。
As I've got older, I've started waking up earlier.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|卒業|そつぎょう}と<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>、{f|三年間|さんねんかん}{f|住|す}んだ{f|寮|りょう}を{f|出|で}ることになっている。
When I graduate I'm due to leave the dormitory I've lived in for three years.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|気温|きおん}が{f|上|あ}がると<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>、アイスの{f|売|う}り{f|上|あ}げも{f|伸|の}びた。
As the temperature rose, ice-cream sales climbed with it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|祖父|そふ}は{f|農家|のうか}であると<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>、{f|村|むら}の{f|小|ちい}さな{f|学校|がっこう}の{f|先生|せんせい}でもあった。
My grandfather was a farmer and at the same time a teacher at the village's little school.
</pre>

<pre>
スマホが{f|広|ひろ}まると<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>、{f|紙|かみ}の{f|地図|ちず}を{f|持|も}ち{f|歩|ある}く{f|人|ひと}はほとんどいなくなった。
As smartphones spread, hardly anyone carries a paper map any more.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本日|ほんじつ}は{f|皆様|みなさま}と<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>この{f|日|ひ}を{f|祝|いわ}えることを、たいへんうれしく{f|思|おも}います。
I am delighted to be able to mark this day together with all of you.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工場|こうじょう}が{f|郊外|こうがい}へ{f|移|うつ}ると<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>、{f|駅前|えきまえ}の{f|商店街|しょうてんがい}も{f|少|すこ}しずつ{f|静|しず}かになっていった。
As the factory moved out to the suburbs, the shopping street by the station gradually grew quieter too.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>と{f|共|とも}に</code> belongs to **written and formal spoken** Japanese: essays,
reports, news copy, and speeches. In everyday conversation the accompaniment
sense sounds comically grand - 「{f|友|とも}だちとともにラーメンを{f|食|た}べた」 is the sort
of thing said as a joke. Use <code>と{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</code> there.

The word <code>{f|共|とも}に</code> also lives on its own as an adverb meaning "together",
and it turns up in set encouragements: 「{f|共|とも}に{f|頑張|がんば}りましょう」,
「{f|共|とも}に{f|歩|あゆ}む」. This is speech-making register, warm and slightly
old-fashioned, and it is one of the few places the pattern feels emotional rather
than neutral.

The <code>であると{f|共|とも}に</code> use - "A and also B" - is firmly essay language. It
sets two roles or two qualities side by side and is usually followed by `～でもある` or `～でもあった`. In speech you would say 「{f|医者|いしゃ}でもあり、{f|作家|さっか}でもある」 instead.

One point of care with the "in step" sense: both halves have to be things that
can **move**. Growing older, prices rising, technology advancing, a season
turning - these work. A fixed fact does not: 「{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}いとともに」 is not
Japanese.

## Contrast

### と{f|共|とも}に vs と{f|一緒|いっしょ}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for plain accompaniment they mean the same thing;
<code>と{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</code> is spoken and neutral, <code>と{f|共|とも}に</code> is written and
ceremonious. Only <code>と{f|共|とも}に</code> can do simultaneity and change-in-step.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昼休|ひるやす}みに{f|同僚|どうりょう}と<b>{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</b>{f|弁当|べんとう}を{f|食|た}べた。
I ate my packed lunch with a colleague at lunchtime.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|成長|せいちょう}と<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>、{f|社員|しゃいん}の{f|数|かず}も{f|三倍|さんばい}になった。
As the company grew, the number of employees tripled along with it.
</pre>

Substituting <code>と{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</code> into the second sentence fails: growth is not
a companion you do things alongside.

### と{f|共|とも}に vs につれて・にしたがって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `につれて` needs **gradual, continuous change on both sides**.
<code>と{f|共|とも}に</code> also accepts a single moment or a one-off event.
`にしたがって` overlaps heavily with `につれて`, but it has a second, unrelated
everyday sense: "in accordance with (instructions)".

</div>

Gradual on both sides - all three are possible, and `につれて` is the most
idiomatic:

<pre>
{f|試合|しあい}が{f|進|すす}む<b>につれて</b>、{f|応援|おうえん}の{f|声|こえ}が{f|大|おお}きくなっていった。
As the match went on, the cheering grew louder and louder.
</pre>

A single moment - only <code>と{f|共|とも}に</code> works:

<pre>
{f|開店|かいてん}と<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>、{f|客|きゃく}が{f|次々|つぎつぎ}と{f|店|みせ}に{f|入|はい}ってきた。
The moment the shop opened, customers began pouring in.
</pre>

And the other `にしたがって`, which has nothing to do with gradual change at all:

<pre>
{f|係員|かかりいん}の{f|指示|しじ}<b>にしたがって</b>、{f|外|そと}へ{f|出|で}てください。
Please go outside in accordance with the staff's instructions.
</pre>

Be honest with yourself about the first group: in the gradual-change sense,
`につれて` and `にしたがって` are very close, and most sentences accept either.
`にしたがって` is a shade more formal and is the one you meet in written reports.

### と{f|共|とも}に vs と{f|同時|どうじ}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> pins two things to the **same instant**.
<code>と{f|共|とも}に</code> can do that too, but it also stretches over a period, which
<code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> does not.

</div>

<pre>
ベルが{f|鳴|な}る<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>、{f|生徒|せいと}たちが{f|廊下|ろうか}へ{f|飛|と}び{f|出|だ}した。
The instant the bell rang, the pupils shot out into the corridor.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|時|とき}が{f|経|た}つ<b>と{f|共|とも}に</b>、あのときの{f|悔|くや}しさもうすれてきた。
As time has passed, the bitterness of that day has faded.
</pre>

Put <code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> in the second sentence and it claims that time passing
and the feeling fading happened at one identifiable moment, which is not what
anyone means.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|友|とも}だちと<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>カラオケに{f|行|い}った。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|友|とも}だちと<b>{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</b>カラオケに{f|行|い}った。
  Not ungrammatical, but the register is three sizes too big for a night out.

- ❌ この{f|店|みせ}は{f|安|やす}いと<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>、{f|品物|しなもの}もいい。 → ✅ この{f|店|みせ}は{f|安|やす}い<b>うえに</b>、{f|品物|しなもの}もいい。
  い-adjectives do not attach to <code>と{f|共|とも}に</code>. For "and on top of that",
  use `うえに` or `し`.

- ❌ {f|父|ちち}は{f|教師|きょうし}と<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>{f|画家|がか}でもあった。 → ✅ {f|父|ちち}は{f|教師|きょうし}<b>であると{f|共|とも}に</b>{f|画家|がか}でもあった。
  In the "A and also B" use, a noun needs `である` in front. Without it the
  sentence reads as "together with a teacher".

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ると<b>{f|共|とも}に</b>、{f|試合|しあい}をやめた。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ってきた<b>ので</b>、{f|試合|しあい}をやめた。
  <code>と{f|共|とも}に</code> reports that two things move together; it is not a way of
  saying "because".

## Practice

**1.** {f|技術|ぎじゅつ}の{f|進歩|しんぽ}____、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|問題|もんだい}も{f|生|う}まれてきた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}の{f|進歩|しんぽ}<b>と{f|共|とも}に</b>、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|問題|もんだい}も{f|生|う}まれてきた。

Both sides are things that develop over time, and the register - an abstract
noun plus <code>{f|生|う}まれてきた</code> - is written Japanese. <code>につれて</code> would also
fit here; <code>と{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</code> would not.

</details>

**2.** Choose: ドアが{f|開|ひら}く（とともに・につれて）、{f|冷|つめ}たい{f|風|かぜ}が{f|入|はい}ってきた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ドアが{f|開|ひら}く<b>とともに</b>、{f|冷|つめ}たい{f|風|かぜ}が{f|入|はい}ってきた。

Opening a door is a single event, not a gradual process, and `につれて` requires
change on both sides that unfolds by degrees. Where you can substitute "the
moment that", <code>とともに</code> is the pattern.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "As the population fell, the number of shops in the town fell as well."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|人口|じんこう}が{f|減|へ}る<b>と{f|共|とも}に</b>、{f|町|まち}の{f|店|みせ}の{f|数|かず}も{f|減|へ}っていった。

Two quantities moving in the same direction over time - the core "in step" use.
The `も` in the second half is doing useful work: it marks the shops as
following the population rather than falling independently.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|研究者|けんきゅうしゃ}とともに、{f|優|すぐ}れた{f|教師|きょうし}でもある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|研究者|けんきゅうしゃ}<b>であるとともに</b>、{f|優|すぐ}れた{f|教師|きょうし}でもある。

As written, the sentence says she is an excellent teacher *alongside a
researcher* - some other person. To stack two roles on one subject, the noun
takes `である` before the pattern, and the second half keeps `でもある`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|春|はる}が{f|来|く}る<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>、{f|庭|にわ}の{f|花|はな}が{f|咲|さ}き{f|始|はじ}めた。
(b) {f|春|はる}が{f|来|く}る<b>と{f|共|とも}に</b>、{f|庭|にわ}の{f|花|はな}が{f|咲|さ}き{f|始|はじ}めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) fixes a moment: the arrival of spring and the first flowers are presented as
coinciding, almost as if one triggered the other on a given day. It has a
slightly dramatic, "no sooner than" feel.

(b) is looser and warmer. Spring and the flowers are moving along together over
a stretch of weeks, with no claim about a single instant. For a seasonal
description this is the more natural of the two.

</details>
