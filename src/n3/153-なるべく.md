# なるべく

**Meaning** · as far as possible; if you can - a preference stated without making
it an order

`なるべく` softens whatever follows it into an aim rather than a requirement. Put
it in front of a request and the request becomes a wish; put it in front of your
own plan and the plan becomes an intention you will try to keep.

## Usage

`なるべく` is an adverb. It goes immediately in front of the part it qualifies - 
usually a verb, often an adverb of degree such as {f|早|はや}く or {f|多|おお}く - 
and it does not change form.

Three places it lives:

- **Requests.** なるべく{f|早|はや}めにお{f|返事|へんじ}をください。 The deadline is
  still a deadline, but you have signalled that you understand life happens.
- **Negative requests.** なるべく{f|音|おと}を{f|立|た}てないでください。 Very common
  on notices, where an outright prohibition would be too strong.
- **Resolutions and habits.** なるべく{f|歩|ある}くようにしています。 Here it pairs
  naturally with <code>ようにする</code>, which is itself a "make an effort to" pattern.

`なるべく` belongs with things that are still open - requests, intentions, advice.
It sits badly on a finished fact: ❌なるべく{f|早|はや}く{f|来|き}ました reports
what you did, and there is nothing left to try for. Use できるだけ{f|急|いそ}いで
{f|来|き}ました, or simply {f|急|いそ}いで{f|来|き}ました.

In kanji it is {f|成|な}るべく, but the kana spelling is overwhelmingly the norm and
the kanji looks archaic.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>なるべく</b>{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}ります。
I'll be home as early as I can.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|夜|よる}は<b>なるべく</b>コーヒーを{f|飲|の}まないようにしている。
I try not to drink coffee at night if I can help it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}が{f|狭|せま}いので、<b>なるべく</b>{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|少|すく}なめにお{f|願|ねが}いします。
The venue is small, so please keep your luggage to a minimum if you can.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}は{f|急|いそ}ぎませんが、<b>なるべく</b>{f|今週中|こんしゅうちゅう}にいただけると{f|助|たす}かります。
There's no rush on the reply, but it would help if I could have it this week if at all possible.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`なるべく` is neutral to slightly informal, and works fine inside ます-form speech - 
なるべく{f|早|はや}くお{f|送|おく}りします is perfectly good business Japanese. It is
common in notices and instructions, where its softness is the whole point.

The softness cuts both ways. Attach `なるべく` to something that genuinely must
happen and you have accidentally made it optional: a hard deadline announced as
なるべく{f|明日|あした}までに will be read as "tomorrow would be nice". When the
requirement is firm, drop the adverb, or use {f|必|かなら}ず.

Received rather than given, `なるべく` is a small courtesy: the speaker is telling
you they know what they are asking may be inconvenient. Answering
「はい、なるべく{f|早|はや}く」 in return is a natural way to accept without
promising a time.

## Contrast

### なるべく vs できるだけ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same request, measured differently. `なるべく` asks for
willingness; できるだけ asks for the maximum that is actually possible.

</div>

<pre>
<b>なるべく</b>{f|静|しず}かにしてください。
Please keep it down if you can.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>できるだけ</b>{f|静|しず}かにしてください。
Please be as quiet as you possibly can.
</pre>

Both are polite and both are common; できるだけ is the firmer of the two because it
names the limit of your ability and asks you to go to it. It is also the one that
suits objective, written contexts - できるだけ{f|正確|せいかく}に{f|記入|きにゅう}して
ください on a form. Stronger still, and distinctly formal, is
できる{f|限|かぎ}り. Where a difference is felt, the ordering runs
`なるべく` - できるだけ - できる{f|限|かぎ}り, but in most everyday sentences the
first two are interchangeable.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|九時|くじ}に{f|来|き}てください。なるべく{f|遅|おく}れないでください。 → ✅ …<b>{f|絶対|ぜったい}に</b>{f|遅|おく}れないでください。
  If lateness is genuinely not acceptable, `なるべく` gives permission you did not
  mean to give. Save it for things you would forgive.

- ❌ なるべく{f|昨日|きのう}{f|片付|かたづ}けました。 → ✅ <b>できるだけ</b>{f|片付|かたづ}けました。
  A completed action leaves nothing to aim at. `なるべく` needs a request, a plan
  or a habit - something still in front of you.

- ❌ なるべくの{f|早|はや}い{f|時間|じかん}にお{f|願|ねが}いします。 → ✅ <b>なるべく</b>{f|早|はや}い{f|時間|じかん}にお{f|願|ねが}いします。
  `なるべく` is an adverb and takes no `の`. It attaches straight to what it
  modifies.

## Practice

**1.** {f|席|せき}が{f|少|すく}ないので、____{f|早|はや}めにお{f|越|こ}しください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>なるべく</b>{f|早|はや}めにお{f|越|こ}しください。

The request is real but the host is not in a position to insist, so the softening
adverb fits exactly. できるだけ would also work and would push a little harder.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: {f|昨日|きのう}はなるべく{f|急|いそ}いで{f|作|つく}りました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は<b>できるだけ</b>{f|急|いそ}いで{f|作|つく}りました。

The action is finished, so there is no remaining effort for `なるべく` to point at.
できるだけ works because it describes the extent of the effort that was actually
made.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) なるべく{f|明日|あした}までに{f|出|だ}してください。
(b) {f|必|かなら}ず{f|明日|あした}までに{f|出|だ}してください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (a) states a preferred deadline and quietly accepts that it might slip;
the listener will not feel they have broken a promise if they submit on the day
after. Sentence (b) is an instruction with no give in it. Choosing between them is
really a choice about what you will do if the deadline is missed.

</details>
