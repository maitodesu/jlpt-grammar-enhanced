# 敬語

**Meaning** · the honorific system - three separate mechanisms for showing where
you stand in relation to the person you are talking to and the person you are
talking about

{f|敬語|けいご} is not one politeness dial. It is three tools with different jobs:
{f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご} raises the other person, {f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご}
lowers you, and {f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご} keeps the sentence itself polite. You
choose the first two by asking *whose action it is*; the third you simply keep on.

## Formation

The individual frames belong to their own pages; the table below is the map. Read
"Attaches to" as "the form the frame is built on".

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb - {f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご} | お + ます-stem + になる | {f|使|つか}う → お{f|使|つか}いになる |
| Verb - {f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご}, light | passive form ～れる／られる | {f|帰|かえ}る → {f|帰|かえ}られる |
| Noun + する - {f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご} | ご／お + noun + なさる・になる | {f|出席|しゅっせき}する → ご{f|出席|しゅっせき}なさる |
| Verb - {f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご} | お + ます-stem + する／いたす | {f|届|とど}ける → お{f|届|とど}けいたします |
| Noun + する - {f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご} | ご／お + noun + する／いたす | {f|説明|せつめい}する → ご{f|説明|せつめい}いたします |
| Verb - {f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご} | ます／です | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}きます |
| Noun - {f|美化|びか}{f|語|ご} | お／ご + noun | {f|茶|ちゃ} → お{f|茶|ちゃ} |
| Common verbs | a separate word replaces the verb | {f|見|み}る → ご{f|覧|らん}になる／{f|拝見|はいけん}する |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ask one question: **whose action is it?** Their action takes
{f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご}. Your action takes {f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご}. Both
sit on top of {f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご}, which never goes away in a polite
conversation.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the line between "us" and "them" moves. Inside your company your
manager is above you and takes {f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご}. On the phone to a
client, your manager is on your side of the line and takes
{f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご}:
{f|田中|たなか}はただ{f|今|いま}{f|外出|がいしゅつ}しております. This is the single
biggest structural difference from European politeness systems, and the mistake
that most marks out a beginner.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}はもう<b>お{f|帰|かえ}りになりました</b>か。
Has the teacher already gone home?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}まで<b>ご{f|案内|あんない}します</b>。
I'll show you to the reception desk.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|資料|しりょう}は<b>{f|拝見|はいけん}しました</b>。ありがとうございます。
I've read the documents. Thank you.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}の{f|三時|さんじ}に、そちらへ<b>{f|伺|うかが}います</b>。
I'll call on you at three tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございません、{f|田中|たなか}はただ{f|今|いま}{f|席|せき}を<b>{f|外|はず}しております</b>。
I'm sorry - Tanaka is away from his desk at the moment.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|社長|しゃちょう}が<b>おっしゃった</b>{f|方針|ほうしん}に{f|沿|そ}って{f|進|すす}めております。
We are proceeding along the lines the president set out.
</pre>

<pre>

I'm sorry to trouble you when you're busy, but might I have a moment of your time?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

{f|敬語|けいご} is not simply "very polite Japanese". It is a way of drawing
relationships, and the relationships it draws are situational rather than fixed:
seniority at work, customer and shop, host and guest, first meeting versus tenth.
The same two people may use it in the morning meeting and drop it entirely at
dinner.

Two practical points. First, **volume matters less than direction**. A sentence
built with the wrong branch - honorific verbs for your own actions - is far more
jarring than a plain ます sentence would have been. When you are unsure, staying in
{f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご} and speaking clearly is safe; guessing at
{f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご} and pointing it at yourself is not.

Second, **more is not better**. Stacking two honorific mechanisms on one verb
produces {f|二重|にじゅう}{f|敬語|けいご} - おっしゃられる, お{f|読|よ}みになられる
 - which is widely regarded as incorrect, though you will certainly hear it. So-called
manual keigo (よろしかったでしょうか, ～のほう) has the same over-polished quality
and is criticised for the same reason.

Regionally and generationally there is variation: Kansai speech uses ～はる as a
light honorific, and older speakers tend to use fuller forms than younger ones.
Written business Japanese is the most conservative register of all, which is why
email templates are worth collecting.

## Contrast

### {f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご} vs {f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the branch is chosen by the **actor**, not by who you are
speaking to. Their action, {f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご}; your action,
{f|謙譲|けんじょう}{f|語|ご}.

</div>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}が{f|資料|しりょう}を<b>ご{f|覧|らん}になりました</b>。
The department head looked at the documents.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}が{f|資料|しりょう}を<b>{f|拝見|はいけん}しました</b>。
I looked at the documents.
</pre>

Both sentences are addressed to the same superior and both are polite. Swapping the
verbs - ❌{f|私|わたし}がご{f|覧|らん}になりました - claims honour for yourself, and
it is the error native listeners notice fastest.

### お○○になる vs お○○する

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one frame, two endings, opposite directions. `になる` raises
the actor; `する` lowers the actor.

</div>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}が<b>お{f|待|ま}ちになっています</b>。
The teacher is waiting for you.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|外|そと}で<b>お{f|待|ま}ちします</b>。
I'll wait outside.
</pre>

The trap is that they look almost identical. A useful check: if the subject is
you, the ending must be `する` or `いたす`. There is no situation in which
❌お{f|待|ま}ちになります describes your own waiting.

### {f|丁寧|ていねい}{f|語|ご} vs {f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ます` is politeness to the **listener**. It says nothing at
all about the person in the sentence.

</div>

<pre>
{f|課長|かちょう}は{f|三時|さんじ}に{f|来|き}ます。
The section chief is coming at three.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|課長|かちょう}は{f|三時|さんじ}に<b>いらっしゃいます</b>。
The section chief will be arriving at three.
</pre>

The first sentence is polite Japanese and perfectly grammatical; spoken to a
visitor about your own section chief it is exactly right. Spoken to a junior
colleague about the chief, the second is the expected form. Politeness and honour
are separate axes, and `ます` only moves one of them.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|社長|しゃちょう}がおっしゃられました。 → ✅ {f|社長|しゃちょう}が<b>おっしゃいました</b>。
  おっしゃる is already honorific; adding the honorific passive `られる` makes it
  {f|二重|にじゅう}{f|敬語|けいご}. One mechanism per verb.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}が{f|明日|あした}いらっしゃいます。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}が{f|明日|あした}<b>{f|伺|うかが}います</b>。
  いらっしゃる raises its subject, so pointing it at yourself is the one thing
  {f|敬語|けいご} never allows.

- ❌（{f|取引先|とりひきさき}への{f|電話|でんわ}で）{f|部長|ぶちょう}はいらっしゃいません。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}の{f|山田|やまだ}は<b>おりません</b>。
  To someone outside the company, your own manager belongs to your side and takes
  humble forms - and the honorific `さん` and title drop away too.

- ❌ どうぞ{f|拝見|はいけん}してください。 → ✅ どうぞ<b>ご{f|覧|らん}ください</b>。
  {f|拝見|はいけん}する is humble, so inviting someone to do it lowers them. The
  honorific counterpart is ご{f|覧|らん}になる.

## Practice

**1.** {f|先生|せんせい}はコーヒーを____か。（{f|飲|の}む → {f|尊敬|そんけい}{f|語|ご}）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|先生|せんせい}はコーヒーを<b>{f|召|め}し{f|上|あ}がります</b>か。

The action belongs to the teacher, so it takes an honorific form, and
{f|飲|の}む is one of the verbs with its own replacement word rather than a frame.
お{f|飲|の}みになりますか is also correct, if a little stiffer.

</details>

**2.** Choose the correct form: （{f|私|わたし}が・{f|部長|ぶちょう}が）お{f|持|も}ちします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|私|わたし}が</b>お{f|持|も}ちします。

The frame <code>お～する</code> is humble, so its subject has to be you or someone on
your side. With {f|部長|ぶちょう} as subject you would need
お{f|持|も}ちになります instead.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, speaking to a client on the phone about your
colleague {f|鈴木|すずき}: "Suzuki is out at the moment."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|鈴木|すずき}はただ{f|今|いま}<b>{f|外出|がいしゅつ}しております</b>。

Your colleague is inside your circle and the client is outside it, so Suzuki takes
humble forms and loses the `さん`. おります is the humble counterpart of います, and
`ておる` is its て-form partner.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|田中|たなか}{f|様|さま}がお{f|越|こ}しになられました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|田中|たなか}{f|様|さま}が<b>お{f|越|こ}しになりました</b>。

<code>お{f|越|こ}しになる</code> is already an honorific frame, and `られる` adds a
second one on top. Doubling the mechanism is the classic
{f|二重|にじゅう}{f|敬語|けいご} error.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|山田|やまだ}{f|先生|せんせい}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|来|き}ます。
(b) {f|山田|やまだ}{f|先生|せんせい}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}いらっしゃいます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are polite to the listener, because both end in `ます`. Only (b) also raises
Professor Yamada himself. Sentence (a) is what you would say to someone outside the
school if the professor were a colleague of yours; said to a student about their own
teacher, it sounds oddly flat, as though you did not think the professor merited the
honorific.

</details>
