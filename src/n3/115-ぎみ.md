# ～ぎみ

**Meaning** · showing a slight touch of; a bit on the ～ side

`～ぎみ` (written {f|気味|きみ} when it stands alone, voiced to ぎみ as a suffix)
reports that something is **a little way** toward a state, right now. Not fully
there - just far enough that you can see it. It is the word for a cold you have not
properly caught yet and a train that is running four minutes behind.

## Formation

The result is a **noun**. It predicates with だ/です, modifies a following noun with
`の`, and takes `な` before `ので` and `のに` exactly as any noun does.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + ぎみ | {f|風邪|かぜ} → {f|風邪|かぜ}ぎみ |
| Noun (する-verb noun) | Noun + ぎみ | {f|緊張|きんちょう} → {f|緊張|きんちょう}ぎみ |
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + ぎみ | {f|太|ふと}ります → {f|太|ふと}りぎみ |
| Predicate | ～ぎみ + だ / です | {f|疲|つか}れぎみです |
| Before a noun | ～ぎみ + の + Noun | {f|疲|つか}れぎみの{f|顔|かお} |
| Before ので・のに | ～ぎみ + な + ので | {f|遅|おく}れぎみなので |
| As an adverb | ～ぎみ + に + Verb | {f|焦|あせ}りぎみに{f|話|はな}す |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the reading is ぎみ, with rendaku. The unvoiced {f|気味|きみ} is a
separate word meaning "sensation", and it survives in
<code>{f|気味|きみ}が{f|悪|わる}い</code> - "creepy, unsettling" - which has nothing
to do with this suffix. Say きみわるい for that and ぎみ for this.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}はちょっと{f|風邪|かぜ}<b>ぎみ</b>です。
I've got a bit of a cold today.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|最近|さいきん}、{f|少|すこ}し{f|太|ふと}り<b>ぎみ</b>だ。
I've put on a little weight lately.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|予定|よてい}より{f|遅|おく}れ<b>ぎみ</b>で{f|進|すす}んでいる。
The meeting is running slightly behind schedule.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|疲|つか}れ<b>ぎみ</b>の{f|顔|かお}をしていたので、{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}るように{f|言|い}った。
He looked a bit worn out, so I told him to go home early.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|緊張|きんちょう}<b>ぎみ</b>の{f|声|こえ}で、{f|新人|しんじん}は{f|自己紹介|じこしょうかい}を{f|始|はじ}めた。
In a slightly nervous voice, the new employee began her self-introduction.
</pre>

<pre>
このところ{f|売|う}り{f|上|あ}げが{f|下|さ}がり<b>ぎみ</b>なので、{f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|広告|こうこく}を{f|増|ふ}やします。
Sales have been trending a little down lately, so we'll step up the advertising from next month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|受験|じゅけん}が{f|近|ちか}づいてから{f|寝不足|ねぶそく}<b>ぎみ</b>で、{f|朝|あさ}なかなか{f|起|お}きられない。
Since the entrance exams got close my son has been a bit short of sleep and struggles to get up.
</pre>

<pre>

The manager spoke with a hint of impatience, but in the end nothing was decided.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`～ぎみ` almost always points **away from normal in an unwelcome direction**. Health
that is slipping, a schedule that is slipping, a number that is slipping. You will
not hear ❌<code>{f|元気|げんき}ぎみ</code> or ❌<code>{f|上手|じょうず}ぎみ</code>:
there is no such thing as being slightly too well.

It is also about **degree, not frequency**. {f|風邪|かぜ}ぎみ does not mean "I often
catch colds"; it means "right now, a small amount of cold". That is why it pairs so
naturally with {f|少|すこ}し and ちょっと, which reinforce rather than contradict it:
{f|少|すこ}し{f|疲|つか}れぎみです.

The register is neutral and it is entirely at home in business Japanese:
{f|下|さ}がりぎみ, {f|遅|おく}れぎみ and {f|伸|の}びぎみ are ordinary vocabulary in a
sales report. In writing it is normally spelled with the kanji {f|気味|きみ}; the
reading of the suffix is still ぎみ.

One social use worth knowing: `～ぎみ` softens. Telling a colleague
{f|疲|つか}れぎみですね is far gentler than {f|疲|つか}れていますね, because it
presents the observation as slight and provisional rather than as a verdict on how
they look. The same hedging instinct makes {f|風邪|かぜ}ぎみなので{f|休|やす}みます a
polite way to call in sick without claiming to be seriously ill.

## Contrast

### ～ぎみ vs ～がち

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ぎみ` is **how much**, `がち` is **how often**. `ぎみ` says one
state is slightly present now; `がち` says an event keeps recurring.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今朝|けさ}の{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|遅|おく}れ<b>ぎみ</b>だった。
This morning's train was running a bit late.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|路線|ろせん}の{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|遅|おく}れ<b>がち</b>だ。
Trains on this line tend to run late.
</pre>

The first sentence is about one morning and a small number of minutes. The second
is about a pattern across many mornings and says nothing about how late. Use the
wrong one and you make a claim you did not intend: telling your boss
{f|遅|おく}れがちです about a single delayed delivery accuses the whole operation of
being unreliable.

They also behave differently in a sentence. `ぎみ` builds a noun and takes `の`
before a following noun; `がち` builds a な-adjective and takes `な`:
{f|疲|つか}れぎみ<b>の</b>{f|顔|かお} but {f|病気|びょうき}がち<b>な</b>{f|子|こ}ども.

### ～ぎみ vs ～っぽい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ぎみ` says a *little of the real thing is present*; `っぽい`
says it *feels like* the thing, whether or not it is.

</div>

The pair that shows this best is a cold, because both are said constantly:

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}から{f|風邪|かぜ}<b>ぎみ</b>で、{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}んでいる。
I've had a touch of a cold since yesterday, so I'm taking medicine.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|何|なん}となく{f|風邪|かぜ}<b>っぽい</b>から、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|早|はや}く{f|寝|ね}る。
I feel like I might be coming down with something, so I'm going to bed early.
</pre>

`ぎみ` states a mild fact: there is a cold, in small quantity. `っぽい` states an
impression: something about how I feel resembles a cold. Which is why
{f|風邪|かぜ}っぽい is what you say before you are sure, and {f|風邪|かぜ}ぎみ once
you are.

Grammatically they part company completely. `っぽい` produces an い-adjective - 
{f|風邪|かぜ}っぽかった, {f|風邪|かぜ}っぽくない - while `ぎみ` produces a noun:
{f|風邪|かぜ}ぎみだった, {f|風邪|かぜ}ぎみではない.

### ～ぎみ vs ちょっと・すこし

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ちょっと turns *any* predicate down a notch. `ぎみ` only attaches
to a fixed kind of word, and it carries a judgement that ちょっと does not.

</div>

For a plain matter of degree, an adverb is the natural choice, and `ぎみ` is simply
not available:

<pre>
このコーヒーは<b>ちょっと</b>{f|苦|にが}い。
This coffee is a little bitter.
</pre>

❌<code>{f|苦|にが}いぎみ</code> is impossible - `ぎみ` does not attach to
い-adjectives at all. Nor does it attach to happy states:
❌<code>うれしいぎみ</code>, ❌<code>{f|楽|たの}しみぎみ</code>.

Where both are possible, `ぎみ` adds the sense of a **deviation from where things
should be**:

<pre>
{f|体重|たいじゅう}が{f|増|ふ}え<b>ぎみ</b>なので、{f|夜|よる}は{f|食|た}べないようにしている。
My weight has been creeping up, so I'm trying not to eat at night.
</pre>

<code>{f|体重|たいじゅう}が{f|少|すこ}し{f|増|ふ}えた</code> is a neutral measurement.
<code>{f|増|ふ}えぎみ</code> is a measurement plus a frown. And the two combine
happily: {f|少|すこ}し{f|増|ふ}えぎみ.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|太|ふと}る<b>ぎみ</b> → ✅ {f|太|ふと}り<b>ぎみ</b>
  With verbs, `ぎみ` attaches to the ます-stem, never the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|風邪|かぜ}ぎみ<b>の</b>です。 → ✅ {f|風邪|かぜ}ぎみ<b>です</b>。
  `の` goes between ～ぎみ and a following **noun**
  （<code>{f|風邪|かぜ}ぎみの{f|学生|がくせい}</code>）, not before です.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|元気|げんき}<b>ぎみ</b>です。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|元気|げんき}です。
  `ぎみ` marks a slide toward something unwanted. There is no polite way to be a
  little bit too healthy.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|学校|がっこう}を{f|休|やす}み<b>ぎみ</b>だ。（meaning "he's often absent"） → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|学校|がっこう}を{f|休|やす}み<b>がち</b>だ。
  Repeated absences are frequency, and frequency is `がち`.

- ❌ かぜきみです。 → ✅ かぜぎみです。
  The suffix voices. きみ unvoiced belongs to
  <code>{f|気味|きみ}が{f|悪|わる}い</code>, an unrelated expression.

## Practice

**1.** {f|昨日|きのう}あまり{f|寝|ね}ていないので、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|疲|つか}れ____です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|疲|つか}れ<b>ぎみ</b>です。

<code>{f|疲|つか}れる</code> takes its ます-stem <code>{f|疲|つか}れ</code> before
ぎみ, and the result is a noun, so です attaches directly. The sentence claims mild
tiredness right now, not a habit of being tired.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|時計|とけい}は{f|少|すこ}し（{f|進|すす}みぎみ・{f|進|すす}みがち）なので、{f|時間|じかん}を{f|信|しん}じすぎないほうがいい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|時計|とけい}は{f|少|すこ}し<b>{f|進|すす}みぎみ</b>なので…

The clock is permanently a small amount fast - that is degree, so `ぎみ`. `がち`
would need repeated separate events, which does not fit a clock that simply runs
slightly ahead. Note the `な` before ので, which is the noun pattern.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The numbers have been a little low this month, so let's meet on Friday."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|今月|こんげつ}は{f|数字|すうじ}が{f|下|さ}がり<b>ぎみ</b>なので、{f|金曜日|きんようび}に{f|集|あつ}まりましょう。

<code>{f|下|さ}がりぎみ</code> is standard business phrasing for a mild downward
trend, and it is softer than <code>{f|下|さ}がっています</code> - which is exactly
why people reach for it in meetings.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|最近|さいきん}の{f|天気|てんき}は{f|曇|くも}りぎみで、{f|洗濯物|せんたくもの}が{f|乾|かわ}かない{f|日|ひ}が{f|多|おお}い。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|最近|さいきん}の{f|天気|てんき}は{f|曇|くも}り<b>がち</b>で、{f|洗濯物|せんたくもの}が{f|乾|かわ}かない{f|日|ひ}が{f|多|おお}い。

The clue is <code>{f|日|ひ}が{f|多|おお}い</code> - the sentence is counting days,
so it is about how often, not how much. Cloudiness recurring across many days is
<code>{f|曇|くも}りがち</code>, a fixed and very common combination.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|緊張|きんちょう}<b>ぎみ</b>だった。
(b) {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|緊張|きんちょう}していた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports a small, visible amount of nerves - her voice a little tight, her hands
a little busy - and it hedges. The speaker is describing an impression from the
outside and leaving room to be wrong.

(b) states plainly that she was nervous, with no ceiling on how much. It is the
neutral description and could cover anything from mild to badly shaken. Between
colleagues, (a) is the kinder thing to say out loud.

</details>
