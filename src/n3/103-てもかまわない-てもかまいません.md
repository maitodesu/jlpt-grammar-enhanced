# ～てもかまわない・～てもかまいません

**Meaning** · it's fine either way; I don't mind - permission given by disclaiming any objection

`かまう` means "to mind, to be bothered". So `～てもかまわない` does not say the
action is *good*; it says the speaker has **no objection** to it. That small
difference in angle is why the pattern is at home in written rules and business
Japanese, and why it can sound a shade cooler than a warm "go ahead".

## Formation

`かまわない` sits on top of the same `～ても` you already know. Everything before it
takes the ordinary `ても` shapes.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + もかまわない | {f|使|つか}う → {f|使|つか}ってもかまわない |
| Verb (negative) | なくて + もかまわない | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}なくてもかまわない |
| い-adjective | ～くて + もかまわない | {f|狭|せま}い → {f|狭|せま}くてもかまわない |
| な-adjective | ～で + もかまわない | {f|不便|ふべん} → {f|不便|ふべん}でもかまわない |
| Noun | Noun + でもかまわない | {f|明日|あした} → {f|明日|あした}でもかまわない |
| Polite | ～てもかまいません | {f|使|つか}ってもかまいません |
| Asking permission | ～てもかまいませんか | {f|使|つか}ってもかまいませんか |
| Past | ～てもかまわなかった | {f|使|つか}ってもかまわなかった |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the kanji is {f|構|かま}う, but this pattern is written in kana far more
often than not. Both <code>かまいません</code> and
<code>{f|構|かま}いません</code> are correct; kana is the safer default.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
ここに{f|座|すわ}っ<b>てもかまいませんか</b>。
Do you mind if I sit here?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}では{f|辞書|じしょ}を{f|使|つか}っ<b>てもかまいません</b>。
You may use a dictionary in the exam.
</pre>

<pre>

If you're short of time, you don't have to do all of it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}く<b>てもかまわない</b>ので、{f|駅|えき}に{f|近|ちか}いところがいいです。
I don't mind a small flat - I'd rather have somewhere near the station.
</pre>

<pre>

You're welcome to pay by cash or by card.
</pre>

It doesn't matter if the format isn't quite right - just hand something in first.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}の{f|集合|しゅうごう}は{f|八時|はちじ}でも{f|九時|くじ}でも<b>かまわない</b>と{f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|言|い}っていました。
The manager said eight or nine would both be fine for tomorrow's start.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|撮|と}っ<b>てもかまいませんか</b>。{f|後|あと}でブログに{f|載|の}せたいのですが。
Would you mind if I took a photo? I'd like to put it on my blog later.
</pre>

<pre>

There's no rush, so it's perfectly all right if you reply next week.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`てもいい` and `てもかまわない` are close, but they are not saying quite the same
thing. `てもいい` calls the action **good** - a green light. `てもかまわない` says
the speaker **is not bothered** - the light was never red in the first place. Out
of that comes the pattern's characteristic flatness: it is generous without being
warm.

That flatness is an advantage in **writing and business**, which is where you meet
it most: exam instructions, application forms, hotel policies, emails from
suppliers. <code>キャンセルは{f|前日|ぜんじつ}までにご{f|連絡|れんらく}いただければかまいません</code>
sounds like an institution, and is meant to.

It is also, spoken with the wrong intonation, the sentence of someone who is
politely annoyed. <code>{f|別|べつ}にかまいませんけど</code> ("I don't *mind*, but…")
is a recognisable way of not being fine at all. Learners often reach for
<code>かまいません</code> when they mean to be reassuring and land somewhere colder
than intended; if warmth is the goal,
<code>もちろんいいですよ</code> or <code>{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}ですよ</code> does
the job better.

As a **question**, <code>～てもかまいませんか</code> is politer and more formal
than <code>～てもいいですか</code>, and slightly less formal than
<code>～てもよろしいでしょうか</code>. All three are usable at work; the middle one
is the safe everyday choice with someone you do not know well.

One direction to avoid: **granting** permission to someone senior. Telling your
manager <code>{f|座|すわ}ってもかまいませんよ</code> puts you in charge of the
chairs. Answering their question with a bare <code>かまいません</code> is normal
and fine - it is the unprompted granting that presumes.

## Contrast

### ～てもかまわない vs ～てもいい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `てもいい` says the action is **all right**. `てもかまわない`
says the speaker **doesn't mind**. The first is permission; the second is the
absence of objection.

</div>

<pre>
そのペン、{f|使|つか}っ<b>てもいいよ</b>。
Sure, use that pen.
</pre>

<pre>
どのペンを{f|使|つか}っ<b>てもかまいません</b>。
It doesn't matter which pen you use.
</pre>

The first is a friend handing something over. The second is an invigilator stating
that pen choice is not regulated. Notice how naturally `かまわない` takes
どの, どちら, いつ and でも - it is the form of choice for saying "any of these is
acceptable", because indifference is exactly what it expresses.

In casual conversation between friends, `てもいい` and simply
<code>いいよ</code> are far commoner. <code>かまわないよ</code> is not wrong there,
but it sounds a little grown-up and measured.

### ～てもかまいませんか vs ～てもよろしいでしょうか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same request, three rungs of politeness:
<code>～てもいいですか</code> ＜ <code>～てもかまいませんか</code> ＜
<code>～てもよろしいでしょうか</code>.

</div>

<pre>
{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}け<b>てもいいですか</b>。
Can I open the window?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|少|すこ}し{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}け<b>てもかまいませんか</b>。
Would you mind if I opened the window a little?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|恐|おそ}れ{f|入|い}りますが、{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}け<b>てもよろしいでしょうか</b>。
Excuse me - would it be all right if I opened the window?
</pre>

Treat these as a ladder rather than as synonyms. The first is what you say to a
friend or a shop assistant, the second to a colleague or a stranger, the third to
a customer or in a formal setting. Going too far up the ladder is a mild error
too: <code>よろしいでしょうか</code> to a close friend sounds like a joke.

### ～てもかまわない vs ～ても{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different worlds.
<code>ても{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}</code> is the spoken, everyday version;
`てもかまわない` is the written and formal one.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}、{f|少|すこ}し{f|遅|おく}れ<b>ても{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}ですか</b>。
Is it OK if I'm a bit late tomorrow?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|提出|ていしゅつ}が{f|明日|あした}になっ<b>てもかまいません</b>。
It is acceptable for submission to be tomorrow.
</pre>

<code>{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}</code> has spread enormously in modern spoken
Japanese and now covers most of what `かまわない` used to do in conversation. The
reverse swap is what sounds off: an official notice reading
<code>{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}です</code> feels chatty, and in a contract it would
be unusable.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}く**でもかまいません**。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}っ<b>てもかまいません</b>。
  Verbs come in on the て-form. `でもかまわない` is for nouns and な-adjectives:
  <code>{f|明日|あした}でもかまいません</code>.

- ❌ {f|値段|ねだん}が{f|高|たか}い**でもかまいません**。 → ✅ {f|値段|ねだん}が{f|高|たか}く<b>てもかまいません</b>。
  An い-adjective becomes <code>～くても</code> first. The `でも` route is not open
  to it.

- ❌ {f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|読|よ}まなく**でもかまいません**。 → ✅ {f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|読|よ}まなく<b>てもかまいません</b>。
  The negative is <code>～なくても</code>. `なくで` does not exist.

- ❌（to your manager, unprompted）{f|部長|ぶちょう}、こちらの{f|席|せき}に{f|座|すわ}っ**てもかまいませんよ**。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、こちらの{f|席|せき}に<b>どうぞ</b>。
  Handing out permission upwards presumes authority you do not have. Offer, don't
  permit.

## Practice

**1.** コピーの{f|紙|かみ}は{f|裏|うら}が{f|使|つか}ってあるものでも____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|裏|うら}が{f|使|つか}ってあるものでも<b>かまいません</b>。

The thing being permitted is a **noun** - paper of a certain kind - so it takes
<code>でもかまいません</code> rather than the て-form. The sentence says the
speaker has no objection either way, which is precisely this pattern rather than
<code>いいです</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: （{f|友達|ともだち}に）このおかし、{f|一|ひと}つ{f|食|た}べ（てもいい?・てもかまいませんか?）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一|ひと}つ{f|食|た}べ<b>てもいい?</b>

To a friend, <code>てもいい?</code> is the natural register.
<code>てもかまいませんか</code> is a step up in formality and would sound as though
you had suddenly started addressing them as a client - which is occasionally a
joke, but not the default.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "You don't have to write your name if you'd rather not."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|書|か}きたくなければ、お{f|名前|なまえ}は{f|書|か}か<b>なくてもかまいません</b>。

The negative permission is <code>～なくてもかまいません</code>. On a survey form
this is exactly the register you want - it disclaims any requirement without
sounding as if the form is doing you a favour.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部屋|へや}が{f|静|しず}かくてもかまいませんから、{f|安|やす}いほうがいいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部屋|へや}が<b>{f|静|しず}かでもかまいません</b>から、{f|安|やす}いほうがいいです。

<code>{f|静|しず}か</code> is a な-adjective, so it takes `でも`, not
<code>～くても</code>; ❌<code>{f|静|しず}かくて</code> is not a form. (Note the
sentence is odd in meaning too - nobody objects to quiet - so
<code>{f|狭|せま}くてもかまいません</code> would make a better sentence.)

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|明日|あした}でもいいですよ。
(b) {f|明日|あした}でもかまいませんよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) offers tomorrow as a positively acceptable option. It is warm and helpful, the
sort of thing you say to put someone at ease about a deadline.

(b) says you would not be bothered by tomorrow. The content is the same, but the
tone is a step cooler and more businesslike - it disclaims an objection rather
than extending an offer. In an email to a client that neutrality is an advantage;
to a friend who is apologising for being late, (a) is the kinder sentence.

</details>
