# ～きる

**Meaning** · do all of it, right to the end; be completely in a state

`～きる` (from {f|切|き}る, "to cut") takes a verb and adds **completion with nothing
left over**. Not merely finishing, but getting through the whole quantity: the last
page, the last spoonful, the last yen. A second use pushes a state to its limit - 
utterly exhausted, thoroughly cold, perfectly obvious.

## Formation

`きる` attaches to the **ます-stem** and produces a verb. Because it is
{f|切|き}る, it conjugates as an **う-verb** (a Group 1 / {f|五段|ごだん} verb),
despite ending in -る.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + きる | {f|食|た}べます → {f|食|た}べきる |
| する-verb | Noun + し + きる | {f|整理|せいり}します → {f|整理|せいり}しきる |
| Past | ～きった | {f|読|よ}みきった |
| て-form | ～きって | {f|使|つか}いきって |
| Negative (う-verb!) | ～きらない | {f|食|た}べきらない |
| Potential | ～きれる / ～きれない | {f|食|た}べきれない |
| State pushed to its limit | ～きった + Noun | {f|疲|つか}れきった{f|顔|かお} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the completion sense needs a **quantity or a course** to run to the end
of: a plateful, a book, a savings account, a race. The intensifying sense needs a
verb of **change of state**: {f|疲|つか}れる, {f|冷|ひ}える, {f|弱|よわ}る,
{f|澄|す}む, {f|困|こま}る. Verbs with neither - ❌<code>{f|見|み}きる</code> in the
sense "look completely", ❌<code>{f|住|す}みきる</code> - do not take it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大|おお}きなカレーを{f|一人|ひとり}で<b>{f|食|た}べきった</b>。
I finished off a huge plate of curry by myself.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今月|こんげつ}のお{f|金|かね}を、もう<b>{f|使|つか}いきって</b>しまった。
I've already spent all of this month's money.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|長|なが}い{f|小説|しょうせつ}を{f|三日|みっか}で<b>{f|読|よ}みきった</b>。
I got through a long novel in three days.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|最後|さいご}まで<b>{f|走|はし}りきった</b>{f|選手|せんしゅ}に、{f|大|おお}きな{f|拍手|はくしゅ}が{f|送|おく}られた。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}しの{f|片|かた}づけで<b>{f|疲|つか}れきって</b>、{f|夕|ゆう}ご{f|飯|はん}も{f|食|た}べずに{f|寝|ね}た。
I was completely worn out from unpacking after the move and went to bed without dinner.
</pre>

<pre>
そんなことは<b>{f|分|わ}かりきった</b>{f|話|はなし}だから、わざわざ{f|言|い}わなくていい。
That's blindingly obvious, so there's no need to spell it out.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|冬|ふゆ}の{f|朝|あさ}、<b>{f|冷|ひ}えきった</b>{f|部屋|へや}でストーブをつけるのは、なかなかつらい。
On winter mornings, lighting the heater in a room that's gone stone cold is pretty grim.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三年|さんねん}{f|分|ぶん}の{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|一|いっ}{f|週間|しゅうかん}で<b>{f|整理|せいり}しきる</b>のは、さすがに{f|無理|むり}だと{f|思|おも}います。
Getting three years of documents completely sorted in one week honestly isn't going to happen.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The completion sense carries a note of **achievement or effort**.
{f|読|よ}みきった is not a neutral "I finished reading"; it says the book was long
and you got to the end of it. This is why the pattern is common in sports reporting
and in talking about anything demanding - {f|走|はし}りきる, やりきる,
{f|戦|たたか}いきる.

The intensifying sense produces a small set of very common `～きった` adjectives.
{f|疲|つか}れきった, {f|困|こま}りきった, {f|冷|ひ}えきった,
{f|澄|す}みきった{f|空|そら}, {f|分|わ}かりきったこと. These feel a shade literary
before a noun and are entirely ordinary in speech as predicates
（もう{f|疲|つか}れきっています）.

Register is neutral. Several combinations have hardened into everyday vocabulary and
no longer feel like grammar: {f|売|う}り{f|切|き}れる ("sell out"),
{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}り ("deadline"), {f|思|おも}い{f|切|き}って ("taking the
plunge"). You already know those as words; this page is about the productive pattern
behind them.

The conjugation trap is worth stating plainly. {f|切|き}る **is an う-verb**, so the
negative is {f|食|た}べきらない, not ❌<code>{f|食|た}べきない</code>, and the
polite past is {f|食|た}べきりました. Learners who see the -る and treat it as a
る-verb get every form wrong at once.

## Contrast

### ～きる vs ～てしまう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `てしまう` is about the action being **over and irreversible**,
often with regret. `きる` is about the **quantity being exhausted**, often with
satisfaction.

</div>

<pre>
{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}のケーキを{f|食|た}べ<b>てしまった</b>。
I've gone and eaten the cake that was in the fridge.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大|おお}きなケーキだったが、みんなで{f|食|た}べ<b>きった</b>。
It was a big cake, but between us we finished the lot.
</pre>

The first invites "…and I shouldn't have". The second invites "…and we're rather
pleased". They stack, too, and the combination is very common:
{f|使|つか}いきってしまった - used it all up, and now there is none left.

### ～きる vs ～{f|終|お}わる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|終|お}わる</code> means the **activity stopped**
because you reached the end of it. `きる` means the **whole amount was got through**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}やっとレポートを{f|書|か}き<b>{f|終|お}わった</b>。
I finally finished writing the report yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三|さん}{f|時間|じかん}かけて、{f|残|のこ}っていた{f|問題|もんだい}を{f|全部|ぜんぶ}やり<b>きった</b>。
I spent three hours on it and got through every one of the remaining questions.
</pre>

<code>{f|終|お}わる</code> is the neutral, always-available choice and attaches to
almost any verb. `きる` adds difficulty and completeness, and it needs something
countable to be complete *of*. That is why
<code>{f|書|か}き{f|終|お}わる</code> is unremarkable while
{f|書|か}ききる sounds like the report nearly defeated you.

### ～きる vs ～{f|抜|ぬ}く

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both mean "to the very end". `きる` stresses that **nothing was
left**; <code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> stresses that you **kept going despite hardship**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|苦|くる}しかったが、{f|最後|さいご}まで<b>{f|走|はし}りきった</b>。
It hurt, but I made it all the way to the end.
</pre>

<pre>
どんなに{f|反対|はんたい}されても、{f|最後|さいご}までやり<b>{f|抜|ぬ}く</b>つもりだ。
However much people object, I intend to see it through to the end.
</pre>

With a race, either works and they shade differently: {f|走|はし}りきる is about
covering the whole distance, {f|走|はし}り{f|抜|ぬ}く about enduring it. With a
quantity - money, food, paper - only `きる` fits, because there is nothing to endure.
<code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> belongs to a later level, but the pair is worth seeing now
so that `きる` does not get stretched to cover perseverance.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|食|た}べる<b>きる</b> → ✅ {f|食|た}べ<b>きる</b>
  `きる` attaches to the ます-stem.

- ❌ {f|食|た}べ<b>きない</b> → ✅ {f|食|た}べ<b>きらない</b>
  {f|切|き}る is an う-verb, so its negative is きらない. The same goes for
  きります, きって, きった.

- ❌ {f|財布|さいふ}をなくし<b>きった</b>。 → ✅ {f|財布|さいふ}をなくし<b>てしまった</b>。
  A wallet is one object, not a quantity to work through, so there is nothing for
  `きる` to exhaust. Regret over a single completed action is `てしまう`.

- ❌ この{f|町|まち}に{f|三十年|さんじゅうねん}{f|住|す}み<b>きった</b>。 → ✅ この{f|町|まち}に{f|三十年|さんじゅうねん}{f|住|す}んでいる。
  <code>{f|住|す}む</code> has no endpoint, so it takes neither sense of `きる`.

- ❌ {f|疲|つか}れきる{f|顔|かお} → ✅ {f|疲|つか}れきった{f|顔|かお}
  In the intensifying sense, the state has already been reached, so the form before
  a noun is the past <code>きった</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}に{f|残|のこ}っていた{f|野菜|やさい}を、{f|全部|ぜんぶ}スープに{f|入|い}れて{f|使|つか}い____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|使|つか}い<b>きった</b>。

The point is that every last vegetable went into the pot - a quantity worked through
to nothing, which is exactly what `きる` marks. The past is
<code>きった</code>, formed as for any う-verb ending in -る.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|昨日|きのう}のレポートは{f|夜中|よなか}に（{f|書|か}き{f|終|お}わった・{f|書|か}ききった）ので、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|出|だ}せます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨日|きのう}のレポートは{f|夜中|よなか}に<b>{f|書|か}き{f|終|お}わった</b>ので、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|出|だ}せます。

The sentence is a plain report that the writing reached its end, and
<code>{f|書|か}き{f|終|お}わる</code> is the neutral way to say that.
<code>{f|書|か}ききった</code> would add a note of struggle that the matter-of-fact
second half does not support.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "By the time the match ended, every player was completely worn out."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|試合|しあい}が{f|終|お}わるころには、{f|選手|せんしゅ}は{f|全員|ぜんいん}<b>{f|疲|つか}れきって</b>いた。

This is the intensifying sense: the tiredness has been pushed to its limit.
<code>{f|疲|つか}れきっていた</code> is much stronger than
<code>{f|疲|つか}れていた</code>, and it is the standard way to say "utterly
exhausted".

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|量|りょう}なら、{f|一人|ひとり}でも{f|食|た}べきないことはないと{f|思|おも}う。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|量|りょう}なら、{f|一人|ひとり}でも{f|食|た}べ<b>きらない</b>ことはないと{f|思|おも}う。

<code>きる</code> is {f|切|き}る, an う-verb, so its plain negative is
<code>きらない</code>. Treating it as a る-verb and writing
<code>きない</code> is the single commonest error with this pattern.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ビールを{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|飲|の}ん<b>でしまった</b>。
(b) ビールを{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|飲|の}み<b>きった</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports that the beer is gone and colours it with the speaker's feeling about
that - most often "oops", sometimes just finality. The focus is on the situation
now: there is none left.

(b) focuses on the drinking having covered the whole amount, and it carries a hint
of effort or of a job done. It is what you would say about finishing a large crate
between friends, not about carelessly emptying the fridge.

</details>
