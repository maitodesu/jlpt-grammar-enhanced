# ～ばかりだ

**Meaning** · things only go one way, and it is the wrong way - "it just keeps getting ～"

Put a verb of change in front of `ばかりだ` and you get a one-directional slide with
a complaint attached. The pain only gets sharper, the misunderstanding only grows,
the field only gets more overgrown. Where <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> reports a trend from
a distance, `ばかりだ` is the voice of someone watching it happen and not liking it.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb of change | V(dictionary) + ばかりだ | {f|悪|わる}くなる → {f|悪|わる}くなるばかりだ |
| Polite | ～ばかりです | {f|増|ふ}える → {f|増|ふ}えるばかりです |
| Past | ～ばかりだった | {f|深|ふか}くなる → {f|深|ふか}くなるばかりだった |
| Continuing the sentence | ～ばかりで、… | {f|荒|あ}れる → {f|荒|あ}れるばかりで、{f|人|ひと}が{f|来|こ}ない |
| い-adjective (via なる) | ～くなる + ばかりだ | ひどい → ひどくなるばかりだ |
| な-adjective / Noun (via なる) | ～になる + ばかりだ | {f|不安|ふあん} → {f|不安|ふあん}になるばかりだ |
| "all that is left" | あとは + V(dictionary) + ばかりだ | {f|待|ま}つ → あとは{f|待|ま}つばかりだ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the verb in front stays in the **dictionary form**. Swap it for the
た-form and you have the completely different N4 pattern
<code>～たばかりだ</code>, "have just done": <code>{f|着|つ}いたばかりだ</code> means
"I've only just arrived".

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|痛|いた}みは{f|強|つよ}くなる<b>ばかりだ</b>。
The pain just keeps getting worse.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|何|なに}も{f|言|い}わなければ、{f|誤解|ごかい}は{f|大|おお}きくなる<b>ばかりです</b>。
If you say nothing, the misunderstanding will only grow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}っても、{f|二人|ふたり}の{f|溝|みぞ}は{f|深|ふか}くなる<b>ばかりだった</b>。
However much they talked, the gap between them only got deeper.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}んでも、せきはひどくなる<b>ばかりだった</b>。
Even with the medicine, my cough did nothing but get worse.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|畑|はたけ}は{f|荒|あ}れる<b>ばかりで</b>、{f|手入|てい}れをする{f|人|ひと}がいない。
The fields just get more overgrown, and there's no one to look after them.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|準備|じゅんび}は{f|終|お}わりました。あとは{f|本番|ほんばん}を{f|待|ま}つ<b>ばかりです</b>。
The preparations are done. All that's left now is to wait for the real thing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人|ひと}が{f|減|へ}った{f|商店街|しょうてんがい}では、シャッターの{f|閉|し}まった{f|店|みせ}が{f|増|ふ}える<b>ばかりだ</b>。
In a shopping street that has lost its people, the number of shuttered shops does nothing but grow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|車|くるま}に{f|積|つ}んだので、あとは{f|明日|あした}の{f|朝|あさ}{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}する<b>ばかりです</b>。
The luggage is all in the car, so all that remains is to set off tomorrow morning.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ばかりだ` in this sense is more literary than
<code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code>. It is at home in narration, in a written complaint, in a
doctor's notes and in the sentence a novel uses to end a paragraph;
in ordinary chat people say <code>どんどんひどくなってる</code>. Using it aloud gives
what you say a slightly grave, resigned colour - which is often exactly the point.

It carries an **evaluation**, and the evaluation is negative. That is the clearest
difference from <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code>, which is happy to report good news.
Native speakers find <code>{f|元気|げんき}になるばかりだ</code> odd for a recovering
patient, because the pattern is reaching for the wrong emotional register; the trend
version of that sentence is
<code>{f|元気|げんき}になる{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code>.

Two form notes. The casual contraction `ばっかり` belongs to the "nothing but"
`ばかり` you met at N4 - <code>マンガばっかり{f|読|よ}んでる</code> - and is rarely
heard in this trend use, where the tone is too serious for it. And the
`あとは～ばかりだ` frame, "everything is done except this", is a set expression with a
faintly formal, tidy feel; the everyday equivalent is
<code>あとは～だけだ</code>.

## Contrast

### ～ばかりだ vs ～{f|一方|いっぽう}だ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same one-way movement, different speaker. <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code>
reports; `ばかりだ` complains. Only <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> can be used about a change
you welcome.

</div>

<pre>
{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}は{f|進|すす}む<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</b>。
Technology keeps advancing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|待|ま}たされて、いらいらが{f|増|ふ}える<b>ばかりだった</b>。
Being kept waiting, I only got more and more irritated.
</pre>

You could put `ばかりだ` on the first sentence, but it would turn progress into a
threat. Going the other way,
<code>いらいらが{f|増|ふ}える{f|一方|いっぽう}だった</code> sounds like a report on
someone else's mood. Register differs too: <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> is the newspaper,
`ばかりだ` is the diary.

### {f|変化|へんか}の{f|動詞|どうし} vs {f|動作|どうさ}の{f|動詞|どうし} in front of ばかりだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the verb decides the meaning. A verb of **change** gives "keeps
getting worse". A verb of **action** gives "does nothing but ～".

</div>

<pre>
{f|状況|じょうきょう}は{f|悪|わる}くなる<b>ばかりだ</b>。
The situation only gets worse.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}う<b>ばかりで</b>、{f|何|なに}も{f|手伝|てつだ}ってくれない。
All he does is complain - he doesn't help with anything.
</pre>

<code>{f|悪|わる}くなる</code> moves along a scale, so the first is a trend.
<code>{f|言|い}う</code> is something a person chooses to do, so the second is about
exclusivity: complaining and nothing else. With an ongoing action the more common
shape is <code>～てばかりいる</code> - 
<code>{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}ってばかりいる</code> - and the two are close, with
`ばかりで` leaning towards "…and therefore nothing else gets done".

### V(dictionary)ばかりだ vs V(た)ばかりだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - dictionary form before `ばかり` = a change that keeps going.
た-form before `ばかり` = something that finished a moment ago.

</div>

<pre>
{f|外|そと}は{f|暗|くら}くなる<b>ばかりだ</b>。
It's only getting darker outside.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昼|ひる}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べた<b>ばかりで</b>、まだおなかがいっぱいだ。
I've only just eaten lunch, so I'm still full.
</pre>

One string, two grammars, and the only visible difference is the form of the verb.
Read the verb ending before you decide which one you are looking at - this is one of
the most reliable ways to misread an N3 sentence.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|寒|さむ}い**ばかりだ**。（meaning "it keeps getting colder"） → ✅ {f|寒|さむ}くなる**ばかりだ**。
  The pattern needs a verb with movement in it. An い-adjective must first become
  <code>～くなる</code>.

- ❌ {f|病気|びょうき}は<b>{f|良|よ}くなるばかりだ</b>。 → ✅ {f|病気|びょうき}は<b>{f|良|よ}くなる{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</b>。
  Not a form error but a tone error: `ばかりだ` frames the change as unwelcome, so
  using it about a recovery sounds contradictory.

- ❌ あとは{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}**した**ばかりだ。 → ✅ あとは{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}**する**ばかりだ。
  In the "all that's left" frame the verb is still in the future, so it takes the
  dictionary form. With the た-form the sentence flips to "we've only just left".

- ❌ {f|不満|ふまん}が<b>{f|大|おお}きくなったばかりだった</b>。 → ✅ {f|不満|ふまん}が<b>{f|大|おお}きくなるばかりだった</b>。
  Tense lives on <code>だ</code> at the end. The verb in front of `ばかり` stays in
  the dictionary form however far in the past the events are.

## Practice

**1.** {f|問題|もんだい}を{f|放|ほう}っておくと、{f|状況|じょうきょう}は{f|悪|わる}く____ばかりだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|状況|じょうきょう}は{f|悪|わる}く<b>なる</b>ばかりだ。

`ばかりだ` in this sense attaches to a dictionary-form verb of change, and
<code>なる</code> is what turns the い-adjective <code>{f|悪|わる}い</code> into one.
<code>{f|悪|わる}いばかりだ</code> has no movement in it and cannot express a trend.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|輸出|ゆしゅつ}は{f|伸|の}びる（ばかりだ・{f|一方|いっぽう}だ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|輸出|ゆしゅつ}は{f|伸|の}びる<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</b>。

Growing exports are good news, and `ばかりだ` colours its trend as unwelcome. The
neutral, report-like <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> is what a newspaper would print
here.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Everything's ready - all that's left is to press the button."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|準備|じゅんび}はできました。あとはボタンを{f|押|お}す<b>ばかりです</b>。

The <code>あとは～ばかりだ</code> frame says every other step is finished and one
action remains. The remaining action is still to come, so the verb stays in the
dictionary form.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|雨|あめ}がやまなくて、{f|水|みず}が{f|増|ふ}えたばかりだった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|雨|あめ}がやまなくて、{f|水|みず}が{f|増|ふ}える<b>ばかりだった</b>。

As written, the sentence says the water had only just risen. For the trend reading
the verb must be <code>{f|増|ふ}える</code>, with the past tense carried by
<code>だった</code> at the end.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|弟|おとうと}はゲームをする<b>ばかりで</b>、{f|勉強|べんきょう}しない。
(b) {f|弟|おとうと}の{f|成績|せいせき}は{f|下|さ}がる<b>ばかりだ</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is about exclusivity. <code>する</code> is an action he chooses, so `ばかり`
says that action fills the whole picture and crowds everything else out.

(b) is about direction. <code>{f|下|さ}がる</code> is a movement along a scale, so
`ばかり` says the marks have gone one way only, with the speaker's disapproval
attached. Same word, and the verb type is what tells them apart.

</details>
