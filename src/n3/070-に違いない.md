# ～に違いない

**Meaning** · it must be ～; I'm sure ～ - read as *ちがいない*, the speaker's near-certainty about something they have worked out rather than been told

<code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> is the top of the guessing range. You have not been told, and you
cannot check; but the evidence in front of you leaves you in no real doubt. The
conviction is the speaker's own, which is why the pattern belongs to inference and
not to fact - you never use it about something you simply know.

## Formation

Attaches to a **plain form**, with the usual noun and な-adjective trimming: the `だ`
disappears.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + に{f|違|ちが}いない | {f|落|お}とした → {f|落|お}としたに{f|違|ちが}いない |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + に{f|違|ちが}いない | {f|知|し}らない → {f|知|し}らないに{f|違|ちが}いない |
| い-adjective | plain form + に{f|違|ちが}いない | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}いに{f|違|ちが}いない |
| な-adjective | drop だ + に{f|違|ちが}いない | {f|不安|ふあん}だ → {f|不安|ふあん}に{f|違|ちが}いない |
| Noun | Noun + に{f|違|ちが}いない | {f|本物|ほんもの} → {f|本物|ほんもの}に{f|違|ちが}いない |
| Polite | … + に{f|違|ちが}いありません | {f|疲|つか}れている → {f|疲|つか}れているに{f|違|ちが}いありません |
| Very formal / legal writing | … + に{f|相違|そうい}ない | {f|事実|じじつ} → {f|事実|じじつ}に{f|相違|そうい}ない |

The lights are off, so they must have gone to bed.

</pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

Everything from `かもしれない` up to a flat assertion is a ladder, and it pays to
have the whole thing in view rather than learning each rung separately.

| Pattern | English | What it rests on |
|---|---|---|
| かもしれない | might, could | one possibility among several |
| だろう / でしょう | probably | a general guess, no working shown |
| はずだ | should be, is supposed to be | a fact or rule you can point to |
| に{f|違|ちが}いない | must be, I'm certain | evidence plus the speaker's conviction |
| に{f|決|き}まっている | obviously, of course | conviction alone, stated bluntly |

<code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> is a strong, slightly dramatic ending. It is completely at home in
writing - novels lean on it for a character's inner deduction, and essays use it to
press a point - and in speech it sounds emphatic and a little declamatory. It is
not rude; it just takes up space. In relaxed conversation people more often reach
for きっと with a plain ending, or for <code>に{f|決|き}まってる</code>, or simply `はずだ`.

<code>に{f|違|ちが}いありません</code> is the polite form and is unremarkable in a careful spoken report.
<code>に{f|相違|そうい}ない</code> is the same pattern in legal and formal-document register and is worth
recognising rather than using.

Two things it will not do. It does not stack with other hedges - ❌<code>に{f|違|ちが}いないでしょう</code>
and ❌<code>に{f|違|ちが}いないかもしれない</code> cancel the certainty they just claimed - and it does not
apply to your own plans and feelings, which are not things you deduce. Say
{f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}きます, not ❌<code>{f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}くに{f|違|ちが}いない</code>.

## Contrast

### に{f|違|ちが}いない vs はずだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `はずだ` reasons from something you can point to and survives
being wrong; <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> states your conviction and does not.

</div>

`はずだ` has an escape hatch built in. It says "given what I know, this follows",
which is why <code>はずだったのに</code> - "it was supposed to be, and yet" - is such a
common sentence. <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> has no such form; you cannot walk back a certainty you
just declared.

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}は3{f|時|じ}に{f|始|はじ}まる<b>はず</b>です。
The meeting should start at three.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|様子|ようす}では、{f|会議|かいぎ}はまだ{f|終|お}わっていない<b>に{f|違|ちが}いない</b>。
Going by the look of things, the meeting can't have finished yet.
</pre>

The first reads off a schedule; you did not deduce anything, so <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> there
would sound oddly overwrought. The second has no schedule to read - only evidence
and the speaker's reading of it, which is exactly <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> territory.

### に{f|違|ちが}いない vs かもしれない・だろう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same guess, three strengths: `かもしれない` leaves the other
possibilities open, `だろう` picks one without much working, <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> closes the
question.

</div>

<pre>
あの{f|音|おと}は{f|雷|かみなり}<b>かもしれない</b>。
That noise might be thunder.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|音|おと}は{f|雷|かみなり}<b>だろう</b>。
That noise is probably thunder.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|音|おと}は{f|雷|かみなり}<b>に{f|違|ちが}いない</b>。
That noise must be thunder.
</pre>

Notice what changes with the evidence. The first is fine when you barely heard it;
the third needs the sky to have flashed a second earlier. Using the top rung on
thin evidence is the commonest way learners sound strange with this pattern - it is
not wrong, it is simply overclaiming.

### に{f|違|ちが}いない vs に{f|決|き}まっている

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> is a considered conclusion; <code>に{f|決|き}まっている</code> is a
blunt "obviously", often impatient, and much more casual.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}から{f|走|はし}っても{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わない<b>に{f|決|き}まっている</b>よ。
Of course we won't make it, even if we run.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|時間|じかん}に{f|出|で}たのだから、{f|渋滞|じゅうたい}に{f|巻|ま}き{f|込|こ}まれた<b>に{f|違|ちが}いない</b>。
Leaving at that hour, they must have got caught in traffic.
</pre>

<code>に{f|決|き}まっている</code> treats the conclusion as too obvious to argue with, which can come
across as dismissive of the person you are talking to. <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> shows its
working and stays civil.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|辺|へん}は{f|静|しず}か<b>だに{f|違|ちが}いない</b>。 → ✅ この{f|辺|へん}は{f|静|しず}か<b>に{f|違|ちが}いない</b>。
  The `だ` of a な-adjective or a noun drops before <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code>, exactly as it does
  before `らしい`.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|大阪|おおさか}に{f|行|い}く<b>に{f|違|ちが}いない</b>。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|大阪|おおさか}に{f|行|い}きます。
  Your own plans are not something you deduce. The pattern is for conclusions about
  the world outside your head.

- ❌ {f|受付|うけつけ}は9{f|時|じ}からだ<b>に{f|違|ちが}いない</b>ので、{f|少|すこ}し{f|待|ま}ちましょう。 → ✅ {f|受付|うけつけ}は9{f|時|じ}から<b>のはず</b>なので、{f|少|すこ}し{f|待|ま}ちましょう。
  Opening times come from a notice, not from deduction. Facts you can look up take
  `はずだ`.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|知|し}っていた<b>に{f|違|ちが}いないでしょう</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|知|し}っていた<b>に{f|違|ちが}いありません</b>。
  `でしょう` weakens what <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> has just declared. Use the polite form instead
  of piling a hedge on top.

## Practice

**1.** {f|靴|くつ}がぬれているから、{f|外|そと}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っている____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|外|そと}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っている<b>に{f|違|ちが}いない</b>。

Wet shoes are evidence, and the speaker draws a confident conclusion from them
rather than reading a fact off a timetable - the classic setting for <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|新幹線|しんかんせん}は{f|定刻|ていこく}に{f|着|つ}く（はずです・に{f|違|ちが}いありません）から、{f|駅|えき}で{f|待|ま}っていてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|新幹線|しんかんせん}は{f|定刻|ていこく}に{f|着|つ}く<b>はずです</b>から、{f|駅|えき}で{f|待|ま}っていてください。

The arrival time comes from the timetable, so this is expectation built on a known
fact - `はずだ`. <code>に{f|違|ちが}いありません</code> would present a published schedule as a personal
deduction, which sounds overblown.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "There's no reply from the office, so everyone must have gone home already."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|事務所|じむしょ}から{f|返事|へんじ}がないので、みんなもう{f|帰|かえ}った<b>に{f|違|ちが}いない</b>。

Silence from the office is the evidence, and "must have gone home" is the
conclusion. Past-tense conclusions attach to the た-form with no change:
<code>{f|帰|かえ}ったに{f|違|ちが}いない</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|有名|ゆうめい}だに{f|違|ちが}いないかもしれません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|有名|ゆうめい}<b>に{f|違|ちが}いありません</b>。

Two repairs. The `だ` drops after a な-adjective, and `かもしれません` cannot sit on
top of <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> - one says "maybe", the other says "certainly", and together
they say nothing.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|山田|やまだ}さんはもう{f|着|つ}いている<b>はずです</b>。
(b) {f|山田|やまだ}さんはもう{f|着|つ}いている<b>に{f|違|ちが}いありません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is expectation with its reasoning available: he left at nine, the trip takes an
hour, so by now he should be there. If it turns out he is not, you say
<code>{f|着|つ}いているはずなのに…</code> and nobody is embarrassed.

(b) is the speaker's conviction, usually prompted by fresh evidence - his coat is on
the chair, his laptop is open. It is stronger, more personal, and harder to retreat
from; you have said the question is settled.

</details>
