# ～たびに

**Meaning** · every time X happens, Y happens - without exception, as far as the speaker has seen

`たびに` counts **occasions**, not intervals. Each time the first event comes
round, the second one comes with it. It is the pattern you reach for when a
pairing has become reliable enough to comment on: every time I see him, every
time it rains, every time that song comes on.

## Formation

`たび` is the noun <code>{f|度|たび}</code>, "an occurrence", so a clause modifies it in
the plain form and a noun attaches with `の`. The verb is in the **dictionary
form** no matter when the events happened.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (dictionary) | dictionary + たびに | {f|会|あ}う → {f|会|あ}うたびに |
| Action noun | Noun + の + たびに | {f|試験|しけん} → {f|試験|しけん}のたびに |
| Written style | ～{f|度|たび}に | {f|見|み}る{f|度|たび}に |
| As a connector | そのたびに | …。そのたびに{f|注意|ちゅうい}している |
| ❌ た-form | ❌{f|会|あ}ったたびに | ✅{f|会|あ}うたびに |
| ❌ Noun without の | ❌{f|試験|しけん}たびに | ✅{f|試験|しけん}のたびに |
| ❌ A measured interval | ❌{f|三日|みっか}たびに | ✅{f|三日|みっか}ごとに |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if you can put "every time" in front of an English *clause*,
you want `たびに`. If what follows "every" is a **number plus a unit** - every
three days, every two rows - you want <code>ごとに</code> instead.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}に{f|会|あ}う<b>たびに</b>、{f|元気|げんき}をもらう。
Every time I see him I come away with more energy.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|歌|うた}を{f|聞|き}く<b>たびに</b>、{f|高校|こうこう}{f|時代|じだい}を{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}す。
Every time I hear this song I think of my high-school days.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|旅行|りょこう}の<b>たびに</b>、{f|同|おな}じかばんを{f|使|つか}っています。
I use the same bag every time I travel.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|く}る<b>たびに</b>、この{f|辺|へん}は{f|停電|ていでん}する。
Every time a typhoon comes through, this area loses power.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|甥|おい}は{f|会|あ}う<b>たびに</b>{f|背|せ}が{f|伸|の}びていて、おどろかされる。
My nephew is taller every time I see him - it's startling.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}しの<b>たびに</b>、{f|本|ほん}を{f|何|なん}{f|箱|ばこ}も{f|捨|す}てることになる。
Every move ends with me throwing out several boxes of books.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|祖母|そぼ}は{f|私|わたし}が{f|訪|たず}ねる<b>たびに</b>、{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}の{f|中|なか}のものを{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|持|も}たせようとする。
Every time I visit, my grandmother tries to send me home with the entire contents of her fridge.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}が{f|変|か}わる<b>たびに</b>{f|同|おな}じ{f|説明|せつめい}をやり{f|直|なお}さなければならず、{f|正直|しょうじき}うんざりしています。
Having to start the whole explanation again every time they change our contact person is, frankly, wearing me down.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`たびに` is register-neutral: it appears in casual speech, in polite speech and in
writing without changing shape, and the kanji spelling <code>{f|度|たび}</code> is simply
a written-style choice. What it does carry is a mild **emotional charge**. The
speaker has noticed the pattern and usually has a feeling about it - affection,
irritation, nostalgia, surprise. Sentences with `たびに` very often end in
<code>{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}す</code>, <code>うれしくなる</code>, <code>{f|困|こま}る</code> or a
passive of surprise.

That is also why it loves **change over time**. With a comparative or a verb of
growth, `たびに` produces the "more and more each time" reading:
{f|読|よ}むたびに{f|新|あたら}しい{f|発見|はっけん}がある。 Nothing extra is added
to the grammar; the repetition does the work.

<code>そのたびに</code> is worth learning as a fixed connector for narrating: you
describe a recurring situation in one sentence, then open the next with
そのたびに - "and each time that happened…". It saves you repeating the whole
clause.

One limit: because the pattern generalises, it does not fit a **single** future
occasion. {f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|会|あ}うたびに makes no sense, because you are
meeting once.

## Contrast

### ～たびに vs ～ごとに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たびに` counts **events**; <code>ごとに</code> counts **units**. If
a number and a counter come before it, use <code>ごとに</code>; if a clause comes
before it, use `たびに`.

</div>

<pre>
バスは{f|十五分|じゅうごふん}ごとに{f|出|で}ています。
The buses run every fifteen minutes.
</pre>

You cannot say ❌{f|十五分|じゅうごふん}たびに, because fifteen minutes is a
measured interval rather than an occasion. The other direction fails just as
hard:

<pre>
{f|母|はは}は{f|電話|でんわ}する<b>たびに</b>、{f|同|おな}じ{f|話|はなし}をする。
Every time I ring, my mother tells me the same story.
</pre>

Here the phone calls happen when they happen - there is no fixed interval - and
<code>ごとに</code> would be wrong. There is a small overlap with nouns:
{f|試合|しあい}のたびに and {f|試合|しあい}ごとに are both possible, but the first
means "each time there is a match" and the second is closer to "match by match",
tracking a change across a series.

### ～たびに vs ～と

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `と` states an **automatic consequence**: this causes that,
every time, as a matter of how the world works. `たびに` merely reports that the
two keep coming together, and it can carry the speaker's reaction.

</div>

<pre>
{f|春|はる}になると、この{f|川|かわ}の{f|水|みず}が{f|増|ふ}えます。
When spring comes, the water in this river rises.
</pre>

That is nature, not commentary. Compare an observation with a person in it:

<pre>
{f|父|ちち}は{f|酒|さけ}を{f|飲|の}む<b>たびに</b>、{f|若|わか}いころの{f|話|はなし}を{f|始|はじ}める。
Every time my father drinks, he starts on stories about his youth.
</pre>

`と` would also be grammatical there, but it would present the storytelling as a
mechanical reflex. `たびに` keeps the speaker in the sentence - you can hear the
raised eyebrow. Conversely, `と` accepts states and conditions
({f|安|やす}いと{f|売|う}れる) where `たびに` needs a repeatable event.

### ～たびに vs ～{f|時|とき}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|時|とき}に</code> picks out **one** occasion, or is neutral
about how many. `たびに` insists on **all** of them.

</div>

<pre>
{f|京都|きょうと}へ{f|行|い}った{f|時|とき}に、この{f|茶碗|ちゃわん}を{f|買|か}いました。
I bought this teacup when I went to Kyoto.
</pre>

One trip, one purchase. Now the generalising version:

<pre>
{f|京都|きょうと}へ{f|行|い}く<b>たびに</b>、{f|同|おな}じ{f|店|みせ}で{f|器|うつわ}を{f|買|か}ってしまう。
Every time I go to Kyoto I end up buying pottery at the same shop.
</pre>

The tense rule also differs. <code>{f|時|とき}</code> chooses between dictionary and
た-form to show whether the main clause happens before or after it; `たびに` has
no such choice, because it is always the dictionary form.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|日本|にほん}に{f|行|い}ったたびに、{f|温泉|おんせん}に{f|入|はい}ります。 → ✅ {f|日本|にほん}に{f|行|い}くたびに、{f|温泉|おんせん}に{f|入|はい}ります。
  `たびに` always follows the dictionary form. The past reading, if any, comes
  from the main clause.

- ❌ {f|会議|かいぎ}たびに、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|作|つく}り{f|直|なお}す。 → ✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}のたびに、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|作|つく}り{f|直|なお}す。
  <code>たび</code> is a noun, so another noun has to be linked with `の`.

- ❌ {f|二年|にねん}たびに、{f|車|くるま}を{f|買|か}い{f|替|か}えている。 → ✅ {f|二年|にねん}ごとに、{f|車|くるま}を{f|買|か}い{f|替|か}えている。
  A number plus a unit of time is an interval, and intervals take
  <code>ごとに</code> or <code>おきに</code>, never `たびに`.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|会|あ}うたびに、この{f|話|はなし}をします。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|会|あ}った{f|時|とき}に、この{f|話|はなし}をします。
  One scheduled meeting is not a repeated occasion, so there is nothing for
  `たびに` to generalise over.

## Practice

**1.** {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}く____、お{f|土産|みやげ}を{f|買|か}ってきてくれる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}く<b>たびに</b>、お{f|土産|みやげ}を{f|買|か}ってきてくれる。

The trips are occasions rather than measured intervals, and the souvenir arrives
on all of them - the core "every time" meaning. Note the dictionary form of the
verb.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|薬|くすり}は{f|六時間|ろくじかん}（たびに・ごとに）{f|飲|の}んでください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|薬|くすり}は{f|六時間|ろくじかん}<b>ごとに</b>{f|飲|の}んでください。

What precedes is a number plus a unit of time, which makes it an interval.
`たびに` needs a clause or an event noun in front of it and cannot count hours.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Every time I read this letter I want to cry."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|手紙|てがみ}を{f|読|よ}む<b>たびに</b>、{f|泣|な}きたくなる。

A repeated occasion plus an emotional reaction is the classic environment for
`たびに`. <code>{f|泣|な}きたくなる</code> rather than
<code>{f|泣|な}きたい</code> captures the "comes over me each time" feel.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|大雨|おおあめ}が{f|降|ふ}ったたびに、この{f|道|みち}は{f|通|とお}れなくなる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|大雨|おおあめ}が{f|降|ふ}るたびに、この{f|道|みち}は{f|通|とお}れなくなる。

The た-form is not available before `たびに`. The sentence describes a standing
tendency, and the dictionary form is what expresses it.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|見|み}る<b>たびに</b>、{f|父|ちち}のことを{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}します。
(b) この{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|見|み}ると、{f|父|ちち}のことを{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}します。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) counts the occasions and quietly says there have been many of them. The
speaker is reflecting on a habit of their own life, and the sentence carries
feeling.

(b) states a straightforward cause and effect: this photograph triggers that
memory. It is flatter and more matter-of-fact, and it would suit explaining why
the photograph is kept in a drawer.

</details>
