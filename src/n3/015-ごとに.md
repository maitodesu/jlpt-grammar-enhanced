# ～ごとに

**Meaning** · each; every; at intervals of - one unit at a time, with none skipped

`ごとに` divides something into equal units and says that whatever follows applies
to **every one of them**. The units can be measured - three days, five metres - 
or they can be categories: each country, each department, each class. Either way,
nothing is left out and nothing is skipped over.

## Formation

`ごと` is a suffix. It attaches straight to a quantity or to a noun, with no
particle in between.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Number + counter (time) | Quantity + ごとに | {f|三日|みっか} → {f|三日|みっか}ごとに |
| Number + counter (distance, things) | Quantity + ごとに | {f|五|ご}メートル → {f|五|ご}メートルごとに |
| Noun as a category | Noun + ごとに | {f|国|くに} → {f|国|くに}ごとに |
| Verb (dictionary), literary | dictionary + ごとに | {f|年|とし}を{f|取|と}る → {f|年|とし}を{f|取|と}るごとに |
| Before a noun | ～ごとの + Noun | {f|部署|ぶしょ}ごとの{f|予算|よさん} |
| ❌ の between noun and ごと | ❌{f|国|くに}のごとに | ✅{f|国|くに}ごとに |
| ❌ な before a noun | ❌{f|一年|いちねん}ごとな{f|検査|けんさ} | ✅{f|一年|いちねん}ごとの{f|検査|けんさ} |
| ❌ Doubling with {f|毎|まい}～ | ❌{f|毎日|まいにち}ごとに | ✅{f|毎日|まいにち} / {f|一日|いちにち}ごとに |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ごとに` states the **length of the cycle**, counting the unit
itself. {f|一日|いちにち}ごとに is therefore *every day*, and
{f|二日|ふつか}ごとに is *every second day*. Do not translate `ごと` as "other".

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|水|みず}は{f|二日|ふつか}<b>ごとに</b>やってください。
Please water it every two days.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|国|くに}<b>ごとに</b>{f|習慣|しゅうかん}が{f|違|ちが}います。
Customs differ from country to country.
</pre>

<pre>
クラス<b>ごとに</b>{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|撮|と}ります。
We'll take the photographs class by class.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|検査|けんさ}は{f|半年|はんとし}<b>ごとに</b>{f|受|う}けることになっている。
You're supposed to have this check-up every six months.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|道|みち}の{f|両側|りょうがわ}に、{f|五|ご}メートル<b>ごとに</b>{f|木|き}が{f|植|う}えてある。
Trees have been planted every five metres along both sides of the road.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部署|ぶしょ}<b>ごとに</b>{f|予算|よさん}が{f|決|き}められているので、{f|勝手|かって}には{f|使|つか}えません。
The budget is set department by department, so I can't just spend it as I like.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|年|とし}を{f|取|と}る<b>ごとに</b>、{f|時間|じかん}が{f|過|す}ぎるのが{f|速|はや}く{f|感|かん}じられるようになった。
With each passing year, time seems to move faster.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|会社|かいしゃ}ではプロジェクト<b>ごとに</b>チームを{f|組|く}み{f|直|なお}すので、{f|同|おな}じ{f|顔|かお}ぶれで{f|働|はたら}くことは{f|少|すく}ない。
This company rebuilds its teams for each project, so you rarely work with the same faces twice.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ごとに` is neutral and slightly businesslike. It is the word of timetables,
manuals, survey results and internal rules - anything that has to state a rate or
a breakdown precisely - and it sounds perfectly normal in speech too. It is not
formal in the way <code>{f|際|さい}に</code> is; it is simply exact.

The two jobs it does feel quite different in use. With a **number** it gives a
rate, and English usually says "every X". With a **category noun** it gives a
breakdown, and English switches to "by" or "each": {f|地域|ちいき}ごとに →
"region by region"; {f|家庭|かてい}ごとに → "in each household". That second use
is extremely common in reports, almost always paired with
<code>{f|違|ちが}う</code>, <code>{f|異|こと}なる</code> or <code>{f|決|き}める</code>.

The verb form - {f|見|み}るごとに、{f|会|あ}うごとに - is real but literary, and
in ordinary speech `たびに` has taken the job over. You can safely read it and
avoid writing it.

<div class="note">

**Note** - a different `ごと` exists, with no `に`, meaning "together with the
whole of it": りんごを{f|皮|かわ}ごと{f|食|た}べる, "eat the apple skin and all";
{f|丸|まる}ごと, "the whole thing". It attaches to a noun exactly like ours, so
read the sentence, not the suffix - if there is no `に` and no counting, it is
this other word.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～ごとに vs ～おきに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ごとに` measures the **cycle**; <code>おきに</code> measures the
**gap left empty** between one occurrence and the next. With days and countable
things the two therefore differ by one.

</div>

<pre>
{f|一日|いちにち}<b>ごとに</b>{f|記録|きろく}をつけています。
I keep a record every day.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一日|いちにち}おきに{f|記録|きろく}をつけています。
I keep a record every other day.
</pre>

Same number, different arithmetic: the first is Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday; the
second is Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Rewritten with `ごとに`, the second sentence
becomes {f|二日|ふつか}ごとに.

With units of duration or length the two happen to coincide, because there is no
"container" to leave empty: {f|十分|じゅっぷん}ごとに and
{f|十分|じゅっぷん}おきに both describe something happening every ten minutes.
That coincidence is exactly why learners generalise from minutes to days and get
it wrong. When the unit is a **day, a week, a seat or an item**, stop and check
which one you mean.

`ごとに` also has a whole second life that <code>おきに</code> does not: the category
use. {f|学年|がくねん}ごとに{f|時間割|じかんわり}が{f|違|ちが}う is fine, and
❌{f|学年|がくねん}おきに would mean skipping alternate year groups.

### ～ごとに vs ～たびに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ごとに` follows a **unit**; <code>たびに</code> follows a
**clause** describing an event. "Every three days" is `ごとに`; "every time I go"
is <code>たびに</code>.

</div>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}の<b>たびに</b>{f|胃|い}が{f|痛|いた}くなる。
My stomach hurts every time there's an exam.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}<b>ごとに</b>{f|成績|せいせき}を{f|記録|きろく}しています。
We record the results for each exam.
</pre>

Both are grammatical, and the pair shows the difference cleanly. The first counts
occasions and reports the speaker's reaction to all of them. The second treats
the exams as a series of units and does something *per unit* - a breakdown, not a
reaction. That is why `ごとに` is at home in a spreadsheet and
<code>たびに</code> is at home in a diary.

### ～ごとに vs ～ずつ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ずつ</code> distributes an **amount**: how much each one
gets. `ごとに` marks the **unit itself**: for each of these, such-and-such is
true.

</div>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}に{f|二|ふた}つずつ{f|配|くば}ってください。
Please hand out two to each person.
</pre>

The number that matters there is the two. Now a sentence where the number that
matters is the unit:

<pre>
{f|三人|さんにん}<b>ごとに</b>グループを{f|作|つく}ってください。
Please form groups of three.
</pre>

A useful diagnostic: <code>ずつ</code> answers "how many each?", `ごとに` answers "per
what?". They can appear together - 
{f|一人|ひとり}ごとに{f|一|ひと}つずつ{f|渡|わた}す - and when they do, each is
doing its own half of the job.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|毎週|まいしゅう}ごとに{f|会議|かいぎ}があります。 → ✅ {f|毎週|まいしゅう}{f|会議|かいぎ}があります。
  <code>{f|毎|まい}～</code> already means "every", so adding `ごとに` says it twice. Use
  one or the other.

- ❌ {f|一日|いちにち}ごとに{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}みます。（intending every other day） → ✅ {f|一日|いちにち}おきに{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}みます。
  `ごとに` counts the cycle, so {f|一日|いちにち}ごとに is *every* day. "Every
  other" needs <code>おきに</code>, or {f|二日|ふつか}ごとに.

- ❌ {f|三年|さんねん}ごとな{f|点検|てんけん}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。 → ✅ {f|三年|さんねん}ごとの{f|点検|てんけん}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。
  Before a noun the linker is `の`. `ごと` is not a な-adjective.

- ❌ プリントを{f|一|いち}まいごとに{f|配|くば}ってください。 → ✅ プリントを{f|一|いち}まいずつ{f|配|くば}ってください。
  The sentence is about how many each person receives, which is
  <code>ずつ</code>. `ごとに` would make each sheet a unit that something is done per.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|町|まち}では、{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}____バスが{f|来|き}ます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|町|まち}では、{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}<b>ごとに</b>バスが{f|来|き}ます。

A number plus a unit of time gives a rate, which is what `ごとに` is for. With
minutes, <code>おきに</code> would mean the same thing, since there is no whole unit
being skipped.

</details>

**2.** Choose. {f|練習|れんしゅう}は{f|月|げつ}・{f|水|すい}・{f|金|きん}にあります。つまり、{f|二日|ふつか}（ごとに・おきに）{f|練習|れんしゅう}があります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

つまり、{f|二日|ふつか}<b>ごとに</b>{f|練習|れんしゅう}があります。

Monday, Wednesday and Friday leave one day empty, which makes the cycle two days
long. {f|二日|ふつか}おきに would leave two days empty and give Monday, Thursday,
Sunday; the equivalent of the correct answer is {f|一日|いちにち}おきに.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The price is different for each size."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

サイズ<b>ごとに</b>{f|値段|ねだん}が{f|違|ちが}います。

This is the category use: sizes are the units, and the statement holds for each
of them. <code>{f|違|ちが}う</code> and <code>{f|異|こと}なる</code> are the verbs this use
attracts.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|学校|がっこう}のごとに{f|規則|きそく}が{f|違|ちが}う。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|学校|がっこう}ごとに{f|規則|きそく}が{f|違|ちが}う。

`ごと` is a suffix and attaches directly to the noun. The `の` belongs only in
front of a following noun, as in {f|学校|がっこう}ごとの{f|規則|きそく}.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|一年|いちねん}<b>ごとに</b>{f|契約|けいやく}を{f|見直|みなお}します。
(b) {f|契約|けいやく}を{f|更新|こうしん}する<b>たびに</b>{f|条件|じょうけん}を{f|見直|みなお}します。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) states a schedule: the review happens on a fixed one-year cycle, whether or
not anything has changed. It is the sentence for a rulebook.

(b) attaches the review to an event instead. Renewals might come at irregular
intervals, and the sentence promises only that each one triggers a review.

</details>
