# ～ことだ

**Meaning** · the thing to do is… - advice offered as the obvious answer to a problem

`～ことだ` hands over a remedy. It does not weigh options or hedge; it names the
one action that solves the problem, in the tone of somebody who has seen this
situation before. That confidence is the whole flavour of the pattern, and it is
also the reason it has to be used carefully.

## Formation

Only verbs. The sentence almost always follows a clause that sets up the problem - 
a `たら`, a `なら`, a `ければ`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + ことだ | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}むことだ |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + ことだ | {f|無理|むり}をする → {f|無理|むり}をしないことだ |
| Verb (する) | ～することだ | {f|相談|そうだん}する → {f|相談|そうだん}することだ |
| Polite | ～ことです | {f|休|やす}むことです |
| Softened | ～ことですね・～ことでしょうね | {f|休|やす}むことですね |
| Typical frame | ～たい／～たら + ～ことだ | {f|治|なお}したいなら、{f|休|やす}むことだ |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the `だ`/`です` is what makes this advice. Strip it and you get
the notice pattern <code>{f|休|やす}むこと。</code>, which is a rule on a wall
rather than a suggestion to a friend.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|風邪|かぜ}のときは、よく{f|寝|ね}る<b>ことです</b>。
When you've got a cold, the thing to do is sleep.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|急|いそ}ぐなら、タクシーに{f|乗|の}る<b>ことだ</b>。
If you're in a hurry, take a taxi.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|上手|じょうず}になりたいなら、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|少|すこ}しずつ{f|続|つづ}ける<b>ことだ</b>。
If you want to get good, keep at it a little every day.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|心配|しんぱい}なら、{f|先|さき}に{f|電話|でんわ}して{f|確|たし}かめる<b>ことです</b>。
If you're worried, ring ahead and check.
</pre>

<pre>

If you want to lose weight, don't eat late at night.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|漢字|かんじ}がなかなか{f|覚|おぼ}えられないなら、{f|手|て}を{f|動|うご}かして{f|何度|なんど}も{f|書|か}いてみる<b>ことです</b>。
If kanji won't stick, write them out by hand over and over.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|山|やま}で{f|道|みち}に{f|迷|まよ}ったら、あちこち{f|歩|ある}き{f|回|まわ}らないで、その{f|場|ば}で{f|待|ま}つ<b>ことだ</b>。
If you get lost in the mountains, don't wander about - stay where you are.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|面接|めんせつ}が{f|不安|ふあん}なら、{f|前|まえ}の{f|日|ひ}に{f|一度|いちど}{f|声|こえ}に{f|出|だ}して{f|練習|れんしゅう}してみる<b>ことです</b>。
If you're nervous about the interview, run through it out loud the day before.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|嫌|いや}なことがあった{f|日|ひ}は、{f|無理|むり}に{f|考|かんが}えないで{f|早|はや}く{f|寝|ね}てしまう<b>ことだ</b>。
On a day when something horrible has happened, don't force yourself to think it through - just go to bed early.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The speaker of a `ことだ` sentence has taken a position: *I know the answer to
this.* That is fine coming from a coach, a teacher, a doctor, a parent, or a
friend who has been through the same thing, and it is the standard voice of advice
columns and self-help writing. It is much less comfortable between strangers, and
it is not appropriate aimed upwards - telling your section chief
<code>{f|休|やす}むことです</code> puts you in the role of the person who knows
better.

`ことです` is polite in form, but politeness of form does not remove the stance.
If you want to advise a senior colleague, leave this pattern alone and use
<code>お{f|休|やす}みになってはいかがでしょうか</code> or
<code>ほうがよろしいかと{f|思|おも}います</code>.

The advice is **general**, not a request about one occasion. It answers "what
should I do about X?" rather than "will you do X for me?" So
<code>{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けることだ</code> is not a way to ask someone to open a
window; it is what you say when the room's stuffiness is a recurring problem.

You cannot use it about **yourself**. ❌<code>{f|私|わたし}は{f|早|はや}く{f|寝|ね}ることです</code>
is not a plan; for that you want `つもりだ` or `ようにしている`.

<div class="note">

**Note** - a different `ことだ` turns up in exclamations:
<code>なんと{f|静|しず}かなことだろう</code>, <code>{f|困|こま}ったことだ</code>.
That one takes adjectives and な-adjectives, sits at the end of a written or
old-fashioned sentence, and expresses feeling rather than advice. Context separates
them easily - advice always has an action verb in front.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～ことだ vs ～ほうがいい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ほうがいい` **compares**: this option beats that one.
`ことだ` **prescribes**: this is what solves it, and no comparison is offered.

</div>

<pre>
{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}んだ<b>ほうがいいですよ</b>。
You'd be better off taking something for it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|熱|ねつ}が{f|下|さ}がらないなら、{f|病院|びょういん}へ{f|行|い}く<b>ことです</b>。
If the fever isn't coming down, go to a doctor.
</pre>

`ほうがいい` leaves the decision with the listener and is the gentler everyday
choice - it is what you say to a friend at their desk. `ことだ` closes the
question. That is why it fits the second sentence, where the situation has already
gone past the point of weighing things up.

### ～ことだ vs ～べきだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ことだ` is **practical**: here is the route to the goal *you*
said you wanted. `べきだ` is **moral**: here is what is right, whether you want it
or not.

</div>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}に{f|受|う}かりたいなら、{f|過去|かこ}{f|問|もん}を{f|解|と}く<b>ことだ</b>。
If you want to pass the exam, work through past papers.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|約束|やくそく}した{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|最後|さいご}までやる<b>べきだ</b>。
Having promised, you ought to see it through.
</pre>

The first is conditional on your goal: no goal, no advice, and you are free to
drop the exam. The second holds regardless of what you want - that is what makes
it a judgement. In practice `ことだ` follows <code>～たいなら</code> and `べきだ`
follows <code>～のだから</code>.

### ～ことだ vs ～ものだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ことだ` advises **this person about this problem**. `ものだ`
states a **general truth or social norm** that applies to everyone.

</div>

<pre>
{f|先輩|せんぱい}に{f|会|あ}ったら、{f|自分|じぶん}から{f|挨拶|あいさつ}する<b>ものだ</b>。
When you meet someone senior, you greet them first - that's how it's done.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|職場|しょくば}になじみたいなら、{f|自分|じぶん}から{f|挨拶|あいさつ}する<b>ことだ</b>。
If you want to settle in at work, be the one who says hello first.
</pre>

The same action, two different arguments. `ものだ` appeals to custom and would be
just as true if you did not care about fitting in; `ことだ` is a piece of tactical
advice tied to the goal in the first clause. `ものだ` therefore sounds
old-fashioned and moralising in a way `ことだ` does not - and can be dropped into a
scolding, which `ことだ` cannot.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|明日|あした}{f|早|はや}く{f|起|お}きる**ことです**。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|明日|あした}{f|早|はや}く{f|起|お}きる<b>つもりです</b>。
  `ことだ` is advice you give someone else. It cannot announce your own plan.

- ❌ すみません、この{f|書類|しょるい}をコピーする**ことです**。 → ✅ すみません、この{f|書類|しょるい}をコピーし<b>ていただけますか</b>。
  A request about one occasion is not advice. `ことだ` prescribes a general
  solution, not a favour.

- ❌ {f|元気|げんき}な**ことです**。 → ✅ {f|元気|げんき}を{f|出|だ}す<b>ことです</b>。
  Advice `ことだ` takes a verb. An adjective has to become an action first.

- ❌ {f|課長|かちょう}、たまには{f|休|やす}む**ことですよ**。 → ✅ {f|課長|かちょう}、たまにはお{f|休|やす}みになってはいかがでしょうか。
  Polite endings do not change the stance. Advising upwards needs a different
  pattern altogether.

- ❌ {f|遅|おく}れる{f|場合|ばあい}は{f|連絡|れんらく}する**ことです**。（as a posted rule） → ✅ {f|遅|おく}れる{f|場合|ばあい}は{f|連絡|れんらく}する<b>こと</b>。
  A notice drops the `です`. With it, the wall appears to be offering friendly
  guidance.

## Practice

**1.** {f|日本語|にほんご}が{f|上達|じょうたつ}したいなら、まちがいをおそれずにたくさん{f|話|はな}す____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

たくさん{f|話|はな}す<b>ことだ</b>。

The first clause names a goal with <code>～たいなら</code>, which is the standard
set-up for `ことだ`. The sentence then hands over the single action that gets you
there, with no weighing of alternatives.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|明日|あした}{f|試験|しけん}なんだから、{f|今夜|こんや}は{f|早|はや}く（{f|寝|ね}たほうがいいよ・{f|寝|ね}ることだ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|早|はや}く<b>{f|寝|ね}たほうがいいよ</b>。

Between friends about tonight in particular, `ほうがいい` is the natural register - 
it compares tonight's options and leaves the choice with them.
<code>{f|寝|ね}ることだ</code> would sound like a coach issuing instructions, which
is possible but noticeably heavier.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If you want to stop being nervous, the thing to do is practise in front of people."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

きんちょうしなくなりたいなら、{f|人|ひと}の{f|前|まえ}で{f|練習|れんしゅう}する<b>ことです</b>。

<code>～なりたいなら</code> sets up the goal and `ことだ` delivers the remedy. The
polite <code>ことです</code> keeps the form courteous, though the stance - "I know
the answer" - is the same either way.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|体|からだ}のためにたばこをやめることです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|体|からだ}のためにも、たばこは<b>おやめになったほうがよろしいのではないでしょうか</b>。

The grammar of the original is fine; the problem is who it is aimed at. `ことだ`
places the speaker above the listener, so it does not work upwards no matter how
polite the ending. Advice to a superior needs a hedged
<code>ほうがいい</code> or a question form.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|困|こま}ったときは、すぐに{f|相談|そうだん}することだ。
(b) {f|困|こま}ったときは、すぐに{f|相談|そうだん}すること。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is advice from one person to another. There is a speaker with an opinion
behind it, and the listener could argue back or ignore it.

(b) is the same words as a rule. Without `だ` it becomes the notice form - 
something printed in a staff handbook, addressed to nobody in particular, with no
speaker to argue with. The single syllable changes the sentence from a
conversation into a regulation.

</details>
