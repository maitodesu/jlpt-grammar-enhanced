# 数量 + は - at least

**Meaning** · at least this much; this much as a minimum (reading: すうりょう + は)

Put `は` on a number and the number stops being a measurement and becomes a
**floor**. <code>{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}かかります</code> reports a journey time;
<code>{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}はかかります</code> says "reckon on thirty minutes - 
it will not be less, and it may well be more." It is the same contrastive `は` you
already know, doing arithmetic.

## Formation

Any expression of amount can take this `は` - a number plus counter, a money
amount, a period of time, or a vague quantity word. The predicate is normally
**positive**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Number + counter | Quantity + は + predicate | {f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん} → {f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}はかかる |
| Money | Amount + は + する | {f|五千円|ごせんえん} → {f|五千円|ごせんえん}はする |
| Number of people | Counter + は + いる／{f|来|く}る | {f|十人|じゅうにん} → {f|十人|じゅうにん}はいる |
| Object of a verb | Quantity + は + Vた（を drops） | {f|三回|さんかい} → {f|三回|さんかい}は{f|読|よ}んだ |
| Vague quantity | {f|半分|はんぶん}／ある{f|程度|ていど} + は | {f|半分|はんぶん} → {f|半分|はんぶん}は{f|終|お}わった |
| With an approximator | Quantity + くらい／ぐらい + は | {f|一時間|いちじかん}ぐらいはかかる |
| With an estimate marker | Quantity + は + だろう／はずだ／{f|必要|ひつよう}だ | {f|二日|ふつか}はかかるだろう |
| Negative predicate - **different meaning** | Quantity + は + negative | {f|全部|ぜんぶ}は{f|覚|おぼ}えていない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the `は` goes on the **quantity**, not on the noun.
<code>{f|水|みず}を{f|二本|にほん}は{f|買|か}っておこう</code>, not
❌<code>{f|水|みず}は{f|二本|にほん}{f|買|か}っておこう</code>, which would be
contrasting water with something else instead of setting a minimum.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - an approximator and this `は` can stack, and the order is fixed:
<code>ぐらいは</code>, never ❌<code>はぐらい</code>. The combination softens the
floor to "about that much at the very least."

</div>

<pre>

</pre>

この{f|辞書|じしょ}なら{f|五千円|ごせんえん}<b>は</b>するでしょう。
A dictionary like this must cost five thousand yen at least.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|店|みせ}の{f|前|まえ}にはいつも{f|十人|じゅうにん}<b>は</b>{f|並|なら}んでいる。
There are always at least ten people queuing outside that shop.
</pre>

<pre>

I've read that novel at least three times.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

I'd say a hundred people had gathered at the venue, though I don't know the exact number.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a plain contrastive `は` and it is register-neutral: it appears in casual
speech, in polite speech and in writing without changing shape. In conversation the
**quantity takes the stress** - <code>さんじゅっぷんは かかるよ</code> - and that
stress is doing much of the work, so listen for it.

What the pattern conveys is **confidence about the low end**. The speaker is not
claiming precision; they are claiming that the true figure will not fall below the
one given. That is why it pairs so naturally with hedges:
<code>だろう</code>, <code>でしょう</code>, <code>と{f|思|おも}う</code>,
<code>はずだ</code>, <code>そうです</code>. Combining a floor with a hedge is not a
contradiction - "at least this much, probably more, I can't say exactly."

The pattern is common in three everyday situations: estimating **time and cost**
(<code>{f|一時間|いちじかん}はかかる</code>,
<code>{f|一万円|いちまんえん}はする</code>), asserting **effort you have already put
in** (<code>{f|三回|さんかい}は{f|確認|かくにん}した</code> - "I checked it three times,
thank you very much"), and setting a **requirement** for someone else
(<code>{f|三日|みっか}は{f|休|やす}んだほうがいい</code>). The second of these often
carries a defensive edge, because the contrastive `は` invites the comparison "and
that's more than you're giving me credit for."

Beside it stands the adverb <code>{f|少|すく}なくとも</code>, which says "at least"
explicitly. It is a shade more formal and more logical in tone, it does not replace
the `は`, and the two frequently appear together in the same sentence.

## Contrast

### {f|数量|すうりょう} + は vs {f|数量|すうりょう} + も

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `は` sets a **floor**: at least this much. `も` expresses
**surprise at the size**: as much as this. `は` is sober; `も` is impressed or
appalled.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|二時間|にじかん}<b>は</b>かかります。
The meeting will take two hours at least.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|二時間|にじかん}<b>も</b>かかりました。
The meeting dragged on for two whole hours.
</pre>

The first is planning; the second is a complaint. Note the tense that comes with
them: `は` looks forward to an amount not yet known, so it sits with non-past
and with hedges, while `も` reacts to an amount already established, so it sits
comfortably in the past. Using `も` for a forecast - 
❌<code>{f|二時間|にじかん}もかかります</code> as a neutral estimate - makes you sound
outraged by your own schedule.

### {f|数量|すうりょう} + は vs {f|少|すく}なくとも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|少|すく}なくとも</code> is an adverb and states the
minimum in words; the `は` marks it grammatically. Use `は` in speech, add
<code>{f|少|すく}なくとも</code> when you want the floor spelled out.

</div>

<pre>
{f|少|すく}なくとも{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}<b>は</b>{f|待|ま}たされた。
I was kept waiting at least half an hour.
</pre>

<code>{f|少|すく}なくとも</code> can also modify things that are not quantities at all
 - <code>{f|少|すく}なくとも{f|連絡|れんらく}くらいはしてほしい</code>, "I'd at least like
to be told" - where the number-plus-`は` pattern has nothing to attach to. In
return, `は` is the one that works when no adverb is wanted, which in ordinary
conversation is most of the time.

### {f|数量|すうりょう} + は with a positive predicate vs with a negative one

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same shape does two opposite jobs. **Positive** predicate:
at least this much. **Negative** predicate: **not all** of this much - partial
negation.

</div>

<pre>
{f|漢字|かんじ}を{f|百|ひゃく}<b>は</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えた。
I've learned a hundred kanji at least.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|漢字|かんじ}を{f|全部|ぜんぶ}<b>は</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えていない。
I haven't learned all the kanji. (I have learned some)
</pre>

This is not two grammars; it is one `は` marking a contrast, with the negative
scoping over the quantity rather than over the verb. Still, the practical effect is
that a careless negative reverses your meaning. If you want to say "it takes at
least thirty minutes", the verb must be positive:
<code>{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}はかかります</code>.
<code>{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}はかかりません</code> tells the listener the opposite - 
it will not take a full thirty minutes.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|一時間|いちじかん}<b>も</b>かかると{f|思|おも}うので、{f|早|はや}めに{f|出|で}ましょう。 → ✅ {f|一時間|いちじかん}<b>は</b>かかると{f|思|おも}うので、{f|早|はや}めに{f|出|で}ましょう。
  For a forward-looking minimum you need `は`. `も` would make the hour sound
  outrageous, which is odd in a sentence that is calmly planning around it.

- ❌ {f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}<b>は</b>かかりません。（meaning "it takes at least thirty minutes"） → ✅ {f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}<b>は</b>かかります。
  With a negative predicate the pattern flips to partial negation: "it doesn't take
  a full thirty minutes."

- ❌ {f|一時間|いちじかん}<b>はぐらい</b>{f|待|ま}った。 → ✅ {f|一時間|いちじかん}<b>ぐらいは</b>{f|待|ま}った。
  The approximator goes next to the number and `は` goes outside it. The order is
  fixed.

- ❌ {f|水|みず}<b>は</b>{f|二本|にほん}{f|買|か}っておきます。（meaning "I'll buy at least two bottles"） → ✅ {f|水|みず}を{f|二本|にほん}<b>は</b>{f|買|か}っておきます。
  The `は` has to land on the quantity. On the noun it contrasts water with the
  other shopping instead.

- ❌ {f|少|すく}なくとも{f|三日|みっか}<b>も</b>{f|必要|ひつよう}です。 → ✅ {f|少|すく}なくとも{f|三日|みっか}<b>は</b>{f|必要|ひつよう}です。
  <code>{f|少|すく}なくとも</code> already announces a minimum, so the particle that
  goes with it is `は`. `も` pulls in the opposite direction.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|送|おく}るのに、{f|二千円|にせんえん}____かかりますよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|二千円|にせんえん}<b>は</b>かかりますよ。

The speaker is warning about a cost that has not been paid yet, so the figure is a
floor rather than a measurement. <code>{f|二千円|にせんえん}も</code> would sound like
shock at the price, and bare <code>{f|二千円|にせんえん}かかります</code> would claim a
precision the speaker does not have.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|昨日|きのう}のパーティーには{f|五十人|ごじゅうにん}（は・も）{f|来|き}て、{f|席|せき}が{f|足|た}りなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|五十人|ごじゅうにん}<b>も</b>{f|来|き}て、{f|席|せき}が{f|足|た}りなかった。

The party is over and the number is known; the sentence is reacting to how large it
was, which is `も`. `は` would set a minimum for something already counted, which
makes no sense once the guests have gone home.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "You'll need at least two weeks to get used to this job."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|仕事|しごと}に{f|慣|な}れるには、{f|二週間|にしゅうかん}<b>は</b>かかります。

Putting `は` on <code>{f|二週間|にしゅうかん}</code> makes it the minimum.
<code>{f|少|すく}なくとも{f|二週間|にしゅうかん}は{f|必要|ひつよう}です</code> is equally
good and states the floor twice over, which is common in careful speech.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: レポートは{f|全部|ぜんぶ}は{f|書|か}きました。あと{f|少|すこ}しで{f|終|お}わります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ レポートは{f|半分|はんぶん}<b>は</b>{f|書|か}きました。あと{f|少|すこ}しで{f|終|お}わります。

<code>{f|全部|ぜんぶ}は</code> with a positive verb clashes with the second sentence:
if you have written all of it, nothing is left. A quantity plus `は` claims a floor,
so the quantity has to be one you have genuinely passed - 
<code>{f|半分|はんぶん}は</code>, <code>{f|八割|はちわり}は</code>, and so on.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|三時間|さんじかん}<b>は</b>{f|寝|ね}た。
(b) {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|三時間|さんじかん}<b>しか</b>{f|寝|ね}ていない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) treats three hours as a floor and, by implication, as enough to get by on:
"I got a good three hours." The speaker is arguing that the amount was not as bad
as it might sound.

(b) treats the same three hours as a shortfall: "three hours and no more." The
`しか` frame needs its negative verb and carries an unmistakable complaint. The
number is identical in both sentences - only the particle decides whether it counts
as sufficient or as scandalous.

</details>
