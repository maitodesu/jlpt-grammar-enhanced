# ～において / ～における

**Meaning** · in; at; on - the formal written stand-in for で, naming the place, time, situation or field in which something holds（において・における）

`において` sets the **stage**. It tells you where something happens, when it
happens, or within what field or respect a statement is true. Everything it does,
the particle `で` also does - but `で` is the everyday word and `において` is the
one that appears in announcements, papers, contracts and ceremonies.

`における` is the same word in noun-modifying dress: <code>{f|会場|かいじょう}において{f|行|おこな}う</code>
("carry out at the venue") but <code>{f|会場|かいじょう}における{f|注意|ちゅうい}{f|事項|じこう}</code>
("points to note at the venue").

## Formation

Attaches to a bare noun. The `に` is part of the pattern and is not replaceable.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (place), modifying a verb | Noun + において | {f|本社|ほんしゃ} → {f|本社|ほんしゃ}において{f|行|おこな}う |
| Noun (time or era) | Noun + において | {f|明治|めいじ}{f|時代|じだい} → {f|明治|めいじ}{f|時代|じだい}において |
| Noun (field or respect) | Noun + において | {f|技術|ぎじゅつ}{f|面|めん} → {f|技術|ぎじゅつ}{f|面|めん}において |
| Noun, modifying a noun | Noun + における + Noun | {f|職場|しょくば} → {f|職場|しょくば}における{f|関係|かんけい} |
| Noun, as topic | Noun + においては | {f|日本|にほん} → {f|日本|にほん}においては |
| Noun, as addition | Noun + においても | {f|海外|かいがい} → {f|海外|かいがい}においても |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>においての</code> exists but is uncommon and slightly clumsy. Before a
noun, reach for `における`. Before a verb or an adjective, `において`.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|本社|ほんしゃ}<b>において</b>{f|行|おこな}われます。
The meeting will be held at head office.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|点|てん}<b>において</b>、{f|両者|りょうしゃ}の{f|意見|いけん}は{f|一致|いっち}している。
On this point, the two sides are in agreement.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大会|たいかい}は{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|七日|なのか}、{f|市民|しみん}ホール<b>において</b>{f|開催|かいさい}いたします。
The tournament will be held on the seventh of next month at the civic hall.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|江戸|えど}{f|時代|じだい}<b>における</b>{f|町人|ちょうにん}の{f|暮|く}らしを{f|調|しら}べています。
I'm researching the lives of townspeople in the Edo period.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|医療|いりょう}の{f|分野|ぶんや}<b>における</b>AIの{f|利用|りよう}が{f|急速|きゅうそく}に{f|広|ひろ}がっている。
The use of AI in the field of medicine is spreading rapidly.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|面接|めんせつ}<b>においては</b>、{f|話|はな}す{f|内容|ないよう}だけでなく{f|話|はな}し{f|方|かた}も{f|見|み}られている。
In an interview it is not only what you say but how you say it that is being judged.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|我|わ}が{f|国|くに}<b>においても</b>、{f|人口|じんこう}の{f|減少|げんしょう}は{f|深刻|しんこく}な{f|問題|もんだい}となりつつある。
In our country too, the falling population is becoming a serious problem.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|価格|かかく}<b>においては</b>{f|他社|たしゃ}に{f|負|ま}けているが、{f|品質|ひんしつ}<b>においては</b>{f|自信|じしん}がある。
We lose to our competitors on price, but where quality is concerned we are confident.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This pattern is **formal and written**, with essentially no casual life at all.
Notices, minutes, academic papers, legal text, ceremonial speech and the polished
register of the news are its habitat. Saying
❌<code>コンビニにおいてパンを{f|買|か}った</code> is not a grammar error - it is a
tonal disaster, roughly like writing "I did procure bread at the convenience store."

Two practical consequences.

First, **`において` covers only some of what `で` covers**. `で` marks place, but
also means ("with a knife"), cause ("because of a cold"), material, and limit
("within a week"). `において` inherits only the **place, time, situation and field**
senses. There is no ❌<code>ナイフにおいて{f|切|き}る</code>.

Second, the "field or respect" sense is where `において` earns its keep, because
`で` there can be ambiguous. <code>この{f|点|てん}において</code> - "in this respect" - 
is crisper in formal prose than <code>この{f|点|てん}で</code>, which could momentarily
be read as a location.

`において` also carries a faint ceremonial weight when used of an event. A wedding
invitation says <code>ホテルの{f|大|だい}ホールにおいて</code>; a text to a friend says
<code>ホテルで</code>. The formality is doing real work in the first case - it signals
that this is an occasion.

One last thing worth noticing: `における` is a fixture of **titles**.
<code>{f|現代|げんだい}{f|社会|しゃかい}における{f|家族|かぞく}の{f|役割|やくわり}</code> is exactly
the shape of an essay title, a chapter heading, a conference paper. If you are
writing academic Japanese, this is a phrase you will use constantly.

## Contrast

### ～において vs ～で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register, narrower coverage. `において`
can replace the **place / time / situation / field** uses of `で` and nothing else,
and only in formal writing or formal speech.

</div>

<pre>
{f|試合|しあい}は{f|大阪|おおさか}<b>で</b>{f|行|おこな}われた。
The match was held in Osaka.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試合|しあい}は{f|大阪|おおさか}<b>において</b>{f|行|おこな}われた。
The match was held in Osaka. (announcement register)
</pre>

Identical in meaning; the second belongs in a written report. Now watch it fail:

<pre>
{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|事故|じこ}<b>で</b>{f|止|と}まっている。
The trains are stopped because of an accident.
</pre>

❌<code>{f|事故|じこ}において{f|止|と}まっている</code> is ungrammatical, because this
`で` marks a **cause**, and `において` has no cause sense. The same goes for
<code>バスで{f|行|い}く</code> (means) and <code>{f|木|き}で{f|作|つく}る</code>
(material) - neither converts.

### ～における + Noun vs ～の + Noun

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `の` says the two nouns are connected somehow;
`における` says specifically **"within / inside / in the context of"**. When the
relationship needs pinning down, `における` does it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|日本|にほん}<b>の</b>{f|教育|きょういく}
Japanese education
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日本|にほん}<b>における</b>{f|外国語|がいこくご}{f|教育|きょういく}
foreign-language education in Japan
</pre>

The first could mean education that is Japanese in style, education owned by Japan,
education about Japan - `の` does not say. The second is unambiguous: it is
education happening **inside** Japan, seen as a setting. This precision is why
academic titles reach for `における` rather than `の`, and why replacing it with `の`
in an essay title makes the title vaguer even though it stays grammatical.

Between the two sits `での`, which is the neutral-register version of the same idea:
<code>{f|日本|にほん}での{f|生活|せいかつ}</code>, "life in Japan". Use `での` when you
are speaking, `における` when you are writing formally.

### ～において vs ～について

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `において` is the **setting**; `について` is the **subject
matter**. "Discussed at the meeting" is `において`; "discussed about the meeting" is
`について`.

</div>

Both are formal-ish, both hang off `に`, and English "regarding" is offered as a
translation of both, which is how learners end up swapping them.

<pre>
その{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|先日|せんじつ}の{f|会議|かいぎ}<b>において</b>{f|議論|ぎろん}された。
That issue was debated at the meeting the other day.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先日|せんじつ}の{f|会議|かいぎ}<b>について</b>{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}を{f|書|か}いた。
I wrote a report about the meeting the other day.
</pre>

In the first, the meeting is the setting the debating happened in. In the second, the
meeting is what the report is on. Substituting one for the other produces a
sentence that is grammatical and simply says something else - which is the most
dangerous kind of mistake.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ はさみにおいて{f|紙|かみ}を{f|切|き}った。 → ✅ はさみ<b>で</b>{f|紙|かみ}を{f|切|き}った。
  `において` inherits only the place, time, situation and field senses of `で`. It
  never marks an instrument.

- ❌ {f|台風|たいふう}において{f|学校|がっこう}が{f|休|やす}みになった。 → ✅ {f|台風|たいふう}<b>で</b>{f|学校|がっこう}が{f|休|やす}みになった。
  This `で` is a cause, and there is no cause sense in `において`. If you want the
  formal register, use <code>{f|台風|たいふう}のため</code>.

- ❌ {f|職場|しょくば}において{f|問題|もんだい}が{f|増|ふ}えている。（intending "problems in the workplace") → ✅ {f|職場|しょくば}<b>における</b>{f|問題|もんだい}が{f|増|ふ}えている。
  Before a noun you need `における`. As written, the sentence says the increasing is
  happening at the workplace, which is not quite the same claim.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}、{f|駅前|えきまえ}のカフェにおいて{f|友|とも}だちに{f|会|あ}った。 → ✅ {f|駅前|えきまえ}のカフェ<b>で</b>{f|友|とも}だちに{f|会|あ}った。
  Grammatically fine, tonally absurd. A private, everyday event does not take
  announcement vocabulary.

## Practice

**1.** 20{f|世紀|せいき}____、{f|人々|ひとびと}の{f|暮|く}らしは{f|大|おお}きく{f|変|か}わった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

20{f|世紀|せいき}<b>において</b>、{f|人々|ひとびと}の{f|暮|く}らしは{f|大|おお}きく{f|変|か}わった。

A century is a **time setting**, which is one of the four senses `において` inherits
from `で`. The sentence is written prose, so the formal form fits;
<code>20{f|世紀|せいき}に</code> would also be possible and plainer.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}（において・について）{f|他社|たしゃ}より{f|進|すす}んでいる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}<b>において</b>{f|他社|たしゃ}より{f|進|すす}んでいる。

The sentence names the **respect** in which the company is ahead, which is the
field sense of `において`. `について` would make technology the subject matter of
some statement, leaving <code>{f|進|すす}んでいる</code> with nothing to be true *of*.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, as an essay title: "The role of the family in modern society."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|現代|げんだい}{f|社会|しゃかい}<b>における</b>{f|家族|かぞく}の{f|役割|やくわり}

Before a noun, `において` becomes `における`. This is the standard shape of an
academic title in Japanese. <code>{f|現代|げんだい}{f|社会|しゃかい}での{f|家族|かぞく}の{f|役割|やくわり}</code>
means the same but is too plain for a title, and
<code>{f|現代|げんだい}{f|社会|しゃかい}の{f|家族|かぞく}の{f|役割|やくわり}</code> stacks two `の`
and blurs the relationship.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|大雨|おおあめ}において{f|試合|しあい}が{f|中止|ちゅうし}になり、{f|来週|らいしゅう}、{f|同|おな}じ{f|球場|きゅうじょう}において{f|行|おこな}われることになった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|大雨|おおあめ}<b>のため</b>{f|試合|しあい}が{f|中止|ちゅうし}になり、{f|来週|らいしゅう}、{f|同|おな}じ{f|球場|きゅうじょう}において{f|行|おこな}われることになった。

The second `において` is correct - a stadium is a place. The first is not: heavy
rain is the **cause** of the cancellation, and `において` has no cause sense. In this
formal register <code>のため</code> is the natural repair; in speech you would say
<code>{f|大雨|おおあめ}で</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会場|かいじょう}<b>で</b>のアンケート
(b) {f|会場|かいじょう}<b>における</b>アンケート

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both mean "a survey at the venue", and neither is wrong. The difference is
register and, faintly, framing.

(a) is ordinary Japanese - spoken, or written in an email to colleagues. `での` is
just `で` fitted to modify a noun.

(b) is document Japanese. It is what would be printed on the cover of the report,
and it frames the venue as a **defined setting** whose conditions matter, rather
than merely as the spot where the clipboards were handed out. Choosing (b) in
conversation would sound as though you were quoting the report out loud.

</details>
