# ～た結果・～の結果

**Meaning** · as a result of ～ - the outcome of something that was deliberately worked through

<code>～た{f|結果|けっか}</code> packs a process and its outcome into a single sentence.
The clause in front names something that was carried out - a discussion, an
investigation, repeated attempts - and what follows is what came of it. With a noun,
<code>～の{f|結果|けっか}</code> does the same job in fewer syllables. Both are the
language of reports and decisions.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (past) | た-form + {f|結果|けっか} | {f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}った → {f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}った{f|結果|けっか} |
| Noun of activity | Noun + の + {f|結果|けっか} | {f|検査|けんさ} → {f|検査|けんさ}の{f|結果|けっか} |
| Very formal writing | ます-form (past) + {f|結果|けっか} | {f|検討|けんとう}いたしました{f|結果|けっか} |
| Sentence-final | ～のは～した{f|結果|けっか}だ | {f|残|のこ}ったのは{f|努力|どりょく}した{f|結果|けっか}です |
| Ordinary noun (not this pattern) | {f|結果|けっか} + を／が／は | {f|結果|けっか}が{f|出|で}るまで{f|待|ま}つ |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the verb must be **past tense** and must name something somebody
*did*: {f|調|しら}べる, {f|相談|そうだん}する, {f|議論|ぎろん}する, {f|続|つづ}ける,
{f|繰|く}り{f|返|かえ}す. A momentary accident that simply happened to you does not
take this pattern.

</div>

The nouns that work with <code>の{f|結果|けっか}</code> are the matching ones - 
{f|調査|ちょうさ}, {f|検査|けんさ}, {f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}い, {f|投票|とうひょう},
{f|努力|どりょく}, {f|選考|せんこう} - all of them names of activities rather than of
things.

After talking it over we ended up changing the venue.
</pre>

<pre>

The tests came back showing nothing wrong.
</pre>

Working through the sums again and again turned up one error in the figures.
</pre>

</pre>

<pre>
{f|家族|かぞく}で{f|相談|そうだん}した<b>{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|祖父|そふ}の{f|家|いえ}は{f|売|う}らないことに{f|決|き}めました。
Having talked it through as a family, we've decided not to sell my grandfather's house.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|無理|むり}な{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|続|つづ}けた<b>{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|肩|かた}を{f|痛|いた}めてしまった。

</pre>

Following our internal review, we have decided to pass on the opportunity this time.

Interviews with farmers around the country and a comparison of ten years of records showed that the harvest is creeping steadily earlier.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This belongs to written and formal-spoken Japanese: minutes, reports, medical
results, business email, the news. It is not stiff enough to be comic in
conversation - a colleague may well say
{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}った{f|結果|けっか}こうなりました - but among friends the ordinary
choice is `から`, `ので` or simply `て`.

Its most conspicuous habitat is the **polite refusal**. The frame
{f|検討|けんとう}いたしました{f|結果|けっか}、～{f|見送|みおく}らせていただきます is close to a fixed
formula in business Japanese, and the `結果` is doing real work in it: by presenting
the answer as the outcome of a process, the writer makes the refusal sound
considered rather than personal. Note the shape there - a ます-form in front of
`結果`, which is otherwise unusual and belongs to this very formal register only.

Two things to keep in view. `結果` is evaluatively neutral, so what follows can be
good, bad or merely numerical; that is why it is safe in writing where blame would
be awkward. And the pattern insists on a **process**: 
{f|転|ころ}んだ{f|結果|けっか}、けがをした is odd, because falling over is not something
you conduct. Where the cause is an accident or a state of affairs, the neutral
`ため` or the blaming `せいで` fit better.

Finally, <code>{f|検査|けんさ}の{f|結果|けっか}、～</code> can be read two ways: as this
connective pattern, or as the plain noun phrase "the results of the test" used
adverbially. Nothing turns on the distinction - both readings deliver the same
meaning - but it explains why `結果` also turns up with `を`, `が` and `は` in
sentences that have nothing to do with this pattern.

## Contrast

### ～た{f|結果|けっか} vs その{f|結果|けっか}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same idea, different architecture.
<code>～た{f|結果|けっか}</code> tucks the process into a subordinate clause;
<code>その{f|結果|けっか}</code> gives it a sentence of its own and then comments on it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|条件|じょうけん}を{f|見直|みなお}した<b>{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|契約|けいやく}がまとまりました。
Revising the terms brought the contract together.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|条件|じょうけん}を{f|何度|なんど}も{f|見直|みなお}しました。<b>その{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|契約|けいやく}がまとまりました。
We revised the terms several times over. As a result, the contract came together.
</pre>

Use the subordinate version when the process is background and the outcome is the
news. Use the two-sentence version when the process deserves description in its own
right - which is why it is the one that appears after a long account of what was
tried.

### ～た{f|結果|けっか} vs ～たあとで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たあとで` is pure sequence: this happened, then that.
<code>た{f|結果|けっか}</code> claims the second thing **came out of** the first.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わった<b>あとで</b>、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|配|くば}りました。
I handed out the documents after the meeting finished.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|長|なが}く{f|議論|ぎろん}した<b>{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|作|つく}り{f|直|なお}すことになりました。
A long discussion ended with our having to redo the documents.
</pre>

Handing out papers is not produced by the meeting ending; it merely follows it. If
you can insert "and then" and lose nothing, you want `あとで`. If the second clause
answers "what came of it?", you want `結果`.

### ～た{f|結果|けっか} vs ～たら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both can report what you found out by doing something. `たら` is
the spoken discovery - "I did X, and lo". <code>た{f|結果|けっか}</code> is its written
counterpart and frames the finding as the product of a procedure.

</div>

<pre>
{f|調|しら}べて<b>みたら</b>、{f|去年|きょねん}も{f|同|おな}じことが{f|起|お}きていた。
I looked into it, and it turns out the same thing happened last year too.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|過去|かこ}の{f|記録|きろく}を{f|調|しら}べた<b>{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|去年|きょねん}にも{f|同|おな}じ{f|事例|じれい}が{f|確認|かくにん}された。
An examination of past records confirmed a comparable case last year as well.
</pre>

`たら` carries surprise and belongs to speech; `結果` carries none and belongs to the
report that gets written afterwards. Notice how the whole sentence changes register
with it - {f|事例|じれい} and {f|確認|かくにん}された arrive in its company.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|転|ころ}んだ<b>{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|足|あし}をけがしました。 → ✅ {f|転|ころ}んで{f|足|あし}をけがしました。
  Falling over is an accident, not a procedure. `結果` needs something that was
  carried out on purpose or sustained over time.

- ❌ {f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}う<b>{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|決|き}めましょう。 → ✅ {f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}った<b>{f|結果|けっか}</b>、こうなりました。
  The verb has to be in the past. An outcome cannot precede the process that
  produced it.

- ❌ {f|検討|けんとう}した{f|結果|けっか}で、{f|中止|ちゅうし}と{f|決|き}まりました。 → ✅ {f|検討|けんとう}した{f|結果|けっか}、{f|中止|ちゅうし}と{f|決|き}まりました。
  No particle after `結果` in this pattern - a comma does the joining.

- ❌ {f|調査|ちょうさ}した{f|結果|けっか}、{f|来週|らいしゅう}までに{f|報告|ほうこく}してください。 → ✅ {f|調査|ちょうさ}が{f|終|お}わりました<b>ので</b>、{f|来週|らいしゅう}までに{f|報告|ほうこく}してください。
  An instruction is not an outcome. Requests take `ので`.

- ❌ {f|努力|どりょく}した{f|結果|けっか}の<b>おかげで</b>{f|合格|ごうかく}しました。 → ✅ {f|努力|どりょく}した<b>{f|結果|けっか}</b>{f|合格|ごうかく}しました。 ／ ✅ {f|努力|どりょく}の<b>おかげで</b>{f|合格|ごうかく}しました。
  Stacking the two is redundant, and it mixes a neutral report with a word of
  thanks. Pick one.

## Practice

**1.** {f|三日|みっか}かけて{f|全員|ぜんいん}に{f|聞|き}き{f|取|と}りを{f|行|おこな}った____、{f|原因|げんいん}が{f|手順書|てじゅんしょ}の{f|古|ふる}さにあると{f|分|わ}かった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

…{f|行|おこな}った<b>{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|原因|げんいん}が{f|手順書|てじゅんしょ}の{f|古|ふる}さにあると{f|分|わ}かった。

Three days of interviews is a deliberate, sustained procedure, and the second clause
is what that procedure produced. The past-tense verb in front is obligatory.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べた（{f|結果|けっか}・あとで）、{f|散歩|さんぽ}に{f|出|で}ました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昼|ひる}ごはんを{f|食|た}べた<b>あとで</b>、{f|散歩|さんぽ}に{f|出|で}ました。

The walk merely follows lunch; lunch did not produce it. `結果` would claim that
eating was a process undertaken in order to arrive at some outcome, which turns an
ordinary afternoon into an experiment.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, in the register of a company email: "After careful consideration, we have decided to postpone the project."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|慎重|しんちょう}に{f|検討|けんとう}いたしました<b>{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|本件|ほんけん}は{f|延期|えんき}させていただくことになりました。

This is the stock business frame. The ます-form in front of `結果` is unusual anywhere
else, and the point of the whole construction is to present an unwelcome decision as
the product of a process rather than as somebody's preference.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|急|きゅう}に{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った{f|結果|けっか}、{f|洗濯物|せんたくもの}がぬれてしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|急|きゅう}に{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った<b>せいで</b>、{f|洗濯物|せんたくもの}がぬれてしまった。

A sudden shower is an event that happened to the speaker, not a process they worked
through, so `結果` does not fit. `せいで` grumbles about it; <code>{f|雨|あめ}のため</code>
would state it neutrally.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|一年|いちねん}{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}った<b>{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になりました。
(b) {f|一年|いちねん}{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いました。<b>{f|結局|けっきょく}</b>{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) presents the cancellation as the considered product of a year of discussion. It
is neutral, and it is what the official announcement would say.

(b) uses <code>{f|結局|けっきょく}</code>, which looks back over the year and closes it
with a sigh: all that talking, and this is where we ended up. The facts are
identical; (b) adds the sense that the outcome was disappointing, or at least that
the effort was disproportionate to it.

</details>
