# ～に従って

**Meaning** · (1) in accordance with ～, following ～; (2) as ～ progresses, ～ changes with it

<code>～に{f|従|したが}って</code> is the て-form of the verb <code>{f|従|したが}う</code>, "to
follow, to obey", and both of its meanings come straight out of that. Follow a set
of instructions and you get sense (1). Let one change follow another and you get
sense (2), where it is close to <code>～につれて</code>. Written as a single
sentence-opening word, したがって is a third thing entirely: "therefore".

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun: rule, order, model | Noun + に{f|従|したが}って | {f|説明書|せつめいしょ} → {f|説明書|せつめいしょ}に{f|従|したが}って |
| Noun naming a process | Noun + に{f|従|したが}って | {f|時代|じだい}の{f|変化|へんか} → {f|時代|じだい}の{f|変化|へんか}に{f|従|したが}って |
| Verb (change of state) | V(dictionary) + に{f|従|したが}って | {f|進|すす}む → {f|進|すす}むに{f|従|したが}って |
| Formal writing | ～に{f|従|したが}い | {f|規則|きそく} → {f|規則|きそく}に{f|従|したが}い |
| Modifying a noun | ～に{f|従|したが}った + Noun | {f|指示|しじ} → {f|指示|しじ}に{f|従|したが}った{f|行動|こうどう} |
| As an ordinary verb | Noun + に{f|従|したが}う | ルールに{f|従|したが}う |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|従|したが}う</code> is an う-verb ({f|五段|ごだん} / godan / Group 1), so
its て-form is <code>{f|従|したが}って</code> with a small つ. ❌<code>{f|従|したが}て</code>
and ❌<code>{f|従|したが}いて</code> are both wrong. As with
<code>～につれて</code>, the clause before it stays **non-past**.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|矢印|やじるし}<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>{f|進|すす}んでください。
Please go on, following the arrows.
</pre>

<pre>
ルール<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>ゲームをしましょう。
Let's play the game by the rules.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|医者|いしゃ}の{f|注意|ちゅうい}<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>、{f|塩分|えんぶん}を{f|減|へ}らしています。
I'm cutting down on salt as the doctor told me to.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|年|とし}を{f|取|と}る<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>、{f|夜|よ}{f|更|ふ}かしがつらくなってきた。
As I get older, staying up late has started to take its toll.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}が{f|進|すす}む<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>、{f|昔|むかし}の{f|道具|どうぐ}は{f|見|み}かけなくなった。
As technology moved on, the old tools stopped being a common sight.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|地元|じもと}の{f|習慣|しゅうかん}<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>、{f|靴|くつ}を{f|脱|ぬ}いで{f|部屋|へや}に{f|上|あ}がった。
Following the local custom, I took off my shoes and stepped up into the room.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|法律|ほうりつ}の{f|改正|かいせい}<b>に{f|従|したが}い</b>、{f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|手続|てつづ}きが{f|変|か}わります。
In accordance with the revision of the law, the procedure changes from next month.
</pre>

<pre>
マニュアル<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>{f|操作|そうさ}したはずなのに、{f|機械|きかい}はまったく{f|動|うご}かなかった。
I'm sure I operated it according to the manual, but the machine wouldn't move at all.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a formal expression in both of its senses. In sense (1) it carries the
weight of the verb it comes from: obeying, complying, submitting. Told to a friend
that you cooked <code>レシピに{f|従|したが}って</code>, it sounds oddly solemn - 
conversation prefers <code>レシピどおりに</code> or
<code>レシピを{f|見|み}ながら</code>. Where it belongs is signage, safety
announcements, contracts, court reporting and workplace instructions.

Trimming to <code>に{f|従|したが}い</code> is the written-only variant, one register above the
て-form and common in notices: <code>{f|規則|きそく}に{f|従|したが}い{f|処理|しょり}いたします</code>.

Note who is doing the following. <code>に{f|従|したが}って</code> puts the subject in the
position of complying with something with authority behind it - a rule, an order,
a doctor, a custom. Using it about a mere suggestion overstates the case, and using
it about a person can sound like obedience rather than agreement:
<code>{f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|従|したが}って</code> is "doing as the manager
directs", not "along with the manager".

In sense (2), the parallel-change use, it is drier and more analytical than
<code>～につれて</code>, and turns up mostly in writing that is explaining a trend.
It also has a faint implication that the second change is *driven* by the first
rather than merely accompanying it.

<div class="note">

**Note** - sentence-initially, したがって means "therefore". It is one of the most
formal connectives in the language, at home in academic papers and legal writing:
{f|台風|たいふう}が{f|接近|せっきん}している。したがって、{f|明日|あす}の{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}とする。
In speech you would say だから or ですから.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～に{f|従|したが}って vs ～につれて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for two changes moving together, both work and `に従って` is
the stiffer choice. For following instructions, only `に従って` is possible.

</div>

<pre>
{f|南|みなみ}へ{f|下|くだ}る<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>、{f|畑|はたけ}が{f|広|ひろ}くなっていく。
As you go further south, the fields get wider.
</pre>

Replace `に従って` with `につれて` there and almost nothing changes; the sentence
just sounds a shade less like a geography textbook. Now the half `につれて` cannot
reach:

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}のアドバイス<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>、{f|書|か}き{f|直|なお}してみました。
I rewrote it following my teacher's advice.
</pre>

There is no gradual change here at all - just compliance - so
❌<code>アドバイスにつれて</code> is impossible. Test it in English: if "as" can
become "in accordance with", it is `に従って`.

### ～に{f|従|したが}って vs ～とおりに・～どおりに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `どおりに` means the result **matches the model exactly**.
`に従って` means you **took the instruction as your authority**. Everyday speech
prefers `どおりに`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}の<b>とおりに</b>{f|組|く}み{f|立|た}てたら、ねじが{f|一|ひと}つ{f|余|あま}った。
I assembled it exactly as the instructions said, and had one screw left over.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|方針|ほうしん}<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>、{f|報告|ほうこく}{f|書|しょ}の{f|形式|けいしき}を{f|変|か}えました。
We changed the format of the report in line with company policy.
</pre>

A policy is an authority you obey; a diagram is a model you copy. Note also the
shape of the word: after a noun with `の` it is <code>とおりに</code>
(<code>{f|思|おも}ったとおりに</code>), but glued straight onto a noun it becomes
<code>どおりに</code> - <code>{f|予定|よてい}どおりに</code>,
<code>{f|時間|じかん}どおりに</code>.

### に{f|従|したが}って inside a sentence vs したがって at the head of one

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if the word is attached to a noun or verb, it means
"following". If it is standing alone at the start of a sentence with a comma after
it, it means "therefore".

</div>

<pre>
{f|前例|ぜんれい}<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>{f|判断|はんだん}しました。
We made the decision following precedent.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|前例|ぜんれい}がない。<b>したがって</b>、{f|慎重|しんちょう}に{f|判断|はんだん}する{f|必要|ひつよう}がある。
There is no precedent. Therefore, the decision must be made carefully.
</pre>

Same characters, unrelated jobs. The connective is normally written in kana in
modern Japanese, which helps, but not reliably - and hearing it aloud, only the
position in the sentence tells you which one it is.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|説明書|せつめいしょ}に{f|従|したが}**て** → ✅ {f|説明書|せつめいしょ}に{f|従|したが}**って**
  <code>{f|従|したが}う</code> is an う-verb, so its て-form doubles the consonant:
  <code>{f|従|したが}って</code>.

- ❌ {f|指示|しじ}<b>につれて</b>{f|動|うご}いてください。 → ✅ {f|指示|しじ}<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>{f|動|うご}いてください。
  Instructions are obeyed, not gradually undergone. `につれて` has no "in
  accordance with" meaning at all.

- ❌ {f|年|とし}を{f|取|と}<b>ったに</b>{f|従|したが}って → ✅ {f|年|とし}を{f|取|と}<b>るに</b>{f|従|したが}って
  In the parallel-change sense the verb before it stays in the dictionary form,
  whatever the tense of the sentence.

- ❌ {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|き}ました。<b>{f|従|したが}って</b>{f|中止|ちゅうし}です。（in casual speech） → ✅ {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|き}ました。**だから**{f|中止|ちゅうし}です。
  Not ungrammatical, but したがって in a chat sounds like reading out a legal
  notice. Keep it for writing.

## Practice

**1.** {f|係員|かかりいん}の{f|指示|しじ}____{f|従|したが}って、ゆっくり{f|外|そと}へ{f|出|で}てください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|係員|かかりいん}の{f|指示|しじ}<b>に</b>{f|従|したが}って…

<code>{f|従|したが}う</code> takes its object with `に`, never `を` - you comply
*to* something in Japanese. The same `に` shows up in
<code>{f|規則|きそく}に{f|従|したが}う</code> and
<code>{f|命令|めいれい}に{f|従|したが}う</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|川|かわ}を{f|下|くだ}る（に{f|従|したが}って・のとおりに）、{f|水|みず}の{f|色|いろ}が{f|茶色|ちゃいろ}くなった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|川|かわ}を{f|下|くだ}る<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>、{f|水|みず}の{f|色|いろ}が{f|茶色|ちゃいろ}くなった。

This is the parallel-change sense: two things shifting together along the length of
the river. <code>とおりに</code> would need a model to copy, and a river is not
giving anybody instructions.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Following the doctor's advice, I stopped drinking coffee at night."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|医者|いしゃ}のアドバイス<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>、{f|夜|よる}はコーヒーを{f|飲|の}むのをやめました。

Advice from a doctor counts as an authority you comply with, so
<code>に{f|従|したが}って</code> fits. In casual conversation
<code>{f|医者|いしゃ}に{f|言|い}われたとおりに</code> would be the lighter way to
say the same thing.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|時代|じだい}が{f|変|か}わったに{f|従|したが}い、{f|働|はたら}き{f|方|かた}も{f|変|か}わってきた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|時代|じだい}が{f|変|か}わる<b>に{f|従|したが}い</b>、{f|働|はたら}き{f|方|かた}も{f|変|か}わってきた。

The verb in front has to be non-past. The past sense is already carried by
<code>{f|変|か}わってきた</code> at the end, and doubling it up is what makes the
original ungrammatical.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|先輩|せんぱい}のやり{f|方|かた}<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>{f|作|つく}りました。
(b) {f|先輩|せんぱい}のやり{f|方|かた}の<b>とおりに</b>{f|作|つく}りました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) frames the senior colleague's method as something you were expected to comply
with. It suits a workplace where that method is the rule, and it quietly says you
did not deviate from it.

(b) is about the product matching the model: you copied the method, step for step.
It says nothing about authority, so it is the neutral, more conversational choice - 
and it is what you would say if you simply thought their way was good.

</details>
