# おそらく

**Meaning** · in all likelihood; probably - a considered estimate, stated carefully

`おそらく` is the measured, slightly formal cousin of たぶん. It says the speaker
has weighed what they know and arrived at a probable answer. The weighing is the
point: `おそらく` sounds like a judgement, not a shrug.

## Usage

`おそらく` is an adverb and stands at the head of the sentence or immediately
before the predicate. It never conjugates and takes no particles.

What matters is the **end of the sentence**. `おそらく` almost always pairs with a
marker of conjecture:

- **おそらく～だろう／でしょう** - the default.
  おそらく{f|明日|あした}には{f|届|とど}くでしょう。
- **おそらく～と{f|思|おも}う／と{f|思|おも}われる** - reasoning out loud, or in
  writing.
  おそらく{f|原因|げんいん}は{f|配線|はいせん}だと{f|思|おも}われます。
- **おそらく～はずだ／に{f|違|ちが}いない** - when the estimate is close to a
  conclusion.
  おそらく{f|彼|かれ}はもう{f|家|いえ}に{f|着|つ}いているはずだ。

Ending a plain assertion with nothing at all - ❌おそらく{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る - 
sounds unfinished to a native ear. The hedge at the end is not decoration; it is
what `おそらく` is agreeing with.

In kanji it is {f|恐|おそ}らく. The kanji spelling is common in print; the kana is
common in ordinary writing. It has nothing to do with fear despite the character.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>おそらく</b>{f|彼|かれ}はもう{f|知|し}っているでしょう。
He probably already knows.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|時間|じかん}なら、<b>おそらく</b>{f|道|みち}は{f|空|す}いているだろう。
At this hour the roads are most likely clear.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}がないところを{f|見|み}ると、<b>おそらく</b>{f|忙|いそが}しいのだろう。
Judging by the lack of a reply, he's probably swamped.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|不具合|ふぐあい}は、<b>おそらく</b>{f|先月|せんげつ}の{f|更新|こうしん}が{f|原因|げんいん}だと{f|考|かんが}えられます。
This fault is in all likelihood caused by last month's update.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`おそらく` leans formal. It is the natural choice in a report, a news article, a
presentation or a careful explanation to a client, and it sounds a little stiff
between friends deciding where to eat, where たぶん is what people actually say.

It also leans toward **inference about circumstances** rather than announcements
about your own plans. たぶん{f|行|い}きます ("I'll probably go") is ordinary;
おそらく{f|行|い}きます sounds oddly detached, as though you were forecasting the
behaviour of a stranger who happens to be you. Reserve `おそらく` for things
outside your control.

On the confidence scale, `おそらく` sits high - above もしかしたら, roughly level
with or a shade above たぶん - but it is still a guess. If you know, say so
plainly; hedging a fact with `おそらく` reads as evasive, and in business that
impression is hard to shake off.

## Contrast

### おそらく vs たぶん

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same probability, different room. `たぶん` is spoken and
casual; `おそらく` is written and considered.

</div>

<pre>
<b>たぶん</b>{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}うと{f|思|おも}うよ。
We'll probably make it, I reckon.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>おそらく</b>{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}うものと{f|思|おも}われます。
We are likely to arrive in time.
</pre>

`たぶん` can be tossed off with no evidence at all; `おそらく` implies you looked at
something first. In casual conversation `おそらく` is not wrong, merely bookish.

### おそらく vs きっと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `おそらく` estimates. `きっと` believes.

</div>

<pre>
<b>きっと</b>{f|喜|よろこ}んでくれるよ。
She's going to love it.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>おそらく</b>{f|喜|よろこ}んでくれるだろう。
She will probably be pleased.
</pre>

`きっと` carries the speaker's feeling - hope, conviction, encouragement - and is
what you say when reassuring someone. `おそらく` carries none of that warmth, which
makes it excellent for analysis and poor for comfort.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ おそらく{f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ります。 → ✅ おそらく{f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>でしょう</b>。
  A flat ます-ending states a fact, which contradicts the word at the front.
  `おそらく` needs `でしょう`, `だろう` or と{f|思|おも}います to lean on.

- ❌ おそらく{f|私|わたし}が{f|作|つく}りました。 → ✅ <b>たぶん</b>{f|私|わたし}が{f|作|つく}ったと{f|思|おも}います。
  You do not estimate your own past actions from the outside. `おそらく` is for
  inference about things you did not do.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}のパーティー、おそらく{f|行|い}くね。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}のパーティー、<b>たぶん</b>{f|行|い}くね。
  Announcing your own plans to a friend calls for the casual word. `おそらく` with
  the final particle `ね` mixes two registers in one short sentence.

## Practice

**1.** {f|電気|でんき}が{f|消|き}えているから、____もう{f|寝|ね}ているだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>おそらく</b>もう{f|寝|ね}ているだろう。

The speaker reasons from visible evidence - the lights are off - to a probable
conclusion, and the `だろう` at the end is the partner this adverb expects.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: この{f|結果|けっか}は、（きっと・おそらく）{f|気温|きおん}の{f|影響|えいきょう}によるものと{f|思|おも}われる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>おそらく</b>{f|気温|きおん}の{f|影響|えいきょう}によるものと{f|思|おも}われる。

The sentence is written analysis, complete with the formal
と{f|思|おも}われる ending. `きっと` would import a note of personal conviction that
does not belong in a report.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) おそらく{f|彼|かれ}は{f|来|こ}ないでしょう。
(b) もしかしたら{f|彼|かれ}は{f|来|こ}ないかもしれません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (a) is a considered estimate: the speaker has reasons and expects him not
to turn up. Sentence (b) merely floats the possibility, and the speaker would not
be surprised either way. In practice (a) is what you say when planning around his
absence, and (b) is what you say when raising a risk nobody had mentioned yet.

</details>
