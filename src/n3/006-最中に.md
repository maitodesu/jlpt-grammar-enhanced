# ～最中に

**Meaning** · right in the thick of it - the peak of an activity, cut across by something else

<code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}</code> is <code>{f|中|ちゅう}</code> with the kanji for
"most" bolted on the front, and that is exactly what it adds: not merely *during*, but
*at the busiest, most involved point of*. Nine times out of ten the main clause is an
interruption - the phone rings, the power fails, somebody walks in. If nothing cuts
across, you probably wanted <code>{f|中|ちゅう}</code> or
<code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code>.

## Formation

<code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}</code> is a noun, so what precedes it is in noun-modifying
shape. Unlike the suffix <code>{f|中|ちゅう}</code>, it **requires** the `の` after a
noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Activity noun | Noun + の + {f|最中|さいちゅう}に | {f|試合|しあい} → {f|試合|しあい}の{f|最中|さいちゅう}に |
| Verb | ている + {f|最中|さいちゅう}に | {f|話|はな}す → {f|話|はな}している{f|最中|さいちゅう}に |
| As a predicate | …の{f|最中|さいちゅう}だ／です | {f|準備|じゅんび} → {f|準備|じゅんび}の{f|最中|さいちゅう}です |
| Emphatic | {f|真|ま}っ{f|最中|さいちゅう} | {f|工事|こうじ} → {f|工事|こうじ}の{f|真|ま}っ{f|最中|さいちゅう}だ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the noun in front has to name an **activity with a middle**:
{f|会議|かいぎ}, {f|食事|しょくじ}, {f|試合|しあい}, {f|授業|じゅぎょう},
{f|発表|はっぴょう}, {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}し. States and life stages are refused:
❌{f|若|わか}い{f|最中|さいちゅう}, ❌{f|留守|るす}の{f|最中|さいちゅう}.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the same two kanji, <code>最中</code>, also spell <b>もなか</b>, a wafer-and-bean-paste
sweet. Context separates them without difficulty, but the word does exist and you will
meet it in a confectionery shop.

</div>

</pre>

</pre>

</pre>

The power went out while I was in the middle of making dinner, and in the end we made do with instant noodles.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral in itself, and usable in both ます and plain speech. What marks it is
**tone**: <code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</code> is vivid. It dramatises the timing, and the
speaker is nearly always annoyed, embarrassed or startled. A neutral report of when
something happened does not want it - that is
<code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code>'s job.

Because of that, the pattern is at home in complaints and anecdotes and comparatively
rare in formal writing, where <code>{f|中|ちゅう}</code> or
<code>{f|際|さい}に</code> would be used instead. A company notice says
{f|工事|こうじ}{f|中|ちゅう}; a colleague telling you about their week says
{f|工事|こうじ}の{f|最中|さいちゅう}に.

<code>{f|真|ま}っ{f|最中|さいちゅう}</code> intensifies it further and is common as a
whole predicate: {f|今|いま}{f|準備|じゅんび}の{f|真|ま}っ{f|最中|さいちゅう}です. Note
that the intensified form usually appears *without* `に`, describing the current state
rather than locating an event.

One structural habit worth internalising: the clause before
<code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}</code> is almost always ている or a noun + `の`. A bare
dictionary form is wrong - ❌{f|話|はな}す{f|最中|さいちゅう}に - because the pattern
needs the activity to be visibly under way, and only ている says that.

## Contrast

### {f|最中|さいちゅう}に vs {f|間|あいだ}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code> puts an event anywhere in the window,
including the quiet edges. <code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</code> puts it at the **peak**,
and implies it should not have happened there.

</div>

<pre>
{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}ている<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>{f|二回|にかい}メールが{f|来|き}た。
Two emails came in while I was watching the film.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|映画|えいが}の{f|一番|いちばん}いいところの<b>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</b>{f|前|まえ}の{f|人|ひと}が{f|立|た}ち{f|上|あ}がった。
The person in front stood up right at the best bit of the film.
</pre>

The first is a neutral count of when the mail arrived.
The second is a grievance. Note also that
<code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</code> resists a durative main clause:
❌{f|映画|えいが}の{f|最中|さいちゅう}にずっと{f|寝|ね}ていた does not work, because a
peak is a point and cannot host something lasting. Use
<code>{f|間|あいだ}</code>, with no `に`, for that.

### {f|最中|さいちゅう}に vs ～{f|中|ちゅう}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|中|ちゅう}</code> is a **label**: this activity is on.
<code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}</code> is a **judgement**: this activity was at full tilt, and
look what happened.

</div>

<pre>
{f|運転|うんてん}<b>{f|中|ちゅう}</b>は{f|電話|でんわ}に{f|出|で}られません。
I can't take calls while driving.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|運転|うんてん}している<b>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</b>タイヤがパンクした。
I had a blowout in the middle of driving.
</pre>

The forms differ too, and the difference is easy to get wrong:
<code>{f|運転|うんてん}{f|中|ちゅう}</code> glues on with no `の`, whereas
<code>{f|運転|うんてん}の{f|最中|さいちゅう}</code> demands one.
<code>{f|中|ちゅう}</code> also happily takes `は` for a rule that holds throughout;
<code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}</code> almost never does, because a peak is not a period you
lay rules over.

### {f|最中|さいちゅう}に vs ～ているところに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both host interruptions, but
<code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}</code> emphasises **how deep into it** you were, while
<code>ところ</code> emphasises **which stage** you were at - about to, just doing, just
finished.

</div>

<pre>
ケーキを{f|焼|や}いている<b>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</b>{f|客|きゃく}が{f|来|き}た。
Guests turned up while I was right in the middle of baking a cake.
</pre>

<pre>
ケーキを{f|焼|や}こうとしている<b>ところに</b>{f|客|きゃく}が{f|来|き}た。
Guests turned up just as I was about to bake a cake.
</pre>

<code>ところ</code> can slide along the timeline - {f|焼|や}くところ,
{f|焼|や}いているところ, {f|焼|や}いたところ - and each position means something
different. <code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}</code> has only one position: the middle. That is
why it cannot be used for "just about to" or "just after".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|会議|かいぎ}<b>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</b>{f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}った。 → ✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}<b>の{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</b>{f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}った。
  A noun in front needs `の`. It is the suffix <code>{f|中|ちゅう}</code> that attaches
  bare - {f|会議|かいぎ}{f|中|ちゅう} - and mixing the two rules is the commonest slip.

- ❌ {f|勉強|べんきょう}<b>する{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</b>{f|眠|ねむ}くなった。 → ✅ {f|勉強|べんきょう}<b>している{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</b>{f|眠|ねむ}くなった。
  The activity has to be shown as under way, which means ている. A dictionary form points
  at the activity as a whole, not at its middle.

- ❌ <b>{f|若|わか}い{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</b>{f|海外|かいがい}に{f|住|す}みたい。 → ✅ <b>{f|若|わか}いうちに</b>{f|海外|かいがい}に{f|住|す}みたい。
  Youth is a state, not an activity in progress, so it has no peak for
  <code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}</code> to point at. States take <code>うちに</code>.

- ❌ {f|旅行|りょこう}の{f|最中|さいちゅう}に<b>ずっと</b>{f|天気|てんき}がよかった。 → ✅ {f|旅行|りょこう}の{f|間|あいだ}<b>ずっと</b>{f|天気|てんき}がよかった。
  <code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</code> wants a single intruding event. Something that
  lasted the whole trip belongs with <code>{f|間|あいだ}</code> and no `に`.

## Practice

**1.** シャワーを{f|浴|あ}びている____に{f|宅配便|たくはいびん}が{f|来|き}てしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

シャワーを{f|浴|あ}びている<b>{f|最中|さいちゅう}</b>に{f|宅配便|たくはいびん}が{f|来|き}てしまった。

A delivery arriving mid-shower is the textbook case: an activity at full tilt, cut
across by something inconvenient. てしまった confirms the speaker's irritation, which is
what <code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</code> exists to convey.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|工場|こうじょう}は（{f|操業|そうぎょう}{f|中|ちゅう}・{f|操業|そうぎょう}の{f|最中|さいちゅう}）ですので、{f|見学|けんがく}はできません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|工場|こうじょう}は<b>{f|操業|そうぎょう}{f|中|ちゅう}</b>ですので、{f|見学|けんがく}はできません。

This is a plain status label of the kind that appears on notices, and nothing is cutting
across the activity. <code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}</code> would inject a note of drama that a
factory sign has no use for.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The alarm went off in the middle of the exam and everyone jumped."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|試験|しけん}の<b>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</b>{f|非常|ひじょう}ベルが{f|鳴|な}って、みんなびっくりした。

An exam is an activity with a clear middle, and the alarm is a single unwelcome
intrusion - the exact shape this pattern is built for. Note the `の`: nouns cannot
attach to <code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}</code> bare.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|母|はは}が{f|料理|りょうり}する{f|最中|さいちゅう}に、{f|大|おお}きな{f|地震|じしん}があった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|母|はは}が{f|料理|りょうり}<b>している{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</b>、{f|大|おお}きな{f|地震|じしん}があった。

The verb has to be in ている. You could equally write
{f|料理|りょうり}<b>の</b>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に, treating {f|料理|りょうり} as a noun - 
but then the `の` becomes obligatory.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|掃除|そうじ}を<b>している{f|間|あいだ}に</b>、{f|友|とも}だちから{f|連絡|れんらく}があった。
(b) {f|掃除|そうじ}の<b>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</b>、{f|友|とも}だちから{f|連絡|れんらく}があった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply locates the message somewhere inside the cleaning. It might have arrived as
you were putting the vacuum away, and the sentence carries no attitude at all.

(b) says you were up to your elbows in it when the message came, and colours the event
as an interruption - mildly exasperating, or at least worth remarking on. The facts can
be identical; what changes is how the speaker feels about the timing.

</details>
