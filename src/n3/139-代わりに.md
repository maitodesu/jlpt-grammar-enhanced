# 代わりに

**Meaning** · instead of; in place of - and, in its other life, in return for

Read <code>{f|代|か}わりに</code>. Two senses live in this one word and learners
routinely melt them together. **Substitution**: A was going to fill the slot, but
B fills it instead. **Compensation**: A and B are both true, and the second
balances out the first. Which one you get depends almost entirely on what the
word attaches to.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + の + {f|代|か}わりに | バター → バターの{f|代|か}わりにオリーブオイル |
| Verb (plain, non-past) | Verb + {f|代|か}わりに | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}く{f|代|か}わりに |
| い-adjective | い-adj + {f|代|か}わりに | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}い{f|代|か}わりに |
| な-adjective | な-adj + な + {f|代|か}わりに | {f|静|しず}か → {f|静|しず}かな{f|代|か}わりに |
| Standalone adverb | …。{f|代|か}わりに… | バスが{f|来|こ}ない。{f|代|か}わりに{f|歩|ある}いた |
| Sentence connector | …。その{f|代|か}わり(に)、… | {f|今日|きょう}は{f|休|やす}む。その{f|代|か}わり{f|明日|あした}やる |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the attachment decides the sense. An **adjective** in front can only
give the compensation reading, because an adjective is not something that can
fill a slot for something else. A **noun** or a **verb** in front leaves both
readings open, and context settles it.

</div>

I took the dog for a walk instead of my sister.

</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>{f|代|か}わりに</code> is register-neutral. It works in conversation, in email and
in writing, and neither sense sounds childish or stiff. In casual writing it is
often left in kana as かわりに; in print, kanji. Watch the kanji itself - <code>代</code> is
"substitute", and <code>変</code> ("change") is a different word that an IME will offer you.

The standalone connector その{f|代|か}わり is extremely common in speech and usually
loses its `に`: 「わかった。その{f|代|か}わり、{f|次|つぎ}は{f|君|きみ}ね。」 It is the fastest way
to propose a deal in Japanese, and it can be said with a smile or with an edge
depending on tone alone.

The compensation sense is neutral about which side is the good one. 「{f|安|やす}い{f|代|か}わりに{f|遠|とお}い」 and 「{f|遠|とお}い{f|代|か}わりに{f|安|やす}い」 are both fine; the word
only asserts that the two facts come as a package. What it will not do is join two
facts that have nothing to do with each other - there must be a felt balance
between them.

When the exchange is a favour you are asking for, <code>{f|代|か}わりに</code> makes the
bargain explicit, which is friendly among equals and blunt with a superior.
Softened forms - 「{f|手伝|てつだ}いますので、その{f|代|か}わりと{f|言|い}っては{f|何|なん}ですが…」 - 
exist precisely because the bare version is so direct.

For business and ceremonial situations, the formal counterpart of the
substitution sense is `にかわって` / `にかわりまして`, not this word.

## Contrast

### Substitution {f|代|か}わりに vs compensation {f|代|か}わりに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if the two things **compete for one slot** (only one of them
actually happens), it is substitution. If **both are true** and the second
offsets the first, it is compensation. An adjective in front forces the second
reading.

</div>

Substitution - the honey is in the cake, the sugar is not:

<pre>
{f|牛乳|ぎゅうにゅう}の<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>{f|豆乳|とうにゅう}を{f|使|つか}っても{f|作|つく}れます。
You can make it with soy milk in place of cow's milk.
</pre>

Compensation - both halves happened:

<pre>
{f|給料|きゅうりょう}は{f|少|すく}ない<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>、{f|休|やす}みがきちんと{f|取|と}れる。
The pay is low, but on the other side of it you can actually take your holidays.
</pre>

With a verb, context decides. 「{f|私|わたし}が{f|運転|うんてん}する{f|代|か}わりに{f|兄|あに}が{f|寝|ね}ていた」 is substitution (one of us drove). 「{f|私|わたし}が{f|運転|うんてん}する{f|代|か}わりに{f|昼|ひる}ごはんをおごってね」 is compensation (I drive *and* you pay). The tell is
whether the second half is a **consequence for the other person** - if it is, you
are trading, not substituting.

### {f|代|か}わりに vs にかわって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same substitution meaning at a different altitude.
`にかわって` is formal, attaches only to nouns, and has no compensation sense.

</div>

<pre>
{f|急|きゅう}に{f|休|やす}んだ{f|同僚|どうりょう}<b>にかわって</b>、{f|受付|うけつけ}に{f|立|た}つことになった。
I ended up on the front desk in place of a colleague who called in sick.
</pre>

Said to a friend that evening, the same fact comes out as 「{f|同僚|どうりょう}の{f|代|か}わりに{f|受付|うけつけ}やらされた」. Neither is more correct; they belong to
different rooms.

### {f|代|か}わりに vs ～ないで・～ずに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - English "instead of doing X" is `～ないで` when X is simply
**not done**. <code>{f|代|か}わりに</code> is only right when X and Y are genuine
alternatives for one job, or when they trade off against each other.

</div>

<pre>
{f|朝|あさ}ごはんを<b>{f|食|た}べないで</b>{f|学校|がっこう}へ{f|行|い}きました。
I went to school without eating breakfast.
</pre>

❌<code>{f|朝|あさ}ごはんを{f|食|た}べる{f|代|か}わりに{f|学校|がっこう}へ{f|行|い}きました</code> is odd
because going to school is not an alternative way of having breakfast. Give the
two things the same job and the pattern comes back:

<pre>
{f|朝|あさ}ごはんの<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>バナナを{f|一本|いっぽん}{f|食|た}べました。
I had a banana in place of breakfast.
</pre>

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|母|はは}<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>{f|私|わたし}が{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ {f|母|はは}の<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>{f|私|わたし}が{f|行|い}きます。
  A noun needs `の` in front of the pattern. Only verbs and adjectives attach
  bare.

- ❌ {f|安|やす}いの<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>、{f|味|あじ}は{f|普通|ふつう}です。 → ✅ {f|安|やす}い<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>、{f|味|あじ}は{f|普通|ふつう}です。
  The `の` belongs after nouns only. An い-adjective connects directly.

- ❌ テレビを{f|見|み}る<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>{f|早|はや}く{f|寝|ね}ました。（meaning: I skipped TV and went to bed） → ✅ テレビを{f|見|み}<b>ないで</b>{f|早|はや}く{f|寝|ね}ました。
  Going to bed is not a substitute way of watching television. Where nothing is
  standing in for anything, use `～ないで`.

- ❌ この{f|店|みせ}は{f|静|しず}か<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>、{f|値段|ねだん}が{f|高|たか}い。 → ✅ この{f|店|みせ}は{f|静|しず}かな<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>、{f|値段|ねだん}が{f|高|たか}い。
  A な-adjective keeps its `な` before the pattern.

## Practice

**1.** {f|今日|きょう}は{f|時間|じかん}がないので、メールの____{f|電話|でんわ}で{f|伝|つた}えます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

メールの<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>{f|電話|でんわ}で{f|伝|つた}えます。

Email and a phone call are two ways of doing the same job, and only one of them
will happen - that is the substitution sense. Note the `の`, which a noun always
requires here.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|大変|たいへん}（な{f|代|か}わりに・の{f|代|か}わりに）、{f|勉強|べんきょう}になることが{f|多|おお}い。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|大変|たいへん}<b>な{f|代|か}わりに</b>、{f|勉強|べんきょう}になることが{f|多|おお}い。

<code>{f|大変|たいへん}</code> is a な-adjective, so it keeps `な`. The sense is
compensation: the job is hard *and* it teaches you a lot, and the sentence
presents those two as a package deal.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'll look after your cat while you're away - in return, bring me back some sweets."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|留守|るす}の{f|間|あいだ}ねこの{f|世話|せわ}をしてあげる<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>、おみやげにお{f|菓子|かし}を{f|買|か}ってきてね。

Both halves are going to happen, and the second is your price for the first, so
this is the compensation sense. You could equally break it in two and use
その{f|代|か}わり as a connector at the start of the second sentence.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|運動|うんどう}する{f|代|か}わりに、テレビばかり{f|見|み}ていました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|運動|うんどう}し<b>ないで</b>、テレビばかり{f|見|み}ていました。

Watching television is not a way of getting exercise, so nothing is filling
anything else's slot. When the first activity is simply omitted, `～ないで` or
`～ずに` is the pattern. <code>{f|代|か}わりに</code> would only work if the second
activity did the same job - for example
「ジムに{f|行|い}く{f|代|か}わりに{f|家|いえ}で{f|体操|たいそう}をした」.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|弟|おとうと}が{f|皿|さら}を{f|洗|あら}う<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>、{f|私|わたし}がごみを{f|出|だ}した。
(b) {f|弟|おとうと}が{f|皿|さら}を{f|洗|あら}う<b>{f|代|か}わりに</b>、{f|私|わたし}がごみを{f|出|だ}してあげた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is most naturally read as substitution or as a straight division of labour:
he took the dishes, I took the rubbish, and between us the chores were done.

(b) tips into compensation because of <code>～てあげた</code>, which frames the rubbish
as a favour done *for him*. Now the sentence sounds like a deal: he washes up,
and in return I handle the bins on his behalf. The grammar is identical - the
benefactive verb is doing all the work.

</details>
