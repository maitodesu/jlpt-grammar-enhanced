# まるで

**Meaning** · just like; as if - flagging a comparison that everyone knows is not
literally true

`まるで` is the word that turns a plain statement into a simile. It tells the
listener in advance: what I am about to say is an exaggeration, take it as a
picture rather than a fact.

## Usage

`まるで` is an adverb and normally opens the clause. The lesson of this page is the
ending, because `まるで` cannot stand alone - it must be answered by a
likeness-marker:

- **まるで～ようだ／ような／ように** - the standard, and the one to learn first.
  まるで{f|夢|ゆめ}のようだ。
- **まるで～みたいだ／みたいな／みたいに** - the same thing in casual speech.
  まるで{f|子供|こども}みたいに{f|喜|よろこ}んでいた。
- **まるで～かのようだ** - literary and a shade more emphatic; common in writing.
  まるで{f|何|なに}もなかったかのように{f|話|はな}し{f|続|つづ}けた。

A sentence like ❌まるで{f|夢|ゆめ}だ is broken. Whatever comes after `まるで` has to
be marked as a comparison; without that, you have asserted the impossible thing
instead of comparing to it.

There is a second, quite different use. With a **negative** predicate, `まるで`
means "not at all": まるで{f|話|はなし}にならない, まるで{f|覚|おぼ}えていない. Here
it is an intensifier of the negation, and no `ようだ` appears. This use is
emphatic and a little literary; ぜんぜん and まったく are the everyday equivalents.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}は<b>まるで</b>{f|春|はる}のように{f|暖|あたた}かい。
It's as warm as spring today.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|犬|いぬ}は<b>まるで</b>ぬいぐるみみたいだった。
That dog looked just like a stuffed toy.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一年|いちねん}ぶりに{f|来|き}たのに、<b>まるで</b>{f|昨日|きのう}のことのように{f|覚|おぼ}えている。
I hadn't been back in a year, yet I remember it as though it were yesterday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|質問|しつもん}には{f|一|ひと}つも{f|答|こた}えず、<b>まるで</b>{f|聞|き}こえていないかのように{f|窓|まど}の{f|外|そと}を{f|見|み}ていた。
He answered not one question and stared out of the window as if he could not hear us.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and common in speech, novels and journalism alike. The choice of
ending carries the register instead: `みたい` is casual, `ようだ` is neutral,
`かのようだ` is written and rather literary.

`まるで` is an exaggeration marker, so the listener understands the comparison to be
false. That makes it a good tool for complaint and for praise, and a bad one for
description. If the resemblance is real - a child who genuinely looks like her
mother - the natural word is そっくり: {f|母親|ははおや}にそっくりだ. Saying
まるで{f|母親|ははおや}のようだ suggests the likeness is uncanny rather than simply
present.

Also note that `まるで` is optional. {f|夢|ゆめ}のようだ is a complete and natural
sentence; adding `まるで` in front of it only turns the volume up. Overusing it in
writing quickly sounds breathless.

The negative use is a different register again. まるで{f|分|わ}からない is
emphatic, slightly old-fashioned in flavour, and more common in writing and in
grumbling than in neutral conversation.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}はまるで{f|王様|おうさま}だ。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}はまるで{f|王様|おうさま}<b>のようだ</b>。
  Without a likeness-marker the sentence claims he *is* a king. `まるで` sets up a
  comparison and needs `ようだ`, `みたいだ` or `かのようだ` to complete it.

- ❌ {f|姉|あね}はまるで{f|母|はは}のようです。（meaning they really do look alike）→ ✅ {f|姉|あね}は{f|母|はは}に<b>そっくり</b>です。
  `まるで` says the likeness is a figure of speech. For an actual family
  resemblance it undercuts what you meant.

- ❌ まるで{f|漢字|かんじ}が{f|読|よ}めます。 → ✅ まるで{f|漢字|かんじ}が{f|読|よ}め<b>ません</b>。
  In its intensifier use, `まるで` attaches only to a negative. There is no
  affirmative version of that sense.

## Practice

**1.** {f|雨|あめ}にぬれて、____ぬれた{f|猫|ねこ}____{f|姿|すがた}だった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|雨|あめ}にぬれて、<b>まるで</b>ぬれた{f|猫|ねこ}<b>のような</b>{f|姿|すがた}だった。

The two blanks are one frame: the adverb opens the comparison and
<code>のような</code> attaches it to the noun. Because a noun follows,
<code>のように</code> would be wrong here.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|部屋|へや}はまるでホテルだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|部屋|へや}はまるでホテル<b>のようだ</b>。

As written, the sentence says her room *is* a hotel. Adding <code>のようだ</code> - or
`みたいだ` in casual speech - turns it back into the comparison the speaker
intended.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|説明|せつめい}はまるで{f|分|わ}からなかった。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|説明|せつめい}はあまり{f|分|わ}からなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (a) uses the intensifier sense: nothing at all got through, and the tone
is emphatic, close to a complaint. Sentence (b) is measured - some of it landed,
much of it did not. Note that (a) contains no comparison at all, which is why no
<code>ようだ</code> appears in it.

</details>
