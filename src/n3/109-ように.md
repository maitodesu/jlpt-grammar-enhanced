# ～ように - in order to

**Meaning** · so that something will be the case - you act now with an eye on a result you cannot simply perform yourself

The clause in front of `ように` is not another thing you do. It is a **state you are
aiming at**: something becoming possible, something not happening, something turning
out right. You then do whatever the main clause says in the hope of getting there.
<code>{f|見|み}えるように{f|前|まえ}に{f|座|すわ}る</code> - "sit at the front so I can
see." Sitting is the action; being able to see is the target.

That is the whole of it, and it is also what separates `ように` from
<code>ために</code>, which takes an action you fully control.

## Formation

The clause before `ように` describes a result, so it is built from verbs that are not
straightforwardly willed: potentials, negatives, and intransitives.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (potential) | potential form + ように | {f|聞|き}こえる → {f|聞|き}こえるように |
| Verb (intransitive / uncontrollable) | dictionary form + ように | {f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}う → {f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}うように |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + ように | {f|忘|わす}れない → {f|忘|わす}れないように |
| As an instruction | ように + してください | {f|遅|おく}れないようにしてください |
| As a wish or prayer | ますように（sentence-final） | {f|受|う}かる → {f|受|う}かりますように |
| Reporting an instruction | ようにと + {f|言|い}う・{f|伝|つた}える | {f|休|やす}むようにと{f|伝|つた}える |
| Formal written | ように → よう | {f|注意|ちゅうい}なさいますよう |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - nouns and adjectives cannot sit in front of purpose `ように`. Turn
the goal into a verb first: not ❌<code>{f|静|しず}かなように</code> but
<code>{f|静|しず}かになるように</code> or <code>{f|静|しず}かにするように</code>.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - a `ように` you meet after a noun plus `の`, or after a verb meaning "as I
said", is the *comparison* `ように` from N4: <code>{f|鳥|とり}のように</code>, "like a
bird". Same syllables, unrelated job. Purpose `ように` always has a full clause in
front of it and a second clause after it.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`ように` itself is neutral, but it turns up in three settings often enough to be worth
recognising.

**Instructions and notices.** Written Japanese drops the `に` and uses bare `よう`:
<code>{f|足元|あしもと}にご{f|注意|ちゅうい}くださいますよう、お{f|願|ねが}い{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます</code>.
That `よう` is stiff and formal, and it is everywhere in announcements, signs and
business letters. Learners often read it as a typo for `ように`; it is not.

**A softened order.** A sentence can stop at `ように` and let the verb be understood:
<code>{f|明日|あした}は{f|遅|おく}れないように。</code> - "Don't be late tomorrow, now."
This is common from teachers and supervisors and lands rather more gently than
<code>～ないでください</code>, because grammatically it is a statement of the goal
rather than a command aimed at a person.

**Wishes.** The prayer form is fixed as <code>～ますように</code> and keeps its `ます`
even in the most casual speech: <code>{f|元気|げんき}で{f|過|す}ごせますように</code>
written on a shrine tablet, or muttered while crossing your fingers. Note that it has
no main clause at all - the sentence simply ends there, which is why it feels like a
prayer rather than a plan.

Finally, `ように` inside reported speech becomes <code>ようにと</code> before verbs of
saying, asking and hoping: <code>{f|早|はや}く{f|治|なお}るようにと{f|祈|いの}っている</code>.
The `と` is optional in speech and usual in writing.

## Contrast

### ～ように vs ～ために

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ために` takes a goal **you can simply go and do**, and the two
clauses share a subject. `ように` takes a goal **that has to come about**, and the
subjects may differ.

</div>

<pre>
{f|留学|りゅうがく}<b>するために</b>、お{f|金|かね}をためています。
I'm saving up in order to study abroad.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}が{f|留学|りゅうがく}<b>できるように</b>、お{f|金|かね}をためています。
I'm saving up so that my son can study abroad.
</pre>

In the first, going abroad is an act the saver will perform: same person, fully
volitional, so `ために`. In the second the goal belongs to somebody else and is phrased
as a possibility - hence the potential form and `ように`. Notice that changing the verb
to a potential is often all it takes to move a sentence from one pattern to the other:
<code>{f|読|よ}むために</code> versus <code>{f|読|よ}めるように</code>.

A useful side effect: because `ために` promises a deliberate act, it sounds purposeful
and even a little formal in a plan or a proposal. `ように` sounds considerate, because
it usually has someone else's convenience in it.

### ～ないように vs ～ないために

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for a **negative** purpose, use `ないように`. `ないために` normally
gets read as a *cause* - "because ～ not" - which is not what you meant.

</div>

<pre>
{f|遅|おく}れ<b>ないように</b>、{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}{f|早|はや}く{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}た。
I left half an hour early so as not to be late.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ら<b>ないために</b>、{f|野菜|やさい}の{f|値段|ねだん}が{f|上|あ}がっている。
Vegetable prices are rising because there has been no rain.
</pre>

The asymmetry is easy to explain: "not doing something" is not an act you perform, so
it fails `ために`'s volitional test from the start. Learners who translate "in order
not to" word by word land on ❌<code>{f|忘|わす}れないために</code> almost every time.
The fix is mechanical - any English "so as not to" or "in order not to" becomes
`ないように`.

### purpose ～ように vs ～のに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ように` sets a **goal for an action**. `のに` names the **task a
thing is for**, and the clause after it judges that thing: it is useful, it takes
time, it costs money, you need one.

</div>

<pre>
{f|野菜|やさい}を<b>{f|切|き}るのに</b>、この{f|包丁|ほうちょう}が{f|便利|べんり}です。
This knife is handy for cutting vegetables.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|野菜|やさい}が{f|早|はや}く<b>{f|切|き}れるように</b>、{f|包丁|ほうちょう}を{f|研|と}いだ。
I sharpened the knife so the vegetables would cut quickly.
</pre>

The `のに` sentence is an evaluation - what follows is an adjective or a verb of
needing, taking or costing. The `ように` sentence is an action taken with an outcome in
mind. If you can put "for the job of ～ing" in the English, it is `のに`; if you can put
"so that", it is `ように`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|日本|にほん}へ{f|行|い}くように、お{f|金|かね}をためています。 → ✅ {f|日本|にほん}へ<b>{f|行|い}くために</b>、お{f|金|かね}をためています。
  Going to Japan is something the speaker will simply do. A controllable act with the
  same subject takes `ために`.

- ❌ {f|忘|わす}れないために、カレンダーに{f|書|か}いた。 → ✅ <b>{f|忘|わす}れないように</b>、カレンダーに{f|書|か}いた。
  Negative purpose is always `ないように`. `ないために` would be heard as "because I
  didn't forget".

- ❌ {f|部屋|へや}が{f|静|しず}かなように、ドアを{f|閉|し}めた。 → ✅ {f|部屋|へや}が<b>{f|静|しず}かになるように</b>、ドアを{f|閉|し}めた。
  A な-adjective cannot stand in front of purpose `ように`. Give the goal a verb - 
  `なる` or `する` - and the sentence works.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}{f|晴|は}れるようにします。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}<b>{f|晴|は}れますように</b>。
  You cannot undertake to make the weather behave. When the goal is entirely out of
  your hands, the sentence is a wish, not a plan.

## Practice

**1.** {f|後|うし}ろの{f|席|せき}からも____ように、スクリーンを{f|高|たか}くしました。（{f|見|み}える）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|後|うし}ろの{f|席|せき}からも<b>{f|見|み}えるように</b>、スクリーンを{f|高|たか}くしました。

The goal is that the screen *be visible* to other people - a state, not an act the
speaker performs - so the potential form plus `ように` is correct. Raising the screen
is the action, and it goes in the main clause.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|医者|いしゃ}に（なるように・なるために）、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|遅|おそ}くまで{f|勉強|べんきょう}している。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|医者|いしゃ}に<b>なるために</b>、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|遅|おそ}くまで{f|勉強|べんきょう}している。

Becoming a doctor is the speaker's own deliberate aim, and both clauses have the same
subject, so it takes `ために`. `ように` would suggest the outcome were somehow out of
the speaker's hands, which sits oddly with studying every night to achieve it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Please write your name clearly so the staff can read it."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|係|かかり}の{f|人|ひと}が<b>{f|読|よ}めるように</b>、{f|名前|なまえ}をはっきり{f|書|か}いてください。

The person who reads and the person who writes are different, and "can read" is a
potential - both signals point to `ように`. The polite request then goes on the main
verb, where it belongs.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|風邪|かぜ}をひかないために、マスクをしています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|風邪|かぜ}を<b>ひかないように</b>、マスクをしています。

"In order not to" is `ないように` without exception. Catching a cold is not something
you choose to do, so the negative goal fails `ために`'s requirement of a willed action
twice over.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|子|こ}どもが<b>{f|寝|ね}られるように</b>、テレビを{f|消|け}した。
(b) {f|子|こ}どもを<b>{f|寝|ね}かせるために</b>、テレビを{f|消|け}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) puts the child's sleep in the position of a hoped-for outcome: the speaker removes
an obstacle and the child, one hopes, drops off. It sounds considerate, and it leaves
room for the child to stay awake anyway.

(b) makes it the speaker's project. The causative <code>{f|寝|ね}かせる</code> is
something the parent actively does, so `ために` fits, and the sentence sounds more like
a plan being carried out than a kindness being offered.

</details>
