# ～たらいい / ～といい - I hope

**Meaning** · I hope ~ ; here's hoping ~ - a wish about an outcome nobody in the conversation controls

Same words as the advice pattern, different job. The conditional still sketches a world
and calls it good; what changes is that neither speaker nor listener can bring that
world about. The weather, an exam result, somebody's recovery, whether the train is on
time - once the outcome is out of human hands, "you should" is impossible and the only
reading left is **hope**.

This is the everyday Japanese for "I hope so", and the fixed phrase
<code>～といいですね</code> is worth learning as a single unit.

## Formation

Unlike the advice reading, the hope reading takes adjectives and nouns as happily as
verbs, because a hope can be about a *state*.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain non-past + と + いい | {f|晴|は}れる → {f|晴|は}れるといい |
| Verb | plain past + ら + いい | {f|晴|は}れる → {f|晴|は}れたらいい |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + と + いい | {f|降|ふ}らない → {f|降|ふ}らないといい |
| Verb (result already fixed) | ている／ていた + と + いい | {f|受|う}かる → {f|受|う}かっているといい |
| い-adjective | plain form + と + いい | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}いといい |
| な-adjective | stem + **だ** + と + いい | {f|元気|げんき} → {f|元気|げんき}だといい |
| Noun | Noun + **だ** + と + いい | いい{f|天気|てんき} → いい{f|天気|てんき}だといい |
| Hope for the listener | ～といいですね／～といいね | {f|受|う}かるといいですね |
| Hope of your own | ～といいんですが／～といいのですが | {f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}うといいんですが |
| To yourself | ～といいなあ／～たらいいなあ | {f|晴|は}れるといいなあ |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - before `と`, nouns and な-adjectives need `だ`. This is the same `と`
you met as a conditional at N4, and it has the same requirement: ❌<code>いい{f|天気|てんき}といい</code>,
✅<code>いい{f|天気|てんき}だといい</code>.

</div>

## Nuance & register

The pattern is neutral and usable at every level of politeness, but **whose** hope it is
changes the ending, and getting that wrong is the most audible mistake learners make
here.

For a hope about the **listener's** situation, the ending is `ですね` (or plain `ね`).
`ね` asks the listener to share a feeling, and since the feeling is about them, that
works: {f|試合|しあい}に{f|勝|か}てるといいですね。 This is the standard way of wishing
somebody luck, and it is far more common than 「がんばってください」 in careful adult
speech.

For a hope of **your own**, the natural ending trails off: `～といいんですが`,
`～といいのですが`, `～といいんですけど`. The unfinished `が`/`けど` does the work of
English "…though" - it stops the sentence from sounding like a demand.
{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}うといいんですが is modest; {f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}うといいですね about
your own train sounds like you are asking a stranger to agree with your wishes.

`なあ` is the private version. It is spoken to yourself or to somebody very close, it
draws the vowel out, and it is one of the most natural-sounding things a learner can
say: {f|明日|あした}{f|休|やす}みだといいなあ。 In writing it appears mostly in dialogue
and diaries.

Two small points of substance. First, the negative `ないといい` is fully idiomatic for
hoping something **doesn't** happen - {f|渋滞|じゅうたい}していないといいですね - and is
the normal way to say it. Second, when the outcome is already settled but unknown to
you, put the clause into `ている`/`ていた`: {f|忘|わす}れずに{f|持|も}っていってくれて
いるといいんですが. The hope is not that it will happen, but that it already did.

## Contrast

### ～といい vs ～たらいい in the hope reading

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are used and neither is wrong. `と` presents the hoped-for
thing as simply happening, and is the default in the set phrases. `たら` frames it a
little more as a condition being met, so it leans slightly further from expectation.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|旅行|りょこう}、<b>{f|楽|たの}しいといい</b>ですね。
I hope next month's trip is fun.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|宝|たから}くじ、<b>{f|当|あ}たったらいい</b>なあ。
Wouldn't it be something if the lottery came in.
</pre>

The trip is a real event with a decent chance of being fun; `と` fits. Winning the
lottery is remote, and `たら` sits more comfortably with remoteness. Do not turn this
into a rule - <code>{f|当|あ}たるといいなあ</code> is perfectly natural too. What is
close to fixed is the polite formula: 「～といいですね」 is heard far more often than
「～たらいいですね」.

### ～といいですね vs ～といいんですが

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ですね` for somebody else's hope, `んですが` for your own. `ね`
asks the listener to agree, and you cannot ask people to agree that your wishes should
come true.

</div>

<pre>
{f|面接|めんせつ}、<b>うまくいくといい</b>ですね。
I hope your interview goes well.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|面接|めんせつ}、<b>うまくいくといい</b>んですが。
I hope my interview goes well - we'll see.
</pre>

The second is what you say about yourself, and the trailing `が` is not laziness; it is
the politeness. Ending flatly on 「うまくいくといいです」 about your own affairs sounds
blunt in a way that is hard to explain and easy to hear.

### ～といい vs ～てほしい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `てほしい` aims at a **person** and asks them to act. `といい` aims at
the **world** and asks nothing of anybody.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}までに{f|返事|へんじ}を<b>もらえるといい</b>んですが。
I hope I can get an answer by tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}までに{f|返事|へんじ}を<b>もらいたい</b>んですが。
I'd like an answer by tomorrow, please.
</pre>

The first leaves the other party free; the second is a request wearing a soft coat.
Said to the person who owes you the answer, `といい` is a hint and `てほしい`／`たい` is a
push. Choosing the wrong one either fails to get you your answer or annoys the person
who has to give it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}、いい{f|天気|てんき}といいですね。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}、いい{f|天気|てんき}<b>だ</b>といいですね。
  A noun needs `だ` before this `と`. The same goes for な-adjectives:
  {f|元気|げんき}だといいですね.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}の{f|荷物|にもつ}が{f|無事|ぶじ}に{f|着|つ}くといいですね。 → ✅ …{f|着|つ}くといい<b>んですが</b>。
  `ですね` invites agreement, which only works when the hope is about the listener.
  About your own luggage it sounds like you are recruiting them into your worry.

- ❌ {f|手伝|てつだ}ってくれるといいです。（meaning "I want you to help"） → ✅ {f|手伝|てつだ}って<b>ほしい</b>です。
  Said to the person who could help, a hope is not a request. Use `てほしい` when you
  actually want them to act.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}らないといいだね。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}らないといいね。
  `いい` is an い-adjective and never takes `だ` in front of `ね`.

## Practice

**1.** 「{f|来週|らいしゅう}、{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}に{f|行|い}くんです。」「そうですか。{f|雪|ゆき}が____ですね。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|雪|ゆき}が<b>{f|見|み}られるといい</b>ですね。

The hope is about the listener's trip and about something no one controls, so `といい`
plus `ですね` is exactly right. `ですね` is what makes it a wish *for them* rather than a
remark about yourself.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|明日|あした}の{f|発表|はっぴょう}、（{f|練習|れんしゅう}したらいいですよ・うまくいくといいですね）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are natural, but they do different jobs.
<code>{f|練習|れんしゅう}したらいいですよ</code> is advice about something the listener
controls; <code>うまくいくといいですね</code> is a hope about how it turns out. If you
are seeing them off on the day, only the second one still makes sense.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I hope the parcel arrives before Friday, though."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|金曜日|きんようび}までに{f|荷物|にもつ}が<b>{f|届|とど}くといい</b>んですが。

The hope is the speaker's own, so it takes the trailing `んですが` rather than `ですね`.
The English "though" is doing the same softening work as the Japanese `が`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|息子|むすこ}が{f|試験|しけん}に{f|合格|ごうかく}するといいですねと{f|思|おも}っています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|息子|むすこ}が{f|試験|しけん}に{f|合格|ごうかく}すると<b>いいなと{f|思|おも}っています</b>。

`ですね` addresses the listener, so it cannot sit inside a quoted thought. Inside
<code>と{f|思|おも}う</code> the clause goes plain, and `な` is the particle that carries
the wistfulness.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|早|はや}く{f|元気|げんき}に<b>なるといい</b>ですね。
(b) {f|早|はや}く{f|元気|げんき}に<b>なってほしい</b>です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a hope handed to the person you are talking to, or to somebody's family on their
behalf. It asks nothing of anyone and is the safe thing to say to a patient or their
relatives.

(b) puts the recovery on the other person as something you want from them. Between
close family that reads as affection; said to a colleague who is ill it can sound like
you need them back at work. The grammar is fine either way - it is the direction of the
wish that differs.

</details>
