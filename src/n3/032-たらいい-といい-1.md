# ～たらいい / ～といい - should

**Meaning** · you should ~; why not ~ - advice given by describing a good outcome rather than issuing an order

Read the words literally and they say *if you did it, that would be good*. That is the
whole trick. Instead of telling somebody what to do, you sketch a small hypothetical
world in which they have already done it, and pronounce that world good. The listener
is left to draw the obvious conclusion, which is far easier to accept than an
instruction.

This page covers the advice reading only. The identical string also means "I hope" and
"it would be nice if"; what tells them apart is **who acts** and **whether they can**.

## Formation

The advice reading is essentially a **verb** pattern, and the verb has to be something
the listener could actually decide to do.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain past + ら + いい | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}んだらいい |
| Verb | plain non-past + と + いい | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}むといい |
| Verb (る-verb) | plain past + ら + いい | {f|調|しら}べる → {f|調|しら}べたらいい |
| Verb (する) | したら + いい／すると + いい | {f|相談|そうだん}する → {f|相談|そうだん}したらいい |
| Polite | たらいいです／といいですよ | {f|休|やす}んだらいいですよ |
| Softened | たらいいと{f|思|おも}います | {f|休|やす}んだらいいと{f|思|おも}います |
| Softened further | たらいいんじゃないですか | {f|休|やす}んだらいいんじゃないですか |
| Asking for advice | どうしたらいいですか | |

<div class="note">

**Note** - there is no negative in this table on purpose. <code>{f|無理|むり}しないと
いい</code> does not mean "you shouldn't overdo it"; it is heard as a hope that you
won't. Negative advice belongs to <code>ないほうがいい</code>. This is the commonest
place where the advice reading quietly turns into the hope reading behind a learner's
back.

</div>

<pre>
{f|頭|あたま}が{f|痛|いた}いなら、この{f|薬|くすり}を<b>{f|飲|の}むといい</b>よ。
If your head hurts, take this - it works.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|悩|なや}まないで、{f|先生|せんせい}に<b>{f|相談|そうだん}したらいい</b>ですよ。
Don't sit there worrying on your own - go and talk to your teacher.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|漢字|かんじ}は{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|少|すこ}しずつ<b>{f|書|か}くといい</b>ですよ。
With kanji you're best off writing a little every day.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|土産|みやげ}なら、{f|駅|えき}の{f|地下|ちか}で<b>{f|買|か}ったらいい</b>と{f|思|おも}います。
If it's souvenirs you're after, I'd buy them in the basement floor at the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|迷|まよ}っているなら、いちど{f|見|み}に<b>{f|行|い}ってみたらいい</b>んじゃないですか。
If you're torn about it, why not just go and have a look once?
</pre>

<pre>

If polite language is your weak spot, start by copying the dialogue in dramas.

If your laptop is sluggish, try deleting the apps you don't use. It gets startlingly faster.

## Nuance & register

Because the pattern hides advice inside a conditional, it is gentler than
<code>～なさい</code> or the plain imperative, and slightly gentler than
<code>～たほうがいい</code>. It is the register of a helpful colleague, a friendly
teacher, a shop assistant who has decided to be kind to you.

`たらいい` and `といい` are close, with a small difference of angle. `たらいい` responds
to *this* person's situation - the conditional takes their circumstances as read.
`といい` states something closer to a general good practice, which is why it turns up so
often in teacherly and written advice: {f|辞書|じしょ}はときどき{f|引|ひ}くといい。 Both
are entirely ordinary in speech.

The pattern is still advice, and advice flows downhill in Japanese. To a friend or a
junior, {f|休|やす}んだらいいよ is warm. To your section head the same words are
presumptuous, and you climb the ladder of hedges instead:
<code>たらいいと{f|思|おも}います</code> → `たらいいんじゃないでしょうか` → dropping the pattern
altogether for <code>～てはいかがでしょうか</code>.

`たらいいでしょう` deserves a warning. It is grammatical, but it lands as distant and
lecturing - a teacher addressing a hall rather than a person addressing a person. In
conversation prefer `たらいいですよ`.

Two things do **not** happen. `いい` here is an い-adjective, so it never takes `だ`
(❌<code>たらいいだ</code>). And the pattern has no past form of its own: putting `いい`
into the past gives you `たらよかった`, which is regret, not advice.

## Contrast

### ～たらいい vs ～ばいい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ばいい` names a **sufficient** action: do that, and the matter is
settled. `たらいい` makes a **suggestion**: how about doing that? The first closes a
question, the second opens one.

</div>

<pre>
きっぷは{f|自動|じどう}{f|券売機|けんばいき}で<b>{f|買|か}えばいい</b>んです。
You buy the ticket from the machine - that's all there is to it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}があるなら、{f|窓口|まどぐち}で<b>{f|聞|き}いてみたらいい</b>ですよ。
If you've got time, you might try asking at the counter.
</pre>

Neither swap is ungrammatical, but the tone moves. `ばいい` on a suggestion can sound
like you are shutting the conversation down; `たらいい` on a fixed procedure sounds
oddly unsure of a thing everybody knows.

### ～たらいい vs ～たほうがいい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たほうがいい` weighs this course against the alternative and warns
you off the other one. `たらいい` does not weigh anything; it simply offers an idea.

</div>

<pre>
{f|夜中|よなか}に{f|一人|ひとり}であの{f|道|みち}を<b>{f|通|とお}らないほうがいい</b>よ。
You'd better not take that road alone at night.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}がかかってもいいなら、あの{f|道|みち}を<b>{f|通|とお}ったらいい</b>よ。
If you don't mind it taking longer, take that road.
</pre>

The first implies a risk; the second implies a choice. That is also why `たほうがいい`
has a natural negative and `たらいい` does not: warning somebody off is exactly the job
`たほうがいい` was built for.

### The advice reading vs the "I hope" reading

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if the listener could decide to do it, the sentence is advice. If
nobody in the room controls it, the sentence is a hope. Same words, and the test is
about the world, not the grammar.

</div>

<pre>
{f|早|はや}めに<b>{f|予約|よやく}したらいい</b>ですよ。
You'd do well to book early.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|席|せき}が<b>{f|空|あ}いているといい</b>ですね。
I hope there are seats free.
</pre>

Booking is up to the listener, so the first is advice. Whether seats are free is up to
nobody, so the second is a hope - and note that hopes take `ですね`, which invites the
listener to share the feeling, while advice takes `ですよ`, which hands them something
they did not have. The final particle is often the fastest clue.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|少|すこ}し{f|休|やす}むたらいいですよ。 → ✅ {f|少|すこ}し{f|休|やす}<b>んだら</b>いいですよ。
  `たら` attaches to the plain **past**. The dictionary form goes with `と`, not with
  `たら`.

- ❌ {f|早|はや}く{f|寝|ね}たらいいだ。 → ✅ {f|早|はや}く{f|寝|ね}たらいい。
  `いい` is an い-adjective and is already a complete predicate. No `だ` - in the polite
  form it becomes `いいです`.

- ❌ {f|無理|むり}しないといいですよ。（meaning "you shouldn't push yourself"） → ✅ {f|無理|むり}しない<b>ほうが</b>いいですよ。
  A negative clause in front of `といい` is read as a wish, so your advice turns into "I
  hope you don't", which is not what you meant.

- ❌ ({f|部長|ぶちょう}に)「この{f|方法|ほうほう}で{f|進|すす}めたらいいです。」 → ✅ 「この{f|方法|ほうほう}で{f|進|すす}めてはいかがでしょうか。」
  The grammar is fine; the direction is not. Advice aimed upwards needs a different
  frame, not just a `です` on the end.

## Practice

**1.** {f|日本語|にほんご}が{f|上手|じょうず}になりたいなら、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|声|こえ}に{f|出|だ}して____いいですよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|声|こえ}に{f|出|だ}して<b>{f|読|よ}んだら</b>いいですよ。（or <b>{f|読|よ}むと</b>いいですよ）

Reading aloud is something the listener can decide to do, so this is the advice
reading. Either conditional works; `と` sounds a little more like a general
recommendation.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っていないんですか。じゃあ、コンビニで（{f|買|か}えばいい・{f|買|か}ったらいい）ですよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are used, but <b>{f|買|か}えばいい</b>ですよ fits best.

The listener has a concrete problem with one obvious fix, and `ばいい` is the form that
names a sufficient solution. <code>{f|買|か}ったらいい</code> is not wrong, but it
frames an obvious remedy as a tentative idea.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If you're nervous, you should practise in front of a mirror."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|緊張|きんちょう}するなら、{f|鏡|かがみ}の{f|前|まえ}で<b>{f|練習|れんしゅう}したらいい</b>ですよ。

The action is fully under the listener's control, so this is advice rather than hope,
and `ですよ` is the particle that goes with handing somebody a new idea.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|風邪|かぜ}が{f|治|なお}らないなら、{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}まないといいですよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|風邪|かぜ}が{f|治|なお}らないなら、{f|病院|びょういん}に{f|行|い}ったらいいですよ。

<code>{f|飲|の}まないといい</code> would be a hope that the person avoids medicine,
which makes no sense as advice for a cold that won't clear. If you really do want
negative advice, the form is <code>{f|飲|の}まないほうがいい</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|明日|あした}、{f|早|はや}く<b>{f|来|き}たらいい</b>ですよ。
(b) {f|明日|あした}、{f|早|はや}く<b>{f|来|こ}られるといい</b>ですね。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is advice. Coming early is the listener's decision, and `ですよ` presents the idea
as something the speaker is giving them.

(b) is a hope. The potential form <code>{f|来|こ}られる</code> puts the outcome partly
outside the listener's hands - traffic, work, whatever - so the speaker can only wish
for it, and `ですね` invites the listener to wish along. Swapping the two particles
would make both sentences sound slightly off.

</details>
