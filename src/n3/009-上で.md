# ～上で

**Meaning** · after doing X - and on the strength of what X told you - do Y

<code>{f|上|うえ}で</code> (read うえで) is not a plain "after". It says the first action was
done **in order to find something out**, and the second action is the decision you
reached with that information in hand. It is a formal word: paperwork, meetings,
notices.

## Formation

The same word covers two jobs, and the verb form in front tells you which. **た-form
means "after"**; **dictionary form means "in the course of"**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (た-form) - after | た-form + {f|上|うえ}で | {f|調|しら}べる → {f|調|しら}べた{f|上|うえ}で |
| Action noun - after | Noun + の + {f|上|うえ}で | {f|相談|そうだん} → {f|相談|そうだん}の{f|上|うえ}で |
| Verb (dictionary) - in the course of | dictionary + {f|上|うえ}で | {f|暮|く}らす → {f|暮|く}らす{f|上|うえ}で |
| Noun - in the course of | Noun + の + {f|上|うえ}で | {f|仕事|しごと} → {f|仕事|しごと}の{f|上|うえ}で |
| Before a noun | ～{f|上|うえ}での + Noun | {f|検討|けんとう}した{f|上|うえ}での{f|判断|はんだん} |
| Formal notice style | ～の{f|上|うえ}、… | ご{f|記入|きにゅう}の{f|上|うえ}、お{f|出|だ}しください |
| ❌ て-form | ❌{f|調|しら}べて{f|上|うえ}で | ✅{f|調|しら}べた{f|上|うえ}で |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for the "after" sense, ask whether the first action **produces
information** the second action needs. {f|見|み}た{f|上|うえ}で{f|決|き}める works
because looking is what tells you how to decide. {f|歯|は}をみがいた{f|上|うえ}で{f|寝|ね}る
does not, because brushing your teeth informs nothing.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
よく{f|考|かんが}え<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>、お{f|返事|へんじ}します。
I'll give you my answer once I've thought it through.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|両親|りょうしん}と{f|相談|そうだん}し<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>{f|決|き}めます。
I'll decide after talking it over with my parents.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}を{f|見|み}<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>、{f|借|か}りるかどうか{f|決|き}めたいです。
I'd like to see the flat before deciding whether to rent it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|外国|がいこく}で{f|暮|く}ら<b>す{f|上|うえ}で</b>、{f|一番|いちばん}{f|苦労|くろう}するのは{f|言葉|ことば}です。
The hardest part of living abroad is the language.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|続|つづ}け<b>る{f|上|うえ}で</b>、{f|体力|たいりょく}は{f|欠|か}かせません。
Stamina is essential if you're going to keep doing this job.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|資料|しりょう}をお{f|読|よ}みになっ<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>、ご{f|質問|しつもん}をお{f|寄|よ}せください。
Please read the materials and then send us your questions.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工事|こうじ}の{f|費用|ひよう}を{f|正確|せいかく}に{f|計算|けいさん}し<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>、あらためてお{f|見積|みつ}もりをお{f|送|おく}りいたします。

</pre>

{f|皆|みな}さんのご{f|意見|いけん}をうかがっ<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>、{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}で{f|最終|さいしゅう}{f|案|あん}を{f|決|き}めたいと{f|思|おも}います。
I'd like to hear everyone's views first, and then settle the final proposal at next week's meeting.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a **formal** pattern. It belongs to written Japanese, business speech and
public notices, and it sounds stiff in a chat with a friend - where you would
simply say <code>{f|見|み}てから{f|決|き}める</code>. Using it well is one of the fastest ways
to make a work email sound like it was written by an adult.

In notices and forms the `で` is often dropped, leaving ～の{f|上|うえ}、 with a
comma: ご{f|確認|かくにん}の{f|上|うえ}、{f|送信|そうしん}してください。 That clipped form is
almost a fixed register marker; it appears on websites, application forms and
station signs, and never in speech.

Two particles slot in usefully. <code>{f|上|うえ}では</code> narrows the frame to one
domain - <code>{f|法律|ほうりつ}の{f|上|うえ}では{f|問題|もんだい}ありません</code>, "legally
speaking there's no problem". <code>{f|上|うえ}でも</code> adds "for that purpose too".

<div class="note">

**Note** - do not confuse this with <code>～{f|上|うえ}に</code>, which is a completely
different pattern meaning "and what's more":
この{f|店|みせ}は{f|安|やす}い{f|上|うえ}においしい。 Same kanji, `に` instead of `で`,
and it stacks reasons rather than ordering events. There is also
<code>～{f|上|うえ}は</code>, "now that (you have done X)", which is a step above N3.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～た{f|上|うえ}で vs ～てから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>てから</code> only says *later*. <code>{f|上|うえ}で</code> says
*later, and because of what the first step revealed*. Every
<code>{f|上|うえ}で</code> sentence could be <code>てから</code>; the reverse is not true.

</div>

<pre>
{f|手|て}を{f|洗|あら}ってから、ケーキを{f|作|つく}り{f|始|はじ}めた。
I washed my hands and then started making the cake.
</pre>

Washing your hands tells you nothing, so <code>{f|洗|あら}った{f|上|うえ}で</code> would be
strange. Now a case where the first step really does feed the second:

<pre>
アンケートの{f|結果|けっか}を{f|分析|ぶんせき}し<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|企画|きかく}を{f|立|た}てます。
We'll draw up the new plan after analysing the survey results.
</pre>

Register separates them too. <code>てから</code> is neutral and used at every level of
politeness; <code>{f|上|うえ}で</code> raises the whole sentence a notch.

### ～た{f|上|うえ}で vs ～た{f|後|あと}で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|後|あと}で</code> measures **time**: X finished, then Y.
<code>{f|上|うえ}で</code> measures **dependence**: Y is shaped by X.

</div>

<pre>
{f|食事|しょくじ}の{f|後|あと}で、{f|散歩|さんぽ}に{f|行|い}きました。
After the meal we went for a walk.
</pre>

Nothing about the meal determined the walk, so this is <code>{f|後|あと}で</code> and
only <code>{f|後|あと}で</code>. Compare:

<pre>
{f|医者|いしゃ}の{f|話|はなし}を{f|聞|き}い<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>、{f|手術|しゅじゅつ}を{f|受|う}けるかどうか{f|考|かんが}えます。
I'll think about whether to have the operation after hearing what the doctor says.
</pre>

Here <code>{f|後|あと}で</code> would be grammatical but flat; it would lose the point,
which is that the doctor's explanation is the basis of the decision. A second,
harder difference: <code>{f|後|あと}で</code> accepts events nobody controls - 
{f|台風|たいふう}が{f|過|す}ぎた{f|後|あと}で - while <code>{f|上|うえ}で</code> needs a
**deliberate** first action, so ❌{f|台風|たいふう}が{f|過|す}ぎた{f|上|うえ}で is wrong.

### ～る{f|上|うえ}で vs ～た{f|上|うえ}で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - dictionary form + <code>{f|上|うえ}で</code> means "in doing X / when
it comes to X" and points at a whole activity. た-form + <code>{f|上|うえ}で</code> means
"after X" and points at one completed step.

</div>

<pre>
{f|日本語|にほんご}を{f|勉強|べんきょう}す<b>る{f|上|うえ}で</b>、{f|辞書|じしょ}の{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}はとても{f|大切|たいせつ}です。
When it comes to studying Japanese, knowing how to use a dictionary matters a great deal.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日本語|にほんご}を{f|勉強|べんきょう}し<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>、{f|留学|りゅうがく}するかどうか{f|決|き}めます。
I'll study some Japanese first, and then decide whether to go abroad.
</pre>

The first sentence never happened and never will "happen" - it describes the
activity in general. The second describes a step you take and then finish. Getting
the two mixed up is the single most common error with this pattern, and the fix is
always the verb form.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|歯|は}をみがいた{f|上|うえ}で{f|寝|ね}ます。 → ✅ {f|歯|は}をみがいてから{f|寝|ね}ます。
  A routine sequence with no decision in it takes <code>てから</code>. Brushing your
  teeth does not give you information to act on.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った{f|上|うえ}で、{f|試合|しあい}が{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ったので、{f|試合|しあい}が{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。
  The first clause must be something somebody chose to do. Weather is not a
  deliberate act, and here the relation is cause, not preparation.

- ❌ {f|見積|みつ}もりを{f|見|み}て{f|上|うえ}で{f|返事|へんじ}します。 → ✅ {f|見積|みつ}もりを{f|見|み}た{f|上|うえ}で{f|返事|へんじ}します。
  <code>{f|上|うえ}</code> is a noun, so the clause in front of it ends in a plain form - 
  た or dictionary - never て.

- ❌ {f|会社|かいしゃ}で{f|働|はたら}いた{f|上|うえ}で{f|必要|ひつよう}な{f|知識|ちしき} → ✅ {f|会社|かいしゃ}で{f|働|はたら}く{f|上|うえ}で{f|必要|ひつよう}な{f|知識|ちしき}
  "Knowledge you need in order to work at a company" is the *in the course of*
  sense, which takes the dictionary form.

## Practice

**1.** {f|条件|じょうけん}をよく{f|確|たし}かめ____、{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}んでください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|条件|じょうけん}をよく{f|確|たし}かめ<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>、{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}んでください。

Checking the conditions is exactly the kind of deliberate, informing step this
pattern wants, and the application is the decision that follows from it. Note the
た-form: the checking has to be finished.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|安全|あんぜん}に{f|運転|うんてん}（する・した）{f|上|うえ}で、{f|気|き}をつけることは{f|何|なん}ですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|安全|あんぜん}に{f|運転|うんてん}<b>する</b>{f|上|うえ}で、{f|気|き}をつけることは{f|何|なん}ですか。

The question is about driving safely as an ongoing activity, not about something
finished, so it needs the dictionary form and the "in the course of" reading.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "We would like to make a decision after hearing the customer's opinion."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お{f|客様|きゃくさま}のご{f|意見|いけん}をうかがっ<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>、{f|決|き}めたいと{f|思|おも}います。

A business setting, a deliberate first step, and a decision that depends on it - 
all three signals point at <code>{f|上|うえ}で</code> rather than a plain
<code>てから</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|地震|じしん}が{f|起|お}きた{f|上|うえ}で、{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|地震|じしん}が{f|起|お}きたため、{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まりました。

An earthquake is not an action anyone performed to gather information, and the
trains stopping is a consequence rather than a considered decision. This sentence
needs a cause marker such as <code>ため</code> or <code>ので</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|全員|ぜんいん}の{f|意見|いけん}を{f|聞|き}い<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>{f|決|き}めます。
(b) {f|全員|ぜんいん}の{f|意見|いけん}を{f|聞|き}いてから{f|決|き}めます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) commits the speaker to letting those opinions shape the decision. It is the
sentence a manager uses to promise that the consultation is real, and its formal
tone suits a meeting or an email.

(b) only promises an order of events: opinions first, decision second. It is
perfectly polite but leaves open the possibility that the decision was made
already and the listening is a formality.

</details>
