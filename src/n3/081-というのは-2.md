# というのは - because

**Meaning** · opens the sentence that gives the reason for what you have just said - "the reason being that…"

You make a statement. You can hear the listener's silent "why?". <code>というのは</code>
starts a new sentence and answers it. Unlike `から` or `ので`, which live inside the
sentence they explain, this one stands at the head of its own - which is why it can carry
a long, unhurried reason that would sag if you tried to bolt it on.

## Formation

This <code>というのは</code> is a **sentence-opening connective**. It follows a full stop,
takes a comma after it, and the clause it introduces almost always closes on `からだ`,
`ためだ` or `のだ`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| After a finished sentence | …。というのは、…からだ。 | {f|行|い}けない。というのは、{f|熱|ねつ}があるからだ。 |
| Polite style | …。というのは、…からです。 | …{f|熱|ねつ}があるからです。 |
| With のだ instead of から | …。というのは、…のです。 | …{f|急|きゅう}に{f|熱|ねつ}が{f|出|で}たのです。 |
| Near-identical variant | というのも、…からだ | というのも、{f|熱|ねつ}があるからだ |
| Casual speech | っていうのは、…んだ | っていうのは、{f|熱|ねつ}があるんだ |
| Formal writing | なぜなら（ば）、…からである | - |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if you delete the reason clause, what is left must still be a complete,
sensible sentence. <code>というのは</code> is an afterthought by design; it cannot be the
grammatical backbone of anything.

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is a **considered** connective. Splitting the reason off into its own sentence buys
you a beat of pause, so it suits explanations you have thought about: declining an
invitation, justifying a decision, walking a reader through an argument. It is at home in
essays, reports, formal e-mail and prepared speech, and it appears in careful conversation
too - but in fast, casual talk people reach for `だって`, `から` or a bare `のだ` instead.

<code>というのも</code> is the same connective with `も` and is, if anything, slightly more
common in writing. Treat them as interchangeable at this level; `も` adds a faint sense of
"and there is this on top of it", which is why it often follows a reason already given.

Two small register facts. First, the polite version is fully polite - the ending carries
the politeness (<code>…からです</code>), while <code>というのは</code> itself is fixed and
never becomes <code>といいますのは</code> in ordinary speech. Second, dropping the
`からだ` at the end and simply stating the reason flat sounds abrupt in writing, though it
is normal in speech: 「{f|行|い}けない。というのは、{f|熱|ねつ}があってね。」

## Contrast

### というのは vs なぜなら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same slot, different formality. `なぜなら` is written, structured and a
little insistent; <code>というのは</code> is the version you can say out loud without
sounding like a textbook.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|方法|ほうほう}はすすめられない。<b>なぜなら</b>、コストが{f|高|たか}すぎるからである。
This approach cannot be recommended, because the cost is too high.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|方法|ほうほう}はあまりすすめません。<b>というのは</b>、コストが{f|高|たか}すぎるからです。
I wouldn't really recommend this approach - the cost is too high.
</pre>

`なぜなら` all but demands `からだ／からである` at the end; leaving it off feels unfinished.
<code>というのは</code> is looser about it. Note too that `なぜなら` announces a reason
before you have heard any of it, which is what makes it feel like an argument being built.

### というのは vs だって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だって` is the same move at the bottom of the register ladder, and it
carries a defensive tone: you are answering a criticism, not filling in background.

</div>

<pre>
{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|食|た}べちゃった。<b>だって</b>、おいしかったんだもん。
I ate the lot. Well - it was good!
</pre>

<pre>
{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|食|た}べてしまいました。<b>というのは</b>、{f|残|のこ}すと{f|失礼|しつれい}になると{f|思|おも}ったからです。
I finished everything, because I thought leaving food would be rude.
</pre>

`だって` is casual and reads as childish or petulant from an adult in a formal setting.
<code>というのは</code> is neutral. If you are explaining yourself to a superior, the
choice is not stylistic.

### The "because" というのは vs the "means" というのは

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - look at what sits **in front**. A finished sentence and a full stop →
"because". A bare noun or quoted word → "means". The endings confirm it: `…からだ` versus
`…ことだ`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っていったほうがいい。<b>というのは</b>、{f|午後|ごご}から{f|雨|あめ}になるからだ。
You'd better take an umbrella - it's going to rain from the afternoon.
</pre>

<pre>
「{f|夕立|ゆうだち}」<b>というのは</b>、{f|夏|なつ}の{f|午後|ごご}に{f|急|きゅう}に{f|降|ふ}る{f|雨|あめ}のことだ。
"Yūdachi" means the rain that falls suddenly on a summer afternoon.
</pre>

Nothing about the phrase itself distinguishes them; position and closing do all the work.
In speech, a clear pause before <code>というのは</code> also marks the "because" one.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|熱|ねつ}があるからというのは、{f|行|い}けません。 → ✅ {f|行|い}けません。というのは、{f|熱|ねつ}があるからです。
  The order is fixed: statement first, then <code>というのは</code>, then the reason.
  Putting the reason in front leaves the connective with nothing to attach back to.

- ❌ {f|行|い}けません。というのは、{f|熱|ねつ}があります。 → ✅ …{f|熱|ねつ}があるからです。
  In writing, the reason clause needs `からだ／からです`, `ためだ` or `のです` to close it.
  A bare statement leaves the sentence sounding cut off.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っているというのは、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}です。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っているので、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}です。
  Here the reason comes first and the result second - that is `ので`/`から` territory.
  <code>というのは</code> can only look backwards.

- ❌ {f|遅|おく}れてすみません。というのは、{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まったからです。（to a customer, in person）→ ✅ {f|遅|おく}れて{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません。{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まってしまいまして…
  Not ungrammatical, but announcing "the reason is" while apologising sounds like you are
  building a defence. Apologies usually run the reason in with `て` and trail off.

## Practice

**1.** {f|今日|きょう}は{f|外|そと}で{f|食|た}べましょう。____、{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}に{f|何|なに}も{f|残|のこ}っていないからです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

…。<b>というのは</b>、{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}に{f|何|なに}も{f|残|のこ}っていないからです。

The first sentence is complete and the second supplies its reason, closing on
<code>からです</code>. <code>というのも</code> would fit the same slot with almost no
change of meaning.

</details>

**2.** Choose the more natural connective for a written report: この{f|案|あん}は{f|採用|さいよう}できない。（だって・というのは）、{f|予算|よさん}を{f|大|おお}きく{f|超|こ}えているからだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

…（<b>というのは</b>）、{f|予算|よさん}を{f|大|おお}きく{f|超|こ}えているからだ。

`だって` belongs to casual spoken Japanese and sounds defensive, which is wrong for a
report. <code>というのは</code> - or the still more formal `なぜなら` - is what written
argument uses.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I gave up on the plan. The reason is that nobody else was interested."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

その{f|計画|けいかく}はあきらめました。<b>というのは</b>、ほかに{f|興味|きょうみ}を{f|持|も}つ{f|人|ひと}がいなかったからです。

Statement, full stop, connective, reason ending in <code>からです</code>. English "the
reason is that" maps onto this pattern almost exactly.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|明日|あした}は{f|休|やす}みます。というのは、{f|病院|びょういん}へ{f|行|い}きます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|休|やす}みます。というのは、{f|病院|びょういん}へ{f|行|い}くからです。

The reason clause has to be marked as a reason. Without <code>から</code> the second
sentence reads as a second plain announcement, and the connective is left explaining
nothing.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|今日|きょう}は{f|参加|さんか}できません。{f|子|こ}どもが{f|熱|ねつ}を{f|出|だ}したので。
(b) {f|今日|きょう}は{f|参加|さんか}できません。というのは、{f|子|こ}どもが{f|熱|ねつ}を{f|出|だ}したからです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) tucks the reason in behind the statement with a trailing `ので`. It is soft, spoken,
and slightly apologetic - the classic way to excuse yourself without making a production
of it.

(b) formally announces that an explanation is coming, then delivers it. It is clearer and
more deliberate, and it suits writing or a situation where you want the reason on the
record. Said quickly in casual conversation it can sound a shade over-prepared.

</details>
