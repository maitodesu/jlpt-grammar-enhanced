# めったに

**Meaning** · hardly ever; rarely - and only ever with a negative verb

`めったに` says that something happens so seldom it is almost worth remarking on.
The frequency it describes is very low - lower than あまり, close to
ほとんど - and the word is grammatically incomplete without a negative to attach to.

## Usage

`めったに` is an adverb and goes in front of the predicate. The rule that matters:

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `めったに` **requires a negative predicate**. めったに～ない,
めったに～ません. There is no affirmative version.

</div>

❌めったに{f|行|い}く and ❌めったに{f|見|み}ます are not "rarely go" - they are not
Japanese at all. The negative carries the frequency; `めったに` only tells you how
far down the scale it goes.

- {f|外食|がいしょく}はめったにしません。
- {f|彼|かれ}はめったに{f|怒|おこ}らない。
- {f|雪|ゆき}はめったに{f|降|ふ}りませんでした。 - past tense works normally.

One fixed expression is worth learning as a unit: <code>めったにない</code> used before
a noun means "rare, exceptional" - めったにないチャンス, "a chance you don't get
twice". The same negative rule is still doing the work; it has simply become a
noun-modifier.

In kanji it is {f|滅多|めった}に, an ateji spelling that appears in print, but the
kana is more usual.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|父|ちち}は<b>めったに</b>{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}ません。
My father hardly ever watches films.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|辺|へん}では<b>めったに</b>{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}らない。
It almost never snows around here.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|忙|いそが}しくて、{f|平日|へいじつ}に{f|家族|かぞく}そろって{f|夕食|ゆうしょく}をとることは<b>めったに</b>ない。
We're so busy that the whole family is hardly ever at dinner together on a weekday.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|寡黙|かもく}な{f|部長|ぶちょう}が<b>めったに</b>{f|見|み}せない{f|笑顔|えがお}を{f|見|み}せた。
Our famously silent department head produced a smile of a kind he almost never shows.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral: it appears in ます-form and plain form, in speech and in writing,
with no change. It is not marked for age or gender.

What it does carry is a faint note of *comment*. Saying めったに{f|来|こ}ない tends
to imply that the rarity is notable - surprising, disappointing, or precious.
When you simply want to report a low frequency with no colour at all, あまり～ない
is flatter and safer.

Because the frequency is so low, `めったに` and a stated number sit badly together.
{f|月|つき}に{f|一回|いっかい}めったに{f|行|い}かない is contradictory: if you can
count them, they are not "hardly ever". Use たまに{f|行|い}きます for a countable
occasional habit.

Note finally that `めったに` describes frequency, not degree. It cannot modify an
adjective: ❌めったに{f|高|たか}くない is wrong for "not very expensive"; that is
あまり{f|高|たか}くない.

## Contrast

### めったに vs あまり○○ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both need a negative, but `あまり` measures **degree or
moderate frequency**, and `めったに` measures **very low frequency only**.

</div>

<pre>
お{f|酒|さけ}は<b>あまり</b>{f|飲|の}みません。
I don't drink much.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|酒|さけ}は<b>めったに</b>{f|飲|の}みません。
I almost never drink.
</pre>

The first speaker probably has a beer now and then; the second is close to
teetotal. And because `あまり` can also scale degree, it works with adjectives - 
あまり{f|辛|から}くない - where `めったに` cannot. The everyday positive counterpart
of both is たまに, "occasionally", which takes an ordinary affirmative verb:
たまに{f|飲|の}みます.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}はめったに{f|外食|がいしょく}します。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}はめったに{f|外食|がいしょく}し<b>ません</b>。
  The adverb cannot appear with an affirmative predicate. If you mean "I eat out
  occasionally", the word you want is たまに.

- ❌ この{f|店|みせ}はめったに{f|高|たか}くない。 → ✅ この{f|店|みせ}は<b>あまり</b>{f|高|たか}くない。
  `めったに` counts occasions, not degrees, so it cannot modify an adjective.

- ❌ {f|年|ねん}に{f|二回|にかい}ぐらいめったに{f|会|あ}わない。 → ✅ {f|年|ねん}に{f|二回|にかい}ぐらいしか{f|会|あ}わない。
  Naming a frequency and then calling it "hardly ever" pulls in two directions.
  Pick one: either the number, or the adverb.

## Practice

**1.** {f|忙|いそが}しいので、{f|旅行|りょこう}には____{f|行|い}き____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|旅行|りょこう}には<b>めったに</b>{f|行|い}き<b>ません</b>。

The two blanks are a single frame - the adverb is only half a statement until the
negative arrives at the end. Filling the first blank and leaving an affirmative
verb would be ungrammatical rather than merely odd.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: {f|甘|あま}いものは（めったに・あまり）{f|好|す}きじゃない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|甘|あま}いものは<b>あまり</b>{f|好|す}きじゃない。

<code>{f|好|す}き</code> is a state, not a repeated event, so there are no occasions to
count. `あまり` scales the degree of liking, which is what the sentence needs.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|田中|たなか}さんはめったに{f|遅刻|ちこく}しない。
(b) {f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|遅刻|ちこく}しない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (b) is a flat description of a reliable person. Sentence (a) admits that
it has happened once or twice and, by pointing at how seldom it happens, quietly
turns the remark into praise - or, depending on the conversation, into a way of
saying that today's lateness is out of character.

</details>
