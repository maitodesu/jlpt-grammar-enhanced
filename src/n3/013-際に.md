# ～際に

**Meaning** · at the time of; on the occasion of - the formal word for "when"

<code>{f|際|さい}に</code> (read さいに) does the job of <code>{f|時|とき}に</code>, but it belongs
to a different world: notices, manuals, contracts, announcements and careful
business speech. It also asks for a real **occasion** - something that takes
place - rather than any old state of affairs.

## Formation

<code>{f|際|さい}</code> is a noun, so the clause in front of it takes a plain form, and a
noun attaches with `の`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (dictionary) | dictionary + {f|際|さい}に | {f|利用|りよう}する → {f|利用|りよう}する{f|際|さい}に |
| Verb (た-form) | た-form + {f|際|さい}に | {f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}む → {f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}んだ{f|際|さい}に |
| Event noun | Noun + の + {f|際|さい}に | {f|入学|にゅうがく} → {f|入学|にゅうがく}の{f|際|さい}に |
| With は - "in the event of" | ～{f|際|さい}は | お{f|帰|かえ}りの{f|際|さい}は |
| Before a noun | ～{f|際|さい}の + Noun | {f|使用|しよう}の{f|際|さい}の{f|注意|ちゅうい} |
| に dropped, notice style | ～{f|際|さい}、… | ご{f|入場|にゅうじょう}の{f|際|さい}、… |
| ❌ い-adjective | ❌{f|忙|いそが}しい{f|際|さい}に | ✅{f|忙|いそが}しい{f|時|とき}に |
| ❌ Ongoing state | ❌{f|子|こ}どもの{f|際|さい}に | ✅{f|子|こ}どもの{f|時|とき}に |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - two tests. Is the sentence formal enough that
<code>{f|時|とき}</code> would sound too casual? And is the clause an **event** rather
than a state? Both must be yes. Adjectives never pass the second test.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
ご{f|利用|りよう}の<b>{f|際|さい}は</b>、こちらのボタンをお{f|押|お}しください。
Please press this button when using the machine.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|降|お}りの<b>{f|際|さい}は</b>、{f|足元|あしもと}にご{f|注意|ちゅうい}ください。
Please watch your step as you get off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みの<b>{f|際|さい}に</b>、{f|身分証明書|みぶんしょうめいしょ}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。
You will need identification when you apply.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|面接|めんせつ}を{f|受|う}けた<b>{f|際|さい}に</b>、{f|給料|きゅうりょう}のことも{f|聞|き}いておきました。
I asked about the salary as well when I had the interview.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|海外|かいがい}へ{f|出張|しゅっちょう}する<b>{f|際|さい}には</b>、{f|保険|ほけん}に{f|入|はい}っておいたほうがいい。
When you travel abroad on business, you'd do well to take out insurance.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|非常|ひじょう}の<b>{f|際|さい}は</b>、エレベーターを{f|使|つか}わずに{f|階段|かいだん}をご{f|利用|りよう}ください。
In an emergency, please use the stairs rather than the lift.
</pre>

<pre>

<pre>
{f|退職|たいしょく}の<b>{f|際|さい}に</b>お{f|世話|せわ}になった{f|方々|かたがた}に、{f|一人|ひとり}ずつ{f|手紙|てがみ}を{f|書|か}きました。
I wrote individually to everyone who helped me when I left the company.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a **formal** word and it never stops being one. Put it in a conversation
about breakfast and it sounds like a train announcement. The natural homes for it
are: station and shop announcements, instruction manuals, application forms,
company rules, and speech where you are addressing a customer or a superior.

It pairs constantly with honorific prefixes - ご{f|利用|りよう}の{f|際|さい}、
お{f|帰|かえ}りの{f|際|さい}、ご{f|来館|らいかん}の{f|際|さい} - because the same
register that wants <code>{f|際|さい}</code> also wants お／ご. If you are writing a
notice for customers, that combination is the default.

The particle after it shifts the feel slightly. <code>{f|際|さい}に</code> points at a
moment. <code>{f|際|さい}は</code> sets up a condition and is the form you see in
warnings - "should this happen, do that". <code>{f|際|さい}には</code> is both at once
and is very common in careful writing. In printed notices the particle often
disappears altogether, leaving a comma: ご{f|記入|きにゅう}の{f|際|さい}、
{f|黒|くろ}のペンをお{f|使|つか}いください。

<div class="note">

**Note** - a close relative, <code>～に{f|際|さい}して</code>, means "on the occasion of"
in an even weightier way and belongs to N2:
{f|開会|かいかい}に{f|際|さい}して{f|一言|ひとこと}ご{f|挨拶|あいさつ}{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます。
It marks ceremonies and turning points, not everyday procedures.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～{f|際|さい}に vs ～{f|時|とき}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|時|とき}</code> takes anything: verbs, adjectives, nouns,
states, trivia. <code>{f|際|さい}</code> takes only **events**, and only in formal
contexts. Everywhere <code>{f|際|さい}</code> works, <code>{f|時|とき}</code> also works; the
reverse fails constantly.

</div>

<pre>
{f|小|ちい}さい{f|時|とき}、よく{f|祖父|そふ}の{f|家|いえ}に{f|泊|と}まった。
When I was small I often stayed at my grandfather's house.
</pre>

An adjective and a childhood state - <code>{f|際|さい}</code> is impossible here on two
counts. Compare a procedure described to a customer:

<pre>
{f|商品|しょうひん}を{f|返品|へんぴん}される<b>{f|際|さい}は</b>、レシートをお{f|持|も}ちください。
When returning an item, please bring your receipt.
</pre>

There <code>{f|時|とき}</code> would be grammatical but would flatten the register; the
notice would read as if a friend had scribbled it. The tense rule you learned for
<code>{f|時|とき}</code> carries over unchanged: dictionary form for "before/as you do
it", た-form for "after you have done it".

### ～{f|際|さい}に vs ～{f|場合|ばあい}は

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|際|さい}</code> assumes the event **will** happen and tells
you what to do at that moment. <code>{f|場合|ばあい}</code> says "if it should turn out
that…" and branches between possibilities.

</div>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}の{f|場合|ばあい}は、{f|体育館|たいいくかん}で{f|行|おこな}います。
If it rains, it will be held in the gym.
</pre>

Rain may or may not come, and the sentence exists to cover the branch - that is
<code>{f|場合|ばあい}</code>, and <code>{f|際|さい}</code> would be wrong because rain is not an
occasion anyone attends. Now something that is definitely going to happen:

<pre>
ご{f|入場|にゅうじょう}の<b>{f|際|さい}に</b>、チケットを{f|拝見|はいけん}いたします。
We will check your ticket as you enter.
</pre>

Everyone entering will enter, so there is no branch to state. A quick diagnostic:
if the English wants "if", think <code>{f|場合|ばあい}</code>; if it wants "when", think
<code>{f|際|さい}</code>. Both are formal, so register does not separate them.

### ～{f|際|さい}に vs ～た{f|上|うえ}で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both live in the same formal register, but
<code>{f|際|さい}に</code> locates something **at the time of** an event, while
<code>た{f|上|うえ}で</code> puts it **after** the event and makes it depend on the
result.

</div>

<pre>
お{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みの<b>{f|際|さい}に</b>、ご{f|希望|きぼう}の{f|日|ひ}をお{f|書|か}きください。
Please write your preferred date when you apply.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}み{f|内容|ないよう}を{f|確認|かくにん}した{f|上|うえ}で、ご{f|連絡|れんらく}いたします。
We will contact you once we have checked the details of your application.
</pre>

In the first, the writing happens during the application. In the second, the
contact happens afterwards *because of* what the check revealed. Swapping them
produces sentences that are still grammatical but say something the writer did
not mean, which is why forms and letters keep the two carefully apart.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}の{f|際|さい}に、アルバイトをたくさんしました。 → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}の{f|時|とき}に、アルバイトをたくさんしました。
  "Being a student" is a state that lasted for years, not an occasion.
  <code>{f|際|さい}</code> needs a bounded event.

- ❌ {f|眠|ねむ}い{f|際|さい}には、コーヒーを{f|飲|の}みます。 → ✅ {f|眠|ねむ}い{f|時|とき}には、コーヒーを{f|飲|の}みます。
  Adjectives cannot modify <code>{f|際|さい}</code> at all. This is the most reliable
  formal test of the two words.

- ❌ ゲームをする{f|際|さい}に、いつも{f|夜更|よふ}かししてしまう。 → ✅ ゲームをする{f|時|とき}に、いつも{f|夜更|よふ}かししてしまう。
  Grammatical, but the register clashes: a casual admission in the vocabulary of
  a safety notice. Keep <code>{f|際|さい}</code> for formal writing and speech.

- ❌ {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|く}る{f|際|さい}は、{f|授業|じゅぎょう}は{f|休|やす}みになります。 → ✅ {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|く}た{f|場合|ばあい}は、{f|授業|じゅぎょう}は{f|休|やす}みになります。
  The sentence sets out a contingency, so it needs <code>{f|場合|ばあい}</code>.
  <code>{f|際|さい}</code> would presuppose that the typhoon is coming.

## Practice

**1.** お{f|支払|しはら}いの____、カードもご{f|利用|りよう}いただけます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お{f|支払|しはら}いの<b>{f|際|さい}は</b>、カードもご{f|利用|りよう}いただけます。

An event noun with an honorific prefix, addressed to a customer - the natural
home of <code>{f|際|さい}</code>. `は` suits it because the sentence states a standing
policy rather than one moment.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|大学|だいがく}を{f|受|う}ける（{f|際|さい}に・{f|時|とき}に）、{f|願書|がんしょ}と{f|写真|しゃしん}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。 Which fits an official admissions leaflet, and why?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|大学|だいがく}を{f|受|う}ける<b>{f|際|さい}に</b>、{f|願書|がんしょ}と{f|写真|しゃしん}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。

Both are grammatical, since sitting an exam is a genuine event. The leaflet wants
the formal register, and <code>{f|際|さい}</code> supplies it;
<code>{f|時|とき}</code> would read as spoken advice from a friend.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Please contact the front desk when you leave the hotel."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ホテルをお{f|出|で}になる<b>{f|際|さい}は</b>、フロントにご{f|連絡|れんらく}ください。

A guest, a notice, and a definite event - three reasons for <code>{f|際|さい}</code>.
The honorific <code>お{f|出|で}になる</code> matches the register the word brings with
it.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|若|わか}い{f|際|さい}に、もっと{f|旅行|りょこう}しておけばよかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|若|わか}い{f|時|とき}に、もっと{f|旅行|りょこう}しておけばよかった。

<code>{f|際|さい}</code> cannot follow an い-adjective, and "being young" is a stretch of
life rather than an occasion. The sentence is also a private regret, which is not
the register this word lives in.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}しの<b>{f|際|さい}に</b>、{f|住所|じゅうしょ}の{f|変更|へんこう}をお{f|届|とど}けください。
(b) {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}す{f|場合|ばあい}は、{f|住所|じゅうしょ}の{f|変更|へんこう}をお{f|届|とど}けください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) treats the move as something that is going to happen and tells you what to do
at that point. It reads like an instruction to someone who has already announced
they are moving.

(b) treats moving as one possibility among others and covers it in advance. It
reads like a clause in a set of rules that also covers people who never move at
all.

</details>
