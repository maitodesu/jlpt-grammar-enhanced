# ～としたら / ～とすれば

**Meaning** · supposing that A is true, then B - a premise granted for the sake of working out what follows

The `と` here is the quotative `と`, and `する` is "take it as". <code>～としたら</code>
literally means *if we take it that…*. It does not report a condition on its way to
happening; it **grants a proposition** and asks the listener to see what falls out of
it. That is why the main clause is nearly always a judgement - a deduction, an
estimate, a question, a "then there's nothing for it but…" - rather than a plan or an
instruction.

## Formation

Everything goes into its plain form and takes `と`. Nouns and な-adjectives need `だ`
in front - the opposite of `なら`, which forbids it.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain non-past + としたら／とすれば | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}くとしたら |
| Verb (past) | た-form + としたら | {f|落|お}とした → {f|落|お}としたとしたら |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + としたら | {f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わない → {f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わないとしたら |
| い-adjective | plain form + としたら（no だ） | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}いとしたら |
| な-adjective | stem + **だ** + としたら | {f|本当|ほんとう} → {f|本当|ほんとう}だとしたら |
| Noun | Noun + **だ** + としたら | {f|嘘|うそ} → {f|嘘|うそ}だとしたら |
| Explanatory | plain form + のだとしたら | {f|知|し}っている → {f|知|し}っているのだとしたら |
| Variants | とすると／となると | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|く}るとすると |
| Polite (in a clause) | としましたら／といたしますと | {f|事実|じじつ}だ → {f|事実|じじつ}だとしましたら |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the adverbs もし, {f|仮|かり}に and {f|万一|まんいち} sit naturally in front of
this pattern and announce the supposition early: {f|仮|かり}に{f|勝|か}ったとしたら…
They are optional, but they make a long sentence much easier to follow.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}から{f|行|い}く<b>としたら</b>、{f|何時|なんじ}に{f|着|つ}きますか。
If we were to set off now, what time would we get there?
</pre>

<pre>
これが{f|本当|ほんとう}だ<b>とすれば</b>、{f|大|おお}きな{f|問題|もんだい}です。
If this is true, then it's a serious problem.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|全部|ぜんぶ}やる<b>としたら</b>、{f|三日|みっか}はかかるだろう。
If I had to do the whole thing alone, it would take a good three days.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|仮|かり}に{f|宝|たから}くじが{f|当|あ}たった<b>としたら</b>、{f|何|なに}に{f|使|つか}いますか。
Supposing you won the lottery, what would you spend it on?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}が{f|来|こ}ない<b>とすれば</b>、{f|司会|しかい}は{f|私|わたし}がやるしかない。

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

If I were going to study abroad, I'd still want somewhere English gets you by.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}で{f|新幹線|しんかんせん}が{f|止|と}まっている<b>としたら</b>、{f|明日|あした}の{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|延期|えんき}するほかないでしょう。
If the bullet trains are down because of the typhoon, there'll be nothing for it but to postpone tomorrow's meeting.
</pre>

If we're to take this on under the current terms, we'd need two more people on it.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The family is register-neutral in the sense that no member is rude or childish, but
the three are not evenly distributed. <code>としたら</code> is the everyday spoken
member and the one that handles frankly unreal suppositions - 
{f|生|う}まれ{f|変|か}わったとしたら, {f|私|わたし}が{f|社長|しゃちょう}だとしたら.
<code>とすれば</code> leans towards written argument, reports and analysis, where it
introduces an inference the writer is prepared to defend. <code>とすると</code> tends to
mark a consequence the speaker has just this second worked out, and often carries a
small "ah - then…" in it.

Because the clause is subordinate, it stays plain even in the politest sentence:
{f|事実|じじつ}だとすれば、{f|大変|たいへん}{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございません。 If you
want the politeness to reach into the clause itself - service-counter Japanese, a
careful reply to a superior - the forms are <code>としましたら</code> and, at the top of
the scale, <code>といたしますと</code>. These are common at a bank or a travel desk and
sound stiff anywhere else.

One restriction is worth internalising. The main clause is where the **speaker's
judgement** goes: だろう, はずだ, ことになる, しかない, ～たい, or a question. A bare
statement of scheduled fact does not belong there, and neither does a request. If
what you mean is "let me know once you get home", the sentence wants `たら`, not
`としたら` - you are not supposing anything, you are waiting for something.

Finally, `としたら` freely takes a **past** clause in front without referring to past
time: {f|落|お}としたとしたら、どこだろう。 The `た` marks the supposed situation as
already complete at the moment you are reasoning about it, exactly as it does with
`たら`.

## Contrast

### ～としたら vs ～たら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たら` supposes an **event** that could actually turn up; `としたら`
supposes a **proposition** so that you can reason from it. If you could replace the
clause with "assuming that…", you want `としたら`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}に<b>{f|着|つ}いたら</b>{f|電話|でんわ}してね。
Give me a call when you get to the station.
</pre>

That one cannot take `としたら`: arriving is not a hypothesis, it is the plan. Swap in
a genuine supposition and the pattern comes back:

<pre>
{f|終電|しゅうでん}に{f|乗|の}り{f|遅|おく}れた<b>としたら</b>、{f|彼女|かのじょ}はどこで{f|泊|と}まるつもりだろう。
Supposing she did miss the last train, where is she planning to stay?
</pre>

The same split explains why `としたら` sounds odd with an instruction after it. You
cannot act on a premise that is only being entertained.

### ～としたら vs ～なら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なら` picks up something the other person has just put on the
table and answers it, usually with advice or an offer. `としたら` may suppose something
nobody has claimed, and answers itself with a conclusion.

</div>

<pre>
{f|京都|きょうと}に<b>{f|行|い}くなら</b>、{f|朝|あさ}{f|早|はや}く{f|出|で}たほうがいいよ。
If you're going to Kyoto, you'd do better to leave early.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|京都|きょうと}に<b>{f|行|い}くとしたら</b>、{f|日帰|ひがえ}りでは{f|無理|むり}だろうね。
If one were to go to Kyoto, a day trip probably wouldn't work.
</pre>

The first is aimed at a person with a plan; the second weighs an idea. Note also the
form: `なら` refuses `だ` ({f|本当|ほんとう}なら) while `としたら` requires it
({f|本当|ほんとう}だとしたら). They look like a pair and conjugate as opposites.

### ～としたら vs ～とすれば vs ～とすると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - all three grant a premise. `としたら` is the freest and most
spoken; `とすれば` is the reasoning one and dominates writing; `とすると` presents the
consequence as something that simply falls out, and often marks a realisation.

</div>

<pre>
もし{f|私|わたし}が{f|部長|ぶちょう}だ<b>としたら</b>、まずこの{f|制度|せいど}を{f|変|か}えますね。
If I were the manager, the first thing I'd change is this system.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|説明|せつめい}が{f|正|ただ}しい<b>とすれば</b>、{f|原因|げんいん}は{f|一|ひと}つしか{f|考|かんが}えられない。
If this account is correct, only one cause is conceivable.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|木村|きむら}さんがもう{f|出|で}た<b>とすると</b>、さっき{f|廊下|ろうか}ですれ{f|違|ちが}ったのかな。
If Kimura's already left, then maybe that was him I passed in the corridor.
</pre>

<div class="note">

**Note** - `となると` is a fourth cousin and is *not* interchangeable. It takes a
situation that has genuinely arisen and asks what now becomes of things:
{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|出張|しゅっちょう}となると、{f|準備|じゅんび}が{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わない。
Where `としたら` supposes, `となると` concedes.

</div>

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}としたら、{f|中止|ちゅうし}です。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}<b>だ</b>としたら、{f|中止|ちゅうし}です。
  Nouns and な-adjectives take `だ` before `と`. `なら` is the one that drops it, and
  learners routinely apply the wrong half of the pair.

- ❌ {f|安|やす}い<b>だ</b>とすれば{f|買|か}います。 → ✅ {f|安|やす}いとすれば{f|買|か}います。
  い-adjectives are already complete predicates. No `だ`.

- ❌ {f|家|いえ}に{f|着|つ}いたとしたら、{f|連絡|れんらく}してください。 → ✅ {f|家|いえ}に{f|着|つ}いたら、{f|連絡|れんらく}してください。
  Arriving home is expected, not supposed, and the main clause is a request. Both
  point away from `としたら` and towards plain `たら`.

- ❌ {f|春|はる}になるとしたら、{f|桜|さくら}が{f|咲|さ}きます。 → ✅ {f|春|はる}になると、{f|桜|さくら}が{f|咲|さ}きます。
  A regularity that always holds is `と`. Nothing is being supposed.

## Practice

**1.** {f|仮|かり}に{f|明日|あした}{f|大雪|おおゆき}____、{f|学校|がっこう}はどうなりますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|大雪|おおゆき}<b>だとしたら</b>（or <b>だとすれば</b>）

The clause ends in a noun, so `だ` is obligatory before `と`. The main clause is a
question about consequences, which is exactly the slot this pattern was built for.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|荷物|にもつ}が{f|届|とど}い（たら・たとしたら）、すぐ{f|開|あ}けてみてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|荷物|にもつ}が{f|届|とど}い<b>たら</b>、すぐ{f|開|あ}けてみてください。

The parcel is on its way - this is an expected event, not a hypothesis - and the main
clause is a request. `としたら` refuses both of those.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If this report is accurate, we'll have to change the plan."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|報告|ほうこく}が{f|正|ただ}しい<b>とすれば</b>、{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|変|か}えなければなりません。

`とすれば` is the reasoning member of the family and fits a written or workplace
inference. The main clause states what necessarily follows, which is the natural
partner for this pattern.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}が{f|犯人|はんにん}としたら、どうして{f|逃|に}げなかったんだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|犯人|はんにん}<b>だとしたら</b>、どうして{f|逃|に}げなかったんだろう。

<code>{f|犯人|はんにん}</code> is a noun, so `だ` has to appear. Everything else about the
sentence is right: a supposition followed by a puzzled question is a textbook use.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|留学|りゅうがく}する<b>なら</b>、アメリカがいいですよ。
(b) {f|留学|りゅうがく}する<b>としたら</b>、アメリカがいいですね。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) responds to a plan the listener has already announced. You have heard that they
intend to study abroad, and you are advising them where to go - hence the
listener-directed `よ`.

(b) entertains the idea without attributing it to anyone. The speaker is weighing a
hypothetical, quite possibly about themselves, and `ね` invites the listener to weigh
it too. Nobody has necessarily decided anything.

</details>
