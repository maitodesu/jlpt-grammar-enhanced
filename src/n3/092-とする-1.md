# ～(よ)うとする - try to

**Meaning** · make a move to do something - the attempt itself, with the result left hanging

The volitional form carries the will; `とする` turns that will into a physical
move. <code>～(よ)うとする</code> photographs the effort, not the outcome - and
Japanese only bothers to photograph the effort when the outcome was not the
obvious one. That is why so many of these sentences end in `が` or `けれど`.

## Formation

The volitional is frozen. `する` at the end is an ordinary verb and carries all the
tense and politeness for the whole phrase.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | volitional + とする | {f|押|お}す → {f|押|お}そうとする |
| Verb (る-verb) | volitional + とする | {f|逃|に}げる → {f|逃|に}げようとする |
| Verb (する) | しよう + とする | {f|説明|せつめい}する → {f|説明|せつめい}しようとする |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | こよう + とする | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ようとする |
| Attempt already made | ～(よ)うとした | {f|押|お}そうとした |
| Attempt that ran into something | ～(よ)うとしたが／けれど | {f|押|お}そうとしたが、{f|重|おも}かった |
| Attempt plus its consequence | ～(よ)うとして + clause | {f|押|お}そうとして、{f|腰|こし}を{f|痛|いた}めた |
| Polite | する → します | {f|押|お}そうとしました |
| Even if you try | ～(よ)うとしても | {f|押|お}そうとしても |

<div class="note">

**Note** - う-verb and る-verb are the same categories as {f|五段|ごだん} (Group 1)
and {f|一段|いちだん} (Group 2). The only stem to memorise here is <code>{f|来|く}る</code>, whose
volitional is こよう - <code>{f|来|こ}ようとした</code>, never くよう.

</div>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>
{f|逃|に}げた{f|鳥|とり}を<b>つかまえようとして</b>、{f|木|き}に{f|登|のぼ}りました。

</pre>

However hard I try to remember, that one person's name just won't come to me.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The single most useful fact: **in the past tense, the attempt normally failed.**
If you tried and got there, Japanese reports the arrival - 
<code>{f|窓|まど}を{f|閉|し}めました</code> - and says nothing about trying. So
<code>{f|閉|し}めようとしました</code> tells your listener the window is still
open. English "I tried to" is often modest understatement for success; this
pattern is not.

The `て`-form is where the pattern earns its keep at N3.
<code>～(よ)うとして</code> plus a following clause says *the attempt is what
caused what came next*: {f|拾|ひろ}おうとして{f|指|ゆび}を{f|切|き}った - the
cutting happened in the course of the reaching. This is the natural way to narrate
a small accident, and it is far more common in speech than the textbook
<code>～(よ)うとしたが</code>.

The subject has to have a will. That is what separates this sense from the "about
to" sense, where a typhoon or a sunset can be the subject. Here, only a creature
that can decide something gets to try.

For **your own standing effort** the plain non-past is wrong:
❌<code>{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|歩|ある}こうとしています</code>. A policy you are
keeping up is <code>～ようにしている</code>. The plain non-past
<code>～(よ)うとする</code> is comfortable in general statements about people - 
{f|人|ひと}は{f|楽|らく}な{f|道|みち}を{f|選|えら}ぼうとする - where it describes a
tendency rather than one person's morning.

Register is neutral; `する` takes whatever politeness the sentence needs. In very
casual speech the whole thing is often replaced by
<code>～(し)かけた</code> or by <code>{f|一応|いちおう}やってみた</code>, so if
you hear <code>{f|開|あ}けようとしたんだけど</code> in conversation, note that it
already sounds a shade more deliberate than the alternatives.

## Contrast

### ～(よ)うとする vs ～ようと{f|思|おも}う

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～ようと{f|思|おも}う</code> happens in your head;
<code>～(よ)うとする</code> happens with your hands. The first is a decision, the
second is a movement.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来年|らいねん}、{f|車|くるま}を<b>{f|買|か}おうと{f|思|おも}っています</b>。
I'm thinking of buying a car next year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|車|くるま}を<b>{f|買|か}おうとした</b>ら、{f|貯金|ちょきん}が{f|足|た}りませんでした。
I went to buy a car and found I didn't have enough saved.
</pre>

The first sentence is compatible with never going near a dealership. The second
one puts you in front of the price tag. This is also why
<code>～ようと{f|思|おも}う</code> is the polite way to announce a plan and
<code>～(よ)うとする</code> is not: telling your boss
<code>{f|辞|や}めようとしています</code> sounds like you are halfway out of the
building.

### ～(よ)うとする vs ～てみる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are "try", but they doubt different things.
<code>～(よ)うとする</code> doubts whether the action can happen at all.
`～てみる` takes the action for granted and doubts what it will be *like*.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しいアプリを<b>{f|使|つか}ってみました</b>が、{f|前|まえ}のほうが{f|便利|べんり}でした。
I gave the new app a go, but the old one was handier.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しいアプリを<b>{f|使|つか}おうとしました</b>が、{f|登録|とうろく}できませんでした。
I tried to use the new app, but I couldn't even register.
</pre>

In the first the app ran. In the second it never opened. Because they are about
different things, they stack quite happily:
<code>{f|使|つか}ってみようとした</code> - "I made an attempt at giving it a go."

### ～(よ)うとする vs ～ようにする

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～(よ)うとする</code> is one moment of trying.
<code>～ようにする</code> is a rule you have set yourself and are keeping to.

</div>

<pre>
エレベーターを<b>{f|使|つか}わないようにしています</b>。
I make a point of not using the lift.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|階段|かいだん}を<b>{f|上|のぼ}ろうとしました</b>が、{f|足|あし}が{f|痛|いた}くて{f|無理|むり}でした。
I tried to go up the stairs, but my leg hurt too much.
</pre>

Anything with <code>{f|毎日|まいにち}</code>, <code>{f|最近|さいきん}</code> or
なるべく in it wants <code>～ようにする</code>. Anything with
<code>その{f|時|とき}</code> in it wants <code>～(よ)うとする</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|鍵|かぎ}を{f|探|さが}す**とした** → ✅ {f|鍵|かぎ}を{f|探|さが}<b>そうとした</b>
  `とする` here attaches to the **volitional**, not the dictionary form. Dictionary
  form + とする exists, but it is a separate written pattern meaning "supposing
  that", and a learner who mixes them will be misread.

- ❌ {f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|運|はこ}び**ましょうとしました** → ✅ {f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|運|はこ}<b>ぼうとしました</b>
  Only the final `する` gets the polite ending. The volitional inside stays plain - 
  ましょう never appears in front of `と`.

- ❌ その{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}**ようとしました**。とても{f|面白|おもしろ}かったです。 → ✅ その{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}<b>てみました</b>。
  You watched it and formed an opinion, so the attempt succeeded. A successful
  "try it and see" is `てみる`.

- ❌ {f|夜|よる}{f|十一時|じゅういちじ}までに{f|寝|ね}**ようとしています** → ✅ {f|夜|よる}{f|十一時|じゅういちじ}までに{f|寝|ね}<b>るようにしています</b>
  A bedtime you are trying to keep is a standing policy, not a single lunge at the
  duvet.

- ❌ {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|島|しま}に{f|近|ちか}づ**こうとしました** → ✅ {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|島|しま}に{f|近|ちか}づ<b>こうとしていました</b>
  A typhoon cannot attempt anything. With a subject that has no will, only the
  "on the verge of" reading is available, and that reading needs `ている`.

## Practice

**1.** バスに{f|乗|の}____としたとき、{f|定期券|ていきけん}を{f|家|いえ}に{f|忘|わす}れたことに{f|気|き}づいた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

バスに{f|乗|の}<b>ろう</b>としたとき…

<code>{f|乗|の}る</code> is a う-verb, so the volitional is
<code>{f|乗|の}ろう</code>. <code>～(よ)うとしたとき</code> pins the sentence to the
instant before boarding, which is exactly when a missing pass would announce
itself.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|去年|きょねん}から、{f|朝|あさ}ごはんを{f|必|かなら}ず（{f|食|た}べようとしています・{f|食|た}べるようにしています）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|必|かなら}ず<b>{f|食|た}べるようにしています</b>。

<code>{f|去年|きょねん}から</code> and <code>{f|必|かなら}ず</code> make this a rule
you have been keeping for a year - a standing policy, which is
<code>～ようにする</code>. <code>～(よ)うとしている</code> would describe you at one
particular breakfast, fork in hand.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I tried to open the jar, but the lid wouldn't come off."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

びんを<b>{f|開|あ}けようとしました</b>が、ふたが{f|取|と}れませんでした。

The lid stayed on, so the action never completed - that is exactly the job of
<code>～(よ)うとした</code>. Using <code>{f|開|あ}けてみました</code> would say the
jar opened and you looked inside.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}は{f|立|た}つとして、テーブルに{f|手|て}をついた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|立|た}<b>とうとして</b>、テーブルに{f|手|て}をついた。
"He put a hand on the table as he went to stand up."

<code>{f|立|た}つ</code> is a う-verb ending in つ, so the volitional is
<code>{f|立|た}とう</code>. The `て`-form then links the attempt to what it caused - 
the hand on the table happened in the course of getting up.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けてみました。
(b) {f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けようとしました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the window opened. `てみる` is "do it and see what happens", so the
interesting part is what followed - a breeze, a noise from the street, the smell
of rain. The action itself was never in doubt.

(b) says the window did not open, or at least that something interrupted you. The
interesting part is the obstacle, and a listener will wait for the `が` clause that
explains it. Said with no follow-up, (b) sounds unfinished.

</details>
