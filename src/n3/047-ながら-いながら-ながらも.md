# ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも

**Meaning** · although; despite - a quality is granted, and something that does not fit it is asserted anyway

This is the **second** `ながら`. The one you met at N4 stacks two actions on top of each
other ("while eating"); this one concedes. It grants a fact - 
the room is small, he knew the risk, she is only a child - and then states something
that the fact should have ruled out.

The two are told apart by shape, not by guesswork. Concessive `ながら` attaches to
い-adjectives, な-adjective stems, nouns and `ている` forms, none of which the "while"
`ながら` can take. And `ながらも`, with the extra `も`, is always concessive.

On this batch's axis of how much the speaker minds, concessive `ながら` minds least of
all. It is dignified rather than annoyed, and it is often outright warm:
{f|狭|せま}いながらも{f|楽|たの}しい{f|我|わ}が{f|家|や} is a compliment to a small flat.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb of knowing／thinking | ます-stem + ながら | {f|知|し}る → {f|知|し}りながら |
| Verb in ている | てい + ながら（も） | {f|分|わ}かっている → {f|分|わ}かっていながら |
| い-adjective | plain form + ながら（も） | {f|狭|せま}い → {f|狭|せま}いながらも |
| な-adjective | stem + ながら（も） | {f|残念|ざんねん} → {f|残念|ざんねん}ながら |
| Noun | Noun + ながら（も） | {f|子|こ}ども → {f|子|こ}どもながら |
| Noun + に (adverbial) | Noun + ながらに | {f|涙|なみだ} → {f|涙|なみだ}ながらに |
| Existence, fixed | いながらにして | {f|家|いえ}にいながらにして |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - な-adjectives and nouns attach **bare**: ✅
<code>{f|残念|ざんねん}ながら</code>, ❌<code>{f|残念|ざんねん}なながら</code>,
❌<code>{f|子|こ}どもだながら</code>. There is no `な` and no `だ` in front of this
`ながら`.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>いながら</code> is just <code>～ている</code> plus `ながら` with the
`て` swallowed in speech and writing alike: {f|知|し}っていながら. It is the standard
way to concede that somebody **was aware** and acted anyway, and it is noticeably
sharper than a plain `のに` because awareness is built into it.

</div>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

We've reached an age where you can take classes from anywhere in the world without leaving home.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Concessive `ながら` is **written and formal**. In an essay, a notice, a speech or a
carefully worded email it is entirely ordinary; in relaxed conversation it survives
mainly as a handful of set phrases, and using it freely in chat sounds like you have
swallowed a textbook.

The set phrases are worth learning as vocabulary, because they are extremely common:

- {f|残念|ざんねん}ながら - "regrettably", the standard soft refusal.
- {f|失礼|しつれい}ながら - "if I may be so bold", before a delicate question.
- {f|恥|は}ずかしながら - "I'm ashamed to say."
- {f|我|われ}ながら - "even if I say so myself."
- いつもながら - "as ever."
- {f|生|う}まれながら（に） - "from birth, innately."

`ながらも` is the emphatic version. The `も` makes the concession unmistakable, which
is useful precisely because bare `ながら` could be read as "while". It also adds a
faint note of admiration, which is why it collocates so happily with things that are
small, poor or difficult but turn out well.

`いながらにして` is a fixed expression meaning "without stirring from where you are",
and it has drifted into technology writing: {f|自宅|じたく}にいながらにして
{f|診察|しんさつ}が{f|受|う}けられる.

## Contrast

### Concessive ～ながら vs simultaneous ～ながら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - look at what is in front of it. An adjective, a noun or a `ている`
form can only be the **concessive** one. A plain action verb in ます-stem form is
normally the **"while"** one, unless the verb is about knowing or thinking.

</div>

<pre>
{f|音楽|おんがく}を{f|聞|き}き<b>ながら</b>、{f|洗|あら}い{f|物|もの}をしています。
I do the washing up with music on.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|失敗|しっぱい}すると{f|思|おも}い<b>ながら</b>、{f|止|と}めなかった。
I thought it would go wrong, and I didn't stop him.
</pre>

Both take a ます-stem, and the second is concessive only because
<code>{f|思|おも}う</code> is a verb of mental attitude - you cannot "do thinking while"
in the way you can do listening while. When in doubt, add `も`:
<code>{f|思|おも}いながらも</code> can only mean "although."

The "while" `ながら` also demands two activities that last long enough to overlap;
the concessive one has no such requirement, because nothing is overlapping.

### ～ながらも vs ～のに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same contradiction, different voice. `のに` is spoken and carries
the speaker's dissatisfaction. `ながらも` is written and carries composure, and often
respect.

</div>

<pre>
{f|初|はじ}めて<b>ながらも</b>、{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}いて{f|司会|しかい}を{f|務|つと}めていた。
It was his first time, and yet he chaired it calmly.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|初|はじ}めてな<b>のに</b>、ずいぶん{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}いていたね。
For a first go you were awfully calm, weren't you.
</pre>

The `ながらも` version would suit a written report of the event; the `のに` version is
what you say to the person afterwards. Note the small formal difference too: `のに`
needs `な` after a noun, `ながらも` needs nothing.

### ～ながらも vs ～つつも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - near twins, both literary. `つつも` attaches only to a ます-stem,
so it cannot concede an adjective or a noun; `ながらも` can.

</div>

<pre>
{f|無理|むり}だと{f|思|おも}い<b>つつも</b>、もう{f|一度|いちど}{f|応募|おうぼ}してみた。
Even while thinking it was hopeless, I applied one more time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|不安|ふあん}<b>ながらも</b>、{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|決|き}めた{f|道|みち}を{f|選|えら}んだ。
Uneasy though I was, I chose the path I'd decided on myself.
</pre>

`つつも` is the more bookish of the two and is almost always about an inner conflict - 
the speaker knew better and did it anyway. `ながらも` covers a much wider range,
including plain external facts like a room being small.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|知|し}るながら → ✅ {f|知|し}りながら
  `ながら` takes the ます-stem, exactly as the "while" version does. The dictionary
  form never appears before it.

- ❌ {f|残念|ざんねん}なながら → ✅ {f|残念|ざんねん}ながら
  な-adjectives attach by the bare stem. The same goes for nouns:
  ✅<code>{f|子|こ}どもながら</code>, ❌<code>{f|子|こ}どもだながら</code>.

- ❌ {f|残念|ざんねん}ながらも、{f|今回|こんかい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}です → ✅ {f|残念|ざんねん}ながら、{f|今回|こんかい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}です
  <code>{f|残念|ざんねん}ながら</code> is a frozen adverb meaning "regrettably" and does
  not take the extra `も`. The `も` version belongs to live concessions such as
  <code>{f|狭|せま}いながらも</code>.

- ❌ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|勉強|べんきょう}しながら、{f|成績|せいせき}が{f|上|あ}がらない → ✅ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|勉強|べんきょう}している<b>のに</b>、{f|成績|せいせき}が{f|上|あ}がらない
  With an ordinary action verb, `ながら` is read as "while", and "while studying, my
  grades don't rise" is not a sentence. Use `のに`, or make the awareness explicit with
  <code>{f|勉強|べんきょう}していながら</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|小|ちい}さい____、この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|例文|れいぶん}がとても{f|多|おお}い。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|小|ちい}さい<b>ながらも</b>、この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|例文|れいぶん}がとても{f|多|おお}い。

い-adjectives attach in their plain form, and the `も` makes the concessive reading
unmistakable. The sentence has the classic `ながらも` flavour: a drawback granted, and
something admirable asserted in spite of it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|悪|わる}いと（{f|知|し}りながら・{f|知|し}っていながら）、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|報告|ほうこく}しなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are possible, and <b>{f|知|し}っていながら</b> is the stronger choice.

<code>{f|知|し}りながら</code> concedes the knowledge in passing; the `ている` version
says the knowledge was already settled in his mind before he acted, which is what
makes the failure to report culpable. Written reports and criticism favour
`～ていながら` for exactly that reason.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Regrettably, the concert has been cancelled."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|残念|ざんねん}ながら</b>、コンサートは{f|中止|ちゅうし}になりました。

<code>{f|残念|ざんねん}ながら</code> is the fixed adverbial phrase used to deliver bad
news politely, and it opens the sentence. Note that no `な` and no `も` appear - 
adding either breaks the set phrase.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|町|まち}は{f|不便|ふべん}なながら、{f|人|ひと}が{f|温|あたた}かい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|町|まち}は{f|不便|ふべん}<b>ながらも</b>、{f|人|ひと}が{f|温|あたた}かい。

<code>{f|不便|ふべん}</code> is a な-adjective and attaches by its bare stem, so the
`な` has to go. Adding `も` is optional but natural here, since the sentence is
conceding a drawback in order to praise something else.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|体|からだ}に{f|悪|わる}いと{f|知|し}っていながら、{f|夜中|よなか}にラーメンを{f|食|た}べてしまう。
(b) {f|体|からだ}に{f|悪|わる}いと{f|知|し}っているくせに、{f|夜中|よなか}にラーメンを{f|食|た}べている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is measured and slightly literary. The speaker concedes their own awareness and
reports the lapse; `てしまう` adds rueful self-awareness rather than blame. It could
appear in an essay unchanged.

(b) is spoken and accusing. `くせに` turns the same awareness into grounds for
contempt, so this version is aimed at somebody else - and if it is aimed at the
speaker, it is deliberate self-mockery.

</details>
