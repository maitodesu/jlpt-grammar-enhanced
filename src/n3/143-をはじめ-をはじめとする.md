# をはじめ / をはじめとする

**Meaning** · starting with X - X being the leading member of a group, with the rest following

Written ～を{f|始|はじ}め in kanji, though kana is now more usual. The pattern names
one item as the **most prominent or most representative** of a set, and then goes
on to talk about the set as a whole. It is a listing device, not a substitute for
"for example": what follows it has to be the group X belongs to.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + をはじめ、… | {f|京都|きょうと} → {f|京都|きょうと}をはじめ、{f|各地|かくち}から{f|人|ひと}が{f|集|あつ}まる |
| Noun (formal, written) | Noun + をはじめとして、… | {f|校長|こうちょう} → {f|校長|こうちょう}をはじめとして、{f|先生方|せんせいがた}が{f|並|なら}んだ |
| Before a noun | Noun + をはじめとする + Noun | {f|東京|とうきょう} → {f|東京|とうきょう}をはじめとする{f|大都市|だいとし} |
| Before a noun (alternative) | Noun + をはじめとした + Noun | パンダ → パンダをはじめとした{f|動物|どうぶつ}たち |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the clause after the pattern must state the wider group, usually with
a quantity word: {f|全員|ぜんいん}, {f|多|おお}くの～, さまざまな～, ～など. A
sentence that names the leading item and then never mentions the set feels cut
off halfway.

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is **formal, largely written** Japanese: guidebooks, news reports,
company announcements, and the opening of speeches. It is not something you say
about your evening. 「{f|昨日|きのう}はビールをはじめ、いろいろ{f|飲|の}んだ」 sounds like a
press release about a night out; 「ビールとか、いろいろ{f|飲|の}んだ」 is what a person
says.

It is especially common in **thanks and greetings**, where naming the highest-
ranking person first and then sweeping in everyone else is a fixed courtesy:
「{f|会長|かいちょう}をはじめ、{f|皆様|みなさま}に{f|心|こころ}よりお{f|礼|れい}{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます」.
Getting the order right matters - the named person should genuinely be the most
senior or the most representative, and putting a junior name there reads as a
slight.

On the kanji: modern writing usually leaves it in kana. If you do write it,
it is <code>始</code> (as in <code>{f|始|はじ}める</code>, "to begin"), never <code>初</code>. And keep it apart
from <code>{f|初|はじ}めて</code>, "for the first time" - a different word that sounds
almost the same and produces nonsense if it slips in here.

Of the four forms, <code>をはじめ</code> is the everyday one, <code>をはじめとして</code> the most
formal, and <code>をはじめとする</code> / <code>をはじめとした</code> are what you use in front of a
noun. There is no meaning difference between the last two.

## Contrast

### をはじめ vs など

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `など` only says the list is partial. `をはじめ`
additionally **ranks**: the named item leads the group, and the group has to be
stated afterwards.

</div>

<pre>
{f|机|つくえ}の{f|上|うえ}には、ペンやノート<b>など</b>が{f|置|お}いてあった。
There were pens, notebooks and so on lying on the desk.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|図書館|としょかん}には、{f|古|ふる}い{f|地図|ちず}<b>をはじめ</b>、{f|貴重|きちょう}な{f|資料|しりょう}がそろっている。
This library holds a full range of valuable materials, old maps above all.
</pre>

Nothing about the pens is special; the old maps are the library's pride. The two
often appear together - 「Aをはじめ、B や C など」 - with `をはじめ` picking the
headline item and `など` closing the list off.

### をはじめ vs を{f|中心|ちゅうしん}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `をはじめ` opens a **list**; <code>を{f|中心|ちゅうしん}に</code> describes a
**distribution**, with the named thing carrying the most weight and everything
else thinning out around it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|山|やま}の{f|上|うえ}を<b>{f|中心|ちゅうしん}に</b>、{f|夜|よる}のうちに{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|積|つ}もった。
Snow settled overnight, most heavily up the mountain.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|北|きた}の{f|地方|ちほう}<b>をはじめ</b>、{f|各地|かくち}で{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|積|つ}もった。
Snow settled in various places, in the northern regions first of all.
</pre>

The first is a map with a hot spot. The second is a roll call. `をはじめ` needs
the group to be countable in some sense; a gradient of intensity is
<code>{f|中心|ちゅうしん}</code>'s job.

### をはじめ vs {f|例|たと}えば

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|例|たと}えば</code> is an adverb that opens a clause and
introduces an illustration. `をはじめ` is a postposition that sits on a noun
inside the sentence, and it cannot stand on its own.

</div>

<pre>
{f|果物|くだもの}が{f|好|す}きです。<b>{f|例|たと}えば</b>、りんごやぶどうをよく{f|買|か}います。
I like fruit - apples and grapes, for instance, are things I buy often.
</pre>

<pre>
りんご<b>をはじめ</b>、{f|秋|あき}の{f|果物|くだもの}はどれも{f|安|やす}くなってきた。
Autumn fruit has all come down in price, apples first among them.
</pre>

<code>{f|例|たと}えば</code> picks a random illustration and makes no claim about
importance. `をはじめ` claims the item is the leading one, and it needs the group
in the same sentence. ❌「をはじめ、りんごが{f|好|す}きです」 is not a sentence.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ケーキ<b>をはじめて</b>、{f|甘|あま}いものが{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ ケーキ<b>をはじめ</b>、{f|甘|あま}いものが{f|好|す}きです。
  <code>{f|初|はじ}めて</code> ("for the first time") is a different word. The pattern ends
  in はじめ, or in はじめとして.

- ❌ {f|東京|とうきょう}をはじめ{f|都市|とし}で{f|家賃|やちん}が{f|上|あ}がっている。 → ✅ {f|東京|とうきょう}<b>をはじめとする</b>{f|都市|とし}で{f|家賃|やちん}が{f|上|あ}がっている。
  In front of a noun the pattern needs `とする` or `とした`. Without it the
  reader stumbles over which word <code>{f|都市|とし}</code> belongs to.

- ❌ カレー<b>をはじめ</b>、パンも{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ カレー<b>をはじめ</b>、{f|辛|から}い{f|料理|りょうり}が{f|好|す}きです。
  The named item has to be a member of the group that follows. Curry does not
  lead a list whose next entry is bread.

- ❌ {f|山田|やまだ}さん<b>をはじめ</b>、{f|来|き}てくれました。 → ✅ {f|山田|やまだ}さん<b>をはじめ</b>、{f|大勢|おおぜい}の{f|人|ひと}が{f|来|き}てくれました。
  The group must actually appear. On its own the pattern leaves the sentence
  unfinished.

## Practice

**1.** {f|校長|こうちょう}{f|先生|せんせい}____、{f|多|おお}くの{f|先生方|せんせいがた}が{f|見送|みおく}りに{f|来|き}てくださった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|校長|こうちょう}{f|先生|せんせい}<b>をはじめ</b>（or <b>をはじめとして</b>）、{f|多|おお}くの{f|先生方|せんせいがた}が{f|見送|みおく}りに{f|来|き}てくださった。

The principal is the most senior member of the group named in the second half,
and the second half supplies exactly the wider set the pattern needs. The
honorific verb makes <code>をはじめとして</code> equally at home here.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|地方|ちほう}は、りんご（をはじめ・を{f|中心|ちゅうしん}に）{f|果物|くだもの}の{f|産地|さんち}として{f|知|し}られている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

りんご<b>をはじめ</b>、{f|果物|くだもの}の{f|産地|さんち}として{f|知|し}られている。

Apples are one member of the category "fruit", and the sentence goes on to name
that category - a list, not a distribution.
<code>を{f|中心|ちゅうしん}に</code> would suggest apples dominate the output and other
fruit trails off around them, which is a claim about proportions rather than
about what the region is famous for.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Many countries, China and India above all, are increasing their imports."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|中国|ちゅうごく}やインド<b>をはじめとする</b>{f|多|おお}くの{f|国|くに}が、{f|輸入|ゆにゅう}を{f|増|ふ}やしている。

Two items can share the leading position, joined with `や`. Because
<code>{f|多|おお}くの{f|国|くに}</code> is a noun being modified, the pattern takes the `とする`
shape rather than the bare `をはじめ`.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|美術館|びじゅつかん}には{f|有名|ゆうめい}な{f|絵|え}をはじめ、あります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|美術館|びじゅつかん}には{f|有名|ゆうめい}な{f|絵|え}<b>をはじめ</b>、さまざまな{f|作品|さくひん}があります。

The pattern names the leading item and then owes you the group. Left as it was,
the sentence promises a set and never delivers one. Anything that names the
wider category - <code>さまざまな{f|作品|さくひん}</code>,
<code>{f|多|おお}くの{f|美術品|びじゅつひん}</code> - repairs it.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|大学|だいがく}には、アジア<b>をはじめとする</b>{f|世界中|せかいじゅう}の{f|留学生|りゅうがくせい}がいる。
(b) この{f|大学|だいがく}には、アジア<b>を{f|中心|ちゅうしん}に</b>{f|世界中|せかいじゅう}から{f|留学生|りゅうがくせい}が{f|来|き}ている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) presents Asia as the first entry on a list of origins. It says nothing about
how many students come from where - only that Asia is the region you would name
first.

(b) makes a quantitative claim: most of the students are Asian, and the numbers
thin out as you move away from that core. If a reader wanted to know the shape
of the student body, (b) is the sentence that answers them.

</details>
