# ～ような気がする

**Meaning** · I have a feeling that ～ - a hunch, offered without the evidence that would make it a claim

Read as *ような き がする*. The pattern reports an impression that has formed in
you without your being able to show your working. It is the softest thing in the
whole certainty range: weaker than <code>と{f|思|おも}う</code>, weaker than `ようだ`,
and much weaker than `はずだ`. Japanese uses it constantly, both to hedge a real
opinion and to describe a genuine sensation you cannot account for.

## Formation

`ような` is the noun-modifying form of `ようだ`, and <code>{f|気|き}</code> is the
noun it modifies. So the clause attaches exactly the way it does before any noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + ような{f|気|き}がする | {f|忘|わす}れた → {f|忘|わす}れたような{f|気|き}がする |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + ような{f|気|き}がする | {f|合|あ}わない → {f|合|あ}わないような{f|気|き}がする |
| い-adjective | plain form + ような{f|気|き}がする | {f|寒|さむ}い → {f|寒|さむ}いような{f|気|き}がする |
| な-adjective | ～な + ような{f|気|き}がする | {f|無理|むり}な → {f|無理|むり}なような{f|気|き}がする |
| Noun | ～の + ような{f|気|き}がする | {f|夢|ゆめ} → {f|夢|ゆめ}のような{f|気|き}がする |
| Shorter version | plain form + {f|気|き}がする | {f|忘|わす}れた → {f|忘|わす}れた{f|気|き}がする |
| Polite | … + ような{f|気|き}がします | {f|遅|おそ}い → {f|遅|おそ}いような{f|気|き}がします |
| Past (recollection) | … + ような{f|気|き}がした | {f|呼|よ}ばれた → {f|呼|よ}ばれたような{f|気|き}がした |

<div class="note">

**Note** - with な-adjectives the full form is a mouthful, and speakers usually drop
the `よう`: <code>{f|無理|むり}な{f|気|き}がする</code> is far more common than
<code>{f|無理|むり}なような{f|気|き}がする</code>. Both are grammatical.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
どこかで{f|会|あ}った<b>ような{f|気|き}がする</b>。
I feel like I've met them somewhere before.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|部屋|へや}、{f|少|すこ}し{f|寒|さむ}い<b>ような{f|気|き}がします</b>。
This room feels a bit cold to me.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|鍵|かぎ}をかけ{f|忘|わす}れた<b>ような{f|気|き}がして</b>、{f|家|いえ}まで{f|戻|もど}った。
I had a feeling I'd forgotten to lock up, so I went all the way back home.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|説明|せつめい}は、どこか{f|本当|ほんとう}ではない<b>ような{f|気|き}がする</b>。
Something about his explanation feels untrue to me.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|最近|さいきん}、{f|一日|いちにち}が{f|過|す}ぎるのが{f|速|はや}くなった<b>ような{f|気|き}がしませんか</b>。
Don't you feel like the days go by faster lately?
</pre>

<pre>
まだ{f|連絡|れんらく}はないけれど、{f|今回|こんかい}はうまくいく<b>ような{f|気|き}がする</b>。
I haven't heard anything yet, but I've got a feeling it'll work out this time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|初|はじ}めて{f|来|き}た{f|町|まち}のはずなのに、{f|前|まえ}にもこの{f|坂|さか}を{f|歩|ある}いた<b>ような{f|気|き}がした</b>。
It was supposed to be my first time in the town, and yet I felt as though I'd walked up that hill before.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}は{f|分|わ}かりやすかったのですが、{f|一番|いちばん}{f|大事|だいじ}なところを{f|見落|みお}としている<b>ような{f|気|き}がします</b>。
The explanation was clear enough, but I have the feeling we're overlooking the most important part.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and one of the highest-frequency hedges in ordinary conversation.
<code>{f|気|き}がする</code> in plain form is casual, <code>{f|気|き}がします</code> fits any polite situation, and
nothing about it is stiff or literary. In writing, the close relative
<code>～ように{f|思|おも}う</code> does the same job with a more composed tone.

Two flavours share the one form, and context separates them. The first is a real
**sensation**: something smells faintly of smoke, a room feels colder, you half
heard your name. The second is **politeness** - you have an opinion, and you present
it as a mere feeling so that disagreeing with you costs nothing. In a meeting,
<code>この{f|案|あん}は{f|少|すこ}し{f|急|いそ}ぎすぎているような{f|気|き}がします</code> is a
carefully wrapped objection, and everyone hears it as one.

The variant <code>ような{f|気|き}もする</code> is worth having. Replacing `が` with `も`
turns it into "part of me feels", and it is how you sit on the fence out loud:
<code>{f|安|やす}いような{f|気|き}もするし、{f|高|たか}いような{f|気|き}もする</code>.

Do not confuse the phrase with its neighbours. <code>{f|気|き}になる</code> means
something is on your mind or bothering you; <code>{f|気|き}に{f|入|い}る</code> means you
like it. Only <code>{f|気|き}がする</code> means "I have the impression", and the
particle is `が`, never `を`.

## Contrast

### ような{f|気|き}がする vs と{f|思|おも}う

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>と{f|思|おも}う</code> is an opinion you could defend if
pressed. <code>ような{f|気|き}がする</code> is an impression you could not.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|料理|りょうり}には{f|塩|しお}が{f|足|た}りない<b>と{f|思|おも}います</b>。
I think this dish needs more salt.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|料理|りょうり}には{f|塩|しお}が{f|足|た}りない<b>ような{f|気|き}がします</b>。
This dish feels like it could do with more salt.
</pre>

The first invites a reply - agreement, or "no, it's fine as it is". The second
barely puts the claim on the table, and that is often the point. Note the practical
consequence: if a superior asks for your judgment, answering only with
<code>ような{f|気|き}がします</code> can read as evasive rather than modest.

### ような{f|気|き}がする vs ようだ・みたいだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ようだ` is a conclusion **drawn from evidence**;
<code>ような{f|気|き}がする</code> is a feeling that arrives **without** it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|玄関|げんかん}に{f|靴|くつ}がある。{f|誰|だれ}か{f|来|き}ている<b>ようだ</b>。
There are shoes in the entrance. It seems someone's here.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|家|いえ}に{f|誰|だれ}かいる<b>ような{f|気|き}がする</b>。
I have a feeling there's someone in the house.
</pre>

The first points at the shoes; anybody could check the reasoning. The second points
at nothing at all, which is exactly why it is the sentence that belongs in a ghost
story. Because they sit next to each other, the two combine happily:
<code>いるような{f|気|き}がしただけかもしれない</code>.

### ような{f|気|き}がする vs {f|気|き}がする

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the bare <code>{f|気|き}がする</code> is a shade more direct;
adding `よう` puts one more layer of "sort of" between you and the claim.

</div>

<pre>
{f|前|まえ}にも{f|同|おな}じ{f|話|はなし}を{f|聞|き}いた<b>{f|気|き}がする</b>。
I think I've heard this story before.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|前|まえ}にも{f|同|おな}じ{f|話|はなし}を{f|聞|き}いた<b>ような{f|気|き}がする</b>。
I have some vague feeling I've heard this story before.
</pre>

Both are natural, and in casual speech the short form is if anything the commoner of
the two. Choose the long one when you want the extra padding - an awkward opinion,
a memory you distrust, a criticism you would rather not sharpen.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ そのやり{f|方|かた}は{f|危|あぶ}ない<b>{f|気|き}になります</b>。 → ✅ そのやり{f|方|かた}は{f|危|あぶ}ない<b>{f|気|き}がします</b>。
  <code>{f|気|き}になる</code> means "it bothers me / I keep wondering about it". For "I
  have the impression", the verb is <code>{f|気|き}がする</code>.

- ❌ {f|忘|わす}れ{f|物|もの}をしたような<b>{f|気|き}をします</b>。 → ✅ {f|忘|わす}れ{f|物|もの}をしたような<b>{f|気|き}がします</b>。
  The particle is `が`. Nothing is being done to the feeling; the feeling simply
  occurs.

- ❌ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|静|しず}か<b>だような{f|気|き}がする</b>。 → ✅ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|静|しず}か<b>な{f|気|き}がする</b>。
  Before a noun, な-adjectives take `な`, never `だ` - and with this pattern the short
  form is the one people actually say.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|来年|らいねん}30{f|歳|さい}になる<b>ような{f|気|き}がします</b>。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|来年|らいねん}30{f|歳|さい}になります。
  A fact you know about yourself is not a hunch. Hedging it sounds as though you are
  unsure how old you are.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|字|じ}、どこかで{f|見|み}た____{f|気|き}がする。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|字|じ}、どこかで{f|見|み}た<b>ような</b>{f|気|き}がする。

<code>{f|気|き}</code> is a noun, so the clause in front of it has to be in
noun-modifying shape: past plain form plus `ような`. Dropping `よう` altogether is
also fine and slightly more direct.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|窓|まど}が{f|開|あ}いている。{f|誰|だれ}かが{f|入|はい}った（ようだ・ような{f|気|き}がする）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|誰|だれ}かが{f|入|はい}った<b>ようだ</b>。

The open window is concrete evidence, and a conclusion drawn from evidence is
`ようだ`. <code>ような{f|気|き}がする</code> would throw that evidence away and present the whole thing
as an unaccountable feeling.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I get the feeling this train is slower than usual."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|電車|でんしゃ}、いつもより{f|遅|おそ}い<b>ような{f|気|き}がします</b>。

An い-adjective attaches directly to `ような`. The pattern fits because the speaker
has no timetable in hand - only a sense that the journey is dragging.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|映画|えいが}はもう{f|一度|いちど}{f|見|み}たいような{f|気|き}になります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|映画|えいが}はもう{f|一度|いちど}{f|見|み}たいような<b>{f|気|き}がします</b>。

<code>{f|気|き}になる</code> would mean the film is preying on your mind. The verb that
reports an impression is <code>{f|気|き}がする</code>, with `が`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|計画|けいかく}には{f|無理|むり}がある<b>と{f|思|おも}います</b>。
(b) この{f|計画|けいかく}には{f|無理|むり}がある<b>ような{f|気|き}がします</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) states a position. You are prepared to say why the plan is overambitious, and
the room will expect you to.

(b) floats the same worry without committing to it, and invites the others to pick
it up or let it drop. In a meeting it is the politer opening move; the risk is that
if you never move to (a), nobody acts on it.

</details>
