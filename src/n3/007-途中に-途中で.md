# ～途中に / ～途中で

**Meaning** · partway along - on the way from A to B, or partway through something that has a beginning and an end

<code>{f|途中|とちゅう}</code> is "the middle stretch of a route". Japanese uses the same
word for a physical journey ({f|駅|えき}へ{f|行|い}く{f|途中|とちゅう}) and for any
process that runs from start to finish ({f|話|はなし}の{f|途中|とちゅう}), so "on the way"
and "partway through" are one word here. The choice between `で` and `に` is not free:
`で` for something that **happens**, `に` for something that **is there**.

## Formation

<code>{f|途中|とちゅう}</code> is a noun. A verb in front is in plain non-past form - 
even when the whole sentence is in the past.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb of motion | Plain non-past + {f|途中|とちゅう}で／に | {f|帰|かえ}る → {f|帰|かえ}る{f|途中|とちゅう}で |
| Other verb | ている + {f|途中|とちゅう}で | {f|話|はな}す → {f|話|はな}している{f|途中|とちゅう}で |
| Noun | の + {f|途中|とちゅう}で／に | {f|会議|かいぎ} → {f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|途中|とちゅう}で |
| Partial extent | {f|途中|とちゅう}まで／から | {f|読|よ}む → {f|途中|とちゅう}まで{f|読|よ}んだ |
| As a predicate | …の{f|途中|とちゅう}だ／です | {f|準備|じゅんび} → まだ{f|準備|じゅんび}の{f|途中|とちゅう}です |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the plain non-past in front is fixed:
{f|帰|かえ}る{f|途中|とちゅう}で{f|雨|あめ}に{f|降|ふ}られた, never
❌{f|帰|かえ}った{f|途中|とちゅう}で. The verb names the journey, and the journey has not
finished - that is the whole point of being partway along it.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the bare non-past belongs to verbs of **going**: {f|行|い}く,
{f|来|く}る, {f|帰|かえ}る, {f|向|む}かう, {f|登|のぼ}る, {f|戻|もど}る. For other
activities use ている ({f|読|よ}んでいる{f|途中|とちゅう}で) or the noun form
({f|読書|どくしょ}の{f|途中|とちゅう}で).

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|学校|がっこう}へ{f|行|い}く<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|友|とも}だちに{f|会|あ}った。
I ran into a friend on the way to school.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}へ{f|行|い}く<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}に</b>{f|大|おお}きな{f|公園|こうえん}がある。
There's a big park on the way to the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|映画|えいが}の<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|寝|ね}てしまった。
I fell asleep partway through the film.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|帰|かえ}る<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|雨|あめ}に{f|降|ふ}られた。
I got caught in the rain on the way home.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人|ひと}の{f|話|はなし}の<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}つのは{f|失礼|しつれい}だ。
Getting up in the middle of what someone is saying is rude.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>
{f|空港|くうこう}へ{f|向|む}かう<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|事故|じこ}に{f|巻|ま}き{f|込|こ}まれ、{f|飛行機|ひこうき}に{f|乗|の}り{f|遅|おく}れてしまった。
I got caught up in an accident on the way to the airport and missed my flight.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|山|やま}を{f|登|のぼ}る<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}に</b>{f|小|ちい}さな{f|茶屋|ちゃや}があって、そこで{f|休|やす}んでから{f|頂上|ちょうじょう}を{f|目指|めざ}しました。
There's a little teahouse partway up the mountain; we rested there before pressing on for the summit.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and extremely common in everyday speech. There is no polite or plain
variant to learn; only the particle choice matters.

The journey sense is so ordinary that it has produced fixed compounds:
<code>{f|帰|かえ}り{f|道|みち}</code> for the way home (more idiomatic than
{f|帰|かえ}る{f|途中|とちゅう} when you mean the route rather than the timing), and
<code>{f|通勤|つうきん}{f|途中|とちゅう}</code>, <code>{f|帰宅|きたく}{f|途中|とちゅう}</code>
in news writing and insurance forms, where the noun attaches with no `の`.

<code>{f|途中|とちゅう}まで</code> and <code>{f|途中|とちゅう}から</code> are worth
learning as separate items. 「{f|途中|とちゅう}まで{f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|行|い}きましょう」
 - "let's walk part of the way together" - is a normal, friendly thing to say, and
「{f|途中|とちゅう}から{f|見|み}たので、よく{f|分|わ}かりませんでした」 explains that you
joined a film or a meeting late.

Used as a predicate, <code>まだ{f|途中|とちゅう}です</code> is a mild, useful hedge: "I'm
not finished yet." It sounds considerably softer than
まだ{f|終|お}わっていません, because it presents the work as in motion rather than as
incomplete.

## Contrast

### {f|途中|とちゅう}で vs {f|途中|とちゅう}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `で` for an **event** that occurs partway along; `に` for a **thing**
that sits partway along. If the verb is ある or いる, it is `に`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|散歩|さんぽ}の<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|昔|むかし}の{f|同僚|どうりょう}に{f|会|あ}った。
I bumped into a former colleague partway through my walk.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|散歩|さんぽ}コースの<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}に</b>{f|小|ちい}さな{f|神社|じんじゃ}がある。
There's a small shrine partway along the walking route.
</pre>

The distinction mirrors the ordinary `で`/`に` split you already know: `で` marks where
an action takes place, `に` marks where something exists. It follows that the great
majority of <code>{f|途中|とちゅう}</code> sentences take `で`, because most of them
report events. `に` shows up mainly with ある, いる, {f|立|た}っている and the like.

<div class="note">

**Note** - with a noun naming a process rather than a route, `で` is effectively the only
option: {f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|途中|とちゅう}で, {f|説明|せつめい}の{f|途中|とちゅう}で. A
meeting is not a road, so nothing can be standing beside it.

</div>

### {f|途中|とちゅう}で vs {f|最中|さいちゅう}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</code> measures **progress**: you had got
partway and had not finished. <code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</code> measures **intensity**:
you were at the busiest point.

</div>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}の<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|質問|しつもん}してもいいですか。
May I ask a question partway through your explanation?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}の<b>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</b>{f|質問|しつもん}されて、{f|話|はなし}が{f|止|と}まってしまった。
I was asked a question right in the middle of my explanation and lost my thread.
</pre>

The first is a polite request; the second is a complaint. Only
<code>{f|途中|とちゅう}</code> works with a journey - 
{f|駅|えき}へ{f|行|い}く{f|途中|とちゅう}で, never
❌{f|駅|えき}へ{f|行|い}く{f|最中|さいちゅう}に - and only
<code>{f|途中|とちゅう}</code> gives you まで and から, because only
<code>{f|途中|とちゅう}</code> thinks of the activity as a line you can stop on.

### {f|途中|とちゅう}で vs {f|間|あいだ}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code> needs a **window with two ends** and
places an event inside it. <code>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</code> needs a **process with a
direction** and says how far along you were.

</div>

<pre>
{f|授業|じゅぎょう}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>{f|三回|さんかい}{f|質問|しつもん}した。
I asked three questions during the lesson.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|授業|じゅぎょう}の<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|教室|きょうしつ}を{f|出|で}て{f|行|い}った。
He walked out of the classroom partway through the lesson.
</pre>

Both are grammatical here, and the difference is one of emphasis:
<code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code> is neutral about position within the window, while
<code>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</code> insists that the lesson was left unfinished. That is why
<code>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</code> pairs so naturally with verbs of stopping, leaving and
giving up - やめる, {f|帰|かえ}る, {f|降|お}りる, あきらめる.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|家|いえ}に<b>{f|帰|かえ}った{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}をした。 → ✅ {f|家|いえ}に<b>{f|帰|かえ}る{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}をした。
  The verb stays in plain non-past however far in the past the sentence is. A past-tense
  verb would say the journey was already over, which contradicts
  <code>{f|途中|とちゅう}</code>.

- ❌ {f|駅|えき}へ{f|行|い}く{f|途中|とちゅう}<b>で</b>コンビニがあります。 → ✅ {f|駅|えき}へ{f|行|い}く{f|途中|とちゅう}<b>に</b>コンビニがあります。
  あります states existence, and existence takes `に`. Use `で` only when something
  happens there.

- ❌ <b>{f|会議|かいぎ}{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|抜|ぬ}けてもいいですか。 → ✅ <b>{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|抜|ぬ}けてもいいですか。
  An ordinary noun needs `の`. The bare-attachment compounds
  ({f|通勤|つうきん}{f|途中|とちゅう} and similar) are fixed vocabulary items, not a
  general rule.

- ❌ {f|本|ほん}を<b>{f|読|よ}む{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|寝|ね}てしまった。 → ✅ {f|本|ほん}を<b>{f|読|よ}んでいる{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|寝|ね}てしまった。
  Only verbs of going take the bare non-past. Anything else needs ている, or recast it as
  a noun: {f|読書|どくしょ}の{f|途中|とちゅう}で.

## Practice

**1.** {f|銀行|ぎんこう}へ{f|行|い}く{f|途中|とちゅう}____{f|古|ふる}いお{f|寺|てら}があります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|銀行|ぎんこう}へ{f|行|い}く{f|途中|とちゅう}<b>に</b>{f|古|ふる}いお{f|寺|てら}があります。

あります reports that something exists along the route, and existence takes `に`. `で`
would be looking for an action, and standing there is not something the temple does.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|説明|せつめい}の（{f|途中|とちゅう}で・{f|最中|さいちゅう}に）{f|話|はなし}をやめて、{f|質問|しつもん}を{f|受|う}け{f|付|つ}けます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|説明|せつめい}の<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|話|はなし}をやめて、{f|質問|しつもん}を{f|受|う}け{f|付|つ}けます。

This is a planned pause partway through, not an intrusion, and やめる is exactly the kind
of verb <code>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</code> attracts. <code>{f|最中|さいちゅう}に</code>
would frame the speaker's own plan as an unwelcome interruption of themselves.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I noticed on the way to the office that I'd forgotten my wallet."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会社|かいしゃ}へ{f|行|い}く<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|財布|さいふ}を{f|忘|わす}れたことに{f|気|き}がついた。

Realising something is an event, so the particle is `で`, and {f|行|い}く stays in the
plain non-past even though the whole sentence is in the past.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|昨日|きのう}、{f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}いた{f|途中|とちゅう}で{f|財布|さいふ}を{f|落|お}としました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|昨日|きのう}、{f|駅|えき}へ<b>{f|行|い}く{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|財布|さいふ}を{f|落|お}としました。

Two things are wrong. The verb must be plain non-past, and it must be one that describes
the journey rather than its completion - {f|着|つ}く means "arrive", so
{f|着|つ}く{f|途中|とちゅう} is a contradiction. {f|行|い}く or {f|向|む}かう is what you
want.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|駅|えき}へ{f|行|い}く<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}で</b>{f|本屋|ほんや}に{f|寄|よ}った。
(b) {f|駅|えき}へ{f|行|い}く<b>{f|途中|とちゅう}に</b>{f|本屋|ほんや}がある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports an action performed partway along the journey: you interrupted the walk to
drop into the shop. `で` is the particle of "where an action happens", and {f|寄|よ}った is
plainly an action.

(b) reports a fact about the route itself: a bookshop stands somewhere along it,
regardless of whether anyone goes in. `に` is the particle of location, and ある is the
verb that demands it. The two sentences describe the same street and answer completely
different questions.

</details>
