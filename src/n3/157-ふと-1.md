# ふと - a thought that arrives on its own

**Meaning** · suddenly; out of nowhere - used of a thought, a memory or a feeling
that surfaces without being summoned

`ふと` is the word for the moment when something occurs to you. Nobody made it
happen, you were not trying to remember it, and there is no cause worth naming - 
it simply came.

## Usage

`ふと` is an adverb and stands directly in front of the verb. It does not
conjugate and takes no particles.

The lesson of the page is which verbs it goes with. In this sense `ふと` selects
**verbs of the mind**:

- {f|思|おも}う - ふと{f|思|おも}ったんだけど…
- {f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}す - ふと{f|昔|むかし}のことを{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}した。
- {f|気|き}づく - ふと{f|気|き}づくと、{f|外|そと}は{f|暗|くら}くなっていた。
- {f|考|かんが}える, {f|不安|ふあん}になる, {f|心配|しんぱい}になる, {f|頭|あたま}に{f|浮|う}かぶ

Two shapes are near-fixed and worth learning whole:

- <code>ふと{f|思|おも}うことがある</code> - "the thought sometimes strikes me".
- <code>ふと{f|気|き}づくと～ていた</code> - "before I knew it, …". The `と` here is the
  N4 conditional, and the second half is almost always in a past or `ていた` form.

`ふと` describes a mental event happening to you, so it is almost always narrated
in the **past**. ❌ふと{f|思|おも}います, as a statement about the present, does not
work; a thought you are having on purpose is not a `ふと` thought.

It cannot be used of external events. Rain, noises and visitors do not arrive
`ふと` - that is {f|急|きゅう}に or {f|突然|とつぜん}.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>ふと</b>{f|母|はは}の{f|声|こえ}を{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}した。
My mother's voice suddenly came back to me.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>ふと</b>{f|気|き}づくと、{f|電車|でんしゃ}はもう{f|終点|しゅうてん}だった。
Before I knew it, the train had reached the last stop.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|洗|あら}い{f|物|もの}をしているとき、<b>ふと</b>{f|来年|らいねん}のことが{f|心配|しんぱい}になった。
While I was doing the washing-up, a worry about next year came over me out of nowhere.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自分|じぶん}は{f|何|なに}のために{f|働|はたら}いているのだろうと、<b>ふと</b>{f|考|かんが}えてしまうことがある。
Now and then the thought creeps up on me: what exactly am I working for?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ふと` is neutral in politeness - it fits ます-form and plain form equally - but it
is not evenly distributed. It belongs to reflective narration: novels, essays,
diary entries, and the kind of conversation where somebody is telling you what went
through their head. You will hear it far less in brisk, practical talk.

Because of that, `ふと` colours a sentence. It signals that the speaker was not in
control of the thought, which makes it useful for softening: ふと{f|思|おも}った
んだけど、… is a gentle way to introduce a suggestion, since you are presenting it
as something that merely occurred to you rather than a position you have taken.

The word is written in kana. It has no kanji in ordinary use, no contraction, and
no politeness variants.

## Contrast

### ふと vs {f|急|きゅう}に・{f|突然|とつぜん}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - {f|急|きゅう}に and {f|突然|とつぜん} describe an event
changing suddenly. `ふと` describes something surfacing in your own mind.

</div>

<pre>
<b>{f|急|きゅう}に</b>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}した。
It suddenly started to rain.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>ふと</b>、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}ってきていないことに{f|気|き}づいた。
It struck me that I hadn't brought an umbrella.
</pre>

The rain is out there in the world, so it takes {f|急|きゅう}に; the realisation is
inside the speaker's head, so it takes `ふと`. Note also the difference in force:
{f|急|きゅう}に is abrupt and often unwelcome, while `ふと` is quiet - a thought
drifting in, not a door slamming.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ふと{f|電話|でんわ}が{f|鳴|な}った。 → ✅ <b>{f|急|きゅう}に</b>{f|電話|でんわ}が{f|鳴|な}った。
  A ringing phone is an event in the world, not a thought in your mind. `ふと`
  cannot take an external subject.

- ❌ ふと{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}そうとした。 → ✅ <b>ふと</b>{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}した。
  Trying to remember is deliberate, and `ふと` means precisely that no effort was
  involved. The two cancel each other out.

- ❌ {f|毎朝|まいあさ}ふと{f|母|はは}のことを{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}します。 → ✅ {f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|母|はは}のことを{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}します。
  Something that happens every morning is a habit, not a thought arriving
  unbidden. `ふと` needs the occasion to be one-off and unplanned.

## Practice

**1.** {f|散歩|さんぽ}をしていて、____{f|学生時代|がくせいじだい}のことを{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|散歩|さんぽ}をしていて、<b>ふと</b>{f|学生時代|がくせいじだい}のことを{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}した。

A memory surfacing during an unrelated activity, with no trigger the speaker cares
to name, is the core use of this adverb. The past tense is part of the pattern.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: （ふと・{f|急|きゅう}に）{f|後|うし}ろから{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|呼|よ}ばれた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|急|きゅう}に</b>{f|後|うし}ろから{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|呼|よ}ばれた。

Someone else calling your name is an external event, so it needs the adverb for
sudden happenings. `ふと` would only work if the sentence described something
arising inside the speaker's own head.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ふと{f|彼|かれ}の{f|言葉|ことば}を{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}した。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|言葉|ことば}を{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (b) is neutral and would also fit someone deliberately casting their mind
back. Sentence (a) says the memory arrived by itself, unprompted, in the middle of
something else - and because the speaker did not choose to have it, the remark
carries a hint that the words have been sitting with them.

</details>
