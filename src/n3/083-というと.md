# ～というと - speaking of; when you say…

**Meaning** · takes a word that has just been said and uses it as a springboard - either for what it brings to mind, or for a "wait, which one do you mean?"

<code>というと</code> is `と`, the conditional: "**if** we say X, then…". That is why it has
two faces. Point it forward and you get automatic association - 
{f|京都|きょうと}というと、お{f|寺|てら}. Point it back at the person who spoke and you get a
request for clarification - {f|田中|たなか}さんというと、{f|営業|えいぎょう}の? Both start
from the same place: a word has landed, and you are working out what follows from it.

## Formation

The phrase attaches to whatever word you are picking up. It is a connective, so a comma
usually follows.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + というと | {f|京都|きょうと} → {f|京都|きょうと}というと |
| Noun phrase | Noun phrase + というと | {f|冬|ふゆ}の{f|北海道|ほっかいどう} → {f|冬|ふゆ}の{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}というと |
| Clause | plain form + というと | {f|一人|ひとり}で{f|行|い}く → {f|一人|ひとり}で{f|行|い}くというと |
| Standing alone, as a question | というと？ | - |
| Casual | っていうと／ってゆうと | {f|京都|きょうと}っていうと |
| Polite | といいますと | {f|京都|きょうと}といいますと |
| Close relatives | といえば／といったら | {f|京都|きょうと}といえば |

<div class="note">

**Note** - nouns and な-adjectives attach with no `だ`:
❌<code>{f|京都|きょうと}だというと</code>. A bare <code>というと？</code> with rising
intonation is a complete utterance meaning "meaning?" - one of the most useful two-second
phrases in the language.

</div>

## Nuance & register

<code>というと</code> is **reactive**. Something has to be on the table already - a word
the other person used, a place name that has just come up, a topic you yourself introduced
one sentence earlier. That is what separates it from simply starting to talk about
something.

Register is neutral. <code>といいますと</code> is the polite form and is standard in
customer-facing speech: 「{f|来週|らいしゅう}といいますと、{f|何日|なんにち}ごろでしょうか。」
In casual speech it becomes <code>っていうと</code> or, with the vowel stretched,
<code>ってゆうと</code>, which is very common and mildly sloppy in writing.

The clarification use deserves separate practice, because it is the politest way to say
"I'm not sure I follow." A bare <code>というと？</code> puts the burden back on the speaker
without accusing them of being unclear, and in a meeting it is far softer than
「どういう{f|意味|いみ}ですか」. The full form
<code>…というと、…ということですか</code> is the careful version.

One thing <code>というと</code> does not do is give advice. It draws out what a word
implies; it does not tell the listener what to do about it. That job belongs to `なら`.

## Contrast

### というと vs といえば

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - heavily overlapping. As a tendency: `といえば` freely *raises* a topic
by association, including one nobody asked about; <code>というと</code> leans on a word
that has just been said, and is the one that turns into a question.

</div>

<pre>
{f|温泉|おんせん}<b>といえば</b>、{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|草津|くさつ}に{f|行|い}くんです。
Speaking of hot springs - I'm off to Kusatsu next month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|温泉|おんせん}<b>というと</b>、やはり{f|冬|ふゆ}に{f|行|い}きたくなりますね。
Hot springs - you do want to go in winter, don't you.
</pre>

The first changes the subject to the speaker's own news; that is `といえば` territory, and
`そういえば` ("that reminds me") is its most common form. The second stays on the general
association. In the association use they are close to interchangeable, and nobody will
misunderstand you either way.

### というと vs といったら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `といったら` is the emotional one. It sets the noun up so you can
exclaim about it: "when it comes to X - honestly!"

</div>

<pre>
{f|京都|きょうと}の{f|夏|なつ}の{f|暑|あつ}さ<b>といったら</b>、{f|外|そと}を{f|歩|ある}く{f|気|き}にもなれない。
The heat of a Kyoto summer - you don't even feel like stepping outside.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|京都|きょうと}の{f|夏|なつ}<b>というと</b>、{f|祇園祭|ぎおんまつり}を{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}す{f|人|ひと}が{f|多|おお}いでしょう。
Summer in Kyoto brings the Gion Festival to mind for most people.
</pre>

`といったら` almost always ends in something extreme - an exclamation, a negative you
cannot fix, a comparison nobody could beat. Used for a flat, neutral association it sounds
like you started an exclamation and forgot to finish it.

### というと vs なら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both pick up what the other person said. <code>というと</code> asks
what it means or what it evokes; `なら` accepts it as a premise and gives advice.

</div>

<pre>
「{f|京都|きょうと}に{f|行|い}こうと{f|思|おも}っています。」「{f|京都|きょうと}<b>なら</b>、{f|秋|あき}がおすすめですよ。」
"I'm thinking of going to Kyoto." "If it's Kyoto, I'd recommend autumn."
</pre>

<pre>
「{f|京都|きょうと}に{f|行|い}こうと{f|思|おも}っています。」「{f|京都|きょうと}<b>というと</b>、お{f|寺|てら}を{f|回|まわ}るんですか。」
"I'm thinking of going to Kyoto." "Kyoto - so you'll be doing the temples?"
</pre>

Swap them and both replies go wrong: `なら` cannot ask what a word meant, and
<code>というと</code> cannot recommend a season.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|春|はる}になるというと、{f|花見|はなみ}に{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ {f|春|はる}になると、{f|花見|はなみ}に{f|行|い}きます。
  For "when spring comes", plain `と` is the conditional you want.
  <code>というと</code> needs a *word* or a *statement* in front of it, not an event.

- ❌ {f|京都|きょうと}だというと、お{f|寺|てら}ですね。 → ✅ {f|京都|きょうと}というと、お{f|寺|てら}ですね。
  Nouns attach directly. The `だ` version quotes a proposition - "if we say that it is
  Kyoto" - which is not what you mean.

- ❌ 「{f|時間|じかん}がなくて{f|困|こま}っています。」「というと、{f|手伝|てつだ}いましょうか。」 → ✅ 「それなら、{f|手伝|てつだ}いましょうか。」
  <code>というと</code> opens a question about meaning, so following it with an offer
  leaves the listener waiting for the rest of the question.

- ❌ {f|寿司|すし}というと、{f|私|わたし}は{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ {f|寿司|すし}といえば、この{f|前|まえ}おいしい{f|店|みせ}を{f|見|み}つけました。／{f|寿司|すし}は{f|好|す}きです。
  What follows <code>というと</code> should be what the word calls up *in general*, or a
  question about it - not a bare personal fact. For "as for sushi, I like it", plain `は`
  is enough.

## Practice

**1.** {f|日本|にほん}の{f|正月|しょうがつ}____、おせち{f|料理|りょうり}とお{f|雑煮|ぞうに}でしょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|日本|にほん}の{f|正月|しょうがつ}<b>というと</b>、…

The sentence gives what New Year in Japan automatically calls to mind, which is the
association use. `といえば` would also be natural here; `なら` would turn it into advice
about what to eat.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 「{f|来週|らいしゅう}、{f|大阪|おおさか}へ{f|出張|しゅっちょう}です。」「{f|大阪|おおさか}（というと・なら）、{f|安|やす}くていいホテルを{f|知|し}っていますよ。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|大阪|おおさか}<b>なら</b>、{f|安|やす}くていいホテルを{f|知|し}っていますよ。

The reply takes the other person's Osaka trip as a given and offers help on that basis - 
advice, which is `なら`. <code>というと</code> would have to be followed by a question or
by what Osaka brings to mind.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "When you say 'tomorrow morning', do you mean before the meeting?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

「{f|明日|あした}の{f|朝|あさ}」<b>というと</b>、{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|前|まえ}ということですか。

This is the clarification use: you repeat the other person's words, attach
<code>というと</code>, and ask what they cover. <code>…ということですか</code> is the
standard way to close such a question.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|冬|ふゆ}だというと、みかんが{f|食|た}べたくなります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|冬|ふゆ}というと、みかんが{f|食|た}べたくなります。

Drop the `だ` - a noun joins <code>というと</code> directly. With `だ` you are quoting the
statement "it is winter", which turns the sentence into something closer to "if someone
tells me it's winter".

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|山|やま}の{f|上|うえ}の{f|寒|さむ}さ<b>というと</b>、{f|想像|そうぞう}より{f|厳|きび}しいです。
(b) {f|山|やま}の{f|上|うえ}の{f|寒|さむ}さ<b>といったら</b>、{f|手袋|てぶくろ}をしていても{f|指|ゆび}の{f|感覚|かんかく}がなくなるほどでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a measured association: mention the cold up there and this is what it amounts to.
It reads as information, and it could open a guidebook paragraph.

(b) is an exclamation. `といったら` sets the noun up so the speaker can pile on how extreme
it was, and the sentence duly ends in something you could not exaggerate further. It is
speech, it is personal, and it expects a reaction.

</details>
