# つまり

**Meaning** · in other words; which means - restating something more plainly, or
spelling out what it amounts to

`つまり` announces that the same content is coming round again in a clearer shape.
It does two jobs with one word: rewording something complicated, and stating the
consequence nobody has said out loud yet.

## Usage

`つまり` is an adverb and sits at the front of the clause it introduces. Nothing
attaches to it, and it never changes form.

The habit worth building is what comes at the **end** of that clause. A `つまり`
sentence very often closes with <code>ということだ</code>, <code>わけだ</code> or `のだ`,
because you are labelling a conclusion rather than reporting a fresh fact:

- つまり、{f|来|こ}られないということですね。
- {f|三年|さんねん}{f|住|す}んでいた。つまり、{f|地元|じもと}のようなものだ。

The two jobs, one line each:

- **Rewording** - the second half says the same thing in easier words.
  {f|検討|けんとう}します。つまり、{f|今|いま}は{f|決|き}められないということです。
- **Consequence** - the second half is what the first one comes to.
  {f|終電|しゅうでん}はもう{f|出|で}た。つまり、{f|歩|ある}いて{f|帰|かえ}るしかない。

As a standalone check on your understanding, `つまり` with a rising question is
extremely common: 「つまり、{f|明日|あした}は{f|休|やす}みってこと?」

## Examples

<pre>
{f|父|ちち}の{f|姉|あね}、<b>つまり</b>おばです。
My father's older sister - my aunt, in other words.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}がない。<b>つまり</b>、{f|断|ことわ}られたということだ。
There's been no reply. Which means I've been turned down.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|材料費|ざいりょうひ}も{f|送料|そうりょう}も{f|上|あ}がっている。<b>つまり</b>、{f|値上|ねあ}げは{f|避|さ}けられないわけです。
Materials and shipping have both gone up. In other words, a price rise can't be avoided.
</pre>

<pre>
「{f|前向|まえむ}きに{f|検討|けんとう}します」って{f|言|い}われたんだけど、<b>つまり</b>{f|断|ことわ}られたってこと?
They said they'd "consider it positively" - so does that mean I've been refused?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and very frequent. It is at home in a lecture, a contract summary,
a news broadcast and a conversation between friends, with no change of form.

In speech it has a second life as a filler. Some speakers open every third sentence
with つまり、つまりですね… while they assemble a thought, and the word then carries
no summarising meaning at all. Worth recognising, not worth imitating.

One genuine risk: aiming `つまり` at what *somebody else* just said can sound like
a correction - "so what you actually meant was…". A softener fixes it:
つまり、こういうことでしょうか. Aimed at your own words it is completely safe, and
in a presentation it is a useful signpost that the summary has arrived.

`つまり` is not a cause-and-effect connector. `だから` and したがって say "therefore,
here is a new fact"; `つまり` says "the same fact, re-expressed". When the second
sentence contains information the first one did not, you want `だから`.

## Contrast

### つまり vs {f|要|よう}するに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `つまり` re-expresses; <code>{f|要|よう}するに</code> compresses. The
second one claims the right to decide what the point was.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}が{f|変|か}わりました。<b>つまり</b>、{f|駅|えき}の{f|南口|みなみぐち}に{f|集合|しゅうごう}です。
The venue has changed - that is, we meet at the south exit of the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}は{f|長|なが}かったが、<b>{f|要|よう}するに</b>{f|予算|よさん}が{f|足|た}りないのだ。
The explanation went on a while, but the gist is that there isn't enough budget.
</pre>

<code>{f|要|よう}するに</code> implies the original was long-winded, so pointing it at a
superior's remarks is risky. `つまり` carries no such judgement.

### つまり vs すなわち

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same job, different clothes. すなわち is the written, formal
register: it puts an equals sign between two terms.

</div>

<pre>
{f|日本|にほん}の{f|首都|しゅと}、<b>すなわち</b>{f|東京|とうきょう}に{f|本社|ほんしゃ}を{f|置|お}いている。
Our head office is in the capital of Japan, namely Tokyo.
</pre>

In conversation すなわち sounds like a legal document being read aloud. Reach for
`つまり` when you are speaking, and keep すなわち for formal writing, where it is
usually left in kana.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っていた。つまり、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っていた。<b>だから</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。
  The cancellation is a new event, not a restatement of the rain. `つまり` needs
  the second sentence to be the *same* content in another form.

- ❌ つまり{f|三人|さんにん}{f|来|き}ました。 → ✅ {f|全部|ぜんぶ}で{f|三人|さんにん}{f|来|き}ました。
  `つまり` cannot introduce a plain new fact out of nowhere. Something has to have
  been said already for it to restate.

- ❌ {f|要|よう}するに、{f|先生|せんせい}のおっしゃりたいことは…（said to your own teacher）→ ✅ <b>つまり</b>、{f|先生|せんせい}のおっしゃりたいことは…
  Summarising a superior's words with <code>{f|要|よう}するに</code> hints that they
  rambled. `つまり` is the neutral choice whenever the words being restated are not
  your own.

## Practice

**1.** {f|木曜|もくよう}も{f|金曜|きんよう}も{f|予定|よてい}が{f|入|はい}っている。____、{f|今週|こんしゅう}はもう{f|時間|じかん}がない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>つまり</b>、{f|今週|こんしゅう}はもう{f|時間|じかん}がない。

The second sentence adds no new information - it is the first one seen from a step
back. That is exactly the restating job, and `だから` would also be possible if you
wanted to present it as a result rather than a rewording.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: {f|部長|ぶちょう}のお{f|話|はなし}は、{f|要|よう}するに{f|予算|よさん}を{f|減|へ}らせということですね。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}のお{f|話|はなし}は、<b>つまり</b>{f|予算|よさん}を{f|減|へ}らせということですね。

Grammatically the original is fine, but <code>{f|要|よう}するに</code> tells your manager
that his explanation needed boiling down. `つまり` restates without passing
judgement on the length of the original.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会員|かいいん}は{f|無料|むりょう}です。つまり、お{f|金|かね}はかかりません。
(b) {f|会員|かいいん}は{f|無料|むりょう}です。だから、みんな{f|入|はい}りたがります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In (a) the two halves are the same fact twice, the second time in plainer words, so
`つまり` is doing a translation job for the listener. In (b) the second half is a
separate fact that follows from the first, which calls for a causal connector. The
test is simple: if deleting the second clause loses no information at all, `つまり`
is the word you want.

</details>
