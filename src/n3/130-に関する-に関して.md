# ～に関する / ～に関して

**Meaning** · concerning; relating to; in connection with - the formal, largely written cousin of について（かんする・かんして）

<code>に{f|関|かん}する</code> and <code>に{f|関|かん}して</code> mark the subject matter of a
statement, a document, a rule or a piece of research. They mean what `について`
means, but they belong to a different register - reports, notices, contracts,
academic writing, the news - and they draw the topic **wider**: not just the thing
named, but everything connected to it.

The two forms are one word doing two jobs. <code>{f|関|かん}する</code> is the dictionary
form of a verb, so it modifies **nouns**. <code>{f|関|かん}して</code> is its て-form, so it
modifies **verbs**.

## Formation

`に` + <code>{f|関|かん}する</code> ("to be connected to"). The noun in front takes `に`,
never `を`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun, modifying a noun | Noun + に{f|関|かん}する + Noun | {f|安全|あんぜん} → {f|安全|あんぜん}に{f|関|かん}する{f|規則|きそく} |
| Noun, modifying a verb | Noun + に{f|関|かん}して + verb | {f|費用|ひよう} → {f|費用|ひよう}に{f|関|かん}して{f|話|はな}す |
| Noun, as topic | Noun + に{f|関|かん}しては | その{f|点|てん} → その{f|点|てん}に{f|関|かん}しては |
| Noun, as addition | Noun + に{f|関|かん}しても | {f|人数|にんずう} → {f|人数|にんずう}に{f|関|かん}しても |
| Noun, business / keigo | Noun + に{f|関|かん}しまして（は） | {f|納期|のうき} → {f|納期|のうき}に{f|関|かん}しましては |
| Noun, modifying a noun (rarer) | Noun + に{f|関|かん}しての + Noun | {f|事故|じこ} → {f|事故|じこ}に{f|関|かん}しての{f|報告|ほうこく} |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|関|かん}する</code> before a noun, <code>{f|関|かん}して</code>
before a verb. <code>{f|関|かん}するの</code> does not exist: the `の` you are reaching for
is already built into the verb form.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
これは{f|安全|あんぜん}に<b>{f|関|かん}する</b>{f|規則|きそく}です。
These are the safety rules.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|点|てん}に<b>{f|関|かん}して</b>は、{f|後|のち}ほどお{f|答|こた}えします。
I'll answer that point later.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|健康|けんこう}に<b>{f|関|かん}する</b>{f|本|ほん}を{f|何冊|なんさつ}も{f|買|か}ってしまった。
I've ended up buying any number of books on health.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|料金|りょうきん}に<b>{f|関|かん}して</b>ご{f|質問|しつもん}のある{f|方|かた}は、{f|受付|うけつけ}までお{f|越|こ}しください。
If you have questions concerning the fees, please come to the reception desk.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|気候|きこう}の{f|変化|へんか}に<b>{f|関|かん}する</b>{f|研究|けんきゅう}が{f|世界中|せかいじゅう}で{f|進|すす}められている。
Research relating to climate change is being carried forward all over the world.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|事件|じけん}に<b>{f|関|かん}して</b>、{f|警察|けいさつ}はまだ{f|何|なに}も{f|発表|はっぴょう}していない。
The police have released nothing in connection with this incident.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|給与|きゅうよ}に<b>{f|関|かん}して</b>は{f|不満|ふまん}はないが、{f|残業|ざんぎょう}の{f|多|おお}さに<b>{f|関|かん}して</b>は{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|考|かんが}え{f|直|なお}してほしい。
I have no complaints where pay is concerned, but on the amount of overtime I'd like the company to think again.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|個人|こじん}{f|情報|じょうほう}の{f|扱|あつか}いに<b>{f|関|かん}しまして</b>は、{f|当社|とうしゃ}のホームページをご{f|覧|らん}ください。
Regarding the handling of personal data, please see our website.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a **written** pattern first and a formal-spoken pattern second. Its natural
homes are notices, headings, minutes, contracts, textbooks, news copy and the
polished parts of a presentation. If you say it to a friend over coffee you will
sound like you are reading from a slide.

A useful gauge: the more institutional the speaker, the more likely
<code>に{f|関|かん}して</code> is. A company says
<code>{f|停電|ていでん}に{f|関|かん}してお{f|知|し}らせします</code>; a housemate says
<code>{f|停電|ていでん}のことなんだけど</code>. Both are correct in their own place, and
swapping them is the mistake.

<code>に{f|関|かん}しまして</code> raises it one more step, into customer-facing business
Japanese. It is extremely common in announcements and service email, and you should
expect to hear it far more often than you produce it.

There is also a **width** difference that survives the register difference.
`について` picks out a topic; <code>に{f|関|かん}して</code> draws a circle around it.
<code>{f|台風|たいふう}に{f|関|かん}する{f|情報|じょうほう}</code> covers the typhoon, the
train stoppages, the school closures and the shop opening hours - everything
connected to it. <code>{f|台風|たいふう}についての{f|情報|じょうほう}</code> is more likely
to be information about the typhoon itself. This is a tendency rather than a hard
line, but it is why headings and document titles overwhelmingly prefer
<code>に{f|関|かん}する</code>.

Finally, do not confuse the phrase with the noun <code>{f|関係|かんけい}</code>.
<code>{f|関係|かんけい}がある</code> claims a real-world connection between two things;
<code>に{f|関|かん}する</code> merely announces a topic. <code>この{f|問題|もんだい}に{f|関|かん}する{f|資料|しりょう}</code>
is "material on this problem"; <code>この{f|問題|もんだい}と{f|関係|かんけい}がある{f|資料|しりょう}</code>
is "material that turns out to be connected to this problem."

## Contrast

### ～に{f|関|かん}して vs ～について

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different clothes. `について` is the everyday
form and works in speech; <code>に{f|関|かん}して</code> is formal, mostly written, and
covers a broader area around the topic.

</div>

Nearly every sentence accepts both. Choose by asking who is speaking and where.

<pre>
{f|夏休|なつやす}みの{f|予定|よてい}<b>について</b>{f|家族|かぞく}で{f|話|はな}した。
The family talked about plans for the summer holiday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|市|し}の{f|予算|よさん}に<b>{f|関|かん}して</b>、{f|議会|ぎかい}で{f|激|はげ}しい{f|議論|ぎろん}があった。
There was a fierce debate in the assembly concerning the city budget.
</pre>

Put <code>に{f|関|かん}して</code> in the first sentence and the family sounds like a
board of directors. Put `について` in the second and nothing breaks - it is simply
one notch less formal than assembly minutes would be.

### ～に{f|関|かん}する + Noun vs ～についての + Noun

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both attach a topic to a noun, but the shapes differ:
<code>に{f|関|かん}する</code> takes **no** `の`, <code>について</code> takes an obligatory one.
Titles, headings and formal documents prefer <code>に{f|関|かん}する</code>.

</div>

<pre>
{f|留学生|りゅうがくせい}の{f|住|す}まいに<b>{f|関|かん}する</b>アンケート
A survey concerning housing for international students
</pre>

<pre>
{f|留学生|りゅうがくせい}の{f|住|す}まい<b>についての</b>アンケート
A survey about housing for international students
</pre>

The second is perfectly good Japanese and is what you would say out loud. The first
is what would be printed at the top of the form. Note the ❌<code>に{f|関|かん}するの</code>
error is impossible to make with `について`, and the ❌<code>について{f|質問|しつもん}</code>
error (dropping `の`) is impossible to make with <code>に{f|関|かん}する</code> - the two
patterns fail in opposite directions.

### ～に{f|関|かん}して vs ～において

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|関|かん}して</code> gives the **topic**; `において` gives
the **setting** - the place, the era, the field or the situation in which something
holds.

</div>

Both are formal, both translate as "in" or "regarding" at some point in a
dictionary, and both appear in the same kind of document, so they blur together.

<pre>
{f|安全|あんぜん}{f|対策|たいさく}に<b>{f|関|かん}して</b>、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|案|あん}が{f|出|だ}された。
A new proposal has been put forward concerning safety measures.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|建設|けんせつ}{f|現場|げんば}<b>において</b>、{f|安全|あんぜん}{f|対策|たいさく}は{f|何|なに}よりも{f|優先|ゆうせん}される。
On construction sites, safety measures take priority over everything.
</pre>

In the first, safety measures are what the proposal is *about*. In the second, the
building site is *where* the rule applies. If the noun could be preceded by "on the
subject of", it is <code>に{f|関|かん}して</code>; if it could be preceded by "in" or "at",
it is `において`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|環境|かんきょう}に{f|関|かん}する<b>の</b>{f|問題|もんだい} → ✅ {f|環境|かんきょう}に<b>{f|関|かん}する</b>{f|問題|もんだい}
  <code>{f|関|かん}する</code> is already a verb in its noun-modifying form, so it attaches
  to the noun directly. The `の` belongs to <code>についての</code>, not here.

- ❌ {f|事故|じこ}に{f|関|かん}して{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}を{f|見|み}せてください。（intending "the report on the accident"） → ✅ {f|事故|じこ}に<b>{f|関|かん}する</b>{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}を{f|見|み}せてください。
  Before a noun you need <code>{f|関|かん}する</code>. <code>{f|関|かん}して</code> would
  attach the topic to <code>{f|見|み}せる</code> instead - "show me a report, on the
  subject of the accident."

- ❌ ゆうべの{f|飲|の}み{f|会|かい}に{f|関|かん}して{f|話|はな}そうよ。 → ✅ ゆうべの{f|飲|の}み{f|会|かい}<b>のこと</b>{f|話|はな}そうよ。
  Not a grammar error - a register error. Casual invitations do not take formal
  written vocabulary; the sentence sounds like a parody of a manager.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}に{f|関|かん}して、この{f|決定|けってい}は{f|残念|ざんねん}です。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>にとって</b>、この{f|決定|けってい}は{f|残念|ざんねん}です。
  "For me" as a standpoint is `にとって`. <code>に{f|関|かん}して</code> would make *you*
  the subject matter of the decision.

## Practice

**1.** {f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|社員|しゃいん}の{f|健康|けんこう}に____{f|調査|ちょうさ}を{f|行|おこな}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|健康|けんこう}に<b>{f|関|かん}する</b>{f|調査|ちょうさ}を{f|行|おこな}った。

The topic is being hung on the noun <code>{f|調査|ちょうさ}</code>, so you need the
noun-modifying form <code>{f|関|かん}する</code> - with no `の` after it.
<code>{f|健康|けんこう}についての{f|調査|ちょうさ}</code> would also work and would sound
slightly less official.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|場所|ばしょ}（に{f|関|かん}して・において）は、また{f|連絡|れんらく}します。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|場所|ばしょ}<b>に{f|関|かん}して</b>は、また{f|連絡|れんらく}します。

The venue is the **subject matter** of the message you will send, not the place
where the sending happens. `において` would have to mean "I will contact you at the
meeting venue", which is not what the sentence is doing.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, in the register of a company notice: "We are currently investigating the cause of the problem. We will inform you separately regarding compensation."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|現在|げんざい}、{f|問題|もんだい}の{f|原因|げんいん}を{f|調査|ちょうさ}しております。{f|補償|ほしょう}に<b>{f|関|かん}しまして</b>は、{f|別|べつ}にご{f|連絡|れんらく}いたします。

A public notice is exactly where <code>に{f|関|かん}しまして</code> belongs, and the `は`
after it marks compensation as a separate item being set aside for later.
<code>{f|補償|ほしょう}については</code> would be acceptable but slightly plainer than a
formal apology notice usually goes.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|新|あたら}しい{f|制度|せいど}に{f|関|かん}してのついて{f|説明|せつめい}は、{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|行|おこな}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|新|あたら}しい{f|制度|せいど}に<b>{f|関|かん}する</b>{f|説明|せつめい}は、{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|行|おこな}います。

Two patterns have been welded together. Pick one:
<code>{f|制度|せいど}に{f|関|かん}する{f|説明|せつめい}</code> or
<code>{f|制度|せいど}についての{f|説明|せつめい}</code>. Both mean "the explanation of the
new system"; the first is the one you would print on an agenda.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|地震|じしん}に<b>{f|関|かん}する</b>{f|情報|じょうほう}
(b) {f|地震|じしん}<b>についての</b>{f|情報|じょうほう}

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the phrase a broadcaster or a city website would use, and it takes in
everything connected to the earthquake - the tremor itself, the damage, the trains,
the evacuation centres. The topic is drawn as a **field**.

(b) is what you would say in conversation, and it points more narrowly at
information **about the earthquake itself**: how big it was, where it struck. The
difference is a tendency rather than a rule, but the register difference is real
and reliable: (a) is written and institutional, (b) is spoken and ordinary.

</details>
