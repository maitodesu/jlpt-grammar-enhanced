# ～ずに

**Meaning** · without doing - the written, slightly formal twin of `～ないで`

`ずに` and `ないで` mean the same thing. What separates them is texture: `ずに` is
tighter, older and more literary, and it belongs to writing, to careful speech and
to a large family of set expressions. Learn the two irregular stems - 
<code>せずに</code> and <code>{f|来|こ}ずに</code> - and the rest is
straightforward.

## Formation

Take the ない-form, drop the ない, add `ずに`. Two verbs break the rule.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ～ない stem + ずに | {f|言|い}わない → {f|言|い}わずに |
| Verb (る-verb) | ～ない stem + ずに | {f|食|た}べない → {f|食|た}べずに |
| Verb (する) | **せずに** | {f|連絡|れんらく}する → {f|連絡|れんらく}せずに |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | <b>{f|来|こ}ずに</b> | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ずに |
| Joining clauses (literary) | ～ず、 | {f|言|い}わず、{f|去|さ}っていった |
| Common set forms | ～ずにすむ・～ずにおく・～ずにいる | {f|行|い}かずにすんだ |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ❌<code>しずに</code> is the commonest error on this page.
`する` does not use its ordinary stem here; the form is <code>せずに</code>, from
an older negative. Likewise <code>{f|来|く}る</code> gives
<code>{f|来|こ}ずに</code>, never ❌<code>きずに</code>.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>ある</code> and adjectives have no `ずに` form. "Because there
wasn't any" is <code>なくて</code>, and that is a different pattern with a
different job.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|朝|あさ}ごはんを{f|食|た}べ<b>ずに</b>{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}た。
I left the house without eating breakfast.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|何|なに}も{f|言|い}わ<b>ずに</b>{f|帰|かえ}ってしまった。
He went home without saying a word.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}た<b>ずに</b>{f|出|で}かけて、ずぶぬれになりました。
I went out without an umbrella and got soaked.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|誰|だれ}にも{f|相談|そうだん}せ<b>ずに</b>、{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|決|き}めてしまった。
I decided it on my own, without consulting anybody.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}は{f|靴|くつ}もぬが<b>ずに</b>{f|部屋|へや}に{f|上|あ}がってきた。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|辞書|じしょ}を{f|使|つか}わ<b>ずに</b>この{f|文章|ぶんしょう}が{f|読|よ}めたら、なかなかの{f|実力|じつりょく}です。
If you can read this passage without a dictionary, you're doing rather well.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨夜|さくや}は{f|一睡|いっすい}もせ<b>ずに</b>{f|原稿|げんこう}を{f|書|か}いていました。
I was up writing the manuscript last night without a wink of sleep.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事前|じぜん}に{f|連絡|れんらく}せ<b>ずに</b>{f|訪|たず}ねるのは、{f|日本|にほん}では{f|失礼|しつれい}にあたります。
Calling on someone without letting them know in advance counts as rude in Japan.
</pre>

<pre>
あきらめ<b>ずに</b>{f|続|つづ}けていれば、いつか{f|結果|けっか}はついてくるものです。
If you keep going without giving up, results do come eventually.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ずに` is a survival from classical Japanese, and it still carries a faint
tightness that `ないで` does not. In writing - essays, news, instructions, business
email - it is the default. In conversation, `ないで` is the default, and `ずに`
sounds a shade formal or deliberate: <code>{f|寝|ね}ずに{f|待|ま}っていた</code> is
perfectly sayable, just slightly more composed than
<code>{f|寝|ね}ないで{f|待|ま}っていた</code>.

Certain combinations, however, are `ずに` only or nearly so, and they are worth
learning whole:

- <code>～ずにすむ</code> - "get away without having to":
  {f|払|はら}わずにすんだ.
- <code>～ずにはいられない</code> - "can't help doing".
- <code>～ずじまい</code> - "ended up never doing it":
  {f|会|あ}えずじまいだった.

The bare `ず` without `に` is a further step into literary Japanese. It links
clauses rather than modifying a verb - 
<code>{f|振|ふ}り{f|返|かえ}らず、{f|歩|ある}き{f|続|つづ}けた</code> - and it belongs
to novels, speeches and song lyrics. Recognise it; write `ずに`.

The one thing `ずに` cannot do is make a **request**. `ないで` has a second life as
a negative command - <code>{f|触|さわ}らないでください</code> - and `ずに` has no
share in it. ❌<code>{f|触|さわ}らずにください</code> is not a formal version of
anything.

## Contrast

### ～ずに vs ～ないで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register: `ずに` written and formal,
`ないで` spoken and neutral. But only `ないで` can form a **request**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}ま<b>ないで</b>{f|寝|ね}てしまった。
I fell asleep without taking my medicine.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|所定|しょてい}の{f|欄|らん}に{f|記入|きにゅう}せ<b>ずに</b>{f|提出|ていしゅつ}しないでください。
Please do not submit the form without filling in the designated fields.
</pre>

The second sentence shows both at once, and the division of labour is exact: `ずに`
does the "without", `ないで` does the "please don't". Swapping them produces
❌<code>{f|記入|きにゅう}しないで{f|提出|ていしゅつ}せずにください</code>, which is
not repairable.

Note also that the choice is partly one of **rhythm**: a formal written sentence
that has already used `ないで` for its request will reach for `ずに` for the
"without", simply to avoid saying ない twice.

### ～ずに vs ～なくて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ずに` says **without doing it**, describing how the main action
happened. `なくて` says **because it didn't happen**, giving a cause.

</div>

<pre>
{f|地図|ちず}を{f|見|み}<b>ずに</b>{f|駅|えき}まで{f|行|い}けました。
I made it to the station without looking at a map.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|地図|ちず}が{f|読|よ}め<b>なくて</b>、{f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}けませんでした。
I couldn't read the map, so I never got to the station.
</pre>

The first describes your method; the second explains a failure. A quick test: if
you could insert "because" into the English, you want `なくて`. `なくて` also takes
adjectives and `ある` (<code>お{f|金|かね}がなくて</code>), which `ずに` cannot
touch at all.

### ～ずに vs ～ないまま

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ずに` modifies **the action**: you did B without doing A.
`ないまま` describes **a state left unchanged**: A never got done, and things
stayed that way.

</div>

<pre>
{f|電気|でんき}を{f|消|け}さ<b>ずに</b>{f|出|で}かけてしまった。
I went out without turning the light off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電気|でんき}が{f|消|き}え<b>ないまま</b>、{f|三日|みっか}{f|留守|るす}にしていた。
The light stayed on the whole three days I was away.
</pre>

`ずに` is tied to a single moment - the moment of leaving. `ないまま` stretches over
a period and emphasises that nothing changed during it. Where both are possible,
`ないまま` adds a sense of something being left unresolved:
<code>{f|理由|りゆう}を{f|聞|き}かないまま{f|別|わか}れた</code> lingers in a way
<code>{f|聞|き}かずに{f|別|わか}れた</code> does not.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|予約|よやく}**しずに**{f|行|い}ったら、{f|満席|まんせき}でした。 → ✅ {f|予約|よやく}<b>せずに</b>{f|行|い}ったら、{f|満席|まんせき}でした。
  `する` becomes <code>せずに</code>. This is irregular and there is no way to
  derive it from the ordinary stem - memorise it.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|誰|だれ}も<b>{f|来|き}ずに</b>、{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|片付|かたづ}けた。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|誰|だれ}も<b>{f|来|こ}ずに</b>、{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|片付|かたづ}けた。
  <code>{f|来|く}る</code> uses こ- here, the same stem as
  <code>{f|来|こ}ない</code>. き- belongs to <code>{f|来|き}ます</code>.

- ❌ {f|廊下|ろうか}を{f|走|はし}ら**ずにください**。 → ✅ {f|廊下|ろうか}を{f|走|はし}ら<b>ないでください</b>。
  `ずに` never forms a request. There is no formal-sounding upgrade of
  <code>ないでください</code> along these lines.

- ❌ {f|時間|じかん}が**なずに**、{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|見|み}られなかった。 → ✅ {f|時間|じかん}が<b>なくて</b>、{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|見|み}られなかった。
  `ある` has no `ずに` form, and in any case this sentence is giving a reason,
  which is `なくて` territory.

## Practice

**1.** {f|急|いそ}いでいたので、{f|朝|あさ}ごはんも{f|食|た}べ____{f|会社|かいしゃ}へ{f|向|む}かった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|朝|あさ}ごはんも{f|食|た}べ<b>ずに</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}へ{f|向|む}かった。

The sentence describes *how* you set off - without eating - so it wants `ずに`, not
`なくて`. <code>{f|食|た}べないで</code> would be equally correct and simply sound
more conversational.

</details>

**2.** Choose: レポートは{f|下書|したが}きを（せずに・しなくて）いきなり{f|書|か}き{f|始|はじ}めました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|下書|したが}きを<b>せずに</b>いきなり{f|書|か}き{f|始|はじ}めました。

This is the method you used, not the reason for something, so `ずに` is right - and
note the irregular <code>せずに</code> for `する`. <code>しなくて</code> would try
to make the missing draft the *cause* of starting, which is not what the sentence
means.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He left without telling anyone where he was going."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}はどこへ{f|行|い}くのか{f|誰|だれ}にも{f|言|い}わ<b>ずに</b>{f|出|で}ていった。

The "without" attaches to the leaving, so `ずに` (or the more colloquial
<code>{f|言|い}わないで</code>) is the form. In a novel you might see the bare
literary <code>{f|言|い}わず、{f|出|で}ていった</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|危|あぶ}ないですから、{f|工事中|こうじちゅう}の{f|場所|ばしょ}には{f|近|ちか}づかずにください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|危|あぶ}ないですから、{f|工事中|こうじちゅう}の{f|場所|ばしょ}には<b>{f|近|ちか}づかないでください</b>。

`ずに` cannot carry a request. However formal the notice, a negative instruction
takes <code>ないでください</code> - or, in stiffer written style,
<code>{f|近|ちか}づかないこと</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|返事|へんじ}を{f|待|ま}たずに{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}した。
(b) {f|返事|へんじ}が{f|来|こ}なくて{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}できなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) describes the manner of departing: you chose to go, and the reply had not come
yet. The action succeeded, and the missing reply is background.

(b) gives a cause: no reply arrived, and *therefore* you could not set off. The
action failed, and the missing reply is the reason. `ずに` never carries that
causal weight - it only tells you what accompanied the action, or rather what
didn't.

</details>
