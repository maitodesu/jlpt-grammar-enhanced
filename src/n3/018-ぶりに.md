# ～ぶりに

**Meaning** · for the first time in - that much time has passed since it last happened, and now it has happened again

`ぶりに` measures a **gap**. You attach it to a length of time, and the sentence
says two things at once: the last time was that long ago, and it has just
happened again. Both halves matter - if it has not happened again, `ぶりに` is the
wrong word.

## Formation

`ぶり` is a suffix. It attaches directly to a quantity of time, with no particle
in between, and takes `に` when it modifies a verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Length of time + verb | Quantity + ぶりに | {f|三年|さんねん} → {f|三年|さんねん}ぶりに{f|会|あ}う |
| As a predicate | Quantity + ぶりだ／です | {f|十年|じゅうねん}ぶりです |
| Before a noun | Quantity + ぶりの + Noun | {f|五年|ごねん}ぶりの{f|優勝|ゆうしょう} |
| Vague length | {f|久|ひさ}し + ぶりに | {f|久|ひさ}しぶりに{f|話|はな}した |
| As a greeting | お{f|久|ひさ}しぶりです | - |
| ❌ な before a noun | ❌{f|三年|さんねん}ぶりな{f|再会|さいかい} | ✅{f|三年|さんねん}ぶりの{f|再会|さいかい} |
| ❌ Event noun | ❌{f|試験|しけん}ぶりに | ✅{f|試験|しけん}の{f|日|ひ}{f|以来|いらい} |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - what goes in front of `ぶり` is a **stretch of time**, never an
event. {f|三年|さんねん}ぶり and {f|久|ひさ}しぶり are fine;
❌{f|卒業|そつぎょう}ぶり is not. For an event, use
<code>～{f|以来|いらい}</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|三年|さんねん}<b>ぶりに</b>{f|国|くに}へ{f|帰|かえ}った。
I went home for the first time in three years.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|久|ひさ}し<b>ぶりに</b>{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}った。
I went to the cinema for the first time in ages.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}<b>ぶりに</b>{f|晴|は}れましたね。
It's the first clear day in a week, isn't it?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|十年|じゅうねん}<b>ぶりに</b>{f|中学|ちゅうがく}の{f|友|とも}だちから{f|連絡|れんらく}が{f|来|き}た。
A friend from junior high got in touch after ten years.
</pre>

<pre>
うちのチームは{f|五年|ごねん}<b>ぶりの</b>{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}を{f|果|は}たした。
Our team took the title for the first time in five years.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今日|きょう}は{f|三十五度|さんじゅうごど}を{f|超|こ}えて、{f|八年|はちねん}<b>ぶりの</b>{f|暑|あつ}さだそうだ。
It's over thirty-five degrees today - apparently the hottest in eight years.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|忙|いそが}しくて、{f|半年|はんとし}<b>ぶりに</b>{f|美容院|びよういん}へ{f|行|い}ったら、{f|担当|たんとう}の{f|人|ひと}が{f|変|か}わっていた。
Work kept me away, and when I finally got to the hairdresser after six months my usual stylist had gone.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|退院|たいいん}して、{f|二|に}か{f|月|げつ}<b>ぶりに</b>{f|自分|じぶん}のベッドで{f|眠|ねむ}ることができた。
I came out of hospital and slept in my own bed for the first time in two months.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ぶりに` is register-neutral and extremely common in ordinary speech. It carries a
small lift of feeling - pleasure, relief, or simply the sense that a long stretch
has been broken. Sports reporting loves it
({f|三十年|さんじゅうねん}ぶりの{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}), weather reports love it
({f|十年|じゅうねん}ぶりの{f|大雪|おおゆき}), and so does anyone greeting an old
friend.

The greeting forms are worth learning as fixed items.
{f|久|ひさ}しぶり! is casual, お{f|久|ひさ}しぶりです is the everyday polite
version, and in business writing the formal equivalent is
ご{f|無沙汰|ぶさた}しております, which does not use `ぶり` at all. Note the shape:
the greeting has **no** `に`, because it is a predicate - お{f|久|ひさ}しぶりです,
not ❌お{f|久|ひさ}しぶりにです. The `に` appears only when `ぶり` is modifying a
verb.

Speakers happily stretch the pattern for comic effect over absurdly short
intervals - {f|五分|ごふん}ぶりだね, "long time no see (five minutes)". That is a
joke, and it works because the pattern normally implies a genuinely long gap.

<div class="note">

**Note** - a different `ぶり` means "the manner of doing something":
{f|仕事|しごと}ぶり, {f|話|はな}しぶり, {f|飲|の}みっぷり. It attaches to a verb
stem or an activity noun rather than to a length of time, and it never takes the
"for the first time in" reading. {f|彼|かれ}の{f|仕事|しごと}ぶりには{f|感心|かんしん}した
is about *how* he works, not about how long it had been.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～ぶりに vs ～{f|後|ご}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|後|ご}に</code> counts **forward** from a moment already
in the story. `ぶりに` counts **backward** to the last time the same thing
happened.

</div>

<pre>
{f|卒業|そつぎょう}の{f|三年|さんねん}{f|後|ご}に、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|作|つく}った。
Three years after graduating, he started a company.
</pre>

The three years run from graduation to the founding - two different events. Now
the same number doing the other job:

<pre>
{f|三年|さんねん}<b>ぶりに</b>{f|同|おな}じ{f|店|みせ}で{f|待|ま}ち{f|合|あ}わせた。
We met up at the same place for the first time in three years.
</pre>

Here the three years run from the *previous* meeting to this one - the same event
twice. That is the whole distinction, and it is why
{f|三年|さんねん}{f|後|ご}に{f|会|あ}った and
{f|三年|さんねん}ぶりに{f|会|あ}った describe quite different situations. Note the
reading too: in this counting sense, <code>{f|後|ご}</code> is read ご, not あと.

### ～ぶりに vs {f|久|ひさ}しぶりに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same pattern, different precision.
<code>{f|久|ひさ}しぶりに</code> is the version for when you do not want to name a
number, and it is far more frequent in conversation.

</div>

<pre>
{f|久|ひさ}し<b>ぶりに</b>{f|母|はは}の{f|声|こえ}を{f|聞|き}いて、ほっとした。
Hearing my mother's voice after so long was a relief.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|四年|よねん}<b>ぶりに</b>{f|母|はは}に{f|会|あ}い、{f|思|おも}っていたより{f|元気|げんき}そうで{f|安心|あんしん}した。
I saw my mother for the first time in four years, and she looked better than I'd expected.
</pre>

Naming the number makes the sentence a small piece of news, which is why headlines
and announcements prefer it. Leaving it vague makes the sentence about the
feeling. The one place they are not interchangeable is the greeting: only
{f|久|ひさ}しぶり works there, since ❌{f|四年|よねん}ぶりです! is a statement of
fact rather than a hello.

### ～ぶりに vs ～{f|以来|いらい}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ぶり` takes a **length of time**;
<code>{f|以来|いらい}</code> takes an **event or a point in time**, and it does not
require the thing to have happened again.

</div>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|夏|なつ}{f|以来|いらい}、{f|彼|かれ}とは{f|会|あ}っていない。
I haven't seen him since last summer.
</pre>

Notice the negative: nothing has happened again, and `ぶり` could not be used at
all. Compare a sentence whose whole point is that the streak has just ended:

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|夏|なつ}{f|以来|いらい}、{f|半年|はんとし}<b>ぶりに</b>{f|彼|かれ}と{f|会|あ}った。
I saw him for the first time in six months, the first time since last summer.
</pre>

The two happily sit in one sentence, each doing its own job:
<code>{f|以来|いらい}</code> names the starting event, `ぶりに` gives the length of
the gap. This also explains the commonest error with `ぶり` - attaching it to an
event, as in ❌{f|入学|にゅうがく}ぶりに. Swap in
<code>{f|入学|にゅうがく}{f|以来|いらい}</code> and the sentence works.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|卒業|そつぎょう}ぶりに{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|会|あ}いました。 → ✅ {f|卒業|そつぎょう}{f|以来|いらい}、{f|初|はじ}めて{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|会|あ}いました。
  `ぶり` needs a length of time in front of it. Graduation is an event, which
  takes <code>{f|以来|いらい}</code>.

- ❌ {f|十年|じゅうねん}ぶりな{f|再会|さいかい}でした。 → ✅ {f|十年|じゅうねん}ぶりの{f|再会|さいかい}でした。
  Before a noun the linker is `の`. `ぶり` behaves like a noun, not a
  な-adjective.

- ❌ お{f|久|ひさ}しぶりにですね。 → ✅ お{f|久|ひさ}しぶりですね。
  The `に` belongs only when `ぶり` modifies a verb. As a greeting it is a
  predicate and stands alone.

- ❌ {f|二年|にねん}{f|後|ご}に{f|温泉|おんせん}に{f|行|い}った。（intending "went to a hot spring for the first time in two years"） → ✅ {f|二年|にねん}ぶりに{f|温泉|おんせん}に{f|行|い}った。
  <code>{f|後|ご}に</code> would mean two years after some other event. To measure
  back to the previous visit, you need `ぶりに`.

## Practice

**1.** {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|過|す}ぎて、{f|三日|みっか}____{f|青|あお}い{f|空|そら}が{f|見|み}えた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|台風|たいふう}が{f|過|す}ぎて、{f|三日|みっか}<b>ぶりに</b>{f|青|あお}い{f|空|そら}が{f|見|み}えた。

Three days had passed since the sky was last blue, and now it is blue again - 
both halves of what `ぶりに` claims. The `に` is there because it modifies a verb.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|入学|にゅうがく}して{f|一年|いちねん}（{f|後|ご}に・ぶりに）、{f|初|はじ}めて{f|寮|りょう}を{f|出|で}た。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|入学|にゅうがく}して{f|一年|いちねん}<b>{f|後|ご}に</b>、{f|初|はじ}めて{f|寮|りょう}を{f|出|で}た。

The year is counted forward from enrolment, and leaving the dormitory is
happening for the very first time - so there is no previous occasion for `ぶり`
to measure back to.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I got a letter from my grandfather for the first time in two years."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|二年|にねん}<b>ぶりに</b>{f|祖父|そふ}から{f|手紙|てがみ}が{f|届|とど}いた。

"For the first time in two years" maps straight onto
{f|二年|にねん}ぶりに: a named gap, and the event recurring at the end of it.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|五年|ごねん}ぶりな{f|日本|にほん}へ{f|行|い}ってきました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|五年|ごねん}ぶりに{f|日本|にほん}へ{f|行|い}ってきました。

`ぶり` takes `に` in front of a verb and `の` in front of a noun; `な` is never an
option. Here the phrase modifies <code>{f|行|い}く</code>, so `に` is correct.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|五年|ごねん}<b>ぶりに</b>ピアノを{f|弾|ひ}いた。
(b) {f|五年|ごねん}{f|間|かん}、ピアノを{f|弾|ひ}いていなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) puts the emphasis on the moment the gap ended: the playing has just happened,
and the five years are there to tell you how remarkable that is. There is usually
a flicker of feeling attached.

(b) describes the gap itself and says nothing about whether it has ended. It is
the sentence you use to explain why your fingers are stiff, not to announce that
you have started again.

</details>
