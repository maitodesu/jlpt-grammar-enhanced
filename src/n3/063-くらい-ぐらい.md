# くらい / ぐらい

**Meaning** · about, roughly; to the extent that ～; and - dismissively - "no more than that"

`くらい` names a **degree**. Attached to a number it makes the number approximate
("about thirty minutes"); attached to a clause it measures one thing against
another ("so tired I couldn't stand"); attached to a small task it belittles it
("it's only washing up"). Those three uses look unrelated in English, but they are
one idea: this is the level we are talking about.

## Formation

`くらい` and `ぐらい` are the same word. Both attach the same way, and nothing about
the attachment decides which of the two you say.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Number + counter | Quantity + くらい／ぐらい | {f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん} → {f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}ぐらい |
| Noun | Noun + くらい | {f|挨拶|あいさつ} → {f|挨拶|あいさつ}くらい |
| Demonstrative | これ／それ／あれ／どれ + くらい | どれ → どれくらい |
| Verb | plain form + くらい | {f|泣|な}きたい → {f|泣|な}きたいくらい |
| い-adjective | plain form + くらい | {f|痛|いた}い → {f|痛|いた}いくらい |
| な-adjective | ～な + くらい | {f|大変|たいへん}だ → {f|大変|たいへん}なくらい |
| Modifying a noun | ～くらいの + Noun | {f|同|おな}じ → {f|同|おな}じくらいの{f|大|おお}きさ |
| Asking a degree | どのくらい／どれくらい | どのくらいかかりますか |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `くらい` measures **how much**, so it goes with spans, amounts
and degrees. It does not name a point on the calendar or the clock; that is `ごろ`.
<code>{f|三時間|さんじかん}ぐらい</code> but <code>{f|三時|さんじ}ごろ</code>.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>～くらいなら</code> is a related pattern worth recognising: it
rejects one option in favour of another. <code>{f|待|ま}つくらいなら、{f|歩|ある}いて{f|帰|かえ}る</code>
 - "if it comes to waiting, I'd rather walk home." The first clause names the level
the speaker refuses to sink to.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`くらい` and `ぐらい` mean exactly the same thing and both are correct everywhere.
The tendency - and it is only a tendency - is that `ぐらい` is a little more
frequent in speech and after the demonstratives (<code>これぐらい</code>,
<code>どれぐらい</code>), while `くらい` turns up slightly more often in careful
writing. No listener will notice which you chose, so pick one and be consistent
within a sentence.

The **approximate** use is completely neutral and extremely common; Japanese
conversation prefers a hedged number to a bare one, and a bare
<code>{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}かかります</code> can sound oddly exact where
<code>{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}ぐらい</code> is expected.

The **belittling** use is the one to handle with care. Applied to your own effort it
is modest and generous: <code>それくらい、{f|喜|よろこ}んでやります</code> - "that's
nothing, happy to." Applied to someone **else's** effort it says their contribution
is trivial, and in a request it says the bar you are setting is embarrassingly low:
<code>{f|挨拶|あいさつ}くらいしなさい</code> is something a parent says to a child.
Aimed upward at a manager or a customer it is rude, so keep it for peers, family and
yourself.

There is also a superlative frame worth recognising:
<code>～くらい～はない</code>, "nothing is as ～ as this."
<code>{f|夏|なつ}の{f|試験|しけん}くらいつらいものはない</code> - "there's nothing as
grim as summer exams." It behaves the same way as the more formal
<code>～ほど～はない</code>.

## Contrast

### くらい vs ごろ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ごろ` marks a **point in time**; `くらい` marks a **span or an
amount**. If the word answers "when?", use `ごろ`. If it answers "how long?" or
"how many?", use `くらい`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|七時|しちじ}<b>ごろ</b>{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}ます。
I leave the house around seven.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|七時間|しちじかん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|寝|ね}ました。
I slept for about seven hours.
</pre>

The counter tells you which is which:
<code>{f|三時|さんじ}</code> is a clock time and takes `ごろ`;
<code>{f|三時間|さんじかん}</code> is a duration and takes `ぐらい`. Dates and days
work like clock times - <code>{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|水曜日|すいようび}ごろ</code>.
Money, distance, weight and headcount are all amounts, so they can only take
`くらい`: ❌<code>{f|千円|せんえん}ごろ</code> is not Japanese.

<div class="note">

**Note** - you will hear <code>{f|三時|さんじ}ぐらいに</code> in casual speech, and
it is not an error, but `ごろ` is the standard choice for a clock time and the safe
one in writing.

</div>

### くらい vs ほど

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for approximation they overlap, with `ほど` sounding more
formal. For degree, `ほど` points **up** the scale and `くらい` can point **down**
it. Only `くらい` can belittle.

</div>

<pre>
{f|一時間|いちじかん}<b>ほど</b>お{f|待|ま}ちいただけますか。
Could I ask you to wait for about an hour?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一時間|いちじかん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|待|ま}ってよ。
Come on, wait an hour, would you?
</pre>

Those two sentences carry the same number and completely different manners: `ほど`
belongs to the polite register and shows up constantly in service Japanese, while
the second sentence is a friend being pushy.

The split is sharper with degrees. <code>{f|死|し}ぬほど{f|疲|つか}れた</code> and
<code>{f|死|し}ぬくらい{f|疲|つか}れた</code> are both "dead tired", but move to the
bottom of the scale and `ほど` stops working:
<code>お{f|茶|ちゃ}くらい{f|出|だ}してください</code> ("you could at least offer tea")
cannot become ❌<code>お{f|茶|ちゃ}ほど{f|出|だ}してください</code>. Remember also that
`ほど` owns two frames `くらい` does not share:
<code>～ば～ほど</code> and comparative <code>～ほど～ない</code>.

### Belittling くらい vs だけ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だけ` states a limit neutrally. `くらい` states the same limit
and adds a judgement: that little is not worth making a fuss about.

</div>

<pre>
{f|漢字|かんじ}を{f|五|いつ}つ<b>だけ</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えました。
I learned just five kanji.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|漢字|かんじ}を{f|五|いつ}つ<b>ぐらい</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えてください。
Learn five kanji at least, would you.
</pre>

`だけ` reports; `くらい` evaluates. That is why the second sentence works as a
complaint and the first does not, and why swapping them in a request changes what
you are doing socially. <code>{f|一人|ひとり}だけ{f|来|こ}なかった</code> counts the
absentees; <code>{f|一人|ひとり}ぐらい{f|来|こ}なくてもいい</code> waves them away as
unimportant.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|三時間|さんじかん}<b>ごろ</b>{f|勉強|べんきょう}しました。 → ✅ {f|三時間|さんじかん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|勉強|べんきょう}しました。
  `ごろ` needs a point in time. A duration counter such as
  <code>{f|三時間|さんじかん}</code> can only take `くらい`／`ぐらい`.

- ❌ {f|大変|たいへん}くらい{f|忙|いそが}しかった。 → ✅ {f|大変|たいへん}<b>な</b>くらい{f|忙|いそが}しかった。
  A な-adjective keeps its `な` before `くらい`, exactly as it does before `だけ`
  and `ので`.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|確認|かくにん}<b>ぐらい</b>してください。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、ご{f|確認|かくにん}をお{f|願|ねが}いできますか。
  Not a grammar error but a social one. Belittling `くらい` aimed at a superior is
  openly rude; save it for yourself and for people you are close to.

- ❌ {f|千円|せんえん}<b>ごろ</b>の{f|店|みせ} → ✅ {f|千円|せんえん}<b>ぐらい</b>の{f|店|みせ}
  Money is an amount, never a point in time.

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|難|むずか}しい<b>ぐらい</b>です。（meaning "it's really hard"） → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|解|と}けない<b>ぐらい</b>{f|難|むずか}しいです。
  `くらい` needs something to measure the degree **against**. On its own after an
  adjective it produces the opposite, dismissive reading: "it's merely hard."

## Practice

**1.** 「{f|会議|かいぎ}はどの____かかりますか。」「そうですね、{f|一時間半|いちじかんはん}____だと{f|思|おも}います。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

「{f|会議|かいぎ}はどの<b>くらい</b>かかりますか。」「{f|一時間半|いちじかんはん}<b>ぐらい</b>だと{f|思|おも}います。」

Both slots are asking about and answering a **duration**, so both take
`くらい`／`ぐらい`. Note how naturally the hedge fits the answer: a bare
<code>{f|一時間半|いちじかんはん}です</code> would claim a precision meetings rarely
have.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|明日|あした}は{f|八時|はちじ}（ごろ・ぐらい）{f|伺|うかが}います。{f|移動|いどう}に{f|一時間|いちじかん}（ごろ・ぐらい）かかりますので。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|明日|あした}は{f|八時|はちじ}<b>ごろ</b>{f|伺|うかが}います。{f|移動|いどう}に{f|一時間|いちじかん}<b>ぐらい</b>かかりますので。

<code>{f|八時|はちじ}</code> is a clock time - a point - and takes `ごろ`.
<code>{f|一時間|いちじかん}</code> is a length of time and takes `ぐらい`. The polite
register here also makes `ごろ` the safer of the two for the first slot.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I was so surprised I couldn't say anything."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|何|なに}も{f|言|い}えない<b>くらい</b>びっくりしました。

The `くらい` clause gives the yardstick for the degree of surprise: the level at
which speech fails. <code>{f|何|なに}も{f|言|い}えないほど</code> is equally correct and
sounds a little more formal or literary.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|金曜日|きんようび}ぐらいまでに、{f|三千円|さんぜんえん}ごろの{f|品物|しなもの}を{f|用意|ようい}してください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|金曜日|きんようび}<b>ごろ</b>までに、{f|三千円|さんぜんえん}<b>ぐらい</b>の{f|品物|しなもの}を{f|用意|ようい}してください。

The two words have been swapped. A day of the week is a point in time and takes
`ごろ`; a price is an amount and takes `ぐらい`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|自分|じぶん}の{f|部屋|へや}<b>だけ</b>{f|掃除|そうじ}しました。
(b) {f|自分|じぶん}の{f|部屋|へや}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|掃除|そうじ}しなさい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply reports the boundary of what was cleaned: one room, no others. It is
neutral, and it could be an answer to "what did you get done today?"

(b) uses `ぐらい` to belittle the task - cleaning your own room is the bare minimum
anyone could be expected to manage - and pairs it with a command. It is a telling-off,
and it only works aimed at someone below or close to you.

</details>
