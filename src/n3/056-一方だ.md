# ～一方だ

**Meaning** · keeps going in one direction; more and more ～, with no sign of turning back

<code>～{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> reports a **trend**. Something is moving along a scale - 
rising, falling, worsening, spreading - and the speaker's point is that it has been
moving that way steadily and shows no sign of stopping. It is the sentence-final
shape of the newspaper headline "costs continue to climb".

## Formation

Only verbs that contain a direction of change can take it. What goes in front is
always the dictionary form; tense and politeness are carried by the
<code>だ</code> at the end.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb of change | V(dictionary) + {f|一方|いっぽう}だ | {f|増|ふ}える → {f|増|ふ}える{f|一方|いっぽう}だ |
| Polite | ～{f|一方|いっぽう}です | {f|減|へ}る → {f|減|へ}る{f|一方|いっぽう}です |
| Past | ～{f|一方|いっぽう}だった | {f|下|さ}がる → {f|下|さ}がる{f|一方|いっぽう}だった |
| い-adjective (via なる) | ～くなる + {f|一方|いっぽう}だ | {f|忙|いそが}しい → {f|忙|いそが}しくなる{f|一方|いっぽう}だ |
| な-adjective / Noun (via なる) | ～になる + {f|一方|いっぽう}だ | {f|不便|ふべん} → {f|不便|ふべん}になる{f|一方|いっぽう}だ |
| Continuing the sentence | ～{f|一方|いっぽう}で、… | {f|増|ふ}える{f|一方|いっぽう}で、{f|手|て}が{f|足|た}りない |
| Modifying a noun | ～{f|一方|いっぽう}の + Noun | {f|増|ふ}える{f|一方|いっぽう}の{f|仕事|しごと} |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if the verb cannot be pictured as an arrow on a graph, it cannot
take <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code>. Adjectives and nouns get in only by first becoming a
change verb with <code>なる</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|物価|ぶっか}は{f|上|あ}がる<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</b>。
Prices just keep going up.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|村|むら}の{f|人口|じんこう}は{f|減|へ}る<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}です</b>。
The population of this village keeps falling.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|待|ま}っているだけでは、{f|状況|じょうきょう}は{f|悪|わる}くなる<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</b>。
If we just wait, the situation will only get worse.
</pre>

<pre>
スマートフォンのおかげで、{f|生活|せいかつ}は{f|便利|べんり}になる<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</b>。
Thanks to smartphones, life keeps getting more convenient.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}が{f|近|ちか}づき、{f|雨|あめ}は{f|強|つよ}くなる<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だった</b>。
As the typhoon drew closer, the rain just kept getting heavier.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}{f|暮|ぐ}らしを{f|始|はじ}めてから、コンビニに{f|行|い}く{f|回数|かいすう}が{f|増|ふ}える<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</b>。
Since I started living alone, the number of trips I make to the convenience store has done nothing but go up.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|医療|いりょう}{f|費|ひ}は{f|増|ふ}える<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}で</b>、{f|若|わか}い{f|世代|せだい}の{f|負担|ふたん}が{f|重|おも}くなっている。
Medical costs keep on rising, and the burden on the younger generation is growing heavier.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}を{f|何度|なんど}{f|開|ひら}いても{f|結論|けつろん}が{f|出|で}なくて、メンバーの{f|不満|ふまん}は{f|大|おお}きくなる<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}でした</b>。
However many meetings we held, no conclusion came out of them, and the members' frustration only grew.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a written-leaning pattern. It belongs to news copy, reports, essays and the
kind of speech that summarises a situation; in relaxed conversation the same idea
comes out as <code>どんどん{f|増|ふ}えてる</code> or
<code>だんだん{f|悪|わる}くなってる</code>. Saying
<code>{f|宿題|しゅくだい}が{f|増|ふ}える{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> to a friend is not
wrong, but it has a faintly editorial ring - which can be the joke.

The tone is **observational**. The speaker is standing back and reporting a curve,
not reacting to it, and that is why the pattern sits so easily next to numbers and
statistics. It is often used for unwelcome trends, simply because unwelcome trends
are what people write about, but there is nothing negative in the grammar itself:
<code>{f|便利|べんり}になる{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> and
<code>{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}は{f|進|すす}む{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> are perfectly
ordinary.

One structural point. The subject of <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> is normally a situation
or a quantity rather than a person choosing to do something. A trend is not a
decision, so <code>{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|走|はし}る{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> does not
work as "I keep running every day" - what can rise is
<code>{f|走|はし}る{f|距離|きょり}</code>, not the act itself.

## Contrast

### ～{f|一方|いっぽう}だ vs ～ばかりだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both mean "only goes one way". <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> is
neutral and can describe a welcome trend; `ばかりだ` is almost always about
something getting worse, and it sounds more emotional.

</div>

<pre>
{f|輸出|ゆしゅつ}は{f|伸|の}びる<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</b>。
Exports keep growing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}を{f|待|ま}つ{f|間|あいだ}、{f|不安|ふあん}は{f|大|おお}きくなる<b>ばかりだった</b>。
While I waited for a reply, my anxiety did nothing but grow.
</pre>

Swapping them is not ungrammatical but does change the colour.
<code>{f|輸出|ゆしゅつ}は{f|伸|の}びるばかりだ</code> makes growth sound like a
runaway problem, and <code>{f|不安|ふあん}は{f|大|おお}きくなる{f|一方|いっぽう}
だった</code> makes your own anxiety sound like a line on a chart. Choose by whether
you are reporting or complaining.

### ～{f|一方|いっぽう}だ vs ～ていく・～てくる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～ていく</code> and <code>～てくる</code> simply track a
change over time. <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> adds the claim that it has gone **only** that
way and is still going.

</div>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもの{f|数|かず}が{f|少|すく}なくなっ<b>ていく</b>。
The number of children is decreasing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもの{f|数|かず}は{f|少|すく}なくなる<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</b>。
The number of children just keeps decreasing.
</pre>

The first is a neutral description that leaves room for the line to level off; the
second insists it has not and will not. `～ていく` is also the everyday spoken form
and works with any verb at all, while <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> demands a verb of change
and a register to match.

### ～{f|一方|いっぽう}だ vs ～{f|一方|いっぽう}（で） - "on the other hand"

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - at the **end** of a sentence, <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> is a trend.
Between two clauses that describe **different** things, <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}（で）</code> means
"meanwhile, on the other hand".

</div>

<pre>
{f|田舎|いなか}の{f|土地|とち}は{f|安|やす}くなる<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</b>。
Land in the countryside just keeps getting cheaper.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|都心|としん}の{f|家賃|やちん}は{f|上|あ}がっている。<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}</b>、{f|地方|ちほう}では{f|空|あ}き{f|家|や}が{f|増|ふ}えている。
Rents in the city centre are rising. Meanwhile, empty houses are increasing in rural areas.
</pre>

The two are told apart by position and by what surrounds them. A sentence-final
<code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> after a dictionary-form verb of change is the trend
reading; a clause-initial <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}</code>, or
<code>～{f|一方|いっぽう}で</code> joining two clauses about *different* subjects, is
the contrast reading. The tricky middle case - 
<code>{f|増|ふ}える{f|一方|いっぽう}で、{f|人手|ひとで}が{f|足|た}りない</code> - is
the trend pattern in its て-form, and you can tell because both halves are about the
same rising thing.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|人口|じんこう}が<b>{f|少|すく}ない</b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ。 → ✅ {f|人口|じんこう}が<b>{f|減|へ}る</b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ。
  An adjective describes a state, and a state is not a trend. You need a verb with
  movement in it, or an adjective converted with <code>なる</code>.

- ❌ {f|生活|せいかつ}が<b>{f|苦|くる}しい</b>{f|一方|いっぽう}です。 → ✅ {f|生活|せいかつ}が<b>{f|苦|くる}しくなる</b>{f|一方|いっぽう}です。
  Same problem in polite clothing. <code>～くなる</code> is the bridge from an
  い-adjective to a change verb.

- ❌ {f|去年|きょねん}から{f|売上|うりあげ}が<b>{f|増|ふ}えた</b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ。 → ✅ {f|去年|きょねん}から{f|売上|うりあげ}が<b>{f|増|ふ}える</b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ。
  The verb stays in the dictionary form. If you need the past, put it on the end:
  <code>{f|増|ふ}える{f|一方|いっぽう}だった</code>.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は<b>{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|走|はし}る</b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ。 → ✅ {f|走|はし}る<b>{f|距離|きょり}が{f|伸|の}びる</b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ。
  A habit you have chosen is not a trend that is happening to you. The subject has to
  be something that can rise or fall on its own.

## Practice

**1.** {f|地球|ちきゅう}の{f|気温|きおん}は{f|上|あ}がる____で、{f|各国|かっこく}が{f|対策|たいさく}を{f|急|いそ}いでいる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|地球|ちきゅう}の{f|気温|きおん}は{f|上|あ}がる<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}</b>で…

The blank needs the trend pattern in its て-form, which links the rising temperature
to the response it is provoking. Both halves are about the same rise, which is what
distinguishes it from the "on the other hand" <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}で</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|部屋|へや}が（{f|汚|きたな}い・{f|汚|きたな}くなる）{f|一方|いっぽう}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|部屋|へや}が<b>{f|汚|きたな}くなる</b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ。

<code>{f|汚|きたな}い</code> is a state, and this pattern needs a direction of
travel. Turning the adjective into <code>{f|汚|きたな}くなる</code> supplies the
movement that <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> then declares to be one-way.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The number of foreign visitors keeps increasing."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|外国|がいこく}からの{f|観光客|かんこうきゃく}は{f|増|ふ}える<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</b>。

<code>{f|増|ふ}える</code> is already a change verb, so it goes straight into the
dictionary form in front of <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code>. The English "keeps"
needs no separate word - the pattern carries it.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|町|まち}の{f|店|みせ}は{f|閉|し}まった{f|一方|いっぽう}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|町|まち}の{f|店|みせ}は{f|減|へ}る<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</b>。

Two things were wrong. The verb must be non-past, and
<code>{f|閉|し}まる</code> is a one-off event rather than a movement along a scale.
<code>{f|減|へ}る</code> supplies a countable quantity that can keep falling.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|野菜|やさい}の{f|値段|ねだん}が{f|上|あ}がっ<b>ています</b>。
(b) {f|野菜|やさい}の{f|値段|ねだん}は{f|上|あ}がる<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}です</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports the current state of prices: they are up, and they may go on rising or
may not. It is the kind of thing you would say in a shop.

(b) makes a claim about the shape of the whole trend - the price has moved in one
direction only, over some stretch of time, and there is no turn in sight. It sounds
like commentary rather than observation, and it invites a "something must be done"
to follow.

</details>
