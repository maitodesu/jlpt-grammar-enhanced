# ～さえ○○ば / ～さえすれば / ～さえいれば

**Meaning** · if only A, then B - A named as the single condition that is enough on its own

`さえ` picks out **one** thing and the `ば`-clause says that this one thing is all it
takes. Everything else is waved away: it may be missing, it may be against you, it
does not matter. The main clause then reports that the result is assured - or, in the
past, that it *would have been*, which is why this pattern is also how Japanese sighs
over a small thing that was not done.

## Formation

Two halves have to arrive together: the particle `さえ` on the crucial element, and a
`ば`-form on the verb or adjective that governs it. Which helper you need depends on
what the crucial element is.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (subject or object) | Noun + さえ + ば-form（が・を drop） | お{f|金|かね}がある → お{f|金|かね}さえあれば |
| Noun (other particle) | Particle + さえ + ば-form | {f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}く → {f|駅|えき}にさえ{f|着|つ}けば |
| Verb (action) | ます-stem + さえすれば | {f|読|よ}む → {f|読|よ}みさえすれば |
| Verb (ongoing state) | て-form + さえいれば | {f|待|ま}つ → {f|待|ま}ってさえいれば |
| Verb (negative) | ます-stem + さえしなければ | {f|言|い}う → {f|言|い}いさえしなければ |
| Noun + する verb | Noun + さえすれば | {f|練習|れんしゅう}する → {f|練習|れんしゅう}さえすれば |
| い-adjective | く + さえあれば | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}くさえあれば |
| な-adjective | で + さえあれば | {f|静|しず}か → {f|静|しず}かでさえあれば |
| Noun (predicate) | で + さえあれば | {f|本物|ほんもの} → {f|本物|ほんもの}でさえあれば |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `さえ` **replaces** `が` and `を`, and **follows** every other
particle. ❌<code>お{f|金|かね}がさえ</code>, ❌<code>これをさえ</code>; but
<code>{f|駅|えき}にさえ</code>, <code>ここでさえ</code>, <code>{f|友|とも}だちからさえ</code>
are all fine.

</div>

<pre>

</pre>

Learn just this and you'll be fine on the test.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|天気|てんき}<b>さえよければ</b>、{f|明日|あした}の{f|朝|あさ}{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}できます。
As long as the weather holds, we can set off tomorrow morning.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|静|しず}か<b>でさえあれば</b>、{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}くてもかまいません。
So long as it's quiet, I don't mind the room being small.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}を{f|読|よ}み<b>さえすれば</b>、だれでも{f|組|く}み{f|立|た}てられますよ。

</pre>

<pre>

If you'd just kept taking the medicine, the fever would have come down sooner.
</pre>

If I had just said one word of apology back then, none of this would have blown up.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral and extremely common in speech, but it always carries
an attitude, and the attitude changes with the tense of the main clause.

With a **non-past** main clause it is reassuring or businesslike: here is the one
hurdle, clear it and the rest takes care of itself. This is the tone of instructions,
of a manager delegating, of somebody talking a nervous friend down - 
{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}きさえすれば、できるよ。

With a **past** main clause, and especially with `のに` or `んだけど` hanging off the
end, it turns wistful or accusing. The clause names a trivially small thing that was
not done, and the main clause names the disaster that followed. Said about yourself it
is regret; said about the person in front of you it is a reproach, and a fairly sharp
one - {f|連絡|れんらく}さえしてくれれば is not a neutral remark. Soften it, or say it
about the situation rather than the person.

Two spoken habits worth knowing. First, the `ます`-stem construction
(<code>{f|読|よ}みさえすれば</code>) sounds slightly emphatic and bookish; in casual
speech people more often hang `さえ` on a noun and leave the verb alone
(<code>それさえ{f|読|よ}めば</code>). Second, `すら` is a near-synonym of `さえ` in the
"even" sense but is confined to writing and almost never appears in this
minimum-condition frame - do not swap them.

## Contrast

### ～さえ○○ば vs plain ～ば

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - plain `ば` says *this is a condition*. `さえ…ば` says *this is the
only condition*. Removing `さえ` costs you the whole point of the sentence.

</div>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}が<b>あれば</b>、{f|手伝|てつだ}います。
If I have time, I'll help.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}<b>さえあれば</b>、{f|手伝|てつだ}えるんですが。
It's purely a question of time - if I had it, I could help.
</pre>

The second sentence claims that willingness, skill and permission are all already in
place, so that time is the last thing standing in the way. The first claims nothing of
the sort. Notice too that `が` disappears once `さえ` arrives.

### ～さえ○○ば vs ～だけで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だけ` counts; `さえ` selects. `だけで` says the amount is small;
`さえ…ば` says this particular thing is the one that matters, however large it is.

</div>

<pre>
{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|書|か}く<b>だけで</b>{f|参加|さんか}できます。
You can take part just by writing your name.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|名前|なまえ}<b>さえ{f|書|か}けば</b>、あとはこちらでやっておきます。
Once you've put your name down, we'll take care of the rest.
</pre>

`だけで` is about how little effort is involved and is a natural thing to put in an
advertisement. `さえ…ば` is about clearing the one remaining obstacle, and it implies a
list of other things that are already settled. That is also why `さえ…ば` can take a
heavy condition - {f|一億円|いちおくえん}さえあれば - where `だけで` would sound absurd.

### ～さえ○○ば vs さえ meaning "even"

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same particle, opposite ends of the scale. Without a `ば` after it,
`さえ` picks an **extreme** case ("even X"). With a `ば` after it, it picks the
**minimum** sufficient case ("if only X").

</div>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}ども<b>さえ</b>{f|知|し}っている{f|言葉|ことば}です。
It's a word even children know.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}ども<b>さえ{f|静|しず}かにしていれば</b>、いい{f|店|みせ}なんですけどね。
It's a lovely restaurant, as long as the children keep quiet.
</pre>

The first ranks children at the bottom of a scale of knowledge and says the word
reaches even there. The second says the children are the sole problem. The `ば` is what
flips the reading, so leaving it off does not weaken the sentence - it changes it into
a different one.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ お{f|金|かね}<b>が</b>さえあれば → ✅ お{f|金|かね}さえあれば
  `さえ` takes the place of `が` and `を`. It stacks only on `に`, `で`, `から`, `と` and
  the like.

- ❌ {f|来|く}るさえすれば → ✅ {f|来|き}さえすれば
  <code>さえする</code> attaches to the `ます`-stem, never the dictionary form. For a
  `する` verb built on a noun, put `さえ` after the noun instead:
  {f|連絡|れんらく}さえすれば.

- ❌ {f|安|やす}いさえあれば{f|買|か}います。 → ✅ {f|安|やす}くさえあれば{f|買|か}います。
  An い-adjective goes into its `く` form before <code>さえある</code>, exactly as it
  does before <code>ない</code>.

- ❌ {f|時間|じかん}さえある、{f|終|お}わります。 → ✅ {f|時間|じかん}さえあれば、{f|終|お}わります。
  The `ば` is not decoration. Without it the sentence says "even time exists", which is
  not what anybody means.

- ❌ この{f|書類|しょるい}さえ{f|出|だ}せば、{f|受付|うけつけ}に{f|行|い}ってください。 → ✅ この{f|書類|しょるい}を{f|出|だ}したら、{f|受付|うけつけ}に{f|行|い}ってください。
  A sufficient-condition clause is a claim about what will follow, not a step in a set
  of instructions. Like plain `ば` with an action verb, it does not take a request or a
  command after it.

## Practice

**1.** パスポート____、あとは{f|何|なに}もいりません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

パスポート<b>さえあれば</b>

The sentence lists one requirement and dismisses everything else, which is the core
job of this pattern. `が` disappears in front of `さえ`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|本|ほん}を（{f|読|よ}むだけで・{f|読|よ}みさえすれば）、{f|全体|ぜんたい}の{f|流|なが}れが{f|分|わ}かります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are grammatical, but they say different things.
<code>{f|読|よ}むだけで</code> advertises how little work it is; <code>{f|読|よ}みさえすれば</code>
implies you have been putting it off and the reading is the one thing left to do. Pick
the second if the sentence is aimed at somebody who has not opened the book yet.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If only he'd told me earlier, I could have changed the booking."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

もっと{f|早|はや}く{f|言|い}ってくれ<b>さえすれば</b>、{f|予約|よやく}を{f|変|か}えられたのに。

A past counterfactual with `のに` is the standard shape for this feeling. Note that
<code>くれる</code> is what makes the complaint land on him rather than on the
situation.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部屋|へや}が{f|明|あか}るいさえあれば、{f|古|ふる}くてもいいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部屋|へや}が{f|明|あか}る<b>くさえあれば</b>、{f|古|ふる}くてもいいです。

An い-adjective must become `く` before <code>さえある</code>. A tidier version moves
the particle too: {f|部屋|へや}さえ{f|明|あか}るければ、{f|古|ふる}くてもいいです。

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}<b>さえ</b>{f|来|こ}なかった。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}<b>さえ{f|来|こ}なければ</b>、{f|楽|たの}しい{f|会|かい}だったのに。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the "even" `さえ`: of all the people who might have failed to show up, even he
did - he was the last person you expected to miss it.

(b) is the sufficient-condition `さえ…ば`: everything else about the party was fine, and
his presence was the single thing that spoiled it. The `ば` and the counterfactual
`のに` together turn the same particle into a complaint about one man.

</details>
