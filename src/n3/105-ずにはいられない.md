# ～ずにはいられない

**Meaning** · can't help doing it; can't stop myself - an urge that overrides you

This is the only "no choice" pattern in which the pressure comes from **inside**.
Nobody has forbidden anything, no rule applies, and the circumstances have not
closed off your options - you simply cannot keep yourself from doing it. Laughing,
crying, saying the thing you should not say: `ずにはいられない` is the grammar of
being overruled by your own feelings.

## Formation

Built on the `ずに` you already know, plus the potential of `いる`: literally
"cannot be, without doing it".

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ～ない stem + ずにはいられない | {f|笑|わら}う → {f|笑|わら}わずにはいられない |
| Verb (る-verb) | ～ない stem + ずにはいられない | {f|考|かんが}える → {f|考|かんが}えずにはいられない |
| Verb (する) | **せずにはいられない** | {f|心配|しんぱい}する → {f|心配|しんぱい}せずにはいられない |
| Colloquial variant | ～ないではいられない | {f|笑|わら}わないではいられない |
| Shorter variant | ～ずにいられない | {f|笑|わら}わずにいられない |
| Polite | ～ずにはいられません | {f|笑|わら}わずにはいられません |
| Past | ～ずにはいられなかった | {f|笑|わら}わずにはいられなかった |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `する` gives <code>せずには</code>, never
❌<code>しずには</code>, exactly as it does in plain `ずに`. Since half the verbs
that fit this pattern are する-verbs - 
{f|心配|しんぱい}する, {f|同情|どうじょう}する, {f|応援|おうえん}する - this is the
form to drill.

</div>

## Nuance & register

The compulsion is **emotional**, and the verbs that appear here reflect that: to
laugh, to cry, to worry, to remember, to sympathise, to say something, to cheer.
Physical needs and practical necessities do not belong - 
<code>{f|傘|かさ}を{f|買|か}わずにはいられなかった</code> is odd if it merely
started raining, and natural if you saw an umbrella so beautiful you had no
defence against it.

Because it reports an inner state, the subject is normally **you**, or a narrator
speaking of themselves. About a third person you need a hedge, exactly as with
`たい`: <code>{f|彼|かれ}も{f|笑|わら}わずにはいられなかったようだ</code>. Written
flat about someone else, it claims access to a mind you cannot see into.

The register leans **written and expressive**: essays, reviews, letters, blog
posts, novels. Spoken, it is entirely usable but a touch dramatic - in casual
conversation people say
<code>つい{f|笑|わら}っちゃった</code> instead. That is worth knowing, because a
learner who uses `ずにはいられない` about a mildly amusing thing sounds as though
they are narrating their own memoir.

<div class="note">

**Note** - two neighbours, distinguished mainly by register.
<code>～ないではいられない</code> means the same and is a little more
conversational. <code>～ずにいられない</code>, without the `は`, is a shortened
form that turns up in speech and in lyrics. The `は` adds a touch of emphasis;
none of the three differs in meaning.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～ずにはいられない vs ～てしまう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `てしまう` reports that the action **happened despite you**.
`ずにはいられない` reports the **pressure to do it**, and usually says something
about you as a person.

</div>

<pre>
おかしくて{f|笑|わら}っ<b>てしまいました</b>。
It was so funny I ended up laughing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|顔|かお}を{f|見|み}るたびに{f|笑|わら}わ<b>ずにはいられません</b>。
I can't look at his face without laughing.
</pre>

`てしまう` is about one occasion and often carries regret. `ずにはいられない` states
a standing susceptibility - every time, without fail - and carries no regret at
all, only recognition. That is why the second sentence takes
<code>たびに</code> comfortably and the first does not.

### ～ずにはいられない vs ～ないわけにはいかない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both come out as "I have to" in English, and they are
opposites underneath. `ずにはいられない` is a **want** you cannot suppress;
`ないわけにはいかない` is a **duty** you cannot escape.

</div>

<pre>
{f|友達|ともだち}の{f|作品|さくひん}なので、{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}か<b>ないわけにはいかない</b>。
It's my friend's work, so I can hardly not go and see it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|評判|ひょうばん}がよすぎて、{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}か<b>ずにはいられない</b>。
Everyone's raving about it - I can't stay away.
</pre>

In the first you would rather stay home; friendship has removed the exit. In the
second nothing would keep you away. Choosing wrongly says something unfortunate
about your feelings: telling a friend
<code>{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}かないわけにはいかなくて</code> admits you went out of
obligation.

### ～ずにはいられない vs ～しかない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `しかない` is about **the world** running out of options.
`ずにはいられない` is about **you** running out of self-control.

</div>

<pre>
{f|終電|しゅうでん}がないので、{f|歩|ある}いて{f|帰|かえ}る<b>しかない</b>。
There's no last train, so I'll have to walk home.
</pre>

<pre>
あんまり{f|夜|よる}がきれいだったので、{f|遠回|とおまわ}りせ<b>ずにはいられなかった</b>。
The night was so lovely I couldn't help taking the long way round.
</pre>

Both sentences end with the speaker walking, and they explain the walking in
opposite ways. If the sentence would still be true of someone with a completely
different personality, you want `しかない`; if it depends on how *this* person
feels, you want `ずにはいられない`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|結果|けっか}が{f|気|き}になって、{f|確認|かくにん}**しずにはいられない**。 → ✅ {f|結果|けっか}が{f|気|き}になって、{f|確認|かくにん}<b>せずにはいられない</b>。
  `する` takes the irregular <code>せずに</code>. There is no しずに anywhere in
  Japanese.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}までにレポートを{f|出|だ}さ**ずにはいられない**。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}までにレポートを{f|出|だ}さ<b>なければならない</b>。
  A deadline is an obligation, not an urge. This pattern reports a feeling you
  cannot suppress, and nobody is emotionally overwhelmed by a report.

- ❌ {f|遅|おく}れそうだったので、タクシーに{f|乗|の}ら**ずにはいられなかった**。 → ✅ {f|遅|おく}れそうだったので、タクシーに{f|乗|の}る<b>しかなかった</b>。
  Circumstances closing off the alternatives is `しかない`. `ずにはいられない`
  would suggest you have an emotional weakness for taxis.

- ❌ {f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|感動|かんどう}**せずにはいられません**。 → ✅ {f|山田|やまだ}さんも{f|感動|かんどう}<b>せずにはいられなかったようです</b>。
  About someone else, the claim needs hedging - <code>ようだ</code>,
  <code>だろう</code>, <code>らしい</code> - because you cannot see inside their
  head.

## Practice

**1.** あんなに{f|一生懸命|いっしょうけんめい}{f|練習|れんしゅう}していたのを{f|知|し}っているので、{f|応援|おうえん}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|応援|おうえん}<b>せずにはいられません</b>。

`する` gives the irregular <code>せずに</code>. The sentence describes an emotional
pull rather than a duty, which is what makes this pattern right - nobody has asked
the speaker to cheer.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|台風|たいふう}で{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まったので、{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|泊|と}まる（しかなかった・ずにはいられなかった）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|泊|と}まる<b>しかなかった</b>。

A typhoon closing the railway is the world removing your options, which is
`しかない`. <code>{f|泊|と}まらずにはいられなかった</code> would claim you were
seized by a longing to sleep at the office.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Whenever I hear that song, I can't help remembering my school days."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

その{f|曲|きょく}を{f|聞|き}くと、{f|学生|がくせい}{f|時代|じだい}を{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}さ<b>ずにはいられません</b>。

Memory arriving uninvited is a textbook use of the pattern.
<code>{f|思|おも}い{f|出|だ}さないではいられません</code> says the same thing a
little more conversationally.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|頼|たの}まれたので、{f|残業|ざんぎょう}せずにはいられませんでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|頼|たの}まれたので、<b>{f|残業|ざんぎょう}しないわけにはいきませんでした</b>。

Being asked by your manager creates a duty, and duty you cannot escape is
<code>ないわけにはいかない</code>. As written, the sentence says you were unable to
restrain your appetite for overtime.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) その{f|手紙|てがみ}を{f|読|よ}んで、{f|泣|な}いてしまった。
(b) その{f|手紙|てがみ}を{f|読|よ}むと、{f|泣|な}かずにはいられない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports one occasion. You read the letter, and the tears arrived without your
consent; `てしまう` adds a faint sense of "I didn't mean to", which can shade into
embarrassment.

(b) reports a rule about yourself. Note the non-past
<code>{f|読|よ}むと</code>: this happens every time, and the speaker is stating a
known feature of their own reaction. (a) is an event, (b) is a disposition.

</details>
