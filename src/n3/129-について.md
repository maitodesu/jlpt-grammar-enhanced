# ～について

**Meaning** · about; concerning - names the subject matter that the talking, writing, thinking or investigating is *about*

`について` marks the **topic of an activity**. Something is being said, asked,
written, researched or thought - and `について` says what it is about. It does not
say who it is aimed at, and it does not say whose point of view we are taking.
Those are jobs for other members of this same `に` family, and keeping them apart
is most of the work at N3.

## Formation

`について` is a compound: the particle `に` plus the て-form of <code>つく</code> ("attach
to"). It behaves as a unit and attaches to a bare noun - never to a verb or an
adjective.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun, before a verb | Noun + について + verb | {f|事故|じこ} → {f|事故|じこ}について{f|話|はな}す |
| Noun, modifying another noun | Noun + についての + Noun | {f|環境|かんきょう} → {f|環境|かんきょう}についての{f|記事|きじ} |
| Noun, as topic | Noun + については | {f|費用|ひよう} → {f|費用|ひよう}については{f|後|あと}で |
| Noun, as addition | Noun + についても | {f|安全|あんぜん} → {f|安全|あんぜん}についても{f|考|かんが}える |
| Noun, formal / business | Noun + につきまして（は） | {f|日程|にってい} → {f|日程|にってい}につきましては |
| Question word | {f|何|なに} / どこ など + について | {f|何|なに} → {f|何|なに}について{f|書|か}きますか |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the `の` in <code>についての</code> is not optional. `について` is adverbial: it
can modify a verb on its own, but to hang it on a noun you have to convert it with
`の`, exactly as you would with <code>{f|東京|とうきょう}での{f|会議|かいぎ}</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|地震|じしん}<b>について</b>{f|調|しら}べています。
I'm looking into earthquakes.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|件|けん}<b>について</b>{f|質問|しつもん}があります。
I have a question about that matter.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|進路|しんろ}<b>について</b>{f|相談|そうだん}しました。
I talked to my teacher about which path to take after school.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日本|にほん}の{f|祭|まつ}り<b>についての</b>{f|番組|ばんぐみ}を{f|録画|ろくが}しておいた。
I recorded a programme about Japanese festivals.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|旅行|りょこう}の{f|費用|ひよう}<b>については</b>、あとでまとめてメールで{f|知|し}らせます。
As for the cost of the trip, I'll send you the whole thing by email later.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|規則|きそく}<b>について</b>、{f|社員|しゃいん}からいくつも{f|意見|いけん}が{f|出|で}ている。
Staff have raised a number of points about the new rules.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|画家|がか}<b>について</b>{f|何|なに}も{f|知|し}らないまま{f|展覧会|てんらんかい}に{f|行|い}っても、たぶん{f|楽|たの}しめないだろう。
If I go to the exhibition knowing nothing about the painter, I probably won't enjoy it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あす}の{f|説明会|せつめいかい}では、{f|来年度|らいねんど}の{f|予算|よさん}<b>につきまして</b>ご{f|説明|せつめい}いたします。
At tomorrow's briefing I will go over next year's budget.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`について` sits in the middle of the register scale, and that is why it is the one
to learn first. It is at home in a polite conversation, an email, a report, a
university seminar and a news broadcast alike. Nothing about it is stiff; nothing
about it is casual either.

Casual speech mostly does without it. Friends say <code>{f|昨日|きのう}のこと</code>,
<code>あの{f|人|ひと}のこと</code>, or just <code>～って</code>:

- あの{f|映画|えいが}のこと、どう{f|思|おも}った? - "What did you think about that film?"
- {f|試験|しけん}って、いつだっけ。 - "The exam - when was it again?"

Using について there is not wrong, but it lifts the sentence a register: it sounds
like you are opening an agenda item rather than chatting. Conversely, an email to
a client that says <code>{f|納期|のうき}のことですが</code> where it could have said
<code>{f|納期|のうき}につきましては</code> reads as slightly under-dressed.

The `につきまして` form is worth recognising even if you never produce it. It is
the same word with a humble-polite `ます` stem inserted, and it belongs to shop
announcements, customer service and business correspondence:
<code>{f|本日|ほんじつ}の{f|営業|えいぎょう}{f|時間|じかん}につきましては…</code>. In ordinary
polite speech it would sound over-formal, almost parodic.

One structural habit to build: `について` likes to be followed by a verb of
**speaking, writing or finding out** - <code>{f|話|はな}す</code>,
<code>{f|書|か}く</code>, <code>{f|説明|せつめい}する</code>,
<code>{f|調|しら}べる</code>, <code>{f|考|かんが}える</code>,
<code>{f|議論|ぎろん}する</code>, <code>{f|質問|しつもん}する</code>,
<code>{f|発表|はっぴょう}する</code>. If the predicate is an emotion or an evaluation
instead, `について` is usually the wrong tool.

## Contrast

### ～について vs ～に{f|関|かん}して

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register and different width.
`について` points at a **specific** topic and is fine in speech;
<code>に{f|関|かん}して</code> is more formal, mostly written, and takes in the
**surrounding area** of a topic as well.

</div>

You can swap them in most sentences without producing anything ungrammatical. What
changes is how the sentence sounds, and how tightly the topic is drawn.

<pre>
この{f|漢字|かんじ}の{f|読|よ}み{f|方|かた}<b>について</b>{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|聞|き}いた。
I asked the teacher about how this kanji is read.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|外国人|がいこくじん}の{f|受|う}け{f|入|い}れに<b>{f|関|かん}して</b>、{f|市|し}は{f|新|あたら}しい{f|方針|ほうしん}を{f|示|しめ}した。
The city has set out a new policy on accepting foreign residents.
</pre>

The first is one narrow question, asked out loud - `について` fits. The second
covers a whole policy area, in writing, in a public register - <code>に{f|関|かん}して</code>
fits. Reverse them and neither is wrong, but the first would sound like a
committee paper and the second like small talk.

### ～について vs ～に{f|対|たい}して

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `について` is the **topic**; <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> is the
**target**. "Talk about the customer" is `について`; "be polite to the customer" is
<code>に{f|対|たい}して</code>.

</div>

This is the pair that costs learners the most marks, because English "about" and
"toward" both come out as "regarding" in a dictionary.

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|新人|しんじん}<b>について</b>{f|何|なに}か{f|言|い}っていましたか。
Did the manager say anything about the new hire?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|新人|しんじん}に<b>{f|対|たい}して</b>{f|厳|きび}しすぎると{f|思|おも}う。
I think the manager is too hard on the new hire.
</pre>

In the first, the new hire is the subject matter of some remarks. In the second,
the new hire is the person the manager's behaviour is aimed at. A useful test: if
you can replace the phrase with "on the subject of", it is `について`. If you can
replace it with "at / to / against", it is <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code>.

### ～について vs ～のこと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>のこと</code> is the everyday equivalent and, unlike
`について`, it can be the **object of a feeling**. Verbs and adjectives of emotion
take <code>のこと</code> (or a plain particle), never `について`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}のこと<b>を</b>{f|心配|しんぱい}しています。
I'm worried about my little brother.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}<b>について</b>{f|心配|しんぱい}な{f|点|てん}を{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|伝|つた}えました。
I told the teacher the points about my brother that concern me.
</pre>

`について` needs the sentence to be *presenting information on a topic*. In the
first sentence there is no presenting going on - there is only the feeling, and the
brother is its object. The second sentence adds an act of communication, and
`について` becomes available again.

The same trap catches <code>{f|好|す}き</code>, <code>{f|嫌|きら}い</code>,
<code>{f|怒|おこ}る</code> and <code>{f|覚|おぼ}えている</code>. ❌<code>この{f|歌手|かしゅ}について{f|好|す}きです</code>
is simply not Japanese; you want <code>この{f|歌手|かしゅ}が{f|好|す}きです</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|事故|じこ}について{f|記事|きじ}を{f|読|よ}んだ。（intending "an article about the accident"） → ✅ {f|事故|じこ}<b>についての</b>{f|記事|きじ}を{f|読|よ}んだ。
  Without `の`, `について` modifies the verb <code>{f|読|よ}んだ</code>, which gives "I
  read an article, about the accident" - a different and odd sentence. To attach it
  to a noun you need <code>についての</code>.

- ❌ アニメについて{f|好|す}きです。 → ✅ アニメ<b>が</b>{f|好|す}きです。
  `について` introduces the subject matter of an act of communication or thought.
  A feeling takes its object with `が` or `を`, not with `について`.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}についてこの{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|大切|たいせつ}です。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>にとって</b>この{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|大切|たいせつ}です。
  "For me, this job is important" is a standpoint, not a topic. Standpoints take
  `にとって`.

- ❌ {f|客|きゃく}について{f|失礼|しつれい}な{f|態度|たいど}を{f|取|と}った。 → ✅ {f|客|きゃく}に<b>{f|対|たい}して</b>{f|失礼|しつれい}な{f|態度|たいど}を{f|取|と}った。
  An attitude is directed *at* somebody. That is <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code>; `について`
  would mean the rudeness was somehow on the topic of the customer.

## Practice

**1.** {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}では、{f|工場|こうじょう}の{f|移転|いてん}____{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|工場|こうじょう}の{f|移転|いてん}<b>について</b>{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}います。

The factory move is the subject matter of the discussion, and
<code>{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}う</code> is exactly the kind of communication verb `について`
was built for. <code>に{f|関|かん}して</code> would also be grammatical, just a notch
more formal than a normal meeting warrants.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|店長|てんちょう}はアルバイトの{f|学生|がくせい}（について・に{f|対|たい}して）とても{f|親切|しんせつ}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|店長|てんちょう}はアルバイトの{f|学生|がくせい}<b>に{f|対|たい}して</b>とても{f|親切|しんせつ}だ。

<code>{f|親切|しんせつ}だ</code> describes how the manager behaves **toward** somebody,
so the students are the target of an attitude, not a topic of conversation. Say
`について` and the sentence would try to mean that he is kind on the subject of
part-timers, which is nonsense.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'd like to hear your opinion about the new plan."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|新|あたら}しい{f|計画|けいかく}<b>について</b>ご{f|意見|いけん}を{f|伺|うかが}いたいのですが。

<code>ご{f|意見|いけん}</code> is information being given on a topic, so `について` is the
natural choice. In a written request you could raise the register with
<code>{f|新|あたら}しい{f|計画|けいかく}につきましては</code>, and in casual speech you would
drop the whole thing: <code>{f|新|あたら}しい{f|計画|けいかく}、どう{f|思|おも}う?</code>

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|問題|もんだい}についてのレポートを{f|来週|らいしゅう}までに{f|出|だ}してください。それから、{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りについて{f|質問|しつもん}は{f|受|う}け{f|付|つ}けません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ …{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}り<b>についての</b>{f|質問|しつもん}は{f|受|う}け{f|付|つ}けません。

The first `について` is correct because it already carries `の` before the noun
<code>レポート</code>. The second one sits directly in front of the noun
<code>{f|質問|しつもん}</code> and needs the same `の`. This is the single most common
slip with this pattern.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|田中|たなか}さん<b>について</b>{f|話|はな}しました。
(b) {f|田中|たなか}さん<b>と</b>{f|話|はな}しました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) makes {f|田中|たなか}さん the **subject matter**: somebody discussed him, and he
may well not have been in the room. Depending on the context it can sound slightly
ominous, in the same way English "we talked about you" does.

(b) makes him the **conversation partner** - you and he talked, about anything at
all. `と` marks the person you do something jointly with; `について` marks what the
doing is on the topic of. The two can even be stacked:
<code>{f|山田|やまだ}さんと{f|田中|たなか}さんについて{f|話|はな}しました</code> - "I talked with
Yamada about Tanaka."

</details>
