# なぜなら・なぜかというと

**Meaning** · because; the reason is - you make a claim, then turn round and justify it

`から` and `ので` bury the reason inside the sentence, ahead of the point.
`なぜなら` and `なぜかというと` do the opposite: the claim goes first, in a sentence
of its own, and the reason follows in a second sentence flagged as a reason. That
order is the whole value of the pattern - it is how you argue rather than merely
explain.

## Formation

These are sentence-opening phrases, not suffixes, so nothing conjugates in front of
them. What matters is what comes **after**: the explanation is normally closed off
with からだ／からです, and leaving that off makes the sentence sound truncated.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A stated claim (written) | ～。なぜなら、～からだ／からです。 | {f|反対|はんたい}です。なぜなら、{f|危|あぶ}ないからです。 |
| Emphatic written variant | ～。なぜならば、～からである。 | なぜならば、{f|記録|きろく}が{f|残|のこ}らないからである |
| Spoken and explanatory | ～。なぜかというと、～からです。 | なぜかというと、{f|順番|じゅんばん}が{f|逆|ぎゃく}だからです |
| Same frame, other shapes | なぜかといえば／どうしてかというと | どうしてかというと、{f|寒|さむ}いからです |
| Closing with a noun-reason | なぜなら、～ためだ／～のだ | なぜなら、{f|人手|ひとで}が{f|足|た}りないためだ |
| Casual equivalent | だって、～から／～んだもん | だって、{f|高|たか}いんだもん |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the claim comes **first**. `なぜなら` can never open the pair;
if you find yourself writing it at the head of a paragraph with nothing before it,
what you actually want is `から` or `ので`.

</div>

I'm going home early today. The reason is that I have an exam tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>

I wouldn't recommend this method, because it takes far too long.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りは{f|早|はや}めに{f|設定|せってい}しています。<b>なぜかというと</b>、{f|必|かなら}ず{f|何|なに}かが{f|起|お}きるからです。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}は{f|紙|かみ}の{f|辞書|じしょ}を{f|使|つか}い{f|続|つづ}けている。<b>なぜなら</b>、{f|前後|ぜんご}の{f|語|ご}が{f|目|め}に{f|入|はい}るからだ。

</pre>

<pre>

For their first month I give new staff only work where a mistake does no harm - because the ones who get told off in that first month are exactly the ones who stop asking questions later.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`なぜなら` is written Japanese, and specifically **argumentative** written Japanese:
opinion columns, essays, a slide deck, the paragraph in a report where you defend a
recommendation. It marks the sentence after it as *support for a claim*, which is
why it sounds slightly grand in ordinary chat - dropping it into a conversation
about lunch is a little like saying "for the following reason" out loud. `なぜならば`
is the same word with an extra formal weight, at home with `である` endings.

`なぜかというと` is the spoken member of the family. Literally "if you ask why", it
builds the imagined question into the phrase, and that makes it friendly and
explanatory rather than argumentative: teachers, guides and anyone walking a
listener through their thinking use it constantly. `どうしてかというと` is the same
thing a step more casual. All of them still expect からです at the end.

Down at the casual end sits `だって`, which is a different animal - it is defensive
rather than expository, it belongs at the head of a reply, and it pairs with the
sentence-final `もん`. Between friends, {f|行|い}かない。だって{f|眠|ねむ}いんだもん is
what actually gets said.

One usage note that catches learners out: when somebody has **asked** you どうして?,
you do not answer with `なぜなら`. The question has already flagged that a reason is
coming, so the signpost is redundant, and the natural answer is a bare
{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まったからです。 `なぜなら` is for reasons **you** decide to
supply, unprompted.

Finally, do not sprinkle it. An essay in which every paragraph turns on `なぜなら`
reads as mechanical to a Japanese reader, exactly as a run of "because of this" does
in English. Use it where the reason genuinely needs marking as a reason.

## Contrast

### なぜなら vs ～から・～ので

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same content, opposite order. `から` and `ので` put the reason
first and inside the sentence; `なぜなら` puts the claim first and gives the reason a
sentence of its own.

</div>

<pre>
{f|人手|ひとで}が{f|足|た}りない<b>ので</b>、{f|土曜日|どようび}の{f|営業|えいぎょう}をやめました。
We stopped opening on Saturdays because we're short-staffed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|土曜日|どようび}の{f|営業|えいぎょう}をやめました。<b>なぜなら</b>、{f|人手|ひとで}が{f|足|た}りないからです。
We've stopped opening on Saturdays. The reason is that we're short-staffed.
</pre>

Choose by asking what the sentence is *for*. If you are simply explaining a
situation, the `ので` version is shorter and more natural. If you are defending a
decision that the listener may not like, putting it first and then supporting it - 
claim, evidence - is what `なぜなら` was made for. Note that the reason clause still
ends in からです even though `なぜなら` already means "because"; the two are not
redundant in Japanese, they bracket the reason at both ends.

### なぜなら vs なぜかというと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical function, different register. `なぜなら` argues on
paper; `なぜかというと` explains out loud.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|案|あん}は{f|見直|みなお}すべきだ。<b>なぜなら</b>、{f|費用|ひよう}の{f|見積|みつ}もりが{f|甘|あま}すぎるからだ。
This proposal needs revisiting, because the cost estimates are far too optimistic.
</pre>

<pre>
ここだけ{f|先|さき}に{f|説明|せつめい}しますね。<b>なぜかというと</b>、あとの{f|話|はなし}が{f|全部|ぜんぶ}ここにつながるからです。
Let me explain this bit first - the reason being that everything after it hangs on it.
</pre>

The first is a written argument and could not be said aloud without sounding as
though you were reading it. The second builds in the listener's imagined "why?" and
is warm; in a written report it would look chatty. `どうしてかというと` shifts one
notch further towards conversation again.

### なぜなら vs というのは・だって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なぜなら` supports a claim. `というのは` fills in background the
listener is missing. `だって` defends the speaker against criticism.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今週|こんしゅう}は{f|顔|かお}を{f|出|だ}せません。<b>というのは</b>、{f|母|はは}が{f|入院|にゅういん}してしまったんです。
I can't come by this week - the thing is, my mother's been taken into hospital.
</pre>

<pre>
「どうして{f|先|さき}に{f|言|い}わなかったの?」「<b>だって</b>、{f|心配|しんぱい}させたくなかったんだもん。」
"Why didn't you say something earlier?" "Because I didn't want to worry you."
</pre>

`というのは` is doing something closer to "the thing is" - it supplies circumstances
rather than proving a point, and it does not require からです at the end. `だって`
answers an accusation and belongs to casual speech; used to a superior it sounds
like a child talking back. Between them and `なぜなら` you have most of the register
range covered.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>なぜなら</b>、{f|明日|あした}{f|試験|しけん}があります。{f|今日|きょう}は{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}ります。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}ります。<b>なぜなら</b>、{f|明日|あした}{f|試験|しけん}があるからです。
  The claim has to come first. `なぜなら` looks backwards at something already said.

- ❌ {f|行|い}きません。<b>なぜなら</b>、{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っていました。 → ✅ {f|行|い}きません。<b>なぜなら</b>、{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っていたからです。
  The reason clause needs its からだ／からです. Without it the sentence stops in
  mid-air.

- ❌ 「どうして{f|遅|おく}れたんですか。」「なぜなら、{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まったからです。」 → ✅ 「{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まったからです。」
  When the reason has been asked for, announcing that a reason is coming is
  redundant and sounds oddly formal.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}いませんでした。<b>だって</b>、{f|資料|しりょう}が{f|届|とど}かなかったんです。 → ✅ …{f|資料|しりょう}が{f|届|とど}かなかった<b>ものですから</b>。
  `だって` is a child's defence. Aimed upward at a manager it is close to insolent.

- ❌ この{f|色|いろ}が{f|好|す}き。<b>なぜならば</b>、{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}くからである。 （casual chat） → ✅ この{f|色|いろ}が{f|好|す}き。だって{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}くんだもん。
  `なぜならば` and `である` belong to formal writing. In a conversation about
  paint colours they read as a joke.

## Practice

**1.** {f|自転車|じてんしゃ}で{f|通勤|つうきん}することにしました。____、{f|電車|でんしゃ}より{f|早|はや}く{f|着|つ}くことが{f|分|わ}かったからです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>なぜなら</b>、{f|電車|でんしゃ}より{f|早|はや}く{f|着|つ}くことが{f|分|わ}かったからです。

The claim has already been made in the first sentence, and the second exists to
support it - the exact shape this connector marks. <code>なぜかというと</code> would fit
too, and would sound a shade more conversational.

</details>

**2.** Choose, in a conversation with a close friend:
{f|行|い}かないよ。（なぜならば・だって）、{f|会費|かいひ}が{f|高|たか}すぎるんだもん。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|行|い}かないよ。<b>だって</b>、{f|会費|かいひ}が{f|高|たか}すぎるんだもん。

The plain endings and the sentence-final `もん` set the register, and `だって` is the
member of this family that belongs there. `なぜならば` with `んだもん` after it is a
collision of the most formal and the most casual options available.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, as a line in a written report: "We recommend postponing the launch. The reason is that testing is not yet complete."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|発売|はつばい}は{f|延期|えんき}すべきだと{f|考|かんが}えます。<b>なぜなら</b>、{f|試験|しけん}がまだ{f|終|お}わっていないからです。

A recommendation followed by its justification, in writing, is `なぜなら` at its most
typical. Keep the からです: the connector opens the reason and からです closes it.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}って{f|行|い}った{f|方|ほう}がいいですよ。なぜなら、{f|午後|ごご}から{f|雨|あめ}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ …なぜなら、{f|午後|ごご}から{f|雨|あめ}<b>だからです</b>。

The reason clause has to close with からだ／からです, and after a noun that means
`だから`. Without it the second sentence reads as an unfinished thought - the
listener is still waiting for the end of it.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|今年|ことし}は{f|旅行|りょこう}を{f|見送|みおく}ります。<b>なぜなら</b>、{f|工事|こうじ}にお{f|金|かね}がかかるからです。
(b) {f|今年|ことし}は{f|旅行|りょこう}を{f|見送|みおく}ります。<b>というのは</b>、{f|家|いえ}の{f|工事|こうじ}をすることになったんです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) argues. The decision is stated, then defended with a reason marked as a reason,
and the からです closes the argument off. It is what you would write in a message
that expects to be questioned.

(b) confides. <code>というのは</code> supplies background the listener did not have - 
"the thing is, we're having work done on the house" - and it needs no からです.
It is softer and more conversational, and it invites sympathy rather than
agreement.

</details>
