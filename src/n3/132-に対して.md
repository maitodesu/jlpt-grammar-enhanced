# ～に対して

**Meaning** · toward; at; in response to - and, from a clause, "whereas"（にたいして）

<code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> points. It names the **person or thing an action, an
attitude or a reaction is aimed at**: who you are polite to, what you are objecting
to, what a reply is a reply to. Say "about" in your head and you will reach for it
wrongly - this is not the topic of the sentence, it is the target.

A second use falls out of the same image. If you set one thing opposite another,
you get contrast: <code>Aのに{f|対|たい}して、B</code> - "A, whereas B". And a third,
narrower use gives ratios: "per one participant, two bottles."

## Formation

<code>{f|対|たい}する</code> is a verb ("face, confront"), so it inflects: the て-form
modifies verbs and adjectives, the dictionary form modifies nouns.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (target) | Noun + に{f|対|たい}して | {f|子|こ}ども → {f|子|こ}どもに{f|対|たい}してやさしい |
| Noun, modifying a noun | Noun + に{f|対|たい}する + Noun | {f|客|きゃく} → {f|客|きゃく}に{f|対|たい}する{f|態度|たいど} |
| Verb / い-adjective (contrast) | Plain form + のに{f|対|たい}して | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}いのに{f|対|たい}して |
| な-adjective (contrast) | ～な + のに{f|対|たい}して | {f|静|しず}かだ → {f|静|しず}かなのに{f|対|たい}して |
| Noun (contrast) | Noun + なのに / であるのに + {f|対|たい}して | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}なのに{f|対|たい}して |
| Quantity (ratio) | Number + に{f|対|たい}して + Number | {f|一人|ひとり} → {f|一人|ひとり}に{f|対|たい}して{f|二枚|にまい} |
| Written / curt variant | Noun + に{f|対|たい}し | {f|会社|かいしゃ} → {f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|対|たい}し{f|求|もと}める |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the contrastive use needs `の` before it. A clause cannot attach
directly: ❌<code>{f|高|たか}いに{f|対|たい}して</code>. The `の` turns the clause into
something noun-like, which is what <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> requires.

</div>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

Whereas last year's sales didn't grow at all, this year has been strong right through from spring.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The target use leans **formal**, but not so far that it is out of place in ordinary
polite conversation. <code>{f|部下|ぶか}に{f|対|たい}して{f|厳|きび}しい</code> is
something a colleague might say out loud. What marks it as slightly weighty is that
plain `に` would usually do the job:
<code>{f|子|こ}どもにやさしい</code> and <code>{f|子|こ}どもに{f|対|たい}してやさしい</code>
mean the same thing, and the second is more deliberate - it puts the relationship
itself under a spotlight. In relaxed speech you would normally drop it.

Where <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> is not optional is when the noun is the target of
a **reaction**: a protest, a reply, a complaint, a demand, an evaluation.
<code>{f|説明|せつめい}に{f|対|たい}して{f|質問|しつもん}が{f|出|で}た</code> - 
the questions came *back at* the explanation. Bare `に` there is thinner and
sometimes ambiguous.

The contrastive use is squarely **written**. It belongs in comparisons, reports and
essays, and it has a cool, analytical tone: the writer is laying two facts side by
side, not complaining. That distinguishes it from `のに`, which contrasts *and*
carries dissatisfaction.

<code>に{f|対|たい}し</code>, without `て`, is the clipped written form. It is standard
in newspapers and official documents - 
<code>{f|組合|くみあい}は{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|対|たい}し{f|回答|かいとう}を{f|求|もと}めた</code> - 
and sounds stiff if spoken aloud outside a formal announcement.

<div class="note">

**Note** - the ratio use is common in explanations of quantity and in statistics:
<code>{f|人口|じんこう}{f|千人|せんにん}に{f|対|たい}して{f|医師|いし}が{f|三人|さんにん}</code>.
It is the one sense where the noun in front is a **number**, so it is easy to spot
and easy to keep separate from the other two.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～に{f|対|たい}して vs ～について

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> is the **target** of an action or
attitude; `について` is the **subject matter** of a statement. Aim vs topic.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|制度|せいど}<b>について</b>{f|社員|しゃいん}に{f|説明|せつめい}した。
I explained the new system to the staff.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|制度|せいど}<b>に{f|対|たい}して</b>{f|社員|しゃいん}から{f|不満|ふまん}が{f|出|で}た。
Complaints came from the staff against the new system.
</pre>

In the first the system is what the explanation is *about*. In the second it is
what the complaints are aimed *at*. Note that the two sentences have different
verbs, and that is the reliable clue: verbs of **saying, writing and investigating**
take `について`, while verbs of **reacting** - <code>{f|反対|はんたい}する</code>,
<code>{f|抗議|こうぎ}する</code>, <code>{f|答|こた}える</code>,
<code>{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}う</code> - take <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code>.

### ～に{f|対|たい}して vs ～にとって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> looks **outward from the subject
toward** the noun; `にとって` looks **outward from the noun** at the world. Target vs
standpoint.

</div>

<pre>
{f|親|おや}<b>に{f|対|たい}して</b>あんな{f|口|くち}をきくべきではない。
You shouldn't talk to your parents like that.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|親|おや}<b>にとって</b>、{f|子|こ}どもの{f|健康|けんこう}が{f|一番|いちばん}の{f|心配|しんぱい}だ。
For parents, their children's health is the biggest worry.
</pre>

In the first, the parents are on the receiving end of the speaking. In the second,
nothing is aimed at them at all - we have simply climbed inside their heads to judge
what matters. A quick test: `にとって` is nearly always followed by an **evaluation**
(<code>{f|大切|たいせつ}だ</code>, <code>{f|難|むずか}しい</code>,
<code>ありがたい</code>), whereas <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> is followed by an
**action or a stance**.

### ～のに{f|対|たい}して vs ～のに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both put two clauses against each other, but
<code>のに{f|対|たい}して</code> is a **neutral comparison** and `のに` is a
**complaint**. If you are annoyed, you want `のに`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|東京|とうきょう}の{f|冬|ふゆ}は{f|晴|は}れの{f|日|ひ}が{f|多|おお}い<b>のに{f|対|たい}して</b>、{f|日本海側|にほんかいがわ}は{f|雪|ゆき}の{f|日|ひ}が{f|続|つづ}く。
Winter in Tokyo has many clear days, whereas on the Japan Sea side the snow goes on and on.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|約束|やくそく}した<b>のに</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|来|こ}なかった。
He promised, and yet he didn't come.
</pre>

The first sentence has no feeling in it: two regions, two climates, stated side by
side. The second is a grievance. There is also a structural difference - 
<code>のに{f|対|たい}して</code> compares two things **of the same kind** along the
same axis (winters with winters, sales with sales), while `のに` simply says the
second clause is unexpected given the first.

For the same neutral comparison you will also meet
<code>{f|一方|いっぽう}（で）</code>, which does much the same work and joins sentences
rather than clauses.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}に{f|対|たい}して{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いましょう。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}<b>について</b>{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いましょう。
  <code>{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}う</code> takes a topic, not a target. Use
  <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> only when something is being aimed at the noun.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}に{f|対|たい}して、この{f|写真|しゃしん}は{f|宝物|たからもの}です。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>にとって</b>、この{f|写真|しゃしん}は{f|宝物|たからもの}です。
  "For me" meaning "from my point of view" is `にとって`.
  <code>{f|私|わたし}に{f|対|たい}して</code> would mean the photograph is doing
  something to you.

- ❌ {f|兄|あに}は{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}いに{f|対|たい}して、{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|小|ちい}さい。 → ✅ {f|兄|あに}は{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}い<b>のに{f|対|たい}して</b>、{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|小|ちい}さい。
  The contrastive use attaches to a **nominalised** clause. The `の` is compulsory,
  and with a な-adjective it becomes <code>な+のに{f|対|たい}して</code>.

- ❌ {f|上司|じょうし}に{f|対|たい}して{f|態度|たいど}が{f|悪|わる}い。（intending "his attitude toward his boss is bad"） → ✅ {f|上司|じょうし}に<b>{f|対|たい}する</b>{f|態度|たいど}が{f|悪|わる}い。
  Before a noun the verb takes its dictionary form. As written, the sentence
  attaches the target to <code>{f|悪|わる}い</code> and drifts toward "he is badly
  behaved in the direction of his boss."

## Practice

**1.** {f|失礼|しつれい}な{f|意見|いけん}____、{f|先生|せんせい}は{f|静|しず}かに{f|反論|はんろん}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|失礼|しつれい}な{f|意見|いけん}<b>に{f|対|たい}して</b>、{f|先生|せんせい}は{f|静|しず}かに{f|反論|はんろん}した。

<code>{f|反論|はんろん}する</code> is a reaction, and a reaction needs something to be
aimed at. `について` would make the rude opinion merely the topic, losing the sense
that the teacher was answering it back.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|留学生|りゅうがくせい}（に{f|対|たい}して・にとって）、{f|漢字|かんじ}を{f|覚|おぼ}えるのは{f|大変|たいへん}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|留学生|りゅうがくせい}<b>にとって</b>、{f|漢字|かんじ}を{f|覚|おぼ}えるのは{f|大変|たいへん}だ。

<code>{f|大変|たいへん}だ</code> is an evaluation, and the sentence is judging kanji
**from the students' point of view** - nobody is doing anything to them. That is
`にとって`. <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> would need an action or a stance directed at
the students.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Whereas the first floor is a shop, the second floor is used as an office."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一階|いっかい}が{f|店|みせ}である<b>のに{f|対|たい}して</b>、{f|二階|にかい}は{f|事務所|じむしょ}として{f|使|つか}われている。

A neutral side-by-side comparison of two floors, so
<code>のに{f|対|たい}して</code> rather than `のに`. After a noun the clause needs
<code>である</code> (or <code>な</code>) before `のに` - ❌<code>{f|店|みせ}のに{f|対|たい}して</code>
is not possible.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|事故|じこ}に{f|対|たい}して{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|行|おこな}ったが、{f|被害者|ひがいしゃ}について{f|十分|じゅうぶん}な{f|補償|ほしょう}をしていない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|事故|じこ}<b>について</b>{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|行|おこな}ったが、{f|被害者|ひがいしゃ}<b>に{f|対|たい}して</b>{f|十分|じゅうぶん}な{f|補償|ほしょう}をしていない。

The two are the wrong way round. An explanation is *about* the accident - topic, so
`について`. Compensation is paid *to* the victims - target, so
<code>に{f|対|たい}して</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|後輩|こうはい}<b>に</b>{f|厳|きび}しい。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|後輩|こうはい}<b>に{f|対|たい}して</b>{f|厳|きび}しい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both say he is hard on his juniors, and both are correct. (a) is the ordinary
spoken sentence: plain `に` is enough, and nobody would think twice about it.

(b) makes the relationship itself the point. The longer phrase draws attention to
the direction of the strictness - juniors specifically, as opposed to anyone else - 
and it raises the register, so it fits a written assessment, a complaint made
formally, or a sentence that goes on to contrast his behaviour toward someone else:
<code>{f|後輩|こうはい}に{f|対|たい}しては{f|厳|きび}しいが、{f|上司|じょうし}には{f|何|なに}も{f|言|い}わない</code>.

</details>
