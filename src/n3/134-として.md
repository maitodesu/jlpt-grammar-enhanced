# ～として

**Meaning** · as; in the capacity of; in the role of - the hat somebody or something is wearing while it does what it does

`として` assigns a **role**. A person acts *as* a representative, a building is used
*as* a gallery, a sum of money is handed over *as* a gift. The thing itself does not
change; what `として` supplies is the capacity in which it is being treated.

Two extensions follow naturally. `としては` sets a **standard of judgement** - 
"measured as an X" - and, with a person, "speaking for myself". And a handful of
fixed adverbs like <code>{f|原則|げんそく}として</code> and
<code>{f|参考|さんこう}として</code> are simply this pattern frozen solid.

## Formation

Attaches to a bare noun. No `な`, no `の` before it.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (role), modifying a verb | Noun + として | {f|医者|いしゃ} → {f|医者|いしゃ}として{f|働|はたら}く |
| Noun, modifying a noun | Noun + としての + Noun | {f|親|おや} → {f|親|おや}としての{f|責任|せきにん} |
| Noun, as standard or standpoint | Noun + としては | {f|新人|しんじん} → {f|新人|しんじん}としてはよくやっている |
| Noun, as addition | Noun + としても | {f|趣味|しゅみ} → {f|趣味|しゅみ}としても{f|楽|たの}しめる |
| Noun, ruling one out | Noun + としてではなく | {f|仕事|しごと} → {f|仕事|しごと}としてではなく |
| Fixed adverbs | {f|原則|げんそく}／{f|例外|れいがい}／{f|参考|さんこう} + として | {f|原則|げんそく} → {f|原則|げんそく}として{f|禁止|きんし}です |

<div class="note">

**Note** - beyond N3 you will meet <code>{f|一人|ひとり}として…ない</code>,
"not a single person", where `として` combines with a counter and a negative. It is
the same word doing an emphatic job, and it is worth recognising even though you do
not need to produce it yet.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|兄|あに}は{f|医者|いしゃ}<b>として</b>{f|働|はたら}いています。
My older brother works as a doctor.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|趣味|しゅみ}<b>として</b>{f|絵|え}を{f|描|か}いています。
I paint as a hobby.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|代表|だいひょう}<b>として</b>{f|式|しき}に{f|出席|しゅっせき}しました。
I attended the ceremony as the company's representative.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|建物|たてもの}は{f|今|いま}、{f|美術館|びじゅつかん}<b>として</b>{f|使|つか}われている。
This building is now used as an art museum.
</pre>

<pre>

I'll send last year's materials along as well, for reference.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|入|はい}ったばかりの{f|人|ひと}<b>としては</b>、よくやっているほうだと{f|思|おも}う。
For someone who's only just joined, I'd say he's doing well.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|友|とも}だち<b>としては</b>{f|最高|さいこう}だが、{f|仕事|しごと}のパートナー<b>としては</b>{f|少|すこ}し{f|心配|しんぱい}だ。
She's wonderful as a friend, but as a working partner I have my doubts.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|留学|りゅうがく}は{f|語学|ごがく}の{f|勉強|べんきょう}<b>としてだけでなく</b>、{f|人生|じんせい}の{f|経験|けいけん}<b>としても</b>{f|大|おお}きな{f|意味|いみ}がある。
Studying abroad matters not only as language study but as a life experience too.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`として` is **register-neutral**. It appears in casual conversation, in job
interviews, in newspapers and in contracts without changing shape or feeling.
Nothing about it is stiff.

What is register-marked is the company it keeps. The fixed adverbs - 
<code>{f|原則|げんそく}として</code> ("as a rule"),
<code>{f|例外|れいがい}として</code> ("by way of exception"),
<code>{f|結果|けっか}として</code> ("as a result") - belong to written and formal
speech. A friend says <code>だいたいダメだよ</code>; a notice says
<code>{f|原則|げんそく}としてお{f|断|ことわ}りしております</code>.

`としては` deserves its own line, because it does two different things and speakers
rely on both.

- **As a category standard**: <code>{f|中古|ちゅうこ}としては{f|高|たか}い</code> - 
  "expensive for a second-hand one." The judgement is made against what is normal
  for the category.
- **As a personal position**: <code>{f|私|わたし}としては{f|反対|はんたい}です</code> - 
  "my own position is that I'm against it." This is a politeness device. It marks
  the opinion as yours alone and leaves room for the group to decide otherwise,
  which is why it is everywhere in meetings.

The second of those is worth practising. Where English says "personally, I…",
Japanese very often says <code>{f|私|わたし}としては</code>, and a learner who reaches
for <code>{f|私|わたし}は</code> instead sounds more insistent than they meant to.

## Contrast

### ～として vs ～にとって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `として` puts the subject **into** the role; `にとって` looks
out **from** the noun's viewpoint. "As a teacher, I…" is `として`; "for a teacher,
this is…" is `にとって`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|教師|きょうし}<b>として</b>、{f|生徒|せいと}の{f|話|はなし}はきちんと{f|聞|き}きたい。
As a teacher, I want to listen properly to what my students say.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|教師|きょうし}<b>にとって</b>、{f|生徒|せいと}に{f|信頼|しんらい}されることほどうれしいことはない。
For a teacher, nothing is more gratifying than being trusted by the students.
</pre>

In the first sentence the speaker **is** a teacher and is acting in that capacity.
In the second the speaker is standing outside, describing how the world looks to
teachers - and need not be one. The predicate gives it away too: `として` is
followed by an action or an intention, `にとって` by an evaluation.

### ～として vs ～で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `で` can also mark a capacity, but only loosely and only in
casual speech. `として` states the role explicitly and works at any register.

</div>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}はアルバイト<b>で</b>{f|入|はい}っていました。
I was there on a part-time basis last year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}はアルバイト{f|職員|しょくいん}<b>として</b>{f|採用|さいよう}されました。
Last year I was taken on as a part-time member of staff.
</pre>

Both are natural; the second is what you would write on a form. The gap widens when
the role is formal or contested - ❌<code>{f|代表|だいひょう}で{f|意見|いけん}を{f|述|の}べた</code>
is at best vague about whether you were *the* representative, while
<code>{f|代表|だいひょう}として{f|意見|いけん}を{f|述|の}べた</code> states it plainly.
When the role is what matters, use `として`.

### ～としては vs ～にしては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `としては` measures something **within** its category;
`にしては` says the result is **surprising for** that category. Add "considering"
and a raised eyebrow, and you have `にしては`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}ども<b>としては</b>よく{f|考|かんが}えているほうだ。
As children go, he thinks things through well.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}ども<b>にしては</b>よく{f|考|かんが}えている。
He thinks things through well, for a child.
</pre>

The first ranks him among children - a neutral placement inside the category. The
second is mildly astonished: given that he is a child, this is more than expected.
Where they part company completely is the personal-position use.
<code>{f|私|わたし}としては{f|賛成|さんせい}です</code> means "my position is that I'm in
favour"; ❌<code>{f|私|わたし}にしては{f|賛成|さんせい}です</code> would have to mean
"surprisingly for me, I'm in favour", which is rarely what anyone intends.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|通訳|つうやく}になって{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|出|で}ました。（intending "attended as an interpreter"） → ✅ {f|通訳|つうやく}<b>として</b>{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|出|で}ました。
  <code>になる</code> means *become*: the wrong version says you turned into an
  interpreter and then went. `として` names the capacity you attended in.

- ❌ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|倉庫|そうこ}にして{f|使|つか}っています。 → ✅ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|倉庫|そうこ}<b>として</b>{f|使|つか}っています。
  <code>にする</code> is "turn it into"; <code>として{f|使|つか}う</code> is "use it as".
  Only the second describes an ongoing role.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}としての{f|働|はたら}いています。 → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}<b>として</b>{f|働|はたら}いています。
  <code>としての</code> exists only in front of a **noun**
  (<code>{f|先生|せんせい}としての{f|経験|けいけん}</code>). Before a verb, drop the `の`.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}として、この{f|写真|しゃしん}はとても{f|大切|たいせつ}です。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>にとって</b>、この{f|写真|しゃしん}はとても{f|大切|たいせつ}です。
  This is a judgement made from your side, not a role you are performing.
  <code>{f|私|わたし}としては</code> would be possible only if you were stating a
  position in a discussion.

## Practice

**1.** その{f|画家|がか}は{f|長|なが}い{f|間|あいだ}、{f|教師|きょうし}____{f|生活|せいかつ}していた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|教師|きょうし}<b>として</b>{f|生活|せいかつ}していた。

The painter was living **in the capacity of** a teacher - a role, held over a long
period. <code>{f|教師|きょうし}になって</code> would say he became one at a particular
moment, which is a different claim.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|初|はじ}めて{f|作|つく}った(としては・にしては)、なかなかうまくできた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|初|はじ}めて{f|作|つく}った<b>にしては</b>、なかなかうまくできた。

The sentence is expressing surprise: given that it was a first attempt, the result
beat expectations. That is `にしては`. `としては` also attaches to nouns rather than
clauses, so <code>{f|初|はじ}めて{f|作|つく}ったとしては</code> would not work here
anyway.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'd like to send this as a thank-you, not as a gift."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

これはプレゼント<b>としてではなく</b>、お{f|礼|れい}<b>として</b>{f|送|おく}りたいのです。

<code>としてではなく</code> rules out one role before naming the intended one. The
`は` is the ordinary contrastive `は` sitting on top of the pattern, which is why
the shape is <code>として+では+なく</code> rather than <code>としてじゃない</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|山田|やまだ}さんはリーダーにとって{f|優|すぐ}れているが、{f|技術者|ぎじゅつしゃ}としてこの{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|向|む}いていないと{f|思|おも}う。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|山田|やまだ}さんはリーダー<b>としては</b>{f|優|すぐ}れているが、{f|技術者|ぎじゅつしゃ}<b>としては</b>この{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|向|む}いていないと{f|思|おも}う。

Both slots are judging him **within a role**, so both need `として` - and `は` on
each, because the whole sentence is built on setting one role against the other.
`にとって` would make the leader the one doing the judging.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|私|わたし}<b>は</b>この{f|案|あん}に{f|反対|はんたい}です。
(b) {f|私|わたし}<b>としては</b>この{f|案|あん}に{f|反対|はんたい}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a flat statement of opposition. In a meeting it lands as a personal stand
and can sound confrontational, especially if others have already spoken in favour.

(b) frames the same opposition as **your position within the discussion** - "my
own view is against it" - which implicitly grants that the group may decide
otherwise. It is softer and more collegial, and it is the standard shape for
disagreeing in a Japanese meeting. The propositional content is identical; only the
social move differs.

</details>
