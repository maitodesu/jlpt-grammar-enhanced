# ～わけだ

**Meaning** · so that's why; it follows that; that means - presents the sentence as the conclusion that falls out of something already known

<code>わけ</code> is the noun {f|訳|わけ}, "reason, logic, the way a thing works out".
`わけだ` therefore never stands alone: it always looks back at a premise - something
just said, just discovered, or plainly visible - and says "given that, this is how
it adds up." Three flavours share the one form: *no wonder* (a realisation),
*it follows that* (a calculation), and *in other words* (a restatement).

## Formation

<code>わけ</code> is a noun, so the clause in front of it is in noun-modifying shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + わけだ | {f|知|し}っている → {f|知|し}っているわけだ |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + わけだ | {f|通|つう}じない → {f|通|つう}じないわけだ |
| い-adjective | plain form + わけだ | {f|寒|さむ}い → {f|寒|さむ}いわけだ |
| な-adjective | ～な + わけだ | {f|不便|ふべん}な → {f|不便|ふべん}なわけだ |
| Noun | ～な / ～という + わけだ | {f|留学生|りゅうがくせい} → {f|留学生|りゅうがくせい}なわけだ |
| Polite | … + わけです | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}いわけです |
| Restating what was just said | ～という + わけだ | {f|来|こ}られない → {f|来|こ}られないというわけだ |
| Connecting two sentences | というわけで | - → というわけで、{f|中止|ちゅうし}します |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the realisation use is almost always announced in advance by どうりで
("no wonder") or だから. Hearing どうりで, a Japanese listener is already expecting
`わけだ` at the end of the sentence.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|窓|まど}が{f|開|あ}いている。どうりで{f|寒|さむ}い<b>わけだ</b>。
The window's open. No wonder it's cold.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}のご{f|出身|しゅっしん}ですか。{f|雪|ゆき}に{f|慣|な}れている<b>わけですね</b>。
You're from Hokkaido? That explains why snow doesn't bother you.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は10{f|年|ねん}{f|大阪|おおさか}に{f|住|す}んでいたのだから、{f|関西弁|かんさいべん}が{f|話|はな}せる<b>わけだ</b>。
He lived in Osaka for ten years, so of course he can speak Kansai dialect.
</pre>

<pre>
バスが{f|一時間|いちじかん}に{f|一本|いっぽん}しかないのだから、{f|不便|ふべん}な<b>わけだ</b>。
There's only one bus an hour - no wonder the place is inconvenient.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}3{f|時間|じかん}{f|練習|れんしゅう}すれば、{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}で21{f|時間|じかん}になる<b>わけです</b>。
If you practise three hours a day, that works out at twenty-one hours a week.
</pre>

<pre>
つまり、{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|旅行|りょこう}には{f|参加|さんか}できない<b>というわけですね</b>。
So what you're saying is that you can't join this trip.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|夏|なつ}に{f|工場|こうじょう}が{f|閉|し}まったのだから、この{f|町|まち}の{f|人口|じんこう}が{f|減|へ}っている<b>わけだ</b>。
The factory shut last summer, so it stands to reason the town's population is falling.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎朝|まいあさ}5{f|時|じ}に{f|起|お}きて{f|走|はし}っているそうです。あんなに{f|速|はや}く{f|走|はし}れる<b>わけですね</b>。
Apparently he's up at five every morning to run. No wonder he's that fast.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`わけだ` is register-neutral in itself; `わけです` fits any polite setting and turns
up constantly in teaching, briefings and explanations, because its whole job is
showing how a conclusion follows. In casual speech it contracts to `わけ` with the
`だ` dropped - 「あ、それで{f|静|しず}かなわけか」 - and `わけか` is the shape a
realisation takes when you are talking to yourself.

The realisation use is warm and companionable: you have just understood something,
and saying so out loud gives the other person credit for explaining it. The
restating use - <code>つまり…というわけですね</code> - is the standard way to confirm
you have followed a complicated explanation, and in business it is a genuinely
useful move. Handle it with a little care, though. Restating something the listener
already knows perfectly well can sound as if you are explaining them to themselves,
and delivered flatly it comes across as condescending. That is a tendency, not a
rule; tone and context decide.

<code>というわけで</code> deserves separate mention. As a sentence opener it means "and
so", wrapping up a line of reasoning and moving to what follows from it:
<code>というわけで、{f|今日|きょう}はここまでにします</code>. It is extremely common in
spoken explanation and in blog-style writing.

One thing `わけだ` cannot do: announce your own decision. A decision is not something
that follows from evidence, so <code>{f|行|い}くことにしました</code> or
<code>{f|行|い}くつもりです</code> is what you want there.

## Contrast

### わけだ vs のだ・んです

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `んです` supplies the **background** to a situation. `わけだ`
draws the **conclusion** from a background already established.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|休|やす}みました。{f|熱|ねつ}があった<b>んです</b>。
I was off yesterday. I had a temperature.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|熱|ねつ}があったのなら、{f|休|やす}んだ<b>わけだ</b>。
If you had a temperature, then naturally you took the day off.
</pre>

The two point in opposite directions. `んです` runs from the situation back to its
explanation; `わけだ` runs from the explanation forward to the situation. That is
why `わけだ` is so often preceded by だから, どうりで or a `のだから` clause - the
premise has to already be on the table.

### わけだ vs はずだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `はずだ` is an expectation about something you have **not
confirmed**. `わけだ` accounts for something you **already know to be so**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|中国語|ちゅうごくご}が{f|話|はな}せる<b>はずです</b>。
Tanaka should be able to speak Chinese.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|上海|シャンハイ}で{f|育|そだ}ったのだから、{f|中国語|ちゅうごくご}が{f|話|はな}せる<b>わけです</b>。
He grew up in Shanghai, so of course he speaks Chinese.
</pre>

In the first, you have not heard him speak it; you are predicting. In the second you
have just heard him, and the sentence explains why. This is why
<code>はずだったのに</code> exists and there is no natural equivalent for `わけだ` - you
cannot be disappointed by a conclusion about a fact you have already observed.

The overlap that confuses people is that both can translate as "so he must speak
Chinese". Ask which way the sentence is facing: forward to an unverified prediction
(`はずだ`), or backward to explain a known fact (`わけだ`).

### わけだ vs ということだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - in the "that means" sense the two overlap; `わけだ` stresses that
the conclusion **follows logically**, `ということだ` simply **restates** the content.

</div>

<pre>
{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|反対|はんたい}したのなら、この{f|案|あん}は{f|通|とお}らない<b>わけだ</b>。
If everyone opposed it, then the proposal isn't going through.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|反対|はんたい}した。この{f|案|あん}は{f|通|とお}らない<b>ということだ</b>。
Everyone opposed it. That means the proposal isn't going through.
</pre>

The difference is thin here, and in many sentences either will pass. Where they part
company is that `ということだ` also carries the hearsay meaning - "I hear that" - and
`わけだ` never does. If a source is named, only `ということだ` is possible.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|医者|いしゃ}<b>だわけだ</b>。 → ✅ あの{f|人|ひと}は{f|医者|いしゃ}<b>なわけだ</b>。（or {f|医者|いしゃ}というわけだ）
  A noun links to <code>わけ</code> with `な` or `という`, never with `だ`.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>わけだ</b>。（said out of nowhere） → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るでしょう。
  `わけだ` needs a premise behind it. With nothing to conclude *from*, the sentence
  leaves the listener waiting for the missing first half.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|来年|らいねん}{f|留学|りゅうがく}する<b>わけです</b>。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|来年|らいねん}{f|留学|りゅうがく}する<b>つもりです</b>。
  Your own plans are decisions, not conclusions. `わけだ` would suggest your studying
  abroad was somehow deduced from circumstances.

- ❌ どうりで{f|道|みち}が{f|込|こ}んでいる<b>はずだ</b>。 → ✅ どうりで{f|道|みち}が{f|込|こ}んでいる<b>わけだ</b>。
  どうりで introduces an explanation for something you are looking at right now, and
  that pairs with `わけだ`. `はずだ` would turn a fact into a prediction.

## Practice

**1.** {f|時計|とけい}が{f|10分|じゅっぷん}{f|遅|おく}れていたのか。それで{f|電車|でんしゃ}に{f|乗|の}り{f|遅|おく}れた____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

それで{f|電車|でんしゃ}に{f|乗|の}り{f|遅|おく}れた<b>わけだ</b>。

The clock is the premise, and missing the train is the fact being accounted for.
それで plus `わけだ` is the classic realisation shape - "ah, so *that's* why".

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|昨日|きのう}{f|出張|しゅっちょう}に{f|行|い}ったから、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|会社|かいしゃ}にいない（はずだ・わけだ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|今日|きょう}は{f|会社|かいしゃ}にいない<b>はずだ</b>。

You have not checked his desk; you are predicting from what you know. That is
`はずだ`. `わけだ` would fit only if you had already found the desk empty and were
explaining why.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The heating was off all night - no wonder the room is freezing."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一晩中|ひとばんじゅう}{f|暖房|だんぼう}が{f|切|き}れていたのか。どうりで{f|部屋|へや}が{f|寒|さむ}い<b>わけだ</b>。

"No wonder" is どうりで, and the conclusion it introduces takes `わけだ`. The
い-adjective attaches with no change at all.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|元気|げんき}だわけだから、{f|心配|しんぱい}しなくていい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|元気|げんき}<b>なわけだから</b>、{f|心配|しんぱい}しなくていい。

な-adjectives take `な` before <code>わけ</code>. The `だ` of
<code>{f|元気|げんき}だ</code> cannot modify a noun, and <code>わけ</code> is a noun.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}している<b>んです</b>。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}している<b>わけです</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) offers his daily practice as the explanation for something else - why he is
good, why he is never free, why he looks tired. It supplies background.

(b) presents the daily practice as what follows from something already said: you
have just been told he trains with a club six evenings a week, and this sentence
adds it up. Said with no premise in front of it, (b) sounds unfinished.

</details>
