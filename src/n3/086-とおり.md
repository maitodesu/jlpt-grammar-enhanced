# ～とおり - in the way; the same as

**Meaning** · exactly as some model specifies - as told, as taught, as planned, as expected

The noun behind it is <code>{f|道|みち}</code>'s cousin <code>{f|通|とお}り</code>, "a
route", and the metaphor still shows: something has laid down a path - an instruction, a
schedule, a prediction - and the action follows it without stepping off. What
<code>とおり</code> insists on is **exactness**. If there is room for interpretation, this
is the wrong word.

## Formation

Two things decide the shape: whether a verb or a noun comes first, and - for nouns - 
whether you use `の`. The `どおり` form with rendaku is not optional.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain non-past) | V(る) + とおり（に） | {f|言|い}う → {f|言|い}うとおりに |
| Verb (plain past) | V(た) + とおり（に） | {f|習|なら}う → {f|習|なら}ったとおりに |
| Noun, with の | Noun + の + とおり（に） | {f|説明|せつめい} → {f|説明|せつめい}のとおりに |
| Noun, bare | Noun + どおり（に） - rendaku | {f|予定|よてい} → {f|予定|よてい}どおりに |
| Modifying a noun | ～とおりの + Noun | {f|思|おも}ったとおりの{f|味|あじ} |
| Demonstratives | このとおり／そのとおり／あのとおり | そのとおりです |
| Set phrases | ご{f|覧|らん}のとおり、おっしゃるとおり、{f|文字|もじ}どおり | - |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `の` + とおり, or bare noun + **どおり**. Never `の` + どおり, and never
a bare noun + とおり. <code>{f|予定|よてい}のとおり</code> and
<code>{f|予定|よてい}どおり</code> are both correct;
❌<code>{f|予定|よてい}のどおり</code> and ❌<code>{f|予定|よてい}とおり</code> are not.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the `に` is dropped when the phrase is followed by `だ／です` or stands as a
comment: <code>{f|思|おも}ったとおりだ</code>, <code>そのとおりです</code>. Keep the `に`
when it modifies a verb: <code>{f|思|おも}ったとおりに{f|進|すす}んだ</code>. Written in
kanji the bare-noun form is <code>{f|予定通|よていどお}り</code> - same reading, and the
rendaku is still there.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}が{f|言|い}った<b>とおりに</b>やってみます。
I'll try doing it exactly as the teacher said.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|時間|じかん}<b>どおり</b>に{f|着|つ}きました。
The train arrived right on schedule.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}の<b>とおりに</b>{f|組|く}み{f|立|た}てたのに、なぜか{f|動|うご}きません。
I put it together exactly as the manual said, and for some reason it doesn't work.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|思|おも}った<b>とおり</b>、あの{f|店|みせ}は{f|今日|きょう}も{f|混|こ}んでいた。
Just as I thought, that place was packed again today.
</pre>

<pre>
「{f|準備|じゅんび}は{f|早|はや}めにということですね。」「その<b>とおり</b>です。」
"So you're saying we should prepare early." "Exactly."
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|予定|よてい}<b>どおり</b>{f|進|すす}んでいますので、{f|来月|らいげつ}には{f|終|お}わる{f|見|み}こみです。
The work is going to plan, so we expect it to finish next month.
</pre>

<pre>
ご{f|覧|らん}の<b>とおり</b>、{f|会場|かいじょう}はもう{f|満席|まんせき}ですので、{f|立|た}ち{f|見|み}になってしまいます。
As you can see, the hall is already full, so you'd have to stand.
</pre>

<pre>

I thought I'd made it just the way my mother taught me, but it came out tasting like something else entirely.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral and equally at home in a manual, a news report and a
kitchen. What varies is the fixed phrases built on it, and several of them are worth
learning as units.

<code>そのとおりです</code> is strong agreement - "precisely so" - and is common in
business Japanese, where a bare 「はい」 can sound thin. Its polite version,
<code>おっしゃるとおりです</code>, agrees with a superior and is one of the most useful
phrases in an office; <code>おっしゃるとおりですが…</code> is how you begin to disagree
politely. <code>ご{f|覧|らん}のとおり</code> ("as you can see") belongs to presentations,
and <code>{f|文字|もじ}どおり</code> means "literally".

The bare-noun `どおり` forms behave almost like single words - 
<code>{f|予定|よてい}どおり</code>, <code>{f|時間|じかん}どおり</code>,
<code>{f|計画|けいかく}どおり</code>, <code>{f|期待|きたい}どおり</code>,
<code>{f|注文|ちゅうもん}どおり</code> - and they carry a faint official tone, the sound of
things going as they were supposed to. The verb forms with `とおりに` are more personal:
someone told you, taught you or predicted, and you matched it.

One tense point that trips people up: both <code>{f|言|い}うとおりに</code> and
<code>{f|言|い}ったとおりに</code> are correct, and they are not quite the same. The
non-past form is habitual or still ongoing - "as he says", the way he always tells you.
The past form points at one particular occasion - "as he said (that time)".

## Contrast

### とおりに vs ように

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>とおりに</code> means "matching the model exactly". `ように`
means "in a manner resembling", and it also does purpose ("so that"), which
<code>とおりに</code> cannot do at all.

</div>

<pre>
{f|見本|みほん}の<b>とおりに</b>{f|書|か}いてください。
Please write it exactly like the sample.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもにも{f|読|よ}める<b>ように</b>、{f|大|おお}きく{f|書|か}いてください。
Please write it large, so that children can read it too.
</pre>

The second sentence has no model to copy - it states a goal. Note also that `ように` can
take a resemblance with no instruction behind it
（<code>{f|鳥|とり}のように{f|飛|と}ぶ</code>）, where <code>とおりに</code> would be
nonsense: a bird has issued no instructions.

### とおりに vs まま

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>とおりに</code> is about **following** something. `まま` is
about **not changing** something. One copies a model; the other leaves a state alone.

</div>

<pre>
{f|習|なら}った<b>とおりに</b>{f|包丁|ほうちょう}を{f|使|つか}えば、{f|指|ゆび}を{f|切|き}ることはありません。
If you use the knife the way you were taught, you won't cut your finger.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けた<b>まま</b>{f|出|で}かけてしまいました。
I went out and left the window open.
</pre>

The two look similar after a past-tense verb, which is where the confusion starts. Ask what
the previous action is doing: supplying a pattern to imitate (<code>とおりに</code>), or
supplying a condition that persists (`まま`)?

### とおりに vs にしたがって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>にしたがって</code> is the formal "in accordance with", used for
rules, orders and procedures. It also has a second life meaning "as X progresses, Y", which
<code>とおりに</code> never has.

</div>

<pre>
{f|係員|かかりいん}の{f|指示|しじ}<b>にしたがって</b>{f|移動|いどう}してください。
Please move in accordance with the staff's instructions.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|係員|かかりいん}が{f|言|い}った<b>とおりに</b>{f|階段|かいだん}を{f|下|お}りました。
We went down the stairs exactly as the attendant told us.
</pre>

<code>にしたがって</code> sounds like a public announcement; <code>とおりに</code> sounds
like a person recounting what they did. For the "as X increases" meaning - 
<code>{f|年|とし}をとるにしたがって</code> - only <code>したがって</code> works.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|予定|よてい}のどおり{f|進|すす}んでいます。 → ✅ {f|予定|よてい}どおり／{f|予定|よてい}のとおり{f|進|すす}んでいます。
  Pick one: `の` plus <code>とおり</code>, or a bare noun plus `どおり`. The two cannot be
  combined, and this is the single most common error with the pattern.

- ❌ {f|地図|ちず}とおりに{f|歩|ある}きました。 → ✅ {f|地図|ちず}どおりに{f|歩|ある}きました。
  A noun attaching directly triggers rendaku. <code>とおり</code> only stays unvoiced
  after `の` or after a verb.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}が{f|言|い}ったのとおりに → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}が{f|言|い}ったとおりに
  No `の` after a verb. The `の` in <code>{f|説明|せつめい}のとおり</code> belongs to the
  noun, not to the pattern.

- ❌ {f|鳥|とり}のとおりに{f|空|そら}を{f|飛|と}んでみたい。 → ✅ {f|鳥|とり}のように{f|空|そら}を{f|飛|と}んでみたい。
  <code>とおりに</code> needs an instruction, plan or prediction to follow. For plain
  resemblance you want `ように`.

## Practice

**1.** バスは{f|時間|じかん}____{f|来|き}ませんでしたが、{f|会議|かいぎ}には{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}いました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

バスは{f|時間|じかん}<b>どおりに</b>{f|来|き}ませんでしたが、…

A bare noun in front means the rendaku form `どおり`. Writing
<code>{f|時間|じかん}のとおりに</code> is grammatical but sounds oddly heavy;
<code>{f|時間|じかん}どおり</code> is the set phrase everyone uses.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|子|こ}どもが{f|喜|よろこ}ぶ（とおりに・ように）{f|部屋|へや}を{f|飾|かざ}りました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|子|こ}どもが{f|喜|よろこ}ぶ<b>ように</b>{f|部屋|へや}を{f|飾|かざ}りました。

This is a purpose - decorating *so that* the children will be pleased - and purpose is
`ように` only. <code>とおりに</code> would require the children to have specified how the
room was to be decorated.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "It turned out exactly as she predicted."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼女|かのじょ}が{f|言|い}った<b>とおりに</b>なりました。

A prediction is a model that reality then matched, which is exactly what
<code>とおり</code> is for. The past-tense verb points at the one occasion when she said
it. <code>{f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|予想|よそう}どおりになりました</code> is an equally
natural noun version.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|係|かかり}の{f|人|ひと}が{f|説明|せつめい}したのとおりに、{f|書類|しょるい}を{f|出|だ}しました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|係|かかり}の{f|人|ひと}が{f|説明|せつめい}したとおりに、{f|書類|しょるい}を{f|出|だ}しました。

Remove the `の`. Verbs attach straight to <code>とおり</code>; only nouns take `の`, and a
noun version here would be
<code>{f|説明|せつめい}のとおりに</code> or <code>{f|説明|せつめい}どおりに</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|部長|ぶちょう}が{f|言|い}う<b>とおりに</b>やっています。
(b) {f|部長|ぶちょう}が{f|言|い}った<b>とおりに</b>やりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) uses the non-past, so the model is standing and general: this is how the manager
always wants it done, and you are still doing it that way. It describes a working
practice.

(b) uses the past, which pins the instruction to one occasion - he said something, and you
did precisely that. It is the sentence you produce when the result has gone wrong and you
would like it on record whose instructions you were following.

</details>
