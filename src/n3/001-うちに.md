# ～うちに

**Meaning** · while a state still holds - and with the sense that it will not hold much longer

<code>～うちに</code> opens a window and tells you to act inside it. The window is a
state that is currently true - it's hot, you're young, the baby is asleep - and the
pattern quietly carries the reason for the hurry: **that state is going to end.**
A second, softer use drops the urgency and reports a change that crept up on you
while something was going on: <code>{f|聞|き}いているうちに…</code>, "as I listened…".

## Formation

<code>うちに</code> is the noun <code>うち</code> ("interior, span") plus `に`, so
everything in front of it is in the shape that modifies a noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ている) | ている + うちに | {f|待|ま}つ → {f|待|ま}っているうちに |
| Verb (stative, dictionary) | Plain non-past + うちに | いる → {f|日本|にほん}にいるうちに |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + うちに | {f|忘|わす}れる → {f|忘|わす}れないうちに |
| い-adjective | Plain form + うちに | {f|熱|あつ}い → {f|熱|あつ}いうちに |
| な-adjective | な + うちに | {f|元気|げんき}だ → {f|元気|げんき}なうちに |
| Noun | の + うちに | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}のうちに |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the subject inside the <code>うちに</code> clause takes `が`, never `は`:
{f|子|こ}どもが{f|寝|ね}ているうちに. This is the same embedding rule you met with
たら and とき clauses at N4.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - a plain dictionary-form verb before <code>うちに</code> is limited to verbs
that describe a *lasting situation* - いる, {f|住|す}む, {f|勤|つと}める. For an
ordinary action verb you need ている: {f|読|よ}んでいるうちに, not
{f|読|よ}むうちに.

</div>

</pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>うちに</code> is register-neutral. It takes ます or plain endings without changing
character, and it turns up in signage, in advice from your grandmother and in company
email alike.

What is *not* neutral is the shape of the main clause. Because the pattern exists to
say "act before the window shuts", it pulls strongly toward volitional and advisory
endings - よう, たい, ておく, ておこう, てください, たほうがいい, ないと. A flat past
statement with no connection to the window closing sounds off, and that is usually the
signal that you wanted <code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code> instead.

The <code>～ているうちに</code> use is the exception, and it is worth learning as its own
thing. There the main clause is a **change that happened to you**, typically unnoticed
and typically expressed with てくる, てしまう or a なる verb:
{f|歩|ある}いているうちに{f|道|みち}に{f|迷|まよ}ってしまった. Nobody is being advised
to hurry; the sentence is reporting drift.

Two fixed expressions to recognise. <code>そのうちに</code> (often just そのうち) is an
adverb meaning "before long, one of these days" - 
そのうち{f|遊|あそ}びに{f|行|い}きます is a polite, pleasantly vague "I'll drop by
sometime." And <code>{f|知|し}らないうちに</code> means "without my noticing", not "while
I didn't know" in any literal sense.

There is a mild literary flavour to <code>{f|若|わか}いうちに</code> and
<code>{f|元気|げんき}なうちに</code>, which show up in proverbs and in the kind of advice
older speakers give. Nothing about them is stiff, but they carry a whiff of
life-lesson, which is exactly why they are used.

## Contrast

### ～うちに vs ～{f|間|あいだ}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both put an event inside a window, but <code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code>
only says *when*, while <code>うちに</code> adds *before it's too late*. If the window
closing does not matter, use <code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code>.

</div>

<pre>
{f|母|はは}が{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}っている<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>、{f|宅配便|たくはいびん}が{f|届|とど}いた。
A delivery arrived while my mother was out shopping.
</pre>

Nothing here is racing the clock; the shopping trip is just a stretch of time that
happened to contain the delivery. Swap in <code>うちに</code> and the sentence starts to
imply the courier deliberately used her absence, which is not what happened.

<pre>
{f|母|はは}が{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}っている<b>うちに</b>、ケーキを{f|隠|かく}しておこう。
Let's hide the cake while Mum's out shopping.
</pre>

Now it fits: she is coming back, and that is the whole point.

The mirror-image restriction is also useful. <code>うちに</code> resists windows defined
by the clock or the calendar, because a clock window has no "state" to run out:
❌<code>{f|三時|さんじ}から{f|五時|ごじ}のうちに</code> →
✅<code>{f|三時|さんじ}から{f|五時|ごじ}の{f|間|あいだ}に</code>.

### ～うちに vs ～{f|前|まえ}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> orders two events; <code>うちに</code>
judges an opportunity. <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> is neutral, and it looks forward at the
event; <code>うちに</code> looks around at the situation you are still standing in.

</div>

<pre>
{f|年|とし}を{f|取|と}る<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、{f|家|いえ}を{f|建|た}てるつもりだ。
I intend to build a house before I get old.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|若|わか}いうちに</b>、{f|家|いえ}を{f|建|た}てるつもりだ。
I intend to build a house while I'm still young.
</pre>

The first is a plain schedule: house, then old age. The second says youth is what makes
it possible, and that the possibility is draining away. Note also that
<code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> takes a dictionary-form verb naming the *event*, while
<code>うちに</code> takes the *state* that precedes it - which is why the two versions
above look nothing alike.

### ～のうちに vs ～{f|中|ちゅう}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|今日|きょう}{f|中|じゅう}に</code> is a deadline;
<code>{f|今日|きょう}のうちに</code> is an opportunity. Same span, different argument.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|書類|しょるい}は{f|今日|きょう}<b>{f|中|じゅう}に</b>{f|出|だ}してください。
Please submit these documents by the end of today.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|忙|いそが}しいから、{f|今日|きょう}<b>のうちに</b>{f|出|だ}しておこう。
Tomorrow's going to be busy, so let's get it submitted while we still have today.
</pre>

<code>{f|中|じゅう}に</code> states a limit somebody has imposed. <code>のうちに</code>
gives a reason, and the reason is always some version of "afterwards will be worse".
<code>{f|中|ちゅう}に</code> also attaches only to nouns naming a measured span; you
cannot say ❌<code>{f|元気|げんき}{f|中|ちゅう}に</code> for
<code>{f|元気|げんき}なうちに</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>{f|学生|がくせい}うちに</b>{f|留学|りゅうがく}したい。 → ✅ <b>{f|学生|がくせい}のうちに</b>{f|留学|りゅうがく}したい。
  <code>うち</code> is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs `の`. A な-adjective
  likewise needs な: ❌<code>{f|元気|げんき}のうちに</code> →
  ✅<code>{f|元気|げんき}なうちに</code>.

- ❌ {f|九時|くじ}から{f|十時|じゅうじ}<b>のうちに</b>{f|電話|でんわ}します。 → ✅ {f|九時|くじ}から{f|十時|じゅうじ}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>{f|電話|でんわ}します。
  A window fixed by the clock has no state to lose, so <code>うちに</code> has nothing
  to work with. Use <code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code>.

- ❌ {f|本|ほん}を<b>{f|読|よ}むうちに</b>{f|眠|ねむ}くなった。 → ✅ {f|本|ほん}を<b>{f|読|よ}んでいるうちに</b>{f|眠|ねむ}くなった。
  An action verb has to be in ている. The dictionary form is reserved for verbs that
  already describe a standing situation, like いる or {f|住|す}む.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}<b>は</b>{f|寝|ね}ているうちに{f|地震|じしん}があった。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>が</b>{f|寝|ね}ているうちに{f|地震|じしん}があった。
  The subject of a subordinate clause takes `が`. `は` cannot be trapped inside a clause
  that only modifies <code>うち</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|赤|あか}ちゃんが{f|寝|ね}ている____、{f|急|いそ}いで{f|晩|ばん}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|作|つく}ってしまおう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|赤|あか}ちゃんが{f|寝|ね}ている<b>うちに</b>、{f|急|いそ}いで{f|晩|ばん}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|作|つく}ってしまおう。

The baby is going to wake up, and that is exactly why the cooking has to happen now - 
the window is closing, which is <code>うちに</code>'s job. <code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code>
would also be grammatical but would drop the urgency that いそいで and てしまおう are
already signalling.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|試験|しけん}は{f|一時|いちじ}から{f|三時|さんじ}までです。その（うちに・{f|間|あいだ}に）{f|一度|いちど}トイレに{f|行|い}ってもいいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

その<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>{f|一度|いちど}トイレに{f|行|い}ってもいいです。

The window is defined by two clock times, not by a state that is about to expire, so
<code>うちに</code> has no opportunity to point at. This is the plain "at some point
inside the span" meaning, which belongs to <code>{f|間|あいだ}に</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "While I was living in Osaka, I got used to the local accent."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|大阪|おおさか}に{f|住|す}んでいる<b>うちに</b>、{f|関西弁|かんさいべん}に{f|慣|な}れた。

This is the drift use: nobody decided to get used to the accent, it simply happened
over the course of the stay. The main clause is a change of state, which is the
signature of <code>～ているうちに</code>. {f|住|す}む also allows the dictionary form
here, since it names a lasting situation.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|暗|くら}いのうちに{f|山|やま}を{f|下|お}りたほうがいい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ <b>{f|明|あか}るいうちに</b>{f|山|やま}を{f|下|お}りたほうがいい。

Two problems. An い-adjective attaches directly, with no `の` - 
<code>{f|暗|くら}いうちに</code>, not ❌<code>{f|暗|くら}いのうちに</code>. And the
meaning was inverted: <code>{f|暗|くら}いうちに</code> would mean "while it is still
dark", i.e. set off before dawn. The state you want to use up is the daylight.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|日本|にほん}にいる<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>、{f|富士山|ふじさん}に{f|登|のぼ}りました。
(b) {f|日本|にほん}にいる<b>うちに</b>、{f|富士山|ふじさん}に{f|登|のぼ}りました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a neutral report: the climb took place somewhere inside the period of the stay.
It answers "when did you climb it?" and says nothing about why then.

(b) adds a motive. It frames the stay as a limited chance that was running out, so the
climb reads as something done deliberately before the opportunity disappeared - 
closer to "I made sure to climb Fuji while I was still in Japan." With a plain past
main clause (b) also sounds slightly more natural if you keep a marker of intent, such
as {f|登|のぼ}っておきました.

</details>
