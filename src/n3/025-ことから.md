# ～ことから

**Meaning** · from the fact that ～ - the evidence you reasoned from, not the cause of what happened

`ことから` introduces **grounds**, not a cause. What comes before it is something
observed or established; what comes after is the conclusion somebody drew from it.
The two clauses are linked by an act of reasoning, which is why the second half so
often ends in {f|考|かんが}えられる, {f|分|わ}かる, {f|判断|はんだん}した or
{f|呼|よ}ばれている rather than in a plain event.

## Formation

`こと` nominalises the clause in front of it, and `から` here is the `から` of source
 - "out of", "starting from".

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain) | plain form + ことから | {f|似|に}ている → {f|似|に}ていることから |
| Verb (past) | た-form + ことから | {f|続|つづ}いた → {f|続|つづ}いたことから |
| い-adjective | plain form + ことから | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}いことから |
| な-adjective | ～**な**／**である** + ことから | {f|丁寧|ていねい}だ → {f|丁寧|ていねい}なことから |
| Noun | Noun + **である** + ことから | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}であることから |
| Referring back | このことから、～ | このことから、～と{f|言|い}える |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - what follows `ことから` must be a **conclusion**: an inference, a
judgement, a name, or a statement of what is generally said. It cannot be a
decision, a request, an invitation or a plain report of an event.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`ことから` is written Japanese. It belongs to reports, analyses, news copy, museum
captions, academic prose and the more careful kind of explanation - the registers in
which somebody shows their working. In casual speech it sounds like a person reading
aloud from a document; the spoken equivalent is simply `から` plus a verb of
thinking: {f|机|つくえ}が{f|片付|かたづ}いてたから、もう{f|帰|かえ}ったんだと{f|思|おも}った。

Its second home is **naming**. A very large number of Japanese explanations of how
something got its name are built on this pattern - the shape of a mountain, the
colour of a fish, a line in an old poem - and they nearly always close with
{f|呼|よ}ばれるようになった or {f|名前|なまえ}がついた. If you read guidebooks or
{f|由来|ゆらい} sections, you will meet it constantly.

The thing to keep straight is that `ことから` **does not claim causation**. In
{f|電気|でんき}がついていることから、{f|在宅|ざいたく}だと{f|分|わ}かる, the light did not
cause anybody to be at home; it is merely how the speaker knows. Feeding it a
cause-and-effect pair produces a sentence that sounds oddly indirect - as though you
had inferred your own headache from evidence.

There is no politeness dimension to worry about: the pattern itself does not change
shape, and the register lives in the vocabulary and the sentence ending around it.

## Contrast

### ～ことから vs ～から・～ので

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `から` and `ので` join a cause to its result. `ことから` joins
evidence to a conclusion. Ask whether the first clause *made* the second happen, or
merely *revealed* it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}の{f|電気|でんき}がついていた<b>ので</b>、{f|窓|まど}から{f|声|こえ}をかけた。
The light was on in the room, so I called in at the window.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}の{f|電気|でんき}がついている<b>ことから</b>、{f|彼|かれ}はもう{f|戻|もど}っていると{f|判断|はんだん}した。
From the fact that the light was on, I judged that he was already back.
</pre>

In the first, the light is why the speaker acted. In the second, the light is the
speaker's evidence for a belief - and the belief was true before anybody looked. The
`ので` version can be followed by anything at all; the `ことから` version can only be
followed by an inference.

### ～ことから vs ～ことだから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one syllable apart, and neither is a variant of the other.
`ことから` = "from the fact that…". <code>Noun + のことだから</code> = "knowing what X
is like, naturally…".

</div>

<pre>
{f|机|つくえ}に{f|荷物|にもつ}が{f|置|お}いてある<b>ことから</b>、{f|彼女|かのじょ}はもう{f|着|つ}いていると{f|分|わ}かる。
The bag on the desk tells me she has already arrived.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼女|かのじょ}の<b>ことだから</b>、{f|約束|やくそく}の{f|十分|じゅっぷん}{f|前|まえ}には{f|着|つ}いているだろう。
Knowing her, she'll have got there ten minutes early.
</pre>

`ことだから` takes a **person** (or occasionally an organisation) with `の` in front
of it, and its conclusion rests on that person's known character rather than on
anything observed today. `ことから` takes a whole clause and rests on the observation
itself. If you can insert "typical of them" into the English, it is `ことだから`.

### ～ことから vs ～ものだから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same length, opposite worlds. `ことから` is written reasoning
from evidence; `ものだから` is spoken excuse-making. Neither can be swapped for the
other in any sentence.

</div>

<pre>
{f|地面|じめん}がぬれている<b>ことから</b>、{f|夜中|よなか}に{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ったことが{f|分|わ}かる。
The wet ground shows that it rained during the night.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|急|きゅう}に{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ってきた<b>ものだから</b>、{f|途中|とちゅう}で{f|引|ひ}き{f|返|かえ}してきました。
It suddenly started raining, you see, so I turned back halfway.
</pre>

The first sentence is cool and impersonal, and its conclusion is a fact about the
world. The second is warm and defensive, and its conclusion is about what the
speaker did and would rather not be criticised for. Learners who have met both
patterns in the same week tend to blur them; the register test settles it instantly.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|頭|あたま}が{f|痛|いた}い<b>ことから</b>、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|休|やす}みます。 → ✅ {f|頭|あたま}が{f|痛|いた}い<b>ので</b>、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|休|やす}みます。
  A personal reason followed by a decision. `ことから` needs observed evidence and an
  inference, not a headache and a plan.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っている<b>ことから</b>、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}って{f|行|い}ってください。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っている<b>ので</b>、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}って{f|行|い}ってください。
  Requests cannot follow `ことから` at all.

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}のことから、{f|割引|わりびき}が{f|受|う}けられる。 → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>である</b>ことから、{f|割引|わりびき}が{f|受|う}けられる。
  A noun links with `である`, not `の`. (With `の` you have accidentally written the
  opening of <code>のことだから</code>.)

- ❌ {f|静|しず}かだことから → ✅ {f|静|しず}か<b>な</b>ことから ／ {f|静|しず}か<b>である</b>ことから
  Plain `だ` cannot stand in front of `こと`; use the attributive `な`, or `である` in
  formal writing.

- ❌ {f|田中|たなか}さんの<b>ことから</b>、きっと{f|時間|じかん}どおりに{f|来|き}ますよ。 → ✅ {f|田中|たなか}さんの<b>ことだから</b>、きっと{f|時間|じかん}どおりに{f|来|き}ますよ。
  Predicting from somebody's character is the `ことだから` pattern. `ことから` would
  need an observation, not a person.

## Practice

**1.** {f|足跡|あしあと}が{f|一人分|ひとりぶん}しかなかった____、{f|犯人|はんにん}は{f|単独|たんどく}であったと{f|見|み}られている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|足跡|あしあと}が{f|一人分|ひとりぶん}しかなかった<b>ことから</b>、{f|犯人|はんにん}は{f|単独|たんどく}であったと{f|見|み}られている。

Physical evidence in the first clause, an inference marked by
<code>{f|見|み}られている</code> in the second - the textbook shape for `ことから`.
`ので` would suggest the footprints somehow caused the criminal to work alone.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|部長|ぶちょう}の（ことから・ことだから）、{f|今回|こんかい}も{f|細|こま}かいところまで{f|確認|かくにん}してくるはずだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|部長|ぶちょう}の<b>ことだから</b>、{f|今回|こんかい}も{f|細|こま}かいところまで{f|確認|かくにん}してくるはずだ。

The prediction rests on what the manager is *like*, and the phrase in front is a
person plus `の`. That combination is always `ことだから`. `ことから` needs a clause
of observed fact.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The fact that the shop is always crowded shows that the food is good."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

いつも{f|混|こ}んでいる<b>ことから</b>、あの{f|店|みせ}の{f|料理|りょうり}はおいしいと{f|言|い}える。

The crowd is evidence, not a cause, so `ことから` is right and the conclusion is
marked with <code>と{f|言|い}える</code>. Ending with a bare
<code>おいしいです</code> would be odd - a `ことから` clause wants a verb of judgement
after it.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|港|みなと}は{f|波|なみ}が{f|静|しず}かだことから、{f|昔|むかし}から{f|船|ふね}が{f|集|あつ}まった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|港|みなと}は{f|波|なみ}が{f|静|しず}か<b>な</b>ことから、{f|昔|むかし}から{f|船|ふね}が{f|集|あつ}まった。

<code>{f|静|しず}か</code> is a な-adjective and `こと` is a noun, so they join with `な`
 - or with `である` if the surrounding prose is formal. Plain `だ` never appears in
that slot.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|窓|まど}が{f|割|わ}れていた<b>ので</b>、{f|警察|けいさつ}に{f|連絡|れんらく}しました。
(b) {f|窓|まど}が{f|割|わ}れていた<b>ことから</b>、{f|泥棒|どろぼう}が{f|入|はい}ったと{f|考|かんが}えられます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a chain of events: the window was broken, and because of that the speaker
picked up the phone. The second clause is an action.

(b) is a chain of reasoning: the broken window is the evidence, and the burglary is
what the speaker deduces from it. The second clause is a belief, and it is marked as
one by <code>{f|考|かんが}えられます</code>. Put (a)'s action after `ことから` and the
sentence stops making sense; put (b)'s inference after `ので` and you get a looser,
more conversational version of the same thought.

</details>
