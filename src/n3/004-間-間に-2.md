# 間 / 間に - between

**Meaning** · the gap between two things - physically, or between two parties, or across a group

Same word (<code>{f|間|あいだ}</code>), same core idea of "the space in the middle", but
here the two edges are **things** rather than the start and end of a stretch of time.
Between the station and the bank. Between the two of them. Popular among students. The
grammar to get right is which particle follows: `に` for something *located* in the gap,
`で` for something *happening* across a group, `の` when you are modifying a noun.

## Formation

<code>{f|間|あいだ}</code> is a noun. Two nouns joined by `と` form the two edges, and
`の` links the pair to <code>{f|間|あいだ}</code>.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Two nouns (space) | AとBの{f|間|あいだ} | {f|机|つくえ}・いす → {f|机|つくえ}といすの{f|間|あいだ} |
| Location of something | …の{f|間|あいだ}に + ある/いる | {f|木|き} → {f|木|き}と{f|木|き}の{f|間|あいだ}に{f|道|みち}がある |
| Across a group | Noun (plural sense) + の{f|間|あいだ}で | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}の{f|間|あいだ}で |
| Between two parties | AとBの{f|間|あいだ}で / に | {f|両国|りょうこく} → {f|両国|りょうこく}の{f|間|あいだ}で |
| Two endpoints | AからBまでの{f|間|あいだ} | {f|東京|とうきょう}・{f|大阪|おおさか} → {f|東京|とうきょう}から{f|大阪|おおさか}までの{f|間|あいだ} |
| Modifying a noun | …の{f|間|あいだ}の + Noun | {f|二人|ふたり} → {f|二人|ふたり}の{f|間|あいだ}の{f|約束|やくそく} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - with two edges of the same kind you may repeat the noun instead of listing
two: {f|木|き}と{f|木|き}の{f|間|あいだ}, {f|建物|たてもの}と{f|建物|たてもの}の{f|間|あいだ}.
This is idiomatic and reads as "between one X and the next".

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}と{f|銀行|ぎんこう}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>{f|花屋|はなや}がある。
There's a florist between the station and the bank.
</pre>

<pre>
ソファとかべの<b>{f|間|あいだ}</b>から{f|鍵|かぎ}が{f|出|で}てきた。
The keys turned up from between the sofa and the wall.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|歌|うた}は{f|若|わか}い{f|人|ひと}たちの<b>{f|間|あいだ}で</b>はやっているらしい。
That song is apparently a hit among young people.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|九時|くじ}から{f|五時|ごじ}までの<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>{f|電話|でんわ}をください。
Please call me between nine and five.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|東京|とうきょう}と{f|大阪|おおさか}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}</b>を{f|何度|なんど}も{f|往復|おうふく}した。
I shuttled back and forth between Tokyo and Osaka more times than I can count.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|二人|ふたり}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>{f|何|なに}があったのか、だれも{f|知|し}らない。
Nobody knows what went on between those two.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|両国|りょうこく}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}で</b>{f|新|あたら}しい{f|貿易|ぼうえき}の{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いが{f|始|はじ}まった。
Fresh trade talks have opened between the two countries.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|建物|たてもの}と{f|建物|たてもの}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>{f|細|ほそ}い{f|道|みち}があって、そこを{f|通|とお}ると{f|駅|えき}まで{f|五分|ごふん}で{f|着|つ}きます。
There's a narrow lane between the two buildings, and if you cut through it you reach the station in five minutes.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The physical sense is entirely everyday. The other two lean slightly formal.

<code>…の{f|間|あいだ}で</code> meaning "among" belongs to news writing, market reports
and anything describing a trend:
{f|中高生|ちゅうこうせい}の{f|間|あいだ}で{f|人気|にんき}が{f|高|たか}まっている. In casual
speech people usually say <code>…の{f|中|なか}で</code> or just name the group with `は`.
It is not stiff, but it does sound like something you read.

<code>{f|二人|ふたり}の{f|間|あいだ}</code> for a relationship is idiomatic and slightly
delicate. {f|二人|ふたり}の{f|間|あいだ}に{f|子|こ}どもが{f|生|う}まれた is a neutral way
of saying a couple had a child; {f|二人|ふたり}の{f|間|あいだ}に{f|何|なに}かあった
gestures at trouble without naming it, and that discretion is exactly why the phrase
gets used. The related noun <code>{f|間柄|あいだがら}</code> ("the relationship between
parties") is a step more formal again and belongs to writing.

A note on the spatial sense in speech: when the two edges are obvious, Japanese often
drops one and says <code>…の{f|間|あいだ}</code> with a plural or a repeated noun rather
than spelling out A と B. 「{f|本|ほん}の{f|間|あいだ}にはさんでおいた」 - "I slipped it in
between the books" - needs no list.

Finally, this <code>{f|間|あいだ}</code> is a full noun and can take any particle a noun
can: を ({f|間|あいだ}を{f|通|とお}る), から, まで, は. The time-window use on the
previous page is much more restricted.

## Contrast

### {f|間|あいだ} (between) vs {f|間|あいだ} (while)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - look at what precedes it. **Two nouns joined by と**, or a
から…まで pair, gives you *between*. **A clause, or a single noun naming a period**,
gives you *while*.

</div>

<pre>
{f|夏|なつ}と{f|秋|あき}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>もう{f|一度|いちど}{f|海|うみ}へ{f|行|い}きたい。
I want to get to the sea once more between summer and autumn.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|夏|なつ}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>もう{f|一度|いちど}{f|海|うみ}へ{f|行|い}きたい。
I want to get to the sea once more during the summer.
</pre>

One `と` is the entire difference. The first locates the trip in the gap *between* two
seasons; the second locates it *inside* one of them.

The ambiguity is real but rarely a problem, because the two readings need different
material in front of them. Where it does bite is with から…まで:
<code>{f|九時|くじ}から{f|五時|ごじ}までの{f|間|あいだ}に</code> is simultaneously "between
nine and five" and "during nine-to-five", and Japanese does not distinguish them - the
window *is* the gap.

### {f|間|あいだ}に vs {f|間|あいだ}で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `に` puts something **in** the gap; `で` makes the gap the **arena**
in which something goes on. Existence and placement take `に`; activity, exchange and
popularity take `で`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|棚|たな}と{f|壁|かべ}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>ほこりがたまっていた。
Dust had built up between the shelf and the wall.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|社員|しゃいん}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}で</b>その{f|話|はなし}はもう{f|広|ひろ}まっている。
Word of it has already got around among the staff.
</pre>

Both particles are possible with two named parties, and then they say different things:

<pre>
{f|両親|りょうしん}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>{f|問題|もんだい}がある。
There is a problem between my parents. (it exists, sitting there)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|両親|りょうしん}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}で</b>{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いが{f|続|つづ}いている。
Discussions are going on between my parents. (an activity they are conducting)
</pre>

Verbs of existence and appearance - ある, いる, できる, {f|生|う}まれる - take `に`.
Verbs of doing, spreading and agreeing take `で`. If in doubt, ask whether the thing
*sits* there or *happens* there.

### {f|間|あいだ} vs {f|中|なか}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|間|あいだ}</code> is the gap **between** things;
<code>{f|中|なか}</code> is the inside of a **container or a set**. Comparison and
superlatives always take <code>{f|中|なか}</code>.

</div>

<pre>
クラスの<b>{f|中|なか}で</b>{f|彼女|かのじょ}が{f|一番|いちばん}{f|足|あし}が{f|速|はや}い。
She's the fastest in the class.
</pre>

<pre>
クラスの<b>{f|間|あいだ}で</b>そのうわさが{f|広|ひろ}がった。
The rumour spread through the class.
</pre>

<code>{f|中|なか}で</code> looks into a group and picks a member out;
<code>{f|間|あいだ}で</code> looks at the space between the members and describes what
travels across it. That is why ❌{f|三人|さんにん}の{f|間|あいだ}で{f|一番|いちばん}{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}い
is wrong: choosing one of three is a <code>{f|中|なか}</code> job.

<div class="note">

**Note** - the <code>中</code> in this contrast is <b>なか</b>, the ordinary noun. It is a different
word from the suffix <code>中</code> read ちゅう/じゅう, which means "during" or "throughout" and
attaches with no `の`. {f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|中|なか}で = "within the meeting's content";
{f|会議|かいぎ}{f|中|ちゅう} = "the meeting is in progress".

</div>

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|机|つくえ}とベッドの<b>{f|間|あいだ}で</b>{f|本棚|ほんだな}があります。 → ✅ {f|机|つくえ}とベッドの<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>{f|本棚|ほんだな}があります。
  ある and いる state that something is *located* somewhere, and location in a gap takes
  `に`. `で` would need an action to host.

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>{f|人気|にんき}があります。 → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}で</b>{f|人気|にんき}があります。
  Popularity, rumour, trends and discussions circulate **across** a group, and that is
  the `で` use. This one is worth memorising as a set:
  <code>…の{f|間|あいだ}で{f|人気|にんき}がある</code>.

- ❌ {f|四人|よにん}の{f|間|あいだ}で{f|一番|いちばん}{f|若|わか}いのは{f|彼|かれ}です。 → ✅ {f|四人|よにん}の<b>{f|中|なか}</b>で{f|一番|いちばん}{f|若|わか}いのは{f|彼|かれ}です。
  Picking the extreme member of a set is a <code>{f|中|なか}</code> job.
  <code>{f|間|あいだ}</code> would be the space between them, where nobody stands.

- ❌ {f|駅|えき}と{f|郵便局|ゆうびんきょく}<b>{f|間|あいだ}</b>に → ✅ {f|駅|えき}と{f|郵便局|ゆうびんきょく}<b>の{f|間|あいだ}</b>に
  The `の` linking the pair to <code>{f|間|あいだ}</code> is not optional. Both edges go
  in front, joined by `と`, then `の`.

## Practice

**1.** コンビニと{f|銀行|ぎんこう}の{f|間|あいだ}____{f|細|ほそ}い{f|路地|ろじ}があります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

コンビニと{f|銀行|ぎんこう}の{f|間|あいだ}<b>に</b>{f|細|ほそ}い{f|路地|ろじ}があります。

あります reports that something exists in a place, and a place expressed as a gap takes
`に`. `で` would be looking for an action to locate, and existence is not an action.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|新|あたら}しいアプリが{f|高校生|こうこうせい}の（{f|間|あいだ}で・{f|中|なか}で）{f|急|きゅう}に{f|広|ひろ}まった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|高校生|こうこうせい}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}で</b>{f|急|きゅう}に{f|広|ひろ}まった。

Spreading is movement *across* a group rather than selection *from* it, so
<code>{f|間|あいだ}で</code> is the natural choice.
<code>{f|中|なか}で</code> is not impossible here, but it invites you to look for a
member being singled out, which is not what {f|広|ひろ}まる does.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "A small park has been built between the two schools."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|二|ふた}つの{f|学校|がっこう}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>{f|小|ちい}さな{f|公園|こうえん}ができました。

できる belongs with ある and {f|生|う}まれる - verbs of coming into existence - so the
gap takes `に`. You could also write
{f|学校|がっこう}と{f|学校|がっこう}の{f|間|あいだ}に, repeating the noun, which is
idiomatic when the two edges are the same kind of thing.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|五人|ごにん}の{f|間|あいだ}で{f|一番|いちばん}{f|上手|じょうず}に{f|歌|うた}えるのは{f|妹|いもうと}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|五人|ごにん}の<b>{f|中|なか}</b>で{f|一番|いちばん}{f|上手|じょうず}に{f|歌|うた}えるのは{f|妹|いもうと}です。

A superlative compares members inside a set, and the set is a container - that is
<code>{f|中|なか}</code>. <code>{f|間|あいだ}</code> names the space separating the five
people, which is not where the singing happens.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|二人|ふたり}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}に</b>{f|約束|やくそく}があった。
(b) {f|二人|ふたり}の<b>{f|間|あいだ}で</b>{f|約束|やくそく}をした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) presents the promise as something that simply existed between them - a standing
arrangement, perhaps unspoken, reported from outside. ある plus `に` gives it the
feel of a fact about their relationship.

(b) presents it as something the two of them actively did: they made the promise, with
each other, on some occasion. `で` supplies the arena for an action, so the sentence is
about an event rather than a state.

</details>
