# ～て初めて

**Meaning** · not until X did Y become clear - it took X for the penny to drop

<code>て{f|初|はじ}めて</code> is the grammar of hindsight. You did something, or something
happened to you, and only then did you understand, notice or become able to do
the thing in the second clause. The English that fits it best is almost always
"only after…" or "it wasn't until…".

## Formation

The pattern is simply the て-form followed by the adverb <code>{f|初|はじ}めて</code>. Word
order is doing all the work here, so guard it: the て-form comes **first**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (て-form) | て-form + {f|初|はじ}めて | {f|失|うしな}う → {f|失|うしな}って{f|初|はじ}めて |
| Verb + てみる | てみて + {f|初|はじ}めて | {f|使|つか}う → {f|使|つか}ってみて{f|初|はじ}めて |
| Noun (a new status) | Noun + になって + {f|初|はじ}めて | {f|親|おや} → {f|親|おや}になって{f|初|はじ}めて |
| Often rounded off with のだ | ～て{f|初|はじ}めて…のだ | {f|読|よ}んで{f|初|はじ}めてわかったのだ |
| ❌ た-form | ❌{f|失|うしな}った{f|初|はじ}めて | ✅{f|失|うしな}って{f|初|はじ}めて |
| ❌ Adverb before the verb | ❌{f|初|はじ}めて{f|失|うしな}って | ✅{f|失|うしな}って{f|初|はじ}めて |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the main clause has to be a **discovery**: わかる、{f|気|き}づく、
{f|知|し}る、できるようになる、{f|見|み}えてくる. If the second half is something you
decided to do, you want <code>てから</code>, not <code>て{f|初|はじ}めて</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|使|つか}ってみ<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、この{f|辞書|じしょ}のよさが{f|分|わ}かった。
It wasn't until I used it that I saw how good this dictionary is.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|病気|びょうき}になっ<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、{f|健康|けんこう}のありがたさが{f|分|わ}かった。
Only when I got ill did I appreciate being healthy.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|暮|く}らし<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、{f|家事|かじ}の{f|大変|たいへん}さに{f|気|き}づいた。
It took living on my own for me to realise how much work housework is.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|聞|き}い<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、この{f|機械|きかい}の{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}が{f|分|わ}かりました。
I only understood how to use this machine after hearing the explanation.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人|ひと}は{f|失|うしな}っ<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、その{f|大切|たいせつ}さに{f|気|き}づくものだ。
People never see the value of something until they've lost it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|地図|ちず}を{f|見|み}<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、{f|自分|じぶん}がどれだけ{f|遠|とお}くまで{f|歩|ある}いてきたのか{f|知|し}った。
Not until I looked at the map did I realise how far I'd walked.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|外国|がいこく}で{f|生活|せいかつ}してみ<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|国|くに}のことを{f|何|なに}も{f|知|し}らなかったのだと{f|気|き}づかされた。
Living abroad was what made me realise I knew nothing about my own country.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|親|おや}になっ<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、{f|父|ちち}がどうしてあんなに{f|厳|きび}しかったのか{f|理解|りかい}できるようになった。
It was only once I became a parent that I could understand why my father was so strict.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral in form - the ending decides politeness - but it
is **tonally** marked. It draws a lesson, so it turns up in essays, in speeches,
in the reflective part of a conversation, and in the kind of sentence that gets
quoted. Using it for something trivial is faintly comic:
{f|冷蔵庫|れいぞうこ}を{f|開|あ}けて{f|初|はじ}めて、{f|牛乳|ぎゅうにゅう}がないことに{f|気|き}づいた
is grammatical but sounds as though you are narrating your own life as a novel.

Three companions show up constantly. <code>てみて</code> in front, because trying
something is the classic route to a discovery. `のだ`/`んです` at the end, because
the sentence is explaining a state of affairs rather than reporting an event.
And <code>{f|本当|ほんとう}の</code> or <code>{f|本当|ほんとう}に</code> inside the main clause - 
{f|本当|ほんとう}の{f|意味|いみ}が{f|分|わ}かった - because the whole point is that a
shallower version of the understanding existed before.

Written Japanese sometimes reverses the halves for effect:
{f|人|ひと}は{f|気|き}づくのだ、{f|失|うしな}って{f|初|はじ}めて。 That is a stylistic
inversion, not a second pattern, and you will not need to produce it.

## Contrast

### ～て{f|初|はじ}めて vs ～てから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>てから</code> tells you the **order**. <code>て{f|初|はじ}めて</code>
adds a claim about everything before that point: until then, it had not happened
and *could* not have.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大学|だいがく}を{f|出|で}てから、{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|住|す}み{f|始|はじ}めました。
I started living alone after I finished university.
</pre>

That is a timeline. Now the same shape with the "not until" claim added:

<pre>
{f|大学|だいがく}を{f|出|で}<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、{f|親|おや}に{f|感謝|かんしゃ}する{f|気持|きも}ちが{f|生|う}まれた。
It was leaving university that first gave me any sense of gratitude towards my parents.
</pre>

The practical test: can you add "and not before"? If yes,
<code>て{f|初|はじ}めて</code>. If the sentence is just two things in order - and
especially if the second one is planned - it is <code>てから</code>.

### ～て{f|初|はじ}めて vs {f|初|はじ}めて (the adverb)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|初|はじ}めて</code> standing in front of a verb means "for the
first time" and describes the verb. <code>て{f|初|はじ}めて</code> sits **between** two
clauses and says the first one unlocked the second.

</div>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}<b>{f|初|はじ}めて</b>{f|京都|きょうと}へ{f|行|い}きました。
I went to Kyoto for the first time last year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|京都|きょうと}へ{f|行|い}っ<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、{f|日本|にほん}の{f|庭|にわ}の{f|美|うつく}しさが{f|分|わ}かりました。
It was going to Kyoto that showed me how beautiful Japanese gardens are.
</pre>

Both sentences contain the same three words; only the position of
<code>{f|初|はじ}めて</code> differs, and it changes what is being claimed. In the first,
the trip was a first. In the second, the trip is the cause and the understanding
is the first.

### ～て{f|初|はじ}めて vs ～ないと{f|分|わ}からない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>て{f|初|はじ}めて</code> reports something that **actually
happened**. <code>ないと</code>／<code>なければ</code> makes a hypothetical claim about what
would be true in general.

</div>

<pre>
やってみないと、{f|向|む}いているかどうか{f|分|わ}かりませんよ。
You won't know if it suits you unless you give it a go.
</pre>

That is advice, aimed at the future, and nothing has been experienced yet.
Compare the report of an experience already had:

<pre>
やってみ<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、{f|自分|じぶん}に{f|向|む}いていないと{f|分|わ}かりました。
Only after trying it did I work out that it wasn't for me.
</pre>

Because of that difference, <code>て{f|初|はじ}めて</code> almost never appears with a
future-tense main clause, while <code>ないと{f|分|わ}からない</code> almost always does.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|読|よ}んだ{f|初|はじ}めて、{f|作者|さくしゃ}の{f|気持|きも}ちが{f|分|わ}かった。 → ✅ {f|読|よ}んで{f|初|はじ}めて、{f|作者|さくしゃ}の{f|気持|きも}ちが{f|分|わ}かった。
  The verb takes the て-form, not た. The past meaning is already carried by the
  main clause.

- ❌ {f|日本|にほん}に{f|初|はじ}めて{f|来|き}て、{f|文化|ぶんか}の{f|違|ちが}いが{f|分|わ}かった。 → ✅ {f|日本|にほん}に{f|来|き}て{f|初|はじ}めて、{f|文化|ぶんか}の{f|違|ちが}いが{f|分|わ}かった。
  Put <code>{f|初|はじ}めて</code> before the verb and you have said "I came to Japan for
  the first time"; the "not until" reading is gone.

- ❌ {f|卒業|そつぎょう}して{f|初|はじ}めて、{f|働|はたら}こうと{f|思|おも}います。 → ✅ {f|卒業|そつぎょう}してから、{f|働|はたら}こうと{f|思|おも}います。
  The main clause is a plan, and this pattern needs a realisation. A plan takes
  <code>てから</code>.

- ❌ {f|話|はな}して{f|初|はじ}めて、{f|彼|かれ}に{f|電話|でんわ}してください。 → ✅ {f|話|はな}してから、{f|彼|かれ}に{f|電話|でんわ}してください。
  Requests and instructions are impossible here for the same reason: you cannot
  ask somebody to have a realisation.

## Practice

**1.** {f|自分|じぶん}で{f|料理|りょうり}を{f|作|つく}っ____、{f|母|はは}の{f|大変|たいへん}さが{f|分|わ}かった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|料理|りょうり}を{f|作|つく}っ<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、{f|母|はは}の{f|大変|たいへん}さが{f|分|わ}かった。

The second clause is a realisation, and the first is what triggered it - the
signature shape of this pattern. The verb sits in the て-form.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|入院|にゅういん}し（て{f|初|はじ}めて・てから）、{f|家族|かぞく}のありがたさが{f|分|わ}かった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|入院|にゅういん}し<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、{f|家族|かぞく}のありがたさが{f|分|わ}かった。

<code>てから</code> would say only that the gratitude came later in time.
<code>て{f|初|はじ}めて</code> adds the point of the sentence: before the hospital stay,
the speaker had never felt it at all.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "It wasn't until I lost my phone that I realised how much I depended on it."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|携帯|けいたい}をなくし<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、どれだけそれに{f|頼|たよ}っていたかが{f|分|わ}かった。

"It wasn't until X" maps almost mechanically onto
<code>Xて{f|初|はじ}めて</code>, and the realisation verb goes in the main clause in the
past tense.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|初|はじ}めて{f|海外|かいがい}で{f|働|はたら}いて、{f|言葉|ことば}の{f|壁|かべ}の{f|高|たか}さを{f|知|し}りました。（intended: "only after working abroad did I learn how high the language barrier is"）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|海外|かいがい}で{f|働|はたら}いて<b>{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、{f|言葉|ことば}の{f|壁|かべ}の{f|高|たか}さを{f|知|し}りました。

As written, the sentence says the speaker worked abroad for the first time and
then learned something. Moving <code>{f|初|はじ}めて</code> behind the て-form restores
the intended "not until" meaning.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|大|おお}ぜいの{f|前|まえ}で{f|話|はな}し<b>て{f|初|はじ}めて</b>、{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|発音|はつおん}の{f|問題|もんだい}に{f|気|き}づいた。
(b) {f|社会|しゃかい}に{f|出|で}てから、{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|発音|はつおん}の{f|問題|もんだい}に{f|気|き}づいた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) makes that one occasion of speaking in front of a crowd the trigger: nothing
before it had revealed the problem, and the discovery is presented as sudden and
slightly humbling.

(b) simply places the discovery somewhere after the speaker entered working life.
It could have taken years, it could have come from anywhere, and no single event
is being credited.

</details>
