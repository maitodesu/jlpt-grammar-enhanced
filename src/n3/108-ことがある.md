# ～ことがある

**Meaning** · there are times when it happens - occasionally, not as a rule

<code>ことがある</code> takes a situation and says that instances of it *exist*.
Literally "there are occasions of ～". The English that fits is "sometimes", "now and
then", "it can happen that" - and the sentence always implies the opposite is the
norm. If something happens every single day, this is the wrong pattern: the whole
point of <code>ことがある</code> is that the thing is an exception.

Two neighbours share the shape and mean something else entirely. <code>たことがある</code>
with a past verb is *experience* - "I have done it before" - and
<code>ことができる</code> is *ability*. Both are covered earlier in the book; this page
is only about the dictionary form.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + ことがある | {f|遅|おく}れる → {f|遅|おく}れることがある |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + ことがある | {f|来|こ}ない → {f|来|こ}ないことがある |
| い-adjective | plain form + ことがある | {f|寒|さむ}い → {f|寒|さむ}いことがある |
| な-adjective | な + ことがある | {f|不便|ふべん} → {f|不便|ふべん}なことがある |
| Noun | recast with なる or だ | {f|中止|ちゅうし} → {f|中止|ちゅうし}になることがある |
| Past ("there used to be times") | dictionary form + ことがあった | {f|泣|な}く → {f|泣|な}くことがあった |
| Polite | ことがあります | {f|迷|まよ}うことがあります |
| Softened | ことも + ある | {f|一人|ひとり}で{f|行|い}くこともある |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the frequency adverbs that go with this are
<code>ときどき</code>, `たまに`, `まれに`, <code>{f|年|ねん}に{f|数回|すうかい}</code>.
They make explicit what the pattern only implies. Adverbs of *constancy* - 
<code>いつも</code>, <code>{f|毎日|まいにち}</code>, <code>{f|必|かなら}ず</code> - 
contradict it and produce a broken sentence.

</div>

<pre>

</pre>

</pre>

</pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral and conjugates like any `ある` sentence:
<code>ことがある</code> plain, <code>ことがあります</code> polite,
<code>ことがございます</code> in service language. Nothing about choosing it makes you
sound formal or casual.

Where it *is* strongly marked is by **genre**. Instructions, warnings and manuals live
on it, because it is the natural way to state a possible but non-routine outcome
without alarming anyone:
<code>まれに{f|故障|こしょう}することがあります</code> on an appliance leaflet,
<code>{f|副作用|ふくさよう}が{f|出|で}ることがあります</code> on a medicine insert. If
you read Japanese product documentation you will meet it constantly.

In conversation it does a quieter job: it hedges. Answering
<code>{f|自炊|じすい}しますか</code> with <code>することがあります</code> concedes that
you do, sometimes, while refusing to claim a habit. Swapping `が` for `も` - 
<code>することもあります</code> - softens it further and adds an explicit "and other
times I don't", which is why `も` is so common when you are being modest or
non-committal about your own behaviour.

One structural point that trips people up: `ある` here is a genuine verb of existence
and `こと` is its subject. That is why the particle on `こと` can change - 
<code>ことも</code> for "there are also times when", <code>ことは</code> when the phrase
is negated or contrasted - and why frequency adverbs such as `めったに` and `ほとんど`
attach to `ある` rather than to the main verb:
<code>まちがえることはめったにない</code>, "I hardly ever get it wrong." Keep in mind
that <code>～ことはない</code> is also a separate pattern meaning "there's no need
to ～"; the frequency adverb is usually what tells you which one you are looking at.

## Contrast

### ～ことがある vs ～たことがある

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - dictionary form = **sometimes it happens**, an ongoing pattern.
た-form = **I have done it**, an experience in your past. The tense of the verb before
`こと` carries the entire difference.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|夜|よる}まで<b>{f|続|つづ}くことがある</b>。
Meetings sometimes run into the evening.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|夜|よる}まで<b>{f|続|つづ}いたことがある</b>。
There was a time when a meeting ran into the evening.
</pre>

The first is a standing fact about how meetings go here; it says nothing about whether
one has actually run late this month. The second reports that at least one such
meeting has occurred and stops there. Because they answer different questions, the
adverbs differ too: `ときどき` fits the first, <code>{f|一度|いちど}だけ</code> the
second.

### ～ことがある vs a bare adverb

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ときどき{f|遅|おく}れる</code> says the delays are part of the
picture. <code>{f|遅|おく}れることがある</code> says they are the exception, and often
carries a warning or an excuse with it.

</div>

<pre>
このバスは<b>ときどき{f|遅|おく}れます</b>。
This bus runs late from time to time.
</pre>

<pre>
このバスは<b>{f|遅|おく}れることがあります</b>。
This bus is sometimes late. (so leave yourself a margin)
</pre>

Both are true of the same bus timetable, but the second frames lateness as a
possibility you should be aware of rather than a property of the route. That framing
is why the `ことがある` version is what a station announcement or a guidebook uses. The
two can of course stack - <code>ときどき{f|遅|おく}れることがあります</code> - and this
is common; the adverb pins down how often, the pattern keeps the "not normally" tone.

### ～ことがある vs ～かもしれない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ことがある</code> reports what **does** happen, from
experience. <code>かもしれない</code> guesses about what **may** happen, on this
occasion.

</div>

<pre>
{f|冬|ふゆ}は{f|水道|すいどう}が<b>{f|凍|こお}ることがある</b>。
The pipes do freeze here in winter.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今夜|こんや}は{f|水道|すいどう}が<b>{f|凍|こお}るかもしれない</b>。
The pipes might freeze tonight.
</pre>

`ことがある` is a claim about a track record, so it needs more than one occasion behind
it and cannot be used about a single future event: ❌<code>{f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ることがある</code> is wrong for "it might rain
tomorrow." Conversely
`かもしれない` cannot summarise a pattern - it always leaves the outcome open, so it is
useless for explaining what winters are like around here.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|残業|ざんぎょう}することがあります。 → ✅ ときどき<b>{f|残業|ざんぎょう}することがあります</b>。
  "Every day" and "sometimes" cannot share a sentence. Either drop
  <code>ことがある</code> and say <code>{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|残業|ざんぎょう}します</code>,
  or keep the pattern and use an adverb that matches it.

- ❌ {f|富士山|ふじさん}に{f|登|のぼ}ることがあります。（meaning "I have climbed Fuji"）→ ✅ {f|富士山|ふじさん}に<b>{f|登|のぼ}ったことがあります</b>。
  Experience needs the た-form. As written, the sentence claims you climb Fuji from
  time to time, which is a very different life.

- ❌ {f|日本語|にほんご}を{f|話|はな}すことがあります。（meaning "I can speak Japanese"）→ ✅ {f|日本語|にほんご}を<b>{f|話|はな}すことができます</b>。
  Ability is <code>ことができる</code>. <code>ことがある</code> never means "can";
  the two are separated only by the verb at the end.

- ❌ {f|寒|さむ}いなことがある。 → ✅ <b>{f|寒|さむ}いことがある</b>。
  Only な-adjectives take `な` before `こと`. い-adjectives attach in their plain form,
  exactly as they do before any other noun.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|辺|へん}は{f|夏|なつ}でも{f|夜|よる}は____ことがあります。（{f|涼|すず}しい）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|辺|へん}は{f|夏|なつ}でも{f|夜|よる}は<b>{f|涼|すず}しいことがあります</b>。

い-adjectives go in front of `こと` unchanged - no `な`, no `の`. The sentence works
because coolness in summer is presented as an occasional exception, which is what
<code>{f|夏|なつ}でも</code> has already set up.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|海外|かいがい}のホテルではお{f|湯|ゆ}が{f|出|で}ない（ことがある・ことがあった）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|海外|かいがい}のホテルではお{f|湯|ゆ}が{f|出|で}ない<b>ことがある</b>。

The sentence is a general warning about hotels abroad, valid now, so the non-past
`ある` is right. <code>ことがあった</code> would narrow it to something that used to
happen, or to a specific stay in the past - and a warning about a trip already over
helps nobody.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The printer sometimes stops working, so please check it before the meeting."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

プリンターが<b>{f|動|うご}かなくなることがある</b>ので、{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|前|まえ}に{f|確認|かくにん}してください。

The negative goes on the main verb - <code>{f|動|うご}かなくなる</code> - and
<code>ことがある</code> then wraps the whole situation. This is the classic
instruction-manual shape: report the occasional failure, then say what to do about it.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|去年|きょねん}、{f|一度|いちど}だけ{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|遅刻|ちこく}することがあります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|去年|きょねん}、{f|一度|いちど}だけ{f|会社|かいしゃ}に<b>{f|遅刻|ちこく}したことがあります</b>。

<code>{f|一度|いちど}だけ</code> plus a past year describes an experience, not a
recurring pattern, so the verb before `こと` has to be in the た-form. Left as it
stands, the sentence says you are occasionally late - and dates it to last year, which
makes no sense.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まる<b>ことがあります</b>。
(b) {f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まる<b>かもしれません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a statement about the line's history: stoppages happen here from time to time.
It is the sentence for explaining the route to someone new, and it says nothing at all
about today.

(b) is a prediction about the situation at hand - the wind is picking up, so the
trains may well stop. It carries no claim that this has ever happened before, and it
would be strange as a general description of the line.

</details>
