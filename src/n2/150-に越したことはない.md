# ～に越したことはない

**Meaning** · you can't do better than ～; ～ is the ideal, other things being equal

Literally "there is nothing that surpasses ～". The pattern states a mild general
preference, not an instruction: more of this is better, and if you can arrange it you
should. Because it is a preference rather than a requirement, it very often has a
<code>が</code> or <code>けれど</code> hanging off the end, admitting that life may not
allow it.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + に{f|越|こ}したことはない | {f|確|たし}かめる → {f|確|たし}かめるに{f|越|こ}したことはない |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + に{f|越|こ}したことはない | {f|無理|むり}をしない → {f|無理|むり}をしないに{f|越|こ}したことはない |
| い-adjective | plain form + に{f|越|こ}したことはない | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}いに{f|越|こ}したことはない |
| な-adjective | ～である + に{f|越|こ}したことはない | {f|丈夫|じょうぶ}である → {f|丈夫|じょうぶ}であるに{f|越|こ}したことはない |
| Noun | Noun + に{f|越|こ}したことはない | {f|健康|けんこう} → {f|健康|けんこう}に{f|越|こ}したことはない |
| Polite | … + に{f|越|こ}したことはありません | {f|早|はや}い → {f|早|はや}いに{f|越|こ}したことはありません |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the verb is <code>{f|越|こ}す</code>, "to surpass", not
<code>{f|越|こ}える</code>. The whole tail is frozen:
<code>{f|越|こ}したことはない</code>. Changing the <code>は</code> to
<code>が</code> or the <code>ない</code> to <code>ありません</code> mid-phrase breaks it.

</div>

## Nuance & register

The pattern is calm, sensible and slightly proverbial. It is the voice of prudence:
insurance, deadlines, spare batteries, arriving early. It appears in both speech and
writing and is neutral in politeness -
<code>に{f|越|こ}したことはありません</code> is completely at home in a business email.

Two things make it distinctive. First, it is **not an obligation**. It says the option is
preferable, not that it is required, so it can be used precisely to avoid pushing:
telling a colleague <code>{f|確認|かくにん}しておくに{f|越|こ}したことはない</code>
suggests without ordering. That softness is a genuine reason to reach for it.

Second, it **accepts that the ideal may be out of reach**. This is why the
<code>が</code> continuation is so common: <code>{f|安|やす}いに{f|越|こ}したことはないが、{f|質|しつ}も{f|大事|だいじ}だ</code>.
A speaker who says only the first half has already implied the second.

It does not sound old-fashioned, and it is not marked for age or gender. It is a little
more considered than everyday chat - <code>{f|早|はや}いほうがいいよ</code> is what you
say to a friend running late - but nobody would call it stiff.

## Contrast

### に{f|越|こ}したことはない vs に{f|限|かぎ}る

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|限|かぎ}る</code> crowns **one winner** and closes the
discussion. <code>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</code> ranks an option **at the top of a
scale** and leaves the rest of the scale in place.

</div>

<pre>
{f|夏|なつ}の{f|昼|ひる}ごはんは、そうめん<b>に{f|限|かぎ}る</b>。
For lunch in summer it has to be sōmen.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昼|ひる}ごはんは{f|軽|かる}い<b>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</b>が、{f|午後|ごご}まで{f|持|も}たないと{f|困|こま}る。
A light lunch is ideal, though it's no good if it doesn't see you through the afternoon.
</pre>

Three practical differences follow. <code>に{f|限|かぎ}る</code> takes only nouns and
verbs; <code>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</code> takes adjectives too. The first is a
personal declaration, the second a piece of general prudence. And only the second invites
a qualification.

### に{f|越|こ}したことはない vs ～ほうがいい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ほうがいい</code> recommends **to a person**.
<code>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</code> observes that something **is generally
preferable**, with nobody in particular addressed.

</div>

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っていった<b>ほうがいいですよ</b>。
You'd better take an umbrella.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|折|お}りたたみ{f|傘|かさ}は{f|一本|いっぽん}かばんに{f|入|い}れておく<b>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</b>。
Keeping a folding umbrella in your bag is never a bad idea.
</pre>

The first is aimed at the person standing in front of you and can, said flatly, sound
like an instruction. The second is a maxim, so it applies the pressure of common sense
rather than of the speaker. When you want to nudge a superior, the second is much safer.

### に{f|越|こ}したことはない vs なければならない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</code> is **desirable**.
<code>なければならない</code> is **required**. They are not two strengths of the same
thing; one of them permits the alternative and the other does not.

</div>

<pre>
{f|書類|しょるい}は{f|前日|ぜんじつ}までに{f|出|だ}す<b>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</b>。
It's best to hand the documents in by the day before.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|書類|しょるい}は{f|前日|ぜんじつ}までに{f|出|だ}さ<b>なければなりません</b>。
The documents must be handed in by the day before.
</pre>

If missing the date has consequences, only the second is honest. Using
<code>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</code> for a hard rule is a real workplace hazard: the
listener will hear "if you get round to it".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|健康|けんこう}<b>だに{f|越|こ}したことはない</b>。 → ✅ {f|健康|けんこう}<b>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</b>。／{f|健康|けんこう}<b>であるに{f|越|こ}したことはない</b>。
  A noun attaches bare or with <code>である</code>. The plain <code>だ</code> cannot
  sit in front of <code>に</code>.

- ❌ {f|安|やす}い<b>に{f|越|こ}したことがない</b>。 → ✅ {f|安|やす}い<b>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</b>。
  The <code>は</code> is part of the fixed phrase. Swapping in <code>が</code> is a
  very common slip and changes it into something that means nothing.

- ❌ {f|早|はや}めに{f|出|で}る<b>に{f|越|こ}えたことはない</b>。 → ✅ {f|早|はや}めに{f|出|で}る<b>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</b>。
  The verb is <code>{f|越|こ}す</code>. <code>{f|越|こ}える</code> is a different verb
  and does not appear in this phrase.

- ❌ パスポートは{f|必|かなら}ず{f|持|も}っていく<b>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</b>。 → ✅ パスポートは{f|必|かなら}ず{f|持|も}っていか<b>なければならない</b>。
  <code>{f|必|かなら}ず</code> announces a requirement, and this pattern only ever
  expresses a preference. The two contradict each other in the same sentence.

## Practice

**1.** {f|旅行|りょこう}の{f|保険|ほけん}は{f|使|つか}わないかもしれないが、{f|入|はい}っておく____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|入|はい}っておく<b>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</b>。

A verb attaches in the dictionary form, and <code>～ておく</code> fits the pattern's
precautionary flavour perfectly. The first half already concedes the insurance may be
wasted, which is exactly the hedged setting this pattern lives in.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|会場|かいじょう}には{f|開演|かいえん}の{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}{f|前|まえ}までに{f|入|はい}ら（なければなりません・に{f|越|こ}したことはありません）。{f|遅|おく}れた{f|方|かた}はご{f|入場|にゅうじょう}いただけません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|開演|かいえん}の{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}{f|前|まえ}までに{f|入|はい}ら<b>なければなりません</b>。

The second sentence spells out a consequence for being late, so this is a rule, not a
preference. <code>に{f|越|こ}したことはありません</code> would tell the audience the
deadline is optional and then punish them for believing it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "A bag is better light than heavy, but you don't want to leave the charger behind."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

かばんは{f|軽|かる}い<b>に{f|越|こ}したことはない</b>が、{f|充電器|じゅうでんき}を{f|置|お}いていくのは{f|困|こま}る。

An い-adjective attaches with no change, and the <code>が</code> clause is the natural
continuation this pattern expects. English "better… but" and the Japanese shape line up
almost word for word.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部屋|へや}は{f|静|しず}かだに{f|越|こ}したことはない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部屋|へや}は<b>{f|静|しず}かであるに{f|越|こ}したことはない</b>。

な-adjectives take <code>である</code> here. The plain <code>だ</code> is a finished
predicate and cannot be followed by <code>に</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|説明|せつめい}は{f|短|みじか}いに{f|越|こ}したことはない。
(b) {f|説明|せつめい}は{f|短|みじか}いに{f|限|かぎ}る。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a general principle offered with a shrug: shorter is better, all else equal, and
the speaker would accept a longer explanation if there were a reason for it.

(b) is a verdict with the speaker's personality in it - brevity wins, end of discussion.
It is also grammatically strained, because <code>に{f|限|かぎ}る</code> does not sit
comfortably after an い-adjective, which is one more reason (a) is what you would
actually write.

</details>
