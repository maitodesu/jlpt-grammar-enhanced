# 以上 - or more

**Meaning** · that much or more; upwards of; at least - read <code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code>, and note that the stated figure is **included**

Stuck on the end of a quantity, <code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> draws a line and
points upward from it. <code>{f|十八歳|じゅうはっさい}{f|以上|いじょう}</code> means
eighteen and everyone older - an eighteen-year-old qualifies. That inclusiveness is
not a detail: it is the whole reason application forms, contracts and exam rules
use this word instead of a vaguer one.

## Formation

<code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> is a suffix. It attaches directly to whatever
expresses the amount, with no particle in between.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Number + counter | quantity + {f|以上|いじょう} | {f|三回|さんかい} → {f|三回|さんかい}{f|以上|いじょう} |
| Bare number | number + {f|以上|いじょう} | 100 → 100{f|以上|いじょう} |
| Noun naming a level | noun + {f|以上|いじょう} | {f|高校生|こうこうせい} → {f|高校生|こうこうせい}{f|以上|いじょう} |
| Demonstrative | これ・それ + {f|以上|いじょう} | これ → これ{f|以上|いじょう} |
| Before a noun | {f|以上|いじょう}の + noun | {f|予想|よそう} → {f|予想|よそう}{f|以上|いじょう}の{f|人出|ひとで} |
| Before a predicate | {f|以上|いじょう}に + predicate | {f|想像|そうぞう} → {f|想像|そうぞう}{f|以上|いじょう}に{f|難|むずか}しい |
| Closing a statement | {f|以上|いじょう}です | - → {f|以上|いじょう}です |

<div class="note">

**Note** - keep three lookalikes apart.
<code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> = that figure and up (inclusive).
<code>{f|以下|いか}</code> = that figure and down (also inclusive).
<code>{f|以内|いない}</code> = within a limit, counting from zero -
<code>{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}{f|以内|いない}</code>, "inside thirty minutes".
<code>{f|以外|いがい}</code> is a different word altogether: "other than".

</div>

## Nuance & register

<code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> is precise, neutral vocabulary. It belongs to
notices, forms, contracts, statistics and exam rules, and it is equally at home in
ordinary conversation once a number is involved -
<code>{f|二時間|にじかん}{f|以上|いじょう}{f|待|ま}った</code> is what anybody would
say. There is nothing formal about the word itself; the formality of the sentences
you meet it in comes from their subject matter.

The one thing to hold onto is the boundary. Japanese draws the inclusive/exclusive
line lexically where English mumbles it: "over 20" in English is genuinely
ambiguous, whereas <code>20{f|以上|いじょう}</code> definitely includes 20 and
<code>20を{f|超|こ}える</code> definitely does not. In a legal or numerical context
that distinction is load-bearing, and getting it backwards in a job application or
a specification is a real error, not a stylistic one.

Two idiomatic uses are worth learning as units.
<code>これ{f|以上|いじょう}</code> - "any more than this" - is extremely common in
speech and almost always runs into a negative or a limit:
<code>これ{f|以上|いじょう}は{f|無理|むり}</code>,
<code>これ{f|以上|いじょう}{f|言|い}わないで</code>. And
<code>{f|以上|いじょう}です</code> is the standard full stop at the end of a
presentation, an announcement or a written list - the spoken equivalent of drawing
a line under the page. Ending a report with it is polite and expected, not curt.

## Contrast

### {f|以上|いじょう} vs {f|以下|いか}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - mirror images, and **both include the number**.
<code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> counts upward from it,
<code>{f|以下|いか}</code> counts downward.

</div>

<pre>
{f|小学生|しょうがくせい}<b>{f|以下|いか}</b>は{f|無料|むりょう}です。
Primary-school children and younger get in free.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|中学生|ちゅうがくせい}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>は{f|料金|りょうきん}がかかります。
Middle-school age and above pay the fare.
</pre>

Read together, the pair tiles the whole range with no gap and no overlap - which is
exactly how price lists are written. <code>{f|以下|いか}</code> has a second,
non-numerical life meaning "the following": <code>{f|以下|いか}のとおり</code>,
"as below". <code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> mirrors that too, in
<code>{f|以上|いじょう}のように</code>, "as above".

### {f|以上|いじょう} vs ～を{f|超|こ}える・{f|未満|みまん}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> and
<code>{f|以下|いか}</code> include the boundary;
<code>を{f|超|こ}える</code> and <code>{f|未満|みまん}</code> exclude it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|体重|たいじゅう}が50キロ<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>の{f|人|ひと}。
People weighing 50 kg or more (50 counts).
</pre>

<pre>
{f|体重|たいじゅう}が50キロを<b>{f|超|こ}える</b>{f|人|ひと}。
People weighing more than 50 kg (50 does not count).
</pre>

<code>{f|未満|みまん}</code> is the exclusive partner at the bottom end:
<code>{f|十八歳|じゅうはっさい}{f|未満|みまん}</code> is seventeen and younger, and
does *not* include eighteen. Notice that these four words carve up a number line
between them, and that Japanese forms rely on you knowing which is which.

### {f|以上|いじょう} (quantity) vs {f|以上|いじょう} (now that)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - look at what comes **before** it. A number or a level-noun
means "or more". A whole clause - a verb, an adjective, ～である - means "now
that; given that".

</div>

<pre>
{f|三年|さんねん}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>{f|日本|にほん}に{f|住|す}んでいます。
I've been living in Japan for three years or more.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日本|にほん}に{f|住|す}んでいる<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>、{f|税金|ぜいきん}は{f|払|はら}わなければならない。
As long as I'm living in Japan, I have to pay the taxes.
</pre>

Same two kanji, unrelated jobs. The first is arithmetic; the second is a reason
clause meaning roughly the same as `からには`. In practice the front of the phrase
tells you instantly which one you are reading, and there is no sentence where both
are plausible.

## Common mistakes

- ❌「20{f|歳|さい}{f|以上|いじょう}」は21{f|歳|さい}から。 → ✅「20{f|歳|さい}{f|以上|いじょう}」は20{f|歳|さい}から。
  The boundary is included. If you need to exclude it, write
  <code>20{f|歳|さい}を{f|超|こ}える</code>.

- ❌ {f|以上|いじょう}{f|三年|さんねん}の{f|経験|けいけん} → ✅ {f|三年|さんねん}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>の{f|経験|けいけん}
  English puts "more than" in front of the number; Japanese puts
  <code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> after it. This is one of the most common word-order
  slips at N2.

- ❌ {f|一時間|いちじかん}<b>の{f|以上|いじょう}</b>{f|待|ま}った → ✅ {f|一時間|いちじかん}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>{f|待|ま}った
  No particle comes between the quantity and the suffix. The の only appears on the
  other side, when the phrase modifies a following noun.

- ❌ これ{f|以上|いじょう}{f|頑張|がんば}ります。 → ✅ これ{f|以上|いじょう}は{f|頑張|がんば}れません。
  <code>これ{f|以上|いじょう}</code> marks a ceiling, so it runs into a negative or
  a limit. For "I'll try even harder" say
  <code>もっと{f|頑張|がんば}ります</code>.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|映画|えいが}は15{f|歳|さい}____の{f|方|かた}しか{f|見|み}られません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

15{f|歳|さい}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>の{f|方|かた}

The rating sets a floor and lets everyone at or above it through, which is exactly
what <code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> does. Fifteen-year-olds are admitted.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|参加費|さんかひ}は、{f|中学生|ちゅうがくせい}（{f|以上|いじょう}・{f|未満|みまん}）は{f|無料|むりょう}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|中学生|ちゅうがくせい}<b>{f|未満|みまん}</b>は{f|無料|むりょう}です。

Free admission for the youngest visitors means everyone *below* middle-school age,
and <code>{f|未満|みまん}</code> is the exclusive "under". With
<code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> you would be giving free entry to the oldest group
instead.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Applications are open to anyone with two or more years of experience."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|経験|けいけん}が{f|二年|にねん}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>ある{f|方|かた}なら、どなたでも{f|応募|おうぼ}できます。

<code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> goes after the quantity, and because two years
themselves qualify, an applicant with exactly two years is eligible.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|以上|いじょう}20キロだと、{f|追加|ついか}{f|料金|りょうきん}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|荷物|にもつ}が20キロ<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>だと、{f|追加|ついか}{f|料金|りょうきん}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。

The suffix follows the quantity, never precedes it. Airlines would in fact write
<code>20キロを{f|超|こ}える{f|場合|ばあい}</code>, since a bag of exactly 20 kg is
usually still free - a good reminder that the boundary matters.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|一時間|いちじかん}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>かかりました。
(b) {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|一時間|いちじかん}<b>{f|以内|いない}</b>で{f|終|お}わりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) sets a floor: the meeting ran to an hour at minimum and probably longer. It
often carries a note of complaint, because the speaker is measuring how much time
was lost.

(b) sets a ceiling: the whole thing fitted inside an hour.
<code>{f|以内|いない}</code> measures a span from zero up to a limit, so it is the
natural word for deadlines and time budgets, and here it sounds faintly pleased.

</details>
