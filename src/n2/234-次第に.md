# 次第に

**Meaning** · gradually; by degrees - a change that advances steadily in one
direction

<code>{f|次第|しだい}に</code> (しだいに) describes a process, not a moment. Something
is moving from one state towards another, and each stage is a little further along
than the last.

## Usage

An adverb. It goes in front of the **verb or adjective that carries the change**,
and that word has to express change: なる, {f|変|か}わる, {f|増|ふ}える,
{f|減|へ}る, {f|慣|な}れる, {f|近|ちか}づく, {f|消|き}える, {f|強|つよ}まる.

- {f|次第|しだい}に{f|暗|くら}くなる
- {f|次第|しだい}に{f|数|かず}が{f|減|へ}る

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `次第に` needs a **process with a direction**. A single
completed event has no degrees for it to describe.

</div>

It pairs naturally with the aspect helpers that stretch a change over time:

- <code>{f|次第|しだい}に…ていく</code> - the change moves away from now into the future
- <code>{f|次第|しだい}に…てくる</code> - the change has been approaching up to now

Watch the homograph. The same {f|次第|しだい} appears in two unrelated N2 patterns - 
<code>～{f|次第|しだい}</code> "as soon as" ({f|着|つ}き{f|次第|しだい}{f|連絡|れんらく}します)
and <code>～{f|次第|しだい}だ</code> "depends on" ({f|天気|てんき}{f|次第|しだい}だ). Only
the form with `に` after it is the adverb meaning "gradually", and it never
attaches to anything in front of it.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|空|そら}が<b>{f|次第|しだい}に</b>{f|暗|くら}くなってきた。
The sky gradually grew darker.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|薬|くすり}が{f|効|き}いて、{f|痛|いた}みは<b>{f|次第|しだい}に</b>{f|消|き}えていった。
The medicine took effect and the pain faded away little by little.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|最初|さいしょ}は{f|不安|ふあん}だったが、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|職場|しょくば}にも<b>{f|次第|しだい}に</b>{f|慣|な}れてきた。
I was nervous at first, but I've gradually settled in at the new workplace.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人口|じんこう}の{f|減少|げんしょう}にともなって、この{f|通|とお}りから<b>{f|次第|しだい}に</b>{f|店|みせ}が{f|姿|すがた}を{f|消|け}していった。
As the population fell, the shops along this street disappeared one after another.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`次第に` leans **written**. It is the word of narration, news reports, weather
forecasts and essays: {f|雨|あめ}は{f|次第|しだい}に{f|弱|よわ}まるでしょう. In
conversation it is not wrong, but it sounds composed - a speaker choosing their
words rather than chatting.

It also prefers changes that **happen** over changes you **perform**. Something
gets dark, gets used to, gets fewer. When the subject is deliberately doing
something in stages, <code>{f|少|すこ}しずつ</code> or
<code>{f|徐々|じょじょ}に</code> sits more comfortably:
<code>{f|少|すこ}しずつ{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|運|はこ}んだ</code>. This is a tendency, not a
prohibition - <code>{f|徐々|じょじょ}に</code> is the close formal neighbour and
overlaps with `次第に` across most of its range.

The change it describes is also **slow enough to notice in stages**. For a jump
you want <code>{f|急|きゅう}に</code> or <code>{f|一気|いっき}に</code>; `次第に`
promises the listener a gradient.

## Contrast

### {f|次第|しだい}に vs だんだん

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different clothes. `だんだん` is the spoken
form, <code>{f|次第|しだい}に</code> the written one.

</div>

<pre>
{f|日|ひ}が{f|長|なが}くなって、<b>だんだん</b>{f|春|はる}らしくなってきたね。
The days are getting longer - it's starting to feel like spring.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三月|さんがつ}に{f|入|はい}ると、{f|気温|きおん}は<b>{f|次第|しだい}に</b>{f|上昇|じょうしょう}する。
From March onwards the temperature rises steadily.
</pre>

Put `だんだん` in the second sentence and the forecast sounds like a friend
guessing. Put `次第に` in the first and the small talk turns into a bulletin.

`だんだん` also doubles as an everyday intensifier in speech - だんだん{f|眠|ねむ}く
なってきた - where the speaker is simply reporting a feeling. `次第に` keeps a
certain distance from the change, which is why it suits observation and report.

A third neighbour, `どんどん`, is not a synonym: it means the change is
**fast and gathering pace**, the opposite of the measured gradient `次第に`
describes.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|電気|でんき}が<b>{f|次第|しだい}に</b>{f|消|き}えた。 → ✅ {f|電気|でんき}が<b>{f|急|きゅう}に</b>{f|消|き}えた。
  A light going out is instantaneous. `次第に` needs something that can be half
  done.

- ❌ {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わり<b>{f|次第|しだい}に</b>{f|連絡|れんらく}します。 → ✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わり<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>{f|連絡|れんらく}します。
  "As soon as" is <code>～{f|次第|しだい}</code> with no `に`. Adding the `に` here
  produces the adverb, and the sentence stops making sense.

- ❌ {f|次第|しだい}に{f|三|さん}キロ{f|痩|や}せた。 → ✅ {f|次第|しだい}に{f|体重|たいじゅう}が{f|減|へ}っていった。
  A finished total is a result, not a gradient. Pair `次第に` with the process, and
  let ～ていく carry it forward.

## Practice

**1.** {f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|続|つづ}けているうちに、____{f|自信|じしん}がついてきた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|次第|しだい}に</b>{f|自信|じしん}がついてきた。

Confidence builds by degrees, and <code>ついてきた</code> already frames it as a
change arriving up to the present - exactly the aspect `次第に` likes. In casual
speech `だんだん` would be the more natural choice here.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: {f|準備|じゅんび}ができ{f|次第|しだい}に、{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}しましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|準備|じゅんび}ができ<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}しましょう。

This is the "as soon as" pattern attached to a verb stem, which takes no `に`. The
adverb <code>{f|次第|しだい}に</code> is a free-standing word and cannot follow
<code>でき</code>.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|町|まち}の{f|様子|ようす}は{f|次第|しだい}に{f|変|か}わっていった。
(b) {f|町|まち}の{f|様子|ようす}はどんどん{f|変|か}わっていった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both describe change over time, but at different speeds and from different
distances. (a) is measured and observational - the kind of line that opens a
documentary. (b) is faster and more involved: the changes came thick and fast, and
the speaker sounds caught up in them.

</details>
