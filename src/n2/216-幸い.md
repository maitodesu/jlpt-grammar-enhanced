# 幸い

**Meaning** · fortunately - and, in business writing, "I would be grateful if…"

Read <code>{f|幸|さいわ}い</code>. As an adverb it reports relief: something bad
happened, and the damage stopped short of what it might have been. As a noun in a
fixed email formula it does something else entirely, and both are worth learning
together.

## Usage

**As an adverb.** <code>{f|幸|さいわ}い</code>,
<code>{f|幸|さいわ}いに</code> and <code>{f|幸|さいわ}いにも</code> all work, usually
at the head of the clause and often followed by a comma.
<code>{f|幸|さいわ}いにも</code> is the most emphatic and the most written. The
noun-modifying shape is <code>{f|幸|さいわ}いなことに</code>.

The typical sentence has two halves - a setback, then the limit on it:

- {f|事故|じこ}があったが、{f|幸|さいわ}いけが{f|人|にん}はいなかった。
- {f|道|みち}に{f|迷|まよ}ったが、{f|幸|さいわ}い{f|近|ちか}くに{f|交番|こうばん}があった。

That background is what the word needs. It is not a general "how nice" - it is
relief that something worse did not happen.

**As a noun or な-adjective.** <code>{f|不幸中|ふこうちゅう}の{f|幸|さいわ}い</code>
("a small mercy in the middle of a disaster") is a set phrase. And in written
requests:

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～いただければ{f|幸|さいわ}いです</code> is a fixed polite
request, "I would be grateful if you could…". Here
<code>{f|幸|さいわ}い</code> is a **noun**, not the adverb, so it takes
<code>です</code> directly.

</div>

<code>ご{f|検討|けんとう}いただければ{f|幸|さいわ}いです</code>,
<code>ご{f|返信|へんしん}いただけますと{f|幸|さいわ}いです</code>. In very formal
correspondence <code>{f|幸甚|こうじん}です</code> replaces it.

Keep the reading straight: <code>{f|幸|さいわ}い</code> but
<code>{f|幸|しあわ}せ</code> - same kanji, different words.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>{f|幸|さいわ}い</b>{f|雨|あめ}はすぐにやんだ。
Fortunately the rain stopped straight away.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|財布|さいふ}を{f|落|お}としたが、<b>{f|幸|さいわ}いにも</b>{f|交番|こうばん}に{f|届|とど}いていた。
I dropped my wallet, but luckily it had been handed in at the police box.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨夜|さくや}の{f|地震|じしん}は{f|大|おお}きかったが、<b>{f|幸|さいわ}い</b>{f|棚|たな}の{f|物|もの}が{f|落|お}ちただけで{f|済|す}んだ。
Last night's earthquake was a big one, but happily it came to nothing worse than things falling off the shelves.
</pre>

<pre>
ご{f|意見|いけん}をお{f|聞|き}かせいただければ<b>{f|幸|さいわ}いです</b>。
I would be grateful if you could let me know your thoughts.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Neutral to formal, and much commoner in writing than in speech. It is the standard
word of news reports - <code>{f|幸|さいわ}い、けが{f|人|にん}はいませんでした</code>
closes hundreds of them. In conversation people are more likely to say
<code>よかった</code> or <code>{f|運|うん}よく</code>.

The presupposition is the thing to remember: <code>{f|幸|さいわ}い</code> assumes a
bad background. Applied to plain good fortune with nothing dark behind it, it
sounds off - a lottery win is <code>{f|運|うん}よく</code>, not
<code>{f|幸|さいわ}い</code>. Its natural opposite, <code>あいにく</code>
("unfortunately"), works the same way from the other side and is the polite word
for declining: <code>あいにくその{f|日|ひ}は{f|予定|よてい}がありまして</code>.

The email formula deserves separate storage in memory. It is softer than
<code>お{f|願|ねが}いします</code> because it never actually issues a request - it
describes how the writer would feel - and that indirection is exactly why Japanese
business correspondence leans on it so heavily.

## Contrast

### {f|幸|さいわ}い vs {f|運|うん}よく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|運|うん}よく</code> is about **the odds**: chance fell
your way. <code>{f|幸|さいわ}い</code> is about **the outcome**: it could have been
worse and it wasn't.

</div>

<pre>
{f|連休|れんきゅう}なのに、<b>{f|運|うん}よく</b>{f|窓側|まどがわ}の{f|席|せき}が{f|取|と}れた。
It's a holiday weekend, but I got lucky and managed to get a window seat.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|火|ひ}が{f|出|で}たが、<b>{f|幸|さいわ}い</b>{f|建物|たてもの}に{f|被害|ひがい}はなかった。
A fire broke out, but fortunately the building was undamaged.
</pre>

Swap them and both sentences go strange. Getting a seat is a small win against the
odds, with no disaster behind it. A fire is a disaster, and what the second
sentence reports is not luck in a draw but a bad situation that stopped short.

Note too that only <code>{f|幸|さいわ}い</code> has the noun life -
<code>{f|幸|さいわ}いです</code>, <code>{f|不幸中|ふこうちゅう}の{f|幸|さいわ}い</code> -
while <code>{f|運|うん}よく</code> is an adverb and nothing else.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|幸|さいわ}い、{f|宝|たから}くじに{f|当|あ}たりました。 → ✅ <b>{f|運|うん}よく</b>{f|宝|たから}くじに{f|当|あ}たりました。
  Nothing bad was avoided, so there is no relief for
  <code>{f|幸|さいわ}い</code> to express.

- ❌ ご{f|返事|へんじ}をいただければ{f|幸|さいわ}いにです。 → ✅ ご{f|返事|へんじ}をいただければ<b>{f|幸|さいわ}いです</b>。
  In the email formula the word is a noun. The adverbial `に` has nothing to
  attach to and makes the phrase ungrammatical.

- ❌ {f|幸|さいわ}いな、けがはありませんでした。 → ✅ <b>{f|幸|さいわ}い</b>、けがはありませんでした。／<b>{f|幸|さいわ}いなことに</b>、けがはありませんでした。
  The `な` form only appears in front of a noun, which in practice means the fixed
  phrase <code>{f|幸|さいわ}いなことに</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|台風|たいふう}で{f|木|き}が{f|倒|たお}れたが、____{f|近|ちか}くに{f|人|ひと}はいなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|幸|さいわ}い</b>{f|近|ちか}くに{f|人|ひと}はいなかった。

The first clause supplies the bad news and the second reports the limit on the
damage - the shape this word was made for. <code>{f|幸|さいわ}いにも</code> would
also fit and would sound slightly more written.

</details>

**2.** Translate into Japanese (business email): "I would be grateful if you could
reply by Friday."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|金曜日|きんようび}までにご{f|返信|へんしん}いただければ<b>{f|幸|さいわ}いです</b>。

This is the noun use in its fixed frame: a conditional clause, then
<code>{f|幸|さいわ}いです</code>. Because it states the writer's feeling rather than
issuing an instruction, it is noticeably softer than
<code>ご{f|返信|へんしん}ください</code>.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|幸|さいわ}い、{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|動|うご}いていた。
(b) {f|運|うん}よく、{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|動|うご}いていた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are natural, but they imply different stories. (a) suggests something had
gone wrong - a storm, an accident - and the trains running is the good news inside
a bad day.

(b) suggests uncertainty rather than trouble: the speaker did not know whether the
trains would be running and the coin came down the right way. The focus is on the
gamble, not on the relief.

</details>
