# ～に応じて

**Meaning** · depending on; according to; to suit - the second thing is set to **match** the first, and changes when the first changes

<code>{f|応|おう}じる</code> is "to respond, to suit, to comply". What
<code>～に{f|応|おう}じて</code> marks is a **variable**: age, income, budget, the
number of people, the season, the situation. Whatever follows is adjusted to fit
that variable case by case. Change the input and you change the output - that
covariation is the whole pattern, and a noun with no range of values in it will not
take it.

## Formation

The variable is marked with `に`. This pattern takes a **noun only** - it has no
form that attaches to a verb, which is the fastest way to tell it apart from
<code>～につれて</code>.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + に{f|応|おう}じて | {f|年齢|ねんれい} → {f|年齢|ねんれい}に{f|応|おう}じて |
| Noun (stiffer, written) | Noun + に{f|応|おう}じ | {f|状況|じょうきょう} → {f|状況|じょうきょう}に{f|応|おう}じ |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + に{f|応|おう}じた + Noun | {f|能力|のうりょく} → {f|能力|のうりょく}に{f|応|おう}じた{f|仕事|しごと} |
| Fixed adverb | {f|必要|ひつよう}に{f|応|おう}じて | - → {f|必要|ひつよう}に{f|応|おう}じて{f|追加|ついか}する |
| The bare verb (a separate use) | Noun + に{f|応|おう}じる | {f|要求|ようきゅう} → {f|要求|ようきゅう}に{f|応|おう}じる |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the last row is not this pattern. On its own,
<code>{f|応|おう}じる</code> means "agree to, accede to":
<code>{f|取材|しゅざい}に{f|応|おう}じる</code> is to consent to be interviewed,
<code>{f|要求|ようきゅう}に{f|応|おう}じる</code> is to give in to a demand. That second
life is why <code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code> sometimes shades into "in compliance with"
rather than "varying with".

</div>

## Nuance & register

Formal-leaning, but far more everyday than
<code>に{f|基|もと}づいて</code>. You meet it on menus, price lists, job adverts,
insurance documents and council notices, and it is perfectly natural in careful
speech - a shop assistant will say
<code>ご{f|予算|よさん}に{f|応|おう}じて</code> without sounding stiff. What it is not
is chatty; among friends you would just say
<code>{f|人|ひと}によって{f|変|か}える</code>.

<code>に{f|応|おう}じ</code> is the connective form, and it belongs to documents.
<code>に{f|応|おう}じた</code> is the form that modifies a following noun.

Three collocations are worth learning whole:
<code>{f|必要|ひつよう}に{f|応|おう}じて</code> ("as required", "as necessary") has
hardened into a plain adverb and is one of the most common phrases in Japanese
officialese; <code>{f|状況|じょうきょう}に{f|応|おう}じて</code> is "as the situation
demands"; <code>{f|場合|ばあい}に{f|応|おう}じて</code> is "case by case".

There is a soft register point worth flagging.
<code>{f|状況|じょうきょう}に{f|応|おう}じて{f|判断|はんだん}してください</code> is a
genuinely common workplace instruction, and it tends to devolve the risk along with
the decision. Hearing it, a Japanese employee will often ask for the boundaries
before acting.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|応|おう}じて vs ～によって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - in the "varies with" sense the two overlap heavily. `によって`
reports the **bare fact** that things differ. <code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code> presents
the difference as a **deliberate adjustment made to fit**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|習慣|しゅうかん}は{f|国|くに}<b>によって</b>{f|違|ちが}う。
Customs differ from country to country.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|国|くに}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|説明|せつめい}の{f|仕方|しかた}を{f|変|か}えている。
We vary the way we explain it to suit the country.
</pre>

Ask whether anybody is doing the adjusting. If the variation simply exists in the
world, `によって` is the word, and with
<code>{f|違|ちが}う</code> or <code>{f|異|こと}なる</code> it is effectively fixed. If
somebody is tailoring something to fit, <code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code> is available and
sounds more considerate.

Remember also how much `によって` does that this pattern cannot: cause
(<code>{f|事故|じこ}によって</code>), means
(<code>{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いによって</code>) and the agent of a passive
(<code>{f|彼|かれ}によって{f|書|か}かれた</code>). <code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code> has
exactly the one job.

### ～に{f|応|おう}じて vs ～に{f|応|こた}えて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same kanji, two verbs.
<code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code> **matches** a variable.
<code>に{f|応|こた}えて</code> **answers** a request, a demand or an expectation aimed
at you.

</div>

<pre>
{f|注文|ちゅうもん}の{f|数|かず}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|割引|わりびき}いたします。
We give a discount according to the size of the order.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|地元|じもと}の{f|要望|ようぼう}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>{f|終電|しゅうでん}が{f|遅|おそ}くなった。
In response to requests from local people, the last train now runs later.
</pre>

The memory hook: <code>{f|応|こた}える</code> answers somebody;
<code>{f|応|おう}じる</code> matches something. With a request, both readings are
possible and they say different things -
<code>{f|要望|ようぼう}に{f|応|こた}えて</code> is living up to what people hoped for,
while <code>{f|要望|ようぼう}に{f|応|おう}じて</code> is simply acceding to it, with no
warmth attached.

### ～に{f|応|おう}じて vs ～につれて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `につれて` is a **gradual change over time** that nobody
controls. <code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code> is a value **set to match**, case by case,
usually by somebody.

</div>

<pre>
{f|年|とし}をとる<b>につれて</b>、{f|朝|あさ}{f|早|はや}く{f|目|め}が{f|覚|さ}めるようになった。
As I get older, I have started waking up early.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|年齢|ねんれい}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|保険料|ほけんりょう}が{f|決|き}まる。
The premium is set according to age.
</pre>

The formal difference settles most cases: `につれて` takes a **verb clause** and
<code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code> takes a **noun**. <code>{f|年|とし}をとるにつれて</code>
is a process unfolding; <code>{f|年齢|ねんれい}に{f|応|おう}じて</code> is a table of
ages and matching prices, with no unfolding in it at all.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|人数|にんずう}が{f|増|ふ}える<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|席|せき}を{f|足|た}す。 → ✅ {f|人数|にんずう}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|席|せき}を{f|足|た}す。
  A verb cannot attach. Either reduce the clause to its noun, or switch to
  <code>{f|増|ふ}えるにつれて</code>, which does take verbs.

- ❌ この{f|計画|けいかく}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|工事|こうじ}を{f|進|すす}める。 → ✅ この{f|計画|けいかく}<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|工事|こうじ}を{f|進|すす}める。
  One fixed plan has no range of values, so there is nothing for the work to vary
  with. A single document is a line to follow.

- ❌ ファンの{f|期待|きたい}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}した。 → ✅ ファンの{f|期待|きたい}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}した。
  Expectations are answered, not matched. Winning is not something you scaled to the
  size of the hope.

## Practice

**1.** {f|店|みせ}の{f|飾|かざ}りつけは、{f|季節|きせつ}____{f|変|か}えるようにしている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|季節|きせつ}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|変|か}えるようにしている。

The season is a variable with four settings, and the decorations are deliberately
matched to it. Somebody is doing the adjusting, which is what tips this away from
`によって`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: ファンの{f|声|こえ}（に{f|応|おう}じて・に{f|応|こた}えて）{f|再|さい}{f|公演|こうえん}が{f|決|き}まった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ファンの{f|声|こえ}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>{f|再|さい}{f|公演|こうえん}が{f|決|き}まった。

Fans asked, and the company answered them. Nothing is being scaled here - there is
one extra run of the show, not an amount of show proportional to the noise.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The charge varies according to how many hours you use the room."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|料金|りょうきん}は{f|部屋|へや}を{f|使|つか}う{f|時間|じかん}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|変|か}わります。

Hours are a scale and the charge tracks it, so this is the core use. Notice that the
verb clause <code>{f|部屋|へや}を{f|使|つか}う</code> is safely inside the noun phrase -
it modifies <code>{f|時間|じかん}</code>, and it is the noun that attaches.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|習慣|しゅうかん}は{f|地域|ちいき}に{f|応|おう}じて{f|違|ちが}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|習慣|しゅうかん}は{f|地域|ちいき}<b>によって</b>{f|違|ちが}います。

Nobody adjusts local customs to fit the region - they simply differ, and the bare
fact of variation is `によって`'s job. With
<code>{f|違|ちが}う</code> and <code>{f|異|こと}なる</code> the pairing is fixed.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) お{f|客|きゃく}さまのご{f|要望|ようぼう}に{f|応|おう}じて{f|変更|へんこう}いたします。
(b) お{f|客|きゃく}さまのご{f|要望|ようぼう}に{f|応|こた}えて{f|変更|へんこう}いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is procedural: if you ask, we will change it, and what we change will be shaped
to what you asked for. It describes a service that exists, and it is what a menu of
options would say.

(b) is warmer and looks backwards: customers asked for this, and we are living up to
that. It frames the change as a response to people rather than as a setting on a
form, which is why it turns up in announcements rather than in terms and conditions.

</details>
