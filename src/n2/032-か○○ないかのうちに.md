# ～か～ないかのうちに

**Meaning** · had barely finished X when Y - so fast that you could not say whether X was even over

The whole force of this pattern is in the middle: the same verb is said once
affirmative and once negative, so the sentence literally means "in the interval
where you could not tell whether it had happened or not". It is the tightest
"as soon as" Japanese has, and it is always a past report of something watched.

## Formation

The verb appears **twice**, and it must be the same verb both times.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary + か + ない-form + かのうちに | {f|座|すわ}る → {f|座|すわ}るか{f|座|すわ}らないかのうちに |
| Verb (past variant) | た-form + か + ない-form + かのうちに | {f|終|お}わる → {f|終|お}わったか{f|終|お}わらないかのうちに |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | {f|来|く}る か {f|来|こ}ない かのうちに | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|く}るか{f|来|こ}ないかのうちに |
| Passive | passive dictionary + か + passive ない | {f|開|ひら}かれる → {f|開|ひら}かれるか{f|開|ひら}かれないかのうちに |
| ❌ Two different verbs | ❌{f|着|つ}くか{f|座|すわ}らないかのうちに | ✅{f|着|つ}くか{f|着|つ}かないかのうちに |
| ❌ Negative first | ❌{f|閉|し}まらないか{f|閉|し}まるかのうちに | ✅{f|閉|し}まるか{f|閉|し}まらないかのうちに |
| ❌ Non-past main clause | ❌…かのうちに、{f|出|で}かけます | ✅…かのうちに、{f|出|で}かけた |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - affirmative first, negative second, and the main clause in the
**past**. This pattern narrates; it cannot request, invite, promise or predict.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|来|く}る</code> is the one form worth writing out. Its dictionary
form is <code>{f|来|く}る</code> and its negative stem is <code>{f|来|こ}</code>, so
the pair is <code>{f|来|く}るか{f|来|こ}ないか</code> - two different readings of the
same character in the same breath.

</div>

A man leapt aboard just as the doors were closing.

## Nuance & register

This is a **narrative** pattern. It belongs to reportage, fiction, sports
commentary and the kind of anecdote you tell at length; it is long, deliberate and
slightly theatrical, and nobody uses it for something trivial. In everyday speech
the same content comes out as <code>{f|閉|し}まる{f|直前|ちょくぜん}に</code> or a
plain <code>たとたん</code>.

Register aside, it is neutral - no gender or age marking, equally at home in a
novel and in a news bulletin, and it takes ます-form endings without difficulty
({f|飛|と}び{f|乗|の}ってきました).

The reason it is so long is that it is doing something precise. It does not claim
the first event finished; it claims you could not tell. That is why it so often
describes rudeness, impatience or eagerness - somebody who could not wait the extra
half-second. Used of your own actions it is faintly self-mocking, which is a real
use: 「{f|席|せき}に{f|着|つ}くか{f|着|つ}かないかのうちに{f|寝|ね}てしまった」.

Because it narrates, the whole apparatus of intention is unavailable. No
`てください`, no `ましょう`, no `つもり`, no prediction about tomorrow. If you want
the same tightness pointing forward, that is <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code>.

## Contrast

### ～か～ないかのうちに vs ～たとたん

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>たとたん</code> says X **finished** and then Y hit.
<code>か～ないかのうちに</code> says Y arrived **before X was over** - the gap is not
merely zero, it is negative.

</div>

<code>たとたん</code> is the N3 pattern this one is closest to, and both are past-only,
so the split is not tense but the boundary itself.

<pre>
{f|電話|でんわ}を{f|切|き}っ<b>たとたん</b>、また{f|着信|ちゃくしん}があった。
The instant I hung up, another call came in.
</pre>

The call ended; then the phone rang. Two events in order.

<pre>
{f|電話|でんわ}を<b>{f|切|き}るか{f|切|き}らないかのうちに</b>、また{f|着信|ちゃくしん}があった。
The next call was coming in before I'd even got the phone down.
</pre>

Now the two overlap at the edge, and the speaker is admitting they cannot separate
them. There is a second difference of feel: <code>たとたん</code> is built on the
speaker's surprise at the *result*, while this pattern is built on how little time
there was, and the result may be entirely unsurprising.

### ～か～ないかのうちに vs ～ないうちに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ないうちに</code> looks at a window **before** X, and X may
never happen at all. <code>か～ないかのうちに</code> sits exactly **on** X's edge, and
X does happen.

</div>

<pre>
{f|暗|くら}く<b>ならないうちに</b>{f|帰|かえ}りましょう。
Let's head back before it gets dark.
</pre>

That is a plan, made inside a window that is still open - and note that it can be an
invitation.

<pre>
{f|空|そら}が<b>{f|暗|くら}くなるか{f|暗|くら}くならないかのうちに</b>、{f|大|おお}{f|粒|つぶ}の{f|雨|あめ}が{f|落|お}ちてきた。
Fat drops of rain began falling almost before the sky had gone dark.
</pre>

Here the darkening did occur, and the sentence pins the rain to its very edge.
Swapping in <code>ないうちに</code> would change the claim to "while it was still
light", which is a different fact.

### ～か～ないかのうちに vs ～たかと{f|思|おも}うと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both mean "no sooner than". This one is about **measurement**:
the interval was too small to measure. <code>たかと思うと</code> is about the
speaker's **reaction**: the second event contradicts what the first led you to
expect.

</div>

<pre>
{f|空|そら}が{f|明|あか}るくなっ<b>たかと{f|思|おも}うと</b>、また{f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}した。
Just when the sky had brightened, it started raining again.
</pre>

The point is the reversal, not the stopwatch - and this is why
<code>かと{f|思|おも}うと</code> readily describes something repeating
({f|泣|な}いたかと{f|思|おも}うと{f|笑|わら}う) while
<code>か～ないかのうちに</code> reports one occasion only.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|席|せき}に<b>{f|着|つ}くか{f|座|すわ}らないかのうちに</b> → ✅ {f|席|せき}に<b>{f|着|つ}くか{f|着|つ}かないかのうちに</b>
  It has to be the same verb twice. The pattern's meaning comes from asserting and
  denying one single event.

- ❌ ドアが<b>{f|閉|し}まらないか{f|閉|し}まるかのうちに</b> → ✅ ドアが<b>{f|閉|し}まるか{f|閉|し}まらないかのうちに</b>
  Affirmative first. The order is fixed and reversing it sounds like a stumble.

- ❌ {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わるか{f|終|お}わらないかのうちに、{f|連絡|れんらく}してください。 → ✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わり{f|次第|しだい}、{f|連絡|れんらく}してください。
  Requests and plans are out. This pattern only reports what already happened.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}がやむか<b>やまないかのうちで</b> → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}がやむか<b>やまないかのうちに</b>
  The particle is `に`. <code>うち</code> here marks a moment, not a place.

## Practice

**1.** {f|信号|しんごう}が{f|青|あお}に{f|変|か}わる____{f|変|か}わらないかのうちに、バイクが{f|走|はし}り{f|出|だ}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|変|か}わる<b>か</b>{f|変|か}わらないかのうちに

The `か` between the two halves is what makes the sentence mean "you could not tell
which". Both halves use the same verb, affirmative then negative.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|暗|くら}く（なるかならないかのうちに・ならないうちに）、{f|洗濯物|せんたくもの}を{f|取|と}り{f|込|こ}んでおこう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|暗|くら}く<b>ならないうちに</b>、{f|洗濯物|せんたくもの}を{f|取|と}り{f|込|こ}んでおこう。

The sentence ends in a volitional - the speaker is planning. That rules out
<code>か～ないかのうちに</code>, which can only narrate the past, and calls for the
"while there is still time" window of <code>ないうちに</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He had barely got through the door when he started complaining."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|入|はい}ってくるか{f|入|はい}ってこないかのうちに</b>、{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}い{f|始|はじ}めた。

"Barely ... when" is exactly this pattern. The compound
<code>{f|入|はい}ってくる</code> repeats whole, and the main clause is past.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|先生|せんせい}が{f|教室|きょうしつ}に{f|来|く}るか{f|来|く}らないかのうちに、みんな{f|静|しず}かになった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|先生|せんせい}が{f|教室|きょうしつ}に<b>{f|来|く}るか{f|来|こ}ないかのうちに</b>、みんな{f|静|しず}かになった。

<code>{f|来|く}る</code> is irregular: the negative is <code>{f|来|こ}ない</code>, not
❌<code>{f|来|く}らない</code>. The same character is read
<code>く</code> in the first half and <code>こ</code> in the second.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ベルが{f|鳴|な}っ<b>たとたん</b>、{f|生徒|せいと}たちが{f|立|た}ち{f|上|あ}がった。
(b) ベルが<b>{f|鳴|な}るか{f|鳴|な}らないかのうちに</b>、{f|生徒|せいと}たちが{f|立|た}ち{f|上|あ}がった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) puts the two in order - the bell finished, then they stood - and colours the
standing as something the speaker did not expect.

(b) refuses to put them in order. The students were on their feet while the bell was
still going, and the sentence is quietly judging them for it: they could not even
wait for it to finish.

</details>
