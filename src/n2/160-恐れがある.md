# ～恐れがある

**Meaning** · there is a risk that ～; there are fears that ～ - read *おそれ*; the official way to state that something bad may happen

<code>{f|恐|おそ}れ</code> is the noun made from {f|恐|おそ}れる, "to fear", and the pattern
is literally "a fear of ～ exists". Nobody in particular does the fearing, and that is the
point: the risk is presented as a fact about the world rather than as one person's worry.
It is the voice of a weather bulletin, a warning label and a company announcement, and it
attaches only to outcomes people would rather avoid.

## Formation

<code>{f|恐|おそ}れ</code> is a noun, so what comes in front of it is in noun-modifying
shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + {f|恐|おそ}れがある | {f|倒|たお}れる → {f|倒|たお}れる{f|恐|おそ}れがある |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + {f|恐|おそ}れがある | {f|届|とど}かない → {f|届|とど}かない{f|恐|おそ}れがある |
| Noun | Noun + の + {f|恐|おそ}れがある | {f|津波|つなみ} → {f|津波|つなみ}の{f|恐|おそ}れがある |
| Polite | … + {f|恐|おそ}れがあります | {f|遅|おく}れる → {f|遅|おく}れる{f|恐|おそ}れがあります |
| Modifying a noun | … + {f|恐|おそ}れのある + Noun | {f|落|お}ちる → {f|落|お}ちる{f|恐|おそ}れのある{f|場所|ばしょ} |
| Risk newly emerging | … + {f|恐|おそ}れが{f|出|で}てきた | {f|中止|ちゅうし}になる → {f|中止|ちゅうし}になる{f|恐|おそ}れが{f|出|で}てきた |
| No such risk | … + {f|恐|おそ}れはない | {f|広|ひろ}がる → {f|広|ひろ}がる{f|恐|おそ}れはない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - news copy often writes the word in kana as おそれ, and legal drafting uses the
old character {f|虞|おそれ}. All three spellings are the same word. Keep it apart from the
adverb {f|恐|おそ}らく, "probably", which is related but does a completely different job.

</div>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is **public, institutional language**. Its natural homes are weather and disaster
warnings, medical leaflets, product labels, safety notices, news reporting and corporate
statements. What it buys you is distance: the sentence names a risk without naming anyone
who is afraid, and without accusing anyone of causing it. That is exactly what an
announcement needs.

Turned on small private matters it is comically heavy.
<code>{f|遅刻|ちこく}する{f|恐|おそ}れがあります</code> about your own morning sounds like a
transport authority talking about itself. **In ordinary speech, say
<code>{f|遅|おく}れるかもしれません</code> or
<code>{f|遅|おく}れそうです</code>.** The pattern earns its place when the risk is real,
shared and worth announcing - which is why it does turn up in ordinary conversation about
storms, illness and money.

Two restrictions follow from the meaning of the noun. The outcome must be **undesirable**:
there is no such thing as
<code>{f|当|あ}たる{f|恐|おそ}れがある</code> about a lottery win, unless you are joking.
And it wants an **event**, not a static description - adjectives have to be turned into
something that happens: not
<code>{f|高|たか}い{f|恐|おそ}れ</code> but <code>{f|高|たか}くなる{f|恐|おそ}れ</code>.

The negative <code>{f|恐|おそ}れはありません</code> is a set piece of reassurance in public
announcements: "there is no danger of …". It is the sentence a spokesperson says after an
incident, and hearing it, a Japanese audience knows the worst has been ruled out.

## Contrast

### ～{f|恐|おそ}れがある vs ～{f|可能性|かのうせい}がある

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}</code> is **neutral** and works for good
outcomes and bad. <code>{f|恐|おそ}れ</code> is only ever bad.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|店|みせ}が{f|開|ひら}く<b>{f|可能性|かのうせい}があります</b>。
There is a chance a new shop will open next month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大雪|おおゆき}で{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まる<b>{f|恐|おそ}れがあります</b>。
There is a risk the trains will stop because of the heavy snow.
</pre>

Both are formal, and in a report about hazards they are close to interchangeable - which
is why the choice is a good test of whether a writer has understood the sentence. If the
outcome would please anybody, <code>{f|恐|おそ}れ</code> is wrong. Note too that
<code>{f|可能性|かのうせい}</code> takes な-adjectives and nouns through `の`, so it is the
more flexible of the two grammatically.

### ～{f|恐|おそ}れがある vs ～かねない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|恐|おそ}れがある</code> states that a risk **exists**;
`かねない` is somebody **warning** you about it. The first has no speaker in it, the second
is all speaker.

</div>

<pre>
{f|部品|ぶひん}の{f|不足|ふそく}で{f|生産|せいさん}が{f|止|と}まる<b>{f|恐|おそ}れがあります</b>。
There is a risk production will halt owing to the shortage of parts.
</pre>

<pre>
このまま{f|注文|ちゅうもん}を{f|増|ふ}やせば、{f|在庫|ざいこ}を{f|抱|かか}え{f|込|こ}み<b>かねません</b>。
If we keep increasing orders like this we could well end up stuck with stock.
</pre>

The first could appear in a press release; the second is a colleague arguing against a
plan. `かねない` also takes people as subjects, which
<code>{f|恐|おそ}れがある</code> resists: a sentence saying that a named person
<code>{f|恐|おそ}れがある</code> reads like a police report about them.

### ～{f|恐|おそ}れがある vs ～かもしれない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same territory, different altitude. `かもしれない` is what a person
says; <code>{f|恐|おそ}れがある</code> is what an institution announces.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|会場|かいじょう}が{f|混|こ}む<b>かもしれない</b>から、{f|早|はや}めに{f|行|い}こう。
The venue might be crowded tomorrow, so let's go early.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}が{f|大変|たいへん}{f|混雑|こんざつ}する<b>{f|恐|おそ}れがあります</b>ので、{f|時間|じかん}に{f|余裕|よゆう}を{f|持|も}ってお{f|越|こ}しください。
As the venue is expected to become extremely crowded, please allow plenty of time.
</pre>

Identical facts, two registers. The second is the announcement the organisers put on the
website; the first is you texting a friend about it. Learners who have just met
<code>{f|恐|おそ}れがある</code> tend to use it where `かもしれない` belongs, and the effect
is of somebody reading their own life out over a public address system.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|天気|てんき}が{f|回復|かいふく}する<b>{f|恐|おそ}れがあります</b>。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|天気|てんき}が{f|回復|かいふく}する<b>でしょう</b>。
  Good news cannot take <code>{f|恐|おそ}れ</code>. The noun means *fear*, and clearing
  weather is nobody's fear.

- ❌ {f|事故|じこ}<b>{f|恐|おそ}れがある</b>ため、{f|通行止|つうこうど}めです。 → ✅ {f|事故|じこ}<b>の{f|恐|おそ}れがある</b>ため、{f|通行止|つうこうど}めです。
  <code>{f|恐|おそ}れ</code> is a noun, so another noun in front of it needs `の`. Only a
  verb or adjective clause attaches directly.

- ❌ この{f|川|かわ}は{f|危|あぶ}ない<b>{f|恐|おそ}れがあります</b>。 → ✅ この{f|川|かわ}は{f|増水|ぞうすい}する<b>{f|恐|おそ}れがあります</b>。
  The pattern wants an event that might occur, not a description of how things already
  are. Turn the adjective into something that happens.

- ❌ ごめん、{f|待|ま}ち{f|合|あ}わせに{f|遅|おく}れる<b>{f|恐|おそ}れがある</b>。 → ✅ ごめん、{f|待|ま}ち{f|合|あ}わせに{f|遅|おく}れ<b>そう</b>。
  Grammatical, and far too grand for a text message to a friend. Save the pattern for
  risks that would be announced rather than mentioned.

## Practice

**1.** {f|強|つよ}い{f|地震|じしん}のあとは、{f|津波|つなみ}____{f|恐|おそ}れがあります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|津波|つなみ}<b>の</b>{f|恐|おそ}れがあります。

{f|津波|つなみ} is a noun and <code>{f|恐|おそ}れ</code> is a noun, so `の` has to link
them. Had the sentence used a verb - <code>{f|津波|つなみ}が{f|来|く}る</code> - it would
attach directly with no particle.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|今年|ことし}は{f|野菜|やさい}の{f|値段|ねだん}が（{f|下|さ}がる{f|恐|おそ}れ・{f|下|さ}がる{f|可能性|かのうせい}）があります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|下|さ}がる<b>{f|可能性|かのうせい}</b>があります。

For a shopper, falling vegetable prices are good news, and
<code>{f|恐|おそ}れ</code> cannot describe a welcome outcome. Written from a farmer's point
of view the risk version would suddenly make sense - which shows that the choice depends
on whose interests the sentence is written from.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, in the register of a public notice: "Heavy rain may cause the river to overflow. Please move to higher ground early."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|大雨|おおあめ}で{f|川|かわ}の{f|水|みず}があふれる<b>{f|恐|おそ}れがあります</b>。{f|早|はや}めに{f|高|たか}い{f|場所|ばしょ}へ{f|避難|ひなん}してください。

A flood warning is exactly the register this pattern exists for: an impersonal statement
of danger followed by an instruction. The verb あふれる attaches directly.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|建物|たてもの}は{f|古|ふる}くて、{f|倒|たお}れるかもしれない{f|恐|おそ}れがある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|建物|たてもの}は{f|古|ふる}くて、{f|倒|たお}れる<b>{f|恐|おそ}れがある</b>。

<code>{f|恐|おそ}れがある</code> already says the thing may happen, so `かもしれない` in
front of it doubles the guess. Attach the plain form of the verb straight to the noun.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|台風|たいふう}で{f|停電|ていでん}する<b>{f|恐|おそ}れがあります</b>。
(b) {f|台風|たいふう}で{f|停電|ていでん}し<b>かねません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) states the risk impersonally, the way a power company or a broadcaster would. There is
no speaker in the sentence and no implied criticism - it is information, and the listener
can do with it as they like.

(b) puts a person behind it. Someone has looked at the situation and is telling you it
could go wrong, usually as an argument for doing something now - charging a battery,
cancelling a plan. The facts are the same; (b) comes with a recommendation attached.

</details>
