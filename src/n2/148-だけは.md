# ～だけは

**Meaning** · at least the ～ part; this one thing, whatever else - and, in <code>やるだけはやった</code>, "I did all the doing there was to do"

<code>だけ</code> restricts; the <code>は</code> then lifts that one item out and sets
it against everything else. The result is not "only" in the flat sense. It is a **floor**:
whatever failed, whatever is missing, *this much is secured*. In the doubled shape
<code>{f|言|い}うだけは{f|言|い}った</code> the floor is one's own effort, and the
unspoken second half is almost always "…but it didn't work".

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb, doubled | dictionary form + だけは + same verb, past | やる → やるだけはやった |
| Verb, doubled (polite) | dictionary form + だけは + ました | {f|試|ため}す → {f|試|ため}すだけは{f|試|ため}しました |
| Noun | Noun + だけは | これ → これだけは |
| Nominalised clause | ～こと + だけは | {f|謝|あやま}ること → {f|謝|あやま}ることだけは |
| Verb + ～ておく／～てみる | dictionary form + だけは + ～ておく | {f|聞|き}く → {f|聞|き}くだけは{f|聞|き}いておく |
| Negative resolve | ～ことだけは + しない | {f|裏切|うらぎ}ること → {f|裏切|うらぎ}ることだけはしない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - in the doubled shape the two verbs must be the **same verb**, and the first
one stays in the dictionary form no matter what tense the sentence is in. The tense is
carried entirely by the second.

</div>

## Nuance & register

The tone is resignation with dignity. <code>やるだけはやった</code> is what you say
when the outcome has gone against you and you want it on record that the effort was not
the problem. It is warm towards the speaker and slightly braced - there is always an
implied <code>が</code> after it, even when nothing follows.

That implication cuts both ways. Because the pattern separates *the doing* from *the
result*, pairing it with confidence is contradictory:
<code>やるだけはやったから{f|絶対|ぜったい}{f|受|う}かる</code> pulls in two directions
at once. If the result is good, drop the pattern and just say
<code>がんばってよかった</code>.

The noun use - <code>これだけは</code>, <code>{f|命|いのち}だけは</code>,
<code>{f|約束|やくそく}だけは</code> - is a different flavour: not resignation but
**insistence**. It marks the non-negotiable item in a list of things you are otherwise
willing to lose. That makes it strong, and in a request it is close to pleading:
<code>これだけはお{f|願|ねが}いします</code> is heavier than
<code>これをお{f|願|ねが}いします</code>.

Register-wise the pattern is neutral. It works in plain and ます form, in speech and in
writing, and it is not marked for age or gender. What is casual about
<code>やるだけはやった</code> is the sentiment, not the grammar; the same words in
<code>やるだけはやりました</code> are perfectly acceptable in a report to a manager.

## Contrast

### やるだけはやった vs やるだけやった

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - without <code>は</code>, the sentence measures **how much** was done.
With <code>は</code>, it separates the doing from the outcome and quietly promises a
"but".

</div>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}のある{f|限|かぎ}り、やる<b>だけやった</b>。
I did as much as there was time to do.
</pre>

<pre>
やる<b>だけはやった</b>が、{f|結果|けっか}はついてこなかった。
I did the doing part, at least - the results didn't follow.
</pre>

The difference is small enough that in many sentences either passes, and native speakers
will not always agree on a line between them. The reliable part is the pull of the
<code>は</code>: it invites a contrast, so a listener hearing
<code>やるだけはやった</code> is already braced for bad news.

### ～だけは vs ～だけだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>だけは</code> sets a **floor** ("at least this much").
<code>だけだ</code> sets a **ceiling** ("nothing more than this").

</div>

<pre>
{f|言|い}う<b>だけは</b>{f|言|い}いました。
I did at least say it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|言|い}った<b>だけです</b>。
I only said it. (I didn't do anything about it.)
</pre>

The first defends you; the second is what someone says to belittle what you did, and
sits next to <code>にすぎない</code> in feel. Getting these the wrong way round in an
apology is genuinely awkward.

### ～だけは vs ～だけでも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>だけは</code> = *this one is non-negotiable*.
<code>だけでも</code> = *even just this much would do*.

</div>

<pre>
{f|夜|よる}{f|遅|おそ}くなるなら、{f|連絡|れんらく}<b>だけは</b>してね。
If you're going to be late, the one thing you must do is get in touch.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|全部|ぜんぶ}は{f|無理|むり}でも、この{f|一枚|いちまい}<b>だけでも</b>{f|見|み}てもらえませんか。
I know you can't do all of it, but could you at least look at this one page?
</pre>

<code>だけは</code> is a demand or a promise; <code>だけでも</code> is a concession -
it lowers the ask because the full version is out of reach. Put the wrong one in a
request and you either sound stern when you meant to be accommodating, or the reverse.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|宿題|しゅくだい}<b>だけは</b>しました。（meaning "the homework was all I did"） → ✅ {f|宿題|しゅくだい}<b>だけ</b>しました。／{f|宿題|しゅくだい}<b>しか</b>していません。
  <code>だけは</code> means "at least the homework, whatever else went undone" - a
  defence, not a confession. This is the single most common misreading of the pattern.

- ❌ <b>やっただけはやった</b>。 → ✅ <b>やるだけはやった</b>。
  The first verb stays in the dictionary form. Only the second one takes the tense.

- ❌ <b>{f|言|い}うだけは{f|話|はな}した</b>。 → ✅ <b>{f|言|い}うだけは{f|言|い}った</b>。
  The doubled shape needs the very same verb twice. A synonym breaks it.

- ❌ できること<b>だけは</b>やったので、{f|今回|こんかい}は{f|絶対|ぜったい}{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}だ。 → ✅ できること<b>だけは</b>やったので、あとは{f|運|うん}にまかせよう。
  The pattern concedes that the result is out of your hands. Following it with certainty
  contradicts the very thing it just said.

## Practice

**1.** {f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|置|お}いていってもいいけれど、パスポート____{f|持|も}っていってね。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

パスポート<b>だけは</b>{f|持|も}っていってね。

Everything else is negotiable; the passport is the floor. That contrast between one
protected item and the rest is what the <code>は</code> is doing.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|完成|かんせい}はしなかったが、{f|下|した}{f|調|しら}べ（だけ・だけは）しておいたので、{f|次|つぎ}の{f|人|ひと}が{f|楽|らく}になるはずだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|下|した}{f|調|しら}べ<b>だけは</b>しておいた…

The sentence is defending an incomplete job by pointing at what *was* secured, which
calls for the floor reading. Plain <code>だけ</code> would read as "all I did was the
groundwork", which turns the defence into an admission.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I applied, at least - I don't expect to get in."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}む<b>だけは</b>{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}んだ。{f|通|とお}るとは{f|思|おも}っていないけれど。

The doubled shape is the natural fit: the effort is real, the outcome is not being
claimed. The English "at least" lands on the pattern, not on a separate adverb.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|説明|せつめい}するだけは{f|説明|せつめい}するが、{f|聞|き}いてもらえなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|説明|せつめい}する<b>だけは{f|説明|せつめい}した</b>が、{f|聞|き}いてもらえなかった。

The second half is in the past, so the second verb must be too. The first verb stays as
<code>する</code>; only the tail carries the tense.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|片付|かたづ}けるだけは{f|片付|かたづ}けた。
(b) {f|片付|かたづ}けただけだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the tidying itself was done properly, and braces the listener for a "but" -
perhaps the room still looks bad, or nobody noticed. It protects the speaker.

(b) belittles the act: tidying was *all* that happened, as if it hardly counted. Said
about your own work it sounds modest; said about someone else's it sounds dismissive.
The two sentences share every word except the particle, and they take opposite sides.

</details>
