# ～つもりで

**Meaning** · doing something in a particular frame of mind - with that intention, or *as if* it were so

`つもりだ` states an intention. Put it in the `で` form and it stops being the point of
the sentence and becomes the **attitude you carry into** the action that follows. With
a past-tense verb in front, it slides one step further: not "intending to" but
"treating it as though you already had".

## Formation

<code>つもり</code> is a noun, so what comes in front of it is in noun-modifying shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + つもりで | {f|手伝|てつだ}う → {f|手伝|てつだ}うつもりで |
| Verb (past) | ～た + つもりで | {f|買|か}う → {f|買|か}ったつもりで |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + つもりで | {f|言|い}わない → {f|言|い}わないつもりで |
| い-adjective | plain form + つもりで | {f|若|わか}い → {f|若|わか}いつもりで |
| Noun | ～の + つもりで | {f|冗談|じょうだん} → {f|冗談|じょうだん}のつもりで |
| Set phrase | そのつもりで | - → そのつもりでお{f|願|ねが}いします |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|買|か}うつもりで</code> and <code>{f|買|か}ったつもりで</code> are two
different patterns, not two tenses of one. The first means "intending to buy"; the
second means "as if you had bought it" - money you spend in imagination and keep in the
bank.

</div>

</pre>

I'll study it again from scratch, as though I were a new recruit.
</pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

</pre>

<pre>

I set out meaning to finish in half an hour, and in the end it took half a day.

</pre>

## Nuance & register

`つもりで` is register-neutral and turns up as readily in a training manual as in a
conversation between friends. What it changes is the *frame* of the action, so it very
often carries a note of self-persuasion: you are choosing to adopt a mindset that is not
literally true in order to get something done.

The `～たつもりで` shapes are close to idioms and worth memorising whole.
<code>{f|死|し}んだつもりで</code> is "with nothing left to lose"; <code>だまされたつもりで</code>
is "just humour me and try it" - a shopkeeper's phrase;
<code>{f|買|か}ったつもり{f|貯金|ちょきん}</code> is a recognised savings trick. None of them
implies actual deception of anyone else, which is the crucial difference from
<code>ふりをする</code>.

<code>そのつもりで</code> deserves separate mention. As a standalone it means "so bear
that in mind, and act accordingly" - <code>{f|来週|らいしゅう}から{f|担当|たんとう}が{f|変|か}わりますので、そのつもりで</code>.
Delivered flatly it is a warning, and it lands harder than its length suggests; softened
with <code>お{f|願|ねが}いします</code> it is ordinary workplace politeness.

One restriction learners trip on: because `つもりで` describes the *actor's* frame of
mind, the person holding it should be the subject of the following verb. Attributing a
mindset to somebody else while describing your own action produces a sentence that
does not add up.

## Contrast

### ～つもりで vs ～つもりだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `つもりだ` **is** the predicate: it announces the plan. `つもりで`
is adverbial: it colours a different verb that follows.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来年|らいねん}{f|車|くるま}を{f|買|か}う<b>つもりです</b>。
I'm planning to buy a car next year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来年|らいねん}{f|車|くるま}を{f|買|か}う<b>つもりで</b>、{f|毎月|まいつき}{f|少|すこ}しずつ{f|貯|た}めている。
Meaning to buy a car next year, I'm putting a little aside each month.
</pre>

The first sentence is finished at `です`; the second cannot stand without the clause it
introduces. If you find yourself ending a sentence on `つもりで`, the form you actually
wanted was `つもりだ` or `つもりです`.

### ～つもりで vs ～ふりをして

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `つもりで` is a frame of mind you adopt **for yourself**.
`ふりをする` is a pretence staged **for other people**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|寝|ね}た<b>ふりをして</b>、{f|母|はは}が{f|部屋|へや}を{f|出|で}ていくのを{f|待|ま}った。
I pretended to be asleep and waited for my mother to leave the room.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|休|やす}んだ<b>つもりで</b>、また{f|机|つくえ}に{f|向|む}かった。
I told myself I'd had my break and went back to my desk.
</pre>

Nobody is being fooled in the second sentence. `つもりで` is honest about being a
mental device, which is why it can appear in advice and encouragement, where
`ふりをする` would sound like an instruction to deceive.

### ～つもりで vs ～と{f|思|おも}って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>と{f|思|おも}って</code> reports what you believed. `つもりで` reports the
stance you deliberately took up.

</div>

<pre>
{f|練習|れんしゅう}だ<b>と{f|思|おも}って</b>{f|参加|さんか}したら、いきなり{f|本番|ほんばん}だった。
I turned up thinking it was a rehearsal and it was the real thing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|練習|れんしゅう}の<b>つもりで</b>、{f|気楽|きらく}に{f|話|はな}してみてください。
Treat it as practice and just talk without worrying.
</pre>

<code>と{f|思|おも}って</code> can be a mistaken belief - the first speaker was simply wrong.
`つもりで` cannot be a mistake in that sense, because it is chosen on purpose. That is
also why encouragement takes `つもりで`: you are being invited to adopt a stance, not
informed of a fact.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|新人|しんじん}<b>つもりで</b>{f|働|はたら}きます。 → ✅ {f|新人|しんじん}<b>のつもりで</b>{f|働|はたら}きます。
  <code>つもり</code> is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs `の`.

- ❌ {f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}く<b>つもりで</b>、そのお{f|金|かね}を{f|寄付|きふ}した。 → ✅ {f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}った<b>つもりで</b>、そのお{f|金|かね}を{f|寄付|きふ}した。
  For the "as if I already had" sense the verb must be past. The dictionary form gives
  a live intention, which contradicts donating the money instead.

- ❌ {f|来月|らいげつ}{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}す<b>つもりで</b>。 → ✅ {f|来月|らいげつ}{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}す<b>つもりです</b>。
  `つもりで` needs a following clause. On its own at the end of a sentence it leaves
  the listener waiting.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}が{f|来|く}る<b>つもりで</b>、{f|私|わたし}は{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|用意|ようい}した。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}が{f|来|く}ると{f|思|おも}って、{f|私|わたし}は{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|用意|ようい}した。
  The mindset belongs to whoever performs the main action. Someone else's intention has
  to be reported with <code>と{f|思|おも}って</code> or <code>らしいので</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|外国|がいこく}の{f|方|かた}に{f|説明|せつめい}する____、やさしい{f|言葉|ことば}を{f|使|つか}ってください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|説明|せつめい}する<b>つもりで</b>

The listener is being asked to adopt a stance before speaking, and `つもりで` is the
form that frames the following instruction rather than ending the sentence.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|病気|びょうき}の（つもりで・ふりをして）{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|休|やす}んだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|病気|びょうき}の<b>ふりをして</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|休|やす}んだ。

Taking a day off on a false illness is a performance aimed at other people, which is
`ふりをする`. `つもりで` would mean the speaker had privately decided to think of
themselves as ill, which explains nothing about the absence.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Save the money as if you had eaten out, and you'll have quite a sum by the end of the year."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|外食|がいしょく}した<b>つもりで</b>{f|貯|た}めておけば、{f|年末|ねんまつ}にはかなりの{f|額|がく}になりますよ。

The past form <code>{f|外食|がいしょく}した</code> is essential: the trick is to treat
money as already spent, not to plan a meal.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|新|あたら}しい{f|企画|きかく}を{f|出|だ}すつもりで。{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|説明|せつめい}します。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|新|あたら}しい{f|企画|きかく}を{f|出|だ}す<b>つもりです</b>。{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|説明|せつめい}します。

`つもりで` cannot close a sentence. To state the plan itself you need the predicate
form `つもりです`; `つもりで` would have to run straight on into what you did about it.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|一年|いちねん}で{f|完成|かんせい}させる<b>つもりで</b>{f|始|はじ}めた。
(b) {f|一年|いちねん}で{f|完成|かんせい}させた<b>つもりで</b>{f|始|はじ}めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain statement of the aim held at the outset: the speaker began the project
targeting one year, and the sentence usually goes on to say whether that held.

(b) is strange, and worth seeing as strange. A past `つもりで` means "as if it were
already done", so this says the speaker started the work while pretending it was
finished - which is why the dictionary form is the one you want for intentions.

</details>
