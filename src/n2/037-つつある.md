# ～つつある

**Meaning** · is in the process of changing - the shift has begun, has a direction, and is not finished

<code>つつある</code> is not a general "-ing". It reports a **change** that is under
way and heading somewhere: numbers rising, a patient recovering, a custom
disappearing. If nothing is changing, this is the wrong pattern, however ongoing
the action is.

## Formation

<code>つつある</code> follows the **ます-stem**, exactly like the older
<code>つつ</code> it is built from.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | ます-stem + つつある | {f|増|ふ}える → {f|増|ふ}えつつある |
| する-verb | ます-stem + つつある | {f|回復|かいふく}する → {f|回復|かいふく}しつつある |
| Polite | ます-stem + つつあります | {f|減|へ}る → {f|減|へ}りつつあります |
| Past | ます-stem + つつあった | {f|変|か}わる → {f|変|か}わりつつあった |
| Passive | passive ます-stem + つつある | {f|失|うしな}われる → {f|失|うしな}われつつある |
| Modifying a noun | ます-stem + つつある + Noun | {f|消|き}えつつある{f|方言|ほうげん} |
| ❌ Dictionary form | ❌{f|増|ふ}えるつつある | ✅{f|増|ふ}えつつある |
| ❌ Plain activity verb | ❌{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}みつつある | ✅{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}んでいる |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the verb must name a **change of state**: {f|増|ふ}える,
{f|減|へ}る, {f|変|か}わる, {f|進|すす}む, {f|広|ひろ}がる, {f|回復|かいふく}する,
{f|発展|はってん}する, {f|失|うしな}われる, {f|近|ちか}づく. Verbs of plain activity -
{f|読|よ}む, {f|食|た}べる, {f|走|はし}る - have no direction to move in and take
`ている` instead.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - do not confuse this with bare <code>つつ</code>, which is a separate,
literary connector meaning "while" or "although"
({f|悪|わる}いと{f|知|し}りつつ, "knowing it was wrong"). Same morpheme, same stem,
completely different job - and the giveaway is whether ある follows.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|町|まち}の{f|人口|じんこう}は{f|減|へ}り<b>つつある</b>。
This town's population is falling.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|景気|けいき}は{f|少|すこ}しずつ{f|回復|かいふく}し<b>つつあります</b>。
The economy is gradually recovering.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}は{f|北|きた}へ{f|進|すす}み<b>つつある</b>。
The typhoon is moving north.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|祖父|そふ}の{f|体調|たいちょう}は、ゆっくりとよくなり<b>つつある</b>。
My grandfather's condition is slowly improving.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|村|むら}に{f|古|ふる}くから{f|伝|つた}わる{f|習慣|しゅうかん}は{f|失|うしな}われ<b>つつある</b>。
The customs handed down in this village are being lost.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|働|はたら}き{f|方|かた}についての{f|考|かんが}え{f|方|かた}は、この10{f|年|ねん}で{f|大|おお}きく{f|変|か}わり<b>つつある</b>。
Attitudes to how we work have been changing a great deal over the past decade.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|再生|さいせい}エネルギーの{f|利用|りよう}は、{f|世界|せかい}{f|各国|かっこく}で{f|急速|きゅうそく}に{f|広|ひろ}がり<b>つつある</b>。
The use of renewable energy is spreading rapidly in countries around the world.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|治療法|ちりょうほう}のおかげで、この{f|病気|びょうき}は{f|治|なお}る{f|病気|びょうき}になり<b>つつある</b>。
Thanks to a new treatment, this illness is becoming a curable one.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a **written** pattern. Newspapers, white papers, academic prose, company
reports and formal speeches are its home ground, and it is one of the cheapest ways
to make a sentence sound like published Japanese. In conversation it is rare;
people say <code>だんだん{f|増|ふ}えている</code> or
<code>{f|増|ふ}えてきている</code> instead. Using it over dinner is not wrong, but
it will sound as though you are quoting a broadcast - which is occasionally the
joke people intend.

Its emotional temperature is low. Unlike several of its neighbours it passes no
judgement: a population can be falling and a patient recovering in the same
register. What it always insists on is that the change is **not complete**. That is
why it sits badly with {f|完全|かんぜん}に or もう, and why it so often appears with
{f|少|すこ}しずつ, ゆっくりと, {f|急速|きゅうそく}に and
{f|着実|ちゃくじつ}に - adverbs of rate, which is the only thing left to say once
direction is settled.

The noun-modifying use is worth practising:
<code>{f|失|うしな}われつつある{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}</code>, "skills that are being lost".
It is compact, it sounds adult, and learners almost never reach for it.

## Contrast

### ～つつある vs ～ている

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ている` covers **any** ongoing action or resulting state.
<code>つつある</code> covers only a **change in progress**, and adds that it has
further to go.

</div>

<pre>
{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|溶|と}け<b>ている</b>。
The snow is melting. / The snow has melted.
</pre>

That sentence is genuinely ambiguous in Japanese - it can describe the melting or
the puddle it left.

<pre>
{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|溶|と}け<b>つつある</b>。
The snow is in the process of melting.
</pre>

<code>つつある</code> removes the ambiguity: the snow is neither untouched nor gone,
and the sentence is a snapshot of the middle. Where `ている` would be ambiguous or
merely descriptive, <code>つつある</code> is precise and formal. Going the other
way, `ている` is the only option for an activity that is not a change -
❌{f|走|はし}りつつある is not Japanese.

### ～つつある vs ～{f|一方|いっぽう}だ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both describe change with a direction.
<code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> says it **keeps going and shows no sign of stopping**,
usually with dismay. <code>つつある</code> is **neutral** and often implies an
endpoint being approached.

</div>

<code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> is the N3 pattern this one sits closest to, and the
divergence is attitude plus endpoint.

<pre>
{f|医療費|いりょうひ}は{f|増|ふ}える<b>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</b>。
Medical costs just keep going up.
</pre>

There is a note of alarm there, and no suggestion of a ceiling.

<pre>
{f|待|ま}ち{f|時間|じかん}は{f|短|みじか}くなり<b>つつある</b>。
Waiting times are getting shorter.
</pre>

Flat report of a trend, with an implied destination - shorter, and heading towards
acceptable. Note the forms too: <code>{f|一方|いっぽう}だ</code> takes the dictionary
form, <code>つつある</code> takes the ます-stem.

### ～つつある vs ～つつ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with ある it means "**is changing**". Without ある it means
"**while**" or "**even though**", and needs a second clause.

</div>

<pre>
{f|体|からだ}に{f|悪|わる}いと{f|知|し}り<b>つつ</b>、{f|夜|よる}{f|遅|おそ}くまで{f|飲|の}んでしまった。
Even knowing it was bad for me, I drank until late.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|体力|たいりょく}は{f|落|お}ち<b>つつある</b>。
My fitness is declining.
</pre>

The two are built on the same stem and the same particle, which is why they get
mixed up on paper. The test is mechanical: if the sentence stops there,
<code>つつある</code>; if another clause follows and the sense is "while" or "even
so", bare <code>つつ</code>. Both are literary, and neither belongs in casual
speech.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|今|いま}レポートを{f|書|か}き<b>つつある</b>。 → ✅ {f|今|いま}レポートを{f|書|か}い<b>ている</b>。
  Writing a report is an activity, not a change of state. There is no direction for
  <code>つつある</code> to point in.

- ❌ {f|物価|ぶっか}が{f|上|あ}がる<b>つつある</b>。 → ✅ {f|物価|ぶっか}が{f|上|あ}がり<b>つつある</b>。
  The base is the ます-stem, never the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|大学生|だいがくせい}になり<b>つつある</b>。 → ✅ {f|来月|らいげつ}、{f|大学生|だいがくせい}になる。
  Becoming a student happens on one day. A change has to be gradual before it can
  be "in progress".

- ❌ {f|音楽|おんがく}を{f|聞|き}き<b>つつある</b>{f|勉強|べんきょう}する。 → ✅ {f|音楽|おんがく}を{f|聞|き}き<b>つつ</b>{f|勉強|べんきょう}する。
  "While listening" is bare <code>つつ</code>. Adding ある turns the clause into a
  claim that the listening is itself changing.

## Practice

**1.** {f|地球|ちきゅう}の{f|平均|へいきん}{f|気温|きおん}は{f|年々|ねんねん}{f|上昇|じょうしょう}し____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|上昇|じょうしょう}し<b>つつある</b>

<code>{f|上昇|じょうしょう}する</code> is a change with a direction, the subject is a
long-term trend, and the sentence is written in report register - three signals all
pointing the same way.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|弟|おとうと}は{f|今|いま}、{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|部屋|へや}でゲームを（しつつある・している）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|部屋|へや}でゲームを<b>している</b>。

Playing a game is an activity, not a change of state, so
<code>つつある</code> has nothing to work with. The casual subject matter rules it
out a second time.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The number of small bookshops in this area is steadily decreasing."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|地域|ちいき}の{f|小|ちい}さな{f|書店|しょてん}の{f|数|かず}は、{f|着実|ちゃくじつ}に{f|減|へ}り<b>つつある</b>。

A countable trend moving in one direction, described in written register. The
adverb {f|着実|ちゃくじつ}に is the kind of rate word this pattern attracts.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|地方|ちほう}の{f|方言|ほうげん}は{f|完全|かんぜん}に{f|消|き}えつつある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|地方|ちほう}の{f|方言|ほうげん}は{f|消|き}え<b>つつある</b>。（or {f|完全|かんぜん}に{f|消|き}えてしまった）

<code>つつある</code> states that the change is **not** finished, so
{f|完全|かんぜん}に contradicts it. Drop the adverb, or drop the pattern and say
outright that the dialect is gone.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|森|もり}が{f|減|へ}っ<b>ている</b>。
(b) {f|森|もり}が{f|減|へ}り<b>つつある</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the everyday statement. It reports the situation and would be at home in
conversation; on its own it can even be read as a settled result rather than an
ongoing process.

(b) fixes the sentence in the middle of a movement and is unambiguous about it -
the forest is neither intact nor gone. It also raises the register sharply, and
would be the natural choice in a report or a news script.

</details>
