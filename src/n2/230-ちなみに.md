# ちなみに

**Meaning** · incidentally; by the way; for your information

`ちなみに` hangs a footnote off what you have just said. The sentence it
introduces is extra, optional information that is still **about the same thing** - 
a price, a caveat, a related fact the listener may as well have.

## Usage

`ちなみに` is a conjunctive adverb. It stands at the **head of a sentence**,
normally followed by a comma, and joins that sentence to the one before it.

- ちなみに、{f|料金|りょうきん}は{f|前払|まえばら}いです。
- ちなみに{f|私|わたし}は{f|関西|かんさい}{f|出身|しゅっしん}です。

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the sentence after `ちなみに` must be a **side note on the
topic already running**. If you are changing the subject, the word you want is
`ところで`, not `ちなみに`.

</div>

Because it announces a supplement, `ちなみに` needs something in front of it to
supplement. Opening an email or a conversation with it is odd; it wants a
preceding statement to lean on. Two frames it likes:

- <code>ちなみに、～は…です</code> - adding a specification: hours, price, location.
- <code>ちなみに、～はどうですか</code> - a follow-up question on the same subject,
  common in interviews and small talk.

It is written in kana in practice. The kanji spelling {f|因|ちな}みに exists but is
rare outside older or deliberately formal prose.

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}は{f|水曜日|すいようび}が{f|休|やす}みです。<b>ちなみに</b>、{f|駐車場|ちゅうしゃじょう}はありません。
This shop is closed on Wednesdays. Incidentally, there's no car park.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>ちなみに</b>、{f|会費|かいひ}は{f|当日|とうじつ}{f|受付|うけつけ}でお{f|支払|しはら}いください。
For your information, the fee is payable at the desk on the day.
</pre>

{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|三時|さんじ}からです。<b>ちなみに</b>、{f|前回|ぜんかい}の{f|資料|しりょう}はもうメールで{f|送|おく}ってあります。

</pre>

This camera is cheap because it's last year's model. Just so you know, black is the only colour left.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-flexible, which is why it turns up everywhere. The same word works in a
business email ({f|以上|いじょう}、ご{f|確認|かくにん}ください。ちなみに…) and between
friends over lunch (ちなみに{f|俺|おれ}、{f|明日|あした}{f|休|やす}みだから). It is neither
stiff nor slangy, and it carries no age or gender marking.

What it does carry is a slight air of **volunteering** something. The speaker is
saying "you did not ask, but here it is", so it fits an aside, a clarification, or
a piece of trivia. Presenters and writers use it to slip in a supporting figure
without breaking the flow of the argument.

In speech you will also hear the padded <code>ちなみにですが、…</code> and
<code>ちなみになんですけど、…</code>. Both are ordinary polite conversation, softening
the abruptness of dropping in an extra question.

One thing it is not: `ちなみに` never carries the **main** point. If the fact you
are adding is the reason for the whole message, put it in a sentence of its own.
Introducing your actual request with `ちなみに` sounds evasive, as though you were
pretending it were an afterthought.

## Contrast

### ちなみに vs ところで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ちなみに` **stays** on the current topic and adds to it.
`ところで` **leaves** the current topic for a new one.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|新|あたら}しい{f|版|はん}が{f|出|で}ます。<b>ちなみに</b>、{f|値段|ねだん}は{f|変|か}わりません。
A new edition of this dictionary comes out next month. The price, incidentally, is unchanged.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|新|あたら}しい{f|版|はん}が{f|出|で}ます。<b>ところで</b>、{f|昼|ひる}ごはんはもう{f|食|た}べましたか。
A new edition of this dictionary comes out next month. Anyway, have you had lunch yet?
</pre>

Swap them and both sentences break. `ちなみに` in front of the lunch question
promises a connection that never arrives; `ところで` in front of the price
announces a change of subject and then does not change it.

Between them sits `そういえば`, "now that you mention it", which follows an
association triggered by what was said rather than adding information about it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ちなみに、{f|週末|しゅうまつ}{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}きませんか。 → ✅ <b>ところで</b>、{f|週末|しゅうまつ}{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}きませんか。
  An unrelated invitation is a change of topic. `ちなみに` needs the new sentence to
  be about what was already being discussed.

- ❌ ちなみにの{f|情報|じょうほう}ですが… → ✅ <b>{f|参考|さんこう}までに</b>{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げますと…
  `ちなみに` is an adverb and cannot take `の` to modify a noun. For "supplementary
  information" in formal writing, use <code>{f|参考|さんこう}まで(に)</code>.

- ❌ {f|値段|ねだん}は<b>ちなみに</b>{f|三千円|さんぜんえん}です。 → ✅ <b>ちなみに</b>、{f|値段|ねだん}は{f|三千円|さんぜんえん}です。
  It belongs at the front of its sentence, before the topic phrase, not buried in
  the middle of it.

## Practice

**1.** {f|今日|きょう}のセミナーは{f|六時|ろくじ}に{f|終|お}わります。____、{f|資料|しりょう}は{f|後|あと}で{f|全員|ぜんいん}に{f|送|おく}ります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>ちなみに</b>、{f|資料|しりょう}は{f|後|あと}で{f|全員|ぜんいん}に{f|送|おく}ります。

The second sentence is still about the same seminar, so it is a supplement rather
than a new topic. `ところで` would wrongly announce that the subject is about to
change.

</details>

**2.** Translate: "The museum is free on Mondays. Incidentally, photography is not
allowed inside."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|美術館|びじゅつかん}は{f|月曜日|げつようび}は{f|無料|むりょう}です。<b>ちなみに</b>、{f|館内|かんない}での{f|撮影|さつえい}は{f|禁止|きんし}されています。

Both sentences describe the same museum, which is exactly the relationship
`ちなみに` marks. Note that it opens its own sentence and takes a comma; it does
not join the two clauses into one.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ちなみに、{f|来年|らいねん}から{f|料金|りょうきん}が{f|上|あ}がります。
(b) {f|来年|らいねん}から{f|料金|りょうきん}が{f|上|あ}がります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b) is a plain announcement, and the price rise is the point of the message.

(a) frames the same fact as an aside attached to whatever was just said - useful
to know, but not what the speaker called you about. If the price rise really is
the headline, (a) sounds oddly casual about it, and a listener may miss it.

</details>
