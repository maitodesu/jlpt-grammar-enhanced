# 一旦

**Meaning** · いったん - once ～ happens (there is no going back); or: for the
moment

Two jobs, one word. Before a conditional, <code>{f|一旦|いったん}</code> marks a threshold:
cross it and the consequence is unavoidable. Before an ordinary verb it means
"for now", pausing something that will be resumed later.

## Usage

**Sense 1 - the threshold.** This use is grammatically incomplete without a
conditional. <code>{f|一旦|いったん}</code> opens the clause and the clause ends in
`と`, `たら`, `ば`, or - most idiomatically - `～てしまうと`.

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - {f|一旦|いったん}～と／たら needs a **second half that says the
result cannot be undone or stopped**. If nothing irreversible follows, the adverb
has nothing to do.

</div>

- {f|一旦|いったん}{f|決|き}めたら、{f|最後|さいご}までやる。
- {f|一旦|いったん}{f|燃|も}え{f|始|はじ}めると、{f|消|け}すのは{f|難|むずか}しい。
- {f|一旦|いったん}{f|口|くち}に{f|出|だ}してしまうと、もう{f|取|と}り{f|消|け}せない。

The typical second half is a statement of impossibility, of inevitability, or a
resolution: {f|難|むずか}しい, ～ない, もう～できない, {f|必|かなら}ず～.

**Sense 2 - the pause.** Here <code>{f|一旦|いったん}</code> is a plain adverb meaning "once,
for the moment, and then we'll continue": {f|一旦|いったん}{f|家|いえ}に{f|帰|かえ}る, {f|一旦|いったん}{f|中止|ちゅうし}する, {f|一旦|いったん}{f|保留|ほりゅう}にする. It implies the interruption is temporary and that
something will follow.

The two senses do not blur in practice: a conditional after it means sense 1, and
its absence means sense 2.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>{f|一旦|いったん}</b>{f|決|き}めたら、{f|途中|とちゅう}でやめないでください。
Once you've decided, please don't give up halfway.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|一旦|いったん}</b>{f|外|そと}に{f|出|で}て、{f|十分|じゅっぷん}{f|後|ご}にまた{f|集|あつ}まりましょう。
Let's step outside for now and gather again in ten minutes.
</pre>

<pre>
このアプリは<b>{f|一旦|いったん}</b>{f|使|つか}い{f|始|はじ}めると、なかなか{f|手放|てばな}せなくなる。
Once you start using this app you find you can't put it down.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|台風|たいふう}のため<b>{f|一旦|いったん}</b>{f|中止|ちゅうし}し、{f|天候|てんこう}が{f|回復|かいふく}してから{f|再開|さいかい}する{f|予定|よてい}です。
Work will be suspended for the time being because of the typhoon and is due to resume once the weather improves.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Written and formal-spoken. In sense 1 it sounds like a maxim - which is why it
shows up in proverbial statements, safety warnings and speeches - and in sense 2
it is standard business Japanese. 「{f|一旦|いったん}{f|持|も}ち{f|帰|かえ}って{f|検討|けんとう}します」("let me take this back to the office and consider it")
is one of the most heavily used sentences in Japanese corporate life.

In relaxed conversation the same ideas come out differently. For sense 1, a
friend says {f|一度|いちど}{f|始|はじ}めたら instead. For sense 2, they say
とりあえず or ちょっと - although note that 「{f|一旦|いったん}{f|帰|かえ}るね」is perfectly ordinary between adults; the word is formal, not
stiff.

Not marked for age or gender. It is always written with the kanji;
`いったん` in kana is unusual and can be misread as the noun {f|一端|いったん}
("one end; a part").

## Contrast

### {f|一旦|いったん} vs {f|一度|いちど}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|一度|いちど}</code> counts an occasion: **one time**.
<code>{f|一旦|いったん}</code> counts nothing - it marks a **switch being thrown**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|富士山|ふじさん}には<b>{f|一度|いちど}</b>{f|登|のぼ}ったことがあります。
I've climbed Mt Fuji once.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|一旦|いったん}</b>{f|登|のぼ}り{f|始|はじ}めたら、{f|頂上|ちょうじょう}まで{f|行|い}くしかない。
Once you start climbing there's nothing for it but to go to the top.
</pre>

Only <code>{f|一度|いちど}</code> can be a count - ❌{f|一旦|いったん}{f|行|い}ったことがあります is not Japanese. In conditional clauses the two overlap and
{f|一度|いちど}{f|決|き}めたら is the everyday version of the same thought;
<code>{f|一旦|いったん}</code> is the more formal and slightly more emphatic choice, and it
adds the sense that the decision was a point of no return rather than simply an
event that happened once.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|一旦|いったん}{f|京都|きょうと}に{f|行|い}ったことがあります。 → ✅ <b>{f|一度|いちど}</b>{f|京都|きょうと}に{f|行|い}ったことがあります。
  "Once" in the sense of "on one occasion" is {f|一度|いちど}. <code>{f|一旦|いったん}</code>
  never counts.

- ❌ {f|一旦|いったん}{f|始|はじ}めると{f|楽|たの}しいです。 → ✅ <b>{f|一旦|いったん}</b>{f|始|はじ}めると、やめられなくなります。
  The threshold reading needs a consequence you cannot escape. Simply being
  enjoyable is not one, and the sentence reads as a false alarm.

- ❌ {f|一旦|いったん}{f|決|き}めて、{f|変|か}えないでください。 → ✅ <b>{f|一旦|いったん}</b>{f|決|き}め<b>たら</b>、{f|変|か}えないでください。
  In sense 1 the て-form is not enough; the clause has to be a genuine conditional
  in と, たら or ば.

- ❌ {f|一旦|いったん}やめて、もうやりません。 → ✅ {f|一旦|いったん}やめて、{f|来月|らいげつ}また{f|始|はじ}めます。
  Sense 2 promises a resumption. Stopping permanently is just やめる.

## Practice

**1.** ____{f|習慣|しゅうかん}になってしまうと、{f|直|なお}すのは{f|大変|たいへん}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|一旦|いったん}</b>{f|習慣|しゅうかん}になってしまうと、{f|直|なお}すのは{f|大変|たいへん}だ。

The `～てしまうと` clause and the difficulty in the second half are the two halves
of the threshold frame: cross the line and the consequence follows whether you
like it or not.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: {f|沖縄|おきなわ}には（{f|一旦|いったん}・{f|一度|いちど}）{f|行|い}ったことがあります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|沖縄|おきなわ}には<b>{f|一度|いちど}</b>{f|行|い}ったことがあります。

The sentence counts visits, and only {f|一度|いちど} counts.
<code>{f|一旦|いったん}</code> marks a threshold and cannot be attached to a number of
occasions at all.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|一旦|いったん}{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|戻|もど}ります。
(b) {f|一旦|いったん}{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|戻|もど}ったら、もう{f|出|で}られません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the "for the moment" sense: the speaker is going back to the office and
will be out again, and this is ordinary business politeness. (b) is the threshold
sense, and the conditional is what creates it - going back becomes a line that,
once crossed, traps the speaker for the rest of the day. Same adverb, and the
ending decides which of its two lives it is leading.

</details>
