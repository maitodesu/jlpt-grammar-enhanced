# ～ねばならない

**Meaning** · must; have to - the written, weighty twin of なければならない

`ねばならない` says nothing that `なければならない` does not say. What it changes is
the voice. `ね` is an old negative form, and the whole expression carries the
gravity of that older Japanese: it belongs to editorials, speeches, principles and
resolutions rather than to arranging the afternoon.

## Formation

Take the ない-form, drop the `ない`, and add `ねばならない`. The same two verbs are
irregular as always.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ～ない stem + ねばならない | {f|守|まも}らない → {f|守|まも}らねばならない |
| Verb (る-verb) | ～ない stem + ねばならない | {f|決|き}めない → {f|決|き}めねばならない |
| Verb (する) | **せねばならない** | {f|対応|たいおう}する → {f|対応|たいおう}せねばならない |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | <b>{f|来|こ}ねばならない</b> | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ねばならない |
| Polite | ～ねばなりません | {f|決|き}めねばなりません |
| Past | ～ねばならなかった | {f|決|き}めねばならなかった |
| Archaic / literary | ～ねばならぬ | {f|働|はたら}かねばならぬ |
| Short, in speech | ～ねば | やらねば |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the stem is the same one that `ない` uses. ❌
<code>しねばならない</code> and ❌<code>{f|来|き}ねばならない</code> are the two
standard slips; the forms are <code>せねばならない</code> and
<code>{f|来|こ}ねばならない</code>.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the shortened <code>～ねば</code> survives on its own as a mutter of
resolve: そろそろ{f|行|い}かねば, 「やらねば」. Even there it sounds
half-theatrical, which is part of its charm; a speaker using it is aware of the
old-fashioned ring.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}までに{f|決|き}め<b>ねばならない</b>。
We have to decide by tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人|ひと}は{f|過去|かこ}から{f|学|まな}ば<b>ねばならない</b>。

</pre>

<pre>
この{f|問題|もんだい}については、もう{f|一度|いちど}{f|考|かんが}え{f|直|なお}さ<b>ねばなりません</b>。
This is a question we shall have to think through again.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|市民|しみん}の{f|安全|あんぜん}を{f|守|まも}るため、{f|市|し}は{f|速|すみ}やかに{f|対応|たいおう}せ<b>ねばならない</b>。
To keep its citizens safe, the city must act without delay.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自由|じゆう}には{f|責任|せきにん}が{f|伴|ともな}うということを、{f|心|こころ}に{f|留|と}め<b>ねばならない</b>。
We must bear in mind that freedom comes with responsibility.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げにくいのですが、{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|結果|けっか}は{f|残念|ざんねん}だと{f|言|い}わ<b>ねばなりません</b>。

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

Treat this as a **written** form first. Editorials, essays, policy documents,
company statements and the closing paragraph of a formal speech are where it lives.
Reading a newspaper column you will meet
{f|考|かんが}えねばならない and {f|問|と}わねばならない constantly.

In speech it is much narrower. It turns up in prepared remarks, in the speech of
some older men, in period drama, and as a deliberate flourish - a colleague sighing
「そろそろ{f|帰|かえ}らねば」 knows exactly how old-fashioned it sounds. Said to a
friend about an errand, ❌<code>{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}かねばならない</code>
is not wrong, but it lands like announcing a solemn duty to buy milk. The everyday
forms are なきゃ, ないと and なければならない.

There is also a difference in **weight**. Because `ねばならない` is the form used
for principles - {f|守|まも}らねばならない{f|約束|やくそく},
{f|果|は}たさねばならない{f|責任|せきにん} - it tends to frame its obligation as a
matter of duty rather than of schedule. That is a tendency and not a rule: a
newspaper will happily write {f|提出|ていしゅつ}せねばならない about a form. But
if you want a sentence to sound like an appointment rather than a moral
imperative, `なければならない` is the safer choice.

The variant `ねばならぬ` is a step older again. It belongs to fiction, proverbs and
deliberate archaism; recognise it, and leave it alone in your own writing.

## Contrast

### ～ねばならない vs ～なければならない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register. `なければならない` is the
neutral standard; `ねばならない` is its formal, literary counterpart.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|七時|しちじ}に{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}<b>なければなりません</b>。
Tomorrow I have to leave the house at seven.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|我々|われわれ}は{f|次|つぎ}の{f|世代|せだい}に{f|何|なに}を{f|残|のこ}すのかを{f|問|と}わ<b>ねばならない</b>。
We must ask ourselves what we are leaving to the next generation.
</pre>

Neither could comfortably take the other's sentence. A seven o'clock start is a
schedule, and dressing it in `ねばならない` is comic; a question about the next
generation is exactly the register `ねばならない` was made for. Note that in casual
speech both compress - なければ→なきゃ, ねば→ねば as it stands - but only the first
compression is ordinary conversation.

### ～ねばならない vs ～べきだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ねばならない` says there is **no way around it**. `べきだ` says
it is **the right thing to do**, and leaves the choice open.

</div>

<pre>
{f|規則|きそく}である{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|従|したが}わ<b>ねばならない</b>。
As it is a rule, everyone must follow it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|間違|まちが}いに{f|気|き}づいたなら、すぐに{f|報告|ほうこく}する<b>べきだ</b>。
If you have noticed a mistake, you ought to report it at once.
</pre>

`べきだ` is a judgement the speaker is offering; someone could disagree with it and
the sentence would still stand. `ねばならない` presents the obligation as already
settled by the rule, the deadline or the situation. The two overlap most in
editorial writing, where both appear in the same paragraph - and even there,
`べきだ` is the writer's opinion and `ねばならない` is the writer's premise.

### ～ねばならない vs ～ざるを{f|得|え}ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ねばならない` is a **standing obligation**, and says nothing
about how the speaker feels. <code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> is a **reluctant
response to what has just happened**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会員|かいいん}は{f|毎年|まいとし}{f|会費|かいひ}を{f|納|おさ}め<b>ねばならない</b>。
Members are required to pay the annual fee.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|参加者|さんかしゃ}が{f|集|あつ}まらず、{f|大会|たいかい}は{f|延期|えんき}せざるを{f|得|え}なかった。
Not enough people signed up, so we had no choice but to postpone the event.
</pre>

The first is a rule in the constitution of a club; it would be printed on a form.
The second is what somebody decided last week, unwillingly, because reality gave
way. The giveaway is that <code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> nearly always has a
cause attached to it, while `ねばならない` often stands alone.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|報告|ほうこく}<b>しねばならない</b> → ✅ {f|報告|ほうこく}<b>せねばならない</b>
  `する` uses the old stem `せ` in front of `ねば`, exactly as it does in
  <code>せずに</code> and <code>せざるを{f|得|え}ない</code>.

- ❌ {f|決|き}める<b>ねばならない</b> → ✅ {f|決|き}め<b>ねばならない</b>
  The base is the ない-stem, not the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|決|き}めねば<b>ならないです</b>。 → ✅ {f|決|き}めねば<b>なりません</b>。
  A literary frame with a casual polite ending is the worst of both. The polite
  form built into the pattern is <code>なりません</code>.

- ❌ ごめん、そろそろ{f|帰|かえ}ら<b>ねばならない</b>。（to a friend） → ✅ ごめん、そろそろ{f|帰|かえ}ら<b>なきゃ</b>。
  Not ungrammatical, but the register is wrong by a wide margin. Among friends the
  short forms are what you want.

## Practice

**1.** {f|安全|あんぜん}の{f|確認|かくにん}が{f|取|と}れるまで、{f|運転|うんてん}は{f|見合|みあ}わせ____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|見合|みあ}わせ<b>ねばならない</b>（or {f|見合|みあ}わせねばなりません）

A public announcement about suspending services is squarely in this pattern's
register. The base is the ない-stem {f|見合|みあ}わせ, from
<code>{f|見合|みあ}わせる</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|明日|あした}、{f|歯医者|はいしゃ}に（{f|行|い}かねばならない・{f|行|い}かなきゃいけない）んだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|明日|あした}、{f|歯医者|はいしゃ}に<b>{f|行|い}かなきゃ</b>いけないんだ。

A dentist's appointment mentioned in conversation calls for the casual form. The
`んだ` ending in the question is itself casual, and `ねばならない` clashes with it -
mixing a literary obligation form with colloquial sentence-ending particles is one
of the clearest register errors a learner can make.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "We must not forget that this problem concerns everyone."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|問題|もんだい}が{f|全|すべ}ての{f|人|ひと}に{f|関|かか}わることを、{f|我々|われわれ}は{f|忘|わす}れては<b>ならない</b>。

Note the trap: "must not forget" is a prohibition, and prohibitions take
<code>てはならない</code>. ❌<code>{f|忘|わす}れねばならない</code> would mean
"must forget", the opposite of the English.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|委員会|いいんかい}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}までに{f|結論|けつろん}を{f|出|だ}すねばなりません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|委員会|いいんかい}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}までに{f|結論|けつろん}を{f|出|だ}さ<b>ねばなりません</b>。
"The committee must reach a conclusion by next week."

<code>{f|出|だ}す</code> is an う-verb, so its ない-stem is
<code>{f|出|だ}さ</code>. The dictionary form cannot stand in front of `ねば`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|環境|かんきょう}を{f|守|まも}ら<b>ねばならない</b>。
(b) {f|環境|かんきょう}を{f|守|まも}る<b>べきだ</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) treats protecting the environment as a duty that is not up for discussion. It
reads as a premise the writer expects the reader to share, and its register is that
of a speech or a leading article.

(b) puts the same content forward as the speaker's position: this is what we ought
to do. It invites agreement or argument in a way that (a) does not, and it is
noticeably easier to say out loud in ordinary conversation.

</details>
