# および・ならびに

**Meaning** · および / ならびに - and; as well as (formal written enumeration)

Two connectives you will read far more often than you will say. <code>{f|及|およ}び</code>
joins items in official prose - forms, notices, regulations, manuals - and
<code>{f|並|なら}びに</code> does the same job one level up, joining groups rather than
items.

## Usage

Both connect **nouns and noun phrases**. They stand between the items, never at
the head of a sentence.

- {f|住所|じゅうしょ}{f|及|およ}び{f|氏名|しめい}
- {f|保護者|ほごしゃ}{f|並|なら}びに{f|関係者|かんけいしゃ}の{f|皆様|みなさま}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - when a list has **two levels**, legal drafting puts
<code>{f|及|およ}び</code> on the **inner, smaller** grouping and <code>{f|並|なら}びに</code> on the
**outer, larger** one.

</div>

So 「A{f|及|およ}びB{f|並|なら}びにC」parses as (A and B) and C - never as A and (B
and C). This is a fixed convention of Japanese statutory drafting, and it is worth
knowing because it is the only way to read a long clause correctly. The
corresponding pair for "or" is arranged the **opposite** way round:
{f|又|また}は takes the larger grouping and {f|若|も}しくは the smaller. Do not
assume the two systems mirror each other, because they do not.

Outside legal text the distinction relaxes. In speeches and formal announcements
<code>{f|並|なら}びに</code> is simply the grander of the two, used when the writer wants
weight: ご{f|来賓|らいひん}の{f|皆様|みなさま}{f|並|なら}びにご{f|家族|かぞく}の{f|皆様|みなさま}.

Statutes also let <code>{f|及|およ}び</code> join predicates, with a comma:
「{f|報告|ほうこく}し、{f|及|およ}び{f|記録|きろく}する」. Recognise it; do not
imitate it. Treat both words as noun connectors and you will never be wrong in
ordinary formal writing.

Please enter your address and full name on the application form.
</pre>

Eating, drinking and smoking are not permitted inside the building.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本日|ほんじつ}は{f|卒業生|そつぎょうせい}<b>{f|並|なら}びに</b>ご{f|家族|かぞく}の{f|皆様|みなさま}にお{f|集|あつ}まりいただきました。
Today we are joined by our graduating students and by their families.
</pre>

Articles 1 and 2, together with the supplementary provisions, shall apply from the next fiscal year.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

These are reading words. <code>{f|及|およ}び</code> appears on every other government form,
in contracts, in academic papers, in product manuals and on public signage, and it
is completely standard there. Said out loud in conversation it is comic - the
equivalent of saying "and/or the aforementioned" to a friend.

What you would say instead, in order of formality:

- Speech, everyday: {f|住所|じゅうしょ}<b>と</b>{f|名前|なまえ} - `と` is the plain
  connector for a closed list, `や` for an open one
- Speech, polite or presentational: {f|住所|じゅうしょ}<b>と</b>{f|名前|なまえ}、
  or それに to add an item
- Writing, formal but not legal: {f|及|およ}び

<code>{f|並|なら}びに</code> is narrower still. Outside statutes you will meet it mainly in
ceremonial address - opening remarks at a graduation, an awards citation, a
formal letter of thanks - where its function is largely to sound solemn. Reading
it aloud is normal in exactly those settings and nowhere else.

Neither word is old-fashioned or marked for age or gender; they are marked for
**genre**. And neither is a sentence-opener: a paragraph that begins "And in
addition…" starts with また or なお in Japanese, not with {f|及|およ}び.

## Contrast

### {f|及|およ}び・{f|並|なら}びに vs と・や

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different genre. `と` and `や` are what people
say; <code>{f|及|およ}び</code> and <code>{f|並|なら}びに</code> are what documents print.

</div>

<pre>
パスポート<b>と</b>{f|航空券|こうくうけん}を{f|持|も}ってきてね。
Bring your passport and your ticket.
</pre>

<pre>
パスポート<b>{f|及|およ}び</b>{f|航空券|こうくうけん}をご{f|持参|じさん}ください。
Please bring your passport and air ticket with you.
</pre>

One difference is not only stylistic. `と` gives an exhaustive list and `や` an
illustrative one; <code>{f|及|およ}び</code> is exhaustive like `と`, which is precisely why
legal drafting uses it. If a notice says A{f|及|およ}びB, the list is A and B and
that is all.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|山田|やまだ}さん{f|及|およ}び{f|田中|たなか}さんと{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}った。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|山田|やまだ}さん<b>と</b>{f|田中|たなか}さんと{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}った。
  Grammatical and absurd. A night out is not a regulation; use `と`.

- ❌ {f|及|およ}び、{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|変更|へんこう}になりました。 → ✅ <b>なお</b>、{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|変更|へんこう}になりました。
  It connects items inside a sentence and cannot open one. "In addition" at the
  head of a sentence is なお or また.

- ❌ {f|会員|かいいん}{f|並|なら}びに{f|家族|かぞく}{f|及|およ}び{f|友人|ゆうじん} → ✅ {f|会員|かいいん}{f|及|およ}び{f|家族|かぞく}<b>{f|並|なら}びに</b>{f|友人|ゆうじん}
  The levels are the wrong way round. In a two-level list <code>{f|及|およ}び</code> binds
  the inner pair and <code>{f|並|なら}びに</code> joins that pair to the rest.

- ❌ {f|平日|へいじつ}{f|及|およ}び{f|土曜日|どようび}などに{f|営業|えいぎょう}しています。 → ✅ {f|平日|へいじつ}<b>や</b>{f|土曜日|どようび}などに{f|営業|えいぎょう}しています。
  <code>{f|及|およ}び</code> gives a complete list, so pairing it with など - "and so on" -
  contradicts itself.

## Practice

**1.** {f|受験|じゅけん}の{f|際|さい}は、{f|受験票|じゅけんひょう}____{f|身分証明書|みぶんしょうめいしょ}をご{f|持参|じさん}ください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|受験票|じゅけんひょう}<b>{f|及|およ}び</b>{f|身分証明書|みぶんしょうめいしょ}をご{f|持参|じさん}ください。

This is printed instruction to candidates, which is exactly the genre the word
belongs to, and the list is meant to be complete. In speech the same sentence
would use `と`.

</details>

**2.** How would you say the same thing to a friend?
{f|履歴書|りれきしょ}{f|及|およ}び{f|証明写真|しょうめいしゃしん}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|履歴書|りれきしょ}<b>と</b>{f|証明写真|しょうめいしゃしん}が{f|要|い}るよ。

Swap the written connector for `と` and let the rest of the sentence come down to
match. Keeping {f|及|およ}び in casual speech sounds like reading the form aloud
instead of talking.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|社員|しゃいん}{f|及|およ}びその{f|家族|かぞく}
(b) {f|社員|しゃいん}{f|並|なら}びにその{f|家族|かぞく}

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In an ordinary notice the two are interchangeable and (b) simply sounds more
ceremonial - the phrasing of a speech rather than a memo. Inside a statute or a
formal set of rules the choice is structural: (b) signals that the two items are
being joined at the higher of two levels, so the reader expects a
{f|及|およ}び-joined pair somewhere else in the same clause.

</details>
