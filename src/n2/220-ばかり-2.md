# ばかり - about; approximately

**Meaning** · about; roughly; some ～ (after a stated quantity)

Put `ばかり` after a number and it stops meaning "only" and starts meaning
"about": {f|三日|みっか}ばかり is "three days or so". This is the oldest use of the
word and the one a learner is most likely to meet in print rather than in
conversation.

## Formation

`ばかり` follows the whole quantity expression - number plus counter - and never
comes in front of it.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Number + counter | {f|数量|すうりょう} + ばかり | {f|三日|みっか} → {f|三日|みっか}ばかり |
| Amount of money or measure | {f|数量|すうりょう} + ばかり | {f|千円|せんえん} → {f|千円|せんえん}ばかり |
| Quantity adverb | {f|少|すこ}し・ちょっと + ばかり | {f|少|すこ}し → {f|少|すこ}しばかり |
| Quantity + following particle | {f|数量|すうりょう} + ばかり + で・の・{f|前|まえ}に | {f|一週間|いっしゅうかん} → {f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}ばかり{f|前|まえ}に |

Note the last row: `ばかり` slots in where the quantity sat, and any particle the
sentence needed still follows it. The direct-object `を` is normally left out -
{f|水|みず}を{f|一杯|いっぱい}ばかり{f|飲|の}んだ, not ❌{f|一杯|いっぱい}ばかりを.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}まで{f|十分|じゅっぷん}<b>ばかり</b>かかります。
It takes about ten minutes to get to the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|少|すこ}し<b>ばかり</b>お{f|金|かね}を{f|貸|か}してくれませんか。
Could you lend me just a little money?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|二日|ふつか}<b>ばかり</b>{f|休|やす}ませていただきます。
I'll be taking a couple of days off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}<b>ばかり</b>{f|前|まえ}に{f|届|とど}きました。
The parcel arrived about half an hour ago.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}には{f|二十人|にじゅうにん}<b>ばかり</b>{f|集|あつ}まっていた。
Some twenty people had gathered in the hall.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|心|こころ}<b>ばかり</b>の{f|品|しな}ですが、どうぞお{f|受|う}け{f|取|と}りください。
It's the smallest of tokens, but please do accept it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|学生|がくせい}のころ、{f|一年|いちねん}<b>ばかり</b>{f|大阪|おおさか}に{f|住|す}んでいたことがあります。
When I was a student I lived in Osaka for a year or so.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|祖母|そぼ}は{f|七十年|ななじゅうねん}<b>ばかり</b>{f|前|まえ}の{f|話|はなし}を、きのうのことのように{f|語|かた}り{f|始|はじ}めた。
My grandmother began telling a story from some seventy years ago as if it had happened yesterday.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This use of `ばかり` is bookish and a little old-fashioned in speech. You will meet
it constantly in novels, in translated literature, in formal letters, and in the
speech of older characters; a person under forty ordering coffee is far more
likely to say {f|十分|じゅっぷん}ぐらい than {f|十分|じゅっぷん}ばかり. Using it is
not rude and not wrong - it simply reads as a shade literary, the way "some
twenty people" does in English next to "about twenty people".

It also tends to imply that the amount is **modest**, which plain
approximation with ほど does not. That flavour is why the word survives in a
handful of humble set expressions that are still fully alive today:
{f|少|すこ}しばかり ("just a little"), {f|心|こころ}ばかりの{f|品|しな}
("a mere token", written on a gift), {f|形|かたち}ばかりの{f|式|しき}
("a ceremony in name only"). Learn those as units - they are the most likely place
you will actually produce this pattern.

Written in kana. The kanji spelling <code>許り</code> exists in older texts and is essentially
never used now. There is no politeness difference between the plain and ます
forms of the surrounding sentence: `ばかり` itself carries the register, not the
verb ending.

Because the same four kana carry the "only; nothing but" meaning, context does all
the disambiguating - and the context is simply whether a quantity precedes it.

## Contrast

### {f|数量|すうりょう}ばかり vs ほど・ぐらい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - all three approximate a quantity. `ぐらい` is the everyday
spoken one, `ほど` the neutral written one, and `ばかり` the literary one that
also hints the amount is small.

</div>

<pre>
{f|三日|みっか}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|待|ま}ってください。
Please wait about three days.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三日|みっか}<b>ばかり</b>お{f|時間|じかん}をいただけますか。
Might I have three days or so of your time?
</pre>

The two sentences describe the same interval. The second sounds like a letter,
and its `ばかり` quietly presents three days as not much to ask. If you want a
neutral approximation in writing with no such colouring, ほど is the safe default.

### ばかり - "about" vs "only"

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - after a **quantity**, `ばかり` means "about". After a **noun or
a verb**, it means "only; nothing but".

</div>

<pre>
{f|三日|みっか}<b>ばかり</b>{f|東京|とうきょう}にいました。
I was in Tokyo for about three days.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|東京|とうきょう}では{f|仕事|しごと}<b>ばかり</b>していました。
In Tokyo I did nothing but work.
</pre>

The boundary is not perfectly sharp - {f|一人|ひとり}ばかり can be read either way
in isolation - but in practice a counter in front of `ばかり` forces the
approximate reading, and a bare noun forces the exclusive one.

### {f|数量|すうりょう}ばかり vs ～たばかり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ばかり` after the **plain past form of a verb** is a third,
unrelated pattern meaning "have just done".

</div>

<pre>
{f|一時間|いちじかん}<b>ばかり</b>{f|寝|ね}ました。
I slept for an hour or so.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}{f|起|お}き<b>たばかり</b>です。
I've only just woken up.
</pre>

Three patterns, one word. The way to keep them apart is to look at what sits
immediately before `ばかり`: a counter gives "about", a plain past verb gives "just
now", anything else gives "only".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ばかり{f|三日|みっか}かかります。 → ✅ <b>{f|三日|みっか}ばかり</b>かかります。
  `ばかり` always follows the quantity. Nothing in Japanese puts an approximator in
  front of the number the way English puts "about" there.

- ❌ {f|何人|なんにん}ばかり{f|来|き}ましたか。 → ✅ {f|何人|なんにん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|来|き}ましたか。
  Approximating `ばかり` states an amount; it cannot combine with a question word.
  For "roughly how many", use ぐらい or ほど.

- ❌ この{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|重|おも}いばかりです。 → ✅ この{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|少|すこ}し{f|重|おも}いです。
  It approximates quantities, not degrees, so it cannot be hung on an adjective to
  mean "fairly heavy". (The sentence as written is grammatical, but it means "this
  parcel is nothing but heavy".)

- ❌ コーヒーを{f|一杯|いっぱい}ばかりを{f|飲|の}んだ。 → ✅ コーヒーを<b>{f|一杯|いっぱい}ばかり</b>{f|飲|の}んだ。
  Do not add a second `を`. The quantity phrase already carries the one the
  sentence needs.

## Practice

**1.** すみません、{f|五分|ごふん}____{f|待|ま}っていただけますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|五分|ごふん}<b>ばかり</b>{f|待|ま}っていただけますか。

A quantity precedes, so `ばかり` reads as "about five minutes", and the modest
flavour suits a request. In everyday speech ぐらい would be the ordinary choice.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: {f|昨日|きのう}のパーティーには{f|何人|なんにん}（ばかり・ぐらい）{f|来|き}ましたか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|何人|なんにん}<b>ぐらい</b>{f|来|き}ましたか。

The sentence asks for a number rather than supplying one, and approximating
`ばかり` needs an actual quantity in front of it. ほど would also work; `ばかり`
would not.

</details>

**3.** Translate: "I waited about two hours, then went home."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|二時間|にじかん}<b>ばかり</b>{f|待|ま}って、{f|家|いえ}に{f|帰|かえ}りました。

The quantity comes first and `ばかり` follows it. {f|二時間|にじかん}ほど or
{f|二時間|にじかん}ぐらい are equally correct and sound more contemporary.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|机|つくえ}の{f|上|うえ}にばかり{f|十冊|じゅっさつ}の{f|本|ほん}があった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|机|つくえ}の{f|上|うえ}に{f|本|ほん}が<b>{f|十冊|じゅっさつ}ばかり</b>あった。

Two repairs. `ばかり` has to come after the counter, not before it, and once it
does the natural word order puts the counted noun ahead of the quantity phrase.
As originally written the sentence says the books were on the desk and nowhere
else.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|三日|みっか}<b>ばかり</b>{f|休|やす}みました。
(b) {f|三日|みっか}{f|休|やす}ん<b>だばかり</b>です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is about the length of the break: roughly three days, and the speaker presents
that as not very long. (b) is about when the break ended: the three days are over
and the speaker has only just come back. The counter in (a) and the plain past
form in (b) are what select the two readings.

</details>
