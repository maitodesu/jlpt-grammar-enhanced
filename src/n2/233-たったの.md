# たった(の)

**Meaning** · only; a mere; no more than - said of an amount the speaker finds
startlingly small

`たった` sits in front of a number and tells you the speaker's reaction to it. The
figure itself is neutral; `たった` is the raised eyebrow. `たったの` is the same
word with the emphasis turned up.

## Usage

`たった` modifies a **counted amount** - a number plus its counter, a sum of money,
a length of time. It cannot modify a bare noun, a verb or an adjective.

- たった{f|一人|ひとり}、たった{f|三日|みっか}、たった{f|五分|ごふん}
- たったの{f|百円|ひゃくえん}、たったの{f|二回|にかい}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if you cannot count it, `たった` cannot touch it. Behind it
there is always a number.

</div>

The optional `の` is pure emphasis and slightly more colloquial. Both
<code>たった{f|千円|せんえん}</code> and <code>たったの{f|千円|せんえん}</code> are
correct; the second sounds a shade more indignant, and it is the usual choice when
the amount stands alone as a comment - 「これで<code>たったの{f|千円|せんえん}</code>?」

Two partners do most of the work with it:

- <code>たった～しか…ない</code> - {f|三人|さんにん}しか{f|来|こ}なかった becomes
  たった{f|三人|さんにん}しか{f|来|こ}なかった, with the disappointment made explicit.
- <code>たった～で</code> - the small amount as the means or the elapsed time:
  たった{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}で{f|覚|おぼ}えた.

<code>たった{f|今|いま}</code> is a separate fixed expression meaning "just this
moment" ({f|今|いま}{f|着|つ}いたところ). It is not this pattern, and no other time
word can be substituted into it.

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|本|ほん}は<b>たった</b>{f|三日|みっか}で{f|書|か}かれたそうだ。
Apparently this book was written in a mere three days.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|財布|さいふ}には<b>たったの</b>{f|百円|ひゃくえん}しか{f|残|のこ}っていなかった。
There was only a hundred yen left in my wallet.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}を<b>たった</b>{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|回|まわ}しているんですか。それはすごいですね。
You run this shop entirely on your own? That's really something.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|十年|じゅうねん}かけて{f|準備|じゅんび}した{f|計画|けいかく}が、<b>たったの</b>{f|一言|ひとこと}で{f|白紙|はくし}に{f|戻|もど}った。
A plan ten years in the making was wiped off the table by a single remark.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and strongly **emotional**. Speech and writing both use it
freely, but it always editorialises: the speaker is amazed, indignant, or
impressed that so little was enough. A neutral report of the same figure simply
leaves `たった` out.

Which emotion depends on the sentence. <code>たった{f|三日|みっか}で{f|完成|かんせい}した</code>
is admiration - so little time, and yet. <code>たった{f|三日|みっか}しか
{f|休|やす}めない</code> is complaint - so little, and that is all there is. The word
does not choose for you; the predicate does.

Its formal written counterpart is <code>わずか</code>, which says the same thing
without raising its voice: a newspaper writes
<code>わずか{f|三日|みっか}で</code>, a person says
<code>たった{f|三日|みっか}で</code>. `ほんの` is the third member of the family and
covers small amounts that are **not counted** - ほんの{f|少|すこ}し, ほんの{f|一部|いちぶ} - 
exactly the territory `たった` cannot enter.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>たった</b>{f|水|みず}を{f|飲|の}んだ。 → ✅ <b>ただの</b>{f|水|みず}を{f|飲|の}んだ。
  `たった` needs a number. <code>ただの</code> is a different word meaning "plain,
  ordinary", and the two are one small `っ` apart.

- ❌ <b>たった</b>{f|少|すこ}ししか{f|食|た}べていない。 → ✅ <b>ほんの</b>{f|少|すこ}ししか{f|食|た}べていない。
  <code>{f|少|すこ}し</code> is a vague quantity, not a counted one. The natural
  partner for it is `ほんの`.

- ❌ この{f|部屋|へや}は<b>たった</b>{f|狭|せま}い。 → ✅ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|狭|せま}い。
  `たった` is not a general intensifier and cannot modify an adjective. It scales
  amounts, not qualities.

- ❌ <b>たった</b>{f|昨日|きのう}{f|着|つ}いたばかりだ。 → ✅ <b>たった{f|今|いま}</b>{f|着|つ}いたばかりだ。
  <code>たった{f|今|いま}</code> is frozen. You cannot swap in another time word and
  expect it to mean "just".

## Practice

**1.** {f|試験|しけん}に{f|合格|ごうかく}したのは、{f|三十人|さんじゅうにん}のうち____{f|二人|ふたり}だけだった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|三十人|さんじゅうにん}のうち<b>たった</b>{f|二人|ふたり}だけだった。

Two out of thirty is a counted amount, and the sentence is clearly commenting on
how few that is. <code>たったの{f|二人|ふたり}</code> works too and sounds a little
more incredulous.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: この{f|仕事|しごと}はたった{f|大変|たいへん}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|大変|たいへん}です。

`たった` cannot modify a な-adjective - or any adjective. If the intended meaning
was "this job takes only a short time", the number has to be there:
<code>この{f|仕事|しごと}はたった{f|十分|じゅっぷん}で{f|終|お}わります</code>.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|五分|ごふん}{f|待|ま}ってください。
(b) たったの{f|五分|ごふん}{f|待|ま}ってください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain request: give me five minutes.

(b) argues with the listener before they have refused. `たったの` frames five
minutes as a trivial amount, so the sentence reads "it's only five minutes - just
wait". It presses, and with a stranger or a superior that pressure is audible.

</details>
