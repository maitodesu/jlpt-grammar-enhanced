# ～にしても～にしても

**Meaning** · whether A or B; either way - two alternatives are named, both are granted, and the same thing holds for both

Doubling `にしても` turns a single concession into a pair of them. You put both options
on the table - go or don't go, buy or rent, meat or fish - concede each in turn, and
then state the one thing that is true whichever way it falls. The main clause is
always something that survives the choice.

On this batch's two axes - how much the speaker minds, and how formal the frame is - 
this is the *least bothered* and the *most conversational* of the paired concessives.
It is not an argument; it is somebody being practical.

## Formation

Each half takes a plain form or a bare noun, and the two halves must be built the same
way.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb + its negative | V する + にしても + V しない + にしても | {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}くにしても{f|行|い}かないにしても |
| Two verbs | V + にしても + V + にしても | {f|買|か}う・{f|借|か}りる → {f|買|か}うにしても{f|借|か}りるにしても |
| Two nouns | N + にしても + N + にしても | {f|肉|にく}・{f|魚|さかな} → {f|肉|にく}にしても{f|魚|さかな}にしても |
| Two い-adjectives | Adj + にしても + Adj + にしても | {f|高|たか}い・{f|安|やす}い → {f|高|たか}いにしても{f|安|やす}いにしても |
| Two な-adjectives | stem + にしても（×2） | {f|得意|とくい}・{f|苦手|にがて} → {f|得意|とくい}にしても{f|苦手|にがて}にしても |
| Formal written | N + であるにしても（×2） | {f|事実|じじつ}・うそ → {f|事実|じじつ}であるにしてもうそであるにしても |
| Three or more items | A にしても B にしても C にしても | {f|朝|あさ}にしても{f|昼|ひる}にしても{f|夜|よる}にしても |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the two alternatives must **cover the ground**, and the main clause
must be true of **both**. ❌<code>{f|行|い}くにしても{f|行|い}かないにしても、{f|電車|でんしゃ}で{f|行|い}こう</code>
is nonsense: you cannot take the train if you are not going.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - keep the frame parallel. Both halves take `にしても`, both stay in the same
tense, and both are the same part of speech. Mixing in `にしろ` or `ても` for the second
half is a common learner slip and reads as carelessness rather than variety.

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is the everyday member of a family of three. `にしても…にしても` is what people say
out loud; `にしろ…にしろ` is a notch more formal; `にせよ…にせよ` belongs in writing. All
three mean the same thing, and swapping between them changes only how the sentence
sounds in the room.

It is emotionally flat, and that is a virtue. Because it concedes both options equally,
it does not commit the speaker to either, which makes it the natural way to give
advice without taking sides:
<code>{f|続|つづ}けるにしてもやめるにしても、{f|一度|いちど}ご{f|両親|りょうしん}と{f|話|はな}したほうがいい</code>.
Nobody is being criticised, and no option is being pushed.

The pair is often used with a verb and its own negative, and that version is the most
common of all in speech, because it covers every case: <code>やるにしてもやらないにしても</code>,
<code>{f|来|く}るにしても{f|来|こ}ないにしても</code>. Note the irregular verb:
the stem vowel shifts, so it is <code>{f|来|く}る</code> in the positive and
<code>{f|来|こ}ない</code> in the negative.

In fast speech the second `にしても` is sometimes reduced or dropped altogether, leaving
<code>{f|行|い}くにしても{f|行|い}かなくても</code> or just a rising intonation on the
first half. Recognise it, but write the full frame.

## Contrast

### にしても…にしても vs にしろ…にしろ・にせよ…にせよ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning; the register climbs `にしても` → `にしろ` →
`にせよ`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|参加|さんか}する<b>にしても</b>しない<b>にしても</b>、{f|金曜日|きんようび}までに{f|教|おし}えて。
Coming or not, tell me by Friday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|採用|さいよう}する<b>にせよ</b>{f|見送|みおく}る<b>にせよ</b>、{f|理由|りゆう}を{f|文書|ぶんしょ}で{f|残|のこ}すこと。
Whether the candidate is hired or passed over, the reasons are to be recorded in writing.
</pre>

The second sentence is an instruction in a manual; nothing but `にせよ` would fit it.
The first is a message to a friend, and `にせよ` there would be absurd.

### にしても…にしても vs ～ても～なくても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the `ても` pair is the **plain** version and works only with verbs
and adjectives. The `にしても` pair also takes **nouns**, and sounds more deliberate.

</div>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っ<b>ても</b>{f|降|ふ}らなく<b>ても</b>、{f|明日|あした}は{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}します。
Rain or no rain, we set off tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}<b>にしても</b>{f|雪|ゆき}<b>にしても</b>、この{f|道|みち}は{f|通|とお}れなくなる。
Rain or snow, this road becomes impassable.
</pre>

The second cannot be rebuilt with `ても` without turning the nouns into verbs. Where
both are possible, the `ても` pair is shorter and lighter, and the `にしても` pair
sounds as though the options have actually been weighed.

### にしても…にしても vs にかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the paired form **names** the alternatives. `にかかわらず` refers to
the whole variable and names none of them.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大人|おとな}<b>にしても</b>{f|子|こ}ども<b>にしても</b>、{f|料金|りょうきん}は{f|同|おな}じです。
Adults or children, the fare is the same.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|年齢|ねんれい}に<b>かかわらず</b>、{f|料金|りょうきん}は{f|同|おな}じです。
The fare is the same regardless of age.
</pre>

The information is the same; the framing is not. Naming the two ends is warmer and more
concrete, and it works well in speech. `にかかわらず` states a policy, and it is what a
notice on a wall would say.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|買|か}う<b>にしても</b>{f|借|か}りる<b>にしろ</b> → ✅ {f|買|か}う<b>にしても</b>{f|借|か}りる<b>にしても</b>
  Keep the two halves identical. Alternating the forms is not stylistic variation; it
  reads as a slip.

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>だにしても</b>{f|社会人|しゃかいじん}<b>だにしても</b> → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>にしても</b>{f|社会人|しゃかいじん}<b>にしても</b>
  Nouns attach bare. `である` is available in formal writing, but `だ` never is.

- ❌ {f|参加|さんか}する<b>にしても</b>{f|参加|さんか}しない<b>にしても</b>、{f|会場|かいじょう}で{f|会|あ}おう。 → ✅ {f|参加|さんか}する<b>にしても</b>{f|参加|さんか}しない<b>にしても</b>、{f|返事|へんじ}だけはください。
  The main clause has to hold for **both** options. Meeting at the venue is only
  possible under one of them.

- ❌ {f|安|やす}い<b>にしても</b>{f|高|たか}かった<b>にしても</b> → ✅ {f|安|やす}い<b>にしても</b>{f|高|たか}い<b>にしても</b>
  Both halves carry the same tense. A present and a past in the same frame leaves the
  sentence pointing at two different times.

## Practice

**1.** {f|作|つく}る____{f|買|か}う____、{f|材料|ざいりょう}のことは{f|調|しら}べておいたほうがいい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|作|つく}る<b>にしても</b>{f|買|か}う<b>にしても</b>、{f|材料|ざいりょう}のことは{f|調|しら}べておいたほうがいい。

Two alternatives, both conceded, and one piece of advice that holds either way. Both
halves take the plain form and the identical ending.

</details>

**2.** Choose the register: a printed notice at a museum reads「{f|大人|おとな}____{f|子|こ}ども____、{f|入|い}り{f|口|ぐち}で{f|受付|うけつけ}をお{f|願|ねが}いします。」(にしても・にせよ)

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|大人|おとな}<b>にせよ</b>{f|子|こ}ども<b>にせよ</b>、{f|入|い}り{f|口|ぐち}で{f|受付|うけつけ}をお{f|願|ねが}いします。

A printed public notice is written Japanese, and `にせよ` is the written member of the
family. `にしても` would not be wrong, only noticeably chattier than a sign usually is.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Whether you accept the offer or turn it down, tell them today."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

その{f|話|はなし}を{f|受|う}ける<b>にしても</b>{f|断|ことわ}る<b>にしても</b>、{f|今日|きょう}{f|中|じゅう}に{f|伝|つた}えたほうがいい。

Two verbs in the plain form, each followed by `にしても`, and a main clause that applies
to both outcomes. Note that <code>{f|断|ことわ}る</code> here means "decline", not
"refuse permission".

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}くにしても{f|行|い}かないにしても、ホテルを{f|予約|よやく}しよう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}く<b>にしても</b>{f|行|い}かない<b>にしても</b>、{f|早|はや}めに{f|決|き}めよう。

The frame itself was correct; the main clause was not. Booking a hotel is only
sensible if you go, and this pattern requires a main clause that is true under both
alternatives.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|来|く}る<b>にしても</b>{f|来|こ}ない<b>にしても</b>、{f|一言|ひとこと}ほしい。
(b) {f|来|く}るか{f|来|こ}ないか、{f|一言|ひとこと}ほしい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) concedes both possibilities on the way through and then makes a request that holds
for either. The speaker is not asking which one it will be; they are asking to be told
something, whichever it turns out to be.

(b) asks for the answer to the question itself - which of the two is it? The `か…か`
frame poses an alternative question, while the doubled `にしても` sets the question
aside as already settled either way. The two are close in English and quite distinct
in Japanese.

</details>
