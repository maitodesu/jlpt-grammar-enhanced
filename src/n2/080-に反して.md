# ～に反して

**Meaning** · contrary to; against - what actually happened went the other way from what was expected, intended or laid down

<code>に{f|反|はん}して</code> needs something to run against. The noun in front of it
names a prediction, a hope, an intention or a rule, and the sentence reports that
reality did the opposite. That noun is the pattern's whole grammar: no expectation, no
<code>に{f|反|はん}して</code>.

On this batch's two axes - how much the speaker minds, and how formal the frame is - 
this one *minds on somebody else's behalf* and is *written and formal*. It does not
express the speaker's feelings; it records a mismatch between a stated standard and
the facts. That neutrality is why it fills news reports and official findings.

## Formation

Only nouns attach. The form changes according to what it modifies.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (adverbial) | Noun + に{f|反|はん}して | {f|予想|よそう} → {f|予想|よそう}に{f|反|はん}して |
| Noun (written, curt) | Noun + に{f|反|はん}し | {f|予測|よそく} → {f|予測|よそく}に{f|反|はん}し |
| Modifying a noun | Noun + に{f|反|はん}する + Noun | {f|規則|きそく} → {f|規則|きそく}に{f|反|はん}する{f|行為|こうい} |
| Modifying a noun (past) | Noun + に{f|反|はん}した + Noun | {f|期待|きたい} → {f|期待|きたい}に{f|反|はん}した{f|結果|けっか} |
| As a predicate | Noun + に{f|反|はん}する | {f|方針|ほうしん} → {f|方針|ほうしん}に{f|反|はん}する |
| A clause, nominalised first | …こと + に{f|反|はん}して | {f|約束|やくそく}したこと → {f|約束|やくそく}したことに{f|反|はん}して |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the noun must be something that can be **defied**:
{f|予想|よそう}, {f|期待|きたい}, {f|予測|よそく}, {f|意思|いし},
{f|願|ねが}い, {f|命令|めいれい}, {f|規則|きそく}, {f|法律|ほうりつ},
{f|方針|ほうしん}. An ordinary fact cannot go there:
❌<code>{f|雨|あめ}に{f|反|はん}して</code>. For a fact, use `にもかかわらず`.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - two flavours live in one pattern. With
<code>{f|予想|よそう}</code> or <code>{f|期待|きたい}</code> it means "contrary to", and
the tone is surprise. With <code>{f|規則|きそく}</code> or
<code>{f|命令|めいれい}</code> it means "in breach of", and the tone is condemnation.
The noun decides which you have written.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

Sales did not grow, contrary to what had been hoped.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>
{f|親|おや}の{f|願|ねが}い<b>に{f|反|はん}して</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|大学|だいがく}を{f|辞|や}めてしまった。

Contrary to earlier forecasts, this summer was a cool one.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

Against most predictions, the newcomer won the seat.
</pre>

The remark caused trouble as something contrary to company policy.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}{f|書|しょ}の{f|記載|きさい}<b>に{f|反|はん}して</b>、{f|実際|じっさい}の{f|重|おも}さは{f|倍|ばい}{f|近|ちか}くあった。
Contrary to what the manual stated, the actual weight was nearly double.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a written pattern. It belongs to journalism, reports, contracts, notices and
academic prose, and in speech it appears only in formal registers - a presentation, a
news broadcast, a manager explaining a decision. In conversation people say
<code>{f|思|おも}っていたのと{f|違|ちが}って</code> or simply
<code>{f|意外|いがい}に</code>.

It has almost no emotional content of its own. What colour the sentence has comes from
the noun: <code>{f|予想|よそう}に{f|反|はん}して</code> reads as mild surprise,
<code>{f|命令|めいれい}に{f|反|はん}して</code> as insubordination,
<code>{f|意思|いし}に{f|反|はん}して</code> as a violation of someone's rights. The
pattern itself just registers the mismatch, which is precisely why formal writing likes
it - it can report a breach without the writer appearing to editorialise.

The `する`-form is worth practising, because it is where learners stall.
<code>に{f|反|はん}する</code> before a noun is extremely common in fixed collocations:
<code>{f|規則|きそく}に{f|反|はん}する{f|行為|こうい}</code>,
<code>{f|事実|じじつ}に{f|反|はん}する{f|報道|ほうどう}</code>,
<code>{f|良心|りょうしん}に{f|反|はん}する{f|判断|はんだん}</code>. The bare
<code>に{f|反|はん}し</code>, without `て`, is the clipped written form you meet in
headlines and formal prose.

Finally, note that a clause cannot attach directly. If what is being defied is
something someone said or promised, nominalise it first -
<code>{f|約束|やくそく}したことに{f|反|はん}して</code> - or, more naturally, find the
noun that names it.

## Contrast

### に{f|反|はん}して vs とは{f|違|ちが}って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>とは{f|違|ちが}って</code> reports a **difference**.
<code>に{f|反|はん}して</code> reports a **defiance**: something had a claim on the
outcome, and the outcome ignored it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|兄|あに}<b>とは{f|違|ちが}って</b>、{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|人前|ひとまえ}で{f|話|はな}すのが{f|得意|とくい}だ。
Unlike his older brother, the younger one is good at speaking in public.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|周囲|しゅうい}の{f|予想|よそう}<b>に{f|反|はん}して</b>、{f|弟|おとうと}が{f|代表|だいひょう}に{f|選|えら}ばれた。
Contrary to what everyone around them expected, it was the younger brother who was chosen.
</pre>

A person can be different *from* somebody; a person cannot be contrary *to* somebody,
so ❌<code>{f|兄|あに}に{f|反|はん}して</code> is wrong. Only expectations, rules,
intentions and the like can be run against.

### に{f|反|はん}して vs にもかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `にもかかわらず` concedes a **fact** that was overcome.
<code>に{f|反|はん}して</code> names a **standard** that was contradicted.

</div>

<pre>
{f|悪天候|あくてんこう}<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|予定|よてい}どおり{f|進|すす}んだ。
Despite the bad weather, the work went ahead on schedule.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|当初|とうしょ}の{f|計画|けいかく}<b>に{f|反|はん}して</b>、{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|大幅|おおはば}に{f|遅|おく}れた。
Contrary to the original plan, the work fell badly behind.
</pre>

Bad weather is a fact and an obstacle; a plan is a standard the outcome failed to
match. Swap them and both sentences break: weather predicts nothing to be defied, and
a plan is not an obstacle you push through.

### に{f|反|はん}して vs とおりに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - they are opposites built on the same noun. `どおり` = **as
expected**; <code>に{f|反|はん}して</code> = **the other way from what was expected**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|予想|よそう}<b>どおり</b>、{f|決勝|けっしょう}は{f|接戦|せっせん}になった。
As expected, the final was a close-run thing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|予想|よそう}<b>に{f|反|はん}して</b>、{f|決勝|けっしょう}は{f|一方的|いっぽうてき}な{f|試合|しあい}になった。
Contrary to expectations, the final turned into a one-sided affair.
</pre>

Learning them as a pair is efficient, because they take the same nouns:
<code>{f|期待|きたい}どおり</code> and
<code>{f|期待|きたい}に{f|反|はん}して</code>,
<code>{f|指示|しじ}どおり</code> and
<code>{f|指示|しじ}に{f|反|はん}して</code>. Note the rendaku:
<code>{f|予想|よそう}どおり</code>, not とおり, after a noun.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}<b>に{f|反|はん}して</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|行|おこな}われた。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|行|おこな}われた。
  Rain is a fact, not an expectation. Only something that predicts or prescribes can be
  run against.

- ❌ {f|彼女|かのじょ}<b>に{f|反|はん}して</b>、{f|私|わたし}は{f|朝|あさ}が{f|苦手|にがて}だ。 → ✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}<b>とは{f|違|ちが}って</b>、{f|私|わたし}は{f|朝|あさ}が{f|苦手|にがて}だ。
  People are compared with <code>とは{f|違|ちが}って</code>. <code>に{f|反|はん}して</code> cannot take a
  person as its noun.

- ❌ {f|規則|きそく}に{f|反|はん}して{f|行為|こうい} → ✅ {f|規則|きそく}<b>に{f|反|はん}する</b>{f|行為|こうい}
  Modifying a noun needs the `する` form. The `して` form is adverbial and cannot sit in
  front of a noun.

- ❌ {f|思|おも}った<b>に{f|反|はん}して</b> → ✅ {f|予想|よそう}<b>に{f|反|はん}して</b>（or {f|思|おも}っていたのとは{f|違|ちが}って）
  A verb clause cannot attach directly. Use the noun that names the expectation.

## Practice

**1.** {f|事前|じぜん}の{f|予測|よそく}____、{f|来場者|らいじょうしゃ}は{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|半分|はんぶん}{f|以下|いか}だった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|事前|じぜん}の{f|予測|よそく}<b>に{f|反|はん}して</b>、{f|来場者|らいじょうしゃ}は{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|半分|はんぶん}{f|以下|いか}だった。

<code>{f|予測|よそく}</code> is a prediction - exactly the kind of noun that can be
defied - and the outcome went the other way. The written form
<code>{f|予測|よそく}に{f|反|はん}し</code> would work equally well here.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|強|つよ}い{f|風|かぜ}（に{f|反|はん}して・にもかかわらず）、{f|飛行機|ひこうき}は{f|定刻|ていこく}に{f|到着|とうちゃく}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|強|つよ}い{f|風|かぜ}<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|飛行機|ひこうき}は{f|定刻|ていこく}に{f|到着|とうちゃく}した。

Wind is a physical obstacle, not a standard the flight could contradict. `にもかかわらず`
concedes the obstacle; <code>に{f|反|はん}して</code> would need a noun like
<code>{f|予報|よほう}</code> or <code>{f|予定|よてい}</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, formally: "Any use of the data contrary to the terms of the agreement will be reported."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|契約|けいやく}の{f|条件|じょうけん}<b>に{f|反|はん}する</b>データの{f|利用|りよう}は、{f|報告|ほうこく}の{f|対象|たいしょう}となります。

The phrase modifies <code>{f|利用|りよう}</code>, so the `する` form is required. The
noun in front - contractual terms - is a standard that can be breached, which is the
"in violation of" flavour of the pattern.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|上司|じょうし}の{f|指示|しじ}に{f|反|はん}して{f|報告|ほうこく}{f|書|しょ}は、{f|受|う}け{f|取|と}れません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|上司|じょうし}の{f|指示|しじ}<b>に{f|反|はん}する</b>{f|報告|ほうこく}{f|書|しょ}は、{f|受|う}け{f|取|と}れません。

<code>に{f|反|はん}して</code> is adverbial and modifies a verb. To modify the noun
<code>{f|報告|ほうこく}{f|書|しょ}</code>, the form has to be
<code>に{f|反|はん}する</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|方針|ほうしん}<b>に{f|反|はん}して</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|取材|しゅざい}に{f|応|おう}じた。
(b) {f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|方針|ほうしん}<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|取材|しゅざい}に{f|応|おう}じた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says he acted **in breach of** company policy. The policy set a standard, and what
he did was the opposite of it. There is a clear implication of transgression, and this
is how a written report would put it.

(b) treats the policy as an obstacle he got past rather than a rule he broke. It is
possible Japanese, but weaker and slightly odd here, because a policy is exactly the
sort of thing that prescribes rather than obstructs. Where a rule is at stake,
<code>に{f|反|はん}して</code> is the precise choice.

</details>
