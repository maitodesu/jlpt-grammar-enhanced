# ～げ

**Meaning** · with an air of ～; looking ～ - describes the outward look of a feeling
you can only see from outside

`げ` is an old suffix that turns a word about an inner state into a word about its
visible surface. <code>{f|寂|さび}しい</code> is how a person feels;
<code>{f|寂|さび}しげ</code> is how they look to somebody watching. The difference
matters in Japanese, which does not let you announce another person's feelings as
fact.

## Formation

`げ` produces a な-adjective, which then behaves like any other.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective | drop い + げ | {f|寂|さび}しい → {f|寂|さび}しげ |
| な-adjective | stem + げ | {f|得意|とくい}だ → {f|得意|とくい}げ |
| ある | あり + げ | {f|自信|じしん}がある → {f|自信|じしん}ありげ |
| Modifying a noun | ～げな + Noun | {f|不安|ふあん}げ → {f|不安|ふあん}げな{f|顔|かお} |
| Modifying a verb | ～げに + verb | {f|楽|たの}しげ → {f|楽|たの}しげに{f|歌|うた}う |
| As a noun | ～げ + が／も | {f|悲|かな}しげ → {f|悲|かな}しげもなく |

<div class="note">

**Note** - `げ` is not productive. It attaches to a smallish, fixed list of words
about feeling and sensation - {f|寂|さび}しげ, {f|悲|かな}しげ,
{f|楽|たの}しげ, {f|嬉|うれ}しげ, {f|不安|ふあん}げ, {f|得意|とくい}げ,
{f|満足|まんぞく}げ, {f|眠|ねむ}たげ, {f|恥|は}ずかしげ, {f|自信|じしん}ありげ,
{f|意味|いみ}ありげ - and you cannot coin new ones freely. When in doubt, use
<code>そう</code>.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - a few `げ` words have frozen into fixed vocabulary and no longer follow
the pattern's logic: <code>{f|大人|おとな}げない</code> ("childish"),
<code>{f|何|なに}{f|気|げ}ない</code> ("casual, offhand"),
<code>{f|事|こと}もなげに</code> ("as though it were nothing"). Learn these as
words, not as constructions.

</div>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>
{f|先輩|せんぱい}は{f|意味|いみ}あり<b>げ</b>な{f|笑|え}みを{f|浮|う}かべて、{f|何|なに}も{f|言|い}わなかった。

</pre>

My son had won his match, and showed me the medal with obvious pride.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}の{f|隅|すみ}で、{f|一人|ひとり}の{f|女性|じょせい}が{f|不安|ふあん}<b>げ</b>に{f|時計|とけい}を{f|見|み}ていた。
In the corner of the hall a woman was watching the clock anxiously.

</pre>

## Nuance & register

`げ` is written and observational. It belongs to novels, essays, song lyrics and
careful description; in everyday speech <code>そう</code> does the same work and
sounds normal, while `げ` sounds a shade poetic. Nothing about it is impolite - it
simply raises the register of the sentence, which is why a spoken
<code>{f|楽|たの}しげでしたね</code> can come across as gently literary rather than
stiff.

Two restrictions follow from what the suffix means. First, it is for **other
people**. Since it describes an appearance from outside, using it about yourself is
odd in the same way <code>{f|寒|さむ}がっている</code> is odd about yourself: you do
not need to infer your own feelings. Second, it only handles **feelings**. There is
no ❌{f|高|たか}げ or ❌{f|赤|あか}げ, because a price and a colour have no inner
life to show on the surface.

There is also a quiet nuance of *restraint*. <code>そう</code> reports a plain
impression; `げ` suggests something showing through that the person has not stated -
a hint rather than a display. <code>{f|悲|かな}しげ</code> is not a person crying;
it is a person not quite hiding it. That is a tendency rather than a rule, but it is
why `げ` is so at home in description-heavy writing, and why translators reach for
"with an air of".

## Contrast

### ～げ vs ～そう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>そう</code> is the everyday, unrestricted one. `げ` is
written, attaches only to feeling words, and hints rather than reports.

</div>

<pre>
その{f|人|ひと}は{f|眠|ねむ}<b>そう</b>な{f|顔|かお}をしていた。
He looked sleepy.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|人|ひと}は{f|眠|ねむ}た<b>げ</b>に{f|目|め}をこすった。
He rubbed his eyes drowsily.
</pre>

Beyond register, one difference is decisive: <code>そうだ</code> can predict, and
`げ` cannot. <code>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}りそうだ</code> says rain is on the way;
there is no ❌{f|降|ふ}りげ, because a suffix about visible feeling has nothing to
say about the weather's intentions. If your sentence is about what is *going to
happen*, `げ` is not an option at all.

<code>そう</code> also attaches to verb stems and to almost any adjective. `げ` has
its short list and stops there.

### ～げ vs ～がる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `がる` makes a **verb**: the person keeps behaving as though they
feel it. `げ` makes an **adjective**: at this moment they look it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|母|はは}は{f|犬|いぬ}が{f|死|し}んでから{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|悲|かな}し<b>がって</b>いた。
After the dog died my mother went about grieving day after day.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|母|はは}は{f|犬|いぬ}の{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|悲|かな}し<b>げ</b>に{f|見|み}ていた。
My mother was looking at the photograph of the dog with sadness in her face.
</pre>

The verb describes a stretch of behaviour and often implies the feeling was being
shown - fussing, complaining, weeping. The adjective describes one moment's
appearance and implies the opposite: something visible that was not being announced.

The two suffixes also take overlapping but different lists, so you cannot assume a
pair exists. <code>{f|怖|こわ}がる</code> is everyday Japanese;
❌{f|怖|こわ}しげ is not a word at all, and the natural partner there is
<code>{f|怖|こわ}そうに</code>.

### ～げ vs ～っぽい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `げ` reports a **feeling showing on the surface**. `っぽい`
reports a **resemblance or a tendency**, and often carries a shrug.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|子|こ}ども<b>っぽい</b>ところがある。
There's something childish about him.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|悲|かな}し<b>げ</b>な{f|目|め}をしていた。
His eyes had a sad look to them.
</pre>

`っぽい` attaches happily to nouns, verbs and adjectives alike and is casual to the
point of being slangy; `げ` is none of those things. They collide only in sentences
about people's manner, and the test is whether an emotion is involved: if the word
in front names a feeling, `げ` is possible; if it names a type, `っぽい` is.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|寂|さび}し<b>げ</b>だ。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}は{f|寂|さび}しい。
  `げ` describes how somebody looks from outside. About your own feelings you simply
  use the adjective - there is nothing to infer.

- ❌ {f|高|たか}<b>げ</b>な{f|店|みせ} → ✅ {f|高|たか}<b>そう</b>な{f|店|みせ}
  Only words about feeling and sensation take `げ`. For an impression about a thing,
  <code>そう</code> is the suffix.

- ❌ {f|悲|かな}しい<b>げ</b>な{f|声|こえ} → ✅ {f|悲|かな}し<b>げ</b>な{f|声|こえ}
  The い of the adjective is dropped before `げ`, exactly as before
  <code>そう</code> and `がる`.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}り<b>げ</b>だ。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}り<b>そう</b>だ。
  `げ` never attaches to a verb stem and never predicts. Appearance-of-a-coming-event
  is <code>そう</code>'s territory.

## Practice

**1.** {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|返事|へんじ}をせず、____{f|窓|まど}の{f|外|そと}を{f|見|み}ていた。（{f|寂|さび}しい）

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|寂|さび}し<b>げ</b>に{f|窓|まど}の{f|外|そと}を{f|見|み}ていた

The い is dropped and `げ` takes `に` to modify the verb. The sentence is describing
someone else from outside, in a narrative register - exactly where `げ` belongs.
<code>{f|寂|さび}しそうに</code> would also be correct and would sound more
conversational.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|空|そら}が{f|暗|くら}くなってきた。{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}り（げだ・そうだ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}り<b>そうだ</b>。

Two things rule `げ` out at once: it cannot attach to a verb stem, and it cannot
express a prediction about what is going to happen. Only <code>そうだ</code> reads
the sky.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The little girl looked up at me with an anxious expression."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|女|おんな}の{f|子|こ}は{f|不安|ふあん}<b>げな</b>{f|顔|かお}で{f|私|わたし}を{f|見上|みあ}げた。

<code>{f|不安|ふあん}</code> is a な-adjective, so `げ` attaches straight to the
stem, and <code>げな</code> is the form that modifies
<code>{f|顔|かお}</code>. "With an ～ expression" is one of the phrases `げ` was made
for.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|忙|いそが}しげだから、{f|私|わたし}も{f|忙|いそが}しげです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|忙|いそが}し<b>そう</b>だから、{f|私|わたし}も{f|忙|いそが}しいです。

Two problems. The second half describes the speaker's own state, where no
appearance-suffix belongs at all. And <code>{f|忙|いそが}しげ</code> is a poor fit
even for the manager: busyness is a circumstance rather than an emotion showing on a
face, so <code>{f|忙|いそが}しそう</code> is what a Japanese speaker would say.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|嬉|うれ}し<b>そうに</b>{f|報告|ほうこく}した。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|嬉|うれ}し<b>げに</b>{f|報告|ほうこく}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the ordinary sentence: he reported it and looked pleased. It would sit
comfortably in an email or in speech.

(b) says the same thing in a writer's voice. The register lifts - you would expect
it in a novel or a considered piece of prose - and `げ` adds a faint suggestion that
the pleasure was showing through rather than being displayed. The difference is one
of texture, not of fact, and in a conversation (b) would sound as though the speaker
were narrating the scene.

</details>
