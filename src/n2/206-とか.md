# ～とか

**Meaning** · I gather that…, something about… - relaying information at arm's length, without vouching for the details

This `とか` reports what you heard while quietly declining to guarantee it. Two
things drive it: you genuinely are not sure of the details, and - just as often -
you do not want to be the person who stated something firmly about somebody else.
It is hearsay with a shrug built in, and in adult speech that shrug is frequently a
courtesy rather than vagueness.

## Formation

`とか` follows the plain form. The reporting verb behind it -
<code>{f|聞|き}きました</code>, <code>{f|言|い}っていました</code> - is optional, and
leaving it off is what produces the characteristic trailing sentence.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + とか | {f|戻|もど}る → {f|戻|もど}るとか |
| Verb (past) | plain past + とか | {f|辞|や}めた → {f|辞|や}めたとか |
| い-adjective | plain form + とか | {f|忙|いそが}しい → {f|忙|いそが}しいとか |
| な-adjective | ～だ + とか | {f|大変|たいへん}だ → {f|大変|たいへん}だとか |
| Noun | noun（+ だ）+ とか | {f|風邪|かぜ} → {f|風邪|かぜ}だとか |
| With the verb spelled out | … とか{f|聞|き}きました・{f|言|い}っていました | {f|来|こ}ないとか{f|聞|き}きました |
| As a hedged reason | … とかで | {f|事故|じこ}があったとかで{f|遅|おく}れています |
| Naming something vaguely | … とかいう + noun | {f|田中|たなか}とかいう{f|人|ひと} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - a sentence may simply stop at `とか`. <code>{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|出張|しゅっちょう}だとか。</code> is complete as spoken Japanese: the "…is what I
heard" is left for the listener to supply, and the omission is part of the softness.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|課長|かちょう}は{f|風邪|かぜ}だ<b>とか</b>で、{f|今日|きょう}はお{f|休|やす}みです。
The section chief is off today - something about a cold.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|二人|ふたり}、{f|来年|らいねん}{f|結婚|けっこん}する<b>とか</b>{f|聞|き}きました。
I heard those two are getting married next year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}<b>とか</b>いう{f|人|ひと}から{f|電話|でんわ}がありましたよ。
Someone called - Tanaka, or something like that.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工事|こうじ}のせいで{f|来月|らいげつ}まで{f|通|とお}れない<b>とか</b>{f|言|い}っていましたよ。
They were saying it's closed to traffic until next month because of the works.
</pre>

<pre>
なんでも、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|社長|しゃちょう}はまだ30{f|代|だい}だ<b>とか</b>。
Word is the new president is still in his thirties.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|体調|たいちょう}を{f|崩|くず}された<b>とか</b>で、{f|今回|こんかい}のご{f|出席|しゅっせき}は{f|難|むずか}しいそうです。
He's apparently unwell, so attending this time is going to be difficult for him.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|辺|あた}りは{f|昔|むかし}{f|川|かわ}だった<b>とか</b>で、{f|地面|じめん}が{f|弱|よわ}いらしいですよ。
That area used to be a river, from what I hear, so the ground is supposed to be soft.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}さんが{f|海外|かいがい}の{f|大学|だいがく}に{f|進|すす}まれた<b>とか</b>、{f|奥|おく}さんから{f|伺|うかが}いました。
I gather from your wife that your son has gone on to a university abroad.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`とか` is spoken Japanese, but unlike most casual hearsay markers it is at home in
polite adult conversation. Combined with honorific verbs -
<code>{f|退職|たいしょく}なさるとか</code>,
<code>お{f|戻|もど}りになるとか</code> - it is a standard way to raise something you
have heard about a third party without asserting it. The hedge protects everyone:
if the report turns out to be wrong, you never claimed it was right.

That same hedge is its weakness. In a context where accuracy is the point - a
handover, a report to a manager, a written record - `とか` reads as if you could not
be bothered to check. <code>{f|中止|ちゅうし}になるとか</code> to your boss invites
「とか、じゃなくて、どっちなの」. Use `とのことです` or name the source when it
matters.

Two related uses are worth keeping straight from this one. `とか` also lists
examples - <code>お{f|茶|ちゃ}とかコーヒーとか</code> - and in modern casual speech
that listing `とか` has spread into a general softener attached to single nouns
(<code>お{f|茶|ちゃ}とか{f|飲|の}む？</code>). That softener leans young and is one of
the forms criticised as vague, padded speech; it is not the hearsay pattern, though
the family resemblance is obvious - both are ways of not being definite.

<code>～とかいう</code> deserves its own line. Attached to a name it means "some X
or other" and carries a faint distancing: you do not know the person, or you are
signalling that they are of no great importance to you. Said about someone the
listener is close to, it can sting.

## Contrast

### ～とか vs ～そうだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `そうだ` reports and stands behind the report. `とか` reports
and steps back from it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|料金|りょうきん}が{f|上|あ}がる<b>そうです</b>。
I hear the fee is going up from next month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|料金|りょうきん}が{f|上|あ}がる<b>とか</b>。
Something about the fee going up next month.
</pre>

Both are hearsay and both are polite. `そうです` presents the information as
reliable enough to act on; `とか` leaves the door open for it to be wrong, or for
the details to be off. If a colleague asks you to confirm a date, `そうです` answers
the question and `とか` does not.

### ～とか vs ～んだって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `んだって` is casual and delivers the news. `とか` can be
polite, and softens the news on the way out.

</div>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さん、{f|会社|かいしゃ}{f|辞|や}める<b>んだって</b>。
Tanaka's quitting, apparently.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|会社|かいしゃ}をお{f|辞|や}めになる<b>とか</b>。
I gather Mr Tanaka may be leaving the company.
</pre>

The first is what you say to a friend at lunch; the second is what you say to a
senior colleague when you are not certain and would rather not be quoted. `んだって`
cannot be made polite enough for the second situation - `んですって` is still chatty
- and `とか` in the first would sound oddly guarded among friends.

### hearsay ～とか vs listing ～とか…とか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - after a **clause**, `とか` is hearsay. After **nouns, in a
series**, it is "things like X and Y".

</div>

<pre>
{f|週末|しゅうまつ}は{f|映画|えいが}<b>とか</b>{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}<b>とか</b>に{f|行|い}きます。
At weekends I go to the cinema, shopping, that kind of thing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|週末|しゅうまつ}は{f|出|で}かけられない<b>とか</b>{f|言|い}っていましたよ。
She was saying something about not being able to go out at the weekend.
</pre>

The listing use needs at least the possibility of a second item and attaches to
nouns; the hearsay use follows a finished clause and usually stands alone or leads
into a verb of saying. In practice the shape in front of `とか` settles it
immediately.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|来|き}ます<b>とか</b>{f|言|い}っていました。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|来|く}る<b>とか</b>{f|言|い}っていました。
  The clause in front of `とか` is plain form, not ます.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}、{f|私|わたし}も{f|会場|かいじょう}で{f|見|み}た<b>とか</b>。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}、{f|私|わたし}も{f|会場|かいじょう}で{f|見|み}ました。
  You were there. Hedged hearsay about your own direct experience sounds either
  evasive or as though you doubt your own eyes.

- ❌ （reporting to your manager）{f|納品|のうひん}が{f|遅|おく}れる<b>とか</b>です。 → ✅ {f|納品|のうひん}が{f|遅|おく}れる<b>とのことです</b>。
  Where the information has to be acted on, drop the hedge and name the fact - or
  the source.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>とかいう</b>{f|人|ひと}に{f|聞|き}いてください。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|聞|き}いてください。
  <code>とかいう</code> distances you from the person named. Applied to someone the
  listener works under, it is close to insulting.

## Practice

**1.** Fill the blank: {f|山田|やまだ}さんは{f|急|きゅう}な{f|用事|ようじ}ができた____で、{f|先|さき}に{f|帰|かえ}られました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|用事|ようじ}ができた<b>とか</b>で、{f|先|さき}に{f|帰|かえ}られました。

<code>とかで</code> is the hedged-reason shape: you are passing on the reason you
were given without vouching for it. <code>ということで</code> would state the reason
as established.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|開始|かいし}は{f|10時|じゅうじ}に{f|変更|へんこう}になった（とか・とのこと）です。 You are
telling the whole team the time has moved.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|変更|へんこう}になった<b>とのこと</b>です。

Everyone is about to plan their morning around this, so the hedge is a liability.
`とか` would leave the team unsure whether to turn up at ten, and someone would have
to ask you again.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Apparently he'd hurt his leg, so he didn't run in the end."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|足|あし}を{f|痛|いた}めた<b>とか</b>で、{f|結局|けっきょく}{f|走|はし}らなかったそうです。

<code>とかで</code> carries "apparently, because…" in one piece. The main clause can
keep its own hearsay marker; the two are reporting different layers, the reason and
the outcome.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|先生|せんせい}が{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|試験|しけん}をしますとか{f|言|い}っていました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|先生|せんせい}が{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|試験|しけん}をする<b>とか</b>{f|言|い}っていました。

`とか` takes the plain form. The politeness of the sentence belongs at the end, on
<code>{f|言|い}っていました</code> - quoted clauses in Japanese are not made polite
to match the frame.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|新|あたら}しい{f|支店|してん}が{f|来年|らいねん}できる<b>そうです</b>。
(b) {f|新|あたら}しい{f|支店|してん}が{f|来年|らいねん}できる<b>とか</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) passes on the information as something you consider solid. The listener can
repeat it, and if pressed you would be willing to say where you heard it.

(b) passes on the same words while declining to stand behind them - the timing, or
the whole plan, might be wrong. In polite conversation this is often deliberate
courtesy rather than ignorance: you are raising a rumour about the company without
appearing to spread it. In a briefing, though, it would sound underprepared.

</details>
