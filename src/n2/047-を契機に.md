# ～を契機に / ～を機に

**Meaning** · taking X as the turning point; in the wake of X - formal, and used of events with some weight behind them

<code>{f|契機|けいき}</code> is a heavy Sino-Japanese noun meaning "the moment a thing
turns". The pattern points at an event and says that things went a different way
from there: a merger, a disaster, an anniversary, a court ruling. Structurally it is
identical to `をきっかけに`; the difference is altitude. This one belongs to
newspapers, company statements and history writing.

## Formation

<code>{f|契機|けいき}</code> and <code>{f|機|き}</code> are nouns, so a verb clause is
nominalised with `の` first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event noun | Noun + を{f|契機|けいき}に | {f|上場|じょうじょう} → {f|上場|じょうじょう}を{f|契機|けいき}に |
| Event noun (emphatic) | Noun + を{f|契機|けいき}にして | {f|合併|がっぺい} → {f|合併|がっぺい}を{f|契機|けいき}にして |
| Event noun (written) | Noun + を{f|契機|けいき}として | {f|進出|しんしゅつ} → {f|進出|しんしゅつ}を{f|契機|けいき}として |
| Shorter, lighter variant | Noun + を{f|機|き}に | {f|退職|たいしょく} → {f|退職|たいしょく}を{f|機|き}に |
| Verb clause | plain past + のを{f|契機|けいき}に | {f|公開|こうかい}された → {f|公開|こうかい}されたのを{f|契機|けいき}に |
| As the subject | Noun + が{f|契機|けいき}となって | {f|報道|ほうどう} → {f|報道|ほうどう}が{f|契機|けいき}となって |
| ❌ Bare verb | ❌{f|辞|や}めるを{f|契機|けいき}に | ✅{f|辞|や}めたのを{f|契機|けいき}に |
| ❌ Wrong particle | ❌{f|報道|ほうどう}が{f|契機|けいき}に | ✅{f|報道|ほうどう}が{f|契機|けいき}となって |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>を{f|機|き}に</code> is the same idea in four fewer syllables. It is
lighter on the page and turns up constantly in press releases and in personal
announcements of the <code>{f|定年|ていねん}を{f|機|き}に</code> kind.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|地震|じしん}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、{f|防災|ぼうさい}{f|計画|けいかく}が{f|見直|みなお}された。
The earthquake was the turning point at which the disaster plan was reviewed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|退職|たいしょく}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|生|う}まれた{f|町|まち}へ{f|帰|かえ}ることにした。
On retiring, I decided to move back to the town where I was born.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大会|たいかい}での{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、クラブの{f|入会者|にゅうかいしゃ}が{f|急|きゅう}に{f|増|ふ}えた。
Winning the tournament was the point from which club membership shot up.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|出産|しゅっさん}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|働|はたら}き{f|方|かた}を{f|変|か}えた{f|人|ひと}は{f|少|すく}なくない。
Plenty of people change the way they work when a child is born.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|海外|かいがい}{f|進出|しんしゅつ}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}として</b>、{f|社内|しゃない}の{f|公用語|こうようご}が{f|英語|えいご}に{f|改|あらた}められた。
With the move overseas as the turning point, the company language was changed to English.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工場|こうじょう}の{f|事故|じこ}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、{f|安全|あんぜん}{f|基準|きじゅん}が{f|全面的|ぜんめんてき}に{f|見直|みなお}された。
The factory accident was what led to a wholesale review of the safety standards.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|創立|そうりつ}{f|百周年|ひゃくしゅうねん}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、{f|大学|だいがく}は{f|図書館|としょかん}を{f|建|た}て{f|直|なお}す{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|発表|はっぴょう}した。
Marking a hundred years since its founding, the university announced plans to rebuild the library.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一冊|いっさつ}の{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}が{f|公開|こうかい}されたの<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、{f|長|なが}く{f|放置|ほうち}されてきた{f|問題|もんだい}に{f|光|ひかり}が{f|当|あ}たり{f|始|はじ}めた。
The release of a single report was the turning point after which a long-neglected problem began to get attention.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Written and formal. <code>を{f|契機|けいき}に</code> is at home in editorials,
annual reports, museum captions and academic prose; said in casual conversation it
sounds as though you are quoting a document. <code>を{f|機|き}に</code> is a step
lighter and does appear in careful speech and in the announcements people make about
their own lives, but it is still a long way from `をきっかけに`.

The syllabus gloss "as a good opportunity" is worth qualifying. The pattern does not
require a welcome event: <code>{f|震災|しんさい}を{f|契機|けいき}に</code> and
<code>{f|事故|じこ}を{f|契機|けいき}に</code> are entirely normal, and much of its use
is about turns for the worse forcing a change. What is true is that when the event
*is* favourable, the sentence reads as seizing it -
<code>{f|受賞|じゅしょう}を{f|機|き}に</code> has exactly that flavour.

What the pattern will not take is the trivial. A friend's offhand joke, a drama you
happened to watch, a chance meeting in a lift: these are `きっかけ` material, and
<code>{f|契機|けいき}</code> over them is comically grand. The event should be one an
outsider would also count as significant.

Note finally that the turning point is presented as **historical**. The sentence
looks back and traces a line, which is why the main clause is so often in the past
and so often passive: {f|見直|みなお}された, {f|改|あらた}められた,
{f|進|すす}んだ.

## Contrast

### ～を{f|契機|けいき}に vs ～をきっかけに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one structure, two registers. `をきっかけに` is neutral and
takes small private triggers; <code>を{f|契機|けいき}に</code> is formal and wants
an event of public weight.

</div>

<pre>
{f|同僚|どうりょう}にすすめられたの<b>をきっかけに</b>、{f|朝|あさ}{f|走|はし}るようになった。
A colleague suggesting it was what got me running in the mornings.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|法改正|ほうかいせい}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、{f|各社|かくしゃ}が{f|表示|ひょうじ}の{f|方法|ほうほう}を{f|改|あらた}めた。
The change in the law was the turning point at which firms revised their labelling.
</pre>

Swap the two and both sentences misfire: a colleague's suggestion is not a national
turning point, and a legal reform reported with `きっかけ` sounds like gossip. In
the middle - a marriage, a move, a company anniversary - either is possible, and the
register of the surrounding text decides.

### ～を{f|契機|けいき}に vs ～を{f|機|き}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different weight.
<code>を{f|機|き}に</code> is the shorter, lighter one and often reports a choice
somebody made; <code>を{f|契機|けいき}に</code> is heavier and comfortable describing
an impersonal historical turn.

</div>

<pre>
{f|結婚|けっこん}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|通勤|つうきん}に{f|便利|べんり}な{f|町|まち}へ{f|移|うつ}った。
When we married, we moved somewhere handier for commuting.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|鉄道|てつどう}の{f|開通|かいつう}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、この{f|地域|ちいき}は{f|大|おお}きく{f|発展|はってん}した。
The opening of the railway was the turning point from which this area developed.
</pre>

The first is a decision within one family; the second is a change nobody in
particular decided. Either form would be understood in either sentence, so treat
this as a leaning rather than a rule - but the leaning is consistent enough to be
worth following.

### ～を{f|契機|けいき}に vs ～にあたって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|契機|けいき}に</code> looks **back** at an event and
traces what changed. `にあたって` stands **at** an event and reports what is done
there and then.

</div>

<pre>
{f|移転|いてん}<b>にあたって</b>、{f|取引先|とりひきさき}に{f|案内状|あんないじょう}をお{f|送|おく}りしました。
On relocating, we sent a notice to our business partners.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|移転|いてん}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、{f|紙|かみ}の{f|書類|しょるい}をほとんど{f|廃止|はいし}した。
The relocation was the point at which we did away with paper documents almost entirely.
</pre>

The notice is part of relocating; scrapping the paperwork is a change the move set
off. Ask whether the main clause is **business belonging to the occasion** or
**a lasting consequence of it**.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|友|とも}だちの{f|冗談|じょうだん}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、みんなが{f|笑|わら}った。 → ✅ {f|友|とも}だちの{f|冗談|じょうだん}<b>で</b>、みんなが{f|笑|わら}った。
  A joke is not a turning point. Small triggers take `をきっかけに`, and immediate
  reactions take plain で.

- ❌ {f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|辞|や}める<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、{f|資格|しかく}を{f|取|と}った。 → ✅ {f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|辞|や}めたの<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、{f|資格|しかく}を{f|取|と}った。
  <code>{f|契機|けいき}</code> is a noun. A clause has to be nominalised with
  <code>のを</code>, and the past form is what you want, since the event came first.

- ❌ {f|報道|ほうどう}<b>が{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、{f|調査|ちょうさ}が{f|始|はじ}まった。 → ✅ {f|報道|ほうどう}<b>が{f|契機|けいき}となって</b>、{f|調査|ちょうさ}が{f|始|はじ}まった。
  With `を` the pattern ends in `に`; with `が` it needs
  <code>となって</code>. The two frames cannot be half-mixed.

- ❌ {f|大雪|おおゆき}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、{f|道路|どうろ}が{f|閉鎖|へいさ}された。 → ✅ {f|大雪|おおゆき}<b>のため</b>、{f|道路|どうろ}が{f|閉鎖|へいさ}された。
  Closing a road is the immediate effect of the snow, not a new direction taken
  afterwards. A turning point needs something to have turned.

## Practice

**1.** {f|社長|しゃちょう}の{f|入院|にゅういん}____、{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|健康|けんこう}{f|管理|かんり}の{f|制度|せいど}を{f|整|ととの}えた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|社長|しゃちょう}の{f|入院|にゅういん}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>（or を{f|機|き}に）

A hospital admission that leads an organisation to rebuild its health policy is a
genuine turning point, and the sentence is written in report register. The lighter
<code>をきっかけに</code> would also be understood, just less official.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}たの（をきっかけに・を{f|契機|けいき}に）、{f|山登|やまのぼ}りを{f|始|はじ}めた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}たの<b>をきっかけに</b>

Watching a film is a private, small-scale trigger and the sentence is a personal
anecdote. <code>{f|契機|けいき}</code> over it would sound as though the film had
altered the course of the nation.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The pandemic was the turning point at which many companies introduced working from home."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|感染症|かんせんしょう}の{f|流行|りゅうこう}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、{f|多|おお}くの{f|企業|きぎょう}が{f|在宅勤務|ざいたくきんむ}を{f|導入|どうにゅう}した。

A society-wide event and a society-wide response: this is exactly the altitude the
pattern is written for, and the past-tense main clause traces the line backwards
from now.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|転勤|てんきん}を{f|契機|けいき}で、{f|車|くるま}を{f|買|か}い{f|替|か}えた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|転勤|てんきん}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、{f|車|くるま}を{f|買|か}い{f|替|か}えた。

The frame is <code>を…に</code>, not <code>を…で</code>. If you want `で`, drop the
pattern altogether and say <code>{f|転勤|てんきん}で{f|車|くるま}を{f|買|か}い{f|替|か}えた</code>,
which states a plain reason instead of a turning point.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|受賞|じゅしょう}<b>を{f|機|き}に</b>、{f|作家|さっか}は{f|新|あたら}しい{f|連載|れんさい}を{f|始|はじ}めた。
(b) {f|受賞|じゅしょう}<b>にあたって</b>、{f|作家|さっか}は{f|感謝|かんしゃ}の{f|言葉|ことば}を{f|述|の}べた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) treats the prize as an opening the writer took: the serial is a consequence, and
the sentence looks back from after the change.

(b) stands at the ceremony itself. The words of thanks are part of the occasion, not
something the prize set in motion, and only `にあたって` will carry that.

</details>
