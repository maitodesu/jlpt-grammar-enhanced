# 何より

**Meaning** · above all; more than anything else; nothing could be better

<code>{f|何|なに}より</code> (なにより) is a comparison against everything at once.
Whatever else is on the table, this outranks it - and in its set-phrase use,
`何よりです`, it is how Japanese says "I'm so glad."

## Usage

`何より` is <code>{f|何|なに}</code> plus the comparative particle
<code>より</code>, and it behaves in three ways.

**1. Before an adjective** - "more ～ than anything".

- {f|健康|けんこう}が{f|何|なに}より{f|大切|たいせつ}だ。
- {f|何|なに}より{f|嬉|うれ}しい。

The emphatic form <code>{f|何|なに}よりも</code> is common and slightly stronger.

**2. The noun-modifying form** - <code>{f|何|なに}よりの</code> before a noun means
"the finest possible ～". It takes `の`, never `な`.

- {f|何|なに}よりの{f|証拠|しょうこ}
- {f|何|なに}よりのごちそう

**3. The set phrase** - <code>{f|何|なに}よりです</code>.

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～で{f|何|なに}よりです</code> is a **reaction to good
news about someone else**: "that they are well / that it went safely is the best
thing of all." It is not used to report your own good fortune.

</div>

- お{f|元気|げんき}そうで{f|何|なに}よりです。
- {f|大事|だいじ}にいたらず{f|何|なに}よりでした。

The past form <code>{f|何|なに}よりでした</code> is used when the worry is now over.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|体|からだ}が{f|丈夫|じょうぶ}なことが<b>{f|何|なに}より</b>ありがたい。
Being in good health is what I'm most grateful for.
</pre>

<pre>
ご{f|家族|かぞく}が{f|無事|ぶじ}で<b>{f|何|なに}より</b>です。
I'm just relieved your family is safe.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎年|まいとし}この{f|席|せき}が{f|埋|う}まることが、{f|続|つづ}けてきたことの<b>{f|何|なに}よりの</b>{f|証拠|しょうこ}だ。
That these seats fill every year is the best proof there is that it was worth keeping going.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|給料|きゅうりょう}も{f|大事|だいじ}だが、{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|時間|じかん}が{f|持|も}てることが<b>{f|何|なに}よりも</b>ありがたい。
The pay matters too, but more than anything I'm grateful for having time of my own.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Neutral to formal, and heavier in tone than its plain English gloss. `何より`
sounds like a considered judgement, which is why it turns up in speeches, letters
and thank-you notes as often as in conversation.

<code>{f|何|なに}よりです</code> in particular is a **polite social formula**, and it
is worth learning as a unit. You use it on hearing that someone has recovered, got
home safely, or come through something unpleasant. It only ever reports good news:
attaching it to a bad outcome is not merely unidiomatic, it will read as
sarcasm. When speaking to a superior, the fuller
<code>{f|何|なに}よりでございます</code> or a plain
<code>よかったです</code> are both safer than clipping it to a bare
<code>{f|何|なに}より</code>, which sounds abrupt.

The reading is **なにより**. <code>{f|何|なに}</code> takes なに before よ, in the
same way as <code>{f|何|なに}か</code> and <code>{f|何|なに}が</code>; なん appears
before で, だ and の.

## Contrast

### {f|何|なに}より vs いちばん

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `いちばん` ranks within a **set you have in mind**; `何より`
measures against **everything there is**.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|中|なか}では、これが<b>いちばん</b>{f|安|やす}い。
Of these, this one is the cheapest.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|安全|あんぜん}が<b>{f|何|なに}より</b>{f|大切|たいせつ}です。
Safety matters more than anything.
</pre>

Put `何より` in the first sentence and it overreaches - you are not claiming the
item is the cheapest in existence, only the cheapest on this shelf. Put `いちばん`
in the second and the declaration shrinks into a ranking exercise.

`いちばん` is also the everyday word: it takes any adjective and any set, and it is
what you say in a shop. `何より` is evaluative and emphatic, closer to "above all",
and it carries the two constructions - <code>{f|何|なに}よりの</code> and
<code>{f|何|なに}よりです</code> - that `いちばん` does not share.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|何|なに}より<b>な</b>{f|証拠|しょうこ}だ。 → ✅ <b>{f|何|なに}よりの</b>{f|証拠|しょうこ}だ。
  In front of a noun it takes `の`. There is no な-adjective here.

- ❌ {f|事故|じこ}に{f|遭|あ}われたそうで{f|何|なに}よりです。 → ✅ {f|事故|じこ}に{f|遭|あ}われたそうで、お{f|見舞|みま}い{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます。
  <code>{f|何|なに}よりです</code> celebrates. Applied to bad news it sounds as
  though you are pleased about the accident.

- ❌ {f|三|さん}つの{f|中|なか}で、これが{f|何|なに}より{f|軽|かる}い。 → ✅ {f|三|さん}つの{f|中|なか}で、これが<b>いちばん</b>{f|軽|かる}い。
  A closed set of three calls for `いちばん`. `何より` claims a comparison against
  everything, which contradicts the stated set.

## Practice

**1.** {f|試験|しけん}に{f|合格|ごうかく}されたそうで、____です。おめでとうございます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|合格|ごうかく}されたそうで、<b>{f|何|なに}より</b>です。

Good news about the listener, received second-hand with <code>そうで</code>, is
exactly the slot for this formula. <code>よかったですね</code> is the plainer
alternative and works with friends.

</details>

**2.** Translate: "Nothing makes me happier than a letter from my grandchildren."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|孫|まご}からの{f|手紙|てがみ}が<b>{f|何|なに}より</b>{f|嬉|うれ}しい。

`何より` goes straight in front of the adjective, and no explicit "than" phrase is
needed - the word already means "than anything".
<code>{f|何|なに}よりの{f|楽|たの}しみだ</code>, "my greatest pleasure", is a natural
variant using the noun-modifying form.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|家族|かぞく}の{f|健康|けんこう}がいちばん{f|大事|だいじ}です。
(b) {f|家族|かぞく}の{f|健康|けんこう}が{f|何|なに}より{f|大事|だいじ}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both put family health at the top, and either would pass unremarked in
conversation.

(a) implies a list - work, money, health - with health at number one. (b) refuses
the list altogether: nothing else is even in the running. (b) is the more emphatic
and the more formal of the two, and it is the one you would write in a card.

</details>
