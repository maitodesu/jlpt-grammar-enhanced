# ～かねる

**Meaning** · I am not in a position to do that; I cannot bring myself to - a formal, face-saving "no"

`～かねる` is not physical inability. Everything it refuses is perfectly possible;
what blocks it is the speaker's role, the company's rules, or a genuine inability
to settle on something. In business and service Japanese it is the standard way of
saying no without ever using the word できません.

## Formation

`かねる` attaches to the **ます-stem** and then conjugates as a る-verb (a Group 2 /
{f|一段|いちだん} verb).

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + かねる | わかる → わかりかねる |
| Polite - by far the commonest shape | ～かねます | わかりかねます |
| する-verb | Noun + し + かねる | {f|賛成|さんせい}する → {f|賛成|さんせい}しかねる |
| Humble | お + ます-stem + しかねます | {f|答|こた}える → お{f|答|こた}えしかねます |
| Past | ～かねた | {f|決|き}めかねた |
| Progressive | ～かねている | {f|決|き}めかねている |
| て-form (set phrase) | {f|見|み}るに{f|見|み}かねて | - |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `かねる` takes verbs of **judging, answering, agreeing and
deciding**: わかる, {f|答|こた}える, {f|決|き}める, {f|判断|はんだん}する,
{f|賛成|さんせい}する, {f|承知|しょうち}する, {f|認|みと}める. It does not take
verbs of physical action. ❌<code>{f|重|おも}くて{f|持|も}ちかねる</code> is not
Japanese; that is <code>{f|持|も}てない</code>.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>かねない</code> is **not** the negative of this pattern. It means
"may well do (something unwelcome)":
<code>この{f|寒|さむ}さでは{f|凍|こお}りかねない</code>. The negative of
<code>かねる</code> in the "cannot" sense simply does not get used - if you can do
it, you say so straightforwardly.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
その{f|件|けん}については、お{f|答|こた}え<b>しかねます</b>。
I am afraid I cannot answer on that matter.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありませんが、{f|返品|へんぴん}は<b>いたしかねます</b>。
I'm very sorry, but we are unable to accept returns.
</pre>

<pre>
どちらの{f|案|あん}がいいか、{f|決|き}め<b>かねて</b>います。
I can't make up my mind which of the two proposals is better.
</pre>

<pre>
ご{f|事情|じじょう}は{f|分|わ}かりますが、{f|例外|れいがい}は{f|認|みと}め<b>かねます</b>。
I do understand your situation, but we cannot make an exception.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}は{f|返事|へんじ}を{f|決|き}め<b>かね</b>ている{f|様子|ようす}だった。
The manager looked as though he could not settle on an answer.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|重|おも}い{f|箱|はこ}を{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|運|はこ}んでいるのを{f|見|み}るに{f|見|み}<b>かねて</b>、{f|声|こえ}をかけた。
I couldn't just stand there watching her carry that heavy box alone, so I offered to help.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|条件|じょうけん}では、{f|弊社|へいしゃ}としては{f|承知|しょうち}し<b>かねます</b>。
On those terms, we as a company are not in a position to agree.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|客様|きゃくさま}の{f|個人|こじん}{f|情報|じょうほう}に{f|関|かん}するご{f|質問|しつもん}には、{f|電話|でんわ}では{f|回答|かいとう}し<b>かねます</b>。
Questions concerning customers' personal information are not something we can answer by telephone.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`かねる` is **formal**, and in practice it is nearly always polite: `かねます`,
`いたしかねます`, `お～しかねます`. Plain <code>かねる</code> appears in writing and
in narration, but at a counter or on the phone you will hear the ます form almost
without exception.

Its social work is worth understanding. <code>できません</code> aims the refusal
at the listener: *I* will not do this. <code>かねます</code> presents the same
answer as something the speaker's position does not allow - the refusal comes from
the situation, not from a person - which is exactly why service Japanese leans on
it so heavily. Paired with {f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございませんが or
あいにく, it is about as soft as a flat no can be made.

The other sense - "cannot bring oneself to" - is more emotional and less formal.
{f|決|き}めかねる and {f|言|い}い{f|出|だ}しかねる describe a person stuck in front
of a choice or a difficult sentence. The frozen phrase
{f|見|み}るに{f|見|み}かねて, "unable to go on watching", is common in ordinary
narrative and does not sound stiff at all. So is
{f|待|ま}ちかねる, which has drifted to mean waiting impatiently rather than being
unable to wait.

One caution. Because `かねます` is so heavily associated with counters, call
centres and formal correspondence, dropping it into casual conversation with a
friend sounds like play-acting a customer-service voice. Among friends the honest
form is {f|無理|むり}, できない, or ちょっと….

## Contrast

### ～かねる vs ～できません

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same answer, different face. `できません` states an inability;
`かねます` presents the refusal as coming from the speaker's position, and softens
it.

</div>

<pre>
その{f|質問|しつもん}にはお{f|答|こた}え<b>しかねます</b>。
I'm afraid that is not something I can answer.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|質問|しつもん}には{f|答|こた}えられません。
I can't answer that question.
</pre>

Both refuse. The second is blunt enough that, said to a customer, it can read as
unhelpful; the first leaves the impression that the speaker would answer if only
they could. Note also that `かねます` is not a euphemism for "maybe later" - it is a
real refusal, and a Japanese listener hears it as one.

### ～かねる vs ～かねない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - despite the shapes, these are not a positive-negative pair.
`かねる` = **cannot**. `かねない` = **might well**, and only of bad outcomes.

</div>

<pre>
その{f|判断|はんだん}には{f|同意|どうい}し<b>かねます</b>。
I cannot agree with that decision.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|判断|はんだん}は{f|事故|じこ}につながりかねません。
That decision could well lead to an accident.
</pre>

The first is a refusal by the speaker; the second is a warning about the world.
Historically `かねる` meant "find hard to bear", so "hard to bear that it might
happen" drifted into "it may well happen" - which is how one form ended up facing
in two directions.

### ～かねる vs ～がたい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `がたい` says the thing is **hard to do at all**, for anyone.
`かねる` says **this speaker will not do it**, here, now.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}の{f|説明|せつめい}は{f|信|しん}じ<b>がたい</b>。
His explanation is hard to believe.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|説明|せつめい}では{f|納得|なっとく}し<b>かねます</b>。
I'm afraid that explanation doesn't satisfy me.
</pre>

`がたい` is a judgement on the thing - anyone would struggle to believe it - and it
attaches happily to verbs of perception and evaluation
({f|忘|わす}れがたい, {f|理解|りかい}しがたい). `かねる` reports the speaker's own
stance and belongs to the language of dealing with people. `にくい` is a third,
lower-register member of this family, and it covers **physical** awkwardness -
{f|書|か}きにくいペン - which neither of the other two can do.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|箱|はこ}は{f|重|おも}すぎて{f|持|も}ち<b>かねます</b>。 → ✅ この{f|箱|はこ}は{f|重|おも}すぎて{f|持|も}てません。
  Physical inability is the negative potential. `かねる` needs a decision, a
  judgement or a response to refuse.

- ❌ その{f|理由|りゆう}は<b>わかりかねません</b>。 → ✅ その{f|理由|りゆう}は<b>わかりかねます</b>。
  `かねません` would mean "it may well turn out that I understand". The refusal is
  the affirmative form.

- ❌ お{f|受|う}け<b>かねます</b>。 → ✅ お{f|受|う}け<b>しかねます</b>。
  The humble frame is お + ます-stem + する, so the `し` has to be there before
  `かねる`.

- ❌ ごめん、{f|明日|あした}は{f|行|い}き<b>かねる</b>。 → ✅ ごめん、{f|明日|あした}は{f|行|い}けない。
  Aimed at a friend, `かねる` sounds like a shop assistant reading from a script.

## Practice

**1.** {f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございませんが、{f|当日|とうじつ}のキャンセルはお{f|受|う}け____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お{f|受|う}け<b>しかねます</b>

The shop is refusing on the basis of its own rules, which is precisely the `かねる`
sense, and the humble frame お{f|受|う}けする supplies the `し`.
<code>{f|受|う}けられません</code> would be understood, but it is markedly blunter
at a counter.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|夜中|よなか}に{f|大声|おおごえ}で{f|騒|さわ}げば、{f|近所|きんじょ}から{f|苦情|くじょう}が（{f|来|き}かねます・{f|来|き}かねません）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|近所|きんじょ}から{f|苦情|くじょう}が<b>{f|来|き}かねません</b>。

The sentence warns about an unwelcome thing that may well happen, which is
`かねない`. <code>かねます</code> would be a refusal, and complaints are not
something the speaker can decline to make.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'm afraid I am not in a position to comment on another department's decision."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|他|ほか}の{f|部署|ぶしょ}の{f|決定|けってい}については、コメントし<b>かねます</b>。

"Not in a position to" is the heart of `かねる`: the speaker could comment, but
their role does not allow it. Keep the ます form - this is the register in which
the English sentence would be said, and plain
<code>コメントしかねる</code> would sound like a line from a report.



</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: どのプランにするか、まだ{f|決|き}めるかねています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ どのプランにするか、まだ{f|決|き}め<b>かねています</b>。
"I still can't decide which plan to go with."

`かねる` attaches to the ます-stem ({f|決|き}め), never to the dictionary form. The
progressive <code>かねている</code> is the natural shape here, because being stuck
is a state that has lasted a while.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) その{f|提案|ていあん}には{f|賛成|さんせい}し<b>かねます</b>。
(b) その{f|提案|ていあん}には{f|賛成|さんせい}できません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) frames the disagreement as something the speaker's position or judgement will
not permit. It is measured, it keeps the meeting civil, and it is the sentence you
would say in front of people you have to keep working with.

(b) is a direct statement of refusal. Nothing about it is rude in itself, but it
puts the speaker squarely behind the no, and in a formal discussion it lands
noticeably harder than (a).

</details>
