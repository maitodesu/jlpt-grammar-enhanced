# かえって

**Meaning** · on the contrary; if anything - the result came out backwards from what was intended or expected

Somebody tries to fix something and makes it worse. Somebody takes the fast route
and arrives later. `かえって` is the word that marks that reversal, and it always
points at a real outcome, not at an opinion.

## Usage

`かえって` is an ordinary adverb, so unlike それなのに or しかし it lives **inside**
the second clause rather than in front of the sentence. It normally sits just
before the verb or adjective that names the backwards result.

- After a ～たら / ～と clause: {f|急|いそ}いだら、かえって{f|時間|じかん}がかかった。
- After a full stop: {f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けた。かえって{f|暑|あつ}くなった。
- After a ～ては clause: {f|手伝|てつだ}っては、かえって{f|迷惑|めいわく}だろう。

Two things have to be in place. First, an action or a condition that gave you a
reason to expect one thing. Second, an outcome that is the reverse of it.
`かえって` cannot open a conversation, because on its own it has no expectation to
overturn.

Usually written in kana. The kanji {f|却|かえ}って appears in print, and is worth
recognising.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|近道|ちかみち}をしたら、<b>かえって</b>{f|時間|じかん}がかかった。
I took a shortcut and it actually took longer.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けたら、<b>かえって</b>{f|暑|あつ}くなった。
I opened the window and it just got hotter.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|手伝|てつだ}おうとしたが、{f|不慣|ふな}れで<b>かえって</b>{f|邪魔|じゃま}になってしまった。
I tried to help, but I didn't know what I was doing and only got in the way.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもに{f|勉強|べんきょう}しろと{f|言|い}いすぎると、<b>かえって</b>やる{f|気|き}をなくしてしまうものだ。
Push a child to study too hard and you end up killing their motivation instead.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and common in speech and writing alike. Nothing about it is
formal or casual, and it is not marked for age or gender.

The reversal it reports is usually unwelcome - the medicine that keeps you awake,
the apology that makes things worse - but it does not have to be. 
{f|遅|おく}れたおかげで、かえってゆっくり{f|座|すわ}れた, "being late actually got me a
seat", is perfectly natural. What is fixed is the reversal, not the misfortune.

It is also the polite way to decline a return favour. When someone gives you
something back for a gift, the set line is
かえって{f|気|き}をつかわせてしまって、{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません - "I've ended up
putting you to trouble instead". Learning that one sentence buys you a very
common social moment.

## Contrast

### かえって vs むしろ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `かえって` reports a **result** that came out backwards.
`むしろ` states the speaker's **judgement** that of two descriptions, the second
fits better.

</div>

<pre>
{f|謝|あやま}ったら、<b>かえって</b>{f|怒|おこ}らせてしまった。
I apologised and only made him angrier.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|冷|つめ}たいというより、<b>むしろ</b>{f|照|て}れているのだと{f|思|おも}う。
He isn't cold so much as embarrassed, I'd say.
</pre>

An English test that works surprisingly well: if you can say "and it only made
things worse" or "it ended up the other way round", the word is `かえって`. If you
can say "if anything, I'd say…", it is `むしろ`. The first describes the world,
the second describes the speaker's take on it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|兄|あに}より、かえって{f|弟|おとうと}のほうが{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}い。 → ✅ {f|兄|あに}より、<b>むしろ</b>{f|弟|おとうと}のほうが{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}い。
  Simply comparing two people is not a reversal of anything. Without a thwarted
  intention behind it, `かえって` has nothing to reverse.

- ❌ かえって、{f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るそうです。 → ✅ ところが、{f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るそうです。
  `かえって` is not a sentence connector meaning "however". It needs a stated
  action or condition in the same breath, and it sits next to the verb.

- ❌ {f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}んだら、{f|熱|ねつ}がかえって{f|下|さ}がった。 → ✅ {f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}んだら、{f|熱|ねつ}が{f|下|さ}がった。
  Medicine bringing a fever down is exactly what was expected, so nothing has
  been reversed. Adding `かえって` makes the sentence contradict itself.

## Practice

**1.** {f|雨|あめ}の{f|日|ひ}に{f|車|くるま}で{f|行|い}ったら、{f|渋滞|じゅうたい}で____{f|遅|おそ}くなった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|渋滞|じゅうたい}で<b>かえって</b>{f|遅|おそ}くなった。

Taking the car was meant to save time in the rain and did the opposite. The
adverb goes immediately before the phrase that states the backwards outcome.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural adverb:
{f|静|しず}かな{f|場所|ばしょ}より、（かえって・むしろ）{f|少|すこ}し{f|音|おと}がある{f|場所|ばしょ}のほうが{f|集中|しゅうちゅう}できる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>むしろ</b>{f|少|すこ}し{f|音|おと}がある{f|場所|ばしょ}のほうが{f|集中|しゅうちゅう}できる。

Nothing has gone wrong here; the speaker is choosing between two kinds of place
and saying which one suits them. That preference between stated alternatives is
`むしろ`, not `かえって`.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}んだら、{f|眠|ねむ}れなくなった。
(b) {f|薬|くすり}を{f|飲|の}んだら、<b>かえって</b>{f|眠|ねむ}れなくなった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Sentence (a) just reports two events in order: the pill was taken, and sleep did
not come. Sentence (b) adds the speaker's point - the pill was taken *in order*
to sleep, and it produced the exact opposite. `かえって` is what tells the
listener that an intention was defeated rather than merely unfulfilled.

</details>
