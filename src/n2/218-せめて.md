# せめて

**Meaning** · at least - the reduced version of a wish that cannot be had in full

`せめて` is a compromise, spoken aloud. The speaker has accepted that the thing
they really want is out of reach, and names the smallest version they could live
with instead.

## Usage

`せめて` is an adverb and stands in front of the reduced amount. The rule that
matters is about what comes *after* it:

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `せめて` belongs to a **wish, a request or a plan**, never to a
plain statement of fact. Expect ～たい, ～てほしい, ～てください,
～たかった, ～ばよかった, or a volitional.

</div>

- せめて{f|一目|ひとめ}{f|会|あ}いたい
- せめて{f|連絡|れんらく}くらいしてほしかった
- せめて{f|今日|きょう}だけは{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}ろう

Three particles do most of the work alongside it, and they all say "if not the
whole thing, then this much":

- <code>せめて～だけでも</code> - せめて{f|水|みず}だけでも{f|飲|の}んでください
- <code>せめて～くらい(は)</code> - せめてあいさつくらいはしてほしい
- <code>せめて～だけは</code> - せめてこれだけは{f|守|まも}ってください

Because the whole word is built on giving something up, the sentence usually
contains the abandoned ideal too: <code>{f|全部|ぜんぶ}は{f|無理|むり}でも、せめて…</code>,
<code>{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}できなくても、せめて…</code>.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|全部|ぜんぶ}は{f|覚|おぼ}えられないが、<b>せめて</b>{f|名前|なまえ}だけは{f|覚|おぼ}えたい。
I can't memorise all of it, but I want to learn at least the names.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>せめて</b>{f|電話|でんわ}くらいしてほしかった。
You could at least have phoned.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|忙|いそが}しいのは{f|分|わ}かるけれど、<b>せめて</b>{f|朝|あさ}ごはんだけでも{f|食|た}べていったら?
I know you're busy, but couldn't you at least eat something before you go?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}は{f|難|むずか}しいだろうが、<b>せめて</b>{f|一勝|いっしょう}はして{f|帰|かえ}りたい。
Winning the whole thing is probably beyond us, but I'd like to take at least one match home.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and very common in speech. It works with plain and polite forms
alike and is not marked for age or gender.

Its emotional colour is not neutral, though. `せめて` always carries a shadow of
disappointment, because it announces that the real hope has already been written
off. In <code>せめて{f|連絡|れんらく}くらいしてほしかった</code> the complaint is
audible: you did not have to come, but you could have done *this much*. That makes
it a common opening for reproach, and a risky word to use about something someone
has given you - <code>せめてこれくらいはいただきたい</code> tells your host that
what they offered fell short.

Turned on yourself it is modest and rather appealing:
<code>せめて{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|分|ぶん}だけは{f|払|はら}わせてください</code> - if I
can't treat you, at least let me cover my own share.

## Contrast

### せめて vs {f|少|すく}なくとも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|少|すく}なくとも</code> states an **objective**
minimum: the true figure is this or higher. `せめて` states a **desired** minimum:
this is the least the speaker would accept.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}には<b>{f|少|すく}なくとも</b>{f|三百人|さんびゃくにん}は{f|来|き}ていた。
There were at least three hundred people in the hall.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>せめて</b>{f|三百人|さんびゃくにん}は{f|集|あつ}めたいところだ。
We'd like to get at least three hundred people in.
</pre>

The first is a count. It reports what happened, and `せめて` cannot do that:
❌<code>せめて{f|三百人|さんびゃくにん}{f|来|き}た</code> is not Japanese. The second
is a target, and <code>{f|少|すく}なくとも</code> would flatten it into arithmetic.

<code>{f|少|すく}なくとも</code> has a second life in argument, meaning "at any rate,
whatever else may be true" - <code>{f|少|すく}なくとも{f|私|わたし}はそう{f|思|おも}う</code>.
`せめて` has no such use.

### せめて vs せいぜい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `せめて` is a floor under a **wish**; `せいぜい` is a ceiling
over an **estimate**.

</div>

<pre>
<b>せめて</b>{f|一日|いちにち}くらい{f|休|やす}みたい。
I'd like at least one day off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|休|やす}めるのは<b>せいぜい</b>{f|一日|いちにち}だろう。
A day off is about the most I'll get.
</pre>

Same number, opposite direction. The first asks for it, the second concedes it.
Learners meeting the two glosses "at least" and "at most" side by side reliably mix
them up; the test is whether the sentence is describing what the speaker *wants* or
what they *expect*.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ せめて{f|三人|さんにん}{f|来|き}ました。 → ✅ <b>{f|少|すく}なくとも</b>{f|三人|さんにん}{f|来|き}ました。
  A completed fact has no wish in it. `せめて` cannot report attendance.

- ❌ せめて{f|千円|せんえん}しか{f|持|も}っていない。 → ✅ <b>せいぜい</b>{f|千円|せんえん}くらいしか{f|持|も}っていない。
  <code>しか～ない</code> caps the amount, which is the opposite of what `せめて`
  does.

- ❌ せめて{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|終|お}わらせたい。 → ✅ せめて<b>{f|半分|はんぶん}だけでも</b>{f|終|お}わらせたい。
  `せめて` introduces the scaled-down target, not the full ideal. Naming the whole
  thing after it contradicts the word.

## Practice

**1.** {f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}く{f|時間|じかん}はないけれど、____{f|近|ちか}くの{f|海|うみ}くらいは{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}きたい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>せめて</b>{f|近|ちか}くの{f|海|うみ}くらいは{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}きたい。

The first clause abandons the real plan, and the second names a smaller version of
it with <code>たい</code>. Both halves of the `せめて` pattern are present: a wish,
and an ideal already given up on.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: きのうのパーティーには、せめて{f|二十人|にじゅうにん}{f|来|き}ました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ きのうのパーティーには、<b>{f|少|すく}なくとも</b>{f|二十人|にじゅうにん}{f|来|き}ました。

The sentence reports a fact about a party that has already happened, so there is
nothing for `せめて` to wish for. <code>{f|少|すく}なくとも</code> is the word for an
objective floor under a count.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) せめて{f|一言|ひとこと}{f|説明|せつめい}してほしかった。
(b) {f|一言|ひとこと}{f|説明|せつめい}してほしかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b) is a straightforward complaint: I wanted a word of explanation and did not get
one.

(a) adds the compromise. `せめて` concedes that a full account was perhaps too much
to ask for, and then points out that even the smallest possible version - one
sentence - was withheld. Paradoxically that makes the reproach sharper, because it
shows the speaker had already lowered the bar.

</details>
