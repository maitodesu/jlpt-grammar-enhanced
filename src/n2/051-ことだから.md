# ～ことだから

**Meaning** · knowing them as we both do, it follows that… - a prediction argued from someone's established character

`ことだから` does not explain what happened. It reaches into what you and your
listener **already know about a person** and predicts from it: *he's the sort who
always…, so of course he'll…*. The reason it gives is a reputation, and the clause
that follows is a guess, never a report. Socially it is a vouching move - you are
staking your prediction on shared knowledge, and inviting the listener to agree.

## Formation

`こと` here is a noun, so the thing in front joins with `の`. The overwhelmingly
common shape is **a person the two of you know** + `の` + `ことだから`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Person (name) | Noun + の + ことだから | {f|田中|たなか}さん → {f|田中|たなか}さんのことだから |
| Person (pronoun) | Noun + の + ことだから | {f|彼|かれ} → {f|彼|かれ}のことだから |
| Described person | Modifier + Noun + の + ことだから | {f|優|やさ}しい{f|母|はは} → {f|優|やさ}しい{f|母|はは}のことだから |
| Group or organisation | Noun + の + ことだから | あの{f|会社|かいしゃ} → あの{f|会社|かいしゃ}のことだから |
| A known circumstance | Noun + の + ことだから | {f|初|はじ}めて → {f|初|はじ}めてのことだから |
| な-adjective | ～な + ことだから | {f|急|きゅう}だ → {f|急|きゅう}なことだから |
| Polite | ～のことですから | {f|部長|ぶちょう} → {f|部長|ぶちょう}のことですから |
| Typical ending | …だろう／に{f|違|ちが}いない／はずだ／でしょう | きっと{f|来|く}るだろう |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the `の` is not optional, and the main clause must be a
**conjecture**. ❌<code>{f|彼|かれ}ことだから</code> and
❌<code>{f|彼|かれ}のことだから、{f|昨日|きのう}{f|来|き}ました</code> are both broken.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`ことだから` runs on shared knowledge. If your listener has never heard of the person,
the sentence collapses, because there is no reputation to reason from - you would
have to spell the character out first, or use plain `から`. This is why the noun so
often comes with a describing phrase attached:
<code>{f|心配性|しんぱいしょう}の{f|姉|あね}</code>,
<code>{f|時間|じかん}に{f|正確|せいかく}な{f|彼|かれ}</code>. The modifier is doing the
work of reminding the listener what you both know.

The tone is usually warm or wry. Said about a friend it is affectionate; said about
a company or an official body it is often faintly cynical -
「あの{f|役所|やくしょ}のことだから、{f|返事|へんじ}は{f|来月|らいげつ}になるよ」. It is not
neutral either way; there is always a judgement of character riding along.

Register is unremarkable - `のことだから` in casual speech, `のことですから` in polite
speech and in letters. It is common in both, and there is no written/spoken split
worth worrying about.

The one thing it cannot do is state a fact. `ことだから` sets up a guess, so the
sentence ends in だろう, でしょう, はずだ, に{f|違|ちが}いない, かもしれない, or a
prediction phrased as a plain non-past. A past-tense report after it is wrong, even
if the report happens to match the person's character.

## Contrast

### ～ことだから vs ～ものだから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ものだから` excuses something that **already happened**;
`ことだから` predicts something that **has not happened yet**.

</div>

The two look alike and do opposite jobs. `ものだから` takes a whole clause in front
of it and faces backwards; `ことだから` takes a person's name and faces forwards.

<pre>
{f|道|みち}が{f|混|こ}んでいた<b>ものだから</b>、{f|開演|かいえん}に{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わなかった。
The traffic was bad, so I didn't make it before the curtain.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|慎重|しんちょう}な{f|彼|かれ}<b>のことだから</b>、{f|一時間|いちじかん}{f|前|まえ}には{f|着|つ}いているだろう。
Careful as he is, he'll have been there an hour early.
</pre>

Swapping them is not a nuance error but a structural one: ❌<code>{f|彼|かれ}のものだから</code>
means "it belongs to him", and ❌<code>{f|道|みち}が{f|混|こ}んでいたことだから</code>
is not Japanese at all.

### ～ことだから vs ～から

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `から` needs no shared knowledge and accepts any main clause.
`ことだから` needs both a known character and a conjecture.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|朝|あさ}が{f|苦手|にがて}だ<b>から</b>、{f|早朝|そうちょう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}はやめよう。
He's not a morning person, so let's drop the early meeting.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|朝|あさ}が{f|苦手|にがて}な{f|彼|かれ}<b>のことだから</b>、{f|早朝|そうちょう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}には{f|来|こ}ないだろう。
Knowing how bad he is in the mornings, he won't show up to an early meeting.
</pre>

The `から` sentence proposes an action; the `ことだから` sentence forecasts a
behaviour. Only `から` can be followed by やめよう.

### ～ことだから vs ～ことだし

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ことだから` argues from **who someone is**. `ことだし` piles up
**circumstances** - and there is usually more than one - to justify a decision.

</div>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}も{f|降|ふ}っている<b>ことだし</b>、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|家|いえ}で{f|過|す}ごそう。
It's raining, among other things, so let's stay in today.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|出無精|でぶしょう}の{f|兄|あに}<b>のことだから</b>、{f|誘|さそ}っても{f|来|こ}ないと{f|思|おも}う。
My brother being the homebody he is, I doubt he'd come even if we asked.
</pre>

`ことだし` leads to what the speaker will *do*; `ことだから` leads to what someone else
will *turn out to have done*. They are not interchangeable in either direction.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}<b>ことだから</b>、きっと{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}だ。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}<b>のことだから</b>、きっと{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}だ。
  <code>こと</code> is a noun and the phrase in front modifies it, so `の` is
  obligatory.

- ❌ {f|田中|たなか}さんのことだから、{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|来|き}ました。 → ✅ {f|田中|たなか}さんのことだから、{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}にも{f|来|き}ていたはずだ。
  The main clause has to be a conjecture. A flat report of what happened leaves the
  reputation with nothing to do.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った<b>ことだから</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った<b>から</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。
  `ことだから` is not a general-purpose "because". It cannot take an ordinary event
  clause as its reason.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}{f|会|あ}ったばかりの{f|人|ひと}のことだから、たぶん{f|来|こ}ないだろう。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}{f|会|あ}ったばかりだから、{f|来|く}るかどうか{f|分|わ}からない。
  You cannot argue from a character neither of you knows. `ことだから` presupposes
  that the listener recognises the person and will nod along.

## Practice

**1.** {f|時間|じかん}に{f|正確|せいかく}な{f|山田|やまだ}さん____、もう{f|会場|かいじょう}に{f|着|つ}いているでしょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|山田|やまだ}さん<b>のことですから</b>

The sentence reasons from a known trait - punctuality - to a guess about where he is
now, which is exactly the job of <code>のことだから</code>. Note the `の`, and note
that the ending でしょう supplies the required conjecture.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|急|きゅう}に{f|熱|ねつ}が{f|出|で}た（ことだから・ものだから）、{f|旅行|りょこう}を{f|取|と}り{f|消|け}しました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|急|きゅう}に{f|熱|ねつ}が{f|出|で}た<b>ものだから</b>、{f|旅行|りょこう}を{f|取|と}り{f|消|け}しました。

This is an account of something already done, offered as an excuse - `ものだから`.
`ことだから` would need a person's name in front of it and a prediction after it,
and it has neither here.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Knowing how stubborn my father is, he probably hasn't changed his mind."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|頑固|がんこ}な{f|父|ちち}<b>のことだから</b>、たぶん{f|考|かんが}えを{f|変|か}えていないだろう。

The describing phrase <code>{f|頑固|がんこ}な</code> reminds the listener of the trait
being reasoned from, and だろう supplies the conjecture. Dropping the `の` or ending
in a bare past tense would both break the pattern.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|優|やさ}しい{f|先生|せんせい}のことですから、{f|昨日|きのう}{f|遅刻|ちこく}した{f|学生|がくせい}を{f|叱|しか}りました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|優|やさ}しい{f|先生|せんせい}のことですから、{f|遅刻|ちこく}した{f|学生|がくせい}も{f|叱|しか}らなかったでしょう。

Two problems in the original: a past-tense report where a conjecture belongs, and a
prediction that contradicts the trait being cited. `ことだから` only works when the
main clause is what that character would lead you to expect.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|準備|じゅんび}がいい<b>から</b>、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っているだろう。
(b) {f|準備|じゅんび}のいい{f|彼女|かのじょ}<b>のことだから</b>、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っているだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) states a fact about her and draws a guess from it. It works even if the listener
has never met her - the sentence supplies the information it needs.

(b) does not supply the information so much as *invoke* it. The speaker is treating
her thoroughness as something the two of them already agree on, and the sentence has
a nod in it: "you know what she's like." It also sounds a shade more confident, and
faintly fond.

</details>
