# 一気に

**Meaning** · in one go; in a single burst, without stopping partway

<code>{f|一気|いっき}に</code> (いっきに) is one breath, one push. Whatever the action
is, it runs from start to finish without a pause - and often with more force than
usual.

## Usage

An adverb, in front of the verb. It combines with three kinds of predicate.

- **Consuming or finishing something in one stretch**: {f|飲|の}む, {f|食|た}べる,
  {f|読|よ}む, {f|書|か}く, {f|片付|かたづ}ける, {f|終|お}わらせる
- **A steep change in a quantity**: {f|下|さ}がる, {f|上|あ}がる, {f|増|ふ}える,
  {f|減|へ}る
- **Covering distance or ground in one move**: {f|駆|か}け{f|上|あ}がる,
  {f|追|お}い{f|抜|ぬ}く, {f|進|すす}む

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `一気に` says there was **no break in the middle**. If the
action was done in instalments, the word is wrong however fast it went.

</div>

It therefore sits badly with anything that must be spread out, and it clashes
outright with <code>{f|少|すこ}しずつ</code> and <code>{f|次第|しだい}に</code>.

The noun {f|一気|いっき} barely stands alone in modern Japanese. It survives in
<code>{f|一気|いっき}{f|飲|の}み</code>, downing a drink in one - a piece of student
drinking culture with a bad reputation and a chant to match - and in
<code>{f|一気|いっき}に…{f|片|かた}をつける</code>. Use the adverbial form with `に`
and nothing else.

## Examples

<pre>
のどが{f|渇|かわ}いていたので、{f|水|みず}を<b>{f|一気|いっき}に</b>{f|飲|の}んだ。
I was thirsty, so I drank the water straight down.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|続|つづ}きが{f|気|き}になって、この{f|本|ほん}を<b>{f|一気|いっき}に</b>{f|読|よ}んでしまった。
I had to know what happened next, so I read the book in one sitting.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日|ひ}が{f|暮|く}れると、{f|気温|きおん}が<b>{f|一気|いっき}に</b>{f|下|さ}がった。
Once the sun went down the temperature dropped sharply.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|残|のこ}っていた{f|仕事|しごと}を<b>{f|一気|いっき}に</b>{f|片付|かたづ}けて、{f|久|ひさ}しぶりに{f|定時|ていじ}で{f|帰|かえ}った。
I cleared the rest of my work in one push and got away on time for the first time in ages.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and very common in both speech and writing. Nothing about it is
formal or slangy, and it is not marked for age or gender.

What it adds is **momentum**. A sentence with `一気に` sounds energetic, decisive,
sometimes reckless: the speaker did not stop to think, and there was no
opportunity for the thing to lose steam. That is why sports commentary, business
writing and headlines all reach for it -
<code>{f|後半|こうはん}に{f|一気|いっき}に{f|攻|せ}めた</code>,
<code>{f|株価|かぶか}が{f|一気|いっき}に{f|回復|かいふく}した</code>.

Because momentum is the point, `一気に` can carry a warning as easily as praise.
<code>{f|一気|いっき}に{f|飲|の}ませる</code> describes something genuinely
dangerous, and <code>{f|一気|いっき}に{f|決|き}めてしまった</code> can mean a decision
was rushed. Read the surrounding sentence for which colour is meant.

## Contrast

### {f|一気|いっき}に vs {f|一度|いちど}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|一気|いっき}に</code> means **without stopping**;
<code>{f|一度|いちど}に</code> means **at the same time**, in one batch.

</div>

<pre>
{f|階段|かいだん}を<b>{f|一気|いっき}に</b>{f|駆|か}け{f|上|あ}がった。
I ran up the stairs without a break.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|二人|ふたり}に<b>{f|一度|いちど}に</b>{f|話|はな}しかけられて、{f|返事|へんじ}ができなかった。
Both of them spoke to me at once and I couldn't answer either.
</pre>

The stairs are a single continuous effort - a duration with no pause in it. The
two people are simultaneous, and no effort is being sustained at all, so
`一気に` has nothing to describe.

Where both are possible the emphasis shifts.
<code>{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|一気|いっき}に{f|運|はこ}んだ</code> praises the unbroken
effort; <code>{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|一度|いちど}に{f|運|はこ}んだ</code> says you did not
need a second trip.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|三人|さんにん}に<b>{f|一気|いっき}に</b>{f|質問|しつもん}された。 → ✅ {f|三人|さんにん}に<b>{f|一度|いちど}に</b>{f|質問|しつもん}された。
  Three simultaneous questions are a batch, not a sustained push.

- ❌ <b>{f|一気|いっき}な</b>{f|変化|へんか}に{f|驚|おどろ}いた。 → ✅ <b>{f|急|きゅう}な</b>{f|変化|へんか}に{f|驚|おどろ}いた。
  `一気に` is an adverb only. There is no な-adjective {f|一気|いっき}な, and the noun
  cannot modify {f|変化|へんか} directly either.

- ❌ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|少|すこ}しずつ<b>{f|一気|いっき}に</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えた。 → ✅ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|少|すこ}しずつ{f|覚|おぼ}えた。
  The two adverbs contradict each other. Something learned in daily instalments is
  by definition not done in one go.

## Practice

**1.** {f|坂道|さかみち}を{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}で____{f|上|のぼ}りきったので、{f|息|いき}が{f|切|き}れている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|坂道|さかみち}を{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}で<b>{f|一気|いっき}に</b>{f|上|のぼ}りきった。

<code>{f|上|のぼ}りきる</code> already means getting all the way up, and
<code>{f|息|いき}が{f|切|き}れる</code> tells you it was done in one unbroken effort.
That is `一気に` exactly.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|洗濯物|せんたくもの}を（{f|一気|いっき}に / {f|一度|いちど}に）{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|洗濯機|せんたくき}に{f|入|い}れた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|洗濯物|せんたくもの}を<b>{f|一度|いちど}に</b>{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|洗濯機|せんたくき}に{f|入|い}れた。

Loading everything in a single batch is simultaneity, not stamina. Choosing
<code>{f|一気|いっき}に</code> would suggest a strenuous continuous act, which
stuffing a washing machine is not - though it would be fine if you were describing
getting all the laundry done in one unbroken session.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) レポートを{f|三日|みっか}かけて{f|書|か}いた。
(b) レポートを{f|一気|いっき}に{f|書|か}き{f|上|あ}げた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply states how long it took, and implies the work was spread over three
days.

(b) says it was written in one sitting, start to finish. `一気に` adds both speed
and drive, and <code>{f|書|か}き{f|上|あ}げた</code> confirms that the momentum
carried all the way to the end.

</details>
