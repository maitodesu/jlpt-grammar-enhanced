# ～に加えて

**Meaning** · in addition to; on top of - adds a second item to one already named,
in a report voice

<code>に{f|加|くわ}えて</code> is the て-form of <code>{f|加|くわ}える</code>, "to add",
frozen into a connector. Whatever it follows is the thing already on the table; what
comes after is the second item being put beside it. It is arithmetic rather than
emotion - the sentence does not say the addition is welcome or unwelcome, only that
it is there.

## Formation

The single most important fact about this pattern: **it attaches to a noun.** A
clause has to be nominalised first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + に{f|加|くわ}えて | {f|会費|かいひ} → {f|会費|かいひ}に{f|加|くわ}えて |
| Noun (written, clipped) | Noun + に{f|加|くわ}え | {f|高騰|こうとう} → {f|高騰|こうとう}に{f|加|くわ}え |
| Verb (nominalised) | plain form + こと + に{f|加|くわ}えて | {f|広|ひろ}がる → {f|広|ひろ}がったことに{f|加|くわ}えて |
| い-adjective (nominalised) | ～こと／～さ + に{f|加|くわ}えて | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}いことに{f|加|くわ}えて |
| な-adjective (nominalised) | ～なこと／～さ + に{f|加|くわ}えて | {f|静|しず}かな → {f|静|しず}かなことに{f|加|くわ}えて |
| Sentence opener | {f|加|くわ}えて、… | - → {f|加|くわ}えて、{f|費用|ひよう}も{f|上|あ}がる |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the added element normally takes も or まで. Without it the sentence still
parses, but it reads as though the second item were a separate announcement rather
than an addition: …に{f|加|くわ}えて、{f|送料|そうりょう}<b>も</b>かかります。

</div>

On top of the membership fee, there is also a charge for materials.
</pre>

<pre>

As well as English, she's learning Spanish.
</pre>

On top of the strong wind the swell was high too, so the ferry was cancelled.
</pre>

Our opening hours are being cut from next month. In addition, we will close all day on Sundays.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is written Japanese. It belongs to news copy, notices, contracts, minutes,
academic prose and formal presentation, and it is what a company writes when it has
two things to tell you. Said to a friend in a café it lands like a press release;
there, それに or <code>その{f|上|うえ}</code> is what you want.

The clipped form <code>に{f|加|くわ}え</code>, without て, is one notch more formal
again, and is standard in headlines and in the middle of long written sentences.
Bare <code>{f|加|くわ}えて、</code> at the head of a sentence is the same word doing
the job of "In addition," in an English paragraph.

Because the pattern is neutral, it is the safe choice when the two items are simply
facts - fees, requirements, causes, symptoms. It does not scold and it does not
enthuse. In practice the two items usually do point the same way, since people
rarely bother to add a good thing to a bad one, but that is a habit of use rather
than a rule the grammar enforces: <code>{f|上|うえ}に</code> would actually be blocked
in a mixed sentence, and this pattern merely sounds odd.

One genuine convenience: unlike <code>{f|上|うえ}に</code>, the second half may be a
request or an instruction. <code>{f|印鑑|いんかん}に{f|加|くわ}えて{f|通帳|つうちょう}もお{f|持|も}ちください</code>
is normal officialese.

## Contrast

### に{f|加|くわ}えて vs {f|上|うえ}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|加|くわ}えて</code> follows a **noun** and reports;
<code>{f|上|うえ}に</code> follows a **clause** and evaluates.

</div>

<pre>
{f|運賃|うんちん}の{f|値上|ねあ}げ<b>に{f|加|くわ}えて</b>、{f|本数|ほんすう}の{f|削減|さくげん}も{f|発表|はっぴょう}された。
Along with the fare rise, a cut in the number of services was announced.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|運賃|うんちん}が{f|上|あ}がった<b>{f|上|うえ}に</b>、{f|本数|ほんすう}まで{f|減|へ}らされた。
Fares went up, and on top of that they cut the number of services.
</pre>

The first is a transport authority's announcement; the second is a commuter's
complaint. Note the shape as well as the tone: the first hangs off the noun
<code>{f|値上|ねあ}げ</code>, the second off the finished clause
<code>{f|運賃|うんちん}が{f|上|あ}がった</code>. Getting this backwards -
❌<code>{f|上|あ}がるに{f|加|くわ}えて</code> - is the single commonest error with this
pattern.

### に{f|加|くわ}えて vs だけでなく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だけでなく` **corrects an assumption** ("not only A").
<code>に{f|加|くわ}えて</code> makes no claim about what you assumed; it just adds.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|制度|せいど}は{f|社員|しゃいん}<b>だけでなく</b>アルバイトにも{f|適用|てきよう}される。
This scheme applies not just to permanent staff but to part-timers as well.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|制度|せいど}は{f|社員|しゃいん}<b>に{f|加|くわ}えて</b>アルバイトにも{f|適用|てきよう}される。
This scheme applies to part-timers in addition to permanent staff.
</pre>

`だけでなく` says the reader probably thought the scheme stopped at permanent staff,
and pushes the boundary outwards. <code>に{f|加|くわ}えて</code> simply lists two
covered groups. Where the point of the sentence is that the second item is
*surprising*, the additive connector wastes the effect.

### に{f|加|くわ}えて vs のみならず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are formal, but <code>のみならず</code> carries the "not
merely" argument; <code>に{f|加|くわ}えて</code> carries no argument at all.

</div>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}の{f|被害|ひがい}は{f|農地|のうち}<b>に{f|加|くわ}えて</b>{f|漁港|ぎょこう}にも{f|及|およ}んだ。
The typhoon damage extended to the fishing port as well as to farmland.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}の{f|被害|ひがい}は{f|農地|のうち}<b>のみならず</b>{f|漁港|ぎょこう}にまで{f|及|およ}んだ。
The typhoon damage reached not merely the farmland but even the fishing port.
</pre>

<code>のみならず</code> also attaches to whole clauses, which
<code>に{f|加|くわ}えて</code> cannot do without a nominaliser. If you find yourself
adding こと just to make the grammar fit, check whether
<code>のみならず</code> or <code>{f|上|うえ}に</code> was the shape you actually wanted.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|安|やす}い<b>に{f|加|くわ}えて</b>{f|広|ひろ}い。 → ✅ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|安|やす}い<b>ことに{f|加|くわ}えて</b>{f|広|ひろ}い。（or {f|安|やす}い{f|上|うえ}に{f|広|ひろ}い）
  The pattern needs a noun in front of it. An adjective or verb has to be
  nominalised with こと first, and in speech <code>{f|上|うえ}に</code> is simpler.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}<b>を{f|加|くわ}えて</b>{f|風|かぜ}も{f|強|つよ}かった。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}<b>に{f|加|くわ}えて</b>{f|風|かぜ}も{f|強|つよ}かった。
  <code>を{f|加|くわ}える</code> is the live verb "add something". The connector is
  fixed with に and never varies.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}さ、{f|残業|ざんぎょう}<b>に{f|加|くわ}えて</b>{f|終電|しゅうでん}も{f|逃|のが}したんだ。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}さ、{f|残業|ざんぎょう}した<b>{f|上|うえ}に</b>{f|終電|しゅうでん}も{f|逃|のが}したんだ。
  Not ungrammatical, just tone-deaf: a written connector in a grumble to a friend.
  Casual complaint takes <code>{f|上|うえ}に</code>, その{f|上|うえ} or おまけに.

## Practice

**1.** {f|入場料|にゅうじょうりょう}____、ガイドツアーの{f|料金|りょうきん}も{f|別|べつ}にかかります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|入場料|にゅうじょうりょう}<b>に{f|加|くわ}えて</b>、ガイドツアーの{f|料金|りょうきん}も{f|別|べつ}にかかります。

<code>{f|入場料|にゅうじょうりょう}</code> is a noun, so the pattern attaches directly,
and the notice register fits a fee announcement exactly. The も on the second charge
is what marks it as the added item.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|今日|きょう}は{f|寝坊|ねぼう}した（{f|上|うえ}に・に{f|加|くわ}えて）{f|鍵|かぎ}まで{f|失|な}くした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|寝坊|ねぼう}した<b>{f|上|うえ}に</b>{f|鍵|かぎ}まで{f|失|な}くした。

Two reasons. Grammatically, <code>{f|寝坊|ねぼう}した</code> is a clause, and
<code>に{f|加|くわ}えて</code> needs a noun. Tonally, this is a bad-day story, and the
report voice would be comic in the wrong way.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "In addition to the written test, an interview will be held."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|筆記試験|ひっきしけん}<b>に{f|加|くわ}えて</b>、{f|面接|めんせつ}も{f|行|おこな}われます。

Both items are nouns and the sentence is an official announcement, which is this
pattern's home ground. <code>に{f|加|くわ}え</code> without て would be equally correct
and a shade more formal.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|物価|ぶっか}が{f|上|あ}がるに{f|加|くわ}えて、{f|給料|きゅうりょう}は{f|下|さ}がっている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|物価|ぶっか}の{f|上昇|じょうしょう}<b>に{f|加|くわ}えて</b>、{f|給料|きゅうりょう}も{f|下|さ}がっている。

Two repairs. A plain verb cannot sit in front of the pattern, so the clause becomes
the noun <code>{f|上昇|じょうしょう}</code>. And は in the second half fights the
additive sense - it sets up a contrast - so も is the particle you want.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会員|かいいん}{f|証|しょう}に{f|加|くわ}えて、{f|保険証|ほけんしょう}もお{f|持|も}ちください。
(b) {f|会員|かいいん}{f|証|しょう}だけでなく、{f|保険証|ほけんしょう}もお{f|持|も}ちください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reads as a plain list of what to bring: two documents, stated neutrally. It is
what a clinic prints on a form.

(b) implies the reader may have been planning to bring only the membership card, and
corrects that. Spoken to someone at a counter it can sound faintly like a
reminder-with-eyebrows; on a printed notice it is fine, and useful precisely when
people do forget the second item.

</details>
