# もう少しで

**Meaning** · almost; nearly; a little more and…

<code>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</code> measures a remaining distance and finds it tiny. It
does two jobs with that one measurement: "we are nearly there", said of something
still coming, and "we nearly had a disaster", said of something that never arrived
at all.

## Usage

The word is transparent: <code>もう{f|少|すこ}し</code> is "a little more" and `で`
turns it into "with a little more". Everything then depends on what the rest of the
sentence does with that little.

With an ordinary **non-past** clause it means the thing is close and still on its
way - <code>もう{f|少|すこ}しで{f|春|はる}だ</code>, <code>もう{f|少|すこ}しで{f|着|つ}きます</code>.
Nothing is being avoided here; the sentence is simply counting down.

With a **near-miss ending** it means the opposite: the thing came within a hair and
did not happen. The endings that do this are
<code>ところだった</code>, <code>そうになった</code> and
<code>てしまうところだった</code>. This is worth being clear about, because
<code>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</code> **does not itself create** the "nearly" meaning - the
ending does. On its own in front of a plain past verb the adverb is helpless, which
is where the pattern's one real trap lies.

Two forms sit right beside it. <code>もうちょっとで</code> is the casual version and
is extremely common in speech. <code>あと{f|少|すこ}しで</code> is close to
interchangeable, and leans a shade more towards counting down what is left.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>{f|夏|なつ}だ。
It's nearly summer.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>{f|宿題|しゅくだい}が{f|終|お}わる。
I'm almost done with my homework.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>{f|大切|たいせつ}な{f|約束|やくそく}をすっぽかすところだった。
I came within a hair of standing someone up on an important appointment.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>{f|完成|かんせい}というところで、パソコンが{f|固|かた}まってしまった。
With the thing all but finished, the computer froze on me.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Completely register-neutral. It is as ordinary in a text message as in a report, it
carries no gender or age marking, and it needs no politeness adjustment of its own -
the ending of the sentence handles that.

Tone comes from which of the two uses you are in. The countdown use is cheerful and
encouraging; it is what you say to someone struggling up a hill. The near-miss use
is charged with relief, and in speech it usually arrives with あぶない, ああ or
よかった attached, plus a `ところだった` at the far end. Between the adverb and the
ending, a listener hears twice over that nothing bad actually happened.

One structural habit is worth copying: <code>もう{f|少|すこ}しで…というところで</code>
freezes a scene at the last moment before completion - もう{f|少|すこ}しで{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}というところで
{f|逆転|ぎゃくてん}された. It is a neat, natural-sounding shape and learners rarely
produce it.

## Contrast

### もう{f|少|すこ}しで vs もう{f|少|すこ}し

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with `で` it modifies a whole **event** ("a little more and X").
Without `で` it modifies a **quantity or degree** ("a little more X").

</div>

<pre>
<b>もう{f|少|すこ}し</b>{f|待|ま}ってください。
Please wait a little longer.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>{f|順番|じゅんばん}が{f|回|まわ}ってきます。
Your turn will come round very shortly.
</pre>

The first asks for more of something - more waiting, more salt, a bigger size. The
second says the event is nearly upon us. Dropping or adding the `で` changes which
of those two you said, and the mistake is common enough to be worth a deliberate
check.

### もう{f|少|すこ}しで vs {f|危|あや}うく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|危|あや}うく</code> is **only** for near misses, and only
bad ones. <code>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</code> covers near misses **and** ordinary
countdowns.

</div>

<pre>
<b>{f|危|あや}うく</b>{f|反対|はんたい}{f|方向|ほうこう}の{f|電車|でんしゃ}に{f|乗|の}るところだった。
I almost got on a train going the wrong way.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>{f|目的地|もくてきち}です。
We're nearly at our destination.
</pre>

Swap them and only one direction works.
<code>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</code> would be fine in the first sentence;
<code>{f|危|あや}うく</code> is impossible in the second, because arriving is not a
danger. <code>{f|危|あや}うく</code> is also the stronger and slightly more written
of the two, and it always wants a `ところだった` behind it.

### もう{f|少|すこ}しで vs まもなく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - in the countdown sense, <code>まもなく</code> measures **time
until** an event. <code>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</code> measures **how much is left** of
whatever you are doing.

</div>

<pre>
<b>まもなく</b>{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|参|まい}ります。
The train will arrive shortly.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>{f|頂上|ちょうじょう}だ。がんばろう。
Nearly at the summit - keep going.
</pre>

<code>まもなく</code> is an announcement word: stations, airports, broadcasts. It is
formal, it says nothing about effort, and it cannot be used for near misses at all.
<code>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</code> is personal and encouraging, and it is the one you
use when there is a remaining stretch someone has to get through.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>コップを{f|割|わ}った。 → ✅ <b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>コップを{f|割|わ}るところだった。
  A plain past verb says the glass broke, which contradicts
  <code>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</code>. The adverb needs a near-miss ending to lean on.

- ❌ <b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>{f|待|ま}ってください。 → ✅ <b>もう{f|少|すこ}し</b>{f|待|ま}ってください。
  Asking for more waiting is a matter of degree, so the `で` has no place. This is
  the single most frequent slip with this word.

- ❌ <b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>{f|大|おお}きいサイズはありますか。 → ✅ <b>もう{f|少|すこ}し</b>{f|大|おお}きいサイズはありますか。
  Same fault inside a noun phrase: what is "a little more" here is the size, not an
  event.

- ❌ <b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>{f|来年|らいねん}{f|留学|りゅうがく}するところだった。 → ✅ <b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>{f|留学|りゅうがく}をあきらめるところだった。
  A near miss is a matter of a hair's breadth, not of months.
  <code>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</code> will not stretch to a distant date.

## Practice

**1.** あぶない！ ____{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}とぶつかるところだった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>（or {f|危|あや}うく）

The `ところだった` ending is already there, so what the blank needs is the adverb
that measures how close it came. あぶない at the head of the sentence tells you it
is a near miss rather than a countdown.

</details>

**2.** Choose: すみません、（もう{f|少|すこ}し・もう{f|少|すこ}しで）{f|静|しず}かにしていただけますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>もう{f|少|すこ}し</b>{f|静|しず}かにしていただけますか。

You are asking for more quiet - a degree, not an event. The `で` form would try to
mean "a little more and it will be quiet", which is not a request at all.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) <b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>{f|仕事|しごと}が{f|終|お}わる。
(b) <b>もう{f|少|すこ}しで</b>{f|仕事|しごと}が{f|終|お}わらないところだった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a countdown. The work is nearly done and the speaker is reporting progress,
probably with some satisfaction.

(b) is a near miss, and the negative inside it is doing the work: the job very
nearly failed to get finished. The relief in (b) is the opposite feeling from the
steady progress in (a), and the only structural difference is the ending the adverb
is leaning on.

</details>
