# ～ものの

**Meaning** · but; although - the first clause is granted as true, and then the thing it led you to expect turns out not to have happened

`ものの` concedes a fact and immediately reports the shortfall. You applied, and did
not get in. You bought it, and never used it. The tone is not accusation; it is the
slightly deflated voice of someone reporting that reality did not follow through.

On this batch's two axes - how much the speaker minds, and how formal the frame is - 
`ものの` sits at *mildly resigned* and *decidedly written*. `のに` in the same slot
would sound aggrieved and would sound like speech; `ものの` sounds like a report.

## Formation

`ものの` follows a plain form. It began as the noun <code>もの</code>, which is why a
noun needs `である` and a な-adjective needs `な` in front of it.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + ものの | {f|通|かよ}う → {f|通|かよ}うものの |
| Verb (past) | plain past + ものの | {f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}んだ → {f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}んだものの |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + ものの | {f|届|とど}かない → {f|届|とど}かないものの |
| い-adjective | plain form + ものの | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}いものの |
| な-adjective | な + ものの | {f|便利|べんり} → {f|便利|べんり}なものの |
| Noun | である + ものの | {f|新人|しんじん} → {f|新人|しんじん}であるものの |
| Emphatic noun / な-adjective | ではある + ものの | {f|不安|ふあん} → {f|不安|ふあん}ではあるものの |
| Fixed opener | とはいうものの + remark | - → とはいうものの、{f|時間|じかん}がない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the clause after `ものの` **states what is actually so**. It is a
report, not a demand. A request, an invitation or an order does not go there:
❌<code>{f|高|たか}いものの、{f|買|か}ってください</code>. Use `が` or `けど` for that.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>とはいうものの</code> is the same word doing sentence-opener duty:
you have just stated something, and now you take part of it back. It is one of the
most useful written connectives at this level, and it is a shade less stiff than
`とはいえ`.

</div>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

I did reply, though I hadn't read it properly.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|免許|めんきょ}は{f|持|も}っている<b>ものの</b>、もう{f|十年|じゅうねん}{f|運転|うんてん}していない。
I have a licence, but I haven't driven in ten years.
</pre>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ものの` is a written-register word. It belongs in reports, articles, essays and
formal e-mail, and in speech it turns up mainly in prepared remarks - a presentation,
a speech at a leaving party - rather than in chatting. Nobody says
<code>チケットは{f|買|か}ったものの…</code> to a friend over coffee; they say
<code>{f|買|か}ったんだけどさ</code>. Learners who put `ものの` into casual conversation
sound like they are reading aloud.

There is no politeness switch on `ものの` itself. It is a conjunction in the middle of
the sentence, so politeness is decided at the end:
<code>{f|安|やす}いものの、おすすめしません</code> is perfectly polite.

Emotionally it is cool. `のに` leaks disappointment; `ものの` merely records it. That
detachment is why `ものの` is so common in self-description - people use it to admit a
gap in their own situation without whining about it. The first clause is very often
in the past and very often something the speaker themselves did, which is why
<code>～たものの</code> is the shape you meet most.

The two clauses usually share a subject, but they need not:
<code>{f|彼|かれ}は{f|約束|やくそく}したものの、{f|上司|じょうし}が{f|許|ゆる}さなかった</code>
is fine. That freedom is worth remembering, because the close relative `ながらも` is
much fussier about it.

## Contrast

### ものの vs のに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `のに` is a **complaint**, and it belongs to speech. `ものの` is a
**report**, and it belongs to writing.

</div>

<pre>
{f|三時間|さんじかん}も{f|並|なら}んだ<b>のに</b>、{f|売|う}り{f|切|き}れだった。
I queued for three hours and it was sold out! (and I'm annoyed)
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三時間|さんじかん}{f|並|なら}んだ<b>ものの</b>、{f|目当|めあ}ての{f|品|しな}は{f|買|か}えなかった。
Although I queued for three hours, I couldn't get what I wanted.
</pre>

There is also a shape difference that decides some sentences for you: `のに` can end a
sentence on its own - <code>{f|来|き}てくれると{f|言|い}ったのに。</code> trailing off is
a whole reproach. `ものの` cannot. It is a joint between two clauses and needs both of
them.

### ものの vs が・けれど

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `が` and `けれど` join **any** two clauses. `ものの` needs a real
expectation to defeat.

</div>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}と{f|申|もう}します<b>が</b>、{f|山田|やまだ}さんはいらっしゃいますか。
My name is Tanaka - is Yamada in?
</pre>

That `が` is pure punctuation; nothing is being conceded, so `ものの` is impossible
there. Where something *is* conceded, both work and `ものの` is the more formal choice:

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}は{f|受|う}けた<b>ものの</b>、{f|仕組|しく}みはよく{f|分|わ}からなかった。
I did get an explanation, but I never really understood how it works.
</pre>

### ものの vs ながらも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are bookish concessives, but `ながらも` grants a **quality or
state** and keeps one subject; `ものの` grants a **whole event** and may change subject.

</div>

<pre>
{f|小|ちい}さい<b>ながらも</b>、{f|手入|てい}れの{f|行|ゆ}き{f|届|とど}いた{f|庭|にわ}だった。
It was a small garden, but a beautifully kept one.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|始|はじ}まった<b>ものの</b>、{f|資金|しきん}が{f|足|た}りず{f|途中|とちゅう}で{f|止|と}まった。
The work did begin, but the money ran out and it stopped halfway.
</pre>

Swap them and both sentences go wrong: the garden sentence needs a state, and the
building-site sentence needs an event with a past tense in front of the joint.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>だものの</b> → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>であるものの</b>（or {f|学生|がくせい}ではあるものの）
  <code>もの</code> is a noun, so a noun in front of it links with `である`, never `だ`.

- ❌ {f|便利|べんり}<b>だものの</b>、{f|高|たか}い。 → ✅ {f|便利|べんり}<b>なものの</b>、{f|高|たか}い。
  な-adjectives take `な`, exactly as they do before any other noun.

- ❌ {f|遠|とお}いものの、ぜひ{f|来|き}てください。 → ✅ {f|遠|とお}いですが、ぜひ{f|来|き}てください。
  The second clause of a `ものの` sentence reports a fact. Invitations and requests
  need `が` or `けど`.

- ❌ {f|待|ま}っていたものの。 → ✅ {f|待|ま}っていたのに。
  `ものの` cannot end a sentence. `のに` can, and that dangling `のに` is the reproach
  you were reaching for.

## Practice

**1.** {f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|引|ひ}いた____、{f|意味|いみ}がやはり{f|分|わ}からなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|引|ひ}いた<b>ものの</b>、{f|意味|いみ}がやはり{f|分|わ}からなかった。

The first clause is granted - you really did look it up - and the second reports that
it did not deliver. A past-tense verb plus `ものの` is the pattern's commonest shape.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|約束|やくそく}の{f|時間|じかん}に{f|着|つ}いた（ものの・のに）、だれも{f|来|き}ていなかった。ひどいよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|約束|やくそく}の{f|時間|じかん}に{f|着|つ}いた<b>のに</b>、だれも{f|来|き}ていなかった。

The <code>ひどいよ</code> settles it: this is a spoken complaint, and complaints are
`のに` territory. `ものの` would report the same facts in a neutral written voice and
would clash with the grumble that follows.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The machine is old, but it still works without trouble."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|機械|きかい}は{f|古|ふる}い<b>ものの</b>、{f|今|いま}でも{f|問題|もんだい}なく{f|動|うご}いている。

An い-adjective attaches to `ものの` with no change at all. The sentence grants the age
of the machine and then denies the conclusion you would draw from it.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|経験|けいけん}{f|不足|ぶそく}だものの、{f|彼|かれ}にやらせてみたい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|経験|けいけん}{f|不足|ぶそく}<b>ではあるものの</b>、{f|彼|かれ}にやらせてみたい。

<code>{f|経験|けいけん}{f|不足|ぶそく}</code> is a noun, so it needs `である` - and
`ではある` is the natural choice here because the clause is a concession. `だものの`
does not exist.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|返信|へんしん}はもらった<b>ものの</b>、{f|答|こた}えになっていなかった。
(b) {f|返信|へんしん}はもらった<b>けど</b>、{f|答|こた}えになってなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both say the same thing. (a) is written Japanese - a line from a report, a complaint
letter or a summary of what happened, cool in tone and structured as a concession
followed by its shortfall.

(b) is what you would actually say out loud to a colleague. `けど` is register-neutral
and carries no bookish weight, and the contracted <code>なってなかった</code> pins it
firmly to speech. Writing (a) in a chat message would read as oddly stiff.

</details>
