# しかしながら

**Meaning** · however; nevertheless - the formal written form of しかし

`しかしながら` reverses the direction of an argument. It means exactly what
`しかし` means; what it adds is **weight**. This is the "however" of reports,
papers, official notices and prepared speeches.

## Usage

`しかしながら` is a sentence-opening conjunction. It stands at the **head of a new
sentence**, after a full stop, and is normally followed by a comma.

- {f|結果|けっか}は{f|良好|りょうこう}だった。しかしながら、{f|問題|もんだい}も{f|残|のこ}っている。
- ご{f|要望|ようぼう}は{f|承|うけたまわ}りました。しかしながら、{f|対応|たいおう}は{f|困難|こんなん}です。

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `しかしながら` joins **two sentences**, never two clauses
inside one sentence. To turn a corner mid-sentence, use `が` or `けれども`.

</div>

The `ながら` in it is the old concessive `ながら`, "even so" - the same element as
in <code>{f|残念|ざんねん}ながら</code> - and not the `ながら` meaning "while doing".
Nothing about the word implies simultaneity, and it never attaches to a verb stem.

Two places it reliably appears:

- **Written argument.** A paper states a finding, then しかしながら introduces the
  limitation.
- **Business correspondence.** A refusal is softened by first accepting the
  request, then しかしながら. It is a standard hinge in a polite decline.

## Examples

However, this method too has its problems.
</pre>

The construction went smoothly. Costs, however, greatly exceeded projections.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

Technology is advancing rapidly. Nevertheless, that does not automatically translate into human happiness.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a **written** word. In an essay, a white paper, a set of minutes or a
company announcement it is completely at home. Spoken, it appears only where the
speech is itself written first - a ceremony address, a press conference, a formal
presentation.

Say it to a friend and it lands like reading aloud from a memo. The everyday
spoken chain runs roughly:

- でも - casual, the default in conversation
- けど・けれども - conversational, joins clauses
- しかし - neutral; fine in writing, and in speech when the point is serious
- しかしながら - formal written, heavier than all of the above

That ordering is a tendency rather than a rule, and the boundary between
`しかし` and `しかしながら` is mostly one of **length and gravity**: writers reach for
the longer form when the reversal is important and they want the reader to slow
down. Used on every third sentence it becomes ponderous, so even formal prose
tends to space it out and fall back on `しかし`.

In business email it is often stretched further into
<code>しかしながら、{f|誠|まこと}に{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございませんが…</code>, which is
the polite refusal in its full costume.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|安|やす}いしかしながら{f|品質|ひんしつ}が{f|悪|わる}い。 → ✅ {f|安|やす}い。<b>しかしながら</b>、{f|品質|ひんしつ}が{f|悪|わる}い。
  It opens a sentence; it cannot sit between two clauses. Inside one sentence you
  need <code>{f|安|やす}いが</code> or <code>{f|安|やす}いけれども</code>.

- ❌ しかしながらも、{f|時間|じかん}がない。 → ✅ <b>しかしながら</b>、{f|時間|じかん}がない。
  There is no `しかしながらも`. The word is fixed and takes no further particles.

- ❌ (to a friend, about the weather) しかしながら、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|暑|あつ}いね。 → ✅ <b>でも</b>、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|暑|あつ}いね。
  Not wrong grammatically, but the register is three steps too high for chat. It
  will read as a joke.

- ❌ {f|音楽|おんがく}を{f|聞|き}きしかしながら{f|勉強|べんきょう}する。 → ✅ {f|音楽|おんがく}を{f|聞|き}きながら{f|勉強|べんきょう}する。
  The `ながら` inside `しかしながら` is not the "while doing" `ながら`. The two cannot
  be substituted for each other.

## Practice

**1.** {f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}み{f|書類|しょるい}は{f|受|う}け{f|取|と}りました。____、{f|記入|きにゅう}{f|漏|も}れがございました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>しかしながら</b>、{f|記入|きにゅう}{f|漏|も}れがございました。

The surrounding sentences are in formal business register - {f|受|う}け{f|取|と}りました,
ございました - and the second sentence reverses the first. That is precisely the
slot `しかしながら` fills.

</details>

**2.** Rewrite in casual speech: {f|参加|さんか}したい{f|気持|きも}ちはある。しかしながら、{f|今週|こんしゅう}は{f|時間|じかん}が{f|取|と}れない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|行|い}きたいんだけど、{f|今週|こんしゅう}はちょっと{f|時間|じかん}ないんだよね。

Casual Japanese does the same reversal with `けど` inside a single sentence, or
with `でも` at the head of the next one. Keeping `しかしながら` here would sound like
a formal announcement to one friend.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|売上|うりあげ}は{f|増|ふ}えた。でも、{f|利益|りえき}は{f|減|へ}った。
(b) {f|売上|うりあげ}は{f|増|ふ}えた。しかしながら、{f|利益|りえき}は{f|減|へ}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

The facts are identical; the setting is not. (a) is someone talking - a colleague
summarising over coffee. (b) is someone writing - a report, a board paper, a press
release - and the heavier connective signals that the reversal is a considered
point rather than a passing remark.

</details>
