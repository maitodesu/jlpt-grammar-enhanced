# ～に応えて

**Meaning** · in response to; living up to - answers a request, a hope or a demand that other people directed at you, and satisfies it

<code>{f|応|こた}える</code> is "to answer, to live up to". The noun in front of
<code>～に{f|応|こた}えて</code> is something aimed at you from outside:
{f|期待|きたい}, {f|要望|ようぼう}, {f|声|こえ}, {f|要求|ようきゅう},
{f|声援|せいえん}, ニーズ. What follows is your answer to it - and, importantly,
an answer that **delivers**. The pattern carries success in it, which is why the
negative has to be spelled out separately.

## Formation

Take a **noun only**, and one that names a call of some kind. `に` marks it.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + に{f|応|こた}えて | {f|期待|きたい} → {f|期待|きたい}に{f|応|こた}えて |
| Noun (stiffer, written) | Noun + に{f|応|こた}え | {f|要請|ようせい} → {f|要請|ようせい}に{f|応|こた}え |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + に{f|応|こた}える + Noun | {f|期待|きたい} → {f|期待|きたい}に{f|応|こた}える{f|活躍|かつやく} |
| Negative | Noun + に{f|応|こた}えられず | {f|信頼|しんらい} → {f|信頼|しんらい}に{f|応|こた}えられず |
| As the plain predicate | Noun + に{f|応|こた}える | {f|要望|ようぼう} → {f|要望|ようぼう}に{f|応|こた}える |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the same verb has an unrelated second life meaning "to take a toll":
{f|寒|さむ}さが{f|身|み}に{f|応|こた}える, "the cold really gets to you". Nothing about
requests there; if the subject is cold, tiredness or age, it is this other sense.

</div>

<pre>
みんなの{f|期待|きたい}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|一位|いちい}になった。
Living up to everyone's hopes, he came first.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|客|きゃく}さんの{f|声|こえ}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>、{f|営業|えいぎょう}{f|時間|じかん}を{f|延|の}ばしました。
In response to what customers told us, we extended our opening hours.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|市民|しみん}の{f|要望|ようぼう}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>、{f|駅前|えきまえ}に{f|新|あたら}しい{f|図書館|としょかん}が{f|建|た}てられた。
In response to residents' requests, a new library was built by the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|応援|おうえん}してくれた{f|人|ひと}たちの{f|気持|きも}ち<b>に{f|応|こた}えたい</b>。
I want to live up to the feelings of the people who cheered me on.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|農家|のうか}の{f|要請|ようせい}<b>に{f|応|こた}え</b>、{f|県|けん}は{f|補助|ほじょ}の{f|拡大|かくだい}を{f|決|き}めた。
Answering appeals from farmers, the prefecture decided to widen the subsidy.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}からの{f|拍手|はくしゅ}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>、{f|歌手|かしゅ}はもう{f|一曲|いっきょく}{f|歌|うた}ってくれた。
Answering the applause from the hall, the singer gave us one more song.
</pre>

<pre>

Precisely in order to live up to the trust of the regulars who have come for years, we intend to keep the price rise to a minimum.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Formal, written and public-facing. It belongs to announcements, press releases,
speeches, sports reporting and interviews, and it presupposes that the call being
answered is **weighty** - a public demand, a body of expectation, a crowd's support.
Using it for a small favour is comic: nobody answers
<code>お{f|茶|ちゃ}を{f|入|い}れてという{f|要望|ようぼう}</code>. Say
<code>{f|言|い}われたとおりにした</code> for that.

The negative is fixed and worth memorising:
<code>ご{f|期待|きたい}に{f|応|こた}えられず{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございません</code> -
"I'm sorry to have let you down." It is the standard apology of a losing team, a
failed candidate and a company withdrawing a product.

Two spelling traps sit right next to each other, and both are read こたえる.
<code>{f|答|こた}える</code> answers a **question**:
<code>{f|質問|しつもん}に{f|答|こた}える</code>. <code>{f|応|こた}える</code> lives up to a
**hope or demand**: <code>{f|期待|きたい}に{f|応|こた}える</code>. And then the same
kanji read おうじる gives you <code>{f|応|おう}じる</code>, which matches a variable.
One kanji, two readings, three jobs; keep them apart by asking what the noun in front
is - a question, a hope, or a scale.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|応|こた}えて vs ～に{f|応|おう}じて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|応|こた}えて</code> **answers people**.
<code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code> **matches a variable**, or accedes to a demand with no
warmth attached.

</div>

<pre>
{f|読者|どくしゃ}の{f|要望|ようぼう}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>、{f|続編|ぞくへん}が{f|出|で}ることになった。
In response to readers' requests, a sequel is now to be published.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|注文|ちゅうもん}の{f|内容|ないよう}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|包装|ほうそう}を{f|変|か}えます。
We vary the wrapping to suit what has been ordered.
</pre>

Two tests. If "living up to" fits the English, the reading is こたえて. If "for each,
according to each" fits, it is おうじて. Only
<code>{f|応|こた}える</code> takes <code>{f|期待|きたい}</code> in the sense of
fulfilling it - <code>{f|期待|きたい}に{f|応|おう}じて</code> would mean adjusting
something to the size of the hope, which is not a thing people say.

### ～に{f|応|こた}えて vs ～を{f|受|う}けて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|受|う}けて</code> reports a **trigger**: X happened, and
this followed. <code>に{f|応|こた}えて</code> says X was a **call aimed at you** and
you satisfied it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|事故|じこ}<b>を{f|受|う}けて</b>、{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|安全|あんぜん}{f|対策|たいさく}を{f|見直|みなお}した。
Following the accident, the company reviewed its safety measures.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|住民|じゅうみん}の{f|訴|うった}え<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>、{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|点検|てんけん}の{f|回数|かいすう}を{f|増|ふ}やした。
Answering complaints from residents, the company increased how often it inspected.
</pre>

An accident asks for nothing, so it can never take
<code>に{f|応|こた}えて</code>. Where the trigger genuinely is a request, both are
available and journalists choose between them on purpose:
<code>を{f|受|う}けて</code> is cool and merely sequential, while
<code>に{f|応|こた}えて</code> credits the company with wanting to satisfy people.

### ～に{f|応|こた}えて vs ～に{f|対|たい}して

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> aims an action **at** something and
says nothing about the outcome. <code>に{f|応|こた}えて</code> says the demand **got
what it wanted**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|批判|ひはん}<b>に{f|対|たい}して</b>、{f|市長|しちょう}は{f|何|なに}も{f|答|こた}えなかった。
In the face of the criticism, the mayor said nothing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|批判|ひはん}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>、{f|市長|しちょう}は{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|見直|みなお}した。
Responding to the criticism, the mayor reviewed the plan.
</pre>

The first sentence is perfectly compatible with refusal; the second is not.
Success is built into <code>に{f|応|こた}えて</code>, which is why
<code>{f|期待|きたい}に{f|応|こた}えて{f|負|ま}けた</code> is a contradiction and the
negative form exists to carry the other half of the meaning.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ メールの{f|質問|しつもん}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>おきました。 → ✅ メールの{f|質問|しつもん}<b>に{f|答|こた}えて</b>おきました。
  A question is answered with <code>{f|答|こた}える</code>. Same reading, different
  kanji, and the wrong one turns a reply into a heroic act of living up to something.

- ❌ {f|台風|たいふう}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>{f|営業|えいぎょう}を{f|中止|ちゅうし}した。 → ✅ {f|台風|たいふう}<b>を{f|受|う}けて</b>{f|営業|えいぎょう}を{f|中止|ちゅうし}した。
  A typhoon is a trigger, not a request. Only things that ask for something can be
  answered.

- ❌ {f|期待|きたい}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>{f|失敗|しっぱい}してしまった。 → ✅ {f|期待|きたい}<b>に{f|応|こた}えられず</b>、{f|失敗|しっぱい}してしまった。
  The pattern entails that the hope was met, so it cannot head a sentence about
  falling short. Use the potential negative instead.

## Practice

**1.** {f|社員|しゃいん}の{f|声|こえ}____、{f|在宅|ざいたく}{f|勤務|きんむ}が{f|認|みと}められることになった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|社員|しゃいん}の{f|声|こえ}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>、{f|在宅|ざいたく}{f|勤務|きんむ}が{f|認|みと}められることになった。

Staff asked for something and management delivered it, which is exactly what this
pattern reports. Note the reading - こたえて, not おうじて, because people are being
answered rather than a scale being matched.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|使|つか}う{f|人|ひと}の{f|体格|たいかく}（に{f|応|こた}えて・に{f|応|おう}じて）{f|高|たか}さを{f|調節|ちょうせつ}できます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|体格|たいかく}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|高|たか}さを{f|調節|ちょうせつ}できます。

Build is a scale, and the height is set to match it - pure covariation. A body is
not making a request, so nothing here can be answered.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I want to live up to the trust my teacher has placed in me."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|先生|せんせい}の{f|信頼|しんらい}<b>に{f|応|こた}えたい</b>と{f|思|おも}っています。

Trust is something placed in you from outside, so it belongs to the same family as
{f|期待|きたい} and {f|要望|ようぼう}. The verb keeps its own volitional endings -
the て-form is only one of its shapes.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|要望|ようぼう}が{f|多|おお}いに{f|応|こた}えて、{f|再|さい}{f|放送|ほうそう}が{f|決|き}まった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|多|おお}くの{f|要望|ようぼう}<b>に{f|応|こた}えて</b>、{f|再|さい}{f|放送|ほうそう}が{f|決|き}まった。

Only a noun can attach, so the い-adjective has to be moved inside the noun phrase.
<code>{f|多|おお}くの{f|要望|ようぼう}</code> is the ordinary way to say it, and
<code>{f|視聴者|しちょうしゃ}の{f|要望|ようぼう}</code> would work equally well.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|住民|じゅうみん}の{f|反対|はんたい}に{f|応|こた}えて、{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}された。
(b) {f|住民|じゅうみん}の{f|反対|はんたい}を{f|受|う}けて、{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}された。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) frames the cancellation as the developer honouring what residents asked for. It
is sympathetic to both sides, and a company would use it about itself.

(b) is neutral reporting: the opposition happened, and the cancellation followed. It
leaves open that the developer gave in reluctantly, and it is what a newspaper would
write. Journalists pick between these two deliberately.

</details>
