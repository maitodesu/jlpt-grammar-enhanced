# ～かいがある

**Meaning** · the effort was worth it - something came of it that makes the trouble look well spent

{f|甲斐|かい}, almost always written in kana, is the *return* on an effort. A
sentence with `かいがある` in it is doing two things at once: reporting a result,
and saying that the work behind it was justified. The negative
<code>かいもなく</code> is the same sentence with the payoff taken away, and it is
just as common.

## Formation

Two shapes matter, and they behave differently. The **clause** pattern
<code>～かいがある</code> reports a payoff; the **suffix** `がい`, voiced, builds
a noun out of a ます-stem.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (た-form) - the usual shape | ～た + かいがある | {f|練習|れんしゅう}した → {f|練習|れんしゅう}したかいがある |
| Leading into the result | ～た + かいがあって、… | {f|待|ま}ったかいがあって、{f|席|せき}が{f|取|と}れた |
| Verb (dictionary) - "worth doing" | ～る + かいがある | {f|読|よ}む → {f|読|よ}むかいがある |
| Noun | Noun + の + かいがある | {f|努力|どりょく}のかいがある |
| Negative | ～かいもなく ／ ～かいがない | {f|努力|どりょく}のかいもなく |
| Suffix on a ます-stem | ます-stem + がい | やる → やりがい |
| The suffix before a noun | ～がいのある + Noun | やりがいのある{f|仕事|しごと} |
| Common suffix members | {f|生|い}きがい・{f|働|はたら}きがい・{f|教|おし}えがい・{f|作|つく}りがい | - |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - when you are reporting a **result**, the verb is in the
**た-form**: <code>{f|練習|れんしゅう}したかいがあった</code>. The dictionary form
means "worth doing" and looks forward instead - a different sentence, and one for
which <code>{f|価値|かち}がある</code> is often the more usual phrasing.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the suffix voices: かい → **がい**. It attaches to the ます-stem, so
やる → やり**がい**, {f|生|い}きる → {f|生|い}き**がい**, {f|働|はたら}く →
{f|働|はたら}き**がい**. These are ordinary nouns and take が, は and の like any
other.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}した<b>かいがあった</b>。
All that daily practice paid off.
</pre>

<pre>
やり<b>がいのある</b>{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|探|さが}しています。
I'm looking for work that feels worth doing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|早起|はやお}きした<b>かいがあって</b>、きれいな{f|朝日|あさひ}が{f|見|み}られた。
Getting up early paid off - I got to watch a beautiful sunrise.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|祖父|そふ}にとって、{f|畑|はたけ}の{f|世話|せわ}が{f|生|い}き<b>がい</b>になっている。
Looking after the vegetable patch is what makes life worth living for my grandfather.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|半年|はんとし}{f|教室|きょうしつ}に{f|通|かよ}った<b>かいがあって</b>、{f|会話|かいわ}が{f|楽|たの}しくなってきた。
Six months of classes have paid off, and conversation is starting to be fun.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|苦労|くろう}して{f|予約|よやく}した<b>かいがある</b>{f|店|みせ}だった。
It turned out to be a restaurant worth all the trouble of booking.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|皆|みな}で{f|夜|よる}{f|遅|おそ}くまで{f|準備|じゅんび}した<b>かいがあって</b>、{f|発表|はっぴょう}は{f|大成功|だいせいこう}だった。
All those late nights of preparation paid off - the presentation was a great success.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三年間|さんねんかん}{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|重|かさ}ねた<b>かいもなく</b>、チームは{f|一回戦|いっかいせん}で{f|敗|やぶ}れた。
For all three years of accumulated training, the team went out in the first round.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`かいがある` is **register-neutral and warm**. It fits ordinary conversation, a
thank-you message, a speech at a party, a magazine profile. Nothing about it is
formal, and nothing about it is slangy. What it always carries is a small emotional
charge: satisfaction that effort was not wasted.

Because of that charge, it needs **effort to have gone in**. A piece of good luck
does not take it - ❌<code>{f|宝|たから}くじが{f|当|あ}たったかいがあった</code>
makes no sense, because nobody worked for a lottery win. The effort can belong to
the speaker, to somebody else, or to a group, but it has to be in the picture.

<code>かいもなく</code> deserves its own line. It is the standard way to say "for
all their effort, …", and Japanese uses it constantly in sports reporting, in
memoirs and in commiseration. The phrase carries genuine sympathy; it is not a
sneer at the effort, but an acknowledgement of it.

The `がい` suffix has grown into a set of high-frequency nouns.
<code>{f|生|い}きがい</code> - what makes life worth living - is culturally weighty
and appears in everything from newspaper columns to retirement planning.
<code>やりがい</code> and <code>{f|働|はたら}きがい</code> are the standard
vocabulary of talking about work. A subtler member is
<code>{f|教|おし}えがいのある{f|学生|がくせい}</code>: a student who *repays* the
teaching, which is a compliment to the student rather than to the teacher.

## Contrast

### ～かいがある vs {f|価値|かち}がある

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `かいがある` measures the return **on somebody's effort**.
<code>{f|価値|かち}がある</code> measures **the thing's own worth**, effort or no
effort.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|絵|え}には{f|見|み}る<b>{f|価値|かち}がある</b>。
This painting is worth seeing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|二時間|にじかん}{f|並|なら}んだ<b>かいがあった</b>。
Queuing for two hours turned out to be worth it.
</pre>

The painting has value whether or not anybody troubled to look at it; the queue has
none until somebody has stood in it. That is why
<code>{f|価値|かち}がある</code> attaches happily to things -
<code>{f|価値|かち}のある{f|情報|じょうほう}</code> - while `かいがある` needs a
verb describing what somebody did.

### ～かいがあって vs ～おかげで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>かいがあって</code> credits **the effort itself**.
`おかげで` credits **somebody or something else** for the good outcome.

</div>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|教|おし}えていただいた<b>おかげで</b>、{f|試験|しけん}に{f|合格|ごうかく}できました。
Thanks to your teaching, I passed the exam.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎晩|まいばん}{f|復習|ふくしゅう}した<b>かいがあって</b>、{f|試験|しけん}に{f|合格|ごうかく}できました。
Reviewing every evening paid off, and I passed the exam.
</pre>

Said to a teacher, the two are very different social moves: the first gives them
the credit, the second keeps it. Both are perfectly polite, but a thank-you note
wants `おかげで`. Note also that `おかげで` takes any cause at all, including luck
and weather, where `かいがある` cannot.

### ～かいがある vs ～だけのことはある

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `かいがある` says **the effort was repaid**.
<code>だけのことはある</code> says **the reputation was deserved** - it lives up to
what you would expect of it.

</div>

<pre>
さすが{f|一流|いちりゅう}の{f|職人|しょくにん}<b>だけのことはある</b>。
It's exactly what you'd expect from a first-rate craftsman.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三日|みっか}かけて{f|磨|みが}いた<b>かいがある</b>{f|仕上|しあ}がりだ。
It's a finish that justifies the three days spent polishing it.
</pre>

<code>だけのことはある</code> looks back at a *reason to expect quality* - training,
price, a reputation - and confirms it. `かいがある` looks back at *work* and
confirms that it bought something. The two often fit the same scene, and they
compliment different things.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|練習|れんしゅう}する<b>かいがあって</b>、{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}できた。 → ✅ {f|練習|れんしゅう}した<b>かいがあって</b>、{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}できた。
  When you are reporting a payoff that has already arrived, the verb goes into the
  た-form.

- ❌ やる<b>かい</b>のある{f|仕事|しごと} → ✅ やり<b>がい</b>のある{f|仕事|しごと}
  The suffix attaches to the ます-stem and voices to `がい`. It is a noun in its own
  right, not the clause pattern.

- ❌ {f|努力|どりょく}<b>かいがあって</b> → ✅ {f|努力|どりょく}<b>の</b>かいがあって
  A noun links to <code>かい</code> with `の`, since <code>かい</code> is itself a
  noun.

- ❌ {f|宝|たから}くじが{f|当|あ}たった<b>かいがあった</b>。 → ✅ {f|宝|たから}くじが{f|当|あ}たって{f|嬉|うれ}しかった。
  There has to be effort to be repaid. Pure luck leaves the pattern with nothing
  to weigh.

## Practice

**1.** {f|三時間|さんじかん}{f|並|なら}んだ____、{f|限定|げんてい}のケーキが{f|買|か}えた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|三時間|さんじかん}{f|並|なら}んだ<b>かいがあって</b>

The queue is the effort and the cake is the return, which is the core shape of this
pattern. <code>かいがあって</code> is the connective form that leads straight into
the result.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|高|たか}いが、{f|一生|いっしょう}{f|使|つか}えるので{f|買|か}う（かいがある・{f|価値|かち}がある）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|買|か}う<b>{f|価値|かち}がある</b>。

The sentence is weighing the dictionary itself, before anybody has bought it. That
is <code>{f|価値|かち}がある</code>. `かいがある` would need an effort already made
- <code>{f|無理|むり}して{f|買|か}ったかいがあった</code> would work after the
purchase.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "For all the months we spent preparing, the event was cancelled because of the typhoon."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|何|なん}か{f|月|げつ}も{f|準備|じゅんび}した<b>かいもなく</b>、イベントは{f|台風|たいふう}で{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。

"For all the …" with a disappointing outcome is exactly
<code>かいもなく</code>. Watch the reading of {f|何|なん}か{f|月|げつ}: it is
なんかげつ, because the か is the counter that is also written ヶ. That is a
different か from the なにか of "something".

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|走|はし}った{f|甲斐|かい}のあって、5キロ{f|痩|や}せた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|走|はし}った<b>かいがあって</b>、5キロ{f|痩|や}せた。
"Running every day paid off - I lost five kilos."

The particle is `が`, not `の`: <code>かいがある</code> is a noun plus
<code>ある</code>. The `の` shape belongs to the suffix construction, as in
<code>やりがいのある{f|仕事|しごと}</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|先輩|せんぱい}が{f|手伝|てつだ}ってくれた<b>おかげで</b>、{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}った。
(b) {f|徹夜|てつや}した<b>かいがあって</b>、{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) hands the credit to somebody else. It is the polite, socially warm choice, and
it is what you would say in front of the person who helped.

(b) credits the speaker's own all-nighter. It is not boastful in itself - the tone
is relief more than pride - but it keeps the achievement inside the speaker's own
effort, which makes it the wrong sentence to say to a senior colleague who spent
the night helping you.

</details>
