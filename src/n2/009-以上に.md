# ～以上に

**Meaning** · even more than; more than just - the thing named was already at a high
level, and this goes past it

<code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> is the noun "that amount and above". Used as
<code>{f|以上|いじょう}に</code> it sets a benchmark and says the sentence clears it.
That benchmark is the whole point: <code>{f|去年|きょねん}{f|以上|いじょう}に{f|暑|あつ}い</code>
does not just mean "hotter than last year" - it takes for granted that last year was
hot, and says this year beats it.

## Formation

<code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> is a noun, so it hangs off a noun or a noun-modifying
clause with nothing in between.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + {f|以上|いじょう}に | {f|去年|きょねん} → {f|去年|きょねん}{f|以上|いじょう}に |
| Verb (past) | た-form + {f|以上|いじょう}に | {f|思|おも}った → {f|思|おも}った{f|以上|いじょう}に |
| Verb (progressive) | ～ている + {f|以上|いじょう}に | {f|考|かんが}えている{f|以上|いじょう}に |
| Demonstrative | これ／それ + {f|以上|いじょう}に | これ{f|以上|いじょう}に{f|安|やす}い{f|店|みせ} |
| Modifying a noun | ～{f|以上|いじょう}の + Noun | {f|予想|よそう}{f|以上|いじょう}の{f|反響|はんきょう} |
| Set phrases | {f|必要|ひつよう}／{f|予想|よそう}／{f|想像|そうぞう} + {f|以上|いじょう}に | {f|想像|そうぞう}{f|以上|いじょう}に{f|大|おお}きい |

<div class="note">

**Note** - に or の depends on what comes next. Before a predicate the form is
<code>{f|以上|いじょう}に</code>; before a noun it is <code>{f|以上|いじょう}の</code>.
{f|予想|よそう}{f|以上|いじょう}<b>に</b>{f|売|う}れた／{f|予想|よそう}{f|以上|いじょう}<b>の</b>{f|売|う}れ{f|行|ゆ}き。

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|今年|ことし}の{f|夏|なつ}は{f|去年|きょねん}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>{f|暑|あつ}い。
This summer is even hotter than last year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}は{f|思|おも}った<b>{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>{f|難|むずか}しかった。
The exam was harder than I'd expected.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|必要|ひつよう}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>{f|心配|しんぱい}している。
He's worrying more than the situation warrants.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|映画|えいが}は{f|評判|ひょうばん}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>おもしろかった。
The film was even better than its reputation.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|路線|ろせん}は{f|予想|よそう}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>{f|利用者|りようしゃ}が{f|多|おお}く、{f|朝|あさ}の{f|混雑|こんざつ}が{f|問題|もんだい}になっている。
The new line has far more passengers than anticipated, and morning crowding has become a problem.
</pre>

<pre>

I explained it badly and made him angrier than I ever imagined I would.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|留学|りゅうがく}して{f|分|わ}かったのは、{f|語学力|ごがくりょく}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>{f|人|ひと}に{f|頼|たよ}る{f|勇気|ゆうき}が{f|要|い}るということだった。
What studying abroad taught me is that you need the nerve to lean on people even more than you need the language.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}の{f|接客|せっきゃく}は、{f|料理|りょうり}の{f|味|あじ}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>{f|客|きゃく}を{f|引|ひ}き{f|止|と}めているのだと{f|思|おも}う。
I suspect the service at this place keeps customers coming back even more than the food does.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral. It is at home in conversation, in a review, in a report and in an
academic paper, with no adjustment. A handful of collocations do most of the work in
practice - <code>{f|思|おも}った{f|以上|いじょう}に</code>,
<code>{f|予想|よそう}{f|以上|いじょう}に</code>,
<code>{f|想像|そうぞう}{f|以上|いじょう}に</code>,
<code>{f|期待|きたい}{f|以上|いじょう}に</code> - and learning those four as units gets
you most of the way.

Two of the fixed expressions carry their own colour.
<code>{f|必要|ひつよう}{f|以上|いじょう}に</code> means "more than is called for" and is
almost always a criticism: worrying too much, apologising too much, charging too
much. And <code>これ{f|以上|いじょう}</code>, "beyond this point", is usually followed by
a negative - <code>これ{f|以上|いじょう}{f|待|ま}てない</code>, "I can't wait any longer" -
and in that use it drops the に.

The comparison is not always about degree. In the last two examples above the
sentence is ranking two *factors* rather than measuring one thing twice: the nerve
matters more than the language, the service more than the food. This is a very common
rhetorical move in Japanese essays and interviews, and it is where the "no less than"
side of the gloss comes from.

One thing the pattern quietly asserts: the benchmark was already substantial. Said of
a mild summer, <code>{f|去年|きょねん}{f|以上|いじょう}に{f|暑|あつ}い</code> would strike
a listener as odd, because there was nothing much to exceed. Plain より carries no such
implication and is the neutral choice for ordinary comparison.

## Contrast

### {f|以上|いじょう}に vs より

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - より simply **compares**. <code>{f|以上|いじょう}に</code> compares
**and treats the benchmark as already high**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|私|わたし}<b>より</b>{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}い。
My younger brother is taller than me.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|私|わたし}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>{f|背|せ}が{f|高|たか}い。
My brother is even taller than I am.
</pre>

The first is a measurement and says nothing about whether either of us is tall. The
second implies that I am tall, and that he clears even that. Note also that the two
are not interchangeable in shape: より sits between the two things compared, while
<code>{f|以上|いじょう}に</code> attaches to the benchmark and then runs straight into
the predicate. Stacking them - ❌<code>{f|私|わたし}より{f|以上|いじょう}に</code> - is a
common slip and says the same thing twice.

### {f|以上|いじょう}に vs {f|以上|いじょう}は

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|以上|いじょう}に</code> is a **comparison**;
<code>{f|以上|いじょう}は</code> is a **premise** - "now that", "since".

</div>

<pre>
{f|予想|よそう}していた<b>{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>{f|時間|じかん}がかかった。
It took longer than I had anticipated.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}けた<b>{f|以上|いじょう}は</b>、{f|最後|さいご}までやります。
Now that I've taken it on, I'll see it through.
</pre>

Same three characters, unrelated jobs. The に one measures; the は one reasons, and it
is usually followed by a resolution, an obligation or a should. If the second half of
your sentence contains <code>べきだ</code>, つもり or a determined ます-form, you almost
certainly wanted は.

### {f|以上|いじょう}に vs さらに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|以上|いじょう}に</code> **names** the benchmark it is
exceeding; `さらに` leaves it implicit.

</div>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}は{f|去年|きょねん}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>{f|高|たか}くなっている。
Prices are higher still than they were last year.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}は<b>さらに</b>{f|高|たか}くなっている。
Prices have gone up further.
</pre>

Both say the level has risen past something. `さらに` relies on the listener already
knowing what that something was, which is why it belongs in the second or third
sentence of a paragraph. <code>{f|以上|いじょう}に</code> puts the benchmark on the page,
so it can open a paragraph cold.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|去年|きょねん}<b>より{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|多|おお}い。 → ✅ {f|去年|きょねん}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|多|おお}い。
  <code>{f|以上|いじょう}に</code> already contains the comparison. Adding より stacks
  two comparatives on one benchmark.

- ❌ {f|予想|よそう}{f|以上|いじょう}<b>に</b>{f|結果|けっか}が{f|出|で}た。（meaning "a better-than-expected result"） → ✅ {f|予想|よそう}{f|以上|いじょう}<b>の</b>{f|結果|けっか}が{f|出|で}た。
  Before a noun the form is の. Keep に for when a predicate follows.

- ❌ {f|約束|やくそく}した{f|以上|いじょう}<b>に</b>{f|守|まも}るべきだ。 → ✅ {f|約束|やくそく}した{f|以上|いじょう}<b>は</b>{f|守|まも}るべきだ。
  This is the "since you've promised" pattern, not a comparison. べきだ in the second
  half is the clue.

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}はこれ{f|以上|いじょう}に{f|考|かんが}えても{f|仕方|しかた}がない。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は<b>これ{f|以上|いじょう}</b>{f|考|かんが}えても{f|仕方|しかた}がない。
  <code>これ{f|以上|いじょう}</code> meaning "any further" works adverbially on its own.
  The に appears only when you are genuinely comparing against "this":
  <code>これ{f|以上|いじょう}に{f|安|やす}い{f|物|もの}はない</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|今回|こんかい}のイベントには、{f|昨年|さくねん}____{f|多|おお}くの{f|人|ひと}が{f|集|あつ}まった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昨年|さくねん}<b>{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>{f|多|おお}くの{f|人|ひと}が{f|集|あつ}まった。

The sentence takes for granted that last year's turnout was already good, and says
this year beat it. <code>{f|昨年|さくねん}より</code> would also be grammatical but
would drop that implication.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}けた（{f|以上|いじょう}に・{f|以上|いじょう}は）、{f|途中|とちゅう}でやめるわけにはいかない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}けた<b>{f|以上|いじょう}は</b>、{f|途中|とちゅう}でやめるわけにはいかない。

Nothing is being compared. The first clause is the premise that generates the
obligation in the second, and <code>わけにはいかない</code> is the sort of ending that
premise takes.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The repairs cost more than I had expected."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|修理|しゅうり}{f|代|だい}は{f|思|おも}っていた<b>{f|以上|いじょう}に</b>{f|高|たか}かった。

<code>{f|思|おも}っていた{f|以上|いじょう}に</code> is one of the four collocations worth
memorising whole. The past progressive is what fits here: the expectation was in
place before the bill arrived.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}は{f|必要|ひつよう}{f|以上|いじょう}の{f|謝|あやま}っている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|必要|ひつよう}{f|以上|いじょう}<b>に</b>{f|謝|あやま}っている。

<code>{f|謝|あやま}っている</code> is a predicate, not a noun, so the adverbial に is
required. The の form would need a noun after it, as in
<code>{f|必要|ひつよう}{f|以上|いじょう}の{f|説明|せつめい}</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|経験|けいけん}より{f|体力|たいりょく}が{f|必要|ひつよう}だ。
(b) この{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|経験|けいけん}{f|以上|いじょう}に{f|体力|たいりょく}が{f|必要|ひつよう}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) ranks the two flatly: of the two, stamina matters more. It leaves open the
possibility that experience hardly matters at all.

(b) grants that experience matters - a lot - and then says stamina matters even more.
It is the more generous and the more persuasive sentence, which is why it is what you
would say to someone who has just told you they have no experience.

</details>
