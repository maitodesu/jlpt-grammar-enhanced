# ただし

**Meaning** · provided that; note however that - attaches a condition or an exception to a statement that has already been made

`ただし` does not take back what was said before it. The main statement stands;
what follows fences it. Admission is free - provided you book. Returns are
accepted - within a week. This is the language of notices, rules, timetables and
contracts, and it is written in a voice that has authority over the reader.

## Formation

`ただし` attaches to nothing: it opens the sentence that carries the proviso.
What is worth tabulating is the shape of what follows it, because the endings are
formulaic and recognising them is most of the work.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing; it opens the proviso sentence | ～。ただし、～ | {f|入場|にゅうじょう}{f|無料|むりょう}。ただし、{f|予約|よやく}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。 |
| Written rules | ただし、～こと | ただし、{f|中学生|ちゅうがくせい}{f|以下|いか}は{f|保護者|ほごしゃ}{f|同伴|どうはん}のこと |
| Exceptions to a stated range | ただし、～を{f|除|のぞ}く | {f|年中|ねんじゅう}{f|無休|むきゅう}。ただし、{f|年末年始|ねんまつねんし}を{f|除|のぞ}く |
| Limits | ただし、～に{f|限|かぎ}る | ただし、お{f|一人|ひとり}{f|様|さま}{f|一点|いってん}に{f|限|かぎ}ります |
| Polite announcements | ただし、～ます・です | ただし、{f|当日|とうじつ}の{f|変更|へんこう}はできません |

<div class="note">

**Note** - a proviso clause in a contract or a regulation is itself called a
ただし{f|書|が}き. If a Japanese colleague says
「ただし{f|書|が}きを{f|読|よ}んでください」, they mean the small print that limits
what the main clause promises.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Formal and written-leaning. `ただし` belongs to anything that sets terms: signs,
application forms, staff handbooks, product pages, examination instructions. In
speech it appears where somebody is explaining terms out loud -
「ただし、こちらは{f|予約|よやく}が{f|必要|ひつよう}になります」 - and there it sounds
businesslike rather than stiff.

Ordinary conversation uses ただ, the same word minus the し:
「{f|行|い}ってもいいよ。ただ、{f|遅|おそ}くならないでね。」 That pair is worth
memorising as a register ladder - ただし for the notice on the wall, ただ for the
person telling you about it.

The word carries no feeling. It does not object, apologise or regret; it states
the limits of what was just granted. That neutrality is exactly why officials
like it, and why a `ただし` sentence in a personal message can read as chilly.

## Contrast

### ただし vs しかし

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `しかし` **contradicts** the previous sentence. `ただし`
leaves it standing and **limits its scope**.

</div>

<pre>
このプランは{f|安|やす}い。<b>しかし</b>、{f|人気|にんき}がない。
This plan is cheap. It isn't popular, though.
</pre>

<pre>
このプランは{f|安|やす}い。<b>ただし</b>、{f|三年|さんねん}{f|契約|けいやく}が{f|条件|じょうけん}だ。
This plan is cheap - on a three-year contract.
</pre>

After しかし the reader revises their impression; after ただし the plan is still
cheap, and they now know on what terms. A quick test: if the second sentence
could be printed under an asterisk on an advertisement, it is `ただし`.

### ただし vs なお

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ただし` **restricts** what came before. `なお` **adds**
something further that restricts nothing.

</div>

<pre>
{f|申込|もうしこみ}は{f|三月|さんがつ}{f|末|まつ}までです。<b>ただし</b>、{f|定員|ていいん}になり{f|次第|しだい}{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}ります。
Applications close at the end of March - or earlier if we reach capacity.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|申込|もうしこみ}は{f|三月|さんがつ}{f|末|まつ}までです。<b>なお</b>、{f|詳|くわ}しくはホームページをご{f|覧|らん}ください。
Applications close at the end of March. Full details are on our website.
</pre>

Both open the last paragraph of an announcement, which is why they get mixed up.
Ask whether the new sentence changes what the reader may do. If it does, it is
`ただし`; if it merely tells them something extra, it is `なお`.

### ただし vs もっとも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ただし` states a condition that **binds the reader**.
`もっとも` adds the **speaker's own afterthought**, and binds nobody.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|貸|か}し{f|出|だ}しできます。<b>ただし</b>、{f|館内|かんない}のみです。
You may borrow this dictionary, but only within the library.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|辞書|じしょ}はよく{f|使|つか}っています。<b>もっとも</b>、{f|最近|さいきん}はアプリのほうが{f|多|おお}いですが。
I use this dictionary a lot - though these days it's mostly the app, admittedly.
</pre>

`もっとも` softens the speaker's own claim and typically trails off in が or
けれど. `ただし` never trails off: it delivers a rule and stops. Putting もっとも
on a public notice would make the sign sound as though it were musing to itself.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|安|やす}いただし、{f|味|あじ}はよくない。 → ✅ {f|安|やす}い。<b>ただし</b>、{f|味|あじ}はよくない。
  It opens a new sentence. It cannot be pinned onto the end of a clause the way
  が or けど can.

- ❌ この{f|案|あん}はよくできている。ただし、{f|私|わたし}は{f|反対|はんたい}だ。 → ✅ この{f|案|あん}はよくできている。<b>しかし</b>、{f|私|わたし}は{f|反対|はんたい}だ。
  A personal objection contradicts the first sentence rather than limiting it.
  `ただし` cannot carry disagreement.

- ❌ {f|今度|こんど}{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}こう。ただし、{f|金曜日|きんようび}は{f|無理|むり}だけど。 → ✅ {f|今度|こんど}{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}こう。<b>ただ</b>、{f|金曜日|きんようび}は{f|無理|むり}だけど。
  Between friends, ただ is the natural form. `ただし` turns a drink with a mate
  into a clause in a contract.

- ❌ {f|入場|にゅうじょう}{f|無料|むりょう}。ただしでも、{f|予約|よやく}が{f|必要|ひつよう}。 → ✅ {f|入場|にゅうじょう}{f|無料|むりょう}。<b>ただし</b>、{f|予約|よやく}が{f|必要|ひつよう}。
  It takes no particle after it - no でも, no が, no けど. Just a comma.

## Practice

**1.** {f|当店|とうてん}は{f|年中|ねんじゅう}{f|無休|むきゅう}です。____、{f|一月|いちがつ}{f|一日|ついたち}のみお{f|休|やす}みをいただきます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>ただし</b>、{f|一月|いちがつ}{f|一日|ついたち}のみお{f|休|やす}みをいただきます。

"Open every day" is granted and then narrowed by a single exception, which is
precisely the job of this word. Note that the shop is still open all year; only
the scope of the claim has been trimmed.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural connector:
{f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|五時|ごじ}までです。（ただし・なお）、{f|駐車場|ちゅうしゃじょう}は{f|建物|たてもの}の{f|裏|うら}にございます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>なお</b>、{f|駐車場|ちゅうしゃじょう}は{f|建物|たてもの}の{f|裏|うら}にございます。

Where the car park is has no bearing on the five o'clock deadline, so nothing is
being restricted. This is supplementary information, and supplementary
information takes なお.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Photography is permitted, provided you do not use a flash."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|写真|しゃしん}{f|撮影|さつえい}は{f|可能|かのう}です。<b>ただし</b>、フラッシュのご{f|使用|しよう}はご{f|遠慮|えんりょ}ください。

"Provided that" attaching a condition to a permission already granted is
textbook `ただし`, and a museum sign would word it much like this.
ご{f|遠慮|えんりょ}ください is the standard polite way notices forbid something.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|参加|さんか}は{f|自由|じゆう}です。{f|参加|さんか}ただし、{f|事前|じぜん}の{f|登録|とうろく}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|参加|さんか}は{f|自由|じゆう}です。<b>ただし</b>、{f|事前|じぜん}の{f|登録|とうろく}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。

Delete the stray {f|参加|さんか}. The word takes nothing in front of it inside
its own sentence - it comes first, then a comma, then the condition.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会員|かいいん}なら{f|無料|むりょう}です。<b>ただし</b>、{f|身分証|みぶんしょう}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。
(b) {f|会員|かいいん}なら{f|無料|むりょう}です。<b>でも</b>、{f|身分証|みぶんしょう}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both sentences tell you the same two facts. In (a) the identification is a
condition of the offer, stated in the register of a notice: this is what free
admission requires. In (b) the speaker sounds as though they are warning a
friend about an inconvenience, and でも hints that the requirement is a nuisance.
`ただし` sets terms; でも grumbles about them.

</details>
