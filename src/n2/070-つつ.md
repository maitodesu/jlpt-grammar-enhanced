# ～つつ

**Meaning** · while doing; and yet, knowing full well - two things held together in one clause, either at the same time or in open contradiction

`つつ` is the written cousin of `ながら`, and it has the same two jobs. Attached to a
verb of action it means *while*: two things going on at once. Attached to a verb of
knowing, thinking or feeling it means *although* - and then it says something sharper
than plain "although", because the knowledge is built into the sentence. The speaker
knew, and did it anyway.

On this batch's two axes - how much the speaker minds, and how formal the frame is - 
concessive `つつ` is *quietly self-reproachful* and *firmly literary*. It is the
concession you write about your own weakness, not one you throw at somebody else.

## Formation

`つつ` attaches to the ます-stem of a verb, and to nothing else. It has no adjective
form and no noun form.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (simultaneous) | ます-stem + つつ | {f|聞|き}く → {f|聞|き}きつつ |
| Verb (concessive) | ます-stem + つつ | {f|知|し}る → {f|知|し}りつつ |
| Verb (concessive, emphatic) | ます-stem + つつも | {f|思|おも}う → {f|思|おも}いつつも |
| する-verb | stem + しつつ | {f|考慮|こうりょ}する → {f|考慮|こうりょ}しつつ |
| Verb (negative) | - | not available; use ないながらも or のに |
| い-adjective, な-adjective, noun | - | not available; use ながらも or ものの |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - **one subject, two clauses.** The person doing the `つつ` verb and
the person doing the main verb must be the same. ❌
<code>{f|弟|おとうと}が{f|泣|な}きつつ、{f|母|はは}がなだめた</code> - two people, so this
needs `のに` or a `て`-clause instead.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - `つつ` is not `つつある`. Same three syllables, unrelated job:
<code>{f|回復|かいふく}しつつある</code> means "is in the process of recovering" and is a
predicate that ends the sentence. `つつ` on its own is a joint in the middle of one.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`つつ` is written Japanese. It lives in essays, novels, reports, minutes and formal
addresses, and in speech it appears only where the speaker is being deliberately
formal - a company announcement, a wedding speech, a lecture. Saying
<code>ラーメンを{f|食|た}べつつ</code> to a friend is not wrong so much as comic; the
spoken word is `ながら`.

The concessive use is narrower than it looks. It nearly always attaches to a verb of
awareness - <code>{f|知|し}る</code>, <code>{f|思|おも}う</code>,
<code>{f|分|わ}かる</code>, <code>{f|感|かん}じる</code> - and nearly always describes
the speaker acting against their own better judgement. That is why so many `つつ`
sentences end in <code>～てしまう</code>. The feeling is rueful rather than angry:
you are not blaming anyone, you are confessing.

`つつも` is the same thing with the contradiction underlined. Where bare `つつ` before a
verb of thinking could in principle still be read as "while", `つつも` can only be
concessive, so writers reach for it whenever the sentence might otherwise be
ambiguous.

One stylistic habit worth copying: because `つつ` is a written form, it pairs
comfortably with other written forms - <code>である</code>, `ぬ`, `～まい`,
<code>まいります</code>. Mixing it into a sentence that is otherwise casual produces a
jarring seam.

## Contrast

### つつ vs ながら (the "while" sense)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical grammar, different register. `ながら` is what you say;
`つつ` is what you write.

</div>

<pre>
テレビを{f|見|み}<b>ながら</b>{f|晩|ばん}ごはんを{f|食|た}べた。
I ate dinner watching television.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|各国|かっこく}の{f|動向|どうこう}を{f|見|み}<b>つつ</b>、{f|対応|たいおう}を{f|決|き}めていく。
We will decide how to respond while watching what other countries do.
</pre>

The subject matter follows the register. Concrete, everyday, physical pairings go to
`ながら`; abstract, deliberative ones go to `つつ`. Swapping them in either direction
sounds like the wrong suit for the occasion.

### つつも vs のに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `つつも` needs **one subject** and a **verb of awareness**. `のに`
takes anything, and points the finger outward.

</div>

<pre>
{f|危|あぶ}ないと{f|分|わ}かり<b>つつも</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|川|かわ}に{f|飛|と}び{f|込|こ}んだ。
He knew it was dangerous, and jumped into the river anyway.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|危|あぶ}ないと{f|言|い}った<b>のに</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|川|かわ}に{f|飛|と}び{f|込|こ}んだ。
I told him it was dangerous, and he jumped in anyway.
</pre>

In the first sentence the knowledge is *his*: it is a portrait of a man overriding
himself. In the second the warning is *mine* and the annoyance is mine too. That
one-subject rule is what makes `つつも` unsuitable for complaining about other people.

### つつ vs つつある

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `つつ` joins two clauses. `つつある` **ends** a clause and means
"is in the middle of becoming".

</div>

<pre>
{f|景気|けいき}は{f|回復|かいふく}し<b>つつある</b>。
The economy is in the process of recovering.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|景気|けいき}の{f|回復|かいふく}を{f|見守|みまも}り<b>つつ</b>、{f|投資|とうし}の{f|時期|じき}を{f|考|かんが}える。
I'll think about when to invest while keeping an eye on the recovery.
</pre>

The test is simple: if `ある` follows, it is the progressive; if a comma and a second
clause follow, it is the joint. Note also that `つつある` describes gradual change and
so refuses instantaneous verbs - ❌<code>{f|死|し}につつある</code> is not how you say
someone is dying; that is <code>{f|死|し}にかけている</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|食|た}べる<b>つつ</b> → ✅ {f|食|た}べ<b>つつ</b>
  `つつ` attaches to the ます-stem, never to the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|給料|きゅうりょう}は{f|安|やす}い<b>つつ</b>、{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|楽|たの}しい。 → ✅ {f|給料|きゅうりょう}は{f|安|やす}い<b>ながらも</b>、{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|楽|たの}しい。
  There is no adjective form of `つつ`. `ながらも`, `ものの` and `が` all take
  adjectives; `つつ` does not.

- ❌ {f|妹|いもうと}が{f|泣|な}き<b>つつ</b>、{f|父|ちち}が{f|慰|なぐさ}めた。 → ✅ {f|妹|いもうと}が{f|泣|な}いているので、{f|父|ちち}が{f|慰|なぐさ}めた。
  Two different subjects. `つつ` binds both clauses to one person.

- ❌ {f|人口|じんこう}は{f|減|へ}り<b>つつ</b>。 → ✅ {f|人口|じんこう}は{f|減|へ}り<b>つつある</b>。
  `つつ` cannot close a sentence. If you mean "is decreasing", you need the `ある`.

## Practice

**1.** {f|遅|おく}れると{f|知|し}り____、{f|連絡|れんらく}を{f|忘|わす}れてしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|遅|おく}れると{f|知|し}り<b>つつ</b>（or {f|知|し}り<b>つつも</b>）、{f|連絡|れんらく}を{f|忘|わす}れてしまった。

A verb of awareness plus `つつ` is the concessive shape: the speaker knew and failed
to act on the knowledge. The <code>～てしまった</code> ending is the give-away that this
is a confession, not a "while".

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|技術|ぎじゅつ}は{f|年々|ねんねん}{f|進歩|しんぽ}し（つつ・つつある）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}は{f|年々|ねんねん}{f|進歩|しんぽ}し<b>つつある</b>。

The sentence ends here, and only `つつある` can end a sentence. Bare `つつ` would leave
the reader waiting for the second clause that never arrives.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, in written style: "While respecting the opinions of local residents, the city will continue the survey."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|市|し}は{f|住民|じゅうみん}の{f|意見|いけん}を{f|尊重|そんちょう}し<b>つつ</b>、{f|調査|ちょうさ}を{f|続|つづ}けていく。

An abstract, institutional "while" is exactly what `つつ` is for. `ながら` here would
not be wrong, but it would read as a press release written by someone in a hurry.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|忙|いそが}しいつつも、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}は{f|走|はし}っている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|忙|いそが}し<b>いながらも</b>、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}は{f|走|はし}っている。

`つつ` has no adjective form. The concessive that does take い-adjectives is
`ながらも`; <code>{f|忙|いそが}しいものの</code> would also work in writing.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|反省|はんせい}し<b>つつ</b>、{f|同|おな}じ{f|失敗|しっぱい}をくり{f|返|かえ}している。
(b) {f|反省|はんせい}している<b>のに</b>、{f|同|おな}じ{f|失敗|しっぱい}をくり{f|返|かえ}している。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a written, self-directed admission. The regret and the repeated failure belong
to one person, and `つつ` presents them as a single contradictory act - I am sorry
about it and I keep doing it. It reads like a diary entry.

(b) is looser and more likely to be about somebody else. `のに` sets the two facts side
by side and expresses surprise or irritation that they coexist, and it does not
require the same subject at all - said of a colleague, (b) is a criticism, whereas (a)
could only be a confession.

</details>
