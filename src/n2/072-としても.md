# ～としても

**Meaning** · even if; even supposing - grant the proposition for the sake of argument, and show that it changes nothing

`としても` is the concession of debate. The `と` is the quoting `と`: you take a whole
proposition, hold it up as a hypothesis - *suppose that is so* - and then knock down
the conclusion somebody wanted to draw from it. Nothing is claimed to be true. The
clause in front is a supposition, and often one the speaker does not believe.

On this batch's two axes - how much the speaker minds, and how formal the frame is - 
`としても` is *unemotional* and *neutral to formal*. It is the pattern that argues
rather than complains, which is why it fills discussion, analysis and negotiation.

## Formation

`としても` follows a plain form. Nouns and な-adjectives need `だ`, because `と` here is
quoting a complete sentence.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + としても | {f|断|ことわ}る → {f|断|ことわ}るとしても |
| Verb (past) | plain past + としても | {f|失敗|しっぱい}した → {f|失敗|しっぱい}したとしても |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + としても | {f|届|とど}かない → {f|届|とど}かないとしても |
| い-adjective | plain form + としても | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}いとしても |
| な-adjective | stem + だ + としても | {f|不便|ふべん} → {f|不便|ふべん}だとしても |
| Noun | Noun + だ + としても | うそ → うそだとしても |
| Formal written | Noun + である + としても | {f|事実|じじつ} → {f|事実|じじつ}であるとしても |
| Marked as hypothetical | {f|仮|かり}に／たとえ + … + としても | - → {f|仮|かり}に{f|勝|か}ったとしても |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だ` is **required** after a noun. ✅<code>うそだとしても</code>,
❌<code>うそとしても</code>. Dropping it produces the unrelated `として` "in the capacity
of", so <code>{f|学生|がくせい}としても</code> means "as a student, too" - a different
sentence entirely.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the past tense inside `としても` does not push the whole sentence into the
past. <code>{f|失敗|しっぱい}したとしても</code> means "even if it should fail", looking
forward: the `た` marks the hypothetical event as completed *relative to* the main
clause, not relative to now.

</div>

<pre>
{f|安|やす}い<b>としても</b>、{f|今月|こんげつ}は{f|買|か}えない。
Even if it's cheap, I can't buy it this month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}った<b>としても</b>、{f|大会|たいかい}は{f|行|おこな}われます。
The tournament will go ahead even if it snows tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
うそだ<b>としても</b>、なかなかよくできた{f|話|はなし}だ。
Even supposing it's a lie, it's a well-made story.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|仮|かり}に{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|賛成|さんせい}した<b>としても</b>、{f|予算|よさん}が{f|足|た}りない。
Even if everyone were in favour, there isn't the budget.
</pre>

<pre>

Even if it fails, I think it will have been worth attempting.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}が{f|犯人|はんにん}だ<b>としても</b>、{f|証拠|しょうこ}がなければ{f|逮捕|たいほ}はできない。
Even if he is the culprit, there can be no arrest without evidence.
</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

`としても` is register-neutral, and it survives in polite and plain style alike. What
marks it is not formality but *stance*: it is analytical. The speaker is reasoning
about a possibility, not reacting to a fact, so the pattern has almost no emotional
colour of its own. That makes it safe in situations where `のに` or `くせに` would be
rude, and it is the reason `としても` is so common in meetings, legal writing, academic
prose and any argument that has to stay civil.

It pairs naturally with the hypothetical adverbs. <code>{f|仮|かり}に</code> is the
formal one, `たとえ` the neutral one, `もし` the loosest; all three simply announce in
advance that a supposition is coming, and none of them changes the meaning.

There is a diplomatic use worth knowing. Prefacing a refusal with `としても` lets you
decline without ever disputing the other side's premise:
<code>ご{f|指摘|してき}のとおりだとしても、{f|今回|こんかい}は{f|見送|みおく}らせていただきます</code>.
You have not called anyone wrong; you have simply said it does not settle the matter.

In casual speech the shorter `～たって` usually does this job, and `としても` sounds a
touch bookish among friends. The one place it stays fully colloquial is with a
question word: <code>{f|何|なに}があったとしても{f|味方|みかた}するよ</code> is ordinary
warm speech.

## Contrast

### としても vs ても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ても` covers both the real and the hypothetical. `としても` is
**always** a supposition, and says so out loud.

</div>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っ<b>ても</b>、{f|毎朝|まいあさ}{f|走|はし}っている。
I run every morning even when it rains.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った<b>としても</b>、{f|予定|よてい}は{f|変|か}えない。
Even if it rains tomorrow, I'm not changing the plan.
</pre>

The first sentence reports a habit and a real recurring fact - `としても` cannot go
there, because there is nothing to suppose. The second is a hypothesis about tomorrow,
and both forms would work; `としても` makes the "let us suppose" explicit.

### としても vs にしても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `にしても` grants something **already on the table**; `としても`
supposes something **not yet established**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|忙|いそが}しかった<b>にしても</b>、{f|一言|ひとこと}{f|連絡|れんらく}するべきだった。
Busy or not - and I accept you were - you should have sent a word.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|忙|いそが}しかった<b>としても</b>、{f|連絡|れんらく}できない{f|理由|りゆう}にはならない。
Even supposing you were busy, that is no reason not to get in touch.
</pre>

Both are objections, but the first accepts the excuse as a fact and objects anyway,
while the second treats it as a claim that has not been proved. The second is
therefore the colder of the two, and in an argument it is heard as scepticism.

### としても vs としたら・とすれば

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same supposition, opposite second half. `としたら` asks **what
follows**; `としても` says **nothing follows**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}を{f|変|か}える<b>としたら</b>、いつまでに{f|決|き}めればいいですか。
If we're going to change the venue, by when do we need to decide?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}を{f|変|か}える<b>としても</b>、{f|日程|にってい}はこのままでいきます。
Even if we change the venue, the dates stay as they are.
</pre>

The `も` is doing all the work. `としたら` opens the door to the consequence; the extra
`も` slams it. If you can insert "then what?" after the clause, you want `としたら`; if
you can insert "so what?", you want `としても`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>としても</b>{f|許|ゆる}されない。（intending "even if he is a student"） → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>だとしても</b>{f|許|ゆる}されない。
  Without `だ` the phrase is `として` "in the role of", plus `も` - "as a student too".
  The `だ` is what makes it a supposition.

- ❌ {f|不便|ふべん}<b>としても</b> → ✅ {f|不便|ふべん}<b>だとしても</b>
  な-adjectives need the same `だ` as nouns, because `と` is quoting a whole sentence.

- ❌ {f|今|いま}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っている<b>としても</b>、{f|出|で}かけた。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っている<b>のに</b>、{f|出|で}かけた。
  You cannot suppose what you can see. For a fact already in front of you, use `のに`
  or `ても`.

- ❌ {f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|来|く}る<b>としても</b>、{f|何時|なんじ}に{f|着|つ}きますか。 → ✅ {f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|来|く}る<b>としたら</b>、{f|何時|なんじ}に{f|着|つ}きますか。
  A question about the consequence needs `としたら`. `としても` announces that the
  consequence does not arise, so it cannot be followed by a question about it.

## Practice

**1.** {f|仮|かり}にその{f|話|はなし}が{f|本当|ほんとう}だ____、{f|今|いま}さらどうにもならない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|仮|かり}にその{f|話|はなし}が{f|本当|ほんとう}<b>だとしても</b>、{f|今|いま}さらどうにもならない。

<code>{f|仮|かり}に</code> flags a supposition, and the noun-plus-`だ` before `としても`
completes it. The main clause then refuses the conclusion: true or not, it is too
late.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|参加|さんか}する（としたら・としても）、{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みはどこですればいいですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|参加|さんか}する<b>としたら</b>、{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みはどこですればいいですか。

The second half asks what follows from the supposition, so the door has to stay open.
`としても` would mean "even if I take part", which cannot be followed by a question
about how to take part.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Even if the price came down, I still wouldn't buy a car that size."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|値段|ねだん}が{f|下|さ}がった<b>としても</b>、あの{f|大|おお}きさの{f|車|くるま}は{f|買|か}わないと{f|思|おも}う。

The price coming down is a hypothesis, so the past tense inside `としても` looks
forward rather than back. The main clause denies the expected consequence of a
cheaper price.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|事実|じじつ}としても、こんな{f|書|か}き{f|方|かた}はよくない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|事実|じじつ}<b>だとしても</b>、こんな{f|書|か}き{f|方|かた}はよくない。
（Formally: {f|事実|じじつ}<b>であるとしても</b>）

<code>{f|事実|じじつ}としても</code> reads as "as a fact, too", which is not the intended
meaning. A noun needs `だ` - or `である` in written style - to become a supposition.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|知|し}らなかった<b>にしても</b>、{f|責任|せきにん}は{f|残|のこ}る。
(b) {f|知|し}らなかった<b>としても</b>、{f|責任|せきにん}は{f|残|のこ}る。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) accepts that you genuinely did not know. The speaker grants the excuse as true and
argues that it does not remove the responsibility - firm, but not doubting you.

(b) leaves the not-knowing as an untested hypothesis: *even supposing you didn't
know*. Because it declines to concede the fact, it can sound sceptical, and in a
dispute it is the more adversarial of the two. Where you want to sound fair-minded,
`にしても` is the safer pick.

</details>
