# そこで

**Meaning** · and so; that is why - the situation was such-and-such, and this is the step that was taken about it

`そこで` looks back at a problem and reports the move made in response to it. The
weight falls on the **action**: something needed doing, and this is what was done
about it. That is why it belongs to reports, explanations and stories, and why it
sits so awkwardly in front of anything that merely happened by itself.

## Formation

`そこで` attaches to nothing - it opens a sentence. What is worth tabulating is
what may stand on either side of it.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing; it opens a sentence | ～。そこで、～ | {f|席|せき}が{f|足|た}りなかった。そこで、{f|椅子|いす}を{f|運|はこ}んだ。 |
| Sentence before | a situation, problem or discovery | {f|辞書|じしょ}が{f|見|み}つからなかった。 |
| Sentence after | an action deliberately taken, usually past | そこで、{f|新|あたら}しいものを{f|買|か}った。 |
| Sentence after | ❌ an order, request or invitation | ❌そこで、{f|手伝|てつだ}ってください |
| Sentence after | ❌ something that simply happened | ❌そこで、{f|風邪|かぜ}をひいた |
| Spoken lead-in to a favour | そこで、～なんですが | そこで、ご{f|相談|そうだん}があるんですが |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the same three syllables are also plain そこ + で, "there, in that
place": {f|駅|えき}で{f|会|あ}って、そこで{f|一時間|いちじかん}{f|話|はな}した。
Position tells you which is which. The conjunction stands at the head of a
sentence and is followed by a comma; the locative sits inside the clause and can
be swapped for あそこで.

</div>

The computer stopped working, so I called someone from support.
</pre>

<pre>

The roads were jammed, so we switched to the train.
</pre>

<pre>

We were asked for the same explanation over and over, so we put together a list of common questions and handed it round.
</pre>

<pre>

## Nuance & register

`そこで` leans written and deliberate. It is at home in reports, minutes,
newspaper features and any narrative that explains how a decision came about. In
ordinary conversation people reach for それで far more often, and a spoken
`そこで` carries a faint storytelling formality: the speaker is presenting the
sequence, not just chatting through it.

The one conversational use that is completely ordinary is the pivot to a request.
You lay out your circumstances, then say そこで、ご{f|相談|そうだん}なんですが or
そこで、お{f|願|ねが}いがあるんですが, and the listener knows the real point of the
conversation has arrived. It is a standard business move and worth learning as a
set phrase.

`そこで` has no emotional colour of its own. It does not complain, insist or
apologise; it reports the step. If you want the sentence to push the listener,
you need だから or ですから.

## Contrast

### そこで vs それで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `それで` covers **any** result, including things that merely
happened to you. `そこで` needs the second half to be a deliberate action.

</div>

<pre>
{f|寝坊|ねぼう}した。<b>それで</b>、{f|遅刻|ちこく}した。
I overslept, and so I was late.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|資料|しりょう}が{f|足|た}りなかった。<b>そこで</b>、{f|急|きゅう}きょ{f|印刷|いんさつ}し{f|直|なお}した。
We were short of handouts, so we hurriedly reprinted them.
</pre>

Being late is not something you chose to do about oversleeping, so `そこで` is
impossible in the first sentence. The reverse swap is usually fine - それで works
in the second one too - which makes `それで` the safer word and `そこで` the more
precise one. `それで` is also the everyday spoken form and doubles as a prompt in
conversation, 「それで?」 meaning "and then?". `そこで` never does that.

### そこで vs だから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だから` gives the **reason** for whatever follows, and what
follows may be a judgement, an order or a plan. `そこで` reports **what was done**,
and nothing else.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|早|はや}い。<b>だから</b>、もう{f|寝|ね}なさい。
We have an early start tomorrow, so go to bed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|早|はや}い。<b>そこで</b>、{f|今夜|こんや}は{f|早|はや}めに{f|休|やす}むことにした。
We have an early start tomorrow, so I decided to turn in early tonight.
</pre>

The imperative test settles it: anything you can follow with ～なさい、～てください
or ～ましょう belongs to だから. `そこで` only ever introduces a completed or
settled action, and it makes no demands on the listener.

### そこで vs すると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `そこで` introduces **what the person did**. `すると`
introduces **what then happened**, outside anyone's control.

</div>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|来|こ}なかった。<b>そこで</b>、もう{f|一度|いちど}メールを{f|送|おく}った。
There was no reply, so I sent the email again.
</pre>

<pre>
もう{f|一度|いちど}メールを{f|送|おく}った。<b>すると</b>、すぐに{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|来|き}た。
I sent the email again, and a reply came straight back.
</pre>

The two run in sequence, and a piece of narration often uses both in turn: the
problem, the step taken, the result observed. Notice that the second sentence
could not take `そこで` - a reply arriving is not a decision anyone made - and the
first could not take `すると`, because sending the email again is precisely a
decision.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|寒|さむ}かった。そこで、{f|風邪|かぜ}をひいた。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|寒|さむ}かった。<b>それで</b>、{f|風邪|かぜ}をひいた。
  Catching a cold is not a step you took. `そこで` needs a deliberate action in
  the second half.

- ❌ {f|時間|じかん}がありません。そこで、{f|静|しず}かにしてください。 → ✅ {f|時間|じかん}がありません。<b>ですから</b>、{f|静|しず}かにしてください。
  Requests and orders cannot follow `そこで`. It reports; it does not instruct.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ってきたそこで、{f|中|なか}に{f|入|はい}った。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ってきた。<b>そこで</b>、{f|中|なか}に{f|入|はい}った。（or {f|降|ふ}ってきた<b>ので</b>）
  It opens a new sentence and never joins two clauses. Inside one sentence, use
  ので or から.

- ❌ {f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}いた。そこで、{f|友|とも}だちを{f|待|ま}った。（meaning "I waited there"） → ✅ {f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}いて、そこで{f|友|とも}だちを{f|待|ま}った。
  Standing at the head of a sentence with a comma after it, そこで reads as the
  conjunction, and the reader waits for a decision. If you mean "in that place",
  keep it inside the clause.

## Practice

**1.** {f|会場|かいじょう}の{f|予約|よやく}が{f|取|と}れなかった。____、{f|日程|にってい}を{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}{f|遅|おく}らせた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>そこで</b>、{f|日程|にってい}を{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}{f|遅|おく}らせた。

A problem in the first sentence, a deliberate step in the second: that is the
shape this word exists for. Note the past tense - the action has already been
taken.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural connector:
{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まってしまった。（そこで・それで）、{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|遅|おく}れてしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>それで</b>、{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|遅|おく}れてしまった。

Being late is a consequence that befell the speaker, not a course of action they
chose. `そこで` would only work with something like
「タクシーに{f|乗|の}り{f|換|か}えた」 in the second half.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The old system kept crashing. So we decided to replace it."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|古|ふる}いシステムが{f|止|と}まってばかりいた。<b>そこで</b>、{f|入|い}れ{f|替|か}えることにした。

The second sentence is a decision taken in response to the trouble described in
the first, which is `そこで` exactly. <code>～ことにした</code> spells the
deliberateness out and is a very common partner for this connector.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|説明書|せつめいしょ}がわかりにくかった。そこで、{f|作|つく}り{f|直|なお}してください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|説明書|せつめいしょ}がわかりにくかった。<b>ですから</b>、{f|作|つく}り{f|直|なお}してください。

The second half is a request, and `そこで` cannot introduce one. To keep そこで,
the sentence has to report what the speaker did instead:
そこで、{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|作|つく}り{f|直|なお}した。

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|問題|もんだい}が{f|見|み}つかった。<b>そこで</b>、{f|調査|ちょうさ}を{f|始|はじ}めた。
(b) {f|問題|もんだい}が{f|見|み}つかった。<b>すると</b>、{f|次々|つぎつぎ}に{f|同|おな}じ{f|例|れい}が{f|出|で}てきた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

In (a) the speaker is the agent: a problem appeared and they responded by
launching an investigation. The sentence is about a choice somebody made.

In (b) nobody chose anything. Once the problem came to light, more cases surfaced
by themselves, and the speaker is reporting what was observed. Putting `そこで`
into (b) would suggest the extra cases appeared because someone decided they
should.

</details>
