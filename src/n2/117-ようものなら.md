# ～（よ）うものなら

**Meaning** · if you so much as dared to do that, look what would happen

The volitional form plus `ものなら`. The condition is not an accident but a *choice* -
if someone were to go ahead and do this - and the consequence is a disaster
disproportionate to the act. The exaggeration is not a side effect; it is the whole
point of the pattern.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| う-verb | volitional ～おう + ものなら | {f|言|い}う → {f|言|い}おうものなら |
| る-verb | volitional ～よう + ものなら | {f|遅|おく}れる → {f|遅|おく}れようものなら |
| する-verb | しよう + ものなら | {f|失敗|しっぱい}する → {f|失敗|しっぱい}しようものなら |
| {f|来|く}る | {f|来|こ}よう + ものなら | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ようものなら |
| Casual contraction | ものなら → もんなら | {f|遅|おく}れようもんなら |
| Main clause | a dire outcome | {f|大変|たいへん}なことになる / ～てしまう / ～か{f|分|わ}からない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the pattern loves a minimiser in front of it:
<code>{f|少|すこ}しでも</code>, <code>{f|一言|ひとこと}でも</code>,
<code>{f|一度|いちど}でも</code>. Tiny cause, enormous effect - that contrast is what the
sentence is built to deliver.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`(よ)うものなら` is theatrical by design. The speaker is not calmly predicting a
consequence; they are telling you how frightening, how strict or how absurd a situation
is by showing what a trivial slip would cost. It therefore turns up in complaints, in
reminiscences about a severe teacher or parent, and in warnings to a newcomer about how
things work here.

Because it dramatises, it is common in the past tense as a description of how life used
to be - <code>{f|口答|くちごた}えしようものなら、{f|殴|なぐ}られたものだ</code> - and there it
carries a slightly nostalgic, slightly appalled tone at once. In the present tense it
is a warning, and it can be affectionate or genuinely alarming depending on delivery.

Register is mid-to-formal in writing and perfectly ordinary in speech, where it
contracts to `もんなら`. It is not stiff, but it is emphatic, so it sounds odd attached
to something the speaker does not actually care about. Two rules never bend: the
consequence must be bad, and the subject must be someone who could choose to act. You
cannot say <code>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ろうものなら</code> of the weather, because rain does
not decide anything.

## Contrast

### ～（よ）うものなら vs ～ものなら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - volitional + `ものなら` = "if you dared to". Potential + `ものなら`
= "if only it were possible".

</div>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}を{f|欠席|けっせき}し<b>ようものなら</b>、あとで{f|何|なに}を{f|言|い}われるか{f|分|わ}からない。
Skip the meeting and heaven knows what they'll say about you afterwards.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}を{f|欠席|けっせき}でき<b>るものなら</b>、{f|今日|きょう}こそ{f|休|やす}みたい。
If I could get out of the meeting, today of all days I'd like to.
</pre>

The first fears the act; the second wishes for the ability. Since both end in the same
five syllables, the form in front is the only clue, so it is worth training your eye on
it: <code>し<b>よう</b></code> against <code>でき<b>る</b></code>.

### ～（よ）うものなら vs ～たら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たら` states a consequence. `(よ)うものなら` states an
**overreaction**, and expects you to notice how out of proportion it is.

</div>

<pre>
{f|電車|でんしゃ}に{f|乗|の}り{f|遅|おく}れ<b>たら</b>、{f|次|つぎ}のに{f|乗|の}ればいいよ。
If you miss the train, just take the next one.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電車|でんしゃ}に{f|乗|の}り{f|遅|おく}れ<b>ようものなら</b>、{f|一時間|いちじかん}{f|待|ま}たされる{f|路線|ろせん}だ。
This is a line where missing your train means an hour's wait.
</pre>

`たら` is neutral and can end kindly, as the first sentence does. `(よ)うものなら` cannot
end kindly at all. If your consequence is mild or pleasant, the pattern is wrong.

### ～（よ）うものなら vs ～ては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ては` complains about something **repeated**. `(よ)うものなら`
inflates a **single, hypothetical** act into a catastrophe.

</div>

<pre>
{f|毎回|まいかい}{f|返事|へんじ}を{f|忘|わす}れ<b>ては</b>、{f|信用|しんよう}されなくなる。
Forgetting to reply every time, you'll stop being trusted.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一度|いちど}でも{f|返事|へんじ}を{f|忘|わす}れ<b>ようものなら</b>、{f|二度|にど}と{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|来|こ}ないだろう。
Forget to reply even once and you'll never get work from them again.
</pre>

`ては` needs the behaviour to be a habit; `(よ)うものなら` works precisely because one
occasion would be enough. That is why the two take opposite adverbs -
<code>{f|毎回|まいかい}</code> against <code>{f|一度|いちど}でも</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|遅|おく}れる<b>ものなら</b>、{f|大変|たいへん}なことになる。 → ✅ {f|遅|おく}れ<b>ようものなら</b>、{f|大変|たいへん}なことになる。
  The dictionary form does not work here. Without the volitional ending the sentence
  looks like the potential pattern with the potential missing.

- ❌ {f|来|き}よう<b>ものなら</b> → ✅ {f|来|こ}よう<b>ものなら</b>
  <code>{f|来|く}る</code> is irregular: the volitional is <code>{f|来|こ}よう</code>,
  built on the こ- stem, not on the き- of <code>{f|来|き}ます</code>.

- ❌ {f|早|はや}く{f|着|つ}こ<b>うものなら</b>、いい{f|席|せき}が{f|取|と}れる。 → ✅ {f|早|はや}く{f|着|つ}け<b>ば</b>、いい{f|席|せき}が{f|取|と}れる。
  The outcome has to be a disaster. A good result cancels the pattern entirely.

- ❌ {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|こ}よ<b>うものなら</b>、{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まる。 → ✅ {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|く}れ<b>ば</b>、{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まる。
  A typhoon does not choose to arrive. The volitional needs a subject capable of
  deciding, which is why this pattern is nearly always about people.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|会社|かいしゃ}では、{f|定時|ていじ}で{f|帰|かえ}ろう____、やる{f|気|き}がないと{f|思|おも}われる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|帰|かえ}ろ<b>うものなら</b>

Leaving on time is a small, ordinary act, and the penalty - being written off as
unmotivated - is wildly out of proportion. That gap is exactly what the pattern
dramatises.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|外|そと}へ（{f|出|で}られるものなら・{f|出|で}ようものなら）、{f|久|ひさ}しぶりに{f|散歩|さんぽ}がしたい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|外|そと}へ<b>{f|出|で}られるものなら</b>、{f|久|ひさ}しぶりに{f|散歩|さんぽ}がしたい。

The sentence ends in a wish, so it needs the potential form. The volitional version
would demand a calamity in the second half instead.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "In that house, if you left so much as one grain of rice, you got told off."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

あの{f|家|いえ}では、ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|一粒|ひとつぶ}でも{f|残|のこ}<b>そうものなら</b>{f|叱|しか}られた。

<code>{f|残|のこ}す</code> is an う-verb, so its volitional is <code>{f|残|のこ}そう</code>,
and <code>{f|一粒|ひとつぶ}でも</code> supplies the minimiser this pattern likes.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|嘘|うそ}をつくものなら、{f|二度|にど}と{f|信|しん}じてもらえない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|嘘|うそ}をつ<b>こうものなら</b>、{f|二度|にど}と{f|信|しん}じてもらえない。

<code>つく</code> has to become the volitional <code>つこう</code>. As written, the
sentence reaches for the potential pattern and finds no potential form there.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|休|やす}ん<b>だら</b>、{f|先輩|せんぱい}に{f|注意|ちゅうい}される。
(b) {f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|休|やす}<b>もうものなら</b>、{f|先輩|せんぱい}に{f|何|なに}を{f|言|い}われるか{f|分|わ}からない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain statement of what follows: skip practice, get a word from your senior.
It sounds like a fair and predictable rule.

(b) makes the senior sound formidable and the consequence unpredictable. The speaker is
not informing you of a rule so much as warning you about an atmosphere, and the
exaggeration is doing that work.

</details>
