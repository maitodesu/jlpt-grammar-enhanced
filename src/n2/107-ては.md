# ～ては

**Meaning** · if that happens - or if that keeps happening - the result is bad

`ては` is the て-form plus the particle `は`, and it is read *wa*. Making a condition
into a topic frames it as a standing state of affairs rather than a single event, so
the sentence almost always closes on a judgement: *if that is how things are, this
will not end well.*

## Formation

The condition goes in front of `ては`; the consequence follows, and it is negative.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + は | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}んでは |
| Verb (negative) | ない → なくては | {f|言|い}う → {f|言|い}わなくては |
| い-adjective | ～く + ては | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}くては |
| な-adjective | stem + では | {f|不便|ふべん} → {f|不便|ふべん}では |
| Noun | Noun + では | {f|雨|あめ} → {f|雨|あめ}では |
| Casual, after て | ては → ちゃ | {f|言|い}っては → {f|言|い}っちゃ |
| Casual, after で | では → じゃ | {f|休|やす}んでは → {f|休|やす}んじゃ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the consequence clause is a fixed shortlist: {f|困|こま}る, {f|大変|たいへん}だ,
だめだ, いけない, {f|無理|むり}だ, or any plain statement of something going wrong. If you
cannot put one of those at the end, `ては` is the wrong pattern.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
そんなに{f|飲|の}ん<b>では</b>、{f|体|からだ}に{f|悪|わる}いよ。
Drinking that much is bad for you.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}<b>では</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}だ。
In this rain the match is off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|約束|やくそく}を{f|忘|わす}れ<b>ては</b>{f|困|こま}ります。
It's a problem if you forget what you promised.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|家賃|やちん}がこんなに{f|高|たか}く<b>ては</b>、{f|一人暮|ひとりぐ}らしは{f|無理|むり}だ。
With rent this high, living alone is out of the question.
</pre>

<pre>
そんなこと{f|言|い}っ<b>ちゃ</b>、{f|彼女|かのじょ}が{f|怒|おこ}るよ。
Say something like that and she'll get angry.
</pre>

<pre>
ここまで{f|来|き}<b>ては</b>、もう{f|後|あと}には{f|引|ひ}けない。
Now that we've come this far, there's no backing out.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}を{f|読|よ}まずに{f|組|く}み{f|立|た}て<b>ては</b>、あとで{f|部品|ぶひん}が{f|余|あま}ることになりますよ。
Assemble it without reading the instructions and you'll end up with parts left over.
</pre>

<pre>

If they cut staff every time sales dip, this company has no future.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ては` itself is register-neutral. It appears in newspaper editorials and in
grandmothers' warnings alike, and it does not change shape for politeness - the
politeness lives in the consequence clause, so <code>{f|困|こま}ります</code> and
<code>{f|困|こま}る</code> are the only difference between the polite and plain versions.

What *is* strongly marked is the contraction. `ては` → `ちゃ` and `では` → `じゃ` are
how this pattern is normally pronounced in conversation, and a learner who has only
seen the written form will not recognise it: <code>{f|見|み}ちゃだめ</code>,
<code>{f|飲|の}んじゃだめ</code>, <code>そんなことしちゃ{f|困|こま}る</code>. The rule is
mechanical - whatever the て-form ends in, `て`+`は` fuses to `ちゃ` and `で`+`は` fuses
to `じゃ`. Contracted forms are casual only; do not write them in a report.

In speech `ては` is often left dangling, with the bad outcome unstated because it is
obvious: 「そんなに{f|急|いそ}いでは…」 - the listener supplies "you'll have an accident".
This trailing-off is polite, not lazy; spelling out the disaster would be blunter.

One hard restriction: the second half of a `ては` sentence cannot be a request, an
invitation, a command or a plan. `ては` delivers a verdict, and a verdict is not
something you can attach 「～てください」 to.

## Contrast

### ～ては vs ～たら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たら` is a neutral "if / when" and lets you say anything next.
`ては` builds the bad outcome into the grammar.

</div>

<pre>
{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}け<b>たら</b>、{f|涼|すず}しい{f|風|かぜ}が{f|入|はい}ってきた。
When I opened the window, a cool breeze came in.
</pre>

<pre>
こんな{f|寒|さむ}い{f|日|ひ}に{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}け<b>ては</b>、{f|風邪|かぜ}をひく。
Opening the window on a cold day like this, you'll catch a cold.
</pre>

`たら` can report a happy discovery, issue an instruction, or set up a plan. `ては`
can do none of those. That is also why 「{f|運動|うんどう}しては{f|健康|けんこう}になる」
is broken - the outcome is good, so the pattern has nothing to complain about.

### ～ては vs ～ても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ては` says the condition **causes** the trouble. `ても` says the
trouble holds **in spite of** the condition.

</div>

<pre>
{f|毎晩|まいばん}{f|夜更|よふ}かしをし<b>ては</b>、{f|体|からだ}が{f|持|も}たない。
Staying up late every night, your body won't hold out.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎晩|まいばん}{f|夜更|よふ}かしをし<b>ても</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|平気|へいき}な{f|顔|かお}をしている。
He stays up late every night and still looks perfectly fine.
</pre>

The single syllable flips the direction of the arrow. Learners who translate both as
"even if" will write the first when they mean the second, and end up blaming the
staying-up for a result the sentence was meant to shrug off.

### ～ては vs ～ていては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - bare `ては` treats one act as representative. `ていては` says the
behaviour is **already under way** and continuing it is what will sink you.

</div>

<pre>
{f|嘘|うそ}をつい<b>ては</b>、{f|信用|しんよう}をなくす。
Tell lies and you lose people's trust.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|嘘|うそ}ばかりつい<b>ていては</b>、だれも{f|信|しん}じてくれなくなるぞ。
If you carry on lying like this, nobody's going to believe you any more.
</pre>

The second is pointed at a person who is doing it right now. The first is closer to a
general maxim. When you want to criticise a habit in front of you, `ていては` is the
form that lands, and in speech it contracts to `てちゃ`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|疲|つか}れ<b>ては</b>、{f|休|やす}んでください。 → ✅ {f|疲|つか}れ<b>たら</b>、{f|休|やす}んでください。
  A `ては` clause cannot be followed by a request. Use `たら` when you want to tell
  someone what to do.

- ❌ {f|高|たか}い<b>では</b>{f|買|か}えない。 → ✅ {f|高|たか}く<b>ては</b>{f|買|か}えない。
  い-adjectives change to `～くて` first. Only nouns and な-adjectives take `では`.

- ❌ {f|不便|ふべん}な<b>では</b>{f|住|す}めない。 → ✅ {f|不便|ふべん}<b>では</b>{f|住|す}めない。
  The `な` of a な-adjective drops before `では`, exactly as it does before `だ`.

- ❌ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|運動|うんどう}し<b>ては</b>、{f|健康|けんこう}になる。 → ✅ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|運動|うんどう}す<b>れば</b>、{f|健康|けんこう}になる。
  A good outcome needs `ば` or `たら`. `ては` is reserved for what goes wrong.

- ❌ そんな{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}ん<b>でちゃ</b>だめ。 → ✅ そんな{f|本|ほん}を{f|読|よ}ん<b>じゃ</b>だめ。
  <code>{f|読|よ}んでちゃ</code> is the contraction of <code>{f|読|よ}んでいては</code>,
  "if you are in the middle of reading". For plain <code>{f|読|よ}んでは</code> the
  contraction is `じゃ`.

## Practice

**1.** {f|部屋|へや}がこんなに{f|散|ち}らかって____、{f|友|とも}だちを{f|呼|よ}べないよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|散|ち}らかって<b>は</b>（casual {f|散|ち}らかってちゃ）

The room being a mess is the standing condition, and the consequence - not being
able to invite anyone - is exactly the kind of trouble `ては` sets up.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|甘|あま}いものを{f|食|た}べすぎ（ては・ても）、{f|歯|は}が{f|悪|わる}くなるよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|食|た}べすぎ<b>ては</b>、{f|歯|は}が{f|悪|わる}くなるよ。

The sweets are the cause of the bad teeth, not something the bad teeth happen in
spite of. `ても` would mean "even if you eat too many sweets", which leaves the
sentence saying something strange.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "You can't get to work in an hour from a place this far out."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

こんなに{f|遠|とお}く<b>ては</b>、{f|一時間|いちじかん}で{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|着|つ}けない。

The い-adjective <code>{f|遠|とお}い</code> becomes <code>{f|遠|とお}くては</code>, and
the impossibility that follows is the negative consequence the pattern requires.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|時間|じかん}がなくては、{f|明日|あした}{f|続|つづ}きをやってください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|時間|じかん}がな<b>ければ</b>、{f|明日|あした}{f|続|つづ}きをやってください。

The problem is the request at the end. `ては` can only be followed by a statement of
what goes wrong, so a sentence ending in 「～てください」 has to switch to `ば` or `たら`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|毎日|まいにち}インスタントラーメン<b>では</b>、{f|栄養|えいよう}が{f|足|た}りない。
(b) {f|毎日|まいにち}インスタントラーメン<b>でも</b>、{f|栄養|えいよう}は{f|足|た}りている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) treats the instant noodles as the cause: a diet like that produces a nutritional
shortfall, and the speaker disapproves.

(b) concedes the same diet and then denies the expected result - the nutrition is
fine anyway. Only `では` blames the noodles; `でも` defends them.

</details>
