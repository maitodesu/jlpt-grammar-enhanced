# ～ないものか

**Meaning** · isn't there some way to ～?; couldn't it somehow ～? - a wish said out loud, half to oneself, that something difficult might be possible after all

The shape is a negative question, but nobody is being asked. `ないものか` is what a speaker
says when a situation is stuck and they want it unstuck: it names the thing they wish for
and hangs a `か` on it, which is a very Japanese way of wishing without demanding. The
frustration is audible, and so is the hope - the speaker has not quite given up.

## Formation

Attaches to the **ない-form of a verb**. Adjectives get in through
<code>～くならない</code>.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + ものか | ならない → ならないものか |
| Potential verb (very common) | potential ない-form + ものか | {f|直|なお}せない → {f|直|なお}せないものか |
| ある | ない + ものか | {f|方法|ほうほう}はない → {f|方法|ほうほう}はないものか |
| い-adjective (through なる) | ～くならない + ものか | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}くならないものか |
| Softer, more musing | ～ない + ものだろうか | できない → できないものだろうか |
| Polite | ～ない + ものでしょうか | {f|届|とど}かない → {f|届|とど}かないものでしょうか |
| Reported wish | ～ないものか + と{f|思|おも}う | {f|会|あ}えない → {f|会|あ}えないものかと{f|思|おも}う |
| Standard opener | {f|何|なん}とか + ～ないものか | ならない → {f|何|なん}とかならないものか |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the negative is not optional. After a **positive** plain form, `ものか` is an
entirely different pattern meaning emphatic denial:
<code>{f|行|い}くものか</code> is "as if I'd go!" See the Contrast section - this is the one
place where getting the polarity wrong reverses the sentence completely.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|何|なん}とかなら<b>ないものか</b>。
Isn't there something that can be done about it?
</pre>

<pre>
もう{f|少|すこ}し{f|安|やす}くなら<b>ないものか</b>。
Couldn't it be a bit cheaper?
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|渋滞|じゅうたい}を{f|何|なん}とかでき<b>ないものだろうか</b>。
Is there really no way of doing something about this congestion?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもたちが{f|安心|あんしん}して{f|遊|あそ}べる{f|場所|ばしょ}を{f|作|つく}れ<b>ないものか</b>。
Could we not make somewhere the children can play safely?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|捨|す}てるには{f|惜|お}しい。{f|誰|だれ}か{f|使|つか}ってくれ<b>ないものだろうか</b>。
It seems a shame to throw it out. Might somebody not have a use for it?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|地元|じもと}の{f|人|ひと}たちの{f|声|こえ}が、もっと{f|市|し}の{f|計画|けいかく}に{f|反映|はんえい}され<b>ないものでしょうか</b>。
Could local people's views not be reflected rather more in the city's plans?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|同|おな}じ{f|失敗|しっぱい}を{f|繰|く}り{f|返|かえ}さずに{f|済|す}む{f|方法|ほうほう}は<b>ないものか</b>と{f|考|かんが}え{f|続|つづ}けている。

</pre>

Precisely because I have used the tool for so long, I keep hoping there is some way to mend it.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Mildly formal, and thoughtful rather than chatty. It is at home in editorials and opinion
columns - <code>{f|行政|ぎょうせい}は{f|何|なん}とかできないものか</code> is the
grammar of a letter to the paper - and equally at home in speech when somebody is genuinely
stuck and thinking aloud. What it is not is casual. Among friends the everyday version is
<code>～ないかなあ</code>: <code>もう{f|少|すこ}し{f|安|やす}くならないかなあ</code>.

The three endings are a politeness gradient, and it is a real one.
<code>ないものか</code> is the bluntest and can sound impatient;
<code>ないものだろうか</code> softens it into musing;
<code>ないものでしょうか</code> is the form to use if anyone senior is listening.

The important pragmatic point: **it is a wish, not a request.** Because the sentence is
formally addressed to nobody, aiming it at a person turns it into a complaint about them.
<code>もう{f|少|すこ}し{f|早|はや}くできないものか</code> said to a colleague is a
rebuke; if you actually want them to hurry, you need a request form such as
<code>～ていただけませんか</code>.

It also wants something **outside the speaker's control** - traffic, prices, other people's
decisions, a broken object. Applied to your own free choices it sounds strange, because
there is nothing to wish about; for those, <code>～たいものだ</code> or
<code>～たらいいのに</code> is what you want.

## Contrast

### ～ないものか vs ～ものか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with `ない`, it is a **wish**. Without `ない`, it is an **emphatic
denial**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|何|なん}とか{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わせられ<b>ないものか</b>。
Isn't there some way of getting it done in time?
</pre>

<pre>
あんな{f|話|はなし}、{f|信|しん}じられる<b>ものか</b>。
As if anyone could believe a story like that!
</pre>

The second is one of the strongest denials in the language, and it is casual and heated -
<code>{f|負|ま}けるものか</code>, <code>{f|誰|だれ}が{f|行|い}くものか</code>. The two
patterns look almost identical on the page, and the whole difference is the `ない`. Read
the polarity first, before you read the tone.

### ～ないものか vs ～ないかなあ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same wish, different room.
<code>ないものか</code> belongs to writing and considered speech;
<code>ないかなあ</code> is what you mutter.

</div>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}、やま<b>ないかなあ</b>。
I wish this rain would let up.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|不便|ふべん}な{f|仕組|しく}みを{f|改|あらた}められ<b>ないものか</b>。
Could this inconvenient system not be reformed?
</pre>

Notice that the subject matter shifts with the register. `ないかなあ` handles small private
wishes - rain, a delivery, a text message - while `ないものか` reaches for things that
concern more people than the speaker. Using the formal one about your own umbrella is
faintly comic; using the casual one in an editorial would be unpublishable.

### ～ないものか vs ～てほしい・～ていただけませんか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ないものか` wishes **into the air**.
<code>～てほしい</code> and <code>～ていただけませんか</code> point at a **person** who is
expected to act.

</div>

<pre>
もう{f|少|すこ}し{f|待|ま}っていただけ<b>ませんか</b>。
Could you possibly wait a little longer?
</pre>

<pre>
もう{f|少|すこ}し{f|待|ま}ってもらえ<b>ないものか</b>。
Is there really no chance of being given a little longer?
</pre>

The first is a request, and the listener can say yes. The second is the speaker's own
frustration, and a listener who hears it aimed at them will hear pressure rather than a
question. If you want an action from someone, ask for it directly; keep
<code>ないものか</code> for the times when there is nobody to ask.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ もう{f|少|すこ}し{f|安|やす}い<b>ものか</b>。 → ✅ もう{f|少|すこ}し{f|安|やす}く<b>ならないものか</b>。
  Two problems at once. An adjective has to come in through <code>なる</code>, and without
  the `ない` the sentence becomes the denial pattern - "cheap, my foot!"

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}が{f|解決|かいけつ}する<b>ものか</b>。（meaning "I wish it could be solved"） → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}を{f|解決|かいけつ}でき<b>ないものか</b>。
  Same trap from the other direction: the positive form denies. The wish needs the negative,
  and the potential makes the wish sound like a search for a way.

- ❌ すみません、{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けてくれ<b>ないものか</b>。 → ✅ すみません、{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けていただけ<b>ませんか</b>。
  A wish addressed to a person is not a polite request; it reads as impatience. Use a
  request form when you want someone to do something.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|来年|らいねん}{f|留学|りゅうがく}でき<b>ないものか</b>。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}も{f|来年|らいねん}{f|留学|りゅうがく}し<b>たいものだ</b>。
  If the decision is yours to make, there is nothing to wish for. The pattern wants
  something the speaker cannot simply go and do.

## Practice

**1.** {f|毎年|まいとし}この{f|坂道|さかみち}で{f|事故|じこ}が{f|起|お}きている。{f|何|なん}とか____ものか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|何|なん}とか<b>ならない</b>ものか。

<code>{f|何|なん}とかならないものか</code> is the pattern's most idiomatic frame: a problem
outside the speaker's control, and a wish that somebody, somehow, will deal with it.
Without the `ない` the sentence would flip into a denial.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|向|む}かって - 「{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありません、{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りを（{f|延|の}ばせないものか・{f|延|の}ばしていただけないでしょうか）。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りを<b>{f|延|の}ばしていただけないでしょうか</b>。

You are asking a specific person to do a specific thing, so you need a request form. The
other version is a wish flung at the ceiling, and directed at a manager it sounds like a
complaint about the deadline rather than a request for help.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Could the bus service not be made a little more frequent?"

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

バスの{f|本数|ほんすう}を、もう{f|少|すこ}し{f|増|ふ}やせ<b>ないものだろうか</b>。

A public matter outside the speaker's control is exactly this pattern's subject, and the
potential <code>{f|増|ふ}やせる</code> turns it into "is there no way to increase it".
<code>ものだろうか</code> keeps it musing rather than impatient.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: あの{f|古|ふる}い{f|写真|しゃしん}、きれいに{f|直|なお}せるものか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ あの{f|古|ふる}い{f|写真|しゃしん}、きれいに{f|直|なお}せ<b>ないものか</b>。

As written, the sentence means "as if that could be restored!" - a flat denial. Adding the
`ない` turns it into the intended wish that some way of restoring the photo might exist.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|値段|ねだん}をもう{f|少|すこ}し{f|下|さ}げてもらえ<b>ないでしょうか</b>。
(b) {f|値段|ねだん}がもう{f|少|すこ}し{f|下|さ}がら<b>ないものでしょうか</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a negotiation. It is addressed to the seller, it uses もらえる to name them as the
one who would act, and it expects an answer - yes, no, or a counter-offer.

(b) steps back from the person. The verb is intransitive, so nobody is being asked to do
anything; the speaker is lamenting the price in general. In a shop (a) may get you a
discount and (b) will get you sympathy, which is sometimes exactly the difference you want.

</details>
