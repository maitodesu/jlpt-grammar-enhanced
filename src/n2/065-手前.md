# ～手前

**Meaning** · (1) just short of; on this side of - a place you reach *before* the landmark; (2) with these people watching, and after what I've said - so I can't very well do otherwise

Read <code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code>. The literal sense is spatial: the near side, the
stretch in front of something. The figurative sense keeps that image and turns it
social - the people in front of you, and the position you have put yourself in
where they can see. <code>{f|子|こ}どもの{f|手前|てまえ}</code> means "with the
children right there"; <code>{f|約束|やくそく}した{f|手前|てまえ}</code> means
"having said it out loud, in front of witnesses". Both are about appearances, and
what follows is almost always something the speaker *cannot* do.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (place) | noun + の + {f|手前|てまえ} | {f|信号|しんごう} → {f|信号|しんごう}の{f|手前|てまえ} |
| Noun (person watching) | noun + の + {f|手前|てまえ} | {f|後輩|こうはい} → {f|後輩|こうはい}の{f|手前|てまえ} |
| Verb (past) | た-form + {f|手前|てまえ} | {f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}けた → {f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}けた{f|手前|てまえ} |
| Verb (ている) | ている + {f|手前|てまえ} | {f|言|い}っている → {f|言|い}っている{f|手前|てまえ} |
| Verb (dictionary) | plain form + {f|手前|てまえ} | {f|教|おし}える → {f|教|おし}える{f|手前|てまえ} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the social sense has a strong preference for what comes after it:
<code>わけにはいかない</code>, <code>できない</code>,
<code>{f|言|い}えない</code>, <code>～にくい</code>,
<code>なければならない</code>. If your second clause is not about being unable or
obliged, <code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> is probably the wrong pattern.

</div>

## Nuance & register

The social <code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> is a *face* word. It does not say the speaker is prevented by
circumstances, or by a rule; it says they are prevented by how it would look to
particular people who are watching or who heard them speak. That is a very
specific feeling, and Japanese speakers use it with a certain wryness - there is
usually a hint that the speaker would rather do the other thing, and can't. It is
common in ordinary conversation and in fiction, and rare in formal writing, where
the same content is put more neutrally.

Because the constraint is about appearances, <code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> also carries a light note of
vanity, self-mockery or duty depending on who is watching. Compare "I can't back
out in front of the students" with "as the person who proposed it, I can't back
out": the first is
<code>{f|学生|がくせい}の{f|手前|てまえ}</code>, the second
<code>{f|提案|ていあん}した{f|手前|てまえ}</code>, and both are things people admit
slightly ruefully.

One warning about the word outside this pattern.
<code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> is also an old first-person pronoun, surviving in the
business humble form <code>{f|手前|てまえ}ども</code> ("we, our firm"). Contracted,
the very same word became <code>てめえ</code>, a genuinely insulting second-person
"you" that you will hear in fights and in anime and should never say. Do not use
<code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> to mean "you" under any circumstances - the polite
route is the person's name, or あなた.

## Contrast

### {f|手前|てまえ} vs {f|前|まえ}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|前|まえ}</code> is the space *in front of* a thing, as
seen from the thing. <code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> is the space *before you reach*
it, as seen from you.

</div>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}の<b>{f|前|まえ}</b>で{f|待|ま}っています。
I'll be waiting in front of the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}の<b>{f|手前|てまえ}</b>に{f|小|ちい}さな{f|橋|はし}があります。
There's a small bridge just before you get to the station.
</pre>

Taxi directions live on this distinction, and getting it wrong puts you a block
away: <code>コンビニの{f|手前|てまえ}で{f|止|と}めてください</code> means stop
short of the convenience store, not outside it. Note also that
<code>{f|前|まえ}</code> covers time as well as space -
<code>{f|食事|しょくじ}の{f|前|まえ}</code> - while
<code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> never refers to time.

### ～{f|手前|てまえ} vs ～からには・～{f|以上|いじょう}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - all three turn a past commitment into a present obligation.
`からには` and <code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> appeal to logic or principle;
<code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> appeals to how it would look.

</div>

<pre>
{f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}けた<b>からには</b>、{f|最後|さいご}まで{f|責任|せきにん}を{f|持|も}ちます。
Having taken it on, I'll see it through to the end.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}けた<b>{f|手前|てまえ}</b>、{f|今|いま}さら{f|断|ことわ}れない。
Having taken it on, I can't very well pull out now.
</pre>

The first is a promise; the speaker sounds committed. The second is a predicament;
the speaker sounds cornered. That difference is why `からには` is what you say in a
speech and <code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> is what you say to a friend afterwards.
Notice too that <code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> can take a *person* rather than an
action - <code>{f|親|おや}の{f|手前|てまえ}</code> - which neither of the others can
do.

### ～{f|手前|てまえ} vs ～もので・～ものだから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> explains why you **have to** do
something you would rather not. `もので` explains why you **failed** to do
something you should have.

</div>

<pre>
{f|急|きゅう}に{f|来客|らいきゃく}があった<b>もので</b>、{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|遅|おく}れました。
A visitor turned up unexpectedly, which is why my reply was late.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}をすると{f|約束|やくそく}した<b>{f|手前|てまえ}</b>、{f|今日中|きょうじゅう}に{f|書|か}かなければならない。
I promised a reply, so I have to write it before the day is out.
</pre>

Both are socially motivated, and both belong to spoken Japanese, but they point in
opposite directions: one asks to be excused, the other admits there is no excuse
available. They are not interchangeable in either direction.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|駅|えき}の<b>{f|手前|てまえ}</b>で{f|待|ま}ち{f|合|あ}わせよう。(meaning "outside the station") → ✅ {f|駅|えき}の<b>{f|前|まえ}</b>で{f|待|ま}ち{f|合|あ}わせよう。
  <code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> puts your friend somewhere short of the station with
  nothing to stand next to.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っている<b>{f|手前|てまえ}</b>、{f|出|で}かけられない。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っている<b>ので</b>、{f|出|で}かけられない。
  Rain has no opinion of you. The social sense needs an audience or a statement you
  made, not a physical cause.

- ❌ {f|約束|やくそく}した<b>{f|手前|てまえ}</b>、とても{f|嬉|うれ}しいです。 → ✅ {f|約束|やくそく}した<b>{f|手前|てまえ}</b>、{f|断|ことわ}るわけにはいきません。
  The second half has to be an obligation or an inability. A pleasant feeling has
  no face to save.

- ❌ <b>{f|手前|てまえ}</b>はどう{f|思|おも}いますか。 → ✅ {f|田中|たなか}さんはどう{f|思|おも}いますか。
  As a pronoun this word is either archaic-humble ("I") or, contracted to
  <code>てめえ</code>, an insult. Never use it for "you".

## Practice

**1.** {f|自分|じぶん}が{f|決|き}めた____、{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}うわけにはいかない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|自分|じぶん}が{f|決|き}めた<b>{f|手前|てまえ}</b>

The speaker made the decision publicly, so complaining now would look bad -
appearances, not logic, are doing the work. <code>わけにはいかない</code> is the
classic second half.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|次|つぎ}の{f|角|かど}の（{f|前|まえ}・{f|手前|てまえ}）で{f|止|と}めてください。ちょうど{f|家|いえ}の{f|入口|いりぐち}があります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|次|つぎ}の{f|角|かど}の<b>{f|手前|てまえ}</b>で{f|止|と}めてください。

The entrance is on the near side, so you want the driver to stop before reaching
the corner. <code>{f|角|かど}の{f|前|まえ}</code> would be an odd thing to say at
all, since a corner has no front.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I told everyone I'd win, so I can't skip training."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

みんなに{f|勝|か}つと{f|言|い}った<b>{f|手前|てまえ}</b>、{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|休|やす}むわけにはいかない。

The boast was made in front of people, which is exactly the setting for
<code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code>, and skipping training would expose it. Note the た-form
before the pattern.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部長|ぶちょう}の{f|手前|てまえ}、{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|長|なが}くなりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}の{f|手前|てまえ}、{f|途中|とちゅう}で{f|帰|かえ}るわけにはいきませんでした。

The original gives a result that has nothing to do with saving face - meetings do
not run long because the boss is present. <code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> needs a
second half about what the speaker could not bring themselves to do.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|先輩|せんぱい}の<b>{f|手前|てまえ}</b>、{f|先|さき}に{f|帰|かえ}れなかった。
(b) {f|先輩|せんぱい}が<b>いたので</b>、{f|先|さき}に{f|帰|かえ}れなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says it was a question of how it would look. The senior colleague was there,
leaving first would have seemed rude, and the speaker stayed for appearances -
usually said with a small sigh.

(b) simply reports a cause and leaves the reason unstated. It could mean the same
thing, but it could equally mean the senior colleague had the car keys.
<code>{f|手前|てまえ}</code> is what makes the social pressure explicit.

</details>
