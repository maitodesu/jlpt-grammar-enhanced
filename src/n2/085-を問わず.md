# ～を問わず

**Meaning** · regardless of; without regard to - this is not something we ask about

<code>{f|問|と}う</code> means "to ask, to call into question", so
<code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> literally says *we do not ask*. That is its flavour: the
speaker is an institution announcing which boxes it will **not** be ticking. Age,
sex, nationality, experience - named and then waved through.

## Formation

<code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> takes a **noun**, and the noun is nearly always a short
one naming a category or a two-way split.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + を{f|問|と}わず | {f|経験|けいけん} → {f|経験|けいけん}を{f|問|と}わず |
| Paired nouns, written together | Noun + Noun + を{f|問|と}わず | {f|男女|だんじょ}・{f|昼夜|ちゅうや}・{f|国内外|こくないがい} を{f|問|と}わず |
| Two nouns joined by か | Noun + か + Noun + かを{f|問|と}わず | {f|新品|しんぴん}か{f|中古|ちゅうこ}かを{f|問|と}わず |
| Noun + の{f|有無|うむ} | Noun + の{f|有無|うむ} + を{f|問|と}わず | {f|資格|しかく}の{f|有無|うむ}を{f|問|と}わず |
| Modifying a noun | Noun + を{f|問|と}わない + Noun | {f|年齢|ねんれい}を{f|問|と}わない{f|募集|ぼしゅう} |
| Written / listing form | Noun + を{f|問|と}わず、… | in notices, before a comma |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> is a fixed adverbial phrase, but
<code>{f|問|と}う</code> underneath it is a live verb, which is why the
noun-modifying form is <code>を{f|問|と}わない</code> and the polite predicate form is
<code>を{f|問|と}いません</code>: <code>{f|経験|けいけん}は{f|問|と}いません</code>,
"experience not required".

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|経験|けいけん}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>{f|募集|ぼしゅう}しています。
We are recruiting regardless of experience.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|男女|だんじょ}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>{f|応募|おうぼ}できます。
Men and women alike may apply.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}は{f|年中|ねんじゅう}{f|無休|むきゅう}で、{f|昼夜|ちゅうや}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>{f|営業|えいぎょう}している。
The shop never closes - it trades day and night alike.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|季節|きせつ}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>{f|楽|たの}しめる{f|温泉|おんせん}として{f|知|し}られています。
It is known as a hot spring you can enjoy in any season.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大人|おとな}{f|子|こ}ども<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>、この{f|映画|えいが}には{f|泣|な}かされる。
Adult or child, this film gets you.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|受賞|じゅしょう}{f|歴|れき}の{f|有無|うむ}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>、どなたでも{f|出品|しゅっぴん}していただけます。
Anyone may submit a piece, whether or not they have won awards before.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|国内外|こくないがい}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>、{f|優|すぐ}れた{f|研究|けんきゅう}には{f|同|おな}じ{f|基準|きじゅん}で{f|助成金|じょせいきん}を{f|出|だ}す{f|方針|ほうしん}です。
Our policy is to fund outstanding research on the same criteria at home and abroad.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|正社員|せいしゃいん}か{f|派遣|はけん}か<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>、{f|同|おな}じ{f|仕事|しごと}には{f|同|おな}じ{f|賃金|ちんぎん}を{f|払|はら}うべきだ。
Permanent or agency, the same work should be paid the same wage.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is public-notice Japanese. It belongs to job ads, competition rules, opening
hours, museum leaflets and terms of service, and its effect is to sound official
and even-handed. Nothing about it is rude in conversation, but it is stiff: a
friend who asked whether your local café is open on Sundays would find
<code>{f|曜日|ようび}を{f|問|と}わず{f|営業|えいぎょう}しています</code> a strange
answer, where <code>{f|毎日|まいにち}やってるよ</code> is what you would actually say.

The nouns that go in front are conventionalised, and learning the stock list is
more useful than learning the rule: {f|年齢|ねんれい}, {f|性別|せいべつ}, {f|国籍|こくせき}, {f|経験|けいけん}, {f|資格|しかく},
{f|学歴|がくれき}, {f|季節|きせつ}, {f|昼夜|ちゅうや}, {f|男女|だんじょ},
{f|新旧|しんきゅう}, {f|大小|だいしょう}, {f|理由|りゆう}. Notice how many are
two-kanji compounds that already contain both poles of a contrast.
{f|昼夜|ちゅうや} is not "daytime"; it is "day-and-night", and that built-in pairing
is why it fits so neatly.

Because <code>{f|問|と}う</code> is a real verb, the phrase carries a faint agent -
somebody who would have been doing the asking. That makes
<code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> natural where there is a screening process to waive, and
slightly odd where there is nobody to do any asking. For a plain fact of nature -
the tide comes in whatever you think - `にかかわらず` sits better.

## Contrast

### ～を{f|問|と}わず vs ～にかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> waives a **criterion**;
`にかかわらず` dismisses a **variable**. Only `にかかわらず` takes a clause
comfortably.

</div>

<pre>
{f|学歴|がくれき}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>{f|採用|さいよう}します。
We hire without regard to educational background.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大学|だいがく}を{f|出|で}ている{f|出|で}ていない<b>にかかわらず</b>{f|採用|さいよう}します。
We hire whether or not you have been to university.
</pre>

The mechanical difference is the one to hold on to. `にかかわらず` will follow a
verb pair or a `かどうか` clause; <code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> wants a noun, and a
long clause in front of it reads like a badly drafted form. Where both are
possible, <code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> is the crisper, more formulaic choice - it is
what the printed advertisement says.

### ～を{f|問|と}わず vs ～を{f|除|のぞ}いて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> says the category is
**irrelevant**; <code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code> says one member is **carved out**.

</div>

This is the sharpest line in this whole group of patterns, and learners blur it
because both English translations start with a dismissive gesture.

<pre>
{f|年齢|ねんれい}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>{f|入場|にゅうじょう}できます。
Anyone can come in, whatever their age.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|小学生|しょうがくせい}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>{f|入場|にゅうじょう}できます。
Everyone can come in except primary-school children.
</pre>

In the first, age never enters the decision - the door is open to all. In the
second, one named group is standing outside it. <code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> takes an
*axis* ({f|年齢|ねんれい}, {f|国籍|こくせき}); <code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code> takes a
*member* ({f|小学生|しょうがくせい}, {f|田中|たなか}さん). If the noun could answer
"which one?", you want <code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code>.

### ～を{f|問|と}わず vs ～もかまわず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> is **neutral policy**;
`もかまわず` is somebody **ignoring what they should have minded**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>ご{f|相談|そうだん}をお{f|受|う}けしています。
We take enquiries at any hour.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}<b>もかまわず</b>{f|電話|でんわ}をかけてくる。
He rings at all hours without a thought.
</pre>

The Japanese is nearly parallel and the attitudes are opposite. The first is a
service being advertised; the second is a complaint. `もかまわず` always implies
that the thing being ignored had a claim on the person's attention, so it cannot be
used for a rule you are announcing about yourself.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|田中|たなか}さん<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>、{f|全員|ぜんいん}{f|来|き}た。 → ✅ {f|田中|たなか}さん<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、{f|全員|ぜんいん}{f|来|き}た。
  A named individual is a member, not a criterion. Waiving a criterion is
  <code>{f|問|と}う</code>; removing a member is <code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code>.

- ❌ {f|参加|さんか}する<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b> → ✅ {f|参加|さんか}するかどうか<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>（or {f|参加|さんか}の{f|有無|うむ}を{f|問|と}わず）
  A bare verb cannot sit in front of it. Nominalise with `かどうか`, or use the noun
  compound with {f|有無|うむ}.

- ❌ {f|国籍|こくせき}<b>に{f|問|と}わず</b> → ✅ {f|国籍|こくせき}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>
  <code>{f|問|と}う</code> is transitive, so its object takes `を`. `に` belongs to
  `にかかわらず`, whose verb is intransitive. Getting the two particles crossed is
  the single commonest slip with this pair.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|天気|てんき}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>{f|寒|さむ}くなるでしょう。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|天気|てんき}<b>にかかわらず</b>{f|寒|さむ}くなるでしょう。
  Nobody is screening the weather. With no implied asker,
  <code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> sounds like a form filled in by mistake.

## Practice

**1.** このコンテストは、プロ・アマ____どなたでも{f|参加|さんか}できます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

プロ・アマ<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>どなたでも{f|参加|さんか}できます。

Professional versus amateur is a two-way category, and the sentence is competition
rules - the exact home of this pattern. The pair is written straight through with
no particle between the two halves.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|割引|わりびき}は、{f|会員|かいいん}（を{f|問|と}わず・を{f|除|のぞ}いて）{f|全員|ぜんいん}に{f|適用|てきよう}されます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会員|かいいん}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>{f|全員|ぜんいん}に{f|適用|てきよう}されます。
"The discount applies to everyone except members."

{f|会員|かいいん} here names a group of people who are being taken out of the set, so
it needs <code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code>. <code>を{f|問|と}わず</code> would only work with
the axis behind it - <code>{f|会員|かいいん}かどうかを{f|問|と}わず</code>, "whether
you are a member or not" - which says the opposite: everyone gets it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Applications are accepted from anyone, with or without a driving licence."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|運転|うんてん}{f|免許|めんきょ}の{f|有無|うむ}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>、どなたでも{f|応募|おうぼ}できます。

"With or without" is the fixed compound {f|有無|うむ}, and
<code>Nの{f|有無|うむ}を{f|問|と}わず</code> is the standard shape in a job
advertisement. <code>{f|免許|めんきょ}を{f|持|も}っているかどうかにかかわらず</code>
says the same thing in plainer, more spoken Japanese.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|理由|りゆう}に{f|問|と}わず、{f|遅刻|ちこく}は{f|遅刻|ちこく}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|理由|りゆう}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>、{f|遅刻|ちこく}は{f|遅刻|ちこく}です。
"Whatever the reason, late is late."

<code>{f|問|と}う</code> takes a direct object, so the particle is `を`. The `に`
version is a crossover from `にかかわらず`, which is built on an intransitive verb.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|曜日|ようび}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>、{f|窓口|まどぐち}は{f|開|あ}いています。
(b) {f|土日|どにち}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、{f|窓口|まどぐち}は{f|開|あ}いています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the day of the week is not a factor at all: the counter is open every day,
weekends included. The axis - which day it is - has been declared irrelevant.

(b) keeps the axis very much alive and removes two of its values. The counter is
open Monday to Friday. A learner who reads both as "regardless of the day" will
turn up on a Saturday and find the shutters down, which is why this pair is worth
saying out loud a few times.

</details>
