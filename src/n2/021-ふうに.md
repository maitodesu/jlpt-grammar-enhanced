# ～ふうに

**Meaning** · in this / that / what way; in the style of ～ - names the *manner* in
which something is done or made

<code>ふう</code> is the noun {f|風|ふう}, "manner, style, air". Attached to a
demonstrative it gives you the everyday way of pointing at a manner -
<code>こんなふうに</code>, "like this". Attached to a noun it gives you "in the
style of" - <code>{f|昔|むかし}ふうの{f|家|いえ}</code>, a house done in the old
manner.

## Formation

<code>ふう</code> is a noun, so it takes `に` to modify a verb, `の` or `な` to
modify a noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| こんな・そんな・あんな・どんな | ～な + ふうに | こんな → こんなふうに{f|書|か}く |
| Noun (adverbial) | Noun + ふうに | ヨーロッパ → ヨーロッパふうに{f|飾|かざ}る |
| Noun (before another noun) | Noun + ふうの + Noun | {f|昔|むかし} → {f|昔|むかし}ふうの{f|建物|たてもの} |
| Noun (before another noun) | Noun + ふうな + Noun | {f|大人|おとな} → {f|大人|おとな}ふうな{f|話|はな}し{f|方|かた} |
| Quoted clause | clause + という + ふうに | {f|嫌|いや}だ → {f|嫌|いや}だというふうに |
| Written in kanji | {f|風|ふう} | こんなふうに → こんな{f|風|ふう}に |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>ふうの</code> and <code>ふうな</code> are both used before a noun,
with <code>ふうの</code> the commoner and more neutral of the two.
<code>ふうな</code> leans slightly towards "having the air of", which is why it
turns up so often with words about people's manner.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - a few noun + {f|風|ふう} combinations have hardened into single words and
are read as compounds: {f|和風|わふう}, {f|洋風|ようふう}, {f|欧風|おうふう}. Those
are vocabulary, not this pattern, and they are always written in kanji. The
productive pattern - the one you can build new phrases with - is usually written in
kana.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Neutral to slightly casual, and extremely common in speech. It is the word you
reach for when demonstrating something with your hands - <code>こんなふうに{f|持|も}って</code> -
and when asking someone to describe a manner rather than a fact -
<code>どんなふうに{f|感|かん}じましたか</code>. In polite conversation it is
entirely at home; in a formal written report it would usually be replaced by
<code>このように</code>.

The demonstrative forms follow the ordinary こ・そ・あ・ど logic. <code>こんなふうに</code>
points at something in front of you or at what you are about to demonstrate;
<code>そんなふうに</code> points at what the other person just did or said, and is
therefore the one that turns up in complaints -
<code>そんなふうに{f|言|い}わなくてもいいでしょう</code>; <code>あんなふうに</code>
points at an absent third party, often admiringly.

The noun-plus-<code>ふう</code> use is a different register again. It is the
vocabulary of design, cooking and description -
<code>{f|中華|ちゅうか}ふうのスープ</code>, <code>{f|昭和|しょうわ}ふうの{f|喫茶店|きっさてん}</code> -
and it makes no claim that the thing is authentic. That is precisely its value: it
means "done in the manner of", with the implication that it is not the real article.

One small caution. Applied to a person, <code>{f|学者|がくしゃ}ふうの{f|男|おとこ}</code>
means someone who *looks* like an academic, not someone who is one. Used about a
real professional it can sound faintly dismissive.

## Contrast

### ～ふうに vs ～ように

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ように</code> compares to a **model**: like that thing.
<code>ふうに</code> names a **manner**: in that style. Where the two overlap,
<code>ように</code> is the more formal and the more literal.

</div>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}が{f|書|か}いた<b>ように</b>{f|書|か}いてください。
Please write it the way the teacher wrote it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先生|せんせい}はこんな<b>ふうに</b>{f|書|か}いていました。
The teacher wrote it something like this.
</pre>

The first sentence points at a specific model and asks you to match it. The second
sketches a manner and leaves room for approximation, which is the flavour
<code>ふうに</code> always carries. That is also why <code>ふうに</code> cannot take
a plain clause in front of it the way <code>ように</code> can: you cannot say
❌<code>{f|先生|せんせい}が{f|書|か}いたふうに</code>. Give it a demonstrative, a
noun, or <code>という</code>.

### こんなふうに vs こう・このように

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - three registers of the same gesture. `こう` is terse,
<code>こんなふうに</code> is ordinary and conversational,
<code>このように</code> is formal and written.

</div>

<pre>
ここを<b>こう</b>{f|押|お}すと{f|開|あ}きます。
Press here like so and it opens.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>このように</b>、{f|数字|すうじ}は{f|三年|さんねん}{f|続|つづ}けて{f|下|さ}がっている。
As you can see, the figures have fallen three years running.
</pre>

`こう` is a single adverb and needs a gesture or an obvious context to complete it.
<code>このように</code> belongs on a slide or in a report and is the one to use when
summing up evidence. <code>こんなふうに</code> sits between them and is the safe
default in speech.

### ～ふうに vs ～{f|方|かた}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|方|かた}</code> is a **noun**: the way of doing
something. <code>ふうに</code> is **adverbial**: doing it in that way.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|漢字|かんじ}の{f|書|か}き<b>{f|方|かた}</b>を{f|教|おし}えてください。
Please teach me how to write this kanji.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|漢字|かんじ}はこんな<b>ふうに</b>{f|書|か}きます。
This kanji is written like this.
</pre>

The first asks for the method as a thing you can be taught; the second demonstrates
it. They combine happily -
<code>{f|書|か}き{f|方|かた}をこんなふうに{f|変|か}えてみた</code> - because they are
doing different jobs in the sentence.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>この</b>ふうに{f|書|か}きます。 → ✅ <b>こんな</b>ふうに{f|書|か}きます。
  <code>ふう</code> is a common noun, and the demonstrative that modifies it is the
  な-adjective series こんな・そんな・あんな・どんな, not この・その・あの・どの.

- ❌ {f|楽|たの}しいふうに{f|歌|うた}っていた。 → ✅ {f|楽|たの}し<b>そうに</b>{f|歌|うた}っていた。
  An adjective cannot sit in front of <code>ふうに</code>. To say someone looks a
  certain way, use <code>そうに</code>.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|言|い}ったふうにやってみた。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|言|い}った<b>ように</b>やってみた。
  A bare clause needs <code>ように</code>. If you want <code>ふうに</code> after a
  clause, <code>という</code> has to intervene.

- ❌ ヨーロッパふう<b>に</b>{f|建物|たてもの} → ✅ ヨーロッパふう<b>の</b>{f|建物|たてもの}
  <code>に</code> modifies a verb; a noun needs <code>の</code> or <code>な</code>.

## Practice

**1.** すみません、この{f|機械|きかい}はどんな____{f|動|うご}かすんですか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

どんな<b>ふうに</b>{f|動|うご}かすんですか

The question is about manner - by what method, in what way - and the questioning
member of the set is <code>どんなふうに</code>. Note the `に`, which is what lets
the phrase modify the verb.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|説明書|せつめいしょ}に{f|書|か}いてある（ふうに・とおりに）{f|組|く}み{f|立|た}てた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}に{f|書|か}いてある<b>とおりに</b>{f|組|く}み{f|立|た}てた。

A plain clause cannot be followed by <code>ふうに</code>. Beyond the grammar, the
meaning wants exactness - assembled *as the instructions say* - and
<code>とおりに</code> is the pattern for matching a model precisely, where
<code>ふうに</code> would suggest a rough approximation.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Don't look at me like that."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

そんな<b>ふうに</b>{f|見|み}ないでよ。

The manner being objected to is the listener's own, which is exactly the そ- slot.
<code>こんなふうに</code> would point at something the speaker is demonstrating, and
<code>あんなふうに</code> at someone else entirely.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部屋|へや}を{f|和風|わふう}ふうに{f|直|なお}したいです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部屋|へや}を<b>{f|和風|わふう}に</b>{f|直|なお}したいです。

{f|和風|わふう} already contains {f|風|ふう}; adding another is like saying
"Japanese-style-style". The hardened compounds - {f|和風|わふう},
{f|洋風|ようふう} - take `に` or `の` directly.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|先生|せんせい}です。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|先生|せんせい}<b>ふう</b>の{f|男|おとこ}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) states a fact about his job.

(b) states only how he strikes the observer: tweed jacket, careful speech, an air of
the classroom. Noun + <code>ふう</code> describes the *appearance* of belonging to a
category and quietly declines to say whether he really does. Said about a person who
genuinely is a teacher it would sound faintly dismissive, as though you were
describing a costume.

</details>
