# ～次第

**Meaning** · as soon as X is done, I will do Y - a promise about what happens the moment something becomes possible

<code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> is the business-Japanese "as soon as". It always looks
**forward**: the first event has not happened yet, and the speaker is committing to
what they will do the second it does. Nothing about it can be a past report.

## Formation

<code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> follows the ます-stem of a verb, or a する-noun with
nothing between them.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | ます-stem + {f|次第|しだい} | {f|着|つ}く → {f|着|つ}き{f|次第|しだい} |
| Verb | ます-stem + {f|次第|しだい} | {f|分|わ}かる → {f|分|わ}かり{f|次第|しだい} |
| する-noun | Noun + {f|次第|しだい} | {f|到着|とうちゃく} → {f|到着|とうちゃく}{f|次第|しだい} |
| する-noun (long form) | Noun + し + {f|次第|しだい} | {f|入荷|にゅうか} → {f|入荷|にゅうか}し{f|次第|しだい} |
| ❌ た-form | ❌{f|着|つ}いた{f|次第|しだい} | ✅{f|着|つ}き{f|次第|しだい} |
| ❌ Dictionary form | ❌{f|届|とど}く{f|次第|しだい} | ✅{f|届|とど}き{f|次第|しだい} |
| ❌ Noun + の | ❌{f|到着|とうちゃく}の{f|次第|しだい} | ✅{f|到着|とうちゃく}{f|次第|しだい} |
| ❌ Past main clause | ❌…{f|次第|しだい}、{f|送|おく}りました | ✅…{f|次第|しだい}、{f|送|おく}ります |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the main clause must be **non-past** and must express somebody's
**will**: a plan, a promise, a request, an instruction. If the second event is just
something that happens by itself, <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> is the wrong word.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the verb in front is almost always one that describes a condition
falling into place rather than an act of will: {f|決|き}まる, {f|分|わ}かる,
{f|届|とど}く, {f|終|お}わる, {f|戻|もど}る, できる, {f|空|あ}く. You are waiting on
it, which is why you cannot say when it will be.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the same word <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> also appears in
<code>Nしだいだ／しだいで</code> ("it depends on N") and in the adverb
<code>{f|次第|しだい}に</code> ("gradually"). Those are separate grammar points. You
can tell this one instantly: it sits on a ます-stem and the sentence carries on
after it.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|準備|じゅんび}ができ<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|出発|しゅっぱつ}します。
We'll set off as soon as we're ready.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|分|わ}かり<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、ご{f|連絡|れんらく}します。
I'll let you know as soon as I find out.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}がやみ<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|試合|しあい}を{f|再開|さいかい}します。
The match will resume as soon as the rain stops.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|席|せき}が{f|空|あ}き<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、お{f|呼|よ}びいたします。
We'll call you the moment a table comes free.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}が{f|戻|もど}り<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、この{f|書類|しょるい}を{f|渡|わた}しておいてください。
Hand over these papers as soon as the manager gets back.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|商品|しょうひん}が{f|入荷|にゅうか}し<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、メールでお{f|知|し}らせします。
We'll email you as soon as the item comes back into stock.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|到着|とうちゃく}<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、フロントで{f|受付|うけつけ}をお{f|済|す}ませください。
Please check in at the front desk as soon as you arrive.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|会場|かいじょう}が{f|決|き}まり<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|参加者|さんかしゃ}{f|全員|ぜんいん}に{f|案内|あんない}をお{f|送|おく}りいたします。
As soon as next month's venue is settled, we'll send the details to everyone taking part.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a **formal, largely written** pattern, and it is everywhere in business
Japanese. Notices, order confirmations, internal mail and telephone Japanese all
run on it, usually alongside {f|敬語|けいご}: 「{f|確認|かくにん}でき{f|次第|しだい}、ご{f|連絡|れんらく}いたします」
is close to a fixed sentence.

What it buys you is a particular kind of politeness. Saying
<code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> concedes that you do not control the timing - you are
waiting on a delivery, a decision, a table - while promising that you will act with
no delay once it lands. That combination of *I can't promise when* and *I promise
it will be immediate* is why it sounds reassuring rather than evasive.

In casual conversation it is unusual. Among friends you would say
<code>{f|決|き}まったらすぐ{f|教|おし}えるね</code>, not
<code>{f|決|き}まり{f|次第|しだい}{f|教|おし}えるね</code> - the latter is not wrong,
but it sounds like you are reading from a form. Younger speakers do use it jokingly
for exactly that effect.

One quiet restriction that trips people up: because the main clause must carry
somebody's intention, <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> cannot be used for two events
that simply follow one another in nature.
<code>{f|気温|きおん}が{f|下|さ}がり{f|次第|しだい}、{f|雪|ゆき}に{f|変|か}わります</code>
is odd, because nobody is deciding to turn the rain into snow.

## Contrast

### ～{f|次第|しだい} vs ～たとたん

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - these two are mirror images. <code>たとたん</code> is
**past + surprise**; <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> is **future + intention**. Neither
can cross into the other's half.

</div>

<code>たとたん</code> is the N3 pattern <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> is closest to in
meaning - both compress the gap between two events to nothing - and everything else
about them is opposite.

<pre>
{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|呼|よ}ばれ<b>たとたん</b>、{f|手|て}が{f|震|ふる}えだした。
The moment my name was called, my hands started shaking.
</pre>

Past, involuntary, and reported with a little shock.

<pre>
{f|名前|なまえ}が{f|呼|よ}ばれ<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、こちらの{f|部屋|へや}にお{f|入|はい}りください。
Please come into this room as soon as your name is called.
</pre>

Future, and an instruction. Try to swap them and both sentences break: there is no
past <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> and no imperative <code>たとたん</code>.

### ～{f|次第|しだい} vs ～たらすぐに / ～てから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たらすぐに` says the same thing in ordinary Japanese.
<code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> adds formality and narrows the first clause to something
outside your control.

</div>

<pre>
{f|家|いえ}に{f|着|つ}い<b>たらすぐに</b>{f|電話|でんわ}するね。
I'll ring you as soon as I get home.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|検査|けんさ}の{f|結果|けっか}が{f|出|で}<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|担当医|たんとうい}よりご{f|説明|せつめい}いたします。
The doctor in charge will explain as soon as the test results are in.
</pre>

Note what the first clauses are. Getting home is your own doing, so `たら` is
natural and <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> would sound overblown. Test results arrive
when they arrive - that is exactly the shape <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> wants.
`てから` differs again: it only orders the events and carries no sense of "with no
delay at all".

### ～{f|次第|しだい} vs ～{f|次第|しだい}だ / ～{f|次第|しだい}で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - what comes **before** it decides which word you are looking at.
A **ます-stem** gives "as soon as"; a **plain noun** gives "depends on".

</div>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|届|とど}き<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|皆|みな}さんにお{f|伝|つた}えします。
I'll pass it on to everyone as soon as the reply arrives.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}くかどうかは{f|天気|てんき}<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>です。
Whether we go tomorrow depends on the weather.
</pre>

The second one ends the sentence with `です` and has a bare noun in front; the first
sits mid-sentence on a ます-stem. Once you look at that one spot the two never
collide - and note that a する-noun like <code>{f|到着|とうちゃく}</code> can feed
either, so read the rest of the sentence before deciding.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}い<b>た{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|電話|でんわ}します。 → ✅ {f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}<b>き{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|電話|でんわ}します。
  The base is the ます-stem. There is no た-form or dictionary form in front of this
  <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code>.

- ❌ {f|荷物|にもつ}が{f|届|とど}き<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|返事|へんじ}を{f|書|か}きました。 → ✅ {f|荷物|にもつ}が{f|届|とど}き<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|返事|へんじ}を{f|書|か}きます。
  The main clause cannot be past. If it already happened, use `たらすぐに` or
  `てすぐ`.

- ❌ {f|春|はる}になり<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|桜|さくら}が{f|咲|さ}きます。 → ✅ {f|春|はる}になると、{f|桜|さくら}が{f|咲|さ}きます。
  Nobody wills the cherry blossom open. With no intention in the main clause,
  <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> has nothing to attach its promise to.

- ❌ {f|出発|しゅっぱつ}<b>する{f|次第|しだい}</b> → ✅ {f|出発|しゅっぱつ}<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>（or {f|出発|しゅっぱつ}し{f|次第|しだい}）
  A する-noun joins bare, or through the stem `し`. The full
  <code>する</code> never appears.

## Practice

**1.** {f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わり____、{f|議事録|ぎじろく}をお{f|送|おく}りします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わり<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>

A ます-stem in front, a polite promise behind - the standard business shape. You are
not deciding when the meeting ends, which is exactly why
<code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> fits.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}け（たとたん・{f|次第|しだい}）、ハチが{f|入|はい}ってきた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}け<b>たとたん</b>、ハチが{f|入|はい}ってきた。

The sentence is a past report of something nobody wanted.
<code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> only points forward and only into an intended action, so
it cannot host a bee.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "We will notify you by post as soon as the examination date is fixed."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|試験|しけん}の{f|日程|にってい}が{f|決|き}まり<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|郵便|ゆうびん}でお{f|知|し}らせします。

<code>{f|決|き}まる</code> is the intransitive "get settled", which is the kind of
verb <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> takes, and the main clause is a polite undertaking.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部品|ぶひん}が{f|入荷|にゅうか}する{f|次第|しだい}、{f|修理|しゅうり}を{f|始|はじ}めました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部品|ぶひん}が{f|入荷|にゅうか}<b>し{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|修理|しゅうり}を{f|始|はじ}め<b>ます</b>。

Two faults. A する-noun joins as <code>{f|入荷|にゅうか}し{f|次第|しだい}</code> or
bare, never with the full <code>する</code>; and the main clause has to be non-past,
because the whole pattern is a promise about what is still to come.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|資料|しりょう}が{f|届|とど}い<b>てから</b>、{f|検討|けんとう}します。
(b) {f|資料|しりょう}が{f|届|とど}き<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、{f|検討|けんとう}します。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) only puts the two in order: the documents arrive first, the review comes after.
It leaves the gap open - it might be next week.

(b) promises there will be no gap at all. It also quietly signals that the delay is
not yours: you are waiting, and the moment the material lands you will act. In an
email (b) is markedly more reassuring than (a).

</details>
