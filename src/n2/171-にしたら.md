# ～にしたら

**Meaning** · from ~'s point of view; as far as ~ is concerned - imagines how a situation feels to a particular person

<code>にしたら</code> hands the sentence over to somebody else's feelings. The noun
in front is always a **person** (or a group, or an animal you are treating as one),
and what follows is what the situation looks like *to them*, usually with an
undertone of "give them some credit". It is the sympathy member of this family:
where <code>からすると</code> can weigh evidence and
<code>から{f|言|い}うと</code> can measure on a scale, <code>にしたら</code> does
one job only.

## Formation

Four interchangeable shapes, all built on する.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Person | Noun + にしたら | {f|親|おや} → {f|親|おや}にしたら |
| Person, slightly more written | Noun + にすれば | {f|部下|ぶか} → {f|部下|ぶか}にすれば |
| Person, emphatic | Noun + にしてみれば | {f|本人|ほんにん} → {f|本人|ほんにん}にしてみれば |
| Person, emphatic and spoken | Noun + にしてみたら | {f|子|こ}ども → {f|子|こ}どもにしてみたら |
| Group or organisation | Noun + にしたら | {f|会社|かいしゃ}{f|側|がわ} → {f|会社|かいしゃ}{f|側|がわ}にしたら |
| Main clause typically | だろう / にちがいない / のだ / かもしれない | … → …つらいだろう |

<div class="note">

**Note** - do not confuse this with the identical-looking
<code>Ａを Ｂ にしたら</code> ("if you make A into B"), as in
{f|部屋|へや}をきれいにしたら. There the する is a real verb with an object; here
the whole <code>にしたら</code> is one unit attached to a person.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Politeness-neutral: the pattern itself does not change, and the main clause carries
です/ます as needed. <code>にしたら</code> and <code>にしてみたら</code> lean
spoken; <code>にすれば</code> and <code>にしてみれば</code> turn up more in
writing. <code>にしてみれば</code> and <code>にしてみたら</code> add the
{f|見|み}る of "try it and see" - literally "if you put yourself in their place and
look" - so they press the empathy a little harder.

The typical shape of the sentence is a **contrast**: the speaker's own view first,
then the other party's. {f|私|わたし}は{f|平気|へいき}だが、{f|彼|かれ}
<b>にしてみれば</b>… That is why it so often introduces an argument for patience
with someone, and why it sits so naturally with だろう and にちがいない - you are
supposing on somebody's behalf, not reporting what they told you.

Two limits worth remembering. First, only animate perspectives. Machines,
statistics and abstractions have no point of view, so ✗{f|数字|すうじ}にしたら is
not available; <code>からすると</code> covers that ground. Second, using it about
yourself is possible - {f|私|わたし}にしてみれば{f|迷惑|めいわく}な{f|話|はなし}だ -
but it is a slightly dramatic way to speak, as if narrating your own case from
outside. For plainly stating your own position, としては is the everyday choice.

## Contrast

### にしたら vs にとって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>にとって</code> takes an **evaluation**
({f|大切|たいせつ}だ, {f|必要|ひつよう}だ). <code>にしたら</code> takes a whole
**judgement or feeling** that the person would have.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|指輪|ゆびわ}は{f|母|はは}<b>にとって</b>{f|特別|とくべつ}なものだ。
This ring is something special to my mother.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|母|はは}<b>にしたら</b>、{f|指輪|ゆびわ}を{f|売|う}るなど{f|考|かんが}えられないのだろう。
For my mother, selling the ring is presumably unthinkable.
</pre>

Swap them and both go wrong. ❌{f|母|はは}にしたら{f|特別|とくべつ}なものだ is
missing the person's own reaction; ❌{f|母|はは}にとって{f|売|う}るなど
{f|考|かんが}えられない is a whole judgement squeezed into a slot built for
adjectives. If the main clause is one word plus だ, reach for
<code>にとって</code>.

### にしたら vs からすれば

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>にしたら</code> takes **only people**;
<code>からすれば</code> takes people **or** evidence.

</div>

<pre>
{f|受験生|じゅけんせい}<b>にしたら</b>、この{f|時期|じき}の{f|風邪|かぜ}は{f|命|いのち}とりだ。
For someone sitting exams, catching a cold at this time of year is fatal.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|過去|かこ}のデータ<b>からすれば</b>、この{f|時期|じき}は{f|風邪|かぜ}が{f|流行|はや}りやすい。
Going by past data, colds spread easily at this time of year.
</pre>

Data has no feelings, so the second cannot take <code>にしたら</code>. Where the
noun *is* a person, the two overlap heavily, and <code>にしたら</code> is simply
the warmer of the pair.

### にしたら vs としては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>としては</code> is where **you** stand;
<code>にしたら</code> is where **someone else** stands, as imagined by you.

</div>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}<b>としては</b>、この{f|条件|じょうけん}で{f|進|すす}めたいと{f|思|おも}っています。
Speaking for myself, I'd like to go ahead on these terms.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|先方|せんぽう}<b>にしたら</b>、この{f|条件|じょうけん}は{f|厳|きび}しすぎるかもしれません。
For the other side, these terms may well be too harsh.
</pre>

The first is a stated position you are responsible for; the second is a supposition
about someone whose mind you cannot see, which is why it ends in かもしれません. Put
としては on the second and you would be claiming to speak for them.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|制度|せいど}<b>にしたら</b>{f|問題|もんだい}がある。 → ✅ この{f|制度|せいど}<b>には</b>{f|問題|もんだい}がある。
  A system has no point of view. <code>にしたら</code> needs an animate one.

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>にしたら</b>{f|必要|ひつよう}な{f|本|ほん}だ。 → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>にとって</b>{f|必要|ひつよう}な{f|本|ほん}だ。
  A bare evaluation belongs to <code>にとって</code>.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}<b>にしたら</b>{f|残業|ざんぎょう}が{f|嫌|いや}だと{f|言|い}った。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}<b>にしたら</b>{f|残業|ざんぎょう}は{f|嫌|いや}なのだろう。
  The pattern supposes; it does not report speech. If he said it, just quote him.

- ❌ {f|子|こ}ども<b>をにしたら</b> → ✅ {f|子|こ}ども<b>にしたら</b>
  The person attaches directly. Adding `を` produces the unrelated "turn A into B"
  construction.

## Practice

**1.** {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|世話|せわ}をしている{f|飼|か}い{f|主|ぬし}____、ペットは{f|家族|かぞく}の{f|一員|いちいん}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|飼|か}い{f|主|ぬし}<b>にしたら</b>、ペットは{f|家族|かぞく}の{f|一員|いちいん}だ。

The owner is a person whose perspective the sentence is adopting, and the main
clause states what the situation amounts to in their eyes rather than rating it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|数字|すうじ}（にしたら・からすると）、{f|来年|らいねん}の{f|採用|さいよう}は{f|難|むずか}しい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|数字|すうじ}<b>からすると</b>、{f|来年|らいねん}の{f|採用|さいよう}は{f|難|むずか}しい。

Figures are evidence, not a party with a viewpoint. Note that
{f|会社|かいしゃ}にしたら would be fine - it is the {f|数字|すうじ} that blocks
<code>にしたら</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "It's only a five-minute walk for me, but for my grandmother it's a long way."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|私|わたし}には{f|歩|ある}いて{f|五分|ごふん}だが、{f|祖母|そぼ}<b>にしてみれば</b>{f|遠|とお}い{f|道|みち}のりだ。

The English "but for my grandmother" is exactly the contrastive shape this pattern
lives in: the speaker's own view first, then the other person's, marked with
<code>にしてみれば</code>.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|新|あたら}しいルールは{f|安全|あんぜん}にしたら{f|必要|ひつよう}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|新|あたら}しいルールは{f|安全|あんぜん}<b>のために</b>{f|必要|ひつよう}だ。
（or {f|安全面|あんぜんめん}から{f|言|い}えば{f|必要|ひつよう}だ）

{f|安全|あんぜん} is neither a person nor a viewpoint holder, so
<code>にしたら</code> cannot attach to it. Safety here is a purpose or a criterion,
and Japanese has other machinery for both.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|留学生|りゅうがくせい}<b>にとって</b>、{f|漢字|かんじ}は{f|大|おお}きな{f|壁|かべ}だ。
(b) {f|留学生|りゅうがくせい}<b>にしてみれば</b>、{f|漢字|かんじ}をやめてほしいくらいだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is an assessment stated from outside: kanji constitute a major obstacle for
that group. It is the kind of sentence a textbook or a report would carry, and the
speaker need not have any feeling about it.

(b) steps inside. It attributes a wish to the students themselves - they would
sooner kanji did not exist - and the だろう admits the speaker is imagining rather
than quoting. It asks the listener to sympathise, which (a) does not.

</details>
