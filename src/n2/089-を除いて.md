# ～を除いて

**Meaning** · except; with the exception of - everything in this set but that one

<code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code> is "to remove, to take out", so
<code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code> subtracts. It presupposes a general claim -
*everyone*, *every day*, *all of them* - and cuts one named member out of it. That
presupposition is what separates it from everything else in this group: nothing is
being postponed and nothing is being called irrelevant, it is simply the one case
where the rule does not hold.

## Formation

<code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code> is a transitive う-verb ({f|五段|ごだん} / Group 1), so the thing
removed takes `を` and the phrase conjugates normally.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + を{f|除|のぞ}いて | {f|日曜日|にちようび} → {f|日曜日|にちようび}を{f|除|のぞ}いて |
| Noun (written / formal) | Noun + を{f|除|のぞ}き | {f|一部|いちぶ} → {f|一部|いちぶ}を{f|除|のぞ}き |
| Noun (with topic は) | Noun + を{f|除|のぞ}いては | {f|彼|かれ} → {f|彼|かれ}を{f|除|のぞ}いては |
| Noun (conditional flavour) | Noun + を{f|除|のぞ}けば | ラスト → ラストを{f|除|のぞ}けば |
| Modifying a noun | Noun + を{f|除|のぞ}く／{f|除|のぞ}いた + Noun | {f|税|ぜい} → {f|税|ぜい}を{f|除|のぞ}いた{f|金額|きんがく} |
| Situation (nominalised) | clause + {f|場合|ばあい}・とき + を{f|除|のぞ}いて | {f|緊急|きんきゅう}の{f|場合|ばあい}を{f|除|のぞ}いて |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the base is <code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code>, so the furigana sits on the single
kanji with く outside. Do not confuse it with <code>{f|覗|のぞ}く</code>, "to peek",
which is a different character with the same reading.

</div>

<pre>
{f|月曜日|げつようび}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}があります。
There's practice every day except Monday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|賛成|さんせい}した。
Everyone but one person was in favour.
</pre>

<pre>

It should be fine tomorrow, apart from in some areas.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|年末年始|ねんまつねんし}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、{f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|午前|ごぜん}９{f|時|じ}から{f|午後|ごご}５{f|時|じ}までです。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|緊急|きんきゅう}の{f|場合|ばあい}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、この{f|扉|とびら}は{f|使用|しよう}できません。
This door is not to be used except in an emergency.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

There's nobody I could hand this job to apart from him.
</pre>

<pre>

Please note that the prices shown do not include tax.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code> is a shade formal. In everyday speech Japanese reaches
for <code>{f|以外|いがい}</code> far more often -
<code>{f|日曜|にちよう}{f|以外|いがい}は{f|毎日|まいにち}やってる</code> - and keeps
<code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code> for writing, announcements, contracts and anywhere a
precise carve-out is being stated. It is not stiff enough to sound wrong in
conversation, but it is the register of the timetable rather than the chat about
the timetable.

The four endings shade differently. <code>を{f|除|のぞ}き</code> is the written
listing form and appears in notices. <code>を{f|除|のぞ}いては</code> puts a topic
marker on the exception, which foregrounds it, and it pairs naturally with a
negative main clause: <code>{f|彼|かれ}を{f|除|のぞ}いては、だれも{f|来|こ}なかった</code>.
<code>を{f|除|のぞ}けば</code> adds a hypothetical tint - "if you leave that out" -
and is the natural form for a review or an assessment, because it invites the
listener to imagine the thing without its flaw. Plain
<code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code> is the neutral default.

<code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code> is also a live verb outside this pattern -
<code>{f|不安|ふあん}を{f|取|と}り{f|除|のぞ}く</code>, "to remove an anxiety" - and it
survives in one piece of signage worth recognising on sight: the parenthesis
<code>（{f|土日|どにち}を{f|除|のぞ}く）</code> printed under a set of opening hours,
using the bare dictionary form rather than the て-form.

## Contrast

### ～を{f|除|のぞ}いて vs ～{f|以外|いがい}（は）

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same job, different part of speech.
<code>{f|以外|いがい}</code> is a **noun** and takes particles;
<code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code> is a **verb phrase** and modifies the clause.

</div>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}<b>{f|以外|いがい}</b>はみんな{f|知|し}っていた。
Everyone except me knew.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、みんな{f|知|し}っていた。
Everyone knew, apart from me.
</pre>

The meanings match. What differs is flexibility and register.
<code>{f|以外|いがい}</code> attaches straight to the noun with no particle in
between and then takes whatever particle the sentence needs -
<code>{f|以外|いがい}は</code>, <code>{f|以外|いがい}に</code>,
<code>{f|以外|いがい}の{f|人|ひと}</code> - which is why it dominates in speech.
<code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code> cannot become a noun modifier without changing form to
<code>を{f|除|のぞ}く</code> or <code>を{f|除|のぞ}いた</code>. When in doubt in
conversation, use <code>{f|以外|いがい}</code>; in a notice, use
<code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code>.

### ～を{f|除|のぞ}いて vs ～だけ・～しか～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code> describes the **majority** and names
the gap. `だけ` and `しか～ない` describe the **remainder**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、みんな{f|反対|はんたい}だった。
Apart from him, everyone was against it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|賛成|さんせい}したのは{f|彼|かれ}<b>だけ</b>だった。
He was the only one in favour.
</pre>

Identical facts, opposite camera angles. The first keeps the group in frame and
mentions the hole in it; the second points at the one person left. Choose by asking
which side of the line your sentence is about. A related consequence: because
<code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code> needs the general claim to exist, it is odd in a sentence
where there is no group -
❌<code>{f|彼|かれ}を{f|除|のぞ}いて{f|二人|ふたり}{f|来|き}た</code> gives the listener
nothing to subtract from.

### ～を{f|除|のぞ}いて vs ～は{f|別|べつ}として

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code> takes a member **out of a set**;
<code>{f|別|べつ}として</code> takes a topic **off the agenda**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新入生|しんにゅうせい}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|試験|しけん}を{f|受|う}けます。
All the students sit the exam except the first-years.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新入生|しんにゅうせい}<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、{f|試験|しけん}の{f|日程|にってい}を{f|決|き}めましょう。
Leaving the first-years aside, let's fix the exam dates.
</pre>

In the first, the first-years are definitively not sitting the exam - a fact about
the world. In the second nothing has been decided about them at all; they are merely
not what this meeting is handling. Learners who reach for
<code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code> when they mean "let's not discuss that now" end up
announcing a decision they never made, which in a work context is an expensive
mistake.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b> → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}の{f|日|ひ}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>（or {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る{f|場合|ばあい}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>）
  <code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code> is transitive and needs a noun as its object. To exclude
  a situation, package it as <code>{f|場合|ばあい}</code>,
  <code>とき</code> or <code>{f|日|ひ}</code> first.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}<b>は{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、みんな{f|行|い}った。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、みんな{f|行|い}った。
  The particle on the excluded item is `を`. `は` only appears *after* the verb, in
  <code>を{f|除|のぞ}いては</code>.

- ❌ {f|税|ぜい}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>{f|金額|きんがく}をご{f|確認|かくにん}ください。 → ✅ {f|税|ぜい}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いた</b>{f|金額|きんがく}をご{f|確認|かくにん}ください。
  To modify a noun you need the plain form
  <code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code> or the past
  <code>{f|除|のぞ}いた</code>. The て-form modifies the verb, not the noun.

- ❌ {f|経験|けいけん}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>{f|採用|さいよう}します。 → ✅ {f|経験|けいけん}<b>を{f|問|と}わず</b>{f|採用|さいよう}します。
  Experience is a criterion, not a member of the group being hired. Criteria are
  waived with <code>{f|問|と}う</code>; people and days are removed with
  <code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|一部|いちぶ}の{f|商品|しょうひん}____、{f|店内|てんない}すべて２{f|割引|わりびき}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一部|いちぶ}の{f|商品|しょうひん}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、{f|店内|てんない}すべて２{f|割引|わりびき}です。

すべて sets up a claim about the whole shop, and a named group of items is being cut
out of it - textbook subtraction. <code>を{f|除|のぞ}き</code> would be equally
correct and slightly more formal, which suits a printed sign.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|会議|かいぎ}には{f|部長|ぶちょう}（を{f|除|のぞ}いて・は{f|別|べつ}として）{f|全員|ぜんいん}{f|出席|しゅっせき}しました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>{f|全員|ぜんいん}{f|出席|しゅっせき}しました。

{f|全員|ぜんいん} makes this a statement about a set, and the department head is a
member who was not there. <code>は{f|別|べつ}として</code> would be about what the
speaker is choosing to discuss, which does not fit a factual report of who attended.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Apart from the price, there was nothing I disliked about this flat."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|値段|ねだん}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}けば</b>、この{f|部屋|へや}に{f|気|き}に{f|入|い}らないところはなかった。

<code>を{f|除|のぞ}けば</code> is the reviewer's form: it invites the listener to
imagine the flat without that one flaw. <code>{f|値段|ねだん}{f|以外|いがい}に</code>
would be the plainer spoken equivalent.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|一部|いちぶ}の{f|地域|ちいき}{f|以外|いがい}を{f|除|のぞ}いて、{f|全国|ぜんこく}どこへでも{f|配達|はいたつ}できます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|一部|いちぶ}の{f|地域|ちいき}を{f|除|のぞ}いて、{f|全国|ぜんこく}どこへでも{f|配達|はいたつ}できます。
or ✅ {f|一部|いちぶ}の{f|地域|ちいき}{f|以外|いがい}は、{f|全国|ぜんこく}どこへでも{f|配達|はいたつ}できます。

<code>{f|以外|いがい}</code> and <code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code> do the same job, so
stacking them subtracts twice - taken literally, the original says delivery is
available only to the areas that were meant to be excluded. Use the noun
<code>{f|以外|いがい}</code> with `は`, or the verb phrase on its own, but not both.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、みんな{f|知|し}っていた。
(b) {f|知|し}らなかったのは{f|彼|かれ}<b>だけ</b>だった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both report the same state of affairs, and either could translate the other. The
difference is where the listener's attention lands.

(a) is a statement about the group: everybody was in the know, with one hole in the
picture. It is the neutral way to report the situation, and it does not by itself
comment on him.

(b) puts the spotlight on him and, because `だけ` isolates, it invites a judgement -
he was the odd one out, and depending on tone that is either pitying or pointed. If
you are telling someone gently that they were left out, (a) is the kinder sentence.

</details>
