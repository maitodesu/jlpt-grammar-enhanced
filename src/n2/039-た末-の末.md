# ～た末 / ～の末

**Meaning** · after a long process of effort or deliberation, this is where it came out

<code>{f|末|すえ}</code> is the far end of something that took a while: months of
thinking, hours of negotiation, years of struggle. The sentence presents the
outcome as the **conclusion that process arrived at**. Unlike its close cousin
<code>あげく</code>, it passes no negative verdict - the effort is treated as having
been real work.

## Formation

<code>{f|末|すえ}</code> is a noun, so it takes a た-form clause or a noun with `の`.
The following `に` is optional and very common.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (た-form) | た-form + {f|末|すえ}（に） | {f|考|かんが}える → {f|考|かんが}えた{f|末|すえ}に |
| Verb (た-form) | た-form + {f|末|すえ} | {f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}う → {f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}った{f|末|すえ} |
| Noun | Noun + の + {f|末|すえ}（に） | {f|苦労|くろう} → {f|苦労|くろう}の{f|末|すえ} |
| Modifying a noun | …{f|末|すえ}の + Noun | {f|努力|どりょく} → {f|努力|どりょく}の{f|末|すえ}の{f|成功|せいこう} |
| ❌ Dictionary form | ❌{f|考|かんが}える{f|末|すえ} | ✅{f|考|かんが}えた{f|末|すえ} |
| ❌ Noun with no の | ❌{f|苦労|くろう}{f|末|すえ} | ✅{f|苦労|くろう}の{f|末|すえ} |
| ❌ A momentary process | ❌ちょっと{f|見|み}た{f|末|すえ}に | ✅{f|何度|なんど}も{f|見|み}{f|比|くら}べた{f|末|すえ}に |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the clause in front must describe something **sustained and
purposeful**: thinking, discussing, searching, struggling. The main clause reports
what it finally produced, and normally reports it in the **past**.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the reading here is <code>すえ</code>. The same character is
<code>まつ</code> in {f|週末|しゅうまつ}, {f|月末|げつまつ} and {f|年末|ねんまつ},
where it means "the end of a period" rather than "the far end of a process". Only
<code>すえ</code> works in this grammar point.

</div>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

After a hard struggle she got a shop of her own.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|激|はげ}しい{f|議論|ぎろん}の<b>{f|末|すえ}</b>、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|案|あん}が{f|採用|さいよう}された。
After a fierce debate the new proposal was adopted.
</pre>

It's a qualification I got through years of effort, so I want to look after it.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>{f|末|すえ}</code> leans **formal and written**. Newspapers use it constantly -
<code>{f|協議|きょうぎ}の{f|末|すえ}、{f|合意|ごうい}に{f|達|たっ}した</code> is close
to boilerplate - and it turns up in company announcements, court reports and
biography. In speech it is used, but it sounds considered rather than chatty; a
friend describing a shopping trip would more likely say
<code>さんざん{f|迷|まよ}って</code>.

The tone is one of respect for the process. Even when the outcome is unwelcome, the
sentence treats the deliberation as having been serious:
<code>{f|検討|けんとう}の{f|末|すえ}、{f|今回|こんかい}は{f|見送|みおく}ることになりました</code>
is a polite refusal precisely because <code>{f|末|すえ}</code> tells the reader you
did not decide lightly. That makes it genuinely useful in business writing.

Two shapes are worth taking whole. <code>…の{f|末|すえ}の…</code> compresses the
whole story into a noun phrase - <code>{f|苦労|くろう}の{f|末|すえ}の{f|成功|せいこう}</code>,
"success won the hard way". And <code>{f|末|すえ}に</code> attracts
やっと, ようやく and ついに in the main clause, which is a reliable signal that you
have picked the right pattern.

## Contrast

### ～た{f|末|すえ} vs ～たあげく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same structure, opposite verdict.
<code>{f|末|すえ}</code> respects the process; <code>あげく</code> says it was
wasted.

</div>

<pre>
{f|一年|いちねん}{f|準備|じゅんび}をした<b>{f|末|すえ}に</b>、{f|店|みせ}を{f|開|ひら}いた。
After a year of preparation, I opened the shop.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一年|いちねん}{f|準備|じゅんび}をした<b>あげく</b>、{f|開店|かいてん}をあきらめた。
After a year of preparation, I ended up giving up on opening at all.
</pre>

The first outcome justifies the year; the second wipes it out.
<code>あげく</code> also insists the outcome be disagreeable, while
<code>{f|末|すえ}</code> is free to be good, bad or simply final - a decision to
postpone is perfectly comfortable with <code>{f|末|すえ}</code>.

### ～た{f|末|すえ} vs ～た{f|結果|けっか}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|結果|けっか}</code> reports **cause and effect** and does
not care how long it took. <code>{f|末|すえ}</code> insists the process was
**long**, and puts the weight on the process rather than the causal link.

</div>

<code>た{f|結果|けっか}</code> is the N3 pattern <code>{f|末|すえ}</code> is closest
to, and this is where they part.

<pre>
アンケートを{f|集計|しゅうけい}し<b>た{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|意外|いがい}な{f|傾向|けいこう}が{f|見|み}えてきた。
Tallying the questionnaires revealed an unexpected trend.
</pre>

Nobody suffered for that; the counting simply produced a finding, and it might have
taken an afternoon. Swap in <code>{f|末|すえ}に</code> and you imply weeks of
grinding work.

<pre>
{f|三年|さんねん}{f|争|あらそ}った<b>{f|末|すえ}に</b>、{f|両者|りょうしゃ}は{f|和解|わかい}した。
After three years of dispute, the two sides settled.
</pre>

Here the three years are the point. <code>{f|結果|けっか}</code> would flatten them
into a mere cause.

### ～た{f|末|すえ} vs ～た{f|上|うえ}で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|末|すえ}</code> looks **back** at a long process that is
over. <code>{f|上|うえ}で</code> looks **forward**: first do X, and only then do Y.

</div>

<pre>
よく{f|検討|けんとう}し<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>、ご{f|返事|へんじ}いたします。
I'll give you my answer after due consideration.
</pre>

That is a promise about what is still to come, and
<code>{f|末|すえ}</code> cannot take its place - there is no completed process to
look back on yet.

<pre>
よく{f|検討|けんとう}し<b>た{f|末|すえ}に</b>、お{f|断|ことわ}りすることにしました。
After careful consideration, we have decided to decline.
</pre>

Now the thinking is finished and the sentence reports where it landed.
<code>{f|上|うえ}で</code> also carries a sense of *necessary preliminary* - you
cannot properly answer without considering - which <code>{f|末|すえ}</code> does not.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|考|かんが}える<b>{f|末|すえ}に</b>{f|決|き}めました。 → ✅ {f|考|かんが}えた<b>{f|末|すえ}に</b>{f|決|き}めました。
  The process is over by the time you report it, so the verb in front is `た`.

- ❌ {f|苦労|くろう}<b>{f|末|すえ}</b>、{f|夢|ゆめ}をかなえた。 → ✅ {f|苦労|くろう}<b>の{f|末|すえ}</b>、{f|夢|ゆめ}をかなえた。
  <code>{f|末|すえ}</code> is a noun and a noun in front of it needs `の`.

- ❌ ずっと{f|迷|まよ}った<b>{f|末|すえ}</b>、{f|時間|じかん}を{f|無駄|むだ}にしただけだった。 → ✅ ずっと{f|迷|まよ}った<b>あげく</b>、{f|時間|じかん}を{f|無駄|むだ}にしただけだった。
  If the whole point is that the effort came to nothing, the pattern you want is
  <code>あげく</code>. <code>{f|末|すえ}</code> would be quietly at odds with the
  main clause.

- ❌ ちょっと{f|迷|まよ}った<b>{f|末|すえ}に</b>、コーヒーにした。 → ✅ {f|少|すこ}し{f|迷|まよ}って、コーヒーにした。
  Ten seconds at a counter is not a process.
  <code>{f|末|すえ}</code> needs something substantial in front of it or it sounds
  like a joke.

## Practice

**1.** {f|何|なん}{f|時間|じかん}も{f|交渉|こうしょう}した____、ようやく{f|条件|じょうけん}がまとまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|交渉|こうしょう}した<b>{f|末|すえ}に</b>

Hours of negotiation is a long, purposeful process, and ようやく in the main clause
is the marker that goes with <code>{f|末|すえ}</code>. The verb in front takes `た`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|一日中|いちにちじゅう}{f|店|みせ}を{f|回|まわ}った（{f|末|すえ}に・あげく）、{f|何|なに}も{f|買|か}わずに{f|帰|かえ}ってきた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一日中|いちにちじゅう}{f|店|みせ}を{f|回|まわ}った<b>あげく</b>、{f|何|なに}も{f|買|か}わずに{f|帰|かえ}ってきた。

A whole day spent and nothing to show for it is a wasted effort, which is
<code>あげく</code>'s territory. <code>{f|末|すえ}</code> would suggest the day had
been well spent.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "After ten years of research, the team finally succeeded in growing the plant."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|十年|じゅうねん}にわたる{f|研究|けんきゅう}の<b>{f|末|すえ}</b>、チームはついにその{f|植物|しょくぶつ}を{f|育|そだ}てることに{f|成功|せいこう}した。

A noun phrase plus `の` plus <code>{f|末|すえ}</code>, and ついに in the main clause.
The sustained, purposeful effort is exactly what this pattern honours.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|長|なが}い{f|議論|ぎろん}{f|末|すえ}に、{f|来週|らいしゅう}もう{f|一度|いちど}{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|長|なが}い{f|議論|ぎろん}<b>の{f|末|すえ}</b>に、もう{f|一度|いちど}{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}うことになりました。

Two faults. The noun {f|議論|ぎろん} needs `の`; and the main clause should report
what the process produced, not announce a future action. Recasting it as
<code>ことになりました</code> makes the decision the outcome.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|検討|けんとう}し<b>た{f|上|うえ}で</b>、ご{f|連絡|れんらく}します。
(b) {f|検討|けんとう}し<b>た{f|末|すえ}に</b>、ご{f|連絡|れんらく}しました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a commitment about the future: the considering has not happened yet, and the
sentence promises that it will happen first, before any answer comes.

(b) looks back on considering that is finished, presents it as having taken real
time, and reports the contact as its conclusion. In business writing (a) buys you
time; (b) explains why your answer took so long.

</details>
