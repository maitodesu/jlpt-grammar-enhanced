# ～はさておき

**Meaning** · setting that aside; never mind that for now - here is what we should be talking about

<code>さておく</code> is <code>さて</code> plus <code>{f|置|お}く</code>: *to put it
over there*. `はさておき` is therefore less a statement about the world than a move
in a conversation. The speaker picks up a topic, sets it down out of the way, and
takes the floor for the thing they think should come next. Where `ともかく` weighs
and <code>{f|別|べつ}として</code> brackets, `さておき` **steers**.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + はさておき | {f|冗談|じょうだん} → {f|冗談|じょうだん}はさておき |
| Noun (て-form variant) | Noun + はさておいて | {f|条件|じょうけん} → {f|条件|じょうけん}はさておいて |
| Yes/no question | plain form + かどうか + はさておき | できる → できるかどうかはさておき |
| Embedded question | question word … か + はさておき | どちらが{f|正|ただ}しいか → どちらが{f|正|ただ}しいかはさておき |
| Fixed opener | それはさておき | - |
| Casual equivalent | ～は{f|置|お}いといて | その{f|話|はなし}は{f|置|お}いといて |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|何|なに}はさておき</code> is **not** this pattern with
{f|何|なに} in the slot. It is a fixed adverb meaning "before anything else":
<code>{f|何|なに}はさておき、まず{f|手|て}を{f|洗|あら}おう</code>. It promotes rather
than postpones, and mixing the two up reverses your sentence.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`はさておき` is a chairing move. Saying it claims, briefly, the right to decide what
the group talks about next, and that is why it turns up so often from the person
running the meeting, the writer organising an essay, or the speaker managing a
presentation. It is polite in form - nobody will take offence at the words - but it
is not modest, and a junior person using it to shelve a senior colleague's point can
land more bluntly than intended. Softer alternatives exist for that case:
<code>{f|後|あと}ほど{f|伺|うかが}うことにして</code>, or simply asking.

Between friends the same move is made with
<code>それは{f|置|お}いといて</code> - literally the same verb, contracted and
relaxed. <code>まあそれはいいとして</code> does the job too. `さておき` in a casual
chat sounds like you are hosting a radio programme, which is occasionally funny and
usually a shade formal.

Two set uses are worth learning whole.
<code>{f|冗談|じょうだん}はさておき</code> is the standard hinge after a light opening
remark, and Japanese speeches use it constantly to pivot from warm-up to substance. <code>それはさておき</code> stands alone as a paragraph opener in writing
and does the work of English "anyway".

Unlike `ともかく`, `さておき` passes no judgement on what it parks. You can set aside
something excellent - <code>{f|結果|けっか}はさておき</code> above is about a runner
who did well - because the sentence is about the agenda, not about quality.

## Contrast

### ～はさておき vs ～はともかく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `さておき` moves the **conversation** on; `ともかく` ranks the
**content** and quietly marks the parked item as the weaker one.

</div>

<pre>
{f|順位|じゅんい}<b>はさておき</b>、まず{f|完走|かんそう}できたことを{f|喜|よろこ}ぼう。
Never mind the placing - let's be glad she finished at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|順位|じゅんい}<b>はともかく</b>、{f|走|はし}り{f|方|かた}はきれいだった。
The placing was what it was, but her running form was lovely.
</pre>

The first is about what to discuss now, and it implies nothing about the result. The
second is a verdict: the placing was disappointing. If you are complimenting
somebody and do not want a backhanded reading, `さておき` is the safer of the two.

There is also a difference in what follows. `さておき` is nearly always followed by a
proposal - <code>ましょう</code>, <code>ませんか</code>, an imperative, a question.
`ともかく` is followed by an assessment.

### ～はさておき vs ～は{f|別|べつ}として

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|別|べつ}として</code> is **analytical**: it isolates a
variable. `さておき` is **conversational**: it manages the floor.

</div>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}の{f|問題|もんだい}<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、この{f|方法|ほうほう}が{f|一番|いちばん}{f|確実|かくじつ}だ。
Leaving the question of time aside, this method is the most reliable.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}の{f|問題|もんだい}<b>はさておき</b>、まずどの{f|方法|ほうほう}にするか{f|決|き}めましょう。
Never mind the timing for now - let's decide on a method first.
</pre>

Both are neutral about the parked item, and in many sentences either would do. The
first is a piece of reasoning and could sit in a written report; the second is
addressed to people in a room and expects them to do something. A reliable tell: if
the sentence continues with <code>ましょう</code> or a question, you are steering,
and `さておき` fits best.

### ～はさておき vs ところで・それはそうと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `さておき` **drops a named topic and returns to the agenda**.
`ところで` **abandons the agenda for something new**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|冗談|じょうだん}<b>はさておき</b>、{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|予定|よてい}を{f|決|き}めましょう。
Joking aside, let's fix next week's schedule.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>ところで</b>、{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|予定|よてい}はもう{f|決|き}まりましたか。
By the way, has next week's schedule been settled?
</pre>

Grammatically the difference is that `さておき` needs something in front of it to
name what is being put down, while `ところで` and
<code>それはそうと</code> stand alone. Socially the difference is direction of
travel. `さておき` signals a return to what we were supposed to be doing, so it reads
as responsible. `ところで` signals a swerve to a new subject, so it reads as a change
of mood - and in a formal meeting it can sound like you have stopped listening.
<code>それはそうと</code> is the warmest of the three and belongs in conversation
rather than on a page.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|結果|けっか}<b>をさておき</b> → ✅ {f|結果|けっか}<b>はさておき</b>
  The particle is `は`. It is the topic marker doing the setting-aside, and `を`
  makes the phrase ungrammatical rather than merely odd.

- ❌ {f|勝|か}てる<b>はさておき</b>、{f|練習|れんしゅう}しよう。 → ✅ {f|勝|か}てるかどうか<b>はさておき</b>、{f|練習|れんしゅう}しよう。
  A verb clause has to be nominalised with `かどうか` (or a question word plus `か`)
  before `は` can attach.

- ❌ {f|何|なに}<b>はさておき</b>、この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|後|あと}で{f|考|かんが}えよう。 → ✅ {f|何|なに}<b>はさておき</b>、まずこの{f|問題|もんだい}から{f|片|かた}づけよう。
  <code>{f|何|なに}はさておき</code> means "before anything else". Using it to
  postpone something says the exact opposite of what you intend.

- ❌ {f|課長|かちょう}のお{f|話|はなし}<b>はさておき</b>、{f|次|つぎ}に{f|進|すす}みます。 → ✅ {f|課長|かちょう}のお{f|話|はなし}については{f|後|あと}ほど{f|伺|うかが}うことにして、{f|次|つぎ}に{f|進|すす}みます。
  Grammatical, but it shelves a superior's contribution with a wave of the hand.
  The pattern is a floor-taking move, so mind who you use it on.

## Practice

**1.** その{f|件|けん}____、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|来年|らいねん}の{f|計画|けいかく}について{f|話|はな}しましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

その{f|件|けん}<b>はさておき</b>、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|来年|らいねん}の{f|計画|けいかく}について{f|話|はな}しましょう。

A named topic is put down and the meeting is steered back to its agenda, and the
sentence ends in a proposal - the clearest sign that the speaker is steering rather
than assessing. A bare noun takes the pattern directly.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|味|あじ}（はさておき・はともかく）、この{f|店|みせ}の{f|雰囲気|ふんいき}は{f|最高|さいこう}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are grammatical, and they say different things.

<b>はともかく</b> is what a speaker would normally choose here, and it tells the
listener the food is not much good. <b>はさておき</b> leaves the food entirely
unjudged and simply announces that atmosphere is the topic - but because the
sentence goes on to an assessment rather than a proposal, it reads a little oddly,
as if the speaker were chairing a meeting about a restaurant.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Whose fault it was can wait - let's fix the problem first."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

だれの{f|責任|せきにん}か<b>はさておき</b>、まず{f|問題|もんだい}を{f|解決|かいけつ}しましょう。

An embedded question with a question word takes plain `か`, not `かどうか`. The
`ましょう` ending is what makes `さておき` the natural choice over
<code>は{f|別|べつ}として</code> here.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|何|なに}はさておき、その{f|件|けん}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ その{f|件|けん}<b>はさておき</b>、{f|来週|らいしゅう}までに{f|決|き}めるべきことを{f|先|さき}に{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いましょう。
or ✅ {f|何|なに}はさておき、その{f|件|けん}から{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}いましょう。

<code>{f|何|なに}はさておき</code> means "before anything else", so the original
sentence promises to deal with the matter immediately and then postpones it by a
week. Either park the specific topic, or keep {f|何|なに}はさておき and put the topic
first.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|細|こま}かい{f|話|はなし}<b>はさておき</b>、{f|全体|ぜんたい}の{f|方針|ほうしん}を{f|決|き}めましょう。
(b) {f|細|こま}かい{f|話|はなし}<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、{f|全体|ぜんたい}の{f|方針|ほうしん}を{f|決|き}めましょう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

They are close, and no listener would object to either. (a) is the more physical
gesture: the speaker is pushing the details off the table so the group can get on.
It carries a faint sense of impatience with the detail, and it is the sentence a
chair says when the meeting is running long.

(b) is cooler. It treats the details as a genuinely separate item that will be
handled in its own right, and it promises nothing about when. In a written agenda
(b) reads better; spoken in a room, (a) is what moves people along.

</details>
