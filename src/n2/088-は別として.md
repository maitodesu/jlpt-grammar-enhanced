# ～は別として

**Meaning** · leaving A out of consideration; A is a separate question - now, about B

<code>{f|別|べつ}</code> is "separate", and <code>は{f|別|べつ}として</code> does
exactly what it says: it takes one item out of the current discussion and treats it
as a different matter, so that the rest can be judged cleanly. It is the neutral
member of this family. Unlike `ともかく` it passes no verdict on what it removes -
the item may be excellent, terrible or simply undecided.

## Formation

The parked item is a noun or a nominalised question, marked with `は`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + は{f|別|べつ}として | {f|費用|ひよう} → {f|費用|ひよう}は{f|別|べつ}として |
| Noun (spoken variant) | Noun + は{f|別|べつ}にして | {f|冗談|じょうだん} → {f|冗談|じょうだん}は{f|別|べつ}にして |
| Yes/no question | plain form + かどうか + は{f|別|べつ}として | できる → できるかどうかは{f|別|べつ}として |
| Embedded question | question word … か + は{f|別|べつ}として | だれが{f|悪|わる}いか → だれが{f|悪|わる}いかは{f|別|べつ}として |
| Marked with を | Noun + を{f|別|べつ}として | {f|一部|いちぶ}の{f|例|れい} → {f|一部|いちぶ}の{f|例|れい}を{f|別|べつ}として |
| As a predicate | Noun + は{f|別|べつ}だ | {f|今回|こんかい} → {f|今回|こんかい}は{f|別|べつ}だ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>は{f|別|べつ}にして</code> is the same pattern in a slightly more
spoken jacket; the two are interchangeable in almost every sentence.
<code>は{f|別|べつ}として</code> is the safer default in writing.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|理由|りゆう}<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、{f|結果|けっか}だけを{f|見|み}よう。
Reasons aside, let's look only at the outcome.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|好|す}き{f|嫌|きら}い<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、まず{f|一口|ひとくち}{f|食|た}べてみてください。
Whether you like it or not is a separate question - just try one bite first.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|可能|かのう}かどうか<b>は{f|別|べつ}にして</b>、アイデアを{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|書|か}き{f|出|だ}してみよう。
Never mind for now whether it's feasible - let's get every idea down on paper.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|費用|ひよう}の{f|問題|もんだい}<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、この{f|計画|けいかく}{f|自体|じたい}には{f|賛成|さんせい}です。
Leaving the question of cost to one side, I'm in favour of the plan itself.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|発音|はつおん}<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、{f|漢字|かんじ}の{f|読|よ}み{f|方|かた}だけは{f|自信|じしん}がある。
My pronunciation is a separate matter, but I'm confident about kanji readings at least.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|例外|れいがい}<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、まず{f|基本|きほん}のルールから{f|覚|おぼ}えましょう。
Exceptions are a different story - start by learning the basic rules.
</pre>

<pre>

Whether or not he was right is another question; speaking up in that room took courage.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|勝|か}ち{f|負|ま}け<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、{f|最後|さいご}まで{f|全力|ぜんりょく}で{f|戦|たたか}った{f|選手|せんしゅ}たちに{f|拍手|はくしゅ}を{f|送|おく}りたい。
Win or lose, I want to applaud the players who gave everything to the final whistle.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and safe almost everywhere. It appears in casual speech, in
meetings, and in written argument, and nothing about it is stiff or soft. If you
learn only one member of this family for productive use, this is the one, because
it is the hardest to offend anyone with.

Its real value is analytical. <code>は{f|別|べつ}として</code> is how you isolate a
variable so the discussion can proceed: you are not dismissing the item, you are
saying *not now*. That implicit promise to return to it is why the pattern is so
common in planning and evaluation - <code>{f|予算|よさん}は{f|別|べつ}として</code>
means the budget will be dealt with, just not in this breath. If you genuinely never
intend to consider the thing, `にかかわらず` says so honestly.

Because it is neutral, it is also the polite choice for talking about people. When
you must praise one aspect of someone's work and not another,
<code>{f|文章|ぶんしょう}の{f|長|なが}さは{f|別|べつ}として</code> lets you do it
without the audible sigh that `ともかく` carries.

Two idioms are worth having. <code>それは{f|別|べつ}として</code> is a compact way to
change tack mid-conversation, and <code>{f|話|はなし}は{f|別|べつ}だ</code> - "that's
a different matter" - is what you say when someone's new information changes your
position: <code>{f|本人|ほんにん}が{f|希望|きぼう}しているなら{f|話|はなし}は{f|別|べつ}だ</code>.

## Contrast

### ～は{f|別|べつ}として vs ～はともかく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both park an item. `ともかく` **judges** it on the way past;
<code>{f|別|べつ}として</code> **does not**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|文字|もじ}の{f|大|おお}きさ<b>はともかく</b>、この{f|資料|しりょう}はよくできている。
Font size aside, this document is well put together.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|文字|もじ}の{f|大|おお}きさ<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、この{f|資料|しりょう}はよくできている。
Leaving the font size as a separate question, this document is well put together.
</pre>

Read the first to the person who made the document and they will go home and change
the font: `ともかく` marks the parked item as the flaw. Read the second and they
will hear only that the layout is a different discussion. The English translations
are nearly identical; the social effect is not.

### ～は{f|別|べつ}として vs ～を{f|除|のぞ}いて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|別|べつ}として</code> removes a **consideration**;
<code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code> removes a **member of a set**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|土日|どにち}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|営業|えいぎょう}しています。
We're open every day except Saturday and Sunday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|土日|どにち}<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、{f|平日|へいじつ}の{f|営業|えいぎょう}{f|時間|じかん}を{f|決|き}めましょう。
Weekends are a separate question - let's settle the weekday hours.
</pre>

The test is whether the sentence after it makes a claim about a *set*. If it does -
"every day", "everyone", "all the members" - and the parked item would have been
inside that set, you need <code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code>. If instead the sentence is
choosing what to talk about, <code>{f|別|べつ}として</code>. A useful symptom: an
answer that could be written as a percentage or a headcount belongs to
<code>{f|除|のぞ}く</code>.

### ～は{f|別|べつ}として vs ～にかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|別|べつ}として</code> **postpones**; `にかかわらず`
**dismisses for good**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|経験|けいけん}<b>にかかわらず</b>、{f|給料|きゅうりょう}は{f|同|おな}じです。
Pay is the same regardless of experience.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|経験|けいけん}<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、まず{f|人柄|ひとがら}を{f|見|み}たい。
Experience is a separate matter - first I want to see what they're like as a person.
</pre>

The first says experience will never affect the salary; the question is closed. The
second says experience will be looked at, later, and something else comes first.
Learners who translate both as "regardless of experience" lose the distinction that
matters most in a negotiation: whether the item is off the table or merely
postponed.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|田中|たなか}さん<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、{f|全員|ぜんいん}{f|来|き}ました。 → ✅ {f|田中|たなか}さん<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、{f|全員|ぜんいん}{f|来|き}ました。
  {f|全員|ぜんいん} is a claim about a set, and Tanaka would have been in it. That is
  subtraction, not bracketing.

- ❌ できる<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、やってみます。 → ✅ できるかどうか<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、やってみます。
  Only a noun or a nominalised question can carry the `は`. A bare verb needs
  `かどうか`.

- ❌ {f|値段|ねだん}<b>が{f|別|べつ}として</b> → ✅ {f|値段|ねだん}<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>
  The particle is `は`, which is what performs the setting-aside. `が` would make
  the price the subject of {f|別|べつ}だ and the sentence would stop making sense.

- ❌ この{f|案|あん}<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、{f|一番|いちばん}いいと{f|思|おも}います。 → ✅ この{f|案|あん}は、{f|細|こま}かい{f|点|てん}<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、{f|一番|いちばん}いいと{f|思|おも}います。
  You cannot set aside the very thing you go on to evaluate. Park a *part* of it,
  then judge the whole.

## Practice

**1.** {f|安|やす}いかどうか____、この{f|機能|きのう}は{f|他社|たしゃ}の{f|製品|せいひん}にはない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|安|やす}いかどうか<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、この{f|機能|きのう}は{f|他社|たしゃ}の{f|製品|せいひん}にはない。

Price is isolated as its own axis so that the feature can be judged on its own
merits, with no verdict passed on the price either way. An い-adjective clause needs
`かどうか` before `は` can attach to it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|一部|いちぶ}の{f|商品|しょうひん}（は{f|別|べつ}として・を{f|除|のぞ}いて）、すべて{f|半額|はんがく}になります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一部|いちぶ}の{f|商品|しょうひん}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>、すべて{f|半額|はんがく}になります。
"Everything is half price except for certain items."

すべて makes this a claim about a set, and the excluded items are members of it - so
the operation is subtraction. <code>は{f|別|べつ}として</code> would suggest those
items are a topic for a later conversation, which is not what a sale sign means.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Setting aside whether it's true, it's an interesting story."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|本当|ほんとう}かどうか<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、{f|面白|おもしろ}い{f|話|はなし}だ。

<code>{f|本当|ほんとう}かどうか</code> is the standard nominalised question, and
<code>{f|別|べつ}として</code> keeps the speaker from committing to the truth of it
either way. <code>はともかく</code> would also be idiomatic here, but it would hint
that the speaker doubts the story.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|好|す}き{f|嫌|きら}いは{f|別|べつ}として、{f|野菜|やさい}が{f|大|だい}{f|好|す}きです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|好|す}き{f|嫌|きら}いは{f|別|べつ}として、{f|野菜|やさい}は{f|体|からだ}にいい。
or ✅ {f|野菜|やさい}が{f|大|だい}{f|好|す}きです。

The original brackets the question of personal taste and then answers it in the same
breath. Once preference has been set aside, the main clause has to be about
something else - here, nutrition - or the pattern has to go.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|予算|よさん}<b>は{f|別|べつ}として</b>、まず{f|理想|りそう}の{f|案|あん}を{f|考|かんが}えよう。
(b) {f|予算|よさん}<b>にかかわらず</b>、{f|理想|りそう}の{f|案|あん}を{f|考|かんが}えよう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) shelves the budget temporarily so that thinking is not cramped by it. Everyone
in the room understands that the numbers will come back, probably next week, and
that the ideal plan will then be trimmed.

(b) is a much bigger claim: the budget is not to influence the design at all,
whatever it turns out to be. Said by a manager it sounds like permission to spend;
said by anyone else it sounds naive. The difference between *postponed* and
*irrelevant* is the whole distance between these two sentences.

</details>
