# ～と同時に

**Meaning** · at the very same moment as; and equally, at the same time

Two jobs share one form. With an event in front of it,
<code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> says the second thing happened in the same instant as
the first - true overlap, not "and then". With a description in front of it, it
says one thing is **both** of two things at once.

## Formation

<code>{f|同時|どうじ}</code> is a noun, and `と` is the ordinary "with" particle: the
literal sense is "simultaneously with X".

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (timing) | dictionary form + と{f|同時|どうじ}に | {f|鳴|な}る → {f|鳴|な}ると{f|同時|どうじ}に |
| Noun (an event) | Noun + と{f|同時|どうじ}に | {f|卒業|そつぎょう} → {f|卒業|そつぎょう}と{f|同時|どうじ}に |
| Noun (two roles) | Noun + であると{f|同時|どうじ}に | {f|医師|いし} → {f|医師|いし}であると{f|同時|どうじ}に |
| な-adjective (two qualities) | ～であると{f|同時|どうじ}に | {f|危険|きけん} → {f|危険|きけん}であると{f|同時|どうじ}に |
| い-adjective (two qualities) | plain form + と{f|同時|どうじ}に | {f|難|むずか}しい → {f|難|むずか}しいと{f|同時|どうじ}に |
| As a bare adverb | {f|同時|どうじ}に + verb | {f|同時|どうじ}に{f|信号|しんごう}が{f|変|か}わった |
| ❌ ます-form | ❌{f|着|つ}きますと{f|同時|どうじ}に | ✅{f|着|つ}くと{f|同時|どうじ}に |

<div class="note">

**Note** - in the timing sense the verb in front stays in the **dictionary form**
however the sentence ends. The tense of the whole sentence lives on the main clause:
<code>ドアが{f|閉|し}まると{f|同時|どうじ}に、{f|電車|でんしゃ}は{f|動|うご}き{f|出|だ}した</code> is
firmly past.

</div>

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - a **noun of an event** joins directly
({f|開店|かいてん}と{f|同時|どうじ}に). A **noun of a category or role** needs
`である` first ({f|作家|さっか}であると{f|同時|どうじ}に). Getting this backwards is
the commonest error on this pattern.

</div>

The moment the bell rang, everyone stood up.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試合|しあい}{f|終了|しゅうりょう}<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>、{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}した。
It started raining just as the match ended.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

A price cut is welcome for customers and, at the same time, a heavy burden on producers.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> is mildly formal. It reads naturally in news copy,
reports, announcements and careful speech; in casual conversation people reach for
`とすぐに` or a plain `たら`. It is not stiff, though - saying
「{f|着|つ}くと{f|同時|どうじ}に{f|電話|でんわ}するね」 to a friend is perfectly
ordinary.

The timing sense is neutral about who is doing what. Unlike most of this family,
the two clauses may have completely different subjects, and either event may be
deliberate. It happily takes a future main clause, a plan, even a promise - which
is what marks it off from the surprised, past-only members of the group.

The two-roles sense belongs to written argument and speeches. It is how you set up
a balanced judgement - "this is X, and equally it is Y" - and it very often pairs
with `でもある` at the end, which reinforces the "both" reading. Learners tend to
under-use it; it is a cheap way to make an essay sentence sound adult.

Bare <code>{f|同時|どうじ}に</code>, with no `と`, is just the adverb
"simultaneously", and it is fully register-neutral.

## Contrast

### ～と{f|同時|どうじ}に vs ～たとたん

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>たとたん</code> is X finishing and Y arriving as a
**surprise**, always in the past. <code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> is genuine
**overlap**, with no surprise required and no tense restriction.

</div>

This is the N3 pattern <code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> sits closest to, and there are
three clean splits: the form of the first verb (`た` vs dictionary), the tense of
the main clause (past only vs anything), and whether the result may be planned.

<pre>
{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}け<b>たとたん</b>、{f|冷|つめ}たい{f|風|かぜ}が{f|吹|ふ}き{f|込|こ}んできた。
The instant I opened the window, cold air blew in.
</pre>

Nobody arranged that draught, and the sentence could not end in `ます` or a plan.

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|終|お}わる<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>、{f|資料|しりょう}をお{f|送|おく}りします。
I'll send you the documents the moment the meeting finishes.
</pre>

A promise about the future - impossible with <code>たとたん</code>, ordinary with
<code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code>.

### ～と{f|同時|どうじ}に vs ～ながら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ながら` needs **one person** doing two things, one of them
background. <code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> joins **two events**, and their subjects
may differ.

</div>

<pre>
{f|音楽|おんがく}を{f|聞|き}き<b>ながら</b>{f|走|はし}っている。
He runs while listening to music.
</pre>

Both actions are his, and the listening is the backdrop.

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}が{f|走|はし}り{f|出|だ}す<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>、{f|審判|しんぱん}が{f|笛|ふえ}を{f|吹|ふ}いた。
The referee blew the whistle the instant he started running.
</pre>

Two people, two events, one instant. `ながら` cannot express that at all, and
<code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> is poor at the durative background `ながら` is built
for.

### ～と{f|同時|どうじ}に vs ～とともに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> points at a single **instant**.
<code>とともに</code> covers a **stretch** over which two things move together.

</div>

<pre>
{f|年|とし}を{f|取|と}る<b>とともに</b>、{f|好|この}みが{f|変|か}わってきた。
My tastes have changed as I've got older.
</pre>

Nothing here happens in an instant; the two changes keep pace with each other over
years. That proportional reading is <code>とともに</code>'s own, and
<code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> does not carry it.

<pre>
{f|社長|しゃちょう}が{f|立|た}ち{f|上|あ}がる<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>、{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|拍手|はくしゅ}した。
Everyone applauded the moment the president stood up.
</pre>

Where the event really is instantaneous, the two overlap and either will pass;
<code>とともに</code> then sounds a shade more written.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}き<b>ますと{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>{f|電話|でんわ}します。 → ✅ {f|駅|えき}に{f|着|つ}く<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>{f|電話|でんわ}します。
  The politeness belongs to the end of the sentence. The verb in front of
  <code>{f|同時|どうじ}</code> stays plain.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|学生|がくせい}<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>、バンドのリーダーでもある。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|学生|がくせい}<b>であると{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>、バンドのリーダーでもある。
  A role noun needs `である`. Without it the sentence reads as timing - "at the
  moment of student", which means nothing.

- ❌ テレビを{f|見|み}る<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べた。 → ✅ テレビを{f|見|み}<b>ながら</b>ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べた。
  Two ongoing activities by one person is `ながら` territory.
  <code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> would claim the two started in the same instant.

- ❌ {f|授業|じゅぎょう}が{f|終|お}わる<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}の</b>、{f|帰|かえ}った。 → ✅ {f|授業|じゅぎょう}が{f|終|お}わる<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>、{f|帰|かえ}った。
  The connective form is `に`. <code>{f|同時|どうじ}の</code> only appears in front of
  a noun ({f|同時|どうじ}の{f|通訳|つうやく}).

## Practice

**1.** {f|幕|まく}が{f|上|あ}がる____、{f|会場|かいじょう}が{f|静|しず}まり{f|返|かえ}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|幕|まく}が{f|上|あ}がる<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>

Two different subjects - the curtain and the audience - and one shared instant.
Note the dictionary form <code>{f|上|あ}がる</code> even though the sentence is past.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|通訳|つうやく}（と・であると）{f|同時|どうじ}に、{f|小説家|しょうせつか}でもある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|通訳|つうやく}<b>であると</b>{f|同時|どうじ}に、{f|小説家|しょうせつか}でもある。

This is the two-roles sense, not timing, so the noun has to be linked with
`である`. The `でもある` at the end is the giveaway.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The moment the door opened, a smell of coffee drifted out."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ドアが{f|開|あ}く<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>、コーヒーの{f|香|かお}りが{f|流|なが}れてきた。

An event noun clause plus the dictionary form. <code>{f|開|あ}いたとたん</code> would
also work and would sound more startled;
<code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> just reports the overlap.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|受|う}け{f|取|と}ったと{f|同時|どうじ}に、サインをお{f|願|ねが}いします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|受|う}け{f|取|と}る<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>、サインをお{f|願|ねが}いします。

The verb in front takes the dictionary form, not `た`, regardless of when the event
happens. A request in the main clause is fine here - that is one of the things
<code>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</code> can do and <code>たとたん</code> cannot.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|部屋|へや}に{f|入|はい}っ<b>たとたん</b>、{f|電気|でんき}が{f|消|き}えた。
(b) {f|部屋|へや}に{f|入|はい}る<b>と{f|同時|どうじ}に</b>、{f|電気|でんき}が{f|消|き}えた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is told from the speaker's shock: the lights going out was unexpected, and the
sentence carries that jolt. It can only ever be a past report.

(b) is flat description of the timing - the entering and the lights going out
occupied the same instant. It leaves open the possibility that the two were
connected on purpose, say by a sensor, which (a) rules out.

</details>
