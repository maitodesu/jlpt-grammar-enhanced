# ～抜く

**Meaning** · to carry something right through to the end, in spite of how hard it is

The verb {f|抜|ぬ}く is "to pull out, to get all the way through". As a suffix it
adds **endurance**: not simply that the action finished, but that the person kept
going when stopping was the easier option. Nothing trivial takes it. You survive an
era, fight a match, think a problem through - you do not {f|抜|ぬ}く a sandwich.

## Formation

<code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> attaches to the **ます-stem** and produces a new verb. It keeps its own
conjugation, which is that of an う-verb (a Group 1 / {f|五段|ごだん} verb).

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + {f|抜|ぬ}く | {f|走|はし}る → {f|走|はし}り{f|抜|ぬ}く |
| Past | ～{f|抜|ぬ}いた | {f|考|かんが}え{f|抜|ぬ}いた |
| て-form | ～{f|抜|ぬ}いて | {f|戦|たたか}い{f|抜|ぬ}いて |
| Negative (う-verb!) | ～{f|抜|ぬ}かない | やり{f|抜|ぬ}かない |
| Potential | ～{f|抜|ぬ}ける | {f|走|はし}り{f|抜|ぬ}ける |
| Before a noun | ～{f|抜|ぬ}いた + Noun | {f|考|かんが}え{f|抜|ぬ}いた{f|計画|けいかく} |
| Common members | {f|生|い}き{f|抜|ぬ}く・やり{f|抜|ぬ}く・{f|考|かんが}え{f|抜|ぬ}く・{f|守|まも}り{f|抜|ぬ}く・{f|悩|なや}み{f|抜|ぬ}く | - |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the verb must describe something **sustained and hard**:
{f|生|い}きる, {f|戦|たたか}う, {f|走|はし}る, {f|守|まも}る, {f|考|かんが}える,
{f|悩|なや}む, やる. A momentary action has nothing to endure, which is why
❌<code>メールを{f|書|か}き{f|抜|ぬ}いた</code> is not Japanese.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - not every verb ending in {f|抜|ぬ}く belongs to this pattern.
{f|見|み}{f|抜|ぬ}く (to see through a lie), {f|追|お}い{f|抜|ぬ}く (to overtake),
{f|引|ひ}き{f|抜|ぬ}く (to pull out, or to poach a member of staff) are ordinary
compound verbs with their own meanings, built on the "pull out" sense rather than
the "endure to the end" one. Learn them as vocabulary.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|最後|さいご}まで<b>やり{f|抜|ぬ}く</b>つもりだ。
I mean to see it through to the end.
</pre>

<pre>
どんな{f|時代|じだい}でも<b>{f|生|い}き{f|抜|ぬ}く</b>{f|力|ちから}がほしい。
I want the strength to come through whatever the times.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|悩|なや}み<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>{f|末|すえ}に、{f|留学|りゅうがく}を{f|決|き}めた。
After agonising over it at length, I decided to study abroad.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|足|あし}を{f|痛|いた}めながらも、{f|最後|さいご}の{f|一周|いっしゅう}を{f|走|はし}り<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>。

</pre>

<pre>
チームは{f|一人|ひとり}{f|退場|たいじょう}になっても、{f|一点|いってん}のリードを{f|守|まも}り<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>。
Even a player down, the team held on to their one-goal lead.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|考|かんが}え<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>{f|企画|きかく}だけあって、{f|説明|せつめい}に{f|無駄|むだ}がない。
You can tell the proposal was thought right through - there isn't a wasted word in it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|祖母|そぼ}は{f|子|こ}どもを{f|三人|さんにん}{f|抱|かか}えて、{f|戦後|せんご}の{f|苦|くる}しい{f|時代|じだい}を{f|生|い}き<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>。
With three children to raise, my grandmother came through the hard years after the war.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|何度|なんど}もやめたくなったが、{f|仲間|なかま}のおかげで{f|最後|さいご}までやり<b>{f|抜|ぬ}く</b>ことができた。
I wanted to give up more than once, but thanks to my teammates I managed to see it through.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> is **emphatic rather than formal**. It fits speeches, sports commentary,
company slogans, memoirs and encouragement between friends, and it works in plain
and polite forms alike. Nothing about it is stiff; what it is, is *earnest*. A
sentence with <code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> in it is paying tribute to somebody's persistence, including
the speaker's own.

That admiration is the reason it cannot be used lightly. Attach it to a small task
and the sentence sounds either boastful or sarcastic - claiming heroism for
answering three emails. The verbs that take it comfortably are ones that come with
a struggle already attached.

Two members have effectively become nouns of their own.
<code>やり{f|抜|ぬ}く{f|力|ちから}</code> is the standard Japanese rendering of
"grit", familiar from education writing, and
<code>{f|生|い}き{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> turns up constantly in war memoirs, disaster
reporting and anything about hard times. If you only ever produce these two, you
will still sound natural.

Watch the conjugation. Because the suffix is {f|抜|ぬ}く, everything built on it
follows う-verb rules: {f|抜|ぬ}いた, {f|抜|ぬ}いて, {f|抜|ぬ}かない,
{f|抜|ぬ}きます. The `-く` ending misleads nobody, but the past
<code>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</code> - not ❌{f|抜|ぬ}くた - is worth saying out loud a few
times.

## Contrast

### ～{f|抜|ぬ}く vs ～きる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `きる` is about **quantity**: every last one of them. <code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> is
about **endurance**: all the way through, and it was hard.

</div>

<pre>
{f|山|やま}のような{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|一晩|ひとばん}で<b>{f|読|よ}みきった</b>。
I got through a mountain of documents in one night.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|苦|くる}しい{f|練習|れんしゅう}に{f|耐|た}え、{f|三年間|さんねんかん}{f|走|はし}り<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>。
He endured the punishing training and ran for three years without dropping out.
</pre>

The first counts pages; swap in <code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> and it starts to sound like an ordeal
survived. The second measures resolve; swap in `きる` and the three years become a
quantity ticked off. There is a second, unrelated use of `きる` - pushing a state to
its limit, as in {f|疲|つか}れきった - that <code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> does not share at all.

### ～{f|抜|ぬ}く vs ～{f|通|とお}す

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|通|とお}す</code> is about **consistency**: keeping
the same line from start to finish. <code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> is about **hardship**: getting through
in spite of it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|意見|いけん}を{f|最後|さいご}まで{f|言|い}い<b>{f|通|とお}した</b>。
He stuck to his opinion right to the end.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|批判|ひはん}に{f|耐|た}えながら、{f|方針|ほうしん}を{f|守|まも}り<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>。
He weathered the criticism and defended his approach to the end.
</pre>

They overlap in <code>やり{f|通|とお}す</code> and
<code>やり{f|抜|ぬ}く</code>, which are close enough to swap in most sentences.
Even there the flavours differ: <code>{f|通|とお}す</code> hints at stubbornness
and can be mildly critical, while <code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> is almost always admiring.

### ～{f|抜|ぬ}く vs ～{f|終|お}える

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|終|お}える</code> is neutral completion. <code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> adds
the difficulty, and the credit for getting past it.

</div>

<pre>
レポートを{f|書|か}き<b>{f|終|お}えて</b>、やっと{f|眠|ねむ}れる。
I've finished writing the report and can finally sleep.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|山道|やまみち}を{f|歩|ある}き<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>。
I walked the whole mountain road alone, all the way.
</pre>

<code>{f|終|お}える</code> says only that the action stopped because it was done, and it fits any
verb at all. <code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> is a comment on the person as much as on the action, and the
narrower list of verbs it accepts follows directly from that.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|走|はし}る<b>{f|抜|ぬ}く</b> → ✅ {f|走|はし}り<b>{f|抜|ぬ}く</b>
  The base is the ます-stem. This is the same shape as
  <code>{f|走|はし}り{f|続|つづ}ける</code>.

- ❌ やり{f|抜|ぬ}く<b>た</b> → ✅ やり<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>
  {f|抜|ぬ}く is an う-verb, so its past is {f|抜|ぬ}いた and its negative is
  {f|抜|ぬ}かない.

- ❌ ケーキを{f|食|た}べ<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>。 → ✅ ケーキを{f|食|た}べ<b>きった</b>。
  Finishing a quantity is `きる`. There is nothing to endure in eating cake, so
  <code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> has no work to do.

- ❌ Reading {f|前|まえ}の{f|走者|そうしゃ}を{f|追|お}い<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b> as "ran to the end" → ✅ it means "overtook the runner ahead".
  <code>{f|追|お}い{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> is an independent verb from the vocabulary,
  built on the "pull past" sense. Check the dictionary before reading a
  {f|抜|ぬ}く compound as endurance.

## Practice

**1.** {f|三年|さんねん}かかったが、{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|辞書|じしょ}を{f|作|つく}り____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|作|つく}り<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>

Three years of solitary work is exactly the sustained difficulty this suffix marks.
Note the う-verb past: <code>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</code>, never ❌{f|抜|ぬ}くた.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|大盛|おおも}りのラーメンを{f|一人|ひとり}で（{f|食|た}べきった・{f|食|た}べ{f|抜|ぬ}いた）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|大盛|おおも}りのラーメンを{f|一人|ひとり}で<b>{f|食|た}べきった</b>。

A large bowl is a quantity, and getting to the bottom of it is `きる`.
<code>{f|食|た}べ{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> is not an expression Japanese uses at all -
eating is not something one endures to the end.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Whatever people said, she protected her students to the very end."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|周|まわ}りに{f|何|なに}を{f|言|い}われても、{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|生徒|せいと}たちを{f|守|まも}り<b>{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>。

<code>{f|守|まも}り{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> is one of the most natural members of this
family, because protecting somebody under pressure is precisely the kind of
sustained effort the suffix marks. Note {f|何|なに} before を - the reading would be
なん only in front of で, だ or の.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}は{f|最後|さいご}まであきらめるぬいて、ゴールした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|最後|さいご}まであきらめ<b>ずに{f|走|はし}り{f|抜|ぬ}いて</b>、ゴールした。
"He reached the finish without ever giving up."

Two problems. The base must be the ます-stem, and more importantly the verb itself
is wrong: <code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> marks carrying something **through**, so it cannot attach to
"giving up". Put the endurance on the running and the negation on the giving up.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|課題|かだい}を{f|最後|さいご}まで<b>やり{f|終|お}えた</b>。
(b) {f|課題|かだい}を{f|最後|さいご}まで<b>やり{f|抜|ぬ}いた</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply reports that the assignment is done. It says nothing about what it cost,
and it would be the ordinary sentence for a piece of homework.

(b) puts the difficulty in the foreground: there was a real temptation to stop, and
the speaker did not. It is a small claim to credit, which is why it fits a long
project and sounds inflated on a short one.

</details>
