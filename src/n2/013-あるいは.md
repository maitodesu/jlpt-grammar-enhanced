# あるいは

**Meaning** · or; alternatively - and, used adverbially, possibly

`あるいは` is the written "or". It sets two options side by side without asking anyone
to choose between them on the spot, which is why it belongs to instructions,
regulations and prose rather than to conversation. Its second life is as an adverb
meaning "possibly", where it hedges a whole sentence rather than joining two things.

## Usage

Two jobs, and it is worth keeping them apart.

**Connecting alternatives.** It stands between two nouns, two phrases or two clauses:

- {f|鉛筆|えんぴつ}あるいはボールペンでご{f|記入|きにゅう}ください。
- {f|窓口|まどぐち}で{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}むか、あるいは{f|郵送|ゆうそう}してください。

When it joins clauses, the first one usually ends in か, exactly as it would before
それとも.

**Hedging a statement.** At the head of a sentence it means "it may be that", and it
almost always ends in かもしれない, だろう or でしょう:

- あるいは{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}うかもしれない。

Nothing attaches to it and it never changes form. The kanji spelling
<code>{f|或|ある}いは</code> exists but is rarely used; kana is standard.

One thing it does not do: ask. `あるいは` presents alternatives to a reader; it does
not put a choice to a listener and wait for an answer. That is それとも's job, and the
two are not interchangeable.

メール<b>あるいは</b>{f|電話|でんわ}でご{f|連絡|れんらく}ください。
Please contact us by email or telephone.
</pre>

Either hand the application in at the desk or send it by post.
</pre>

<pre>

It may be that he already knows about it.
</pre>

<pre>

The work is scheduled for Saturday morning, or alternatively a weekday the following week.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Written and formal. Forms, manuals, contracts, academic papers, official notices and
careful prose are where it lives. Spoken between friends it sounds like a document
being read aloud - there, か does the joining
（<code>{f|土曜|どよう}か{f|日曜|にちよう}</code>）and それとも does the asking.

The adverbial "possibly" use is a shade more literary again, and carries a
reflective tone: the speaker is entertaining a possibility rather than asserting it.
It fits an essay or an internal monologue better than a briefing. Note that it needs
a hedging ending to complete it - `あるいは` followed by a flat assertion leaves the
sentence sounding as though a word has gone missing.

Because the pattern is neutral about which option is preferred, it is the polite
choice in an instruction: telling someone they may do A or B, with no steer either
way. If you *do* want to steer, the ordinary route is to state the preferred option
and add the other with <code>または</code> or a な-clause of explanation.

<div class="note">

**Note** - in a list of three or more, Japanese usually marks only the last boundary:
<code>{f|現金|げんきん}、{f|振|ふ}り{f|込|こ}み、あるいはクレジットカードでお{f|支払|しはら}いいただけます</code>.
Repeating `あるいは` between every pair reads as heavily as it would in English.

</div>

## Contrast

### あるいは vs または

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are the formal "or", and in a plain list they are
interchangeable. <code>または</code> is the stricter **either/or**; `あるいは` also
carries the "or possibly" sense that <code>または</code> lacks.

</div>

<pre>
{f|保険証|ほけんしょう}<b>または</b>{f|運転免許証|うんてんめんきょしょう}をご{f|提示|ていじ}ください。
Please show your health insurance card or your driving licence.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|原因|げんいん}は{f|設定|せってい}の{f|誤|あやま}り、<b>あるいは</b>{f|機器|きき}の{f|故障|こしょう}だと{f|考|かんが}えられる。
The cause is thought to be a setting error, or possibly a hardware fault.
</pre>

The first sentence lists two documents either of which will do - a closed choice, and
<code>または</code> is what a form prints. The second is not offering a choice at all;
it is a writer weighing two explanations, and `あるいは` carries that uncertainty
naturally. Where the sentence is simply "A or B, take your pick",
either word is correct and <code>または</code> is marginally the commoner in
officialese.

### あるいは vs それとも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `あるいは` **presents** alternatives in a statement. `それとも`
**asks** the listener to pick, and only appears in questions.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|駅前|えきまえ}、<b>あるいは</b>{f|市民|しみん}ホールになります。
The venue will be either the space by the station or the civic hall.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|駅前|えきまえ}ですか。<b>それとも</b>{f|市民|しみん}ホールですか。
Is the venue the place by the station, or the civic hall?
</pre>

Put `あるいは` into the second sentence and it stops being a question and becomes a
strange list; put それとも into the first and the reader waits for a question mark that
never comes. The test is simple: if you expect an answer, you want それとも.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ コーヒーあるいは{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}、どちらがいいですか。 → ✅ コーヒーですか。<b>それとも</b>{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}ですか。
  You are asking someone to choose, and `あるいは` cannot do that. In this shape
  「コーヒーか{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}、どちらがいいですか」 also works.

- ❌ {f|今日|きょう}あるいは{f|明日|あした}、{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}こうよ。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}<b>か</b>{f|明日|あした}、{f|飲|の}みに{f|行|い}こうよ。
  An invitation to a friend is spoken Japanese, and spoken Japanese joins two options
  with か. `あるいは` here sounds like a memo.

- ❌ あるいは{f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る。 → ✅ あるいは{f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>かもしれない</b>。
  The adverbial use is a hedge, so it needs an ending that hedges. Attached to a flat
  assertion it leaves the sentence unfinished.

## Practice

**1.** お{f|支払|しはら}いは{f|現金|げんきん}、____クレジットカードでお{f|願|ねが}いいたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|現金|げんきん}、<b>あるいは</b>クレジットカードでお{f|願|ねが}いいたします。

A formal instruction offering two acceptable methods, with no question being asked -
which is exactly the slot this word fills. <code>または</code> would be equally
correct here.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: {f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}きますか。あるいは{f|家|いえ}でゆっくりしますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}きますか。<b>それとも</b>{f|家|いえ}でゆっくりしますか。

Two questions, and the listener is being asked to choose between them. `あるいは`
presents options in a statement; it cannot stand between two questions.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|症状|しょうじょう}は{f|疲|つか}れ、あるいはストレスによるものだ。
(b) この{f|症状|しょうじょう}は{f|疲|つか}れか、それともストレスによるものか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a statement, and a fairly confident one: the writer has narrowed the cause to
two candidates and is reporting both. `あるいは` here shades towards "or possibly",
leaving which of the two open.

(b) is a question the speaker is putting - to a doctor, or to themselves. それとも
sets up a genuine fork that someone is expected to resolve, which is why the sentence
ends in か and not だ.

</details>
