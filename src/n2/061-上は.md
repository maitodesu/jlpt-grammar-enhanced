# ～上は

**Meaning** · now that; since it has come to this - read <code>{f|上|うえ}は</code>. Once the situation has reached a certain point, only one course of action is left

<code>{f|上|うえ}は</code> takes something that has already happened - a decision
announced, a die cast, a fact that can no longer be walked back - and treats it as
closing off the alternatives. What follows is resolve, duty, or resignation, very
often with <code>しかない</code>, <code>ほかない</code> or
<code>なければならない</code>. It is a formal, written-leaning pattern with a
distinctly resolute ring.

## Formation

<code>{f|上|うえ}は</code> follows a clause in noun-modifying (plain) form. The
past tense dominates, because the pattern is about a point already passed.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (past) | た-form + {f|上|うえ}は | {f|決|き}めた → {f|決|き}めた{f|上|うえ}は |
| Verb (non-past) | plain form + {f|上|うえ}は | やる → やる{f|上|うえ}は (uncommon) |
| Fixed opener | こうなった + {f|上|うえ}は | - → こうなった{f|上|うえ}は |
| Noun / な-adjective | ～である + {f|上|うえ}は | {f|社員|しゃいん} → {f|社員|しゃいん}である{f|上|うえ}は (rare) |

<div class="note">

**Note** - three unrelated patterns share this kanji and learners mix them
constantly. <code>{f|上|うえ}は</code> = "now that"; <code>た{f|上|うえ}で</code> =
"after doing, and on that basis"; <code>{f|上|うえ}に</code> = "on top of that,
moreover". A fourth, read <code>じょう</code>, attaches to nouns and means "from
the standpoint of": <code>{f|法律上|ほうりつじょう}</code>,
<code>{f|健康上|けんこうじょう}</code>. Reading and meaning move together.

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is a stiff pattern. You will meet it in written statements, formal
announcements, editorials, courtroom language and the resolute speech of fiction -
and comparatively rarely in ordinary conversation, where `からには` does the same
job and sounds like a person rather than a document. If you say
<code>{f|上|うえ}は</code> over lunch, it will land as deliberately dramatic. That
is sometimes exactly the effect you want; just know that you are reaching for it.

The tone is resolve with the exits closed. There is no cheerfulness in
<code>{f|上|うえ}は</code>: the situation has narrowed, and the speaker is
accepting the narrowing. That is why <code>しかない</code> and
<code>ほかない</code> - "there is no alternative" - are its most frequent
companions, and why <code>こうなった{f|上|うえ}は</code> has become a set opening
for bad news being faced squarely.

Because the pattern is written-leaning, its second half tends to be written-leaning
too: <code>べきだ</code>, <code>なければならない</code>,
<code>{f|覚悟|かくご}だ</code>, <code>つもりだ</code>. A casual ending after it
sounds mismatched - <code>こうなった{f|上|うえ}は、まあいいや</code> is a joke, not
a sentence. Note also that the second half must be something still open: an
intention, an obligation, a judgement. It cannot be a finished report of what
happened next.

## Contrast

### ～{f|上|うえ}は vs ～からには

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the meaning is the same. `からには` is the everyday form;
<code>{f|上|うえ}は</code> is the formal one, and it strongly prefers a completed
action in front of it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|留学|りゅうがく}すると{f|決|き}めた<b>からには</b>、お{f|金|かね}をためないと。
Now that I've decided to study abroad, I need to start saving.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|留学|りゅうがく}を{f|決意|けつい}した<b>{f|上|うえ}は</b>、{f|覚悟|かくご}を{f|決|き}めて{f|準備|じゅんび}を{f|進|すす}めます。
Having resolved to study abroad, I shall steel myself and get on with preparing.
</pre>

Same event, two registers. The second sentence's vocabulary -
<code>{f|決意|けつい}</code>, <code>{f|覚悟|かくご}</code> - is not accidental;
<code>{f|上|うえ}は</code> pulls the whole sentence upward in formality. A
non-past verb, which `からには` takes happily, sits awkwardly before
<code>{f|上|うえ}は</code>.

### ～{f|上|うえ}は vs ～た{f|上|うえ}で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|上|うえ}は</code> is a *reason*: given that X has
happened, Y must follow. <code>た{f|上|うえ}で</code> is a *sequence*: do X first,
then decide Y on that basis.

</div>

<pre>
{f|検査|けんさ}の{f|結果|けっか}が{f|出|で}た<b>{f|上|うえ}は</b>、{f|治療|ちりょう}を{f|始|はじ}めるしかない。
Now that the test results are in, there's no choice but to start treatment.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|検査|けんさ}の{f|結果|けっか}を{f|見|み}た<b>{f|上|うえ}で</b>、{f|治療|ちりょう}の{f|方針|ほうしん}を{f|決|き}めます。
We'll decide on a course of treatment after looking at the test results.
</pre>

One は, one で, and the sentences do different work. The first says the results
have removed the alternatives. The second says the results are an input to a
decision still to be made. Mixing them up produces sentences that sound almost
right and mean something else entirely.

### ～{f|上|うえ}は vs ～{f|以上|いじょう}（は）

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> covers standing states as well
as completed acts; <code>{f|上|うえ}は</code> really wants a completed act.

</div>

<pre>
{f|親|おや}である<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>、{f|子|こ}どもの{f|安全|あんぜん}に{f|責任|せきにん}がある。
As long as you are a parent, you are responsible for your child's safety.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|親|おや}になった<b>{f|上|うえ}は</b>、{f|生活|せいかつ}を{f|見直|みなお}さなければならない。
Now that I've become a parent, I have to rethink how I live.
</pre>

The first describes an ongoing condition and the duty that rides along with it.
The second names the moment things changed and the obligation created by it. This
is a leaning rather than a hard wall, but <code>である{f|上|うえ}は</code> is
distinctly rare, while <code>である{f|以上|いじょう}</code> is everywhere in formal
writing.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}だ<b>{f|上|うえ}は</b> → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}である<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>
  A plain だ cannot precede <code>{f|上|うえ}は</code>, and even the である version
  is unusual here. For a standing state, reach for
  <code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> instead.

- ❌ {f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}けた<b>{f|上|うえ}は</b>、{f|大変|たいへん}でした。 → ✅ {f|引|ひ}き{f|受|う}けた<b>{f|上|うえ}は</b>、{f|最後|さいご}までやります。
  The second half has to be an intention or an obligation, not a report of how it
  turned out.

- ❌ {f|相談|そうだん}した<b>{f|上|うえ}は</b>{f|決|き}めます。 → ✅ {f|相談|そうだん}した<b>{f|上|うえ}で</b>{f|決|き}めます。
  "After consulting" is <code>{f|上|うえ}で</code>. With は the sentence claims the
  consultation left you no choice, which is not what you meant.

- ❌ こうなった<b>{f|上|うえ}は</b>、どうしようかな。 → ✅ こうなった<b>{f|上|うえ}は</b>、{f|自分|じぶん}でやるしかない。
  Wavering undercuts the pattern. <code>{f|上|うえ}は</code> announces that the
  deliberation is over.

## Practice

**1.** {f|試合|しあい}に{f|出|で}ると{f|決|き}まった____、{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|増|ふ}やすしかない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|決|き}まった<b>{f|上|うえ}は</b>

The decision is settled and the second half is <code>しかない</code>, the classic
partner. Note the past tense: <code>{f|上|うえ}は</code> looks back at a point
already passed.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|専門家|せんもんか}の{f|意見|いけん}を{f|聞|き}いた（{f|上|うえ}は・{f|上|うえ}で）、{f|結論|けつろん}を{f|出|だ}したいと{f|思|おも}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|聞|き}いた<b>{f|上|うえ}で</b>、{f|結論|けつろん}を{f|出|だ}したい

The expert's opinion is an input to a decision that has not been made yet, so this
is the sequence pattern <code>{f|上|うえ}で</code>. With は you would be saying the
opinion had already forced your hand.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Now that the deadline has been announced, we have no choice but to work weekends."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りが{f|発表|はっぴょう}された<b>{f|上|うえ}は</b>、{f|週末|しゅうまつ}も{f|働|はたら}くしかない。

The passive past marks the announcement as a done thing, and
<code>しかない</code> supplies the "no choice" that
<code>{f|上|うえ}は</code> is built for.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|社長|しゃちょう}である{f|上|うえ}は、{f|社員|しゃいん}の{f|生活|せいかつ}を{f|守|まも}る{f|義務|ぎむ}がある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|社長|しゃちょう}である<b>{f|以上|いじょう}</b>、{f|社員|しゃいん}の{f|生活|せいかつ}を{f|守|まも}る{f|義務|ぎむ}がある。

Being company president is a standing state, not an event, and standing states
take <code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code>. <code>である{f|上|うえ}は</code> is not
ungrammatical but reads as strained.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}すと{f|決|き}めた<b>{f|上|うえ}は</b>、すぐに{f|準備|じゅんび}を{f|始|はじ}めます。
(b) {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}すことになった<b>ので</b>、すぐに{f|準備|じゅんび}を{f|始|はじ}めます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a declaration. The decision has cut off the other options, and the speaker
is squaring up to the consequences. It sounds formal, and slightly weighty for a
house move.

(b) simply gives the move as the reason for starting to pack. It is neutral,
everyday, and carries no sense of a bridge being burned - which for most house
moves is the more natural thing to say.

</details>
