# ～から見ると

**Meaning** · seen from ~; judging from ~ - the most flexible member of this family, covering both a vantage point and a body of evidence

Read <code>から{f|見|み}ると</code>. It is the literal "looking from" phrase put to
grammatical work, and it kept both jobs. Put a **person or institution** in front
of it and you are borrowing their eyes: {f|外国人|がいこくじん}から{f|見|み}れば.
Put **evidence** in front of it and you are reasoning from what you observed:
{f|空|そら}の{f|様子|ようす}から{f|見|み}て. And it still means plain "seen from"
when the noun is a place.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (person / group) | Noun + から{f|見|み}ると | {f|外国人|がいこくじん} → {f|外国人|がいこくじん}から{f|見|み}ると |
| Noun (evidence) | Noun + から{f|見|み}て | {f|傷|きず}の{f|深|ふか}さ → {f|傷|きず}の{f|深|ふか}さから{f|見|み}て |
| Noun (place) - literal | Noun + から{f|見|み}ると | {f|山|やま}の{f|上|うえ} → {f|山|やま}の{f|上|うえ}から{f|見|み}ると |
| Conditional shade | Noun + から{f|見|み}れば | {f|会社|かいしゃ}{f|側|がわ} → {f|会社|かいしゃ}{f|側|がわ}から{f|見|み}れば |
| Concessive - "even by" | Noun + から{f|見|み}ても | {f|世界|せかい}の{f|基準|きじゅん} → {f|世界|せかい}の{f|基準|きじゅん}から{f|見|み}ても |
| Politeness | carried by the main clause | … → …から{f|見|み}ると{f|大|おお}きいです |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>から{f|見|み}て</code> is the compact written form and is very
common in analysis. It is not the て-form of a request; nothing can follow it but
the conclusion.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral and very widely usable - this is the member of the family to
reach for when you are not sure which one fits. It takes a person, an
organisation, an abstract standard, a set of data or a physical location, and it
sounds equally natural in conversation, in a newspaper column and in a report.

The endings shade as you would expect from their conjugations.
<code>から{f|見|み}ると</code> is the plain one. <code>から{f|見|み}れば</code>
leans towards "if you take that as your position", which suits the standpoint use.
<code>から{f|見|み}て</code> is terse and written, and is the form you will meet in
headlines and analysis. <code>から{f|見|み}ても</code> means "even measured that
way", and is a strong way to close an argument.

Two small cautions. First, the standpoint use normally introduces a view that
differs from the speaker's own, or at least is worth pointing out as one side of
things; used about yourself it is unremarkable but slightly odd, and
{f|私|わたし}としては is the usual choice. Second, the pattern is not an evaluation
frame: {f|子|こ}どもから{f|見|み}て{f|大切|たいせつ}だ is strange, because "being
important to someone" is <code>にとって</code>'s job.

## Contrast

### から{f|見|み}ると vs からすると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - in the inference sense they are near-synonyms. Only
<code>から{f|見|み}ると</code> also works literally, with a place.

</div>

<pre>
ホテルの{f|屋上|おくじょう}<b>から{f|見|み}ると</b>{f|港|みなと}まで{f|見渡|みわた}せる。
From the hotel roof you can see all the way to the harbour.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|記録|きろく}<b>からすると</b>、{f|今年|ことし}も{f|同|おな}じころ{f|咲|さ}くはずだ。
Going by last year's records, they should bloom around the same time this year.
</pre>

Try <code>からすると</code> on the roof and it collapses, because a roof is not
information. Try <code>から{f|見|み}ると</code> on the records and it is perfectly
fine. That asymmetry is the practical difference: one is a superset of the other.

### から{f|見|み}ると vs にとって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>から{f|見|み}ると</code> reports how something
**appears** to someone. <code>にとって</code> reports what something **is worth**
to someone.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新入社員|しんにゅうしゃいん}<b>から{f|見|み}ると</b>、この{f|会社|かいしゃ}は{f|静|しず}かすぎるらしい。
To the new recruits, this company apparently feels far too quiet.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新入社員|しんにゅうしゃいん}<b>にとって</b>、{f|最初|さいしょ}の{f|一年|いちねん}は{f|大切|たいせつ}だ。
For new recruits, the first year matters.
</pre>

The first ends in an impression; the second in a valuation. If you can rewrite the
English as "X looks/seems ~ to them", you want <code>から{f|見|み}ると</code>.

### から{f|見|み}ると vs ところを{f|見|み}ると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both contain {f|見|み}る, and they are not variants of each
other. <code>から{f|見|み}ると</code> takes a **noun**; <code>ところを
{f|見|み}ると</code> takes an **observed clause** and only ever means "judging from".

</div>

<pre>
{f|顔色|かおいろ}<b>から{f|見|み}て</b>、{f|昨夜|さくや}はよく{f|眠|ねむ}れなかったようだ。
By the look of his face, he didn't sleep well last night.
</pre>

<pre>
あくびばかりしている<b>ところを{f|見|み}ると</b>、{f|昨夜|さくや}はよく{f|眠|ねむ}れなかったようだ。
Judging from the fact that he keeps yawning, he didn't sleep well last night.
</pre>

<code>ところを{f|見|み}ると</code> cannot take a standpoint: there is no
✗{f|外国人|がいこくじん}のところを{f|見|み}ると meaning "from a foreigner's point
of view".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っている<b>から{f|見|み}ると</b>、{f|中止|ちゅうし}だろう。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っている<b>ところを{f|見|み}ると</b>、{f|中止|ちゅうし}だろう。
  A whole clause needs ところを; only a noun goes with から.

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>から{f|見|み}て</b>{f|必要|ひつよう}な{f|授業|じゅぎょう}だ。 → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>にとって</b>{f|必要|ひつよう}な{f|授業|じゅぎょう}だ。
  "Necessary for X" is a valuation, and that belongs to <code>にとって</code>.

- ❌ {f|窓|まど}<b>から{f|見|み}て</b>、{f|海|うみ}がきれいです。 → ✅ {f|窓|まど}<b>から{f|見|み}ると</b>、{f|海|うみ}がきれいです。
  In the literal "seen from" use the と form is the natural one;
  <code>から{f|見|み}て</code> has drifted towards "judging from".

- ❌ {f|外国人|がいこくじん}<b>を{f|見|み}ると</b>、{f|敬語|けいご}は{f|難|むずか}しい。 → ✅ {f|外国人|がいこくじん}<b>から{f|見|み}ると</b>、{f|敬語|けいご}は{f|難|むずか}しい。
  With `を` the sentence means "when I look at foreigners", which is a different
  sentence altogether.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|指紋|しもん}の{f|大|おお}きさ____、{f|子|こ}どもがさわったのだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|指紋|しもん}の{f|大|おお}きさ<b>から{f|見|み}て</b>、{f|子|こ}どもがさわったのだろう。
（{f|見|み}ると also fine）

The size of the print is physical evidence, so this is the inference use, and the
conclusion stays a guess with だろう.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|親|おや}（から{f|見|み}れば・ところを{f|見|み}ると）、{f|子|こ}どもはいくつになっても{f|子|こ}どもだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|親|おや}<b>から{f|見|み}れば</b>、{f|子|こ}どもはいくつになっても{f|子|こ}どもだ。

The noun is a person whose eyes we are borrowing.
<code>ところを{f|見|み}ると</code> needs a clause describing something observed, and
has no standpoint meaning at all.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Seen from the train window, the whole valley was white with snow."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|電車|でんしゃ}の{f|窓|まど}<b>から{f|見|み}ると</b>、{f|谷|たに}{f|全体|ぜんたい}が{f|雪|ゆき}で{f|白|しろ}くなっていた。

This is the literal vantage-point use, which is why
<code>から{f|見|み}ると</code> works here and <code>からすると</code> or
<code>から{f|言|い}うと</code> would not.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|学生|がくせい}から{f|見|み}ると、この{f|授業|じゅぎょう}は{f|大切|たいせつ}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>にとって</b>、この{f|授業|じゅぎょう}は{f|大切|たいせつ}です。
（or {f|学生|がくせい}から{f|見|み}ると、この{f|授業|じゅぎょう}は{f|退屈|たいくつ}らしい）

<code>{f|大切|たいせつ}だ</code> states what the class is worth to the students, and
that is <code>にとって</code>. Keep <code>から{f|見|み}ると</code> only if the main
clause reports how the class *looks* to them.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|数字|すうじ}<b>から{f|見|み}ると</b>、{f|経営|けいえい}は{f|順調|じゅんちょう}だ。
(b) {f|数字|すうじ}<b>から{f|見|み}ても</b>、{f|経営|けいえい}は{f|順調|じゅんちょう}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) restricts the verdict to one source of information. It quietly leaves room for
the possibility that something outside the figures tells a different story, and it
often sets up a but-clause.

(b) does the opposite. も means "this measure too", so the figures are being added
to other grounds already accepted - the business looks healthy, and the numbers
confirm it as well. It strengthens rather than hedges.

</details>
