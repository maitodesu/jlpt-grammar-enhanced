# ～に沿って

**Meaning** · along; in line with; in keeping with - runs the length of a line and does not leave it, whether the line is a river or a plan

<code>{f|沿|そ}う</code> is "to run along the side of something". Physically that is a
road beside a river. Figuratively it is an action that keeps to a line already laid
down - a plan, a policy, an agenda, a theme, somebody's wishes - from beginning to
end, without wandering off. The line exists first; the action tracks it.

## Formation

<code>{f|沿|そ}う</code> is intransitive, so the line it follows is marked with `に`.
The pattern takes a **noun** and nothing else.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + に{f|沿|そ}って | {f|川|かわ} → {f|川|かわ}に{f|沿|そ}って |
| Noun (stiffer, written) | Noun + に{f|沿|そ}い | {f|方針|ほうしん} → {f|方針|ほうしん}に{f|沿|そ}い |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + に{f|沿|そ}った + Noun | {f|希望|きぼう} → {f|希望|きぼう}に{f|沿|そ}った{f|提案|ていあん} |
| Same, non-past shape | Noun + に{f|沿|そ}う + Noun | {f|期待|きたい} → {f|期待|きたい}に{f|沿|そ}う{f|結果|けっか} |
| Negative (business set phrase) | Noun + に{f|沿|そ}えず | ご{f|希望|きぼう} → ご{f|希望|きぼう}に{f|沿|そ}えず |
| As a noun suffix | Noun + {f|沿|ぞ}い + の + Noun | {f|線路|せんろ} → {f|線路沿|せんろぞ}いの{f|家|いえ} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the suffix in the last row voices: {f|川沿|かわぞ}い,
{f|海沿|うみぞ}い, {f|線路沿|せんろぞ}い - **ぞ**い, never そい. That is ordinary
rendaku after a noun, and it is a reading learners get wrong for years because the
kanji does not change.

</div>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

I assembled it following the instruction booklet.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|道|みち}<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|桜|さくら}の{f|木|き}が{f|並|なら}んでいる。
Cherry trees stand in a row along the road.
</pre>

<pre>
ご{f|希望|きぼう}<b>に{f|沿|そ}えず</b>、{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございません。
We are sorry that we could not meet your wishes.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|事前|じぜん}に{f|配|くば}られた{f|進行表|しんこうひょう}<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|進|すす}められた。

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

Two registers in one pattern. The **physical** sense -
<code>{f|川|かわ}に{f|沿|そ}って</code>, <code>{f|道|みち}に{f|沿|そ}って</code> - is
completely neutral and turns up in ordinary speech, in directions, in weather
reports. The **figurative** sense - a plan, a policy, an agenda - is formal and
mostly written; in casual talk you would say
<code>{f|決|き}めたとおりに</code> or <code>そのままやる</code> instead.

<code>に{f|沿|そ}い</code> is the stiff connective form, at home in notices and
prepared statements and nowhere else.

Learn the business boilerplate as a block. <code>ご{f|期待|きたい}に{f|沿|そ}えるよう{f|努力|どりょく}いたします</code>
is what a company says when it accepts your custom, and
<code>ご{f|希望|きぼう}に{f|沿|そ}いかねます</code> or
<code>ご{f|期待|きたい}に{f|沿|そ}えず{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございません</code> is what a
rejection letter says. The second one is worth recognising on sight: it is the
polite Japanese for "no".

One spelling footnote. In the "meet someone's wishes" sense you will also see the
kanji <code>{f|添|そ}う</code>, and both spellings are current;
<code>{f|沿|そ}う</code> is the commoner modern choice and the safe one to write.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|沿|そ}って vs ～に{f|従|したが}って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|従|したが}って</code> is **obeying** a source of
instruction. <code>に{f|沿|そ}って</code> is **keeping to the shape** of something that
lays out the whole course.

</div>

<pre>
{f|案内|あんない}の{f|矢印|やじるし}<b>に{f|従|したが}って</b>{f|進|すす}んでください。
Please proceed following the arrows.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|海岸|かいがん}<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|細|ほそ}い{f|道|みち}が{f|続|つづ}いている。
A narrow road runs on along the coast.
</pre>

The physical use separates them cleanly: only
<code>に{f|沿|そ}って</code> can mean "physically alongside", because only
<code>{f|沿|そ}う</code> is about parallel lines.

Where they overlap - a plan can be both obeyed and kept to -
<code>に{f|従|したが}って</code> casts the plan as an instruction you comply with,
step by step, while <code>に{f|沿|そ}って</code> casts it as an outline you stay
inside. <code>に{f|従|したが}って</code> also has a second life meaning "as X
progresses", which <code>に{f|沿|そ}って</code> never has.

### ～に{f|沿|そ}って vs ～に{f|基|もと}づいて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|基|もと}づいて</code> says where the thing **came
from**. <code>に{f|沿|そ}って</code> says what line it **keeps to while it runs**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|実験|じっけん}データ<b>に{f|基|もと}づいて</b>{f|仮説|かせつ}を{f|立|た}てた。
We formed the hypothesis on the basis of the experimental data.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|学会|がっかい}の{f|規定|きてい}<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|論文|ろんぶん}の{f|形式|けいしき}を{f|整|ととの}えた。
I put the paper into shape in line with the society's formatting rules.
</pre>

The quickest test is <code>{f|期待|きたい}</code> and <code>{f|希望|きぼう}</code>.
<code>{f|期待|きたい}に{f|沿|そ}う</code> is fixed and extremely common;
<code>{f|期待|きたい}に{f|基|もと}づく</code> is not Japanese. A wish is a line you try
to keep to, never evidence you can reason from.

### ～に{f|沿|そ}って vs ～に{f|応|おう}じて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|沿|そ}って</code> follows **one fixed line**.
<code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code> means the result **changes as the other thing changes**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|予算|よさん}{f|案|あん}<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|内容|ないよう}を{f|決|き}めます。
We will settle the contents in line with the draft budget.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|予算|よさん}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|内容|ないよう}を{f|変|か}えます。
We adjust the contents according to the budget.
</pre>

The first has one budget document and one outcome that stays inside it. The second
has a range of budgets and a matching range of outcomes - big budget, big plan;
small budget, small plan. If you can add "the bigger the X, the bigger the Y", you
want <code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|川|かわ}<b>を{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|走|はし}る → ✅ {f|川|かわ}<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|走|はし}る
  <code>{f|沿|そ}う</code> is intransitive. The `を` comes from
  <code>{f|川|かわ}を{f|渡|わた}る</code>-type verbs, which cross the thing rather than
  run beside it.

- ❌ {f|決|き}めた<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|進|すす}めよう。 → ✅ {f|決定|けってい}{f|事項|じこう}<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|進|すす}めよう。
  A noun is required. A bare verb cannot attach, so either supply the noun or switch
  to <code>{f|決|き}めたとおりに</code>, which does take a clause.

- ❌ ご{f|期待|きたい}<b>に{f|基|もと}づく</b>{f|結果|けっか}を{f|出|だ}したい。 → ✅ ご{f|期待|きたい}<b>に{f|沿|そ}う</b>{f|結果|けっか}を{f|出|だ}したい。
  Expectations are a line to keep to, not grounds to argue from. This collocation is
  fixed and there is no leeway in it.

- ❌ {f|時間|じかん}{f|帯|たい}<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|料金|りょうきん}が{f|変|か}わります。 → ✅ {f|時間|じかん}{f|帯|たい}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|料金|りょうきん}が{f|変|か}わります。
  Nothing varies inside <code>に{f|沿|そ}って</code>. As soon as the sentence says one
  thing changes with another, you need <code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|線路|せんろ}____、{f|古|ふる}い{f|商店街|しょうてんがい}が{f|続|つづ}いている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|線路|せんろ}<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>、{f|古|ふる}い{f|商店街|しょうてんがい}が{f|続|つづ}いている。

This is the plain physical sense - the shopping street runs parallel to the track.
Note that the same idea as a noun phrase would be
{f|線路沿|せんろぞ}いの{f|商店街|しょうてんがい}, with voiced ぞ.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|参加|さんか}{f|人数|にんずう}（に{f|沿|そ}って・に{f|応|おう}じて）テーブルの{f|数|かず}を{f|増|ふ}やします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|参加|さんか}{f|人数|にんずう}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>テーブルの{f|数|かず}を{f|増|ふ}やします。

The number of tables rises and falls with the number of guests, and that covariation
is what <code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code> is for. <code>に{f|沿|そ}って</code> has no
variation in it at all.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "We are truly sorry that we were unable to meet your wishes on this occasion."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|度|たび}はご{f|希望|きぼう}<b>に{f|沿|そ}う</b>ことができず、{f|誠|まこと}に{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございません。

Wishes take <code>に{f|沿|そ}う</code>, and this whole sentence is standard business
Japanese for a refusal. The shorter
<code>ご{f|希望|きぼう}に{f|沿|そ}えず</code> says the same thing in one clause.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|予定表|よていひょう}に{f|沿|そ}った{f|進|すす}めます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|予定表|よていひょう}<b>に{f|沿|そ}って</b>{f|進|すす}めます。

The <code>{f|沿|そ}った</code> form modifies a *noun* -
{f|予定表|よていひょう}に{f|沿|そ}った{f|進|すす}め{f|方|かた}. To modify the verb you
need the て-form.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) マニュアルに{f|沿|そ}って{f|説明|せつめい}する。
(b) マニュアルに{f|従|したが}って{f|説明|せつめい}する。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) treats the manual as the outline of the explanation: you cover what it covers,
in the order it sets out, and you may put it in your own words. It is the neutral,
professional-sounding option.

(b) treats the manual as something you are obeying. It foregrounds compliance, and
in the wrong context it can carry a faint suggestion that you are doing it because
you were told to rather than because you judged it right.

</details>
