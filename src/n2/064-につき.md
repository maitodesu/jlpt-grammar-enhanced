# ～につき

**Meaning** · (1) owing to; on account of - the reason given on a notice; (2) per; for each; (3) regarding, in stiff written Japanese

`につき` does three unrelated-looking jobs, and the one you meet first depends
entirely on where you are reading. Taped to a shop door it means "because": 
<code>{f|準備中|じゅんびちゅう}につき</code>, "we're getting ready, so…". In a price
list it means "per": <code>{f|一人|ひとり}につき</code>. In a formal letter it means
"concerning". All three attach to a **noun**, never to a verb clause.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun of circumstance (cause) | noun + につき | {f|工事中|こうじちゅう} → {f|工事中|こうじちゅう}につき |
| な-adjective stem (cause) | stem + につき | {f|不在|ふざい} → {f|不在|ふざい}につき |
| Counter phrase (per) | quantity + につき | {f|一人|ひとり} → {f|一人|ひとり}につき |
| Noun (regarding) | noun + につき | この{f|件|けん} → この{f|件|けん}につき |
| Polite "regarding" | noun + につきまして | ご{f|注文|ちゅうもん} → ご{f|注文|ちゅうもん}につきまして |

<div class="note">

**Note** - there is no verb row, and that is the single most useful fact on this
page. <code>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っているにつき</code> is not Japanese. If your
reason is a clause, you need <code>ため</code> or `ので`; if you want `につき`, you
must compress the reason into a noun -
<code>{f|雨天|うてん}</code>, <code>{f|故障|こしょう}</code>,
<code>{f|工事中|こうじちゅう}</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|工事中|こうじちゅう}<b>につき</b>、{f|通行|つうこう}できません。
Closed to traffic owing to roadworks.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|一人様|ひとりさま}<b>につき</b>{f|一|ひと}つまでとさせていただきます。
Limit one per customer.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本日|ほんじつ}は{f|準備中|じゅんびちゅう}<b>につき</b>、しばらくお{f|待|ま}ちください。
We are still setting up today, so please bear with us a little longer.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|参加費|さんかひ}は{f|一人|ひとり}<b>につき</b>3000{f|円|えん}いただきます。
The fee is 3,000 yen per person.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨天|うてん}<b>につき</b>、{f|運動会|うんどうかい}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}に{f|延期|えんき}します。
Owing to rain, sports day is postponed to next week.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|用紙|ようし}は{f|一家庭|いちかてい}<b>につき</b>{f|一枚|いちまい}しか{f|配|くば}られません。
Only one of these forms is distributed per household.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}が{f|不在|ふざい}<b>につき</b>、あらためてご{f|連絡|れんらく}いたします。
The person handling this is away, so we will contact you again later.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}{f|接近|せっきん}<b>につき</b>、{f|明日|あす}の{f|営業|えいぎょう}は{f|午前中|ごぜんちゅう}で{f|終了|しゅうりょう}させていただきます。
With a typhoon approaching, we will be closing at midday tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|件|けん}<b>につきまして</b>は、{f|後日|ごじつ}{f|書面|しょめん}でご{f|回答|かいとう}いたします。
Regarding this matter, we will respond in writing at a later date.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The causal `につき` is notice Japanese. It lives on shop shutters, station boards,
municipal signs, printed apologies and business letters, and it is compact
precisely because those places have no room. Nobody says it over dinner. If a
colleague asks why you were out, the answer is
<code>{f|熱|ねつ}が{f|出|で}たので</code>, not
<code>{f|発熱|はつねつ}につき</code> - the latter would sound like you were reading
your own sick note aloud.

That formality is worth using deliberately. Writing
<code>{f|都合|つごう}によりお{f|休|やす}みします</code> on a hand-written note and
<code>{f|都合|つごう}につき</code> in a printed one are different acts of politeness;
`につき` signals institutional distance. In a personal apology, that distance can
read as cold.

The "per" sense is different in feel: it is neutral, unremarkable and used by
everyone. Prices, allowances, rations, dosages, quotas - anywhere a rate is being
stated - take `につき` without any sense of stiffness. This is the sense you are
most likely to need in ordinary life.

The "regarding" sense is the stiffest of the three, and in practice you will see it
mostly in the polite extended form <code>につきまして</code>, which is the business
register's version of `について`. Recognise it in correspondence; in speech, say
`について`.

## Contrast

### ～につき vs ～ため（に）

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both mean "owing to". <code>ため</code> takes a whole clause;
`につき` takes only a noun, and belongs to printed notices.

</div>

<pre>
{f|機械|きかい}が{f|故障|こしょう}している<b>ため</b>、{f|本日|ほんじつ}は{f|使用|しよう}できません。
Because the machine has broken down, it is unavailable today.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|故障|こしょう}<b>につき</b>{f|使用|しよう}{f|中止|ちゅうし}
Out of order - do not use
</pre>

Look at what happened to the sentence. <code>ため</code> keeps the verb and reads
like prose; `につき` compresses the same reason into two characters and reads like
a sign. When you are writing anything longer than a label,
<code>ため</code> is usually the better fit, and it is the only option if the
reason will not compress into a noun.

### ～につき vs ～あたり・～ごとに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `につき` and <code>あたり</code> both give a **rate** per unit.
<code>ごとに</code> gives an **interval** - every so many units, repeatedly.

</div>

<pre>
{f|一台|いちだい}<b>につき</b>{f|二人|ふたり}まで{f|乗|の}れます。
Up to two people per vehicle.
</pre>

<pre>
バスは20{f|分|ぷん}<b>ごとに</b>{f|出|で}ています。
The bus leaves every twenty minutes.
</pre>

<code>{f|一人|ひとり}あたり</code> and <code>{f|一人|ひとり}につき</code> are close
to interchangeable; <code>あたり</code> is a shade more statistical
(<code>{f|一人|ひとり}あたりのGDP</code>) and `につき` a shade more regulatory.
<code>ごとに</code> is not interchangeable with either - swap it into the first
sentence and you get "two more people every vehicle", which is nonsense.

### ～につき vs ～について

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `について` means "about, concerning" and is everyday. `につき`
means that only in formal writing - and it additionally carries the two meanings
`について` never has, "owing to" and "per".

</div>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|制度|せいど}に<b>ついて</b>{f|説明|せつめい}します。
I'll explain the new system.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|制度|せいど}<b>につきまして</b>ご{f|説明|せつめい}{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます。
Allow me to explain the new system to you.
</pre>

The pair are the same sentence at two altitudes. The trap runs the other way as
well: <code>{f|一人|ひとり}について2{f|枚|まい}</code> does not mean "two sheets per
person" - it reads as "two sheets about one person", which is not what any form
means to say. For rates, only `につき` will do.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っている<b>につき</b>、{f|中止|ちゅうし}します。 → ✅ {f|雨天|うてん}<b>につき</b>、{f|中止|ちゅうし}します。
  `につき` attaches to nouns only. Either compress the reason into a noun or switch
  to <code>ため</code>.

- ❌ {f|風邪|かぜ}<b>につき</b>、{f|今日|きょう}{f|休|やす}むね。 → ✅ {f|風邪|かぜ}をひいたから、{f|今日|きょう}{f|休|やす}むね。
  Signage register in a text message to a friend is comic. Keep `につき` for
  printed notices and business writing.

- ❌ {f|一人|ひとり}に<b>ついて</b>2{f|枚|まい}{f|配|くば}ります。 → ✅ {f|一人|ひとり}<b>につき</b>2{f|枚|まい}{f|配|くば}ります。
  The "per" meaning belongs to `につき` alone. `について` would turn the people into
  a topic.

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}<b>につき</b>{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}おうよ。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}に<b>ついて</b>{f|話|はな}し{f|合|あ}おうよ。
  In casual speech the "concerning" sense is always `について`. `につき` there sounds
  like a memo read out loud.

## Practice

**1.** バスにお{f|乗|の}りの{f|方|かた}は、{f|一人|ひとり}____{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|二|ふた}つまでです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一人|ひとり}<b>につき</b>{f|荷物|にもつ}は{f|二|ふた}つまでです。

This is a rate stated per unit, which is the everyday sense of `につき`.
<code>{f|一人|ひとり}あたり</code> would also be understood, though it sounds more
like a statistic than a rule.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|近|ちか}づいている（につき・ため）、{f|明日|あした}の{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|近|ちか}づいている<b>ため</b>、{f|明日|あした}の{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}です。

The reason is a verb clause, and `につき` cannot follow one. To use `につき` you
would have to compress it to a noun:
<code>{f|台風|たいふう}{f|接近|せっきん}につき</code>.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese as it would appear on a sign: "Closed today for renovations."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|本日|ほんじつ}は{f|改装工事|かいそうこうじ}<b>につき</b>{f|休業|きゅうぎょう}いたします。

Sign Japanese wants a noun reason plus `につき`, and
<code>{f|本日|ほんじつ}</code> rather than <code>{f|今日|きょう}</code> to match the
register. <code>いたします</code> keeps the humble tone notices use.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}が{f|休|やす}んでいるにつき、{f|明日|あした}{f|以降|いこう}のご{f|案内|あんない}になります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}が{f|不在|ふざい}<b>につき</b>、{f|明日|あした}{f|以降|いこう}のご{f|案内|あんない}になります。

<code>{f|休|やす}んでいる</code> is a verb clause, so it cannot take `につき`.
Replacing it with the noun <code>{f|不在|ふざい}</code> fixes the grammar and also
matches the formal register of the rest of the sentence.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|工事|こうじ}<b>につき</b>、この{f|道|みち}は{f|通|とお}れません。
(b) {f|工事|こうじ}をしている<b>ので</b>、この{f|道|みち}は{f|通|とお}れません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is what the barrier says. It is impersonal, printed, and gives the reason in
the fewest possible characters - nobody is speaking to you in particular.

(b) is what a person standing next to the barrier says to you. `ので` keeps the
verb and the softness, and it is the natural choice when you are actually
explaining something to someone face to face.

</details>
