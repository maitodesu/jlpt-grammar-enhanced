# ～に過ぎない

**Meaning** · no more than ～; merely ～ - the thing does not reach beyond this

<code>{f|過|す}ぎない</code> is "does not exceed". The pattern takes something the
listener might have taken seriously - a figure, a claim, a person's role - and caps it:
*that is as far as it goes*. It is not a neutral count. It is an argument, and the point
of making it is usually to stop someone drawing a bigger conclusion.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + にすぎない | {f|言|い}い{f|訳|わけ} → {f|言|い}い{f|訳|わけ}にすぎない |
| Noun (formal) | Noun + である + にすぎない | {f|一例|いちれい} → {f|一例|いちれい}であるにすぎない |
| Verb | plain form + にすぎない | {f|聞|き}いた → {f|聞|き}いたにすぎない |
| Verb (ている) | ～ている + にすぎない | {f|真似|まね}している → {f|真似|まね}しているにすぎない |
| Quantity | number + にすぎない | {f|三割|さんわり} → {f|三割|さんわり}にすぎない |
| Polite | … + にすぎません | {f|想像|そうぞう} → {f|想像|そうぞう}にすぎません |
| Mid-sentence | … + にすぎず、… | {f|一部|いちぶ} → {f|一部|いちぶ}にすぎず、… |

<div class="note">

**Note** - although the page title uses the kanji, running text usually writes
<code>にすぎない</code> in kana. Both are correct; the kana spelling is more common
outside legal and academic writing.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
それは{f|言|い}い{f|訳|わけ}<b>にすぎない</b>。
That's nothing but an excuse.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}は{f|見|み}たことを{f|話|はな}した<b>にすぎません</b>。
I merely reported what I saw.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|回答|かいとう}したのは{f|全体|ぜんたい}の{f|三割|さんわり}<b>にすぎない</b>。
Only thirty percent of them responded - no more than that.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}はただの{f|知|し}り{f|合|あ}い<b>にすぎない</b>ので、{f|詳|くわ}しいことは{f|知|し}らない。
He's no more than an acquaintance, so I don't know the details.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|値上|ねあ}げは、これから{f|続|つづ}く{f|変化|へんか}の{f|始|はじ}まり<b>にすぎない</b>だろう。
This price rise is probably just the beginning of changes still to come.
</pre>

<pre>
これは{f|問題|もんだい}の{f|一部|いちぶ}<b>にすぎず</b>、{f|本当|ほんとう}の{f|原因|げんいん}はもっと{f|深|ふか}いところにある。
This is only one part of the problem; the real cause lies deeper.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|仕事|しごと}をした<b>にすぎない</b>と{f|言|い}うが、あの{f|場面|ばめん}で{f|動|うご}けた{f|人|ひと}はほかにいなかった。
She says she was only doing her job, but nobody else in that moment was able to act.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|統計|とうけい}の{f|数字|すうじ}は{f|現実|げんじつ}の{f|一面|いちめん}を{f|示|しめ}している<b>にすぎず</b>、そこから{f|人|ひと}の{f|暮|く}らしまでは{f|見|み}えてこない。
Statistics show no more than one face of reality; they do not reveal how people actually live.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|多|おお}くの{f|人|ひと}が{f|反対|はんたい}していると{f|言|い}っても、{f|実際|じっさい}に{f|声|こえ}を{f|上|あ}げているのは{f|一部|いちぶ}の{f|人|ひと}たち<b>にすぎません</b>。
People say there is widespread opposition, but those actually raising their voices are only a small number.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a written-leaning, argumentative pattern. Editorials, essays, research summaries
and formal speeches are full of it, because its job is to *deflate a claim* - to say that
a figure is smaller, a person less important, an event less significant than it was made
to look. It appears in speech too, but in speech it sounds considered rather than casual;
you would not use it complaining about the weather.

Because it deflates, it is dismissive by nature. Applied to your own actions it is modest
- <code>{f|当然|とうぜん}のことをしたにすぎません</code> is a graceful way to wave off
thanks. Applied to someone else's work or opinion it is dismissive, and to their face it
is rude. <code>ご{f|意見|いけん}は{f|一|ひと}つの{f|見方|みかた}にすぎません</code>
would be heard as a put-down whatever your tone.

The connective form <code>にすぎず</code> is a written-style linker (the classical
negative <code>ず</code> doing the work of a て-form). It signals essay register very
strongly and would be odd in conversation.

<code>ただの～にすぎない</code> is an extremely common reinforcement, and so is putting
a bare number in front of it. Both are fine and neither is redundant.

## Contrast

### にすぎない vs だけだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>だけ</code> **counts**. <code>にすぎない</code>
**judges**: it says the amount or the role falls short of what someone assumed.

</div>

<pre>
{f|参加者|さんかしゃ}は{f|五人|ごにん}<b>でした</b>。
There were five participants.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|参加者|さんかしゃ}は{f|五人|ごにん}<b>にすぎませんでした</b>。
There were no more than five participants.
</pre>

The first is a count. The second implies a disappointment - five, when the hall was
booked for fifty. This is why <code>にすぎない</code> needs a background expectation to
push against; used where nobody expected anything in particular, it puzzles the reader.
It is also a register step up: <code>だけ</code> belongs to every level of Japanese,
<code>にすぎない</code> to careful prose.

### にすぎない vs でしかない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>にすぎない</code> caps a **quantity or degree** and reasons
about it. <code>でしかない</code> caps an **identity** and does it with feeling.

</div>

<pre>
{f|支持率|しじりつ}は{f|一割|いちわり}<b>にすぎない</b>。
Support stands at no more than ten percent.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}の{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|時間|じかん}の{f|無駄|むだ}<b>でしかない</b>。
The plan as it stands is nothing but a waste of time.
</pre>

Swap them and you can feel the change: a percentage with <code>でしかない</code> sounds
angry, and a flat verdict with <code>にすぎない</code> sounds analytical. Numbers and
verbs go to <code>にすぎない</code> - <code>でしかない</code> cannot take a plain verb
at all. Where both are possible, <code>にすぎない</code> is the cooler of the two.

### にすぎない vs にほかならない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same move in opposite directions. Both say "X is Y and nothing
else"; <code>にすぎない</code> **demotes** X, <code>にほかならない</code> **elevates**
it.

</div>

<pre>
あの{f|発言|はつげん}は{f|個人|こじん}の{f|感想|かんそう}<b>にすぎない</b>。
That remark was no more than one person's impression.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|発言|はつげん}は{f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|本音|ほんね}<b>にほかならない</b>。
That remark was nothing other than the company's real position.
</pre>

Grammatically they are twins - both take a noun and <code>に</code>. Rhetorically they
are opposites, and mixing them up reverses your argument, which makes this the more
dangerous confusion of the two on this page.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ それは{f|冗談|じょうだん}<b>をすぎない</b>。 → ✅ それは{f|冗談|じょうだん}<b>にすぎない</b>。
  The particle is <code>に</code>. <code>を</code> and <code>が</code> both turn up in
  learner writing and neither is possible.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}はまだ{f|学生|がくせい}<b>だにすぎない</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}はまだ{f|学生|がくせい}<b>にすぎない</b>。
  A noun attaches bare, or with <code>である</code> in formal writing. Never with
  <code>だ</code>.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}はアマチュア<b>にすぎる</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}はアマチュア<b>にすぎない</b>。
  <code>～すぎる</code> ("too much") and <code>にすぎない</code> ("no more than") are
  unrelated despite sharing a verb. The negative is not optional here.

- ❌ 「{f|先生|せんせい}のご{f|指摘|してき}は{f|一|ひと}つの{f|見方|みかた}<b>にすぎません</b>」 → ✅ 「{f|先生|せんせい}のご{f|指摘|してき}は{f|大変|たいへん}{f|参考|さんこう}になりました」
  Perfect grammar, badly misjudged. The pattern shrinks whatever it touches, so pointing
  it at a senior person's opinion is a rebuke.

## Practice

**1.** {f|事故|じこ}の{f|原因|げんいん}についての{f|話|はなし}は、まだ____{f|過|す}ぎない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

まだ<b>うわさに</b>{f|過|す}ぎない。

A noun attaches directly with <code>に</code>. The sentence is warning the listener not
to treat the story as established - exactly the deflating job this pattern does.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}で{f|決|き}まったのは{f|日程|にってい}（だけ・にすぎない）です。あとは{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|来週|らいしゅう}に{f|回|まわ}りました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|決|き}まったのは{f|日程|にってい}<b>だけ</b>です。

<code>にすぎない</code> cannot be followed by <code>です</code> - it is already a
predicate. If you want the deflating flavour, the sentence has to be rebuilt as
<code>{f|決|き}まったのは{f|日程|にってい}にすぎません</code>, which adds a note of
frustration that plain <code>だけ</code> does not carry.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "What I did was merely pass on what I had been told."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|私|わたし}は{f|聞|き}いたことを{f|伝|つた}えた<b>にすぎません</b>。

A verb attaches in its plain form, here the past. Used about yourself the pattern reads
as modest rather than dismissive - it declines credit for anything beyond the message.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|数字|すうじ}は{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|半分|はんぶん}にすぎなくて、{f|安心|あんしん}できない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|数字|すうじ}は{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|半分|はんぶん}<b>にすぎず</b>、{f|安心|あんしん}できない。

In this register the connective form is <code>にすぎず</code>, not
<code>にすぎなくて</code>. The <code>て</code>-form of <code>ない</code> sounds
conversational and clashes with the essay-like tone of the rest of the sentence.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|私|わたし}は{f|手伝|てつだ}っただけです。
(b) {f|私|わたし}は{f|手伝|てつだ}ったにすぎません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply states what you did. It is what you would say when someone asks whether you
organised the event, and it carries no particular attitude.

(b) actively pushes back on the credit you are being given: whatever it looked like, your
role did not extend beyond helping. It is more formal, more deliberate, and slightly more
insistent - the sentence of someone declining praise rather than answering a question.

</details>
