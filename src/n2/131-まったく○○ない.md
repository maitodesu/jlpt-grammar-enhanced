# まったく～ない

**Meaning** · not at all; not in the slightest - the neutral, all-register absolute negative

{f|全|まった}く is the word you reach for when you want a negative to be
**complete** and you do not want the sentence to sound casual. It works in speech,
in email, in a legal notice and in an academic paper alike, which is what separates
it from ちっとも. Written {f|全|まった}く or in kana as まったく; both are common,
and the kana spelling is a little softer on the eye in informal writing.

**What is denied: the proposition, in full.** {f|全|まった}く does not touch an
inference and it does not carve out exceptions. Where
<code>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> says "not always" and
<code>わけではない</code> says "that does not follow", {f|全|まった}く～ない says
"none of this, at any point". If you find yourself wanting to add "well, except…",
you have chosen the wrong word.

Be warned early: this same adverb has a second job in affirmative sentences, where
it means "entirely". That double life is the whole difficulty of the word.

## Formation

{f|全|まった}く is an adverb and never changes shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain) | {f|全|まった}く + ない-form | {f|知|し}る → {f|全|まった}く{f|知|し}らない |
| Verb (polite) | {f|全|まった}く + ません | {f|覚|おぼ}える → {f|全|まった}く{f|覚|おぼ}えていません |
| い-adjective | {f|全|まった}く + ～くない | {f|痛|いた}い → {f|全|まった}く{f|痛|いた}くない |
| な-adjective・Noun | {f|全|まった}く + ～ではない | {f|平気|へいき} → {f|全|まった}く{f|平気|へいき}ではない |
| Existence | {f|全|まった}く + ない／ありません | {f|興味|きょうみ} → {f|興味|きょうみ}が{f|全|まった}くない |
| Intensified, formal | {f|全|まった}くもって + negative | - → {f|全|まった}くもって{f|理解|りかい}できない |
| **Affirmative** sense | {f|全|まった}く + {f|同|おな}じ／{f|違|ちが}う／{f|逆|ぎゃく}／その{f|通|とお}り | {f|同|おな}じ → {f|全|まった}く{f|同|おな}じだ |
| **Prenominal** sense | {f|全|まった}くの + Noun | {f|偶然|ぐうぜん} → {f|全|まった}くの{f|偶然|ぐうぜん} |
| Interjection | {f|全|まった}く（もう） | - → {f|全|まった}く、{f|困|こま}った{f|人|ひと}だ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the affirmative rows are not a different word. {f|全|まった}く means
"to the full extent" in every row; a negative predicate simply takes that extent
down to zero. But the two readings pull sentences apart in practice, so the
Contrast section below is worth reading twice.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|意味|いみ}が<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|分|わ}から<b>ない</b>。
I do not understand the meaning at all.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|人|ひと}のことは<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|知|し}り<b>ません</b>。
I do not know that person at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事故|じこ}の{f|前後|ぜんご}のことは<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えて<b>いない</b>。
I have no memory whatsoever of what happened around the accident.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ありませんが、その{f|分野|ぶんや}には<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|自信|じしん}が<b>ございません</b>。
I am sorry, but I have no confidence in that field at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|件|けん}について、{f|会社|かいしゃ}からは<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|説明|せつめい}が<b>ない</b>。
The company has offered no explanation at all regarding this matter.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|検査|けんさ}の{f|結果|けっか}、{f|異常|いじょう}は<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|認|みと}められ<b>ませんでした</b>。
The examination found no abnormality of any kind.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|十年|じゅうねん}ぶりに{f|会|あ}ったのに、{f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|話|はな}し{f|方|かた}は<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|変|か}わって<b>いなかった</b>。
We had not met for ten years, and yet the way she spoke had not changed in the slightest.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|規則|きそく}は{f|現場|げんば}の{f|事情|じじょう}を<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|考慮|こうりょ}して<b>いない</b>と{f|言|い}わざるを{f|得|え}ない。
One is forced to say that this rule takes no account whatsoever of conditions on the ground.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

{f|全|まった}く is the register-neutral choice in this family. It is equally at home
in 「{f|全|まった}く{f|知|し}らなかった」 between friends and in
「{f|全|まった}く{f|該当|がいとう}いたしません」 in a formal reply. That breadth is
its main practical advantage: when you are unsure whether ちっとも is too casual for
the setting, {f|全|まった}く is never wrong.

{f|全|まった}くもって is a stiffer, more emphatic variant that belongs to formal
speech and writing - {f|全|まった}くもって{f|同感|どうかん}です, "I could not agree
more". It is not archaic, but it is noticeably weighty, and overusing it sounds
theatrical.

Then there is the bare interjection. 「{f|全|まった}く。」 or
「{f|全|まった}くもう。」 on its own is an exasperated "honestly!", muttered about
someone who has done something annoying. Learners meet it in drama subtitles and
misread it as agreement. It leans casual, and it is one of those words a listener
can hear as either affectionate exasperation or genuine annoyance depending
entirely on delivery.

Finally, 「{f|全|まった}くです」 is a set reply meaning "quite so, absolutely" - an
affirmative, agreeing with what the other person just said. It has no negative in
it at all.

## Contrast

### {f|全|まった}く～ない vs {f|全|まった}く + affirmative

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - {f|全|まった}く means "100%". Put a negative after it and the
100% lands on the negation ("not at all"); put an affirmative after it and the 100%
lands on the adjective ("completely"). Nothing else changes.

</div>

<pre>
{f|二人|ふたり}の{f|意見|いけん}は<b>{f|全|まった}く{f|違|ちが}う</b>。
Their two opinions are completely different.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|二人|ふたり}の{f|意見|いけん}は<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|違|ちが}わ<b>ない</b>。
Their two opinions do not differ in the slightest.
</pre>

The affirmative use is not free: {f|全|まった}く happily modifies
{f|同|おな}じ, {f|違|ちが}う, {f|逆|ぎゃく}, {f|別|べつ}, {f|新|あたら}しい and その{f|通|とお}り,
but it is not a general-purpose "very". ❌<code>{f|全|まった}く{f|速|はや}い</code>
is not Japanese.

The trap worth memorising is
<code>{f|全|まった}く{f|同|おな}じではない</code>. It reads as "not *exactly* the
same" - the 100% attaches to {f|同|おな}じ and then that is denied - and **not** as
"not the same at all". If you mean the latter, say
<code>{f|全|まった}く{f|違|ちが}う</code>.

### {f|全|まった}く vs ちっとも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning in the negative; {f|全|まった}く is
**register-free**, ちっとも is **spoken and complaining**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昨年度|さくねんど}の{f|指摘|してき}は<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|改善|かいぜん}され<b>ていない</b>。
The points raised last year have not been addressed at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|言|い}ったのに<b>ちっとも</b>{f|直|なお}し<b>てくれない</b>んだよ。
I told them, and they will not fix a single thing.
</pre>

Swap them and both sentences sound wrong for their setting - the first becomes a
grumble in a document, the second becomes oddly official between friends.
{f|少|すこ}しも sits between the two: usable in writing, slightly more emphatic than
{f|全|まった}く.

### {f|全|まった}く～ない vs {f|必|かなら}ずしも～とは{f|限|かぎ}らない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - {f|全|まった}く～ない denies the claim **everywhere**.
{f|必|かなら}ずしも～とは{f|限|かぎ}らない denies only that it holds **every time**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}と{f|品質|ひんしつ}は<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|関係|かんけい}が<b>ない</b>。
Price and quality have nothing to do with each other.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|高|たか}いものが{f|必|かなら}ずしもよい<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。
Expensive things are not always good.
</pre>

The first is a strong, arguable claim about the world; the second concedes the
general tendency and only removes the word "always". Learners who reach for
{f|全|まった}く when they mean "not necessarily" end up asserting far more than they
believe - and then have to defend it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|説明|せつめい}を{f|聞|き}いたが、<b>{f|全|まった}く{f|分|わ}かる</b>。 → ✅ <b>{f|全|まった}く{f|分|わ}からない</b>。
  In the "not at all" sense the negative is compulsory. Without it the sentence
  reads as an odd attempt at "I completely understand", for which you want
  <code>よく{f|分|わ}かりました</code>.

- ❌ この{f|二|ふた}つは<b>{f|全|まった}く{f|同|おな}じではない</b>。（meaning "these are nothing alike"） → ✅ この{f|二|ふた}つは<b>{f|全|まった}く{f|違|ちが}う</b>。
  {f|全|まった}く{f|同|おな}じではない means "not quite identical", which is nearly
  the opposite of what was intended.

- ❌ <b>{f|全|まった}くの{f|知|し}らない{f|人|ひと}</b>に{f|声|こえ}をかけられた。 → ✅ <b>{f|全|まった}く{f|知|し}らない{f|人|ひと}</b>／<b>{f|全|まった}くの{f|他人|たにん}</b>に{f|声|こえ}をかけられた。
  <code>{f|全|まった}くの</code> attaches to a bare noun. In front of a verb
  clause the adverb stays bare.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|全|まった}く{f|急|いそ}いだ</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}は<b>{f|全力|ぜんりょく}で{f|急|いそ}いだ</b>。
  {f|全|まった}く is not a general intensifier for verbs. It scales a negation, or
  it modifies a small set of words like {f|同|おな}じ and {f|違|ちが}う.

## Practice

**1.** {f|会議|かいぎ}で{f|反対|はんたい}した{f|人|ひと}は____いませんでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会議|かいぎ}で{f|反対|はんたい}した{f|人|ひと}は<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>いませんでした。

A meeting report calls for the register-free adverb. ちっとも would be too
conversational here, and {f|一人|ひとり}も is also good if you want to count heads
rather than deny the whole category.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|安|やす}い{f|店|みせ}の{f|料理|りょうり}が（{f|全|まった}くおいしくない・{f|必|かなら}ずしもおいしいとは{f|限|かぎ}らない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|安|やす}い{f|店|みせ}の{f|料理|りょうり}が<b>{f|必|かなら}ずしもおいしいとは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。

The sentence is about a general tendency having exceptions, not about cheap food
being uniformly bad. {f|全|まった}くおいしくない would be a blanket claim that every
cheap restaurant is inedible.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I had absolutely no idea that he had quit."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}が{f|辞|や}めたことを<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|知|し}り<b>ませんでした</b>。

"Absolutely no idea" is the total-negation slot, and {f|全|まった}く fits any
politeness level. In casual speech
<code>{f|全然|ぜんぜん}{f|知|し}らなかった</code> is the everyday equivalent.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}の{f|説明|せつめい}と{f|私|わたし}の{f|記憶|きおく}は{f|全|まった}くの{f|違|ちが}う。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}の{f|説明|せつめい}と{f|私|わたし}の{f|記憶|きおく}は<b>{f|全|まった}く{f|違|ちが}う</b>。

<code>{f|全|まった}くの</code> is the prenominal shape and needs a noun after it
（{f|全|まった}くの{f|別人|べつじん}）. {f|違|ちが}う is a verb, so the plain adverb
{f|全|まった}く is what modifies it.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|意見|いけん}に<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|賛成|さんせい}でき<b>ない</b>。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|意見|いけん}に{f|賛成|さんせい}できる<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a flat refusal covering the whole of his position. There is no part of it
the speaker accepts, and the sentence invites a counter-argument.

(b) does not refuse anything yet. It says agreement is not guaranteed - the speaker
might agree, might not, and is warning the listener against assuming. In a meeting
(a) opens a fight and (b) reserves a position.

</details>
