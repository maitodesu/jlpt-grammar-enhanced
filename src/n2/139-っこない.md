# ～っこない

**Meaning** · there's no chance of ～; ～ couldn't possibly happen - flat, spoken impossibility

`っこない` is what you say when the idea is not worth discussing. It bolts onto the
ます-stem of a verb and declares the thing impossible, with none of the hedging that
<code>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> or <code>わけではない</code> carry. It is
conversational, it is emphatic, and it is often a little defeatist.

**What is denied: the possibility itself.** Set it against the rest of this batch and
the difference is stark. <code>わけではない</code> withdraws an inference;
<code>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> removes the word "always"; `っこない` says the event
will not occur, full stop, and dares the listener to argue. Because it is so
absolute, its casualness is what keeps it socially survivable - it sounds like
grumbling, not like a formal verdict.

## Formation

It attaches to the **ます-stem** and to nothing else. No adjectives, no nouns.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| う-verb | ます-stem + っこない | {f|分|わ}かる → {f|分|わ}かりっこない |
| る-verb | ます-stem + っこない | {f|覚|おぼ}える → {f|覚|おぼ}えっこない |
| Potential form | potential ます-stem + っこない | {f|勝|か}てる → {f|勝|か}てっこない |
| する | し + っこない | できる → できっこない |
| {f|来|く}る | {f|来|き} + っこない | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|き}っこない |
| ある | あり + っこない | ある → ありっこない |
| Polite | … + っこないです／っこありません | {f|届|とど}く → {f|届|とど}きっこありません |
| Common partner | そんなこと／なんて + … + っこない | できる → そんなことできっこない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - う-verbs and る-verbs are also called {f|五段|ごだん} (godan, Group 1) and {f|一段|いちだん} (ichidan, Group 2). The
form you need is the one you would use before ます, so
{f|走|はし}る → {f|走|はし}り, {f|食|た}べる → {f|食|た}べ. The two irregulars follow
the same rule: する → し, {f|来|く}る → {f|来|き}. Note the reading -
<code>{f|来|き}っこない</code>, not こ.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
こんな{f|問題|もんだい}、{f|分|わ}かり<b>っこない</b>。
There's no way I'll get a question like this.
</pre>

<pre>
あのチームに{f|勝|か}て<b>っこない</b>よ。
We haven't a hope against that team.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}から{f|走|はし}っても、{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}い<b>っこない</b>。
Even if we run now, we're never going to make it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|宝|たから}くじなんて{f|当|あ}たり<b>っこない</b>と{f|思|おも}っていた。
I always assumed there was no chance of winning the lottery.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|嘘|うそ}をついてもすぐにばれるんだから、うまくいき<b>っこない</b>。
Lying will be found out straight away, so it can't possibly work.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|漢字|かんじ}を{f|三日|みっか}で{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|覚|おぼ}えられ<b>っこない</b>よ。
There is no way you can learn all the kanji in three days.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}でこんな{f|量|りょう}、{f|運|はこ}べ<b>っこありません</b>。
I could not possibly carry this much on my own.
</pre>

<pre>
あんなに{f|急|きゅう}に{f|言|い}われても、{f|準備|じゅんび}なんてでき<b>っこない</b>じゃないですか。
Sprung on us like that, there was no way we could have got ready, was there?
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`っこない` is **spoken Japanese**, and it stays spoken even when you dress it up.
<code>っこありません</code> exists and is grammatical, but it keeps the flavour of
the plain form, so it lands as an emphatic, slightly emotional protest rather than a
neutral statement. In a report, an email to a client or an exam essay, write
<code>{f|不可能|ふかのう}です</code>, <code>できるはずがありません</code> or
<code>{f|考|かんが}えにくいです</code> instead.

It is neutral as to who says it - all ages and genders use it - but it is
*attitudinally* marked. Depending on the sentence it comes out as resignation
（「{f|受|う}かりっこない」）, as dismissal（「そんな{f|話|はなし}、ありっこない」）or as
protest（「できっこないじゃないですか」）. What it never is, is measured. Telling a
junior colleague 「{f|君|きみ}にはできっこない」 is not a prediction, it is an insult.

It loves company. なんて, そんなこと and どうせ turn up with it constantly, because
they carry the same dismissive posture: 「どうせ{f|勝|か}てっこない」, "we're going to
lose anyway".

A useful softener, if you want the same idea without the sting, is
<code>{f|難|むずか}しいと{f|思|おも}います</code>. Japanese conversation reaches for
that far more often than for a bare impossibility claim, and a learner who deploys
`っこない` at work will sound harsher than they realise.

## Contrast

### ～っこない vs ～はずがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>はずがない</code> reasons: given what I know, that cannot
be so. `っこない` asserts: that is simply not happening.

</div>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さんが{f|忘|わす}れる<b>はずがない</b>。いつも{f|手帳|てちょう}に{f|書|か}いているから。
Tanaka can't have forgotten - he writes everything in his diary.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さんが{f|忘|わす}れ<b>っこない</b>って。
There's no way Tanaka forgot, come on.
</pre>

<code>はずがない</code> invites you to check the reasoning and works in writing;
`っこない` offers no reasoning and belongs to speech. There is also a plain
grammatical difference: <code>はずがない</code> follows a full plain-form clause and
accepts adjectives and nouns（{f|静|しず}かなはずがない）, while `っこない` only ever
follows a verb stem.

### ～っこない vs ～わけがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - near-identical meaning. <code>わけがない</code> is
**register-flexible**; `っこない` is **spoken only**, and blunter.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|値段|ねだん}で{f|本物|ほんもの}の<b>わけがない</b>。
There's no way this is genuine at this price.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|値段|ねだん}で{f|本物|ほんもの}であり<b>っこない</b>。
No chance this is the real thing at this price.
</pre>

Both deny the possibility outright. <code>わけがない</code> can open a paragraph in
an opinion column; `っこない` cannot. When in doubt in writing, use
<code>わけがない</code> - it says the same thing and costs you nothing in force.

### ～っこない vs ～ようがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `っこない` says the outcome **will not happen**.
<code>ようがない</code> says there is **no method available** to bring it about.

</div>

<pre>
{f|連絡先|れんらくさき}を{f|知|し}らないので、{f|知|し}らせ<b>ようがない</b>。
I don't have their contact details, so there's no way for me to let them know.
</pre>

<pre>
あんなに{f|遠|とお}くから、{f|声|こえ}なんて{f|聞|き}こえ<b>っこない</b>。
From that far away there's no chance of hearing a voice.
</pre>

<code>ようがない</code> is about missing means and is register-neutral; you would
write it in a business email without a second thought. `っこない` is about the
outcome being out of the question, and it stays in conversation.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ そんなところに<b>{f|行|い}くっこない</b>。 → ✅ そんなところに<b>{f|行|い}きっこない</b>。
  The base is the ます-stem, not the dictionary form. {f|行|い}く → {f|行|い}き.

- ❌ あの{f|人|ひと}が<b>{f|来|こ}っこない</b>。 → ✅ あの{f|人|ひと}が<b>{f|来|き}っこない</b>。
  {f|来|く}る is irregular, and the ます-stem is {f|来|き}. The こ- shape belongs to
  {f|来|こ}ない and {f|来|こ}よう, not here.

- ❌ こんな{f|話|はなし}が<b>{f|本当|ほんとう}っこない</b>。 → ✅ こんな{f|話|はなし}が<b>{f|本当|ほんとう}のわけがない</b>。
  `っこない` cannot attach to a noun or an adjective. For those, the わけがない and
  はずがない families are what you need.

- ❌（{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}に）その{f|方法|ほうほう}では{f|問題|もんだい}は<b>{f|解決|かいけつ}しっこありません</b>。 → ✅ その{f|方法|ほうほう}では{f|問題|もんだい}は<b>{f|解決|かいけつ}できないと{f|考|かんが}えられます</b>。
  Polite morphology does not make the word formal. In writing, state the
  impossibility with a neutral expression.

## Practice

**1.** {f|一晩|ひとばん}で{f|百|ひゃく}ページも{f|読|よ}め____よ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一晩|ひとばん}で{f|百|ひゃく}ページも{f|読|よ}め<b>っこない</b>よ。

The potential form {f|読|よ}める gives the stem {f|読|よ}め, and `っこない` bolts
straight onto it. The よ at the end fits the casual register the pattern lives in.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|鍵|かぎ}をなくしてしまったので、{f|中|なか}に（{f|入|はい}りっこない・{f|入|はい}りようがない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|中|なか}に<b>{f|入|はい}りようがない</b>。

The sentence gives a reason - there is no key - so what is missing is a *method*.
That is <code>ようがない</code> territory.
<code>{f|入|はい}りっこない</code> would be a flat prediction that nobody is getting
in, which does not follow so neatly from a lost key.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "A beginner could never win against a professional."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|初心者|しょしんしゃ}がプロに{f|勝|か}て<b>っこない</b>。

The potential stem {f|勝|か}て takes `っこない` directly. If you needed this in
writing, <code>{f|勝|か}てるはずがない</code> would carry the same meaning without the
conversational tone.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|明日|あした}の{f|朝|あさ}までに{f|終|お}わるっこないです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|明日|あした}の{f|朝|あさ}までに<b>{f|終|お}わりっこないです</b>。

<code>{f|終|お}わる</code> has to go to its ます-stem, {f|終|お}わり, before `っこない`
attaches. Adding です is acceptable in speech, but note that it does not make the
sentence suitable for a written report.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}に<b>できっこない</b>。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}にできる<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) writes him off. It is a spoken, emotional dismissal with no reasoning attached,
and said in his hearing it would be taken as an insult.

(b) merely refuses a guarantee. It concedes that he might well manage it and only
objects to the assumption that he certainly will - the kind of thing you can say in a
planning meeting without offending anyone.

</details>
