# ～ていては

**Meaning** · if you carry on doing that, it will not work out

Take the `ては` of "if that happens, we have a problem" and build it on `ている`
instead of the plain て-form. The condition is no longer a single act but a habit or a
state that is **already running**, and the sentence says: keep this up and here is the
wall you hit.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | ている-form + は | {f|待|ま}つ → {f|待|ま}っていては |
| Verb (う-verb in ん / い sound) | ～でいる + は | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}んでいては |
| Noun / state, with いる | Noun + でいては | {f|学生|がくせい}のまま → {f|学生|がくせい}のままでいては |
| Casual contraction | ていては → てちゃ | {f|待|ま}っていては → {f|待|ま}ってちゃ |
| Casual contraction | でいては → でちゃ | {f|休|やす}んでいては → {f|休|やす}んでちゃ |
| Polite consequence | ～ていては + ～ません | {f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}いません |

<div class="note">

**Note** - a common written variant is <code>～ていたのでは</code>, which says the same
thing a little more argued and a little less scolding. Both take the same negative
consequence.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`ていては` is aimed at behaviour the speaker can see. That makes it, more often than
not, a scolding or a piece of advice handed down: a parent to a child, a supervisor to
a new hire, a coach to a player. Used upward - to a boss, to a customer - it is
presumptuous, because you are telling them their current conduct is a mistake. Between
equals it is fine if the tone is warm, and it is very common in self-talk:
<code>こんなことで{f|悩|なや}んでいては、{f|前|まえ}に{f|進|すす}めない</code>.

The contraction is where learners come unstuck. `ていては` becomes `てちゃ`, and
`でいては` becomes `でちゃ` - the `い` disappears along with everything else. So
<code>{f|寝|ね}てちゃだめだよ</code> is "you mustn't be lying there asleep", built on
<code>{f|寝|ね}ている</code>, while <code>{f|寝|ね}ちゃだめだよ</code> is "you mustn't go
to sleep", built on the plain て-form. One syllable, two different warnings. Women's and
men's speech use both freely; the sentence-final particle - `よ`, `ぞ`, `わよ` - carries
whatever gender colouring there is, not the contraction itself.

In writing, `ていては` shows up constantly in opinion pieces, where the ongoing thing is
a national habit rather than a person's: 「{f|従来|じゅうらい}のやり{f|方|かた}を{f|続|つづ}けていては、{f|競争|きょうそう}に{f|勝|か}てない」.
There it is neutral and carries no scolding at all.

## Contrast

### ～ていては vs ～ては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ては` puts up one act as a sample. `ていては` says the act is a
**pattern already in progress**, and the trouble comes from its continuing.

</div>

<pre>
{f|夜中|よなか}に{f|甘|あま}いものを{f|食|た}べ<b>ては</b>、{f|太|ふと}るのも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ。
Eating sweets in the middle of the night, of course you put on weight.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|夜中|よなか}に{f|甘|あま}いものを{f|食|た}べ<b>ていては</b>、いくら{f|運動|うんどう}しても{f|意味|いみ}がない。
As long as you keep eating sweets at midnight, no amount of exercise will help.
</pre>

Both criticise, but the second one is about a habit the speaker knows is still going
on, and the consequence is framed as unavoidable *while it lasts*. Where you can insert
"as long as you keep …", `ていては` is the better fit.

### ～ていては vs ～ていたら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ていたら` is a neutral conditional and can end anywhere.
`ていては` is locked to a bad outcome.

</div>

<pre>
{f|駅前|えきまえ}で{f|待|ま}っ<b>ていたら</b>、{f|偶然|ぐうぜん}{f|昔|むかし}の{f|友人|ゆうじん}に{f|会|あ}った。
I was waiting in front of the station and happened to run into an old friend.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|駅前|えきまえ}で{f|待|ま}っ<b>ていては</b>、{f|向|む}こうが{f|見|み}つけられませんよ。
If you stand waiting in front of the station, they'll never spot you.
</pre>

`ていたら` also does the "I was doing X and then discovered Y" job, which `ていては`
cannot touch at all. And `ていたら` can be followed by a request; `ていては` cannot.

### ～ていては vs ～ようでは

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ていては` blames an **ongoing action**. `ようでは` treats a state
of affairs as **evidence** that the person or plan is not up to it.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|程度|ていど}の{f|作業|さぎょう}に{f|一日|いちにち}かかっ<b>ていては</b>、{f|納期|のうき}が{f|守|まも}れない。
If a job this size keeps taking a whole day, we can't meet the delivery date.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|程度|ていど}の{f|作業|さぎょう}に{f|一日|いちにち}もかかる<b>ようでは</b>、{f|一人前|いちにんまえ}とは{f|言|い}えない。
If it takes you a whole day for a job this size, you can't call yourself trained yet.
</pre>

The first is practical - the schedule breaks. The second is a verdict on the person,
read off the slow work as a symptom. `ようでは` looks down; `ていては` looks forward.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|待|ま}つ<b>ていては</b>{f|遅|おそ}くなる。 → ✅ {f|待|ま}っ<b>ていては</b>{f|遅|おそ}くなる。
  The base is the ている-form, so the verb has to reach its て-form first.
  <code>{f|待|ま}つ</code> → <code>{f|待|ま}って</code> → <code>{f|待|ま}っている</code>.

- ❌ そんなところで{f|寝|ね}<b>ていちゃ</b>だめだよ。 → ✅ そんなところで{f|寝|ね}<b>てちゃ</b>だめだよ。
  The contraction swallows the `い` as well: `ていては` → `てちゃ`, never `ていちゃ`.

- ❌ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}し<b>ていては</b>、きっと{f|上手|じょうず}になる。 → ✅ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}し<b>ていれば</b>、きっと{f|上手|じょうず}になる。
  A good result cancels the pattern. Use `ていれば` or `ていたら` when the continuing
  action is a *good* thing.

- ❌ {f|無理|むり}をし<b>ていては</b>、{f|少|すこ}し{f|休|やす}んでください。 → ✅ {f|無理|むり}をし<b>ているなら</b>、{f|少|すこ}し{f|休|やす}んでください。
  A request cannot follow `ていては`. The pattern ends in a statement of what goes
  wrong, not in an instruction.

## Practice

**1.** {f|辞書|じしょ}を{f|一|ひと}つずつ{f|調|しら}べ____、{f|試験|しけん}{f|時間|じかん}が{f|足|た}りなくなりますよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|調|しら}べ<b>ていては</b>（casual {f|調|しら}べてちゃ）

Looking every word up is presented as an ongoing method the listener is using, and
running out of exam time is the wall it leads to - the exact job of `ていては`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|窓|まど}の{f|外|そと}を{f|見|み}（ていては・ていたら）、{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|見|み}<b>ていたら</b>、{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}り{f|出|だ}した。

Nothing bad happens here - the speaker simply notices something while looking out.
`ていては` demands a negative consequence, so it cannot report a discovery.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If you keep worrying about what other people think, you'll never decide anything."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|人|ひと}の{f|目|め}ばかり{f|気|き}にし<b>ていては</b>、{f|何|なに}も{f|決|き}められない。

<code>{f|気|き}にする</code> becomes <code>{f|気|き}にしている</code> before `は`, and
ばかり reinforces that the worrying is a settled habit rather than one occasion.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}っていては、{f|自分|じぶん}でやってみてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}っている<b>なら</b>、{f|自分|じぶん}でやってみてください。

The problem is the request. `ていては` can only be completed by a statement of the bad
result; to tell the complainer to try it themselves you need `なら` or `たら`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会議中|かいぎちゅう}にスマホを{f|見|み}<b>ちゃ</b>だめだよ。
(b) {f|会議中|かいぎちゅう}にスマホを{f|見|み}<b>てちゃ</b>だめだよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) contracts <code>{f|見|み}ては</code> and forbids the act itself: don't look at your
phone during the meeting at all.

(b) contracts <code>{f|見|み}ていては</code> and is said to someone whose eyes are on
the screen right now - "you can't be sitting there staring at your phone". It reports
an ongoing state and tells them to stop it.

</details>
