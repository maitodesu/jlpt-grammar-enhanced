# どうせ

**Meaning** · anyway; whatever happens - said of an outcome the speaker has already
given up on

`どうせ` marks what follows as a foregone conclusion. The facts it states could be
stated without it; what `どうせ` adds is the speaker's resignation, and that
attitude is never optional.

## Usage

`どうせ` is an adverb, and it normally stands at the head of the clause whose
outcome is already settled. It is not the conclusion itself - it is the premise
that the conclusion rests on, so it almost always appears with a second clause.

Three frames carry most of its work.

**どうせ～んだから / のだから + so there is no point.** The commonest shape:
<code>どうせ{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わないんだから、{f|走|はし}っても{f|無駄|むだ}だよ</code>.

**どうせ～なら / どうせ～のなら + so let's do it properly.** Here the tone flips
from defeat to decision: if the thing is happening either way, choose the better
version of it. <code>どうせやるなら{f|楽|たの}しくやろう</code>.

**どうせ + a dismissal.** With no second clause the sentence is pure resignation,
often about oneself: <code>どうせ{f|私|わたし}には{f|無理|むり}だよ</code>. The set
phrase <code>どうせ{f|私|わたし}なんて</code> is a small performance of self-pity and
is used, half-jokingly, all the time.

It often appears with に{f|決|き}まっている, だろう, or a negative predicate,
because those are the shapes a foregone conclusion naturally takes.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>どうせ</b>{f|明日|あした}やるんだから、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|休|やす}もう。
We're going to do it tomorrow anyway, so let's rest today.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>どうせ</b>{f|買|か}うなら、{f|長|なが}く{f|使|つか}えるものがいい。
If I'm buying one anyway, I'd rather have something that lasts.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|急|いそ}いで{f|行|い}っても、<b>どうせ</b>{f|開店|かいてん}まで{f|外|そと}で{f|待|ま}たされるよ。
Even if we hurry, they'll just make us wait outside until opening time anyway.
</pre>

<pre>
「{f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|相談|そうだん}してみたら?」「いや、<b>どうせ</b>だめだと{f|言|い}われるに{f|決|き}まってる。」
"Why not talk to the boss about it?" "Nah - he's bound to say no anyway."
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Colloquial. `どうせ` is a spoken word; formal writing uses
<code>いずれにしても</code> or <code>いずれにせよ</code> for the neutral part of the
meaning and simply omits the attitude.

That attitude is the thing to handle carefully. Aimed at yourself it is
self-deprecating and often endearing. Aimed at *someone else's* effort it is
dismissive - <code>どうせ{f|無理|むり}だよ</code> said to a person who is trying
tells them not to bother, and it stings. Aimed at a customer or a superior it is
close to unusable.

The <code>どうせ～なら</code> frame is the exception, and a genuinely useful one for
learners. There the resignation has been converted into a decision, and the
sentence comes out sounding positive: since it is happening regardless, we may as
well enjoy it. Nothing about that frame is rude.

Note also that `どうせ` needs the outcome to be *settled*, not merely likely. A
weather forecast is a guess, not a fate, so `たぶん` covers it and `どうせ` does not.

## Contrast

### どうせ vs どのみち・どっちみち

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `どのみち` is **logical**: whichever route you take, you end up
at the same place. `どうせ` is **emotional**: the ending is fixed and the speaker
has stopped fighting it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|歩|ある}いてもタクシーに{f|乗|の}っても、<b>どのみち</b>{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わない。
Walking or taking a taxi, either way we won't make it.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>どうせ</b>{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わないよ。{f|連絡|れんらく}だけしておこう。
We're not going to make it anyway. Let's just send word.
</pre>

`どのみち` needs two or more options in view, stated or implied, and it is
neutral - a manager can say it in a meeting. `どうせ` needs no options at all,
because for the speaker there was never a real choice. `どっちみち` is the more
casual pronunciation of `どのみち`, and <code>いずれにしても</code> is the formal
equivalent of both.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ どうせ{f|明日|あした}は{f|晴|は}れるでしょう。 → ✅ <b>たぶん</b>{f|明日|あした}は{f|晴|は}れるでしょう。
  A forecast is a guess. `どうせ` requires an outcome the speaker treats as already
  decided, and it drags in a resignation the sentence does not want.

- ❌ お{f|客様|きゃくさま}、どうせ{f|明日|あした}お{f|届|とど}けいたします。 → ✅ お{f|客様|きゃくさま}、<b>いずれにしても</b>{f|明日|あした}お{f|届|とど}けいたします。
  To a customer the resigned tone is out of place, and the word is too casual for
  the humble form around it.

- ❌ どうせ{f|頑張|がんば}ってください。 → ✅ <b>とにかく</b>{f|頑張|がんば}ってください。
  `どうせ` cannot introduce a request or encouragement - it says the result is fixed,
  which undercuts the very thing you are asking for.

## Practice

**1.** ____{f|同|おな}じお{f|金|かね}を{f|払|はら}うなら、{f|景色|けしき}のいい{f|部屋|へや}に{f|泊|と}まりたい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>どうせ</b>{f|同|おな}じお{f|金|かね}を{f|払|はら}うなら…

This is the <code>どうせ～なら</code> frame. The cost is fixed whatever you choose,
so the speaker turns that fixed fact into an argument for choosing well. The tone
here is upbeat, not defeated.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: {f|電車|でんしゃ}でもバスでも、（どうせ・どのみち）{f|一時間|いちじかん}はかかります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|電車|でんしゃ}でもバスでも、<b>どのみち</b>{f|一時間|いちじかん}はかかります。

Two routes are on the table and both lead to the same travel time, which is exactly
what `どのみち` says. `どうせ` would add a note of "not that it matters", turning a
neutral piece of information into a complaint.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|私|わたし}には{f|無理|むり}です。
(b) どうせ{f|私|わたし}には{f|無理|むり}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain assessment - the task is beyond the speaker, and that is all.

(b) adds a history. `どうせ` says the speaker had already written the outcome off
before anyone asked, and it invites the listener to notice the resignation. Between
friends it reads as fishing for reassurance; in a work conversation it reads as
sulking, which is why it is a risky sentence to say to a manager.

</details>
