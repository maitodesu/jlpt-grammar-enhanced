# ～だけのことはある

**Meaning** · it lives up to it; no wonder - the money, the effort or the reputation behind this thing has been repaid

<code>だけのことはある</code> ends a sentence with a verdict. You have seen the
result - the knife cuts, the queue was worth it, the training shows - and you sign
off on it: *there was something in all that after all*. The premise is always an
investment or a reputation, and the pattern's job is to declare it honoured. It is a
compliment, and it is normally said out loud to or about someone else.

## Formation

The clause in front is in noun-modifying shape. The whole thing sits at the end of
the sentence, so the tense and politeness ride on `ある`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + だけのことはある | {f|練習|れんしゅう}した → {f|練習|れんしゅう}しただけのことはある |
| い-adjective | plain form + だけのことはある | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}いだけのことはある |
| な-adjective | ～な + だけのことはある | {f|有名|ゆうめい}だ → {f|有名|ゆうめい}なだけのことはある |
| Noun | Noun + だけのことはある | プロ → プロだけのことはある |
| Short forms | ～だけある／～だけはある | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}いだけある |
| Past | ～だけのことはあった | {f|待|ま}った → {f|待|ま}っただけのことはあった |
| Polite | ～だけのことはあります | {f|走|はし}っている → {f|走|はし}っているだけのことはあります |
| Continuing a sentence | ～だけのことはあって、… | {f|留学|りゅうがく}した → {f|留学|りゅうがく}しただけのことはあって |
| Usual opener | さすが（に）… | さすが、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|走|はし}っているだけのことはある |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the `は` is fixed. ❌<code>だけのことがある</code> is a different
sentence altogether ("there are things that…"), and it is a common slip.

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is a spoken pattern first and a written one second. It belongs to the moment
after you have tasted, watched or tried something, and it is the standard way to hand
out credit in Japanese without gushing. The polite
<code>だけのことはあります</code> is fine in any setting; the clipped
<code>だけある</code> is what friends actually say.

Three shapes worth keeping apart:

- <code>だけのことはある</code> - the full form, and the most emphatic.
- <code>だけある</code> - the same thing, shorter and more casual.
- <code>だけはある</code> - concessive. The `は` singles the point out and implies a
  reservation elsewhere: 「{f|値段|ねだん}は{f|高|たか}いが、うまいだけはある」 -
  *say what you like about the price, it does taste good*. This is a real difference
  in feel, not just a contraction, and it is worth hearing.

さすが at the head of the sentence is close to obligatory in speech. Two related
formulas do the same work slightly differently:
<code>さすが～だけあって</code> puts the praise mid-sentence, while
<code>さすが～だけのことはある</code> saves it for the end.

Like `だけあって`, this is other-directed. Applied to your own effort it sounds
self-satisfied; the natural way to say your own work paid off is
<code>{f|甲斐|かい}があった</code>. Said about someone else's investment it is warm.
Said flatly about a person whose reputation you doubt, the same words can be heard as
sarcasm - tone carries most of that, so listen for it before you produce it.

## Contrast

### ～だけのことはある vs ～だけあって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>だけのことはある</code> **closes** a sentence with the
verdict. `だけあって` **opens** the second half of one and lets the evidence follow.

</div>

<pre>
{f|海外|かいがい}で{f|働|はたら}いていた<b>だけあって</b>、{f|交渉|こうしょう}が{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}いている。
Having worked abroad, she negotiates with real composure.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|交渉|こうしょう}のしかたが{f|落|お}ち{f|着|つ}いている。{f|海外|かいがい}で{f|働|はたら}いていた<b>だけのことはある</b>。
The way she negotiates is so composed. Working abroad clearly left its mark.
</pre>

They carry the same admiration in a different order, and which you pick depends on
where the evidence already is. If the listener has just seen the result, close with
<code>だけのことはある</code>; if you are about to describe it, open with `だけあって`.

### ～だけのことはある vs ～わけだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `わけだ` works out a **logical consequence**;
<code>だけのことはある</code> passes a **judgement of worth**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|窓|まど}が{f|開|あ}いていたのか。どうりで{f|部屋|へや}が{f|冷|ひ}えている<b>わけだ</b>。
The window was open? No wonder the room's gone cold.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|材料|ざいりょう}から{f|選|えら}んでいるそうだ。この{f|味|あじ}になる<b>だけのことはある</b>。
Apparently they choose the ingredients themselves. That would explain a taste like this.
</pre>

Both English translations can be "no wonder", which is where the confusion starts.
The test is whether anything is being praised. A cold room is not an achievement, so
only `わけだ` will do; a flavour that repays careful sourcing is, so
<code>だけのことはある</code> becomes available - though `わけだ` would still be
possible, just colder.

### ～だけのことはある vs ～かいがある

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|甲斐|かい}がある</code> is the **doer's** relief that
the effort was repaid. <code>だけのことはある</code> is an **onlooker's** verdict that
the thing measures up.

</div>

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|水|みず}をやった<b>かいがあって</b>、やっと{f|花|はな}が{f|咲|さ}いた。
All that daily watering paid off - it's finally flowered.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|水|みず}をやっていた<b>だけのことはある</b>。{f|見事|みごと}な{f|花|はな}だ。
It shows that she watered it every day. What a magnificent bloom.
</pre>

The first is naturally said by the gardener, the second by the visitor. That is why
<code>かいがある</code> is the safe form for talking about your own effort, where
<code>だけのことはある</code> would sound like patting yourself on the back.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|高|たか}い<b>だけのことがある</b>。 → ✅ {f|高|たか}い<b>だけのことはある</b>。
  The particle is `は`, and it is fixed. With `が` the phrase reverts to its literal
  meaning and the sentence stops making sense.

- ❌ {f|有名|ゆうめい}<b>だだけのことはある</b> → ✅ {f|有名|ゆうめい}<b>なだけのことはある</b>
  <code>だけ</code> is a noun, so a な-adjective takes `な` in front of it. A plain
  noun attaches bare: <code>プロだけのことはある</code>.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}した<b>だけのことはあります</b>。 → ✅ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}した<b>{f|甲斐|かい}がありました</b>。
  Turned on yourself the verdict becomes a boast. <code>{f|甲斐|かい}がある</code>
  reports the same satisfaction from the inside.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った<b>だけのことはある</b>。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った<b>わけだ</b>。
  The premise has to be an investment or a reputation. A weather event is nobody's
  effort, so there is nothing for the verdict to endorse.

## Practice

**1.** さすが、{f|三年|さんねん}かけて{f|準備|じゅんび}した____。{f|見事|みごと}な{f|舞台|ぶたい}でした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|準備|じゅんび}した<b>だけのことはあります</b>

The evidence - a magnificent performance - has already been given, so the sentence
delivers the verdict at the end. さすが at the head is the standard companion, and
the polite ending matches でした in the next sentence.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|説明書|せつめいしょ}をなくしてしまった。{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}が{f|分|わ}からない（わけだ・だけのことはある）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}が{f|分|わ}からない<b>わけだ</b>。

Nothing is being praised - the speaker is just working out why they are stuck. That
is `わけだ`. <code>だけのことはある</code> needs an investment worth endorsing, and a
lost manual is not one.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "It really is worth getting up early for - the morning market was wonderful."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|早起|はやお}きした<b>だけのことはあった</b>。{f|朝市|あさいち}は{f|本当|ほんとう}にすばらしかった。

The early start is the investment and the market is the payoff, so the verdict goes
first and the evidence follows, or the two can be reversed. The past
<code>あった</code> matches an experience already had.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|便利|べんり}だだけのことはあって、みんなが{f|使|つか}っている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|便利|べんり}<b>なだけのことはあって</b>、みんなが{f|使|つか}っている。

A な-adjective links to <code>だけ</code> with `な`, never with `だ`. The continuative
<code>だけのことはあって</code> is fine here - it lets the sentence carry on into the
evidence instead of stopping at the verdict.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|店|みせ}は{f|評判|ひょうばん}どおり、うまい<b>だけのことはある</b>。
(b) この{f|店|みせ}は{f|値段|ねだん}は{f|高|たか}いが、うまい<b>だけはある</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is straightforward praise. The reputation set an expectation and the food met it,
and there is no reservation anywhere in the sentence.

(b) concedes. The `は` on <code>だけ</code> singles the taste out as the one thing
that holds up, and the clause in front of it says what does not. You would say (b)
about a place you would recommend with a warning attached - the grumble is part of the
compliment.

</details>
