# ～次第だ / ～次第で

**Meaning** · it depends on; it is decided by - the outcome hangs on the noun in front, and until that noun is settled the outcome is not

The noun <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> is roughly "how things stand, what it comes down
to". Stuck straight onto another noun, it hands that noun **control of the
outcome**: <code>{f|天気|てんき}{f|次第|しだい}だ</code> means the weather decides.
This is not the neutral "varies with" of `によって`, and it is not the tailoring of
<code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code> - it is a result that is genuinely in the balance and
waiting on one deciding factor.

A second, quite separate use closes formal letters:
<code>～{f|次第|しだい}です</code> meaning "that is how it comes to be". And a third
thing that merely looks alike, <code>V-ます{f|次第|しだい}</code>, means "as soon as".

## Formation

The deciding noun attaches **bare** - no `の`, no `な`, no particle at all.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + {f|次第|しだい}だ | {f|努力|どりょく} → {f|努力|どりょく}{f|次第|しだい}だ |
| Noun | Noun + {f|次第|しだい}で | {f|天気|てんき} → {f|天気|てんき}{f|次第|しだい}で |
| Noun (singling out one case) | Noun + {f|次第|しだい}では | {f|返事|へんじ} → {f|返事|へんじ}{f|次第|しだい}では |
| Polite | Noun + {f|次第|しだい}です | {f|相手|あいて} → {f|相手|あいて}{f|次第|しだい}です |
| "that is how it came about" | Clause (plain) + {f|次第|しだい}です | ご{f|連絡|れんらく}{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げた → ご{f|連絡|れんらく}{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げた{f|次第|しだい}です |
| "as soon as" (a different pattern) | V-ます stem + {f|次第|しだい} | {f|決|き}まる → {f|決|き}まり{f|次第|しだい} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|次第|しだい}に</code> is a fourth item and an unrelated one: it is
an adverb meaning "gradually" ({f|次第|しだい}に{f|暗|くら}くなってきた). It is not the
`に` form of anything on this page.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}{f|行|い}くかどうかは{f|天気|てんき}<b>{f|次第|しだい}だ</b>。
Whether we go tomorrow depends on the weather.
</pre>

<pre>
どうするかは、あなた<b>{f|次第|しだい}です</b>。
What to do is up to you.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}<b>{f|次第|しだい}では</b>、{f|中古|ちゅうこ}のものでもかまいません。
Depending on the price, I don't mind it being second-hand.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|結果|けっか}は{f|本人|ほんにん}の{f|努力|どりょく}<b>{f|次第|しだい}で</b>{f|大|おお}きく{f|変|か}わってくる。
The outcome shifts a great deal depending on how hard the person works.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}<b>{f|次第|しだい}で</b>、この{f|道具|どうぐ}は{f|便利|べんり}にも{f|危険|きけん}にもなる。
Depending on how it is used, this tool can be either handy or dangerous.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|同|おな}じ{f|言葉|ことば}でも、{f|言|い}い{f|方|かた}<b>{f|次第|しだい}で</b>{f|相手|あいて}の{f|受|う}け{f|取|と}り{f|方|かた}はまったく{f|違|ちが}ってくる。
Even with the same words, how the other person takes them changes completely depending on how you say them.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来年|らいねん}{f|留学|りゅうがく}できるかどうかは、{f|奨学金|しょうがくきん}の{f|審査|しんさ}の{f|結果|けっか}<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>です。
Whether I can study abroad next year depends on the outcome of the scholarship screening.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|以上|いじょう}のような{f|事情|じじょう}がございまして、{f|急|きゅう}きょご{f|連絡|れんらく}{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げた<b>{f|次第|しだい}です</b>。
Such being the circumstances, that is why I am writing to you at short notice.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The "depends on" use is the friendliest thing in this whole family. It attaches to
a bare noun, it is short, and it is entirely at home in speech:
<code>きみ{f|次第|しだい}だよ</code>, <code>やり{f|方|かた}{f|次第|しだい}だね</code>.
Alongside the stiff <code>に{f|基|もと}づいて</code> and
<code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code>, this is the one you can actually say to a friend.

<code>{f|次第|しだい}では</code> narrows to a single branch: "if the price turns out
right", "if his reply is the right one". The `は` does what it always does and marks
one case off against the others, so the sentence usually continues with something
conditional or hedged.

<code>～{f|次第|しだい}です</code> at the end of a clause is a different animal
altogether - stiff written business Japanese, the close of an explanation in a
letter or an apology. It means roughly what
<code>～というわけです</code> means but sounds several notches more formal. Say it
out loud in conversation and you will sound like a memo.

Finally, <code>V-ます{f|次第|しだい}</code> ("as soon as") is also business register,
and it is future-facing: <code>{f|決|き}まり{f|次第|しだい}ご{f|連絡|れんらく}します</code>
is the standard promise. It carries no `だ` and no `で`.

## Contrast

### ～{f|次第|しだい}で vs ～によって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `によって` reports a **settled pattern** of variation.
<code>{f|次第|しだい}で</code> puts a **single outcome in the balance**, usually one
nobody has decided yet.

</div>

<pre>
{f|開始|かいし}{f|時間|じかん}は{f|曜日|ようび}<b>によって</b>{f|異|こと}なります。
The starting time differs according to the day of the week.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|開始|かいし}できるかどうかは、{f|参加者|さんかしゃ}の{f|数|かず}<b>{f|次第|しだい}です</b>。
Whether we can start at all depends on how many people turn up.
</pre>

The first is a timetable that already exists. The second is a question still open,
and the sentence leans forward. That difference also explains the shapes each
allows: `によって` can carry a cause, a means and the agent of a passive, none of
which <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> can touch.

There is a plain formal difference too. `によって` needs `に`;
<code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> takes its noun bare, with no particle whatsoever.

### ～{f|次第|しだい}で vs ～に{f|応|おう}じて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code>: somebody **adjusts** something to
fit, on a scale that already exists. <code>{f|次第|しだい}で</code>: the thing is
**decided** by it, and it may not happen at all.

</div>

<pre>
{f|人数|にんずう}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|部屋|へや}の{f|大|おお}きさを{f|変|か}えます。
We change the size of the room to fit the number of people.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人数|にんずう}<b>{f|次第|しだい}で</b>、{f|会場|かいじょう}を{f|借|か}りるのをやめるかもしれません。
Depending on numbers, we may drop the idea of hiring a venue at all.
</pre>

<code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code> presupposes a scheme with a setting for every value -
more people, bigger room. <code>{f|次第|しだい}で</code> tolerates an all-or-nothing
result, and that is something <code>に{f|応|おう}じて</code> cannot express, because
it needs a range on both sides.

### Noun + {f|次第|しだい} vs V-ます stem + {f|次第|しだい}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - look at what sits in front. After a **noun** it means "depends
on". After a **ます stem** it means "as soon as", and the two are unrelated.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|時間|じかん}は{f|部長|ぶちょう}の{f|都合|つごう}<b>{f|次第|しだい}です</b>。
The time of the meeting depends on the manager's availability.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}の{f|都合|つごう}が{f|分|わ}かり<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>、ご{f|連絡|れんらく}いたします。
I will be in touch as soon as I know the manager's availability.
</pre>

The "as soon as" one never takes `だ` or `で`, cannot end a sentence, and always
looks forward to something that will certainly happen. If you find
<code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> with no `だ`, no `で` and a comma after it, that is the
one you are reading.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|安|やす}い<b>{f|次第|しだい}で</b>{f|買|か}います。 → ✅ {f|値段|ねだん}<b>{f|次第|しだい}で</b>{f|買|か}います。
  Only a noun attaches. An adjective has to be turned into the noun it is talking
  about - the price, the quality, the size.

- ❌ {f|値段|ねだん}<b>{f|次第|しだい}に</b>{f|買|か}うかどうか{f|決|き}めます。 → ✅ {f|値段|ねだん}<b>{f|次第|しだい}で</b>{f|買|か}うかどうか{f|決|き}めます。
  <code>{f|次第|しだい}に</code> is a separate adverb meaning "gradually". It cannot
  follow a noun in this sense at all.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}{f|次第|しだい}で{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になりました。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}のため{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になりました。
  <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> points at an outcome still hanging. A finished event has
  a cause, and causes take <code>ため</code> or `で`.

- ❌ {f|着|つ}く<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>{f|連絡|れんらく}します。 → ✅ {f|着|つ}き<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>{f|連絡|れんらく}します。
  The "as soon as" pattern takes the ます stem, not the dictionary form.
  <code>{f|到着|とうちゃく}{f|次第|しだい}</code> with the noun works too.

## Practice

**1.** {f|試合|しあい}をやるかどうかは{f|天候|てんこう}____です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|試合|しあい}をやるかどうかは{f|天候|てんこう}<b>{f|次第|しだい}</b>です。

The weather is the one factor that will settle a question still open, which is this
pattern's core job. The noun attaches with nothing in between.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|会員|かいいん}の{f|年齢|ねんれい}（{f|次第|しだい}で・に{f|応|おう}じて）{f|会費|かいひ}が{f|決|き}まっています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|年齢|ねんれい}<b>に{f|応|おう}じて</b>{f|会費|かいひ}が{f|決|き}まっています。

The <code>{f|決|き}まっています</code> gives it away: the scale of fees already exists,
with a price for each age band. <code>{f|次第|しだい}で</code> would mean the fee had
not been worked out yet.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Whether the project goes ahead depends entirely on next month's budget."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

その{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|進|すす}めるかどうかは、{f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|予算|よさん}<b>{f|次第|しだい}です</b>。

"Depends entirely on" is exactly what this pattern says on its own, so no adverb is
needed. The <code>かどうか</code> clause in front is what makes the outcome sound
genuinely undecided.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|返事|へんじ}の{f|次第|しだい}では、{f|考|かんが}え{f|直|なお}すつもりです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|返事|へんじ}<b>{f|次第|しだい}では</b>、{f|考|かんが}え{f|直|なお}すつもりです。

The deciding noun attaches bare. The `の` is pulled in from ordinary noun-plus-noun
phrases, but <code>{f|次第|しだい}</code> in this use behaves like a suffix rather
than a free noun.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|結果|けっか}が{f|出|で}{f|次第|しだい}、お{f|知|し}らせします。
(b) お{f|知|し}らせできるかどうかは{f|結果|けっか}{f|次第|しだい}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the ます-stem pattern: the results *will* come, and the moment they do you
will be told. It is a promise about timing, and it is standard business courtesy.

(b) is the noun pattern: whether you get told at all is in the balance, and the
results decide it. The two sentences share a word and share nothing else - one
guarantees contact, the other refuses to.

</details>
