# ～をきっかけに / ～がきっかけで

**Meaning** · with X as the trigger; X was what started it - names the small event a lasting change can be traced back to

`きっかけ` is the noun "trigger, opening, the thing that got it going". The pattern
looks **back** from a change that has already taken hold and points at the moment it
began: an illness, a chance remark, a film seen in a lecture. The trigger may be
tiny or accidental; what matters is that life went differently afterwards.

## Formation

`きっかけ` is a noun, so a verb clause has to be nominalised with `の` or `こと`
first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event noun | Noun + をきっかけに | {f|入院|にゅういん} → {f|入院|にゅういん}をきっかけに |
| Event noun (emphatic) | Noun + をきっかけにして | {f|失敗|しっぱい} → {f|失敗|しっぱい}をきっかけにして |
| Event noun (formal, written) | Noun + をきっかけとして | {f|受賞|じゅしょう} → {f|受賞|じゅしょう}をきっかけとして |
| Verb clause | plain past + のを／ことをきっかけに | {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}した → {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}したのをきっかけに |
| As the subject (conversational) | Noun + がきっかけで | {f|一言|ひとこと} → {f|一言|ひとこと}がきっかけで |
| As the subject (narrative) | Noun + がきっかけとなって | {f|記事|きじ} → {f|記事|きじ}がきっかけとなって |
| ❌ Bare verb | ❌{f|読|よ}むをきっかけに | ✅{f|読|よ}んだのをきっかけに |
| ❌ Wrong particle | ❌{f|一言|ひとこと}がきっかけに | ✅{f|一言|ひとこと}がきっかけで |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the main clause must be a **change that stuck**: started, gave
up, became, came to. If the second half is just physical damage or an immediate
result, you want で or によって instead.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
けが<b>をきっかけに</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|選手|せんしゅ}をやめた。
An injury was what made him give up playing.
</pre>

<pre>
ドラマ<b>をきっかけに</b>、{f|日本語|にほんご}の{f|勉強|べんきょう}を{f|始|はじ}めました。
A TV drama was what got me started on Japanese.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|友人|ゆうじん}の{f|一言|ひとこと}<b>がきっかけで</b>、{f|転職|てんしょく}を{f|決|き}めた。
Something a friend said was what decided me on changing jobs.
</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>
{f|近所|きんじょ}の{f|祭|まつ}り<b>をきっかけに</b>、{f|隣|となり}の{f|人|ひと}と{f|話|はな}すようになった。
The local festival was how I came to talk to my neighbour.
</pre>

<pre>

Seeing one film in a university class was what set her writing scripts.
</pre>

<pre>

A small earthquake was the spark that sharpened the town's awareness of disaster risk.
</pre>

<pre>

A single letter from my grandmother was what finally ended a quarrel between the brothers that had gone on for years.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is the member of the family you can actually say out loud. It is neutral,
common in interviews and self-introductions - 「{f|何|なに}がきっかけでこの{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|選|えら}んだんですか」
is a standard question - and it appears in essays and magazine profiles just as
happily. Of the shapes, `がきっかけで` is the most conversational, `をきっかけに`
the everyday default, and `をきっかけとして` the one for written prose.

The trigger is allowed to be **small, private or accidental**, and often the point
of the sentence is precisely that something trivial changed everything. That
tolerance is what separates it from <code>を{f|契機|けいき}に</code>, which reaches
for events of some weight.

The pattern is retrospective in feel: you are standing after the change and pointing
back. It is not wrong to look forward from a trigger that has just occurred -
<code>これをきっかけに{f|頑張|がんば}ります</code> is natural - but the trigger has to
exist already. A ceremony still in the diary is the business of `にあたって`, not of
this pattern.

One restriction worth holding on to: the second half should be a **development**,
something taken up, given up or turned around. Direct physical results do not
qualify. A storm can break a window, but it does not "trigger" the breakage.

## Contrast

### ～をきっかけに vs ～を{f|契機|けいき}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same structure at two altitudes. `をきっかけに` takes
anything, including the trivial and the personal.
<code>を{f|契機|けいき}に</code> is formal and prefers events with **weight**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}にすすめられたこと<b>をきっかけに</b>、{f|走|はし}り{f|始|はじ}めた。
My younger brother suggesting it was what got me running.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|創業|そうぎょう}{f|五十周年|ごじゅっしゅうねん}<b>を{f|契機|けいき}に</b>、{f|社名|しゃめい}を{f|改|あらた}めた。
Marking fifty years since its founding, the company changed its name.
</pre>

Put the brother into the second frame and it turns comic; put a fiftieth anniversary
into the first and a company announcement suddenly sounds like a diary entry. The
patterns are otherwise interchangeable, and the choice is one of register.

### ～をきっかけに vs ～て{f|以来|いらい}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `て以来` measures the **period since** an event and says the
state has held ever since. `をきっかけに` names the event as the **cause** of a
change, and says nothing about duration.

</div>

<pre>
{f|東京|とうきょう}に{f|来|き}<b>て{f|以来|いらい}</b>、ずっと{f|同|おな}じアパートに{f|住|す}んでいる。
Ever since I came to Tokyo, I have lived in the same flat.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|東京|とうきょう}に{f|来|き}たの<b>をきっかけに</b>、{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}で{f|通勤|つうきん}するようになった。
Moving to Tokyo was what got me commuting by bicycle.
</pre>

The first sentence is about a stretch of time; the second is about a change of
habit. Note the different attachment as well: `て以来` follows the て-form directly,
while `きっかけ`, being a noun, needs <code>のを</code> in front of it.

### ～をきっかけに vs ～によって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `によって` names a **cause** that directly produced the result.
`をきっかけに` names an **opening** after which someone did something new.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大雨|おおあめ}<b>によって</b>{f|橋|はし}が{f|流|なが}された。
The bridge was washed away by the heavy rain.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大雨|おおあめ}の{f|被害|ひがい}<b>をきっかけに</b>、{f|市|し}は{f|避難|ひなん}{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|作|つく}り{f|直|なお}した。
The damage from the heavy rain prompted the city to redraw its evacuation plan.
</pre>

Rain destroys a bridge by itself; ❌<code>{f|大雨|おおあめ}をきっかけに{f|橋|はし}が{f|流|なが}された</code>
is wrong because nobody decided anything. Redrawing a plan, by contrast, is a
choice people made afterwards - and that is what a trigger triggers.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|雪|ゆき}<b>をきっかけに</b>、{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まった。 → ✅ {f|雪|ゆき}<b>のため</b>、{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まった。
  Trains stopping is a direct effect of snow, not a new development someone
  embarked on. Plain cause takes で, のため or によって.

- ❌ {f|結婚|けっこん}する<b>をきっかけに</b>、{f|料理|りょうり}を{f|習|なら}い{f|始|はじ}めた。 → ✅ {f|結婚|けっこん}<b>をきっかけに</b>、{f|料理|りょうり}を{f|習|なら}い{f|始|はじ}めた。
  `きっかけ` is a noun and takes `を`. Either use the bare event noun, or nominalise
  the clause: <code>{f|結婚|けっこん}したのをきっかけに</code>.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}の{f|言葉|ことば}<b>がきっかけに</b>、{f|考|かんが}えが{f|変|か}わった。 → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}の{f|言葉|ことば}<b>がきっかけで</b>、{f|考|かんが}えが{f|変|か}わった。
  With `が` the noun is the subject of a small clause, so the linking form is で -
  or <code>がきっかけとなって</code>. Mixing the two frames is the commonest slip
  here.

- ❌ {f|事故|じこ}<b>をきっかけに</b>、{f|足|あし}をけがした。 → ✅ {f|事故|じこ}<b>で</b>{f|足|あし}をけがした。
  The injury *is* the accident, not something that followed from it. A trigger and
  its outcome have to be two separate events.

## Practice

**1.** {f|定年|ていねん}____、{f|夫婦|ふうふ}で{f|田舎|いなか}に{f|移|うつ}り{f|住|す}んだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|定年|ていねん}<b>をきっかけに</b>

Retirement is the event that opened the way, and moving to the countryside is the
lasting change that followed. <code>を{f|契機|けいき}に</code> would also work and
would sound like a newspaper profile.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|台風|たいふう}（をきっかけに・によって）、{f|屋根|やね}のかわらが{f|落|お}ちた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|台風|たいふう}<b>によって</b>

Tiles falling is what the typhoon did, directly and physically. There is no later
development for the typhoon to have triggered, so the trigger frame has nothing to
attach to.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "A single photograph was what got her interested in Okinawa."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一枚|いちまい}の{f|写真|しゃしん}<b>がきっかけで</b>、{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|沖縄|おきなわ}に{f|興味|きょうみ}を{f|持|も}つようになった。

A photograph is exactly the kind of small accidental trigger this pattern was made
for, and <code>ようになった</code> supplies the lasting change the main clause needs.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|新聞|しんぶん}の{f|記事|きじ}を{f|読|よ}むをきっかけに、ボランティアを{f|始|はじ}めました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|新聞|しんぶん}の{f|記事|きじ}を{f|読|よ}んだの<b>をきっかけに</b>、ボランティアを{f|始|はじ}めました。

A verb cannot sit directly in front of `きっかけ`, which is a noun. Nominalise the
clause with <code>のを</code>, and use the past form, since the reading happened
before the change.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|病気|びょうき}<b>をきっかけに</b>、たばこをやめた。
(b) {f|病気|びょうき}になっ<b>て{f|以来|いらい}</b>、たばこを{f|吸|す}っていない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) points at the illness as the moment a decision was taken. The interest of the
sentence is the change itself - one day he smoked, then he stopped.

(b) measures the time since falling ill and reports the state that has held
throughout it. Nothing is said about deciding; the sentence simply covers the
period from then until now.

</details>
