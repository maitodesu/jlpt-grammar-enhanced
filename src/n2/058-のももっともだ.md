# ～のももっともだ

**Meaning** · you can hardly blame them; that reaction is perfectly fair - the speaker endorses somebody's feeling or response as justified

もっとも here is an adjective meaning "reasonable, well-founded". Where
<code>のも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> rules an outcome inevitable,
<code>のももっともだ</code> **takes somebody's side**: it looks at an angry customer,
a doubting colleague, a parent who refuses, and says that in their position anyone
would feel the same. It is a concession, and it is almost always about a human
reaction rather than about the way the world works.

## Formation

`の` nominalises the clause; もっとも is a な-adjective, so `だ` / `です` follows and
`な` appears before a noun. It is normally written in kana.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + のももっともだ | {f|怒|おこ}る → {f|怒|おこ}るのももっともだ |
| Verb (past) | た-form + のももっともだ | {f|断|ことわ}った → {f|断|ことわ}ったのももっともだ |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + のももっともだ | {f|信|しん}じない → {f|信|しん}じないのももっともだ |
| い-adjective | plain form + のももっともだ | {f|悔|くや}しい → {f|悔|くや}しいのももっともだ |
| な-adjective | ～な + のももっともだ | {f|不満|ふまん}だ → {f|不満|ふまん}なのももっともだ |
| Polite | …のももっともです | {f|驚|おどろ}く → {f|驚|おどろ}くのももっともです |
| With は | …のはもっともだ | {f|怒|おこ}る → {f|怒|おこ}るのはもっともだ |
| Modifying a noun | もっともな + Noun | {f|意見|いけん} → もっともな{f|意見|いけん} |
| Polite formula | ごもっともです | - → おっしゃることはごもっともです |

<div class="note">

**Note** - kana もっとも hides three different words. This one is the adjective
（{f|尤|もっと}もだ）. There is also the adverb {f|最|もっと}も, "most", and a
conjunction もっとも meaning "though, having said that". Here the giveaways are the
`の` in front and the `だ` / `です` behind.

</div>

## Nuance & register

The move `もっともだ` makes is **conceding**. You are granting that somebody's
reaction is well-founded, and very often you are doing it on the way to a "but":
<code>…のももっともだが、…</code> is one of the most common shapes it takes. That is
worth knowing before you use it, because a bare
<code>おっしゃるとおりです</code> agrees outright, while `もっともだ` agrees with the
*reasonableness* of a position without necessarily agreeing with the position.

It is a measured, slightly formal word. Editorials, mediation, management writing and
careful speech are its home; among friends people say
<code>{f|無理|むり}ないよ</code> or <code>そりゃそうだ</code> instead. Nothing about it
is stiff enough to avoid, but it does sound considered.

<code>ごもっともです</code> deserves separate mention. It is the standard polite
formula for receiving a complaint or a criticism - 「ご{f|指摘|してき}はごもっともです」 -
and it buys you a moment without conceding the substance. It is heard constantly in
customer service and in meetings.

The one real limit is what `もっともだ` can be said about. Its subject has to be a
person's judgement, feeling or behaviour: getting angry, doubting, refusing,
complaining, being surprised, giving up. A purely physical outcome - the room being
cold, the engine not starting - has no reasoning to endorse, and there
<code>のも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> is the word.

## Contrast

### ～のももっともだ vs ～のも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - もっとも judges a **reaction** and finds it fair.
<code>{f|当然|とうぜん}</code> judges an **outcome** and finds it inevitable.

</div>

<pre>
エアコンが{f|壊|こわ}れていたのだから、{f|部屋|へや}が{f|暑|あつ}い<b>のも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>。
The air conditioning was broken, so of course the room was hot.
</pre>

<pre>
エアコンが{f|壊|こわ}れたままなのだから、{f|客|きゃく}が{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}う<b>のももっともだ</b>。
The air conditioning is still broken, so the guests are quite right to complain.
</pre>

Heat has no case to argue; a complaint does. Where the clause *is* a human reaction
both patterns are grammatical, and then the difference is one of stance: `当然だ`
hands down a ruling, `もっともだ` offers sympathy.

### ～のももっともだ vs ～のも{f|無理|むり}はない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|無理|むり}はない</code> says the reaction cannot be
**blamed**. もっともだ says it is positively **reasonable**.

</div>

<pre>
あれだけ{f|待|ま}たされたのだから、{f|怒|おこ}り{f|出|だ}した<b>のも{f|無理|むり}はない</b>。
Kept waiting that long, it's no surprise he lost his temper.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|条件|じょうけん}が{f|説明|せつめい}と{f|違|ちが}うのだから、{f|契約|けいやく}を{f|断|ことわ}った<b>のももっともだ</b>。
The terms weren't what he'd been told, so turning the contract down was perfectly sound.
</pre>

<code>{f|無理|むり}はない</code> excuses; it is compatible with a reaction the speaker
would not defend on its merits - losing your temper is understandable, not admirable.
`もっともだ` endorses the reasoning itself, which is why it is the natural choice for
a considered decision. <code>{f|無理|むり}はない</code> is also the more colloquial of
the two.

### ～のももっともだ vs ～のも{f|仕方|しかた}がない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `もっともだ` **agrees** with someone.
<code>{f|仕方|しかた}がない</code> **gives up** on something.

</div>

<pre>
これだけ{f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}れば、{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まる<b>のも{f|仕方|しかた}がない</b>。
With snow like this there's nothing to be done about the trains stopping.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|安全|あんぜん}が{f|確認|かくにん}できないのだから、{f|乗客|じょうきゃく}が{f|不安|ふあん}がる<b>のももっともだ</b>。
Safety hasn't been confirmed, so the passengers' anxiety is entirely reasonable.
</pre>

<code>{f|仕方|しかた}がない</code> is resignation and takes nobody's side; it works
just as well for weather and machinery as for people. `もっともだ` is an endorsement
and needs somebody whose position can be endorsed.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|怒|おこ}るもっともだ。 → ✅ {f|怒|おこ}る<b>のももっともだ</b>。
  もっとも is an adjective describing a thing, so the clause has to be nominalised
  with `の` first.

- ❌ {f|不満|ふまん}<b>だのももっともだ</b> → ✅ {f|不満|ふまん}<b>なのももっともだ</b>
  な-adjectives take `な` before `の`. もっとも itself is a な-adjective too, hence
  <code>もっともな{f|意見|いけん}</code>.

- ❌ {f|電池|でんち}が{f|切|き}れていたのだから、{f|時計|とけい}が{f|止|と}まっている<b>のももっともだ</b>。 → ✅ …{f|止|と}まっている<b>のも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>。
  A stopped clock has no reasoning behind it. もっともだ needs a person's judgement or
  feeling as its subject.

- ❌ もっとも{f|意見|いけん}ですね。 → ✅ もっとも<b>な</b>{f|意見|いけん}ですね。（or ごもっともです）
  Before a noun the `な` is obligatory. The stand-alone polite acknowledgement is
  <code>ごもっともです</code>, with the honorific `ご`.

## Practice

**1.** {f|説明|せつめい}が{f|二転三転|にてんさんてん}したのだから、{f|住民|じゅうみん}が{f|不信感|ふしんかん}を{f|持|も}つ____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|不信感|ふしんかん}を{f|持|も}つ<b>のももっともだ</b>

The clause names a human reaction - distrust - and the sentence endorses it as a
reasonable response to shifting explanations. The <code>のだから</code> clause in
front supplies the grounds, which this pattern normally expects.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|停電|ていでん}していたのだから、{f|部屋|へや}が{f|真|ま}っ{f|暗|くら}だった（のももっともだ・のも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|部屋|へや}が{f|真|ま}っ{f|暗|くら}だった<b>のも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>

A dark room is a physical consequence, not somebody's judgement, so there is nothing
for もっともだ to side with. Had the clause been 「{f|子供|こども}が{f|怖|こわ}がる」,
either would work.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "They were never told about the change, so it's quite reasonable that the staff are annoyed."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|変更|へんこう}を{f|知|し}らされていなかったのだから、スタッフが{f|怒|おこ}る<b>のももっともだ</b>。

"Quite reasonable" applied to somebody's anger is the core use. Note that the speaker
is siding with the staff; <code>のも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> would make the same
sentence a cool judgement on whoever failed to tell them.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}の{f|意見|いけん}はもっとも{f|意見|いけん}だと{f|思|おも}うが、{f|予算|よさん}が{f|足|た}りない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}の{f|意見|いけん}はもっとも<b>な</b>{f|意見|いけん}だと{f|思|おも}うが、{f|予算|よさん}が{f|足|た}りない。
（more naturally: {f|彼|かれ}の{f|言|い}うことはもっともだが、{f|予算|よさん}が{f|足|た}りない。）

もっとも is a な-adjective, so it needs `な` to modify a noun. The が in the second
half is worth noticing too - conceding with もっともだ and then qualifying is the
pattern's most typical shape.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}が{f|辞退|じたい}した<b>のももっともだ</b>。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}が{f|辞退|じたい}した<b>のも{f|無理|むり}はない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) endorses his reasoning. The speaker has looked at his situation and concluded
that declining was a sound decision - the kind of thing you say when you would have
done the same and think well of him for it.

(b) merely excuses it. It says nobody could hold the withdrawal against him, which
leaves room for the speaker privately wishing he had stayed. (b) is also the warmer
and more colloquial of the two; (a) sounds like a considered verdict.

</details>
