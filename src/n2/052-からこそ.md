# ～からこそ

**Meaning** · precisely because - this reason and no other, and the speaker is arguing for it

`からこそ` is `から` with the N3 emphatic particle `こそ` welded on. That `こそ`
changes what the sentence is *doing*: an ordinary `から` reports a cause, while
`からこそ` **contends** for one. It says the listener may have assumed some other
explanation, or may have read the situation the wrong way round, and here is the
real reason. The cause it names is nearly always one the speaker values - so the
sentence often comes out as a defence of something that looks harsh, or a tribute
to something that looks ordinary.

## Formation

Plain form + `からこそ`. Nouns and な-adjectives keep their `だ`, because `だから` is
what `こそ` is attaching to.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + からこそ | {f|信|しん}じる → {f|信|しん}じるからこそ |
| Verb (past) | た-form + からこそ | {f|苦労|くろう}した → {f|苦労|くろう}したからこそ |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + からこそ | {f|知|し}らない → {f|知|し}らないからこそ |
| い-adjective | plain form + からこそ | {f|難|むずか}しい → {f|難|むずか}しいからこそ |
| な-adjective | ～だ + からこそ | {f|大切|たいせつ}だ → {f|大切|たいせつ}だからこそ |
| Noun | ～だ + からこそ | {f|家族|かぞく} → {f|家族|かぞく}だからこそ |
| Usual ending | …からこそ、…のだ／のです | {f|続|つづ}けられたのです |
| Sentence-final | …のは…からこそだ | {f|勝|か}てたのは{f|練習|れんしゅう}したからこそだ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - `からこそ` and a final <code>のだ／んです</code> travel together. The
sentence is an assertion *about* a cause, and <code>のだ</code> is what marks a
sentence as an explanation. Without it, `からこそ` can feel as though it stops short.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|好|す}きだ<b>からこそ</b>、{f|厳|きび}しいことも{f|言|い}う。
It's because I care that I say the hard things too.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|家族|かぞく}だ<b>からこそ</b>、{f|言|い}えないこともある。
There are things you can't say precisely because you're family.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|失敗|しっぱい}した<b>からこそ</b>、{f|次|つぎ}に{f|何|なに}をすべきかが{f|見|み}えてきた。
It was failing that let me see what to do next.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}が{f|限|かぎ}られている<b>からこそ</b>、{f|一日|いちにち}を{f|大切|たいせつ}にできるのだ。
It is exactly because time is limited that a day can be treasured.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|田舎|いなか}で{f|育|そだ}った<b>からこそ</b>、{f|季節|きせつ}の{f|変|か}わり{f|目|め}に{f|敏感|びんかん}なのかもしれません。
Perhaps it's growing up in the countryside that has made me alert to the turn of the seasons.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|君|きみ}に{f|実力|じつりょく}がある<b>からこそ</b>、{f|部長|ぶちょう}はあえて{f|難|むずか}しい{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|任|まか}せたんだよ。
The boss gave you the hard job on purpose, precisely because you're good enough for it.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}が{f|三十年|さんじゅうねん}{f|続|つづ}いてきたのは、{f|常連|じょうれん}のお{f|客|きゃく}さんに{f|支|ささ}えられてきた<b>からこそ</b>です。
This shop has lasted thirty years only because its regulars have kept it going.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|安全|あんぜん}が{f|当|あ}たり{f|前|まえ}になっている<b>からこそ</b>、{f|日々|ひび}の{f|点検|てんけん}を{f|怠|おこた}ってはいけないのです。
It is exactly because safety is taken for granted that the daily checks must never be skipped.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`からこそ` is emphatic, a little rhetorical, and entirely at home in speeches,
essays, editorials and heartfelt conversation. It is register-neutral in the sense
that nothing about it is rude or over-formal, but it is **marked**: you do not reach
for it to explain why you took an umbrella. Attach it to a humdrum cause and the
sentence sounds as though it is auditioning for something.

The two shapes it most often takes are worth learning whole. Mid-sentence,
<code>…からこそ、…のだ</code>. Sentence-final, <code>…のは…からこそだ</code> - which
front-loads the result and lands the cause at the end, and is a favourite of
closing lines.

Semantically, `からこそ` almost always frames the cause as something **worth having**,
even when the result is uncomfortable. 「{f|大切|たいせつ}だからこそ{f|厳|きび}しくする」
works; a blame sentence like 「{f|彼|かれ}が{f|嘘|うそ}をついたからこそ{f|失敗|しっぱい}した」
does not - for assigning fault Japanese uses `せいで`. Nor does `からこそ` make
excuses. If your reason is a defence of your own behaviour, `ものだから` or `ので` is
the honest choice; `からこそ` would sound as though you were proud of it.

## Contrast

### ～からこそ vs ～から

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `から` gives *a* reason. `からこそ` insists it is *the* reason,
and quietly rules the alternatives out.

</div>

<pre>
{f|心配|しんぱい}だ<b>から</b>、{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|電話|でんわ}している。
I'm worried, so I ring every day.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|心配|しんぱい}だ<b>からこそ</b>、あえて{f|何|なに}も{f|聞|き}かないでいるのです。
It's exactly because I'm worried that I'm deliberately not asking anything.
</pre>

The second sentence is answering an unspoken objection - *if you were worried you'd
ask* - and reversing it. That is the natural habitat of `からこそ`: a cause whose
effect is not the one the listener would have predicted.

### ～からこそ vs ～だけに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `からこそ` **argues** a causal link. `だけに` **measures** one:
given this circumstance, the outcome is proportionately stronger.

</div>

<pre>
{f|期待|きたい}していた<b>だけに</b>、{f|中止|ちゅうし}の{f|知|し}らせはこたえた。
I'd had my hopes up, so the cancellation hit that much harder.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|期待|きたい}していた<b>からこそ</b>、はっきり{f|意見|いけん}を{f|言|い}わせてもらいます。
It's because I had hopes for it that I'm going to say plainly what I think.
</pre>

`だけに` observes a matching of degrees and does not need the listener to agree with
anything; the speaker is commenting. `からこそ` is making a case, and expects a
response. That is also why `だけに` sits comfortably in a news report and `からこそ`
sits comfortably in a speech.

### ～からこそ vs ～てこそ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `からこそ` names the cause of something that **is already so**.
`てこそ` names the action **without which** the result would not come about at all.

</div>

<pre>
{f|支|ささ}えてくれる{f|人|ひと}がいる<b>からこそ</b>、{f|挑戦|ちょうせん}できるのだ。
It's because there are people behind me that I can take the risk.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|足|あし}で{f|歩|ある}い<b>てこそ</b>、その{f|土地|とち}のことが{f|分|わ}かる。
You only get to know a place by walking it yourself.
</pre>

Grammatically they are easy to keep apart - `からこそ` follows a full plain-form
clause, `てこそ` follows a て-form. Semantically, the `からこそ` clause is a fact
already in place; the `てこそ` clause is a condition that has to be met.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|静|しず}か<b>なからこそ</b>、{f|集中|しゅうちゅう}できる。 → ✅ {f|静|しず}か<b>だからこそ</b>、{f|集中|しゅうちゅう}できる。
  `こそ` is attaching to `だから`, so the な-adjective keeps its `だ`. Nouns behave the
  same way: <code>{f|新人|しんじん}だからこそ</code>.

- ❌ {f|信|しん}じている<b>こそ</b>、{f|任|まか}せたのです。 → ✅ {f|信|しん}じている<b>からこそ</b>、{f|任|まか}せたのです。
  A whole clause cannot take bare `こそ`. `こそ` needs a `から` to sit on, or a て-form.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った<b>からこそ</b>、{f|試合|しあい}が{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った<b>ので</b>、{f|試合|しあい}が{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。
  Nothing is being contended here - rain cancels matches, and everyone knows it.
  `からこそ` on a routine cause sounds theatrical.

- ❌ {f|寝坊|ねぼう}した<b>からこそ</b>、{f|遅刻|ちこく}してしまいました。 → ✅ {f|寝坊|ねぼう}した<b>ものですから</b>、{f|遅刻|ちこく}してしまいました。
  `からこそ` cannot carry an excuse. It presents its cause as something to be proud
  of, which is the opposite of what an apology needs.

## Practice

**1.** {f|親|おや}は、{f|子|こ}どものことを{f|思|おも}っている____、{f|時|とき}に{f|厳|きび}しくなるのだろう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|思|おも}っている<b>からこそ</b>

The sentence is defending strictness against the assumption that strictness means
coldness - exactly the reversal `からこそ` exists for. Note the のだろう at the end;
`からこそ` and an explanatory <code>のだ</code> normally arrive together.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|準備|じゅんび}に{f|一年|いちねん}かけた（からこそ・だけに）、{f|中止|ちゅうし}の{f|知|し}らせはつらかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|準備|じゅんび}に{f|一年|いちねん}かけた<b>だけに</b>、{f|中止|ちゅうし}の{f|知|し}らせはつらかった。

The second clause reports how much something hurt, and the first clause explains why
it hurt *that much*. That is degree-matching, which is `だけに`. `からこそ` would
promise an argument the sentence never makes.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "It's precisely because it's a difficult job that I want to try it."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|難|むずか}しい{f|仕事|しごと}だ<b>からこそ</b>、やってみたいのです。

The noun phrase <code>{f|難|むずか}しい{f|仕事|しごと}</code> keeps its `だ` before
`からこそ`. The whole point of the sentence is that difficulty would normally be a
reason *not* to, so the emphatic form is doing real work here.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}がミスをしたからこそ、{f|納品|のうひん}が{f|遅|おく}れてしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}がミスをした<b>せいで</b>、{f|納品|のうひん}が{f|遅|おく}れてしまった。

`からこそ` presents its cause as something of value. Using it to assign blame reads
as bitter sarcasm at best. Fault takes `せいで`, and a neutral report takes `ため`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|経験|けいけん}がない<b>から</b>、{f|自由|じゆう}な{f|発想|はっそう}ができる。
(b) {f|経験|けいけん}がない<b>からこそ</b>、{f|自由|じゆう}な{f|発想|はっそう}ができるのだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply links the two: no experience, hence free thinking. It could be a passing
remark, and a listener might not even notice the claim being made.

(b) plants a flag. `からこそ` marks inexperience as the very thing that makes the
free thinking possible, and does so against the obvious objection that inexperience
is a handicap. The final <code>のだ</code> confirms that the sentence is an argument,
not an observation - it is the kind of line that ends a paragraph.

</details>
