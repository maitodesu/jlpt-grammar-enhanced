# お～願う

**Meaning** · a formal, businesslike "please do this" - the request form of announcements, notices and office correspondence

<code>お + verb stem + {f|願|ねが}います</code> is how an organisation asks you to do
something. Literally it is "I request that you…", and that is how it behaves: it is
unmistakably polite, but it is the politeness of a station platform or a memo
rather than of one person asking another for a favour. Bend it into a real favour
by switching to the potential, <code>{f|願|ねが}えますか</code>.

## Formation

The frame is a honorific prefix, then the verb, then <code>{f|願|ねが}う</code>. The
verb never carries `する` or `て`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb / る-verb) | お + ます-stem + {f|願|ねが}います | {f|待|ま}つ → お{f|待|ま}ち{f|願|ねが}います |
| する-verb (Sino-Japanese noun) | ご + noun + {f|願|ねが}います | {f|連絡|れんらく}する → ご{f|連絡|れんらく}{f|願|ねが}います |
| Softened into a request | お・ご … {f|願|ねが}えますか | {f|確認|かくにん}する → ご{f|確認|かくにん}{f|願|ねが}えますか |
| Softer still | お・ご … {f|願|ねが}えないでしょうか | {f|知|し}らせる → お{f|知|し}らせ{f|願|ねが}えないでしょうか |
| Written / letter style | お・ご … {f|願|ねが}いたく{f|存|ぞん}じます | {f|了承|りょうしょう}する → ご{f|了承|りょうしょう}{f|願|ねが}いたく{f|存|ぞん}じます |
| Prefix exceptions | a few Sino-Japanese nouns take お | {f|電話|でんわ} → お{f|電話|でんわ}{f|願|ねが}います |

<div class="note">

**Note** - う-verb / る-verb are the two regular classes ({f|五段|ごだん} / {f|一段|いちだん}, Group 1 /
Group 2). The rule of thumb for the prefix is お before a native Japanese word and
ご before a Sino-Japanese one, with a short list of everyday exceptions -
お{f|電話|でんわ}, お{f|返事|へんじ}, お{f|食事|しょくじ}, お{f|約束|やくそく} - that
take お despite being Sino-Japanese.

</div>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is institutional Japanese. You meet it in station and airport announcements,
on notices in banks and hospitals, in internal memos, and in business email - and
almost never between friends. Nobody says お{f|待|ま}ち{f|願|ねが}います to a mate
outside a cinema; that is {f|待|ま}ってて.

Its particular flavour is that it is polite without being soft. Grammatically it
reports the speaker's request rather than asking the listener's willingness, so it
does not really leave room for no. That is exactly right for a public
announcement and slightly cold when one person asks another for something. The
potential form is the repair: <code>ご{f|確認|かくにん}{f|願|ねが}えますか</code>
means "could I have your confirmation", and now the listener's ability to agree is
part of the sentence. <code>{f|願|ねが}えないでしょうか</code> is humbler again and
is safe upward, to a client or a senior colleague.

In fully polite correspondence <code>{f|願|ねが}う</code> tends to be absorbed into
the longer formula
<code>ご{f|確認|かくにん}くださいますようお{f|願|ねが}い{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます</code>.
That is the ceiling of written politeness; the bare
<code>ご{f|確認|かくにん}{f|願|ねが}います</code> sits a good way below it and reads as
brisk, internal, everyday business style. Some readers find the bare form curt when
it is aimed at them personally - a tendency worth knowing before you use it on a
customer.

## Contrast

### お～{f|願|ねが}います vs お～ください

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>お～ください</code> frames the action as the listener's
kindness; <code>お～{f|願|ねが}います</code> frames it as the speaker's request.

</div>

<pre>
こちらにお<b>{f|掛|か}けください</b>。
Please have a seat.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|順番|じゅんばん}にお{f|並|なら}び<b>{f|願|ねが}います</b>。
Please queue in order.
</pre>

<code>ください</code> comes from <code>くださる</code>, "to graciously give", so the
listener is doing you a kindness - it is warm, and it is what service staff use
when they want you to feel looked after. <code>{f|願|ねが}います</code> is drier and
more procedural; it is the voice that manages a crowd. Both are formal, and in many
notices either would pass, but the tone is not identical.

### お～{f|願|ねが}えますか vs ～ていただけますか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～ていただけますか</code> is the all-purpose spoken
request. <code>お～{f|願|ねが}えますか</code> is a fixed formula with a written
flavour, and only works with verbs that take the honorific prefix.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|直|なお}して<b>いただけますか</b>。
Could you fix this document for me?
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|資料|しりょう}をご{f|修正|しゅうせい}<b>{f|願|ねが}えますか</b>。
Could I ask for a correction to this document?
</pre>

They sit at a similar level of politeness. The difference is texture: the first
sounds like a person speaking, the second like a line in a message. Note also that
<code>～ていただけますか</code> attaches to any verb at all, while the
<code>お～</code> frame does not - there is no ❌お{f|死|し}に{f|願|ねが}います and no
natural お-form for many ordinary verbs. When in doubt in speech, use
<code>～ていただけますか</code>.

### お～{f|願|ねが}います vs お{f|願|ねが}いします

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>お{f|願|ねが}いします</code> takes a **noun** in front of
it. <code>お～{f|願|ねが}います</code> puts a **verb stem inside** the frame. They are
two constructions, not one.

</div>

<pre>
{f|確認|かくにん}を<b>お{f|願|ねが}いします</b>。
Please check it. / I'd like to ask you to check it.
</pre>

<pre>
ご{f|確認|かくにん}<b>{f|願|ねが}います</b>。
Please confirm.
</pre>

The everyday <code>お{f|願|ねが}いします</code> is the one learners already know, and
it is far more common in speech; it can even stand alone as "please" when the
request is obvious from context. Fusing the two produces
❌<code>お{f|待|ま}ちお{f|願|ねが}いします</code>, which has the frame twice.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ しばらくお<b>{f|待|ま}つ{f|願|ねが}います</b>。 → ✅ しばらくお<b>{f|待|ま}ち{f|願|ねが}います</b>。
  The frame takes the ます-stem, never the dictionary form.

- ❌ ご{f|連絡|れんらく}<b>し{f|願|ねが}います</b>。 → ✅ ご{f|連絡|れんらく}<b>{f|願|ねが}います</b>。
  With a Sino-Japanese noun the `する` disappears entirely -
  <code>ご + noun + {f|願|ねが}います</code>.

- ❌ こちらで<b>{f|待|ま}ち{f|願|ねが}います</b>。 → ✅ こちらでお<b>{f|待|ま}ち{f|願|ねが}います</b>。
  The honorific prefix is part of the pattern, not an optional decoration. Without
  it the sentence is simply broken.

- ❌ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|明日|あす}までにご{f|確認|かくにん}<b>{f|願|ねが}います</b>。 → ✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|明日|あす}までにご{f|確認|かくにん}<b>{f|願|ねが}えないでしょうか</b>。
  Aimed at one senior person, the plain <code>{f|願|ねが}います</code> issues an
  instruction. The potential plus <code>でしょうか</code> turns it back into a
  request.

## Practice

**1.** {f|順番|じゅんばん}が{f|来|き}ましたらお{f|呼|よ}びしますので、あちらの{f|席|せき}でお{f|座|すわ}り____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

あちらの{f|席|せき}でお{f|座|すわ}り<b>{f|願|ねが}います</b>。

A waiting-room instruction to the public, which is the pattern's home ground.
お{f|掛|か}けください would also be natural here and is a shade warmer.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|内容|ないよう}に{f|間違|まちが}いがないか、（お{f|確認|かくにん}{f|願|ねが}います・ご{f|確認|かくにん}{f|願|ねが}います）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>ご{f|確認|かくにん}{f|願|ねが}います</b>。

{f|確認|かくにん} is a Sino-Japanese noun, so it takes ご. お goes with native verbs
in this frame - お{f|待|ま}ち, お{f|知|し}らせ, お{f|下|さ}がり - apart from the short
list of exceptions such as お{f|電話|でんわ} and お{f|返事|へんじ}.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese as a written notice: "Please refrain from eating and drinking inside the library."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|図書館内|としょかんない}での{f|飲食|いんしょく}はご{f|遠慮|えんりょ}<b>{f|願|ねが}います</b>。

<code>ご{f|遠慮|えんりょ}{f|願|ねが}います</code> is the standard notice wording for
"please refrain from". ご{f|遠慮|えんりょ}ください is equally idiomatic and a little
softer.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|恐|おそ}れ{f|入|い}りますが、こちらの{f|用紙|ようし}にお{f|書|か}くお{f|願|ねが}いします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|恐|おそ}れ{f|入|い}りますが、こちらの{f|用紙|ようし}にご{f|記入|きにゅう}<b>{f|願|ねが}います</b>。

Two frames have been welded together, and the verb is in the dictionary form. Pick
one: ご{f|記入|きにゅう}{f|願|ねが}います, or the noun construction
{f|記入|きにゅう}をお{f|願|ねが}いします.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) ご{f|署名|しょめい}<b>{f|願|ねが}います</b>。
(b) ご{f|署名|しょめい}<b>{f|願|ねが}えますでしょうか</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) states the requirement. It is polite and entirely normal from a member of staff
processing a form, but it does not invite a reply - you sign.

(b) puts the request in the potential and adds a hedge, so it asks whether the
listener is willing. It is what you use when the person could reasonably say no, or
when they outrank you. It is also noticeably heavier; stacking politeness like this
is common in customer-facing speech, and some speakers consider
{f|願|ねが}えますでしょうか overdone next to the plainer {f|願|ねが}えますか.

</details>
