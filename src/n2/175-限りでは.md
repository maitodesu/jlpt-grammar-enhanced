# ～限りでは

**Meaning** · as far as I know / have seen / have heard - fences a claim inside the information the speaker actually has

Read <code>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</code>. The verb in front is nearly always one of
**finding out**: {f|知|し}る, {f|見|み}る, {f|聞|き}く, {f|調|しら}べる,
{f|読|よ}む, {f|覚|おぼ}えている. The pattern then says: within that evidence the
following is true, and outside it I make no claim. It is not a marker of viewpoint
and not a marker of grounds for inference - it is a marker of **limits**.

## Formation

{f|限|かぎ}り is a noun, so the clause in front is in noun-modifying shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (dictionary) | plain form + {f|限|かぎ}りでは | {f|知|し}る → {f|私|わたし}の{f|知|し}る{f|限|かぎ}りでは |
| Verb (past) | た-form + {f|限|かぎ}りでは | {f|聞|き}いた → {f|聞|き}いた{f|限|かぎ}りでは |
| Verb (ている) | ている + {f|限|かぎ}りでは | {f|覚|おぼ}えている → {f|覚|おぼ}えている{f|限|かぎ}りでは |
| With a subject | Noun + が / の + Verb + {f|限|かぎ}りでは | {f|私|わたし} → {f|私|わたし}が{f|調|しら}べた{f|限|かぎ}りでは |
| Without では | Verb + {f|限|かぎ}り、… | {f|見|み}る → {f|見|み}る{f|限|かぎ}り、… |
| Politeness | carried by the main clause | … → …{f|問題|もんだい}はありません |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the verb has to be one of perceiving, checking or remembering. There is
no ✗{f|食|た}べた{f|限|かぎ}りでは or ✗{f|走|はし}った{f|限|かぎ}りでは; those verbs
produce no information to limit a claim to.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}の{f|知|し}る<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、{f|彼|かれ}はまだ{f|独身|どくしん}だ。
As far as I know, he's still single.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|外|そと}から{f|見|み}た<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、{f|中|なか}の{f|様子|ようす}はわからなかった。
From what I could see from outside, there was no telling what was going on inside.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新聞|しんぶん}で{f|読|よ}んだ<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、{f|事故|じこ}でけがをした{f|人|ひと}はいなかったようだ。
From what I read in the paper, nobody appears to have been hurt in the accident.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|調|しら}べた<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、その{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|見|み}つかりませんでした。
As far as our checks went, we found nothing wrong with that company.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|天気|てんき}{f|予報|よほう}を{f|聞|き}いた<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、{f|週末|しゅうまつ}は{f|晴|は}れるらしい。
Going by what the forecast said, the weekend should be fine.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}が{f|覚|おぼ}えている<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、この{f|辺|へん}には{f|昔|むかし}{f|畑|はたけ}しかなかった。
As far as I can remember, there was nothing round here but fields.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|一通|ひととお}り{f|読|よ}んだ<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|計画|けいかく}に{f|大|おお}きな{f|無理|むり}はなさそうです。
Having read through the documents once, I don't see anything unworkable in this plan.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電話|でんわ}で{f|話|はな}した<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>{f|元気|げんき}そうだったが、{f|実際|じっさい}に{f|会|あ}ってみないと{f|本当|ほんとう}のところはわからない。
He sounded fine on the phone, but until I actually see him I won't really know.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a **hedge**, and a socially useful one. It says the speaker is being
careful about the boundary of their knowledge, which is why it is standard in
business reporting, in medical and legal contexts, and any time you are asked about
something you only half know. It is register-neutral: the pattern is identical in
plain and polite speech.

{f|私|わたし}の{f|知|し}る{f|限|かぎ}り(では) has hardened into a near-set phrase -
the English "as far as I'm aware" - and is the shape you will hear most often.
{f|見|み}た{f|限|かぎ}りでは and {f|調|しら}べた{f|限|かぎ}りでは are the other two
that come up constantly.

Dropping では costs a little. {f|見|み}る{f|限|かぎ}り, without it, is crisper and
common in writing. Adding では brings in は's contrastive edge - "at least by that
measure, whatever other measures may say" - and it pairs naturally with a following
が clause that admits the limits of the evidence.

One caution: because the pattern advertises incomplete knowledge, it makes an
assertion weaker, not stronger. Using it about something you are sure of sounds
evasive, as though you were leaving yourself an exit. If you know, say so plainly.

## Contrast

### {f|限|かぎ}りでは vs {f|限|かぎ}り（as long as）

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with a **knowing / seeing** verb, {f|限|かぎ}り limits the
**claim**. With a state, it limits the **time or condition**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|聞|き}いた<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、{f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|値上|ねあ}げされるそうだ。
From what I've heard, prices go up from next month.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|体|からだ}が{f|元気|げんき}な<b>{f|限|かぎ}り</b>、{f|働|はたら}き{f|続|つづ}けたい。
As long as I'm in good health, I want to keep working.
</pre>

The second is the "as long as" {f|限|かぎ}り, and its negative partner
ない{f|限|かぎ}り means
"unless". They share a noun and nothing else. The test is the verb: a verb of
information gives you this page's pattern.

### {f|限|かぎ}りでは vs によると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>によると</code> names the **source** of a report;
{f|限|かぎ}りでは names the **extent** of the speaker's own checking.

</div>

<pre>
{f|天気|てんき}{f|予報|よほう}<b>によると</b>、{f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}だそうだ。
According to the forecast, it'll rain tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|確|たし}かめた<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、{f|明日|あした}の{f|予約|よやく}は{f|取|と}れていない。
As far as I've checked myself, there's no booking for tomorrow.
</pre>

<code>によると</code> passes responsibility to someone else and usually ends in
そうだ or らしい. {f|限|かぎ}りでは keeps responsibility with the speaker but caps
how far it extends. The two combine happily:
{f|新聞|しんぶん}で{f|読|よ}んだ{f|限|かぎ}りでは… already implies the source.

### {f|限|かぎ}りでは vs ところを{f|見|み}ると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - {f|限|かぎ}りでは **narrows a claim**;
<code>ところを{f|見|み}ると</code> **supplies grounds for a guess**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|見|み}た<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、{f|部屋|へや}に{f|変|か}わったところはなかった。
As far as I could see, there was nothing unusual about the room.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}が{f|荒|あ}らされている<b>ところを{f|見|み}ると</b>、{f|誰|だれ}かが{f|探|さが}し{f|物|もの}をしたらしい。
Judging by the state of the room, somebody was searching for something.
</pre>

Both involve looking, and they point in opposite directions. The first reports what
was found and admits it may not be everything; the second takes what was found and
leaps to a conclusion. That is why the first ends in a plain statement and the
second must end in a conjecture.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|走|はし}った<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、この{f|道|みち}は{f|安全|あんぜん}だ。 → ✅ {f|走|はし}って<b>みた{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、この{f|道|みち}は{f|安全|あんぜん}そうだ。
  The verb has to yield information. Plain {f|走|はし}る does not; てみる rescues
  it by turning the running into a trial.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}は{f|知|し}る<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b> → ✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>の</b>{f|知|し}る{f|限|かぎ}りでは（or {f|私|わたし}<b>が</b>{f|知|し}っている{f|限|かぎ}りでは）
  The clause modifies a noun, so its subject takes の or が - never は.

- ❌ {f|聞|き}く<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。 → ✅ {f|聞|き}いた<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>{f|中止|ちゅうし}になったそうだ。
  If the hearing has already happened, use the た-form; the dictionary form suits
  standing knowledge like {f|知|し}る.

- ❌ {f|調|しら}べた<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、{f|絶対|ぜったい}に{f|問題|もんだい}はない。 → ✅ {f|調|しら}べた<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、{f|問題|もんだい}はなさそうだ。
  The pattern admits the evidence is partial. {f|絶対|ぜったい}に contradicts it in
  the same breath.

## Practice

**1.** {f|私|わたし}が____{f|限|かぎ}りでは、{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|時間|じかん}はまだ{f|決|き}まっていません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|私|わたし}が<b>{f|聞|き}いている</b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは、{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|時間|じかん}はまだ{f|決|き}まっていません。
（{f|知|し}っている also fits）

A verb of information is required, and the ている form suits knowledge the speaker
currently holds. Note the subject takes が, not は, inside the modifying clause.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|体力|たいりょく}がある（{f|限|かぎ}り・{f|限|かぎ}りでは）、この{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|続|つづ}けたい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|体力|たいりょく}がある<b>{f|限|かぎ}り</b>、この{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|続|つづ}けたい。

This is the "as long as" {f|限|かぎ}り: a state sets a condition on the main clause. The
{f|限|かぎ}りでは of this page needs a verb of knowing or checking in front of it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "As far as I could tell from the photos, the flat looked clean."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|写真|しゃしん}で{f|見|み}た<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、その{f|部屋|へや}はきれいそうだった。

The photos are the whole of the evidence, and the pattern fences the judgement
inside them - which is why そうだった rather than a flat だった is the honest
ending.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|私|わたし}は{f|覚|おぼ}えている{f|限|かぎ}りでは、{f|去年|きょねん}も{f|同|おな}じ{f|場所|ばしょ}でやりました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>が</b>{f|覚|おぼ}えている{f|限|かぎ}りでは、{f|去年|きょねん}も{f|同|おな}じ{f|場所|ばしょ}でやりました。

{f|限|かぎ}り is a noun and the clause in front modifies it, so the subject inside
that clause cannot take は. Swap it for が - or use the の of
{f|私|わたし}の{f|知|し}る{f|限|かぎ}りでは.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|薬|くすり}に{f|副作用|ふくさよう}はありません。
(b) {f|調|しら}べた<b>{f|限|かぎ}りでは</b>、この{f|薬|くすり}に{f|副作用|ふくさよう}は{f|報告|ほうこく}されていません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is an unqualified guarantee. The speaker takes responsibility for the whole
claim, which for a medicine is a very large thing to do.

(b) states exactly how far the speaker's knowledge reaches and stops there. It is
the careful, professional version: nothing has turned up in the checking that was
done, and the sentence quietly admits that other checking might turn up something
else.

</details>
