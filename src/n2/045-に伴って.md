# ～に伴って / ～に伴い

**Meaning** · as; along with; in consequence of - one thing happens or changes, and a second thing comes with it

<code>{f|伴|ともな}う</code> is "to accompany". The pattern names a change or an event,
and says that something else **comes along in its train**: prices move and the
timetable is revised, a typhoon approaches and services stop, a company grows and
the paperwork grows with it. It is the connective of notices, announcements and
news reports, and it is deliberately neutral - it reports the pairing without
blaming anyone for it.

## Formation

The front half is a noun naming a change or an event, or a verb of change in the
dictionary form.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun of change | Noun + に{f|伴|ともな}って | {f|高齢化|こうれいか} → {f|高齢化|こうれいか}に{f|伴|ともな}って |
| Noun (stiffer, written) | Noun + に{f|伴|ともな}い | {f|改正|かいせい} → {f|改正|かいせい}に{f|伴|ともな}い |
| Event noun | Noun + に{f|伴|ともな}い | {f|接近|せっきん} → {f|台風|たいふう}の{f|接近|せっきん}に{f|伴|ともな}い |
| Verb (dictionary form) | dictionary + に{f|伴|ともな}って | {f|増|ふ}える → {f|増|ふ}えるに{f|伴|ともな}って |
| Before a noun | ～に{f|伴|ともな}う + Noun | {f|工事|こうじ} → {f|工事|こうじ}に{f|伴|ともな}う{f|通行|つうこう}{f|止|ど}め |
| ❌ Past form | ❌{f|増|ふ}えたに{f|伴|ともな}い | ✅{f|増|ふ}えるに{f|伴|ともな}い |
| ❌ Adjective | ❌{f|寒|さむ}いに{f|伴|ともな}って | ✅{f|寒|さむ}くなるに{f|伴|ともな}って |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the verb also appears as <code>～を{f|伴|ともな}って</code>, which is a
different structure meaning "bringing X along":
<code>{f|通訳|つうやく}を{f|伴|ともな}って{f|出席|しゅっせき}した</code>, "attended with an
interpreter". The connective always takes `に`.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Formal and written. Its natural habitats are the notice on the door, the customer
email, the local-government leaflet and the news bulletin. In relaxed conversation
people say だから or ので and leave it at that.

`に伴い` is the stiffer stem form and is what printed announcements use; `に伴って`
is a shade lighter and is heard in careful speech. <code>に{f|伴|ともな}う</code>
does the same work in front of a noun, and the phrase
<code>～に{f|伴|ともな}う{f|影響|えいきょう}</code> ("the knock-on effects of ～") is
close to a fixed expression in official writing.

The tone is worth noticing. Because the pattern presents the second event as
something that simply **came with** the first, it is a courteous way to announce an
unwelcome consequence: a price rise, a cancellation, a change of procedure. Nobody
is said to have decided anything. That is precisely why companies reach for it.

Two things it does not do. It does not carry emotion - a sentence about how you
felt as the day approached wants something else. And it does not mean "in the
company of a person"; that job belongs to
<code>～を{f|伴|ともな}って</code> or, in ordinary speech,
<code>～と{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</code>.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|伴|ともな}って vs ～につれて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `につれて` needs **two gradual changes moving together**.
<code>に{f|伴|ともな}って</code> accepts that too, but also takes a **single event**
with consequences hanging off it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|年|とし}を{f|取|と}る<b>につれて</b>、{f|朝|あさ}{f|早|はや}く{f|目|め}が{f|覚|さ}めるようになった。
As I have got older, I have started waking up early.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大雨|おおあめ}<b>に{f|伴|ともな}い</b>、{f|川|かわ}の{f|水位|すいい}が{f|急|きゅう}に{f|上|あ}がった。
With the heavy rain, the river level rose sharply.
</pre>

Ageing is a slow slide, so `につれて` fits perfectly. Heavy rain arriving is an
event, not a slide, and ❌<code>{f|大雨|おおあめ}につれて</code> is wrong for that
reason. Where both halves really are gradual, the two are interchangeable and
<code>に{f|伴|ともな}い</code> simply sounds more official.

### ～に{f|伴|ともな}って vs ～とともに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `とともに` says the two things are **simultaneous** and treats
them as equals. <code>に{f|伴|ともな}って</code> makes one the **main event** and the
other its accompaniment.

</div>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}の{f|終了|しゅうりょう}<b>とともに</b>、{f|教室|きょうしつ}が{f|一気|いっき}に{f|騒|さわ}がしくなった。
The moment the exam ended, the classroom erupted into noise.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|駅|えき}の{f|開業|かいぎょう}<b>に{f|伴|ともな}って</b>、バスの{f|路線|ろせん}が{f|見直|みなお}された。
With the opening of the new station, the bus routes were reviewed.
</pre>

`とともに` also does a job this pattern cannot: it links people and things -
<code>{f|家族|かぞく}とともに{f|暮|く}らす</code>. Put a person in front of
<code>に{f|伴|ともな}って</code> and the sentence breaks.

### ～に{f|伴|ともな}って vs ～によって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `によって` names a **cause** and points at it. <code>に{f|伴|ともな}って</code>
reports an **accompanying** change, and deliberately does not point.

</div>

<pre>
{f|地震|じしん}<b>によって</b>{f|水道管|すいどうかん}が{f|壊|こわ}れた。
The earthquake broke the water mains.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|原料費|げんりょうひ}の{f|上昇|じょうしょう}<b>に{f|伴|ともな}い</b>、{f|価格|かかく}を{f|改定|かいてい}させていただきます。
In line with the rise in raw-material costs, we will be revising our prices.
</pre>

The first sentence assigns responsibility to the earthquake. The second could have
said <code>によって</code> too, but the writer chose not to: the price rise is
presented as something that travels alongside the cost increase, which is both
gentler and vaguer. That choice is what makes this pattern so common in
announcements customers will not enjoy.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|妹|いもうと}<b>に{f|伴|ともな}って</b>{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}った。 → ✅ {f|妹|いもうと}と<b>{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</b>{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}った。
  The connective takes a change or an event, never a person. "Accompanied by" is
  <code>を{f|伴|ともな}って</code>, and in speech
  <code>と{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</code>.

- ❌ {f|給料|きゅうりょう}が{f|上|あ}がった<b>に{f|伴|ともな}い</b>、{f|生活|せいかつ}が{f|楽|らく}になった。 → ✅ {f|給料|きゅうりょう}が{f|上|あ}がる<b>に{f|伴|ともな}い</b>、{f|生活|せいかつ}が{f|楽|らく}になった。
  The verb in front stays non-past. Tense lives on the final verb only.

- ❌ {f|寒|さむ}い<b>に{f|伴|ともな}って</b>、{f|客|きゃく}が{f|減|へ}った。 → ✅ {f|寒|さむ}くなる<b>に{f|伴|ともな}って</b>、{f|客|きゃく}が{f|減|へ}った。
  Adjectives cannot attach. Turn the state into a change -
  <code>{f|寒|さむ}くなる</code> - and the pattern works.

- ❌ {f|値上|ねあ}げ<b>に{f|伴|ともな}った</b>、メニューを{f|新|あたら}しくした。 → ✅ {f|値上|ねあ}げ<b>に{f|伴|ともな}い</b>、メニューを{f|新|あたら}しくした。
  To join two clauses, use the stem <code>{f|伴|ともな}い</code> or the て-form
  <code>{f|伴|ともな}って</code>. <code>{f|伴|ともな}う</code> and
  <code>{f|伴|ともな}った</code> can only sit in front of a noun.

## Practice

**1.** {f|少子化|しょうしか}____、{f|小学校|しょうがっこう}の{f|統合|とうごう}が{f|進|すす}んでいる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|少子化|しょうしか}<b>に{f|伴|ともな}い</b>（or に{f|伴|ともな}って）

A falling birth rate and school mergers are two movements travelling together, and
the register - a report on public policy - is exactly where this pattern lives.
<code>につれて</code> would also pass here, since both halves are gradual.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|大雪|おおゆき}（につれて・に{f|伴|ともな}い）、{f|本日|ほんじつ}の{f|営業|えいぎょう}を{f|中止|ちゅうし}いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|大雪|おおゆき}<b>に{f|伴|ともな}い</b>

Heavy snow is a single event rather than a gradual slide, so `につれて` cannot take
it. The closure is a consequence that arrives with the snow, and the notice
register asks for the stem form.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "As the number of tourists has increased, the town has had to build more car parks."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|観光客|かんこうきゃく}が{f|増|ふ}える<b>に{f|伴|ともな}って</b>、{f|町|まち}は{f|駐車場|ちゅうしゃじょう}を{f|増|ふ}やさなければならなくなった。

Both halves are growth, which is what the pattern likes, and the verb in front
stays in the dictionary form even though the English is a perfect tense.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部長|ぶちょう}に{f|伴|ともな}って{f|工場|こうじょう}を{f|見学|けんがく}しました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}と<b>{f|一緒|いっしょ}に</b>{f|工場|こうじょう}を{f|見学|けんがく}しました。
（formal writing: {f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>を{f|伴|ともな}って</b>…）

A person is not a change or an event, so the `に` connective is impossible. Note
that the `を` version does exist, but it is stiff and normally used by the senior
party about the junior one.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|新|あたら}しいシステムの{f|導入|どうにゅう}<b>に{f|伴|ともな}い</b>、{f|作業|さぎょう}の{f|手順|てじゅん}が{f|変|か}わります。
(b) {f|新|あたら}しいシステムの{f|導入|どうにゅう}<b>によって</b>、{f|作業|さぎょう}の{f|手順|てじゅん}が{f|変|か}わります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) presents the new procedure as something that simply comes with the new system.
It is the standard wording of an internal announcement, and it quietly avoids saying
that anyone chose to change the way people work.

(b) names the system as the cause and lets it take the credit or the blame. It is
more analytical and slightly more pointed, and would be the natural choice in a
report explaining why the procedure changed.

</details>
