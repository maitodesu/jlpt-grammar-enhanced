# ～に限って

**Meaning** · of all times, of all people - singles one case out as the exceptional one

<code>～に{f|限|かぎ}って</code> narrows the field to a single member and then says
something that would not be true of the others. With a time expression the effect is
almost always rueful: *of all the days, it had to be that one*. With a person and a
negative it flips warm: *whoever else might have done it, not her*.

## Formation

The pattern attaches to a **noun**. A clause has to be nominalised first - with
<code>とき</code>, <code>{f|日|ひ}</code>, <code>{f|場合|ばあい}</code> and so on.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (time) | Noun + に{f|限|かぎ}って | {f|今日|きょう} → {f|今日|きょう}に{f|限|かぎ}って |
| Clause (time) | plain form + とき／{f|日|ひ} + に{f|限|かぎ}って | {f|急|いそ}いでいる → {f|急|いそ}いでいるときに{f|限|かぎ}って |
| Noun (person) + negative | Noun + に{f|限|かぎ}って … ない | {f|母|はは} → {f|母|はは}に{f|限|かぎ}って…ない |
| Notice / announcement | Noun + に{f|限|かぎ}り | {f|本日|ほんじつ} → {f|本日|ほんじつ}に{f|限|かぎ}り |
| Topic-marked | Noun + に{f|限|かぎ}っては | {f|今回|こんかい} → {f|今回|こんかい}に{f|限|かぎ}っては |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>に{f|限|かぎ}り</code> is the same word in its stem form. It is the
shape used in written announcements, and it is neutral: it carries none of the "of all
days" irony that <code>に{f|限|かぎ}って</code> picks up in speech.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っていない{f|日|ひ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る。
It rains precisely on the days I don't have an umbrella.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|急|いそ}いでいるとき<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>エレベーターがなかなか{f|来|こ}ない。
Whenever I'm in a hurry, that's when the lift takes forever.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|母|はは}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>、そんなうそをつくはずがない。
My mother, of all people, would never tell a lie like that.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本日|ほんじつ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}り</b>、{f|全品|ぜんぴん}{f|二割引|にわりびき}となっております。
Today only, everything is twenty percent off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}の{f|前|まえ}の{f|日|ひ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>、なぜか{f|部屋|へや}の{f|片付|かたづ}けがしたくなる。
It's always the night before an exam that I suddenly want to tidy my room.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}をかけて{f|仕込|しこ}んだ{f|日|ひ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>、お{f|客|きゃく}さんが{f|一人|ひとり}も{f|来|こ}なかった。
On the very day I'd spent hours on the prep, not one customer came in.
</pre>

<pre>

He's the last person who would stand someone up, I think.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|財布|さいふ}を{f|家|いえ}に{f|置|お}いてきた{f|日|ひ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>、{f|帰|かえ}り{f|道|みち}でおいしそうなパン{f|屋|や}を{f|見|み}つけてしまう。
It's always the day I've left my wallet at home that I stumble on a bakery that smells amazing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|普段|ふだん}は{f|静|しず}かな{f|息子|むすこ}が、{f|大事|だいじ}な{f|電話|でんわ}をしているとき<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>{f|大|おお}きな{f|声|こえ}で{f|歌|うた}い{f|出|だ}す。
My son is normally quiet, and yet the moment I'm on an important call he starts singing at the top of his voice.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The time use is the one you will meet most, and it is emotionally loaded. It is a
complaint about the universe: the speaker has noticed a pattern of bad luck and is
pointing at it. Because of that it fits grumbling, storytelling and comedy, and it
pairs naturally with <code>なぜか</code>, <code>どういうわけか</code> and
<code>～てしまう</code>. It is register-neutral in the sense that
<code>に{f|限|かぎ}って</code> itself is neither polite nor rude, but the *attitude*
is casual, so you would not put it in a formal report.

The person use runs the other way and is a small act of loyalty. It needs a negative
predicate - <code>はずがない</code>, <code>ことはない</code>, <code>わけがない</code> -
because the whole point is "not that one, whatever the others are like". Said about
someone who has just been accused, it is a defence.

<code>に{f|限|かぎ}り</code> is the written cousin and belongs to shop notices, ticket
terms and internal announcements: <code>{f|先着|せんちゃく}{f|百名様|ひゃくめいさま}に{f|限|かぎ}り</code>.
There is nothing rueful about it - it simply carves out a special case. Saying
<code>に{f|限|かぎ}り</code> in casual conversation sounds like reading the small print
aloud.

One thing the pattern cannot do is mark a plain habit. "I only go on Sundays" is
<code>{f|日曜日|にちようび}だけ</code>; putting <code>に{f|限|かぎ}って</code> there would
promise an ironic twist that never arrives.

## Contrast

### に{f|限|かぎ}って vs だけ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>だけ</code> restricts and stops there.
<code>に{f|限|かぎ}って</code> restricts **and comments on it** - either "of all things,
that one" or "that one is the exception".

</div>

<pre>
{f|月曜日|げつようび}<b>だけ</b>{f|朝|あさ}の{f|会議|かいぎ}があります。
There's a morning meeting on Mondays only.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|寝坊|ねぼう}した{f|日|ひ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>{f|朝|あさ}の{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|入|はい}っている。
It's always the mornings I oversleep that turn out to have a meeting in them.
</pre>

The first is a timetable. The second is a grievance. If you can replace the sentence
with a shrug, you want <code>に{f|限|かぎ}って</code>; if you are just listing when
something happens, you want <code>だけ</code>.

### に{f|限|かぎ}って vs こそ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both single one case out, but <code>こそ</code> singles it out as
**the good one** ("this time for sure"), and <code>に{f|限|かぎ}って</code> singles it
out as **the unlucky one**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|今度|こんど}<b>こそ</b>この{f|企画|きかく}を{f|通|とお}してみせる。
This time I really will get this proposal through.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今回|こんかい}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>、いつもは{f|通|とお}る{f|企画|きかく}が{f|通|とお}らなかった。
This time of all times, a proposal that normally sails through didn't get approved.
</pre>

They are not interchangeable even where both are grammatical. <code>こそ</code> looks
forward with resolve; <code>に{f|限|かぎ}って</code> looks back and shakes its head.

### に{f|限|かぎ}って vs に{f|限|かぎ}らず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - mirror images. <code>に{f|限|かぎ}って</code> says *this one and no
other*; <code>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</code> says *not just this one - the others too*.

</div>

<pre>
{f|土日|どにち}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>、この{f|道|みち}はひどく{f|混|こ}む。
It's on Saturdays and Sundays in particular that this road gets badly jammed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|土日|どにち}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>、この{f|道|みち}は{f|平日|へいじつ}も{f|混|こ}んでいる。
It isn't just weekends - this road is jammed on weekdays too.
</pre>

Same noun, same road, opposite claims. The <code>ず</code> is an old negative ending, so
<code>{f|限|かぎ}らず</code> is literally "not limiting it to".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>{f|忙|いそが}しいに{f|限|かぎ}って</b>{f|電話|でんわ}が{f|鳴|な}る。 → ✅ <b>{f|忙|いそが}しいときに{f|限|かぎ}って</b>{f|電話|でんわ}が{f|鳴|な}る。
  The pattern needs a noun in front of it. An adjective or verb clause has to be
  wrapped in <code>とき</code> or <code>{f|日|ひ}</code> first.

- ❌ うちの{f|子|こ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>そんなことをする。 → ✅ うちの{f|子|こ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>そんなことは<b>しない</b>。
  The person use exists to *exclude* someone. Without a negative there is nothing for
  the exclusion to bite on.

- ❌ {f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っている{f|日|ひ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る。 → ✅ {f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っていない{f|日|ひ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る。
  Grammatical, but it misses the point. The pattern reports a mismatch between the
  situation and what happens in it; line them up and the irony disappears.

- ❌ {f|水曜日|すいようび}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>ジムに{f|通|かよ}っています。 → ✅ {f|水曜日|すいようび}<b>だけ</b>ジムに{f|通|かよ}っています。
  A plain habit is <code>だけ</code>. <code>に{f|限|かぎ}って</code> would leave the
  listener waiting for the bad news.

## Practice

**1.** {f|洗|あら}った{f|車|くるま}を{f|外|そと}に{f|止|と}めた{f|日|ひ}____、{f|夕方|ゆうがた}から{f|雨|あめ}になった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|止|と}めた{f|日|ひ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>、{f|夕方|ゆうがた}から{f|雨|あめ}になった。

The clause has already been nominalised with <code>{f|日|ひ}</code>, so the pattern
attaches directly. The washed car and the rain are exactly the kind of mismatch this
pattern exists to complain about.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|美術館|びじゅつかん}は{f|大人|おとな}（に{f|限|かぎ}って・に{f|限|かぎ}らず）、{f|子|こ}どもも{f|楽|たの}しめる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|大人|おとな}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>、{f|子|こ}どもも{f|楽|たの}しめる。

The second half adds children with <code>も</code>, so the sentence is widening the
field, not narrowing it. That is the <code>ず</code> form.
<code>に{f|限|かぎ}って</code> would contradict what follows.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Of all days, the printer broke down on the morning of the presentation."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|発表|はっぴょう}の{f|朝|あさ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>、プリンターが{f|壊|こわ}れた。

<code>{f|朝|あさ}</code> is already a noun, so nothing has to be added. "Of all days"
is carried entirely by the pattern - there is no separate word for it.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|田中|たなか}さんに{f|限|かぎ}って、{f|人|ひと}のものを{f|勝手|かって}に{f|使|つか}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|田中|たなか}さんに{f|限|かぎ}って、{f|人|ひと}のものを{f|勝手|かって}に<b>{f|使|つか}うわけがない</b>。

Applied to a person, this pattern is a vote of confidence, and it needs a negative
predicate to complete the thought. As written, the sentence sets up a defence and then
delivers an accusation.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|本日|ほんじつ}に{f|限|かぎ}り、{f|入場料|にゅうじょうりょう}が{f|無料|むりょう}です。
(b) {f|来|き}てほしくない{f|日|ひ}に{f|限|かぎ}って、{f|親戚|しんせき}が{f|訪|たず}ねてくる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is an announcement. <code>に{f|限|かぎ}り</code> carves out today as a special case
and stops there - no feeling attached, which is why it fits a poster at the entrance.

(b) is a complaint. <code>に{f|限|かぎ}って</code> in speech points at a run of bad
timing and invites the listener to sympathise. Swapping the two forms would make (a)
sound resentful about its own offer and (b) sound like a scheduling notice.

</details>
