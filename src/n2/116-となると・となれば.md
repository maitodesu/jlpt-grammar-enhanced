# ～となると・～となれば

**Meaning** · if it really comes to that - then here is what follows

`となると` takes a situation that is now genuinely on the table and works out its
consequences: *supposing this actually happens, look what it drags with it.* A second,
lighter use shifts the topic - "when it comes to X" - and hands the floor to whatever
the speaker wants to say about X.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + となると / となれば | {f|結婚|けっこん} → {f|結婚|けっこん}となると |
| Verb | plain form + となると | {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}す → {f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}すとなると |
| Verb (past) | ～た + となると | {f|決|き}まった → {f|決|き}まったとなると |
| い-adjective | plain form + となると | {f|忙|いそが}しい → {f|忙|いそが}しいとなると |
| な-adjective | stem + となると | {f|必要|ひつよう} → {f|必要|ひつよう}となると |
| Casual variant | となったら | {f|買|か}う → {f|買|か}うとなったら |

<div class="note">

**Note** - nouns attach bare. There is no `だ`: <code>{f|海外|かいがい}{f|勤務|きんむ}となると</code>,
never <code>{f|海外|かいがい}{f|勤務|きんむ}だとなると</code>. The `と` in `となる` is already
doing that work.

</div>

</pre>

</pre>

<pre>

## Nuance & register

The heart of this pattern is *realism*. `となると` is not for daydreams; it is for a
possibility that has just become concrete enough to have consequences - the transfer
has been floated, the wedding date is being discussed, the budget has arrived. That is
why it so often follows <code>いざ</code>, "when it actually comes to it", and why it
appears at the moment a conversation turns practical.

`となると` and `となれば` are close enough to swap in most sentences. `となると` leans
slightly towards a situation already taking shape; `となれば` leans slightly towards
supposing, and is the more common of the two in writing and in careful speech. The
casual spoken variant is `となったら`, and in relaxed conversation you also hear plain
`だと` doing part of the same work.

The topic-shift use - <code>{f|和食|わしょく}となると、この{f|店|みせ}が{f|一番|いちばん}だ</code> -
carries a hint that the topic is a weighty or specialist one. Applied to something
trivial it sounds mock-grand, which is occasionally the joke. For an everyday "if it's
lunch you're after", `なら` is the natural choice.

The consequence clause is flexible: a prediction, an obligation, a difficulty, or - with
<code>そうとなれば</code> - a call to action. Unlike most of the `では` family in this
group, `となると` does not require the outcome to be bad, only to be a real consequence.

## Contrast

### ～となると vs ～とすると・～としたら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `とすると` **supposes** something for the sake of argument.
`となると` treats the situation as one that is genuinely arising.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}だ<b>とすると</b>、{f|運動会|うんどうかい}は{f|延期|えんき}ですね。
Supposing it rains tomorrow, sports day gets postponed, then.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}が{f|直撃|ちょくげき}する<b>となると</b>、{f|延期|えんき}の{f|連絡|れんらく}を{f|今日中|きょうじゅう}に{f|出|だ}さなければ。
If the typhoon is really going to hit us, we'll have to send out the postponement notice today.
</pre>

Both are conditionals about the same event, but the second speaker has looked at the
forecast and is now organising. `とすると` can entertain anything; `となると` implies the
speaker takes the premise seriously enough to act on it.

### ～となると vs ～なら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なら` picks up what the other person just said. `となると` weighs
what a situation would entail.

</div>

<pre>
{f|京都|きょうと}に{f|行|い}く<b>なら</b>、この{f|地図|ちず}を{f|持|も}っていくといいよ。
If you're going to Kyoto, take this map with you.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|家族|かぞく}{f|全員|ぜんいん}で{f|行|い}く<b>となると</b>、{f|宿|やど}を{f|探|さが}すのが{f|大変|たいへん}だ。
If the whole family is going, finding somewhere to stay is going to be a job.
</pre>

`なら` responds and advises; `となると` calculates. Notice too that `なら` sits happily on
a light, immediate topic, while `となると` wants something with consequences attached -
a decision, a commitment, an expense.

### ～となると vs ～というと

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - in the topic-shift use, `というと` calls up an **association**.
`となると` asks what the topic **demands** or who is best placed for it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}<b>というと</b>、まず{f|雪|ゆき}を{f|思|おも}い{f|浮|う}かべます。
Say Hokkaido and the first thing I picture is snow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}{f|旅行|りょこう}の{f|計画|けいかく}<b>となると</b>、やはり{f|山田|やまだ}さんに{f|相談|そうだん}したほうがいい。
When it comes to planning a Hokkaido trip, you really ought to consult Yamada.
</pre>

`というと` is about what the word brings to mind. `となると` is about what the matter
requires now that it has come up, and it very often ends by nominating a person, a
method or a cost.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|転勤|てんきん}だ<b>となると</b>、{f|家族|かぞく}と{f|相談|そうだん}が{f|必要|ひつよう}だ。 → ✅ {f|転勤|てんきん}<b>となると</b>、{f|家族|かぞく}と{f|相談|そうだん}が{f|必要|ひつよう}だ。
  A noun attaches with nothing in between. The `だ` is already contained in `となる`.

- ❌ {f|宝|たから}くじで{f|一億円|いちおくえん}{f|当|あ}たる<b>となると</b>、{f|世界|せかい}を{f|旅|たび}したい。 → ✅ {f|宝|たから}くじで{f|一億円|いちおくえん}{f|当|あ}たっ<b>たら</b>、{f|世界|せかい}を{f|旅|たび}したい。
  A lottery win is a daydream, not a prospect. `となると` needs a situation that is
  actually in view.

- ❌ {f|春|はる}になる<b>となると</b>、{f|桜|さくら}が{f|咲|さ}く。 → ✅ {f|春|はる}になる<b>と</b>、{f|桜|さくら}が{f|咲|さ}く。
  An automatic natural consequence is the plain `と` of N4. `となると` would suggest
  spring were a decision somebody had to weigh.

- ❌ お{f|昼|ひる}ごはん<b>となると</b>、パンでいいです。 → ✅ お{f|昼|ひる}ごはん<b>なら</b>、パンでいいです。
  Lunch is too small a matter to carry `となると`, which makes the topic sound like a
  grave undertaking.

## Practice

**1.** {f|来月|らいげつ}から{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|担当|たんとう}する____、{f|今|いま}のうちに{f|引|ひ}き{f|継|つ}ぎをしておかないと。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|担当|たんとう}する<b>となると</b>（or となれば）

The handover has become a real prospect with practical consequences attached, which is
precisely when `となると` is used rather than a plain conditional.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|飲|の}み{f|物|もの}は{f|何|なに}がいい? - お{f|茶|ちゃ}（なら・となると）{f|冷|つめ}たいのがいいな。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

お{f|茶|ちゃ}<b>なら</b>{f|冷|つめ}たいのがいいな。

This is a light reply picking up what the other person just offered. `となると` would
make choosing a drink sound like a matter of state.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If we're going to redo the whole thing, we'll need to talk to the client first."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|全部|ぜんぶ}やり{f|直|なお}す<b>となれば</b>、まず{f|取引先|とりひきさき}に{f|話|はなし}を{f|通|とお}す{f|必要|ひつよう}があります。

The redo is a live possibility with knock-on obligations, so `となれば` fits; `となると`
would be equally acceptable here.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: パーティーの{f|司会|しかい}だとなると、{f|準備|じゅんび}が{f|大変|たいへん}そうだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ パーティーの{f|司会|しかい}<b>となると</b>、{f|準備|じゅんび}が{f|大変|たいへん}そうだ。

The noun <code>{f|司会|しかい}</code> attaches directly. Inserting `だ` in front of
`となると` is the single most common slip with this pattern.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}が{f|辞|や}める<b>としたら</b>、{f|後任|こうにん}はだれだろう。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}が{f|辞|や}める<b>となると</b>、{f|後任|こうにん}を{f|急|いそ}いで{f|探|さが}さなければならない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is speculation. The speaker is entertaining a possibility and idly wondering who
would replace him; nothing has happened yet.

(b) treats the resignation as something now in prospect - perhaps he has hinted at it -
and moves straight to the work it creates. `となると` is what turns the thought into a
practical problem.

</details>
