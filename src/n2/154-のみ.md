# ～のみ

**Meaning** · only ～ - the written, formal counterpart of だけ

<code>のみ</code> means exactly what <code>だけ</code> means. That is the honest
starting point: there is no nuance hiding here that <code>だけ</code> lacks. What
differs is **register**. <code>のみ</code> is the word that appears on notices, in
contracts, in academic prose and in headlines, and reading it aloud in a normal
conversation sounds like quoting a document at your friend.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + のみ | {f|会員|かいいん} → {f|会員|かいいん}のみ |
| Noun + case particle | Noun + に／で／から + のみ | {f|書面|しょめん} → {f|書面|しょめん}でのみ |
| Noun (subject / object) | Noun + のみが／のみを | {f|事実|じじつ} → {f|事実|じじつ}のみを |
| Modifying a noun | Noun + のみの | {f|一回|いっかい} → {f|一回|いっかい}のみの |
| Verb | plain form + のみだ | {f|待|ま}つ → {f|待|ま}つのみだ |
| Polite | … + のみです／のみとなります | {f|現金|げんきん} → {f|現金|げんきん}のみとなります |
| Fixed emphatic shape | ただ … のみ | {f|祈|いの}る → ただ{f|祈|いの}るのみ |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>のみ</code> attaches to nouns and to plain verb forms. It does not sit
after an い-adjective: <code>{f|安|やす}いのみだ</code> is wrong, and
<code>{f|安|やす}いだけだ</code> is what you want. The one adjective-friendly shape is
the literary <code>のみならず</code>.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Everything about <code>のみ</code> is register. It is a survival from classical Japanese
and it has stayed in the written language: signage, terms and conditions, official
notices, academic writing, formal announcements. In those places it is not stiff at all -
it is simply correct, and <code>だけ</code> in a contract would look slack.

In speech it is a different matter. <code>{f|昨日|きのう}はラーメンのみ{f|食|た}べた</code>
is grammatical and sounds absurd, in roughly the way "I consumed solely ramen" sounds in
English. Spoken Japanese does use it, but in marked places: staff announcements
(<code>{f|本日|ほんじつ}は{f|現金|げんきん}のみとなります</code>), formal presentations,
and the dramatic <code>ただ～のみだ</code>, which is why sports commentary and
determination speeches reach for <code>あとは{f|走|はし}るのみ</code>.

That <code>ただ～のみ</code> shape is worth learning as a unit. It concentrates the
sentence on one remaining action and carries real weight -
<code>ただ{f|祈|いの}るのみです</code> is what someone says when nothing else can be
done. Note that this is emphasis, not extra meaning: it is still just "only".

There is also <code>のみならず</code> ("not only… but"), the literary sibling of
<code>だけでなく</code>, which has its own page. Recognise it; you will meet it in
editorials.

Practical advice: **read** <code>のみ</code> everywhere, **write** it in formal
documents, and in conversation use <code>だけ</code> unless you are deliberately
quoting a rule.

## Contrast

### のみ vs だけ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same meaning. <code>だけ</code> works at every level of the
language; <code>のみ</code> belongs to the written and the formal, and it attaches to
fewer things.

</div>

<pre>
{f|残|のこ}っているのはこの{f|一枚|いちまい}<b>だけ</b>だよ。
This one sheet is all that's left.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|残部|ざんぶ}は{f|一部|いちぶ}<b>のみ</b>となっております。
Only one copy remains.
</pre>

Both sentences say the same thing about the same piece of paper; one is a friend
speaking, the other a shop assistant. Note also the attachment difference:
<code>だけ</code> follows adjectives and many more shapes than <code>のみ</code> does,
so when in doubt <code>だけ</code> is always available and <code>のみ</code> is not.

### のみ vs しか～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>のみ</code> states the restriction **positively and
neutrally**. <code>しか～ない</code> states it **negatively**, and adds a feeling that
it is not enough.

</div>

<pre>
お{f|支払|しはら}いは{f|現金|げんきん}<b>のみ</b>{f|承|うけたまわ}ります。
Payment is accepted in cash only.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|財布|さいふ}には{f|千円|せんえん}<b>しか</b>{f|入|はい}っていない。
There's only a thousand yen in my wallet.
</pre>

The first is a policy stated flatly. The second is a small complaint. This is why a shop
sign says <code>{f|現金|げんきん}のみ</code> and never
<code>{f|現金|げんきん}しか{f|使|つか}えない</code> - the second would be siding with the
customer's disappointment.

### のみ vs に{f|限|かぎ}る・に{f|限|かぎ}り

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both appear on notices. <code>のみ</code> **labels** the eligible
group; <code>に{f|限|かぎ}る</code> **states the rule** that restricts it to that group.

</div>

<pre>
{f|関係者|かんけいしゃ}<b>のみ</b>{f|入室|にゅうしつ}{f|可|か}。
Authorised personnel only.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|入室|にゅうしつ}は{f|関係者|かんけいしゃ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}ります</b>。
Entry is restricted to authorised personnel.
</pre>

<code>のみ</code> is compact enough for a sign or a form field, which is why it wins
where space is tight. <code>に{f|限|かぎ}る</code> is a full predicate and reads as a
regulation being pronounced. Where a document has room for a sentence, it usually chooses
the second.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|土曜日|どようび}<b>のみ</b>{f|遊|あそ}びに{f|行|い}こうよ。 → ✅ {f|土曜日|どようび}<b>だけ</b>{f|遊|あそ}びに{f|行|い}こうよ。
  An invitation to a friend is spoken Japanese. <code>のみ</code> clashes with the
  casual ending and sounds like a parody of officialese.

- ❌ {f|会員|かいいん}<b>のみしか</b>{f|入|はい}れません。 → ✅ {f|会員|かいいん}<b>のみ</b>{f|入|はい}れます。／{f|会員|かいいん}<b>しか</b>{f|入|はい}れません。
  <code>のみ</code> and <code>しか</code> both carry the restriction, so stacking them
  is redundant. Pick one.

- ❌ この{f|店|みせ}は<b>{f|安|やす}いのみだ</b>。 → ✅ この{f|店|みせ}は<b>{f|安|やす}いだけだ</b>。
  <code>のみ</code> does not follow an い-adjective. Nouns and plain verb forms only.

- ❌ {f|試験|しけん}に{f|必要|ひつよう}なのは{f|鉛筆|えんぴつ}<b>のみだけ</b>です。 → ✅ {f|試験|しけん}に{f|必要|ひつよう}なのは{f|鉛筆|えんぴつ}<b>のみ</b>です。
  Same problem from the other side. The two words are synonyms, not a compound.

## Practice

**1.** ご{f|入場|にゅうじょう}は{f|事前|じぜん}に{f|予約|よやく}された{f|方|かた}____とさせていただきます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|予約|よやく}された{f|方|かた}<b>のみ</b>とさせていただきます。

<code>{f|方|かた}</code> is a noun, so the particle attaches directly, and the formal
<code>とさせていただきます</code> around it is exactly the register
<code>のみ</code> belongs to. <code>だけ</code> would work grammatically but would sit
oddly against the humble ending.

</details>

**2.** Choose: 「ごめん、{f|今日|きょう}の{f|昼|ひる}は（おにぎりのみ・おにぎりだけ）{f|食|た}べたんだ」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

「{f|今日|きょう}の{f|昼|ひる}はおにぎり<b>だけ</b>{f|食|た}べたんだ」

Casual speech, casual particle. <code>のみ</code> would be grammatical and would make
the speaker sound as though they were filing a report on their own lunch.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "This offer is available to first-time customers only."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|特典|とくてん}は{f|初|はじ}めてご{f|利用|りよう}のお{f|客様|きゃくさま}<b>のみ</b>{f|対象|たいしょう}となります。

Promotional copy is written Japanese, so <code>のみ</code> is the natural choice. The
noun phrase in front of it can be as long as you like; the particle simply follows it.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: あとは{f|結果|けっか}を{f|待|ま}つのみしかない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ あとは{f|結果|けっか}を{f|待|ま}つ<b>のみだ</b>。（or あとは{f|結果|けっか}を{f|待|ま}つ<b>しかない</b>）

<code>のみ</code> and <code>しかない</code> are both doing the restricting, so the
sentence says it twice. The first version is the literary "all that remains is to wait";
the second is the plainer "there's nothing to do but wait".

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|資料|しりょう}は{f|社内|しゃない}のみで{f|使用|しよう}してください。
(b) この{f|資料|しりょう}、{f|社内|しゃない}だけで{f|使|つか}ってね。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reads as an instruction attached to the document itself - the sort of line printed in
the footer or written in a covering email. The formality makes it binding in tone, and
nobody would take it personally.

(b) is the same restriction passed on between colleagues who know each other. It relies
on goodwill rather than on policy, and it would look out of place stamped on the page.
The content is identical; only the social setting changes.

</details>
