# 万が一

**Meaning** · まんがいち - in the unlikely event that ～; if by any chance

<code>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</code> says "one chance in ten thousand". It flags the
condition that follows as remote and, almost always, as something you would rather
avoid - which is why the sentence usually ends in a precaution.

## Usage

<code>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</code> opens the clause and hands the work to a conditional.

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</code> **requires a conditional** -
たら, ば, なら, と, or {f|場合|ばあい}は. On its own it is only half a sentence.

</div>

- {f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}{f|地震|じしん}が{f|起|お}き**たら**、テーブルの{f|下|した}に{f|入|はい}ってください。
- {f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わ**なければ**、{f|連絡|れんらく}します。
- {f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}の{f|場合|ばあい}**は**、この{f|番号|ばんごう}にかけてください。

Two other shapes are worth knowing:

- <code>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}の + {f|名詞|めいし}</code> - "of emergency":
  {f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}の{f|場合|ばあい}, {f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}の{f|事故|じこ}
- <code>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}に{f|備|そな}える</code> - a fixed phrase, "to
  prepare for the worst", where the adverb has hardened into a noun

The second half is normally an instruction, a precaution, or a plan: ～てください,
～ことになっている, ～つもりです. A <code>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</code> clause followed by
nothing actionable feels unfinished.

The shorter {f|万一|まんいち} is the same word with the `が` dropped. It is a
degree more clipped and more written, and it is what you will see on printed
safety notices.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</b>{f|遅|おく}れたら、{f|先|さき}に{f|始|はじ}めてください。
If by any chance I'm late, please start without me.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</b>のために、{f|保険|ほけん}に{f|入|はい}っておきました。
I took out insurance, just in case the worst happens.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</b>{f|火事|かじ}が{f|起|お}きた{f|場合|ばあい}は、エレベーターを{f|使|つか}わずに{f|階段|かいだん}で{f|避難|ひなん}してください。
In the event of a fire, please evacuate by the stairs and do not use the lift.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}だとは{f|思|おも}いますが、<b>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</b>データが{f|消|き}えてしまうと{f|取|と}り{f|戻|もど}せませんので、{f|必|かなら}ずバックアップをお{f|願|ねが}いします。
I'm sure it will be fine, but on the small chance the data is lost there's no recovering it, so please do take a backup.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Formal-leaning but in constant everyday use. You meet it in announcements, safety
briefings, insurance documents, business email and instruction manuals, and also
in ordinary careful speech between adults - a parent saying
「{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}のときはここに{f|電話|でんわ}して」is completely natural.
It is not marked for age or gender.

Two things it always adds to a plain もし. First, **improbability**: the speaker
is signalling that they do not expect this. Second, **undesirability**: the
default reading of a <code>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</code> clause is that the event would be
bad. Both matter, and getting them wrong is what makes learner sentences sound
strange. {f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}{f|明日|あした}{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}ったら, said
of a rainy season forecast, is odd not because it is ungrammatical but because
rain tomorrow is neither remote nor a catastrophe.

Used of a windfall - {f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}{f|宝|たから}くじが{f|当|あ}たったら -
the word still works, but it reads as self-deprecating or jokey, because it is
lending the language of disaster planning to good luck. That is a stylistic
choice, not an error, and Japanese speakers make it deliberately.

There is also a euphemistic use: 「{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}のことがあったら」means
"if something should happen to him", i.e. if he dies. Recognise it; it is the
polite way the subject is raised.

## Contrast

### {f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち} vs もし・もしも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `もし` marks a clause as hypothetical and says nothing about
how likely it is. <code>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</code> says the chance is tiny **and** the
outcome is unwelcome.

</div>

<pre>
<b>もし</b>{f|時間|じかん}があったら、{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}きませんか。
If you have time, shall we go and see a film?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</b>{f|時間|じかん}に{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わなかったら、タクシーを{f|使|つか}ってください。
On the off-chance you can't make it in time, take a taxi.
</pre>

Swapping them into each other's sentences shows the difference. もし in the second
would drain the urgency out of a contingency plan; <code>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</code> in
the first would make an invitation sound like a disaster drill. `もしも` sits
between them - more emphatic than もし, but without the "and it would be bad"
component.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っていきます。 → ✅ <b>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</b>{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っ<b>たら</b>{f|困|こま}るので、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っていきます。
  A conditional clause is not optional. Without one the adverb has no condition to
  call unlikely. For a bare "just in case" before a verb, use
  {f|一応|いちおう} or {f|念|ねん}のため.

- ❌ {f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}{f|合格|ごうかく}したら、うれしいです。 → ✅ <b>もし</b>{f|合格|ごうかく}したら、うれしいです。
  Passing is a good outcome, so the disaster framing works against the sentence.
  Said about yourself it can pass as modesty, but it is not the neutral choice.

- ❌ {f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|会議|かいぎ}があったら、{f|参加|さんか}します。 → ✅ <b>もし</b>{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|会議|かいぎ}があったら、{f|参加|さんか}します。
  An ordinary uncertain event is not a one-in-ten-thousand event. Reserve the word
  for things you are genuinely not expecting.

- ❌ {f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}が{f|起|お}きたら、{f|連絡|れんらく}してください。 → ✅ <b>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}のこと</b>が{f|起|お}きたら、{f|連絡|れんらく}してください。
  As a noun it needs `のこと` or <code>の{f|場合|ばあい}</code>. The bare adverb cannot take a
  subject particle.

## Practice

**1.** ____{f|停電|ていでん}になったら、この{f|懐中電灯|かいちゅうでんとう}を{f|使|つか}ってください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</b>{f|停電|ていでん}になったら、この{f|懐中電灯|かいちゅうでんとう}を{f|使|つか}ってください。

A power cut is unlikely and unwelcome, and the second half is an instruction for
dealing with it - the three ingredients this adverb wants. The `たら` supplies the
conditional it cannot do without.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: （もし・{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}）よかったら、{f|今度|こんど}うちに{f|遊|あそ}びに{f|来|き}てください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>もし</b>よかったら、{f|今度|こんど}うちに{f|遊|あそ}びに{f|来|き}てください。

This is an invitation, and there is nothing remote or unfortunate about the
listener being free. <code>{f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}</code> would imply the speaker considers a
visit an unlikely calamity.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) もし{f|事故|じこ}が{f|起|お}きたら、すぐ{f|報告|ほうこく}してください。
(b) {f|万|まん}が{f|一|いち}{f|事故|じこ}が{f|起|お}きたら、すぐ{f|報告|ほうこく}してください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are correct instructions. (a) simply sets up a hypothetical and reads as a
neutral rule in a manual. (b) adds that the speaker regards an accident as very
unlikely, which softens the instruction into reassurance - we do not expect this,
but here is what to do. In a safety briefing (b) is the more considerate wording,
because it says the risk is small before it says what to do about it.

</details>
