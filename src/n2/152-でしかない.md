# ～でしかない

**Meaning** · is nothing but ～; is merely ～ and nothing more

Take it apart and it is transparent: <code>で</code> (the copula) +
<code>しか</code> + <code>ない</code>. "It is only ～ and is not anything else." The
<code>しか～ない</code> you already know is doing all the work, and it brings its
emotional colouring with it. <code>でしかない</code> does not measure - it **verdicts**.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + でしかない | {f|無駄|むだ} → {f|無駄|むだ}でしかない |
| な-adjective (stem) | stem + でしかない | {f|迷惑|めいわく} → {f|迷惑|めいわく}でしかない |
| Nominalised clause | ～こと／もの + でしかない | {f|逃|に}げること → {f|逃|に}げることでしかない |
| Polite | … + でしかありません | {f|想像|そうぞう} → {f|想像|そうぞう}でしかありません |
| Past | … + でしかなかった | {f|夢|ゆめ} → {f|夢|ゆめ}でしかなかった |
| Mid-sentence | … + でしかなく、… | {f|通過点|つうかてん} → {f|通過点|つうかてん}でしかなく、… |

<div class="note">

**Note** - a plain verb cannot attach. <code>{f|行|い}くでしかない</code> is not
Japanese. To put an action inside the pattern, nominalise it
(<code>{f|行|い}くことでしかない</code>) or switch to
<code>{f|行|い}ったにすぎない</code>.

</div>

<pre>
そんなやり{f|方|かた}は{f|時間|じかん}の{f|無駄|むだ}<b>でしかない</b>。

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

What he's saying strikes me as pure self-indulgence and nothing else.

<pre>

A report that just lines up figures is nothing but a nuisance to a manager.
</pre>

<pre>

That promise is nothing now but a bitter memory.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|準備|じゅんび}なしに{f|始|はじ}めた{f|計画|けいかく}は、{f|周|まわ}りを{f|巻|ま}き{f|込|こ}む{f|混乱|こんらん}<b>でしかなかった</b>。

</pre>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>でしかない</code> is emphatic and slightly hot. Because it inherits
<code>しか～ない</code>, it carries that particle's built-in sense of *insufficiency* -
whatever is being named, it falls short. Add the flat copula and you get a verdict: this
is what the thing is, and it is not enough.

That makes it a favourite of opinion writing, reviews, editorials and argumentative
speech, and it is extremely common online. It is a shade more modern and more emotive
than <code>にすぎない</code>, which does the same work in a cooler voice. Neither is
casual chat, but <code>でしかない</code> can appear in an impassioned spoken argument in
a way <code>にすぎず</code> never would.

Two consequences follow for use. First, it is **strong**. Saying
<code>それは{f|言|い}い{f|訳|わけ}でしかありません</code> to a colleague is close to
calling them dishonest; the polite ending does not soften the content. Second, it needs a
**standard to fall short of**. The sentence works because the listener can supply what
the thing *ought* to have been - a real plan, a real argument, real kindness. Without
that background the verdict sounds arbitrary.

There is no casual contraction, and it is not marked for age or gender. What is marked is
temperature.

## Contrast

### でしかない vs にすぎない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different heat.
<code>にすぎない</code> is analytical and takes numbers and verbs;
<code>でしかない</code> is evaluative and takes nouns.

</div>

<pre>
{f|賛成|さんせい}したのは{f|委員|いいん}の{f|半分|はんぶん}<b>にすぎなかった</b>。
No more than half the committee voted in favour.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|中身|なかみ}のない{f|議論|ぎろん}は{f|時間|じかん}のむだづかい<b>でしかなかった</b>。
A discussion with nothing in it was pure squandering of time.
</pre>

Try the swap and the mismatch is audible. A committee count with
<code>でしかなかった</code> sounds like the speaker is furious about it; an angry verdict
with <code>にすぎなかった</code> sounds like a footnote. Also note the grammar: a plain
verb goes only with <code>にすぎない</code>.

### でしかない vs しかない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>しかない</code> after a **verb** means "there's no choice but
to". <code>でしかない</code> after a **noun** means "is nothing but". The
<code>で</code> is the whole difference.

</div>

<pre>
{f|終電|しゅうでん}が{f|行|い}ってしまった。{f|歩|ある}いて{f|帰|かえ}る<b>しかない</b>。
The last train's gone. There's nothing for it but to walk home.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|案|あん}は{f|問題|もんだい}の{f|先送|さきおく}り<b>でしかない</b>。
This proposal is nothing but kicking the problem down the road.
</pre>

Learners produce <code>{f|歩|ある}いて{f|帰|かえ}るでしかない</code> regularly, and it
is not Japanese. Check what precedes: a verb takes bare
<code>しかない</code>, a noun takes <code>でしかない</code>.

### でしかない vs ではない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ではない</code> **rejects** the label.
<code>でしかない</code> **accepts** it and caps it: yes, it is that, and that is all it
is.

</div>

<pre>
これは{f|一時的|いちじてき}な{f|対応|たいおう}<b>ではない</b>。{f|根本|こんぽん}から{f|変|か}えるつもりだ。
This is not a stopgap. We intend to change it at the root.
</pre>

<pre>
これは{f|一時的|いちじてき}な{f|対応|たいおう}<b>でしかない</b>。{f|根本|こんぽん}の{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|残|のこ}ったままだ。
This is nothing but a stopgap. The underlying problem is still there.
</pre>

Same noun, opposite claims, one syllable apart. This is worth reading aloud a few times,
because in fast speech the difference between <code>では</code> and
<code>でしか</code> is easy to miss and the meanings are contradictory.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|無駄|むだ}<b>だでしかない</b>。 → ✅ {f|無駄|むだ}<b>でしかない</b>。
  <code>で</code> is already the copula. Adding <code>だ</code> in front doubles it.

- ❌ {f|待|ま}つ<b>でしかない</b>。 → ✅ {f|待|ま}つ<b>しかない</b>。
  A verb takes <code>しかない</code> on its own. With <code>で</code> the sentence
  becomes ungrammatical, and the meaning you wanted ("no choice but to wait") is a
  different pattern anyway.

- ❌ この{f|絵|え}は<b>{f|美|うつく}しいでしかない</b>。 → ✅ この{f|絵|え}は<b>{f|美|うつく}しいだけだ</b>。
  い-adjectives do not attach. Only nouns and な-adjective stems do.

- ❌ 「お{f|客様|きゃくさま}のご{f|意見|いけん}は{f|個人|こじん}の{f|感想|かんそう}<b>でしかありません</b>」 → ✅ 「{f|貴重|きちょう}なご{f|意見|いけん}をありがとうございます」
  The polite ending does not make the content polite. The pattern dismisses what it
  names, and a customer will hear exactly that.

## Practice

**1.** どんなに{f|立派|りっぱ}な{f|計画|けいかく}でも、{f|実行|じっこう}しなければ{f|紙|かみ}の{f|上|うえ}の{f|話|はなし}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|紙|かみ}の{f|上|うえ}の{f|話|はなし}<b>でしかない</b>。

A noun phrase attaches directly. The first half sets up the standard the plan fails to
meet, which is the background this pattern needs in order to land.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まっているので、タクシーを{f|使|つか}う（しかない・でしかない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

タクシーを{f|使|つか}う<b>しかない</b>。

<code>{f|使|つか}う</code> is a verb, so it takes bare <code>しかない</code> and means
"no choice but to". <code>でしかない</code> needs a noun in front of it and would be
making a judgement about what taxis *are*, which is not the point here.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Without evidence, it's nothing but speculation."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|証拠|しょうこ}がなければ、それは{f|推測|すいそく}<b>でしかない</b>。

The English "nothing but" maps straight onto the pattern, and
<code>{f|推測|すいそく}</code> is a noun so it attaches with no change.
<code>にすぎない</code> would also be grammatical and would sound cooler and more
academic.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}のやり{f|方|かた}は{f|乱暴|らんぼう}だでしかない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}のやり{f|方|かた}は{f|乱暴|らんぼう}<b>でしかない</b>。

<code>{f|乱暴|らんぼう}</code> is a な-adjective, and the pattern takes the bare stem.
The <code>で</code> in <code>でしかない</code> *is* the copula, so
<code>だ</code> has nothing left to do.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|方法|ほうほう}は{f|効率|こうりつ}が{f|悪|わる}いにすぎない。
(b) この{f|方法|ほうほう}は{f|効率|こうりつ}が{f|悪|わる}いやり{f|方|かた}でしかない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reads as a cool qualification: the method's only shortcoming is inefficiency, so
nothing worse is being alleged. It reduces the size of the complaint.

(b) reads as a rejection: the method is *that and nothing else*, so there is no reason to
keep it. The first is a concession made in passing; the second is the sentence you write
when you want the method dropped.

</details>
