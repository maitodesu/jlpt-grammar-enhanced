# ～ざるを得ない

**Meaning** · I have no choice but to; it cannot be helped - circumstances force it, and the speaker is not happy about it

`ざる` is an old negative and {f|得|え}ない is "cannot", so the literal shape is
"cannot not do it". What comes out is compulsion from outside: the rain, the
deadline, the evidence, the family. Reluctance is built in. Nobody says
<code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> about something they wanted to do anyway.

## Formation

Take the ない-form, drop the `ない`, and add <code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code>. Two verbs are irregular.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ～ない stem + ざるを{f|得|え}ない | {f|帰|かえ}らない → {f|帰|かえ}らざるを{f|得|え}ない |
| Verb (る-verb) | ～ない stem + ざるを{f|得|え}ない | {f|認|みと}めない → {f|認|みと}めざるを{f|得|え}ない |
| Verb (する) | **せざるを{f|得|え}ない** | {f|中止|ちゅうし}する → {f|中止|ちゅうし}せざるを{f|得|え}ない |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | <b>{f|来|こ}ざるを{f|得|え}ない</b> | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ざるを{f|得|え}ない |
| Past | ～ざるを{f|得|え}なかった | {f|辞|や}めざるを{f|得|え}なかった |
| Polite | ～ざるを{f|得|え}ません | {f|認|みと}めざるを{f|得|え}ません |
| Set phrase | ～と{f|言|い}わざるを{f|得|え}ない | {f|失敗|しっぱい}だったと{f|言|い}わざるを{f|得|え}ない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ❌<code>しざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> is the standard error.
`する` uses the older negative stem `せ`, giving
<code>せざるを{f|得|え}ない</code>. In the same way
<code>{f|来|く}る</code> gives <code>{f|来|こ}ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code>, never
❌<code>きざるを{f|得|え}ない</code>.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the {f|得|え}ない here is the "cannot" suffix, so it is read
<b>えない</b>, never うない. It is also often written in kana as
<code>ざるをえない</code>, especially in email and on the web; both spellings are
standard.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}なので、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}せ<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</b>。
With this rain there is no choice but to call the match off.
</pre>

<pre>
これだけの{f|証拠|しょうこ}があれば、{f|事実|じじつ}を{f|認|みと}め<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</b>。
With this much evidence, one has to accept the facts.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|終電|しゅうでん}を{f|逃|のが}したので、タクシーで{f|帰|かえ}ら<b>ざるを{f|得|え}なかった</b>。
I missed the last train, so I had no option but to go home by taxi.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部品|ぶひん}が{f|届|とど}かず、{f|工場|こうじょう}は{f|生産|せいさん}を{f|止|と}め<b>ざるを{f|得|え}なかった</b>。
The parts never arrived, and the plant was forced to halt production.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|聞|き}く{f|限|かぎ}り、{f|準備|じゅんび}{f|不足|ぶそく}だったと{f|言|い}わ<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ません</b>。
From what I have heard, one is bound to say the preparation was inadequate.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|親|おや}の{f|介護|かいご}のために、{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|長年|ながねん}{f|勤|つと}めた{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|辞|や}め<b>ざるを{f|得|え}なかった</b>。
To look after her parents she had to leave the company she had worked at for years.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}が{f|近|ちか}づいているため、{f|明日|あした}の{f|便|びん}は{f|欠航|けっこう}と{f|判断|はんだん}せ<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ません</b>。
With the typhoon approaching, we are obliged to treat tomorrow's flight as cancelled.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|条件|じょうけん}には{f|不満|ふまん}が{f|残|のこ}るが、{f|期限|きげん}が{f|迫|せま}っている{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|受|う}け{f|入|い}れ<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</b>。
I am still unhappy with the terms, but with the deadline closing in I have no choice but to accept.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is **formal and largely written**. Editorials, news copy, company
announcements, court decisions and formal apologies are its home ground, and in
speech it belongs to meetings, presentations and interviews rather than to
conversation. Among friends the same idea comes out as
<code>{f|行|い}くしかない</code> or <code>{f|行|い}くしかなかった</code>.

Two things it always carries. First, an **external cause**, usually named in the
same sentence with ので, ため, から or {f|以上|いじょう}. Second, **reluctance**:
the speaker is doing this against their own preference. Both together are what
makes it different from a plain statement of obligation.

Because of that reluctance, the pattern is unusable for good news. ❌
<code>{f|昇進|しょうしん}したので、{f|喜|よろこ}ばざるを{f|得|え}ない</code>
reads as a joke - the speaker is being dragged into happiness. A slightly wry use
of exactly this mismatch does exist among adults ("well, I suppose I shall have to
accept a free holiday"), but treat that as a joke you recognise rather than one you
make.

<code>～と{f|言|い}わざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> deserves separate mention. It is
how a Japanese speaker delivers a harsh verdict politely: the criticism is
presented as something the facts force out of them, not as an opinion they were
keen to volunteer. {f|残念|ざんねん}だと{f|言|い}わざるを{f|得|え}ません is a
standard line in a formal complaint, and it is considerably more cutting than its
mild surface suggests.

## Contrast

### ～ざるを{f|得|え}ない vs ～しかない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `しかない` counts the options and finds one. <code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code>
adds that the speaker is being **pushed** into it, and raises the register several
notches.

</div>

<pre>
バスがないから、{f|歩|ある}く<b>しかない</b>ね。
There's no bus, so we'll just have to walk.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|交通|こうつう}が{f|完全|かんぜん}に{f|止|と}まった{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|徒歩|とほ}で{f|向|む}かわ<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</b>。
With transport at a complete standstill, we have no choice but to proceed on foot.
</pre>

`しかない` is neutral about feelings and can even be cheerful -
{f|楽|たの}しむしかない, "we may as well enjoy it". <code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> cannot do that. It also cannot
attach to nouns, whereas `しかない` can:
<code>{f|方法|ほうほう}はこれしかない</code>.

### ～ざるを{f|得|え}ない vs ～なければならない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なければならない` is obligation from a **rule, duty or plan**.
<code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> is a conclusion **forced by circumstances that have already
happened**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}までに{f|書類|しょるい}を{f|出|だ}さ<b>なければなりません</b>。
I have to submit the documents by next week.
</pre>

<pre>
システムが{f|止|と}まったので、{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りを{f|延|の}ばさ<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</b>。
The system went down, so we have no choice but to push the deadline back.
</pre>

The first is simply how things are arranged; the speaker may be perfectly content
with it. The second is a response to something that went wrong, and the speaker
would undo it if they could. This is also why `なければならない` fits a rule stated
in the abstract - {f|税金|ぜいきん}は{f|払|はら}わなければならない - where
<code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> would sound as though tax had ambushed you.

### ～ざるを{f|得|え}ない vs ～ずにはいられない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both translate as "can't help it", and they point in opposite
directions. <code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> = the situation forces me, **against** my feelings.
`ずにはいられない` = my feelings force me, whatever the situation.

</div>

<pre>
あの{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}ると、{f|毎回|まいかい}{f|泣|な}か<b>ずにはいられない</b>。
I can't watch that film without crying, every single time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|上司|じょうし}の{f|指示|しじ}なので、{f|担当|たんとう}を{f|外|はず}れ<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</b>。
It's the boss's instruction, so I have no choice but to step off the project.
</pre>

Swap them and both sentences fail. Tears are not imposed by circumstance, and
stepping off a project is not an urge welling up from inside. The test: if the
force comes from **inside** the speaker, it is `ずにはいられない`.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|中止|ちゅうし}<b>しざるを{f|得|え}ない</b> → ✅ {f|中止|ちゅうし}<b>せざるを{f|得|え}ない</b>
  `する` takes the older negative stem `せ` here. This is the most frequent error
  with the pattern by a wide margin.

- ❌ {f|帰|かえ}る<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</b> → ✅ {f|帰|かえ}ら<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</b>
  The base is the ない-form stem, not the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|来|き}<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</b> → ✅ {f|来|こ}<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</b>
  {f|来|く}る is irregular: its ない stem is こ, as in {f|来|こ}ない.

- ❌ {f|好|す}きな{f|人|ひと}と{f|話|はな}さ<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</b>。 → ✅ {f|好|す}きな{f|人|ひと}と{f|話|はな}さ<b>ずにはいられない</b>。
  The pattern requires reluctance. Applied to something you want, it turns the
  sentence into a complaint about a pleasure.

- ❌ {f|認|みと}めざるを<b>{f|得|え}ました</b>。 → ✅ {f|認|みと}めざるを<b>{f|得|え}ませんでした</b>。
  The expression is negative throughout; there is no affirmative
  <code>ざるを{f|得|え}る</code> in use.

## Practice

**1.** {f|大雪|おおゆき}で{f|道|みち}が{f|閉|と}ざされ、{f|登山|とざん}は{f|途中|とちゅう}で{f|断念|だんねん}____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|断念|だんねん}<b>せざるを{f|得|え}なかった</b>

Snow made the decision for the climbers, and they gave up unwillingly - exactly the
<code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> situation. {f|断念|だんねん}する is a する-verb, so the stem is
<code>せ</code>, not ❌し.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|久|ひさ}しぶりに{f|故郷|こきょう}の{f|景色|けしき}を{f|見|み}て、{f|感動|かんどう}（せざるを{f|得|え}なかった・せずにはいられなかった）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|感動|かんどう}<b>せずにはいられなかった</b>。

Being moved by a landscape is a feeling rising from inside the speaker, so
`ずにはいられない` is the fit. <code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> would suggest the scenery somehow
compelled the speaker to be moved against their will.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Given the numbers in this report, we are forced to say the plan has failed."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}の{f|数字|すうじ}を{f|見|み}れば、{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|失敗|しっぱい}だったと{f|言|い}わ<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ません</b>。

"We are forced to say" is <code>{f|言|い}わざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> almost word
for word, and the set phrase is the natural way to deliver a hard judgement in a
formal setting. Keep the polite ます form for a report or a meeting.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|方針|ほうしん}が{f|変|か}わったので、{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|見直|みなお}すざるを{f|得|え}ないです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|方針|ほうしん}が{f|変|か}わったので、{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|見直|みなお}さ<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ません</b>。
"The company changed its policy, so we have no choice but to revise the plan."

Two repairs: the base must be the ない stem {f|見直|みなお}さ, and the polite form
of this expression is <code>ざるを{f|得|え}ません</code>. The
<code>ないです</code> ending is too casual for a pattern that only ever appears in
formal Japanese.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|出張|しゅっちょう}には{f|行|い}か<b>なければなりません</b>。
(b) {f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|出張|しゅっちょう}には{f|行|い}か<b>ざるを{f|得|え}ません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports a duty. It says nothing about whether the speaker minds; the trip is on
the schedule and that is that. It is also ordinary spoken Japanese.

(b) says the speaker would rather not go and is being pushed into it - by an
instruction, a cancellation, someone else's absence. The listener will expect a
reason, and the register is noticeably more formal, the sort of thing said in a
meeting rather than to a colleague at lunch.

</details>
