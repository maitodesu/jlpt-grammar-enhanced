# ～に際して / ～に際し

**Meaning** · on the occasion of; at the time of - pins an action to the moment of a specific, usually one-time event

<code>に{f|際|さい}して</code> takes the noun <code>{f|際|さい}</code>, "juncture,
occasion", and turns it into a connective. It says: **this event is the point in time
at which the following happens** - the paperwork you hand over, the instruction you
are given, the words you say. It is the language of contracts, hospital discharge
sheets, station announcements and prepared speeches, and it is comfortable with
occasions that are difficult as well as happy: retirement, cancellation, a disaster.

## Formation

The event may be a noun or a dictionary-form clause. Note that no `の` intervenes -
that `の` belongs to <code>{f|際|さい}に</code>, not to this pattern.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event noun | Noun + に{f|際|さい}して | {f|出発|しゅっぱつ} → {f|出発|しゅっぱつ}に{f|際|さい}して |
| Event noun (stiffer, written) | Noun + に{f|際|さい}し | {f|契約|けいやく} → {f|契約|けいやく}に{f|際|さい}し |
| Verb (dictionary form) | dictionary + に{f|際|さい}して | {f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}む → {f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}むに{f|際|さい}して |
| Marked as the topic | ～に{f|際|さい}しては | {f|解約|かいやく} → {f|解約|かいやく}に{f|際|さい}しては |
| Before a noun | ～に{f|際|さい}しての + Noun | {f|入居|にゅうきょ} → {f|入居|にゅうきょ}に{f|際|さい}しての{f|注意|ちゅうい} |
| Spoken formal address | ～に{f|際|さい}しまして | {f|開会|かいかい} → {f|開会|かいかい}に{f|際|さい}しまして |
| ❌ With の | ❌{f|利用|りよう}のに{f|際|さい}して | ✅{f|利用|りよう}に{f|際|さい}して |
| ❌ Past form | ❌{f|退職|たいしょく}したに{f|際|さい}して | ✅{f|退職|たいしょく}するに{f|際|さい}して |
| ❌ Adjective or state | ❌{f|若|わか}いに{f|際|さい}して | ✅{f|若|わか}い{f|時|とき}に |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the noun on its own survives in a handful of set phrases, above all
<code>{f|非常|ひじょう}の{f|際|さい}</code>, "in an emergency", which is printed beside
half the emergency buttons in Japan.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Firmly written and ceremonial. It belongs to announcements, terms and conditions,
formal letters and speeches; between friends it is unusable, and a sentence about
meeting a friend for coffee wants <code>{f|時|とき}に</code>. The three shapes rank
by stiffness: <code>に{f|際|さい}し</code> is the most formal and the most printed,
<code>に{f|際|さい}して</code> is the everyday formal default, and
<code>に{f|際|さい}しまして</code> is what you say out loud at a microphone.

What the pattern really asks for is a **juncture** - a moment at which something is
transacted or transitioned. That is why the main clause is so often a procedure
(ご{f|記入|きにゅう}ください), an instruction
({f|確認|かくにん}してください), or a formal set of words
(お{f|礼|れい}{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます).

Unlike `にあたって`, it does not need the occasion to be one you are launching with
enthusiasm. Cancellation, resignation, closure and emergencies all take
<code>に{f|際|さい}して</code> naturally. That said, this is a tendency in what people
choose to write, not a grammatical rule: happy openings take it too.

The learner should also keep <code>{f|際|さい}</code> the noun in view. N3 taught
<code>{f|際|さい}に</code>, and the difference in surface shape is one particle:
<code>{f|利用|りよう}の{f|際|さい}に</code> against
<code>{f|利用|りよう}に{f|際|さい}して</code>. Mixing them produces
❌<code>{f|利用|りよう}のに{f|際|さい}して</code>, which is one of the commonest slips
at this level.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|際|さい}して vs ～{f|際|さい}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|際|さい}に</code> is the general formal "when" and takes
**routine, repeatable** occasions happily, including past ones. <code>に{f|際|さい}して</code>
frames the event as a **one-off juncture** and stays non-past.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新幹線|しんかんせん}をご{f|利用|りよう}の<b>{f|際|さい}は</b>、{f|大|おお}きな{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|座席|ざせき}の{f|後|うし}ろにお{f|置|お}きください。
When travelling on the Shinkansen, please put large luggage behind your seat.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会員|かいいん}を{f|退会|たいかい}する<b>に{f|際|さい}して</b>、アンケートにご{f|協力|きょうりょく}をお{f|願|ねが}いしております。
When cancelling your membership, we ask for your help with a short survey.
</pre>

The first is addressed to every passenger on every trip. The second happens once per
member. Grammar follows the meaning: <code>{f|際|さい}に</code> can look back at a
finished occasion with a た-form -
<code>{f|退会|たいかい}した{f|際|さい}に</code> - while
<code>に{f|際|さい}して</code> cannot.

### ～に{f|際|さい}して vs ～にあたって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `にあたって` stands at the **threshold of an undertaking** and
carries preparation and resolve. <code>に{f|際|さい}して</code> merely marks **the
time of the event**, and does not mind if the event is unwelcome.

</div>

<pre>
{f|解散|かいさん}<b>に{f|際|さい}して</b>、メンバーは{f|最後|さいご}の{f|演奏|えんそう}を{f|録音|ろくおん}した。
On disbanding, the members recorded one last performance.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しいクラブを{f|作|つく}る<b>にあたって</b>、まず{f|部員|ぶいん}を{f|集|あつ}めた。
In setting up the new club, we started by gathering members.
</pre>

Swap them and both sentences sit oddly: disbanding is not an undertaking you launch,
and founding a club is exactly that. In the wide middle ground - an opening, a launch,
a signing - either is fine and the choice is one of flavour.

### ～に{f|際|さい}して vs ～において

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>において</code> gives the **setting**: where, in what field,
under what circumstances. <code>に{f|際|さい}して</code> gives the **moment**: at the
juncture of what event.

</div>

<pre>
{f|本社|ほんしゃ}の{f|会議室|かいぎしつ}<b>において</b>、{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|説明会|せつめいかい}を{f|行|おこな}います。
The briefing will be held next week in the meeting room at head office.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明会|せつめいかい}<b>に{f|際|さい}して</b>、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|事前|じぜん}にお{f|配|くば}りします。
For the briefing, we will hand out the materials in advance.
</pre>

<code>において</code> answers "where?", <code>に{f|際|さい}して</code> answers "on
what occasion?". Because a venue is not an event, ❌<code>{f|会議室|かいぎしつ}に{f|際|さい}して</code>
is impossible, and conversely a bare procedural moment cannot be a setting.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ ご{f|利用|りよう}の<b>に{f|際|さい}して</b>は… → ✅ ご{f|利用|りよう}<b>に{f|際|さい}して</b>は… （or ご{f|利用|りよう}の<b>{f|際|さい}は</b>…）
  The `の` belongs to the noun <code>{f|際|さい}</code>. This pattern takes the noun
  directly, with nothing in between.

- ❌ {f|子|こ}ども<b>に{f|際|さい}して</b>、よくこの{f|川|かわ}で{f|泳|およ}いだ。 → ✅ {f|子|こ}どもの<b>{f|時|とき}</b>、よくこの{f|川|かわ}で{f|泳|およ}いだ。
  Childhood is a stretch of life, not an occasion. The pattern needs an **event**
  with a point in time attached to it.

- ❌ {f|留学|りゅうがく}した<b>に{f|際|さい}して</b>、ビザを{f|取|と}った。 → ✅ {f|留学|りゅうがく}する<b>に{f|際|さい}して</b>、ビザを{f|取|と}った。
  The verb stays in the dictionary form; the past tense is carried by the final verb
  of the sentence.

- ❌ {f|友|とも}だちと{f|会|あ}う<b>に{f|際|さい}して</b>、{f|駅|えき}で{f|待|ま}ち{f|合|あ}わせた。 → ✅ {f|友|とも}だちと{f|会|あ}う<b>{f|時|とき}</b>、{f|駅|えき}で{f|待|ま}ち{f|合|あ}わせた。
  Grammatically well formed, socially absurd. Meeting a friend is not a ceremonial
  juncture, and the formal register lands as a joke.

## Practice

**1.** ご{f|入学|にゅうがく}____、{f|制服|せいふく}の{f|採寸|さいすん}を{f|行|おこな}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ご{f|入学|にゅうがく}<b>に{f|際|さい}し</b>（or に{f|際|さい}して）

Enrolment is a one-time formal juncture and the uniform fitting is the procedure
transacted at that point. A school notice would use the stiffer
<code>に{f|際|さい}し</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|機械|きかい}をご{f|使用|しよう}（の{f|際|さい}は・に{f|際|さい}しては）、{f|必|かなら}ず{f|手袋|てぶくろ}をしてください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ご{f|使用|しよう}<b>の{f|際|さい}は</b>

Both are grammatical, but using the machine is something the reader does repeatedly,
and a safety notice is aimed at every use. That is the ordinary formal "when", so
the noun <code>{f|際|さい}</code> with `の` is the natural fit.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "When you cancel, please contact us at least three days in advance."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

キャンセル<b>に{f|際|さい}しては</b>、{f|三日前|みっかまえ}までにご{f|連絡|れんらく}ください。

Cancellation is exactly the kind of unwelcome one-off juncture this pattern handles
without flinching. The `は` marks it as the topic and sets up the instruction that
follows.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|工場|こうじょう}のに{f|際|さい}して、{f|見学|けんがく}の{f|方|かた}は{f|受付|うけつけ}にお{f|越|こ}しください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|工場|こうじょう}{f|見学|けんがく}<b>に{f|際|さい}して</b>は、{f|受付|うけつけ}にお{f|越|こ}しください。

Two things went wrong: the stray `の`, which belongs to
<code>{f|際|さい}に</code> and not here, and the missing event -
<code>{f|工場|こうじょう}</code> is a place, so it has to be turned into an occasion,
<code>{f|工場|こうじょう}{f|見学|けんがく}</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|移転|いてん}<b>に{f|際|さい}して</b>、{f|住所|じゅうしょ}{f|変更|へんこう}の{f|手続|てつづ}きが{f|必要|ひつよう}です。
(b) {f|移転|いてん}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って</b>、{f|住所|じゅうしょ}{f|変更|へんこう}の{f|手続|てつづ}きが{f|必要|ひつよう}です。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply attaches the paperwork to the move as its occasion. It could be done
before, during or just after; the sentence does not say.

(b) puts the paperwork firmly **ahead** of the move and implies a deadline: finish
this first, then relocate. If the timing matters,
<code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って</code> is the sentence that says so.

</details>
