# 直ちに

**Meaning** · at once, with no interval whatever - the formal word for "immediately"

Read <code>{f|直|ただ}ちに</code>. This is the "immediately" of announcements,
manuals, regulations and emergency instructions. It allows no delay at all, and it
sounds official wherever it appears.

## Usage

<code>{f|直|ただ}ちに</code> is an adverb and stands in front of the verb. It does
not modify nouns and has no `な` or `の` form.

Two uses, both worth knowing.

**1. Instructions and reports.** This is the workplace and public-notice use, and
it is by far the commoner of the two. It attaches to verbs of
response and reporting: {f|直|ただ}ちに{f|避難|ひなん}する,
{f|直|ただ}ちに{f|中止|ちゅうし}する, {f|直|ただ}ちに{f|報告|ほうこく}する,
{f|直|ただ}ちに{f|対応|たいおう}する. It very often carries an instruction:
<code>{f|直|ただ}ちに{f|連絡|れんらく}してください</code>,
<code>{f|直|ただ}ちに{f|中止|ちゅうし}すること</code>.

**2. "Not necessarily".** In written argument,
<code>{f|直|ただ}ちに～とは{f|言|い}えない</code> and
<code>{f|直|ただ}ちに～わけではない</code> mean that one fact does not lead
*straight* to a conclusion. This shows up constantly in news and official
statements and is easy to misread as being about time.

Careful with the kanji. {f|直|なお}す is "fix", {f|直|じか}に is "directly, with
nothing in between" (<code>{f|直|じか}に{f|会|あ}って{f|話|はな}す</code>), and
{f|直接|ちょくせつ} is "direct". Only the okurigana `ちに` gives you
{f|直|ただ}ちに, and ただ is a reading of this kanji you will meet almost nowhere
else.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|火事|かじ}のときは<b>{f|直|ただ}ちに</b>{f|外|そと}に{f|出|で}てください。
In the event of a fire, leave the building immediately.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|問題|もんだい}が{f|見|み}つかったら<b>{f|直|ただ}ちに</b>{f|報告|ほうこく}すること。
If a problem is found, report it at once.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|強|つよ}い{f|揺|ゆ}れを{f|感|かん}じたら、<b>{f|直|ただ}ちに</b>{f|海岸|かいがん}から{f|離|はな}れ、{f|高|たか}い{f|場所|ばしょ}へ{f|避難|ひなん}してください。
If you feel strong shaking, move away from the coast immediately and evacuate to high ground.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|数値|すうち}が{f|基準|きじゅん}を{f|超|こ}えたからといって、<b>{f|直|ただ}ちに</b>{f|健康|けんこう}に{f|影響|えいきょう}が{f|出|で}るわけではありません。
The figure exceeding the threshold does not mean that health effects will follow straight away.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Formal and impersonal. <code>{f|直|ただ}ちに</code> belongs to written and public
Japanese: safety announcements, station broadcasts, contracts, internal notices,
the news. It is not marked for age or gender, but it is heavily marked for
authority - the speaker is in a position to require the action.

Said to a friend it is comically stiff.
<code>{f|直|ただ}ちに{f|来|き}てください</code> sounds like a summons rather than an
invitation; the ordinary words are すぐ and <code>すぐに</code>.

Two smaller points. First, it is absolute: <code>{f|直|ただ}ちに</code> means *no
gap*, so it does not combine with a stated interval. Second, in sense 2 the
negative is what carries the argument, and dropping it reverses the meaning
entirely - <code>{f|直|ただ}ちに{f|影響|えいきょう}はない</code> ("no immediate
effect") is a famously cautious formula, not a clean bill of health.

## Contrast

### {f|直|ただ}ちに vs すぐに・{f|早速|さっそく}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `すぐに` is the everyday "right away";
<code>{f|直|ただ}ちに</code> is its formal, no-delay-permitted version; and
<code>{f|早速|さっそく}</code> is "without waiting" in the sense of **eagerly taking
something up**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|洗|あら}い{f|物|もの}は<b>すぐに</b>やっておくね。
I'll do the washing-up right away.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|違反|いはん}を{f|確認|かくにん}した{f|場合|ばあい}は<b>{f|直|ただ}ちに</b>{f|使用|しよう}を{f|停止|ていし}します。
If a violation is confirmed, use will be suspended immediately.
</pre>

<pre>
いただいた{f|本|ほん}を<b>{f|早速|さっそく}</b>{f|読|よ}んでみました。
I've already started on the book you gave me.
</pre>

<code>{f|早速|さっそく}</code> is the odd one out: it is warm, it usually describes
the speaker's own action, and it implies pleasure at the chance to act. It cannot
be used to order somebody about, and <code>{f|直|ただ}ちに</code> cannot be used to
thank anybody.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ おなかがすいたね。{f|直|ただ}ちに{f|食|た}べに{f|行|い}こう。 → ✅ おなかがすいたね。<b>すぐに</b>{f|食|た}べに{f|行|い}こう。
  Between friends the formal word is absurd. Save
  <code>{f|直|ただ}ちに</code> for notices and instructions.

- ❌ お{f|土産|みやげ}、{f|直|ただ}ちに{f|食|た}べました。 → ✅ お{f|土産|みやげ}、<b>{f|早速|さっそく}</b>いただきました。
  Eating a present promptly is eagerness, not compliance.
  <code>{f|早速|さっそく}</code> is the word that carries the pleasure.

- ❌ {f|直|ただ}ちに{f|三日|みっか}{f|以内|いない}にご{f|返事|へんじ}します。 → ✅ {f|三日|みっか}{f|以内|いない}にご{f|返事|へんじ}します。
  <code>{f|直|ただ}ちに</code> means no interval at all, so naming a three-day window
  contradicts it.

## Practice

**1.** {f|地震|じしん}が{f|起|お}きたら、エレベーターを{f|使|つか}わず____{f|階段|かいだん}で{f|外|そと}へ{f|出|で}てください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|使|つか}わず<b>{f|直|ただ}ちに</b>{f|階段|かいだん}で{f|外|そと}へ{f|出|で}てください。

This is a safety instruction addressed to the public, which is the home register of
the word, and the whole point is that no delay is acceptable. `すぐに` would be
understood but would read as advice rather than as a direction.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: {f|新|あたら}しいカメラを{f|買|か}ったので、（{f|直|ただ}ちに・{f|早速|さっそく}）{f|使|つか}ってみた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|新|あたら}しいカメラを{f|買|か}ったので、<b>{f|早速|さっそく}</b>{f|使|つか}ってみた。

Nobody ordered the speaker to use the camera; they were looking forward to it.
<code>{f|早速|さっそく}</code> carries that eagerness, whereas
<code>{f|直|ただ}ちに</code> would make a new toy sound like an emergency drill.

</details>

**3.** Explain what this sentence actually claims:
{f|検査|けんさ}の{f|結果|けっか}、{f|直|ただ}ちに{f|危険|きけん}であるとは{f|言|い}えない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It claims very little. This is sense 2: the results do not lead *straight* to the
conclusion that there is danger. That is not the same as saying it is safe - the
sentence carefully leaves room for a risk that shows up later or under further
testing, which is precisely why officials reach for the phrase.

</details>
