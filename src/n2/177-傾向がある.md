# ～傾向がある

**Meaning** · there is a tendency to ~; ~ tends to happen - a measured statement about which way things lean

Read <code>{f|傾向|けいこう}がある</code>. {f|傾向|けいこう} is the noun
"inclination, trend", and the whole phrase reports a **direction observable across
many cases** - a group, a season, a market, a type of person. It is the vocabulary
of surveys and observation, and unlike ～がち it carries no built-in criticism.

## Formation

{f|傾向|けいこう} is a noun, so what precedes it is in noun-modifying shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + {f|傾向|けいこう}がある | {f|増|ふ}える → {f|増|ふ}える{f|傾向|けいこう}がある |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + {f|傾向|けいこう}がある | {f|読|よ}まない → {f|読|よ}まない{f|傾向|けいこう}がある |
| い-adjective | ～くなる + {f|傾向|けいこう}がある | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}くなる{f|傾向|けいこう}がある |
| な-adjective | ～になる + {f|傾向|けいこう}がある | {f|静|しず}かな → {f|静|しず}かになる{f|傾向|けいこう}がある |
| Noun | Noun + の + {f|傾向|けいこう}がある | {f|上昇|じょうしょう} → {f|上昇|じょうしょう}の{f|傾向|けいこう}がある |
| Compound, more formal | Noun + {f|傾向|けいこう}にある | {f|減少|げんしょう} → {f|減少|げんしょう}{f|傾向|けいこう}にある |
| Other predicates on {f|傾向|けいこう} | …{f|傾向|けいこう}が{f|見|み}られる / {f|強|つよ}まる | - → {f|傾向|けいこう}が{f|見|み}られる |

<div class="note">

**Note** - an adjective cannot attach directly. ✗{f|忙|いそが}しい
{f|傾向|けいこう}がある has to become {f|忙|いそが}しくなる{f|傾向|けいこう}がある:
a tendency is a movement, so the adjective needs なる to supply one.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Analytical and slightly formal, but entirely usable in careful speech. It is the
default phrasing in survey reports, news writing, medical advice and business
analysis, and it also turns up whenever a speaker wants to generalise responsibly -
{f|傾向|けいこう}がある says "on the whole, more often than not", and so protects
the claim from a listener who has a counterexample ready.

Three things follow from what {f|傾向|けいこう} means.

It describes **many cases, not one**. A single occasion is just a fact:
{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|遅|おく}れた, not ✗{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|遅|おく}れる
{f|傾向|けいこう}があった.

It is **neutral**. Unlike ～がち, which almost always reports something unwelcome,
{f|傾向|けいこう}がある is happy with good trends, bad ones and indifferent ones.

<code>{f|傾向|けいこう}にある</code> is stiffer and narrower. It pairs with
directional nouns - {f|増加|ぞうか}, {f|減少|げんしょう}, {f|回復|かいふく}, and
with {f|上昇|じょうしょう} - and belongs to written reporting. Everywhere else,
{f|傾向|けいこう}がある is the form to use.

## Contrast

### {f|傾向|けいこう}がある vs ～がち

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ～がち reports a **failing** of one person or thing, and is
usually negative. <code>{f|傾向|けいこう}がある</code> reports a **distribution**,
and is neutral.

</div>

<pre>
{f|弟|おとうと}は{f|朝|あさ}{f|寝坊|ねぼう}し<b>がち</b>だ。
My little brother has a habit of oversleeping.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|中学生|ちゅうがくせい}は{f|睡眠|すいみん}{f|時間|じかん}が{f|短|みじか}くなる<b>{f|傾向|けいこう}がある</b>。
Middle-school students tend to get less sleep.
</pre>

They also attach differently: がち follows the ます-stem
（{f|忘|わす}れがち, {f|休|やす}みがち）and a few nouns（{f|病気|びょうき}がち）,
while {f|傾向|けいこう} follows the dictionary form. Putting がち on a whole
population sounds like a complaint about them; putting
{f|傾向|けいこう}がある on your own brother sounds like a research finding.

### {f|傾向|けいこう}がある vs ことが{f|多|おお}い

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ことが{f|多|おお}い</code> counts **how often**.
<code>{f|傾向|けいこう}がある</code> claims a **direction**, and often a cause
behind it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|土曜日|どようび}は{f|込|こ}む<b>ことが{f|多|おお}い</b>。
Saturdays are often crowded.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|土曜日|どようび}は{f|時間|じかん}が{f|経|た}つほど{f|込|こ}む<b>{f|傾向|けいこう}がある</b>。
On Saturdays it tends to get busier as the day goes on.
</pre>

The first is a frequency; the second describes a movement with a shape to it. That
is why {f|傾向|けいこう} takes {f|強|つよ}まる, {f|見|み}られる and
{f|続|つづ}く as predicates, and ことが{f|多|おお}い takes none of them.

### {f|傾向|けいこう}がある vs ～っぽい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ～っぽい describes the **character of one individual**.
<code>{f|傾向|けいこう}がある</code> describes a **pattern across a set**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|祖父|そふ}は{f|最近|さいきん}{f|忘|わす}れ<b>っぽく</b>なった。
My grandfather has got forgetful lately.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|年|とし}を{f|取|と}ると{f|名前|なまえ}が{f|出|で}てこなくなる<b>{f|傾向|けいこう}がある</b>。
As people get older they tend to have trouble producing names.
</pre>

っぽい makes a colloquial, impressionistic claim about a person in front of you;
{f|傾向|けいこう}がある makes a cautious general claim about people at large. Only
the second belongs in writing.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|長|なが}くなる<b>{f|傾向|けいこう}があった</b>。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|長|なが}かった。
  One meeting is an event, not a tendency. A trend needs repetition behind it.

- ❌ {f|最近|さいきん}{f|忙|いそが}しい<b>{f|傾向|けいこう}がある</b>。 → ✅ {f|最近|さいきん}{f|忙|いそが}しくなる<b>{f|傾向|けいこう}がある</b>。
  An い-adjective needs なる before {f|傾向|けいこう} - a tendency is a movement,
  not a state.

- ❌ {f|物価|ぶっか}が{f|上|あ}がる<b>{f|傾向|けいこう}をある</b>。 → ✅ {f|物価|ぶっか}が{f|上|あ}がる<b>{f|傾向|けいこう}がある</b>。
  ある takes が. This is the ordinary existence verb, not a transitive one.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|風邪|かぜ}を{f|引|ひ}く<b>{f|傾向|けいこう}がある</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|風邪|かぜ}を{f|引|ひ}き<b>がち</b>だ。
  For one person's recurring weakness, がち is the natural word.
  {f|傾向|けいこう}がある makes him sound like a data set.

## Practice

**1.** {f|都会|とかい}では{f|結婚|けっこん}する{f|年齢|ねんれい}が{f|高|たか}く____{f|傾向|けいこう}がある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|都会|とかい}では{f|結婚|けっこん}する{f|年齢|ねんれい}が{f|高|たか}く<b>なる</b>{f|傾向|けいこう}がある。

{f|高|たか}い is an い-adjective, so なる has to supply the movement that
{f|傾向|けいこう} needs. The claim is about a population, which is what makes the
pattern appropriate.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|姉|あね}は{f|約束|やくそく}の{f|時間|じかん}に（{f|遅|おく}れがち・{f|遅|おく}れる{f|傾向|けいこう}がある）だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|姉|あね}は{f|約束|やくそく}の{f|時間|じかん}に<b>{f|遅|おく}れがち</b>だ。

One family member's habit, and a mildly unwelcome one - exactly がち's territory.
{f|傾向|けいこう}がある would give a personal complaint the tone of a statistical
report.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Recently, more and more young people tend to shop online rather than in shops."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|最近|さいきん}の{f|若者|わかもの}は{f|店|みせ}よりネットで{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}をする<b>{f|傾向|けいこう}が{f|強|つよ}まっている</b>。

The claim is about a whole age group and about a direction that is intensifying, so
{f|傾向|けいこう} takes {f|強|つよ}まる rather than a plain ある.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|売|う}り{f|上|あ}げは{f|増加|ぞうか}する{f|傾向|けいこう}をある。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|会社|かいしゃ}の{f|売|う}り{f|上|あ}げは{f|増加|ぞうか}<b>{f|傾向|けいこう}にある</b>。
（or {f|増|ふ}える{f|傾向|けいこう}<b>が</b>ある）

The particle must be が with ある, never を. With a directional noun like
{f|増加|ぞうか} the tighter compound {f|増加|ぞうか}{f|傾向|けいこう}にある is the
idiomatic written form.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|日本人|にほんじん}ははっきり{f|断|ことわ}らない<b>{f|傾向|けいこう}がある</b>。
(b) {f|日本人|にほんじん}ははっきり{f|断|ことわ}らない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(b) is a flat generalisation about a whole nationality, and it is the kind of claim
a single counterexample destroys. It also sounds like a stereotype stated as fact.

(a) makes the same observation as a **tendency**: more often than not, on the whole.
It admits exceptions in advance, which is both more accurate and more careful, and
it is why {f|傾向|けいこう}がある is the standard phrasing for generalisations about
groups.

</details>
