# ～ばかりか

**Meaning** · not only ... but even ... - and the second thing is further than you
would have expected

<code>ばかり</code> here is "merely", the same word as in
<code>{f|三日|みっか}ばかり</code>. <code>ばかりか</code> says the case is not merely the
first thing, and then names a second that goes beyond it. What it adds over the
N3 pattern <code>だけでなく</code> is **escalation and feeling**: the speaker is
surprised, impressed or appalled, and the sentence expects the listener to be too.

## Formation

The clause in front is in plain, noun-modifying shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + ばかりか | {f|止|と}まる → {f|止|と}まったばかりか |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + ばかりか | {f|謝|あやま}らない → {f|謝|あやま}らないばかりか |
| い-adjective | plain form + ばかりか | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}いばかりか |
| な-adjective | ～な + ばかりか | {f|失礼|しつれい}な → {f|失礼|しつれい}なばかりか |
| Noun | Noun + ばかりか | {f|大人|おとな} → {f|大人|おとな}ばかりか |
| Noun (written) | ～である + ばかりか | {f|専門家|せんもんか}であるばかりか |
| Second clause | …も／さえ／まで + predicate | …{f|道路|どうろ}<b>まで</b>{f|通|とお}れない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the second half must go **further out** than the first. Local fame
extending to the whole country works; national fame "extending" to one city does not.
If your second item is the smaller case, the two halves are in the wrong order.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Neutral in formality but not in temperature. <code>ばかりか</code> is usable in
speech and in writing, in a magazine article or in an account of a bad afternoon, and
it always brings an attitude with it. The listener is meant to react: *even that?*
That is the difference between it and the flat additives, and it is why the pattern
clusters around three feelings - indignation, admiration, and dismay at how far
something has spread.

The escalation is structural. Some element in the second clause almost always carries
も, さえ or まで, and まで and さえ are the strongest of the three because they mean
"even". Without one of them the sentence loses its point and reads like a plain list
with an oddly emphatic connector.

The restriction learners hit hardest: **the second clause states a fact.** It cannot
be a request, an instruction, an invitation or a statement of your own intention. Ask
someone to buy two things and <code>だけでなく</code> or
<code>に{f|加|くわ}えて</code> is what you need. This is because
<code>ばかりか</code> is a comment on how the world turned out, not a way of
organising what you want.

<code>ばかりでなく</code> is the same word with the emotional charge removed - the
straightforward "not only A but also B" that behaves like
<code>だけでなく</code> and does accept a following request. If you find yourself
wanting the escalation without the surprise, that is the form to reach for.

A grammatical detail worth noticing: with a negative first clause -
<code>{f|謝|あやま}らないばかりか</code> - the pattern comes very close to English
"far from apologising". This is one of its most natural uses, and it is
overwhelmingly negative in tone.

## Contrast

### ばかりか vs だけでなく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `だけでなく` is **neutral and flexible**;
<code>ばかりか</code> is **emphatic and descriptive only**.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|学生|がくせい}<b>だけでなく</b>{f|社会人|しゃかいじん}にも{f|役|やく}に{f|立|た}つ。
This dictionary is useful for working adults as well as students.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|学生|がくせい}<b>ばかりか</b>、{f|翻訳者|ほんやくしゃ}まで{f|使|つか}っているそうだ。
This dictionary is apparently used not just by students but even by translators.
</pre>

The first is a product description. The second is a claim with a raised eyebrow in
it. The practical difference is what each will tolerate afterwards: `だけでなく`
happily leads into <code>{f|買|か}ってください</code> or
<code>{f|見|み}てみましょう</code>, while <code>ばかりか</code> cannot end in a request
at all.

### ばかりか vs のみならず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same widening of scope. <code>のみならず</code> is **formal and
flat**; <code>ばかりか</code> is **speakable and loaded**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|被害|ひがい}は{f|沿岸部|えんがんぶ}<b>のみならず</b>{f|内陸部|ないりくぶ}にも{f|及|およ}んだ。
The damage extended to inland areas as well as the coast.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|被害|ひがい}は{f|沿岸部|えんがんぶ}<b>ばかりか</b>{f|内陸部|ないりくぶ}にまで{f|及|およ}んだ。
The damage reached not just the coast but even inland.
</pre>

Both are correct and both would pass in a written report. The first is what an
official summary says; the second is what a feature article says, because it invites
the reader to feel the extent of it. In conversation only the second survives.

### ばかりか vs どころか

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ばかりか</code> **affirms** the first item and adds to it.
<code>どころか</code> **denies** it and replaces it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}った<b>ばかりか</b>、{f|風|かぜ}まで{f|強|つよ}くなった。
It rained, and on top of that the wind got up as well.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>どころか</b>、{f|一日中|いちにちじゅう}{f|晴|は}れていた。
Far from raining, it was sunny all day.
</pre>

This is the pair that catches people out, because both translate with "far from" in
some sentences. Look at whether the first item is true. In the
<code>ばかりか</code> sentence it rained; in the <code>どころか</code> sentence it did
not. When the first clause is negative -
<code>{f|謝|あやま}らないばかりか</code> - the two come close in feel, but even there
<code>ばかりか</code> is asserting that no apology was made, and building on it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|水|みず}ばかりか、パンも{f|買|か}ってきてください。 → ✅ {f|水|みず}<b>だけでなく</b>、パンも{f|買|か}ってきてください。
  The second clause cannot be a request. <code>ばかりか</code> reports; it does not
  ask.

- ❌ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|静|しず}か<b>ばかりか</b>{f|広|ひろ}い。 → ✅ この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|静|しず}か<b>なばかりか</b>{f|広|ひろ}い。
  <code>ばかり</code> follows a noun-modifying form, so な-adjectives keep their な.
  Verbs and い-adjectives attach in the plain form with nothing added.

- ❌ この{f|歌手|かしゅ}は{f|全国|ぜんこく}で{f|有名|ゆうめい}な<b>ばかりか</b>、{f|地元|じもと}でも{f|人気|にんき}がある。 → ✅ この{f|歌手|かしゅ}は{f|地元|じもと}で{f|人気|にんき}がある<b>ばかりか</b>、{f|全国|ぜんこく}でも{f|知|し}られている。
  The escalation runs outwards. Going from the whole country back to one town is a
  step *down*, and the "even" has nothing to do.

- ❌ {f|給料|きゅうりょう}が{f|上|あ}がった<b>ばかりか</b>、{f|残業|ざんぎょう}が{f|増|ふ}えた。 → ✅ {f|給料|きゅうりょう}が{f|上|あ}がった<b>が</b>、{f|残業|ざんぎょう}も{f|増|ふ}えた。
  A gain followed by a loss changes direction, and no additive pattern will carry
  that. Both halves have to point the same way.

## Practice

**1.** その{f|子|こ}は{f|自分|じぶん}の{f|部屋|へや}を{f|片付|かたづ}けた____、{f|弟|おとうと}の{f|机|つくえ}まで{f|整理|せいり}してくれた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|片付|かたづ}けた<b>ばかりか</b>、{f|弟|おとうと}の{f|机|つくえ}まで{f|整理|せいり}してくれた。

Two good deeds, the second further than anyone asked for, and まで marking it as the
surprise. The admiring tone is exactly what this pattern is for.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|牛乳|ぎゅうにゅう}（ばかりか・だけでなく）{f|卵|たまご}も{f|買|か}ってきてくれる?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|牛乳|ぎゅうにゅう}<b>だけでなく</b>{f|卵|たまご}も{f|買|か}ってきてくれる?

The sentence is a request. <code>ばかりか</code> comments on what has happened and
cannot be followed by one, however emphatic you feel about the eggs.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He didn't just forget the meeting - he didn't even answer his phone."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼|かれ}は{f|会議|かいぎ}を{f|忘|わす}れた<b>ばかりか</b>、{f|電話|でんわ}にさえ{f|出|で}なかった。

"Didn't just … didn't even" is the shape this pattern was made for, and さえ carries
the second half. <code>のみならず</code> would be grammatical but would drain the
exasperation out of it.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}は{f|親切|しんせつ}ばかりか、{f|仕事|しごと}も{f|速|はや}い。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|親切|しんせつ}<b>なばかりか</b>、{f|仕事|しごと}も{f|速|はや}い。

<code>{f|親切|しんせつ}</code> is a な-adjective and needs its な before
<code>ばかり</code>. A plain noun would attach directly:
<code>{f|新人|しんじん}ばかりか</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|返事|へんじ}をしないばかりか、{f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}ってしまった。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}は{f|返事|へんじ}をするどころか、{f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}ってしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are complaints, and both end the same way, but they treat the first clause
differently. (a) asserts it: he gave no answer, and on top of that he walked out. The
sentence stacks two facts.

(b) sets up the answer as something that might have been expected and then knocks it
down: far from replying, he got up and left. <code>どころか</code> rejects its first
item, so what follows feels like a correction of the listener's assumption rather
than an addition to the record.

</details>
