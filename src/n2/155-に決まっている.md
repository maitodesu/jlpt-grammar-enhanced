# ～に決まっている

**Meaning** · of course it is; it's bound to be - the speaker refuses to entertain any other answer

<code>に{f|決|き}まっている</code> sits at the loud end of the confidence ladder. `かもしれない`
leaves the door open, `はずだ` produces grounds, <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> reads the
evidence and lands on one answer. <code>に{f|決|き}まっている</code> does none of that: it
declares the question closed. The certainty is the speaker's **attitude**, not a
measurement, which is why it is at home in an argument and out of place in a report.

## Formation

Attaches to a **plain form**. Nouns and な-adjectives connect bare - no `だ`, and no `な`
either.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + に{f|決|き}まっている | {f|勝|か}つ → {f|勝|か}つに{f|決|き}まっている |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + に{f|決|き}まっている | {f|来|こ}ない → {f|来|こ}ないに{f|決|き}まっている |
| Verb (past) | plain past + に{f|決|き}まっている | {f|忘|わす}れた → {f|忘|わす}れたに{f|決|き}まっている |
| い-adjective | plain form + に{f|決|き}まっている | {f|寒|さむ}い → {f|寒|さむ}いに{f|決|き}まっている |
| な-adjective | drop だ + に{f|決|き}まっている | {f|無理|むり}だ → {f|無理|むり}に{f|決|き}まっている |
| Noun | Noun + に{f|決|き}まっている | うそ → うそに{f|決|き}まっている |
| Polite | … + に{f|決|き}まっています | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}いに{f|決|き}まっています |
| Casual speech | … + に{f|決|き}まってる | {f|疲|つか}れる → {f|疲|つか}れるに{f|決|き}まってる |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the form is frozen in the ている shape. There is no
<code>に{f|決|き}まる</code> and no <code>に{f|決|き}まった</code> in this meaning; those
belong to the ordinary verb {f|決|き}まる, which is a different thing entirely - see the
Contrast section.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
そんな{f|話|はなし}、うそ<b>に{f|決|き}まっている</b>。
A story like that is a lie, obviously.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}から{f|急|いそ}いでも{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わない<b>に{f|決|き}まっている</b>よ。
Even if we rush now, there's no way we'll make it.
</pre>

<pre>
こんな{f|時間|じかん}に{f|電話|でんわ}したら、{f|迷惑|めいわく}<b>に{f|決|き}まっています</b>。
Ringing someone at this hour is bound to be a nuisance.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どもがあんなに{f|静|しず}かなときは、いたずらをしている<b>に{f|決|き}まってる</b>。
When the children are that quiet, they're up to something - guaranteed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}の{f|前|まえ}の{f|晩|ばん}に{f|徹夜|てつや}すれば、{f|眠|ねむ}くなる<b>に{f|決|き}まっています</b>。
Stay up all night before the exam and of course you'll be half asleep.
</pre>

<pre>
「{f|部長|ぶちょう}、{f|怒|おこ}るかな。」「{f|怒|おこ}る<b>に{f|決|き}まってる</b>だろ。」
"Reckon the boss will be cross?" "Course he will."
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}も{f|確|たし}かめずに{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}んだら、あとで{f|後悔|こうかい}する<b>に{f|決|き}まっている</b>。
Sign up without even checking the price and you'll regret it later, no question.
</pre>

<pre>
あれだけ{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|休|やす}んでいたのだから、{f|本人|ほんにん}も{f|不安|ふあん}だった<b>に{f|決|き}まっています</b>。
After skipping that much practice, he must have been anxious himself - it could hardly be otherwise.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Blunt, emotional and thoroughly spoken. The contraction
<code>に{f|決|き}まってる</code> is the everyday form, and
<code>に{f|決|き}まってるじゃない</code> or <code>に{f|決|き}まってるでしょ</code> is how it
usually reaches the listener: "obviously - why are you even asking?" That is also the
danger in it. Because the pattern presents your conviction as though it were common
sense, using it on someone who has just offered a different opinion tells them their
opinion was silly. Among friends that is ordinary banter; aimed upward at a manager or a
client it lands close to rude.

<code>に{f|決|き}まっています</code> is grammatically polite and still forceful - です
softens the packaging, not the content. When you need to disagree politely, the gentler
rungs of the ladder are the tools: <code>{f|違|ちが}うと{f|思|おも}います</code>,
<code>そうとは{f|限|かぎ}らないのではないでしょうか</code>.

Two things it will not do. It cannot report somebody else's certainty - the conviction
has to be the speaker's own - and it does not form a genuine question, because you cannot
ask another person to confirm a feeling that only you have.

It is very often turned on oneself, half-jokingly, to head off sympathy in advance:
<code>{f|私|わたし}のことだから、{f|三日|みっか}でやめる<b>に{f|決|き}まってる</b></code>.
That self-directed use is the friendliest home the pattern has.

## Contrast

### に{f|決|き}まっている vs に{f|違|ちが}いない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> is a **conclusion drawn from
evidence**. <code>に{f|決|き}まっている</code> is a **refusal to consider alternatives**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|靴|くつ}が{f|濡|ぬ}れているから、{f|外|そと}は{f|雨|あめ}<b>に{f|違|ちが}いない</b>。
His shoes are wet, so it must be raining outside.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|空|そら}の{f|色|いろ}を{f|見|み}てよ。{f|降|ふ}る<b>に{f|決|き}まってる</b>。
Look at the colour of that sky. It's going to rain, obviously.
</pre>

The first shows its working, and a listener could inspect the shoes and agree. The second
does not argue - it asserts, and dares you to disagree. This is why a report, a paper or
an exam answer takes <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> or
<code>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</code>, however certain the writer happens to feel.

### に{f|決|き}まっている vs はずだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `はずだ` can be **let down**; <code>に{f|決|き}まっている</code>
cannot.

</div>

<pre>
{f|鍵|かぎ}はかばんに{f|入|い}れた<b>はずです</b>。
I'm fairly sure I put the key in my bag.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|鍵|かぎ}はかばんの{f|中|なか}<b>に{f|決|き}まっています</b>。
The key is in the bag - where else would it be?
</pre>

`はずだ` rests on grounds you could produce if asked, and grounds can turn out to be
wrong, which is why <code>はずだったのに</code> is such a common thing to say. There is no
comfortable equivalent for <code>に{f|決|き}まっている</code>: having declared the matter
closed, you have left yourself nothing to be surprised by except your own certainty.

### に{f|決|き}まっている vs the ordinary verb {f|決|き}まる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if `に` marks **when, where or what was settled**, it is the plain
verb {f|決|き}まる. If `に` is glued to the pattern and the sentence could take きっと, it
is the conjecture.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|水曜日|すいようび}<b>に{f|決|き}まっています</b>。
Next month's meeting is fixed for Wednesday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|会議|かいぎ}も{f|長|なが}くなる<b>に{f|決|き}まっています</b>。
Next month's meeting is going to run long as well, needless to say.
</pre>

Identical endings, unrelated meanings. In the first, {f|水曜日|すいようび} is a noun that
somebody chose and `に` marks the choice; the sentence reports an administrative fact. In
the second the pattern hangs off a whole predicate. Notice that the conjecture reading is
blocked after a bare time or place noun, which is exactly why
<code>{f|明日|あした}に{f|決|き}まっている</code> can only mean "it is scheduled for
tomorrow".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}の{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|大切|たいせつ}<b>だに{f|決|き}まっている</b>。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}の{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|大切|たいせつ}<b>に{f|決|き}まっている</b>。
  な-adjectives and nouns attach bare. Both the `だ` and the `な` are dropped.

- ❌ {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|く}る<b>に{f|決|き}まっている</b>と{f|天気予報|てんきよほう}で{f|言|い}っていました。 → ✅ {f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|く}る<b>でしょう</b>と{f|天気予報|てんきよほう}で{f|言|い}っていました。
  The pattern carries the speaker's own conviction, so it cannot be put into somebody
  else's mouth - least of all a forecaster's.

- ❌ その{f|話|はなし}は{f|本当|ほんとう}<b>に{f|決|き}まっていますか</b>。 → ✅ その{f|話|はなし}は{f|本当|ほんとう}<b>でしょうか</b>。
  You cannot ask a listener to confirm your own certainty. The only question shape the
  pattern has is the rhetorical
  <code>～に{f|決|き}まってるじゃないですか</code>.

- ❌ {f|調査|ちょうさ}の{f|結果|けっか}、この{f|数字|すうじ}は{f|誤|あやま}り<b>に{f|決|き}まっている</b>。 → ✅ {f|調査|ちょうさ}の{f|結果|けっか}、この{f|数字|すうじ}は{f|誤|あやま}り<b>だと{f|考|かんが}えられる</b>。
  In a written report the reader wants the evidence, not the writer's temperature. Swap
  to <code>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</code> or <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code>.

## Practice

**1.** {f|朝|あさ}からずっと{f|立|た}ちっぱなしだったのだから、{f|足|あし}が{f|痛|いた}い____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|足|あし}が{f|痛|いた}い<b>に{f|決|き}まっている</b>（or に{f|決|き}まっています）。

The speaker treats sore feet as the only thinkable outcome of standing all morning, so
the closed-question pattern fits. An い-adjective attaches with no change at all.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|新幹線|しんかんせん}は3{f|時|じ}に{f|着|つ}く（はずです・に{f|決|き}まっています）とアプリに{f|出|で}ていました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

3{f|時|じ}に{f|着|つ}く<b>はずです</b>。

The grounds here are an app - somebody else's information, and a timetable that could
still be wrong. That is `はずだ` territory. The other pattern would assert the speaker's
personal conviction, which sits oddly beside a quoted source.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "A theme park on a Sunday? It's going to be packed, obviously."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|日曜日|にちようび}の{f|遊園地|ゆうえんち}なんて、{f|混|こ}んでいる<b>に{f|決|き}まっています</b>。

なんて sets up the dismissive tone and the pattern completes it. Note the ている shape of
{f|混|こ}む: a crowd is a present state, not a coming event.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼女|かのじょ}はそんなことを{f|言|い}わないに{f|決|き}まったよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}はそんなことを{f|言|い}わない<b>に{f|決|き}まってる</b>よ。

The pattern only exists in the ている form. <code>に{f|決|き}まった</code> would be the
plain verb reporting that something got settled, which makes no sense with a negative
clause in front of it. Casual speech drops the い, giving
<code>に{f|決|き}まってる</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}はもう{f|知|し}っている<b>に{f|違|ちが}いない</b>。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}はもう{f|知|し}っている<b>に{f|決|き}まっている</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is detective work. Something told you he knows - his face, a message he sent, the
speed of his reply - and you are stating the conclusion. A listener could reasonably ask
どうして？ and you would have an answer ready.

(b) is exasperation. It says the question was not worth asking: of course he knows,
everybody does. There is usually a second speaker being corrected, and (b) tells them
their doubt was naive. Fine between friends, sharp anywhere else.

</details>
