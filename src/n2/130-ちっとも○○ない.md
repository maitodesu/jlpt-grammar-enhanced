# ちっとも～ない

**Meaning** · not at all; not in the least - the spoken, faintly exasperated member of the "zero" family

`ちっとも` is <code>ちっと</code> ("a tiny bit") plus `も`, and like every
`も`-word it demands a negative to lean on. What it adds to a plain negative is
**disappointment**. Something was supposed to change, work, arrive or improve, and
it has not budged. That expectation is baked in, which is why `ちっとも` sounds
alive in conversation and stiff in a report.

**What is denied: the amount, all the way down to nothing.** `ちっとも` is not
denying an inference the way <code>わけではない</code> does, and not denying that a
rule always holds the way <code>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> does. It denies the
quantity itself: not one bit happened.

## Formation

`ちっとも` is an adverb. It never changes shape; it sits before the predicate, and
the predicate must be negative.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain) | ちっとも + ない-form | {f|変|か}わる → ちっとも{f|変|か}わらない |
| Verb (polite) | ちっとも + ません | {f|届|とど}く → ちっとも{f|届|とど}きません |
| Verb (past) | ちっとも + なかった | {f|進|すす}む → ちっとも{f|進|すす}まなかった |
| い-adjective | ちっとも + ～くない | {f|面白|おもしろ}い → ちっとも{f|面白|おもしろ}くない |
| な-adjective | ちっとも + ～ではない／じゃない | {f|平気|へいき}だ → ちっとも{f|平気|へいき}じゃない |
| Noun | ちっとも + ～ではない | {f|問題|もんだい} → ちっとも{f|問題|もんだい}ではない |
| Change of state | ちっとも + ～くならない | よい → ちっともよくならない |
| Standalone reply | ちっとも。 | 「{f|眠|ねむ}れた?」「ちっとも。」 |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if you can put the word {f|全然|ぜんぜん} there, `ちっとも` fits
grammatically. The standalone 「ちっとも。」 is not an exception - the negative verb
is simply left off, and both speakers hear it anyway.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|薬|くすり}は<b>ちっとも</b>{f|効|き}か<b>ない</b>。
This medicine isn't working at all.
</pre>

<pre>
バスが<b>ちっとも</b>{f|来|こ}<b>ない</b>。
The bus just isn't coming.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|注意|ちゅうい}しても、{f|息子|むすこ}は<b>ちっとも</b>{f|聞|き}いてくれ<b>ません</b>。
I tell him off, but my son doesn't listen to a word of it.
</pre>

<pre>
「{f|仕事|しごと}、{f|進|すす}んだ?」「<b>ちっとも</b>。」
"Get anywhere with the work?" "Not a bit."
</pre>

<pre>
エアコンをつけたのに、この{f|部屋|へや}は<b>ちっとも</b>{f|涼|すず}しく<b>ならない</b>。
I've put the air conditioning on and the room isn't getting any cooler at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}から{f|習|なら}っているのに、{f|発音|はつおん}が<b>ちっとも</b>よく<b>ならなくて</b>{f|困|こま}っています。
I've been taking lessons since last year and my pronunciation isn't improving in the slightest - it's getting me down.
</pre>

<pre>

We brought in a new machine, and the work has not got any easier at all.
</pre>

<pre>

I have been queuing a solid thirty minutes and the line is not moving an inch.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`ちっとも` belongs to speech. It is at home in conversation, in dialogue in a novel,
in a blog post - and it looks out of place in a report, a contract or an academic
paragraph, where {f|全|まった}く or {f|少|すこ}しも does the same work with a straight
face. It is not rude and not childish; it is simply informal, and it carries a
grumble.

That grumble is the point. 「ちっとも{f|変|か}わらないね」 said about an old friend is
affectionate; said about a company's service it is a complaint. The word implies
that the speaker was entitled to expect movement, so it is naturally at home after
のに and after ても: {f|待|ま}っているのに, {f|注意|ちゅうい}しても.

It is neutral as to the speaker's age or gender, though it is one of those words
that can read as slightly homely in writing - younger speakers reach for
{f|全然|ぜんぜん} more often in casual talk. That is a tendency, not a rule;
`ちっとも` is understood by everyone and used by everyone.

It combines happily with ます-form: 「ちっとも{f|存|ぞん}じませんでした」 is perfectly
polite. What makes `ちっとも` informal is the word itself, not the ending you attach
it to, so you cannot rescue a formal document by adding ます.

## Contrast

### ちっとも vs {f|全然|ぜんぜん}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ちっとも` **only** ever takes a negative. {f|全然|ぜんぜん} in
modern casual speech has escaped that rule and now turns up with affirmatives too.

</div>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}は<b>ちっとも</b>むずかしく<b>なかった</b>。
The exam was not difficult in the slightest.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}は<b>{f|全然|ぜんぜん}</b>{f|簡単|かんたん}だったよ。
The exam was dead easy.
</pre>

The second sentence is ordinary spoken Japanese today and the first pattern cannot
copy it: ❌<code>ちっとも{f|簡単|かんたん}だった</code> is broken. Where both are
negative, they overlap almost completely, and the choice is texture -
{f|全然|ぜんぜん} is the neutral default, `ちっとも` adds the note of thwarted
expectation.

### ちっとも vs あまり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `あまり～ない` is **not much**. `ちっとも～ない` is **none**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}は<b>あまり</b>{f|寝|ね}<b>ていない</b>。{f|三時間|さんじかん}くらいかな。
I did not get much sleep last night. About three hours, I would say.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}は<b>ちっとも</b>{f|寝|ね}<b>ていない</b>。
I did not sleep a wink last night.
</pre>

Because `ちっとも` claims zero, it cannot be followed by an exception. Saying
「ちっとも{f|食|た}べなかった。パンは{f|食|た}べたけど」 contradicts itself; あまり is
what that sentence needs.

### ちっとも～ない vs ～わけではない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ちっとも` denies **the fact, at full strength**.
<code>わけではない</code> leaves the fact alone and denies **the conclusion someone
drew from it**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}のやり{f|方|かた}に<b>ちっとも</b>{f|賛成|さんせい}でき<b>ない</b>。
I cannot agree with the way he does things at all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}のやり{f|方|かた}に{f|賛成|さんせい}している<b>わけではない</b>。
It is not that I am endorsing the way he does things.
</pre>

The first shuts the door. The second keeps it open a crack: the speaker may well
have said something that sounded like approval, and is trimming back the
interpretation, not the facts. Reaching for `ちっとも` when you meant to soften is
one of the fastest ways to sound harsher than you intended.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|映画|えいが}は<b>ちっとも{f|面白|おもしろ}かった</b>。 → ✅ この{f|映画|えいが}は<b>ちっとも{f|面白|おもしろ}くなかった</b>。
  `ちっとも` cannot sit in front of an affirmative. The negative is not optional
  decoration; it is what the word is holding on to.

- ❌ {f|昨日|きのう}は<b>ちっとも{f|勉強|べんきょう}しませんでした</b>が、{f|漢字|かんじ}は{f|少|すこ}しやりました。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}は<b>あまり{f|勉強|べんきょう}しませんでした</b>が…
  Zero admits no exceptions. If part of it did happen, you need あまり.

- ❌（{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}に）{f|売上|うりあげ}は<b>ちっとも</b>{f|伸|の}びて<b>いない</b>。 → ✅ {f|売上|うりあげ}は<b>{f|全|まった}く</b>{f|伸|の}びて<b>いない</b>。
  Grammatically fine, tonally wrong. In written business Japanese use
  {f|全|まった}く or {f|少|すこ}しも.

- ❌ {f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}っている<b>ちっともではない</b>。 → ✅ {f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}っている<b>わけではない</b>。
  `ちっとも` is an adverb, not a sentence-ending form. It cannot cancel an
  inference; only <code>わけではない</code> does that job.

## Practice

**1.** {f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}{f|練習|れんしゅう}したのに、____{f|上手|じょうず}にならない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}{f|練習|れんしゅう}したのに、<b>ちっとも</b>{f|上手|じょうず}にならない。

The のに clause has already set up an expectation that was let down, which is
exactly the environment `ちっとも` lives in. {f|全然|ぜんぜん} or {f|少|すこ}しも would
also be grammatical; `ちっとも` is the one that sounds like the speaker is fed up.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|昨日|きのう}は{f|忙|いそが}しくて、（ちっとも・あまり）{f|休|やす}めなかった。{f|昼|ひる}に{f|十分|じゅっぷん}だけ{f|座|すわ}ったが。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>あまり</b>{f|休|やす}めなかった。

Ten minutes of sitting down is not nothing, and `ちっとも` claims nothing at all.
`あまり` scales the amount down without taking it to zero, so it can live alongside
the が clause that admits the exception.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I have read it three times and I still do not understand it at all."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|三回|さんかい}{f|読|よ}んだが、<b>ちっとも</b>{f|分|わ}から<b>ない</b>。

"At all" after an effort that should have worked is the `ちっとも` situation. Note
that the negative goes on the verb - `ちっとも` itself never conjugates.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|読|よ}んでも、ちっともわかるでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|読|よ}んでも、<b>ちっともわかりませんでした</b>。

Two things went wrong: <code>わかる</code> needs a polite past **negative**
（わかりませんでした）, and `ちっとも` requires that negative to be there at all.
<code>わかるでした</code> is not a form of anything.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|寒|さむ}くないですか。- いえ、<b>ちっとも</b>{f|寒|さむ}く<b>ないです</b>。
(b) {f|寒|さむ}くないですか。- いえ、<b>べつに</b>{f|寒|さむ}く<b>ないです</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) answers the question head-on and warmly: there is no cold here whatsoever, so
please stop worrying. It reassures.

(b) says there is nothing about the temperature worth remarking on. It answers a
question the speaker did not think needed asking, and delivered flatly it can sound
like a shrug - which is why べつに so easily reads as sulky and `ちっとも` does not.

</details>
