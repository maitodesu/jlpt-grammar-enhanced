# それとも

**Meaning** · or; or else - puts two alternatives to someone and waits for an answer

`それとも` lives in questions. It stands between two of them and asks the listener to
take one: this, or that? Everything that distinguishes it from the other Japanese
words for "or" follows from that single fact - it needs a question in front of it and
a question after it.

## Usage

The standard frame is two complete questions:

- {f|歩|ある}いて{f|行|い}きますか。それともタクシーを{f|呼|よ}びますか。

The second question is often shortened to just the new part, especially in speech:

- {f|歩|ある}いて{f|行|い}く? それともタクシー?

It also appears inside a single sentence when the speaker is weighing two courses of
action, with each option marked by か:

- {f|今日|きょう}{f|出|だ}すか、それとも{f|明日|あした}にするか、まだ{f|決|き}めていない。

Nothing attaches to it and it never changes form. It is written in kana.

The restriction that matters: **it cannot join two nouns inside a statement.**
❌<code>{f|明日|あした}それとも{f|明後日|あさって}{f|行|い}きます</code> is not Japanese.
Statements join their options with か, または or あるいは; `それとも` is reserved for
the moment a choice is handed over.

## Examples

<pre>
コーヒーにしますか。<b>それとも</b>{f|紅茶|こうちゃ}にしますか。
Would you like coffee, or tea?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今|いま}{f|出|で}ますか、<b>それとも</b>{f|雨|あめ}がやむまで{f|待|ま}ちますか。
Shall we set off now, or wait until the rain stops?
</pre>

<pre>
これ、{f|君|きみ}が{f|描|か}いたの? <b>それとも</b>お{f|姉|ねえ}さん?
Did you draw this? Or was it your big sister?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電話|でんわ}で{f|謝|あやま}るべきか、<b>それとも</b>{f|直接|ちょくせつ}{f|会|あ}いに{f|行|い}くべきか、{f|一晩中|ひとばんじゅう}{f|迷|まよ}っていた。
Whether to apologise by phone or go and see him in person - I was torn all night.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Completely register-neutral. It works in a plain question between friends, in
keigo at a hotel desk, and in a written questionnaire, with no change of form.
Politeness is carried entirely by the endings around it:
<code>…ですか。それとも…ですか</code> is polite,
<code>…? それとも…?</code> is casual, and the word itself is the same.

Two habits of use are worth copying. In speech the second alternative is usually
clipped to the minimum - nobody repeats the whole question - and a rising intonation
does the rest. And when the speaker is talking to themselves rather than asking
anyone, the frame becomes <code>…か、それとも…か</code> followed by a verb of
deliberation: <code>{f|迷|まよ}っている</code>, <code>{f|考|かんが}えている</code>,
<code>{f|分|わ}からない</code>.

One caution about tone. Because `それとも` presents a fork, adding a second option to
someone else's statement can sound like a challenge:
「{f|忘|わす}れたんですか。それともわざとですか」 is an accusation, not a question. That
is a matter of what you put in the second slot, not of the word itself, but the shape
sharpens it.

Finally, `それとも` marks the alternatives as genuinely exclusive - one or the other,
not both. Where both might be true, the sentence wants か, or a plain
<code>{f|両方|りょうほう}</code>.

## Contrast

### それとも vs か

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - か joins two options **inside one question**. `それとも` joins
**two whole questions**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昼|ひる}は{f|和食|わしょく}<b>か</b>{f|中華|ちゅうか}にしませんか。
Shall we have Japanese or Chinese for lunch?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昼|ひる}は{f|和食|わしょく}にしますか。<b>それとも</b>{f|中華|ちゅうか}にしますか。
Shall we have Japanese for lunch? Or Chinese?
</pre>

The first is one question with two nouns in it; the second is two questions, put one
after the other. The second gives each option its own weight and its own moment,
which is why a waiter uses it and a friend in a hurry does not. The particle か is
also the only one of the two that can appear in a statement.

### それとも vs または・あるいは

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `それとも` **asks**; <code>または</code> and `あるいは` **tell**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|一階|いっかい}<b>または</b>{f|二階|にかい}にございます。
Reception is on the ground floor or the first floor.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|一階|いっかい}ですか。<b>それとも</b>{f|二階|にかい}ですか。
Is reception on the ground floor, or the first?
</pre>

The first sentence informs; the alternatives are both true as far as the reader is
concerned and no reply is expected. The second demands one. Swapping them produces
sentences that are not merely odd but incoherent - a notice that asks the reader a
question, and a question that lists.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}それとも{f|明後日|あさって}{f|伺|うかが}います。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}<b>か</b>{f|明後日|あさって}{f|伺|うかが}います。
  A statement, so the choice has to be joined with か. `それとも` needs a question on
  both sides of it.

- ❌ {f|赤|あか}それとも{f|青|あお}を{f|選|えら}んでください。 → ✅ {f|赤|あか}<b>か</b>{f|青|あお}を{f|選|えら}んでください。
  A request is not a question either, even though it offers a choice. The listener is
  being told to pick, not being asked which they would pick.

- ❌ {f|飲|の}み{f|物|もの}はビールですか。それともお{f|酒|さけ}も{f|飲|の}みますか。 → ✅ …ビールですか。それとも<b>ワインですか</b>。
  The two options have to be exclusive. If both could be true, the sentence needs か
  or <code>{f|両方|りょうほう}</code> instead.

## Practice

**1.** {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|今週|こんしゅう}にしますか。____{f|来週|らいしゅう}まで{f|待|ま}ちますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>それとも</b>{f|来週|らいしゅう}まで{f|待|ま}ちますか。

Two complete questions, and the listener is being asked to choose one. That is the
only environment this word lives in. Note that both halves keep their own ですか.

</details>

**2.** Find and fix the error: お{f|支払|しはら}いは{f|現金|げんきん}それともカードでお{f|願|ねが}いします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ お{f|支払|しはら}いは{f|現金|げんきん}<b>か</b>カードでお{f|願|ねが}いします。（or お{f|支払|しはら}いは{f|現金|げんきん}ですか。<b>それとも</b>カードですか。）

As written it is a request, not a question, so `それとも` has nothing to attach to.
Either join the options with か and keep the request, or turn the whole thing into two
questions.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|一人|ひとり}で{f|行|い}きますか、{f|誰|だれ}かと{f|行|い}きますか。
(b) {f|一人|ひとり}で{f|行|い}きますか。それとも{f|誰|だれ}かと{f|行|い}きますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) runs the two questions together and sounds like a form being filled in - brisk,
almost like ticking a box.

(b) puts a beat between them. `それとも` announces that a second, separate possibility
is coming, which gives the listener a moment to consider and makes the question feel
offered rather than processed. In service Japanese that pause is the whole point.

</details>
