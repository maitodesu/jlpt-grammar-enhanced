# いまだに

**Meaning** · still, even now - after all this time, and it should have changed by
now

`いまだに` is not a neutral "still". It says that a situation has gone on far
longer than it ought to have, and it puts the speaker's impatience, disbelief or
resignation into the sentence.

## Usage

`いまだに` is an adverb and sits in front of the predicate, or at the head of the
clause. It is written {f|未|いま}だに in kanji, and just as often in kana.

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `いまだに` needs a state that is **still continuing when it
shouldn't be**. Most often that state is a negative: いまだに～ない,
いまだに～ていない.

</div>

The two shapes it takes:

- With a negative - the commonest by far, and the one to learn first:
  いまだに{f|連絡|れんらく}がない, いまだに{f|原因|げんいん}が{f|分|わ}からない,
  いまだに{f|決|き}まっていない
- With an affirmative, which then has to be a **continuing state**, normally
  `～ている` or an existence verb: いまだに{f|覚|おぼ}えている,
  いまだに{f|人気|にんき}がある, いまだに{f|信|しん}じている

A one-off past event will not do. ❌いまだに{f|来|き}ました is not Japanese; the
sentence has to describe a condition that reaches into the present.

`いまだに` also refuses a stated span of time. ❌いまだに{f|五分|ごふん}しか{f|経|た}っていない is wrong, because five minutes is not "all this time" -
まだ{f|五分|ごふん}しか{f|経|た}っていない is the sentence you want.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}からは<b>いまだに</b>{f|返事|へんじ}がない。
I still haven't had a reply from him.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事故|じこ}の{f|原因|げんいん}は<b>いまだに</b>{f|分|わ}かっていません。
The cause of the accident is still unknown.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|子|こ}どものころに{f|聞|き}いた{f|歌|うた}を、<b>いまだに</b>{f|口|くち}ずさんでしまう。
I still catch myself humming songs I heard as a child.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|申請|しんせい}してから{f|半年|はんとし}になるのに、{f|許可|きょか}が<b>いまだに</b>{f|下|お}りない。
It's been six months since we applied and the permit still hasn't come through.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The word is a shade more formal and more written than まだ, but it is completely
at home in adult conversation, in news reports and in business email alike. It is
not marked for age or gender.

What it always carries is a judgement. いまだに{f|返事|へんじ}がない is a complaint;
いまだに{f|人気|にんき}がある is admiration that the popularity has outlasted
expectation; いまだに{f|信|しん}じられない is astonishment. If you have no such
attitude and simply want to report that something is ongoing, まだ is the honest
choice, and using `いまだに` instead will sound as though you are needling someone.

That makes it worth handling carefully when the person responsible is in the
room. 「いまだに{f|終|お}わっていないんですか」is close to "you *still* haven't
finished?" - grammatical, polite in form, and pointed.

The spelling {f|今|いま}だに is a very common native error and turns up even in
print. The word is {f|未|いま}だ, "not yet", plus `に`; the kanji is <code>未</code>, not <code>今</code>. If
you are unsure, write it in kana.

## Contrast

### いまだに vs まだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `まだ` reports that something is unfinished. `いまだに` reports
that it is unfinished **and that this is unreasonable**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|工事|こうじ}は<b>まだ</b>{f|終|お}わっていません。
The construction isn't finished yet.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|工事|こうじ}は<b>いまだに</b>{f|終|お}わっていません。
The construction still isn't finished, after all this time.
</pre>

`まだ` is neutral and can look forward - まだ{f|時間|じかん}があります, "there's
still time" - which `いまだに` cannot do, because a resource you still have is not
an overdue situation. `まだ` also works with short intervals and with quantities;
`いまだに` works only with stretches long enough for the speaker to have expected a
change.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|今|いま}だに{f|連絡|れんらく}がありません。 → ✅ <b>{f|未|いま}だに</b>{f|連絡|れんらく}がありません。
  The kanji is <code>未</code>, from {f|未|いま}だ "not yet". Writing it with <code>今</code> is a spelling
  error, not a variant.

- ❌ バスが<b>いまだに</b>{f|三分|さんぷん}{f|遅|おく}れています。 → ✅ バスが{f|三分|さんぷん}{f|遅|おく}れています。
  Three minutes is not "after all this time". The adverb needs a delay long enough
  to be an affront.

- ❌ いまだに{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|出|で}ました。 → ✅ {f|昨日|きのう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|出|で}ました。
  A finished one-off event cannot be "still" happening. `いまだに` needs a state
  that continues into the present.

- ❌ まだ{f|十年|じゅうねん}{f|前|まえ}のことを{f|怒|おこ}っている。 → ✅ <b>いまだに</b>{f|十年|じゅうねん}{f|前|まえ}のことを{f|怒|おこ}っている。
  Not ungrammatical, but flat. When the point of the sentence *is* that the
  grudge has lasted absurdly long, `まだ` throws that away.

## Practice

**1.** {f|三年|さんねん}{f|前|まえ}に{f|貸|か}したお{f|金|かね}が、____{f|返|かえ}ってこない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|三年|さんねん}{f|前|まえ}に{f|貸|か}したお{f|金|かね}が、<b>いまだに</b>{f|返|かえ}ってこない。

Three years plus a negative predicate is exactly the shape this adverb wants, and
the complaint is the whole point of the sentence. まだ would be grammatical but
would drain the exasperation out of it.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: {f|急|いそ}がなくていいですよ。（まだ・いまだに）{f|時間|じかん}があります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>まだ</b>{f|時間|じかん}があります。

Here "still" points forward at a resource that has not run out yet, which is a
neutral report. `いまだに` needs an overdue situation, and there is nothing overdue
about having time left.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) あの{f|店|みせ}はまだ{f|開|あ}いている。
(b) あの{f|店|みせ}はいまだに{f|開|あ}いている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) simply says the shop has not closed yet - useful information if you are
hurrying to get there. (b) says the shop is somehow still in business after all
these years, and the speaker is impressed, or faintly amazed, that it has
survived. The same four words describe a shop open late and a shop open for
decades.

</details>
