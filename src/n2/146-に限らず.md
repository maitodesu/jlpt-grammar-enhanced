# ～に限らず

**Meaning** · not just ～; the same is true beyond ～ as well

<code>{f|限|かぎ}らず</code> is the negative stem of <code>{f|限|かぎ}る</code> - "not
limiting it to". The sentence names an obvious category, refuses to stop there, and then
extends the claim outward. The reader's expected boundary is stated precisely so that it
can be knocked down.

## Formation

The pattern attaches to a **noun**. The second clause almost always carries
<code>も</code> or <code>でも</code>, which is what does the extending.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + に{f|限|かぎ}らず | {f|学生|がくせい} → {f|学生|がくせい}に{f|限|かぎ}らず |
| Noun (time) | Noun + に{f|限|かぎ}らず | {f|土日|どにち} → {f|土日|どにち}に{f|限|かぎ}らず |
| Second clause | …に{f|限|かぎ}らず、… も／でも | → {f|大人|おとな}に{f|限|かぎ}らず、{f|子|こ}どもも |
| As a predicate | Noun + に{f|限|かぎ}らない | {f|日本|にほん} → {f|問題|もんだい}は{f|日本|にほん}に{f|限|かぎ}らない |
| Polite predicate | Noun + に{f|限|かぎ}りません | → {f|東京|とうきょう}に{f|限|かぎ}りません |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the connective form is <code>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</code> and the sentence-final
form is <code>に{f|限|かぎ}らない</code>. Do not end a sentence with the
<code>ず</code> form; it is a mid-sentence connector, like the て-form of a negative.

</div>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

<code>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</code> is a written-leaning form. Its natural homes are news
copy, company announcements, academic prose and the kind of careful spoken Japanese you
hear in a presentation. The <code>ず</code> ending is inherited from classical Japanese
and gives the phrase a tidy, slightly formal edge - not stiff, but noticeably more
composed than <code>だけでなく</code>.

It is not rude or archaic, and nobody would blink at it in a meeting. In a chat with a
friend it would simply be more words than the moment needs;
<code>{f|学生|がくせい}だけじゃなくて</code> is what you would actually say.

Because the pattern names a boundary in order to reject it, the noun in front of it
should be one the listener plausibly assumed. <code>{f|学生|がくせい}に{f|限|かぎ}らず</code>
works because a library really does suggest students. Put a random noun there and the
sentence sounds like it is answering a question nobody asked.

There is a stronger, more literary sibling, <code>のみならず</code>, which does the same
job in heavier prose, and an emphatic one, <code>はもとより</code>, which adds "to say
nothing of". Both are treated on their own pages. If you only ever use
<code>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</code>, you will still sound natural.

## Contrast

### に{f|限|かぎ}らず vs だけでなく

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different reach.
<code>だけでなく</code> attaches to almost anything and fits any register;
<code>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</code> takes **nouns only** and leans formal.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}は{f|安|やす}い<b>だけでなく</b>、{f|味|あじ}もいい。
This place isn't just cheap, the food is good too.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値上|ねあ}げは{f|食品|しょくひん}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>、{f|日用品|にちようひん}にも{f|広|ひろ}がっている。
The price rises aren't confined to food - they've spread to household goods as well.
</pre>

The first sentence cannot use <code>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</code> at all, because
<code>{f|安|やす}い</code> is an adjective. The second could use
<code>だけでなく</code> and would simply sound a shade more everyday. When in doubt,
<code>だけでなく</code> is the safe choice; <code>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</code> is the choice
that signals you are writing rather than chatting.

### に{f|限|かぎ}らず vs とは{f|限|かぎ}らない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - they look like the same verb and mean quite different things.
<code>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</code> = "not only X (but also Y)".
<code>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> = "not necessarily X".

</div>

<pre>
{f|参加者|さんかしゃ}は{f|社員|しゃいん}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>、{f|家族|かぞく}も{f|来|き}ています。
The people taking part aren't just employees - families are here too.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|参加者|さんかしゃ}が{f|社員|しゃいん}だ<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。
The participants aren't necessarily employees.
</pre>

The first counts more people in. The second declines to guarantee anything about who
they are. The clue is the particle: <code>に</code> introduces a category being
extended, <code>と</code> introduces a claim being doubted.

### に{f|限|かぎ}らず vs に{f|限|かぎ}って

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same verb, positive and negative.
<code>に{f|限|かぎ}って</code> narrows to one case; <code>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</code>
refuses to narrow at all.

</div>

<pre>
{f|夏|なつ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}って</b>、この{f|坂道|さかみち}は{f|観光客|かんこうきゃく}でいっぱいになる。
It's in summer specifically that this slope fills up with tourists.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|夏|なつ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>、この{f|坂道|さかみち}は{f|一年中|いちねんじゅう}{f|観光客|かんこうきゃく}でいっぱいだ。
It isn't only summer - this slope is packed with tourists all year round.
</pre>

If you swap them by accident the sentence still parses, which is what makes the mistake
hard to notice. Check the second clause: if it adds something with <code>も</code>, you
need <code>らず</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|店|みせ}は<b>{f|安|やす}いに{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>、{f|品物|しなもの}もいい。 → ✅ この{f|店|みせ}は<b>{f|安|やす}いだけでなく</b>、{f|品物|しなもの}もいい。
  Nouns only. An adjective or a verb needs <code>だけでなく</code> or
  <code>ばかりでなく</code>.

- ❌ {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|月曜|げつよう}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>、{f|火曜|かよう}に{f|行|おこな}う。 → ✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|月曜|げつよう}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>、{f|火曜|かよう}<b>にも</b>{f|行|おこな}う。
  Without <code>も</code> the sentence reads as a replacement rather than an addition,
  and the first half is left dangling.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|来|く}る<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|来|く}る<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。
  <code>らず</code> connects; it cannot end a sentence. And with
  <code>と</code> the meaning is "not necessarily", which is a different pattern.

- ❌ {f|問題|もんだい}は{f|東京|とうきょう}<b>だけに{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>… → ✅ {f|問題|もんだい}は{f|東京|とうきょう}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>…
  <code>だけ</code> is doing the same work as <code>{f|限|かぎ}る</code>, so the two
  together are redundant. You hear it, but it is not what you want in writing.

## Practice

**1.** この{f|割引|わりびき}は{f|会員|かいいん}____、どなたでもご{f|利用|りよう}いただけます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会員|かいいん}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>、どなたでもご{f|利用|りよう}いただけます。

A discount at a shop naturally suggests members, so the sentence names that boundary
and then removes it. <code>どなたでも</code> in the second half is what the pattern is
extending to.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|値段|ねだん}が{f|高|たか}ければおいしい（に{f|限|かぎ}らない・とは{f|限|かぎ}らない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|値段|ねだん}が{f|高|たか}ければおいしい<b>とは{f|限|かぎ}らない</b>。

The sentence casts doubt on a general assumption, which is the <code>と</code> pattern.
<code>に{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> would need a noun in front of it and would mean "isn't
confined to", which does not fit here at all.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Overtime is a problem not only in this department but across the whole company."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|残業|ざんぎょう}は、この{f|部署|ぶしょ}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}らず</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}{f|全体|ぜんたい}の{f|問題|もんだい}だ。

<code>{f|部署|ぶしょ}</code> is a noun, so the pattern attaches straight on. The English
"not only… but" maps onto the single Japanese connector; no extra word for "but" is
needed.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|美術館|びじゅつかん}は{f|大人|おとな}に{f|限|かぎ}らずです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|美術館|びじゅつかん}は{f|大人|おとな}<b>に{f|限|かぎ}りません</b>。（or …{f|大人|おとな}に{f|限|かぎ}らず、{f|子|こ}どもも{f|楽|たの}しめます）

<code>らず</code> is a connective and cannot take <code>です</code>. To end the
sentence there, use the plain negative <code>{f|限|かぎ}らない</code> or its polite form.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|夜間|やかん}に{f|限|かぎ}って{f|駐車|ちゅうしゃ}できます。
(b) {f|夜間|やかん}に{f|限|かぎ}らず{f|駐車|ちゅうしゃ}できます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) narrows parking down to the night: at any other hour you may not park. It is the
wording of a restriction.

(b) removes the restriction: you might have assumed night-only, and the sentence tells
you the daytime is fine as well. One letter of difference in the Japanese, and a driver
who reads it wrong gets a ticket.

</details>
