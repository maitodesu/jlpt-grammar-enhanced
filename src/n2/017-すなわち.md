# すなわち

**Meaning** · namely; that is to say - puts an equals sign between two expressions
for the same thing

`すなわち` is the written language's `つまり`. It announces that what follows is
not a new fact but the *same* fact under a different name: a technical term given
its plain equivalent, a date given its calendar form, a category given its precise
extent.

## Usage

`すなわち` is a conjunction. It sits between the two expressions it equates, almost
always after a comma, and it never changes form.

The commonest shape is noun phrase, comma, `すなわち`, noun phrase:

- {f|本社|ほんしゃ}の{f|所在地|しょざいち}、すなわち{f|大阪市|おおさかし}
- {f|創業|そうぎょう}の{f|年|とし}、すなわち{f|一九六二年|せんきゅうひゃくろくじゅうにねん}

It can also join two clauses, in which case the second restates the first in
plainer or more precise terms:

- {f|申込|もうしこみ}は{f|先着順|せんちゃくじゅん}である。すなわち、{f|定員|ていいん}に{f|達|たっ}し{f|次第|しだい}{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}る。

Modern editorial practice writes it in kana. The kanji {f|即|すなわ}ち still turns
up in older or deliberately formal texts.

<div class="note">

**Note** - in classical Japanese and in translations of the Chinese classics
`すなわち` also means "then; thereupon", often spelled {f|則|すなわ}ち. You will meet
it in that sense in a proverb or an old text; it is not available in modern prose,
where the equating sense is the only live one.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|料金|りょうきん}は{f|現地|げんち}の{f|通貨|つうか}、<b>すなわち</b>ユーロで{f|払|はら}う。
Payment is in the local currency, that is, in euros.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|音|おと}の{f|速|はや}さ、<b>すなわち</b>{f|音速|おんそく}は{f|気温|きおん}で{f|変|か}わる。
The speed of sound - the sonic velocity - varies with air temperature.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|本|ほん}{f|契約|けいやく}は{f|締結|ていけつ}の{f|日|ひ}、<b>すなわち</b>{f|四月|しがつ}{f|一日|ついたち}から{f|効力|こうりょく}を{f|持|も}つ。
This contract takes effect on the date of signing, namely 1 April.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|調査|ちょうさ}では{f|回答者|かいとうしゃ}の{f|三分|さんぶん}の{f|二|に}、<b>すなわち</b>{f|約|やく}{f|六百人|ろっぴゃくにん}が{f|値上|ねあ}げに{f|反対|はんたい}であった。
In the survey two-thirds of respondents - some six hundred people - opposed the price rise.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a written word. It belongs to contracts, statutes, academic papers,
technical manuals, annual reports and the printed part of a museum caption. Spoken
aloud it survives in lectures, courtroom argument and formal presentations, where
the speaker is essentially reading. In ordinary conversation it lands like a clause
of a rental agreement read out over dinner, and a learner who deploys it in a café
will get a smile.

What it adds over `つまり`, which you already have from N3, is register and nothing
else - the meaning is the same. That is the honest answer, and it is worth saying
plainly: this page exists so that you recognise `すなわち` on the page and choose
`つまり` when you open your mouth.

One consequence of the equating sense is that `すなわち` commits you. It claims the
two expressions have exactly the same extension, so a writer who uses it and then
turns out to have listed only some examples has made a real error. When you are not
certain the two sides match, `たとえば` or `など` is the safer word.

`すなわち` also has no emotional colour at all. `つまり` can carry mild impatience,
<code>{f|要|よう}するに</code> carries a judgement that the original was long-winded;
`すなわち` carries neither. That neutrality is exactly why formal documents like it.

## Contrast

### すなわち vs つまり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same job, different clothes. `つまり` goes anywhere; `すなわち`
is written-register only, and it prefers two noun phrases rather than two sentences.

</div>

<pre>
{f|第|だい}{f|三条|さんじょう}に{f|定|さだ}める{f|期間|きかん}、<b>すなわち</b>{f|六|ろっ}か{f|月|げつ}を{f|超|こ}えてはならない。
It may not exceed the period laid down in Article 3, namely six months.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|半年|はんとし}まで、<b>つまり</b>{f|六|ろっ}か{f|月|げつ}までってことだね。
Up to half a year - six months, in other words.
</pre>

The content is identical; only the clothes differ. Notice too that the `つまり`
version ends in <code>ってこと</code>, which `すなわち` would never take. The
sentence-final material has to match the register of the connector, and mismatching
them is a very audible mistake.

### すなわち vs {f|言|い}い{f|換|か}えれば

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `すなわち` says the two expressions **are** the same thing.
<code>{f|言|い}い{f|換|か}えれば</code> says the writer is **rephrasing**, and the
rewording may be looser.

</div>

<pre>
{f|出席率|しゅっせきりつ}は{f|七割|ななわり}、<b>すなわち</b>{f|十人|じゅうにん}のうち{f|七人|しちにん}であった。
Attendance was seventy per cent, that is, seven people in ten.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|出席率|しゅっせきりつ}は{f|七割|ななわり}だった。<b>{f|言|い}い{f|換|か}えれば</b>、{f|三割|さんわり}の{f|人|ひと}が{f|来|こ}なかったのである。
Attendance was seventy per cent. To put it another way, three in ten did not come.
</pre>

The second version is not an equation - it turns the figure round and looks at it
from the other side. `すなわち` cannot do that, because the two halves would no
longer be the same statement. When your rewording adds a point of view, reach for
<code>{f|言|い}い{f|換|か}えれば</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ すなわち、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|行|い}けないんだ。（to a friend）→ ✅ <b>つまり</b>、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|行|い}けないんだ。
  The connector and the ending have to agree in register. `すなわち` in front of
  `んだ` sounds like a statute read aloud in a living room.

- ❌ {f|果物|くだもの}、<b>すなわち</b>りんごやみかん → ✅ {f|果物|くだもの}、<b>たとえば</b>りんごやみかん
  `すなわち` claims the two sides are the same set. Apples and oranges are only
  instances of fruit, so this needs `たとえば` or `など`.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|続|つづ}いた。<b>すなわち</b>{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|続|つづ}いた。<b>したがって</b>{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になった。
  A cancellation is a consequence, not a restatement of the rain. In modern
  Japanese `すなわち` does not express cause and effect; したがって or ゆえに does.

## Practice

**1.** {f|申込|もうしこみ}の{f|締切|しめきり}は{f|今年度|こんねんど}{f|末|まつ}、____{f|三月|さんがつ}{f|三十一日|さんじゅういちにち}である。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>すなわち</b>{f|三月|さんがつ}{f|三十一日|さんじゅういちにち}である

The second expression is the same date as the first, written out precisely, and
the sentence ends in `である` - a written-style sentence needs a written-style
connector.

</details>

**2.** Choose: あの{f|人|ひと}、{f|優|やさ}しいっていうか、（すなわち・つまり）{f|断|ことわ}れないだけだと{f|思|おも}う。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

（{f|優|やさ}しいっていうか、）<b>つまり</b>{f|断|ことわ}れないだけだと{f|思|おも}う。

Everything around the blank is casual - <code>っていうか</code>, <code>だけだと{f|思|おも}う</code>.
`すなわち` would clash with all of it, and in speech it is essentially unavailable
outside a lectern.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|会長|かいちょう}、<b>すなわち</b>{f|創業者|そうぎょうしゃ}の{f|孫|まご}が{f|挨拶|あいさつ}した。
(b) {f|会長|かいちょう}、<b>たとえば</b>{f|創業者|そうぎょうしゃ}の{f|孫|まご}が{f|挨拶|あいさつ}した。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) states that the chairman and the founder's grandson are one and the same
person; the second phrase simply identifies who the chairman is. This is the normal
reading and the one the sentence wants.

(b) is broken, and instructively so. `たとえば` presents the second phrase as one
possible instance of the first, which would mean the company has several chairmen
and this happened to be one of them. When the two expressions pick out the same
individual, only `すなわち` works.

</details>
