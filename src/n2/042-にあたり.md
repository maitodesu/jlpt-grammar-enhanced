# ～にあたり / ～にあたって

**Meaning** · on the occasion of; now that we stand at the point of - said at the threshold of something significant that is about to begin

<code>にあたり</code> is built on <code>{f|当|あ}たる</code>, "to face something head on". It
places the sentence at the moment a **significant undertaking starts** - a posting,
a merger, an opening ceremony, a course of study - and reports what is done, felt or
asked for at that threshold. It lives in speeches, announcements, formal letters and
the "before you begin" page of an official form. Used over a trip to the shops it
sounds as though you have stepped up to a lectern.

## Formation

The noun or clause in front names the undertaking. `にあたり` is the {f|連用形|れんようけい}
(the stem-joining form) and `にあたって` the て-form of the
same expression; they mean the same thing.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event noun | Noun + にあたり | {f|開会|かいかい} → {f|開会|かいかい}にあたり |
| Event noun (slightly softer) | Noun + にあたって | {f|入学|にゅうがく} → {f|入学|にゅうがく}にあたって |
| Verb (dictionary form) | dictionary + にあたり／にあたって | {f|出発|しゅっぱつ}する → {f|出発|しゅっぱつ}するにあたって |
| Marked as the topic | ～にあたっては | {f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}む → {f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}むにあたっては |
| Before a noun | ～にあたっての + Noun | {f|着任|ちゃくにん} → {f|着任|ちゃくにん}にあたっての{f|挨拶|あいさつ} |
| Spoken formal address | ～にあたりまして | {f|開店|かいてん} → {f|開店|かいてん}にあたりまして |
| ❌ Past form | ❌{f|卒業|そつぎょう}したにあたって | ✅{f|卒業|そつぎょう}するにあたって |
| ❌ Adjective | ❌{f|忙|いそが}しいにあたって | ✅{f|忙|いそが}しい{f|時|とき}に |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - three tests. Is the occasion **big** enough to make a speech
about? Is it **starting**, not finished? Is the surrounding sentence formal? If any
answer is no, you want <code>{f|時|とき}に</code> or <code>{f|際|さい}に</code> instead.

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is written and ceremonial Japanese. You will meet it in the opening line of a
speech, in the preamble of a notice, in a covering letter, and on the first page of
an official form. You will not meet it between friends, and a learner who deploys it
over a small personal plan sounds unintentionally grand.

Of the two shapes, `にあたり` is the stiffer: it is the form of choice for printed
notices and prepared remarks. `にあたって` is a shade lighter and is what a manager
would say aloud in a meeting. `にあたりまして` is the polite-spoken version and
belongs almost entirely to podium Japanese. There is no casual contraction; the
pattern simply does not go there.

The main clause is nearly always one of four things: a **greeting**
(ご{f|挨拶|あいさつ}{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます), a **request** (ご{f|注意|ちゅうい}ください),
a **preparation** ({f|準備|じゅんび}した、{f|調|しら}べた), or a **resolution**
({f|決|き}めた、{f|約束|やくそく}した). Adverbs such as まず, {f|改|あらた}めて and ぜひ
sit naturally in front of it.

<div class="note">

**Note** - do not confuse this with the ordinary verb <code>{f|当|あ}たる</code> doing
its other jobs: <code>{f|一|いち}ドルは{f|約|やく}{f|百五十円|ひゃくごじゅうえん}に{f|当|あ}たる</code>
("amounts to") and <code>{f|対応|たいおう}に{f|当|あ}たる</code> ("to be assigned to
handle"). Those are Noun + に + verb, not the connective.

</div>

## Contrast

### ～にあたり vs ～{f|際|さい}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|際|さい}に</code> is the formal word for **"when"** and
will take any occasion, including a repeated one or a past one. `にあたり` needs a
**one-off undertaking** and looks forward from its starting line.

</div>

<pre>
ご{f|利用|りよう}の<b>{f|際|さい}は</b>、{f|受付|うけつけ}で{f|番号札|ばんごうふだ}をお{f|取|と}りください。
When you use the service, please take a numbered ticket at reception.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事業|じぎょう}を{f|引|ひ}き{f|継|つ}ぐ<b>にあたり</b>、{f|従業員|じゅうぎょういん}{f|全員|ぜんいん}と{f|面談|めんだん}した。
On taking over the business, I met every employee individually.
</pre>

The first happens every time a customer walks in, and `にあたり` would be absurd
there. The second happens once, and the sentence stands at its beginning. Note also
that <code>{f|際|さい}に</code> attaches happily to a た-form for a past occasion -
<code>{f|面接|めんせつ}を{f|受|う}けた{f|際|さい}に</code> - while `にあたり` stays
non-past even when the whole sentence is in the past.

### ～にあたり vs ～に{f|際|さい}して

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are formal occasion markers and they overlap. The tendency:
`にあたって` is the **threshold of something you are launching**, and carries
preparation and resolve; <code>に{f|際|さい}して</code> simply marks the **time of a
specific event**, and is comfortable with unwelcome ones.

</div>

<pre>
{f|退職|たいしょく}<b>に{f|際|さい}し</b>、{f|長年|ながねん}のご{f|厚意|こうい}にお{f|礼|れい}を{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます。
On my retirement, I would like to thank you for your kindness over many years.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|新|あたら}しい{f|部署|ぶしょ}を{f|立|た}ち{f|上|あ}げる<b>にあたって</b>、{f|三年分|さんねんぶん}の{f|計画|けいかく}を{f|書|か}いた。
In launching the new department, I wrote out a three-year plan.
</pre>

Retirement, cancellation and emergencies take <code>に{f|際|さい}して</code> readily;
`にあたって` there would suggest you were relishing the occasion. Treat this as a
leaning rather than a wall - <code>{f|開店|かいてん}に{f|際|さい}して</code> is
perfectly normal too.

### ～にあたり vs ～に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</code> is strictly **before**: the
main clause happens in the run-up. `にあたり` sits **at** the occasion and covers the
doing of it as well.

</div>

<pre>
{f|開幕|かいまく}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</b>、{f|記者会見|きしゃかいけん}が{f|開|ひら}かれた。
Ahead of the opening, a press conference was held.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|開幕|かいまく}<b>にあたり</b>、{f|監督|かんとく}が{f|今季|こんき}の{f|目標|もくひょう}を{f|語|かた}った。
At the opening of the season, the manager spoke about his goals for the year.
</pre>

The press conference is genuinely earlier than the event; the manager remarks as
part of the occasion itself. Where the main clause is a **preparation**, both are
possible; where it is a **greeting delivered on the day**, only `にあたり` fits.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ コンビニへ{f|行|い}く<b>にあたって</b>、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}った。 → ✅ コンビニへ{f|行|い}く<b>{f|時|とき}に</b>、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}った。
  The occasion has to be worth a speech. A trip to the shop takes
  <code>{f|時|とき}に</code>.

- ❌ {f|卒業|そつぎょう}した<b>にあたって</b>、{f|恩師|おんし}に{f|手紙|てがみ}を{f|書|か}いた。 → ✅ {f|卒業|そつぎょう}する<b>にあたって</b>、{f|恩師|おんし}に{f|手紙|てがみ}を{f|書|か}いた。
  The verb stays in the dictionary form. Tense is carried by the final verb, so a
  past sentence still reads <code>{f|卒業|そつぎょう}するにあたって</code>.

- ❌ {f|新|あたら}しい{f|生活|せいかつ}が{f|不安|ふあん}な<b>にあたって</b>… → ✅ {f|新|あたら}しい{f|生活|せいかつ}を{f|始|はじ}める<b>にあたって</b>…
  Adjectives and states do not attach. `にあたり` wants an **event**: something that
  begins at a point in time.

- ❌ {f|開会|かいかい}<b>に{f|当|あ}たる</b>、{f|会長|かいちょう}が{f|挨拶|あいさつ}した。 → ✅ {f|開会|かいかい}<b>に{f|当|あ}たり</b>、{f|会長|かいちょう}が{f|挨拶|あいさつ}した。
  The connective is the {f|連用形|れんようけい} <code>あたり</code> or the て-form <code>あたって</code>.
  The dictionary form <code>あたる</code> is the plain verb and cannot join two clauses
  like this.

## Practice

**1.** {f|新製品|しんせいひん}を{f|発売|はつばい}する____、{f|大|おお}きな{f|広告|こうこく}を{f|出|だ}すことにした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|発売|はつばい}する<b>にあたり</b>（or にあたって）

A product launch is a one-off undertaking, and the advertising is the preparation
made at its threshold. The verb stays non-past even though the decision is reported
in the past.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|工場|こうじょう}を{f|見学|けんがく}する（にあたって・{f|際|さい}に）は、ヘルメットを{f|着用|ちゃくよう}してください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|見学|けんがく}する<b>{f|際|さい}</b>は

Factory tours happen over and over, and the rule applies to every visitor on every
visit. That is the routine, repeatable occasion <code>{f|際|さい}に</code> was made
for. `にあたって` would frame one particular tour as a landmark event.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "On taking up my new post, I would like to say a few words."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|着任|ちゃくにん}する<b>にあたり</b>、{f|一言|ひとこと}ご{f|挨拶|あいさつ}{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます。

This is the archetypal use: a formal greeting delivered at the threshold of a new
role. <code>{f|着任|ちゃくにん}にあたり</code> with the bare noun is equally good, and
is what a printed notice would say.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|試合|しあい}が{f|終|お}わるにあたって、{f|選手|せんしゅ}たちは{f|握手|あくしゅ}をした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|試合|しあい}が{f|終|お}わった<b>{f|時|とき}</b>、{f|選手|せんしゅ}たちは{f|握手|あくしゅ}をした。

`にあたり` marks the **start** of an undertaking, not its end, so pairing it with
<code>{f|終|お}わる</code> pulls against the pattern. An ending takes
<code>{f|時|とき}</code>, と, or - in formal writing -
<code>{f|終了|しゅうりょう}に{f|際|さい}し</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|入学|にゅうがく}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</b>、{f|健康診断|けんこうしんだん}が{f|行|おこな}われる。
(b) {f|入学|にゅうがく}<b>にあたり</b>、{f|校長|こうちょう}が{f|新入生|しんにゅうせい}に{f|話|はなし}をする。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) puts the medical check clearly **before** enrolment - it is a hurdle in the
run-up, finished by the time term starts.

(b) puts the talk **at** enrolment itself. It is part of the occasion rather than a
preliminary to it, which is why `にあたり` and not
<code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</code> is the natural choice.

</details>
