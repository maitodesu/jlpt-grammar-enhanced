# ～て当然だ

**Meaning** · of course it is; only natural; no surprise there - a verdict that the outcome is exactly what the circumstances called for

<code>{f|当然|とうぜん}</code> (とうぜん) is a noun-cum-な-adjective meaning "a matter of
course". Hung off a て-form, it turns the clause in front of it into a judgement:
given what we both already know, *this* result needed no explaining. It looks
backward at a fact on the table, and it can be warm ("of course you're tired") or
sharp ("naturally they walked out").

## Formation

The pattern is the て-form of the predicate plus <code>{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code>.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + {f|当然|とうぜん}だ | {f|怒|おこ}る → {f|怒|おこ}って{f|当然|とうぜん}だ |
| Verb (negative) | ～なくて + {f|当然|とうぜん}だ | {f|読|よ}めない → {f|読|よ}めなくて{f|当然|とうぜん}だ |
| い-adjective | ～くて + {f|当然|とうぜん}だ | {f|眠|ねむ}い → {f|眠|ねむ}くて{f|当然|とうぜん}だ |
| な-adjective | ～で + {f|当然|とうぜん}だ | {f|不安|ふあん}な → {f|不安|ふあん}で{f|当然|とうぜん}だ |
| Noun | ～で + {f|当然|とうぜん}だ (rare) | {f|無料|むりょう} → {f|無料|むりょう}で{f|当然|とうぜん}だ |
| Polite | … + {f|当然|とうぜん}です | {f|疲|つか}れて{f|当然|とうぜん}です |
| Before a noun | {f|当然|とうぜん}の + noun | {f|当然|とうぜん}の{f|権利|けんり} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the noun row is genuinely uncommon. A bare noun rarely reads as a
result you can pass judgement on, so writers recast it as
<code>{f|無料|むりょう}なのは{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code>. Verbs and adjectives are
where this pattern actually lives.

</div>

## Nuance & register

The form itself is neutral. <code>{f|当然|とうぜん}です</code> is at home in a
report, a meeting or a newspaper column, and <code>{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> in
ordinary plain speech. What carries the tone is not the ending but the *stance*,
and this pattern has two very different ones.

Turned outward at somebody else's feelings it is generous - it grants permission.
<code>{f|不安|ふあん}で{f|当然|とうぜん}だよ</code> tells the listener they are not
overreacting, and that is one of the kindest things this construction does.

Turned toward what you think you are owed, it hardens fast.
<code>これくらいしてもらって{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> - "I'm entitled to at least
this much" - sounds arrogant, and entitlement is the reading a listener will reach
for whenever the subject is the speaker's own advantage. Japanese even has a stock
complaint for it: <code>{f|当然|とうぜん}だと{f|思|おも}っている</code>, "takes it
for granted". Notice which way your sentence points before you say it.

<code>{f|当|あ}たり{f|前|まえ}だ</code> is the everyday spoken twin and is blunter
in both directions. In writing - especially argumentative writing -
<code>～のは{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> and <code>～のも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> are
more frequent than the て-form version, and
<code>{f|当然|とうぜん}のことながら</code> ("as would be expected") is a stiff
adverbial set phrase that belongs to written style only.

## Contrast

### ～て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ vs ～のも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> hangs the verdict straight
off the predicate; <code>のも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> first packages the whole
clause as a noun, so it carries longer, more argued material.

</div>

<pre>
{f|寝|ね}ていないのだから、{f|集中|しゅうちゅう}できなく<b>て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>。
You haven't slept, so of course you can't concentrate.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三人|さんにん}{f|分|ぶん}の{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|一人|ひとり}でやっているのだから、{f|彼女|かのじょ}が{f|倒|たお}れた<b>のも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>。
She's been doing three people's work single-handed, so it's no wonder she collapsed.
</pre>

Because <code>の</code> nominalises, the second shape takes a past tense, a long
subordinate clause or a subject of its own without straining. The first is compact
and lands harder. There is no meaning difference worth agonising over; choose by
the weight of the clause.

### ～て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ vs ～て{f|当|あ}たり{f|前|まえ}だ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same judgement, different room.
<code>{f|当然|とうぜん}</code> is the Sino-Japanese word and survives into writing;
<code>{f|当|あ}たり{f|前|まえ}</code> is native, spoken, and more forceful.

</div>

<pre>
{f|新人|しんじん}なんだから、{f|失敗|しっぱい}し<b>て{f|当|あ}たり{f|前|まえ}だ</b>。
He's brand new - of course he makes mistakes.
</pre>

<code>{f|当|あ}たり{f|前|まえ}</code> can be dismissive on its own -
<code>そんなの{f|当|あ}たり{f|前|まえ}でしょ</code> is close to "well, obviously" -
so it is a poor choice when you are speaking up the hierarchy. In a written
report, use <code>{f|当然|とうぜん}</code>. That is a tendency of register, not a
rule of grammar; both are grammatical anywhere.

### ～て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ vs ～はずだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `はずだ` predicts something you have **not** confirmed.
<code>て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> passes judgement on something you already know
to be so.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|時間|じかん}なら{f|道|みち}はすいている<b>はずだ</b>。
The roads should be clear at this hour.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|連休|れんきゅう}の{f|初日|しょにち}だから、{f|道|みち}が{f|混|こ}ん<b>で{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>。
It's the first day of a long weekend - naturally the roads are jammed.
</pre>

You can be wrong about a `はずだ`, which is why <code>はずだったのに</code> exists.
There is no equivalent regret form here: you cannot be let down by a verdict on a
fact you are looking straight at.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|疲|つか}れる<b>{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b> → ✅ {f|疲|つか}れ<b>て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>
  A dictionary form cannot sit directly in front of <code>{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code>.
  Use the て-form, or nominalise: <code>{f|疲|つか}れるのは{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code>.

- ❌ {f|不安|ふあん}<b>くて{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b> → ✅ {f|不安|ふあん}<b>で{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>
  <code>{f|不安|ふあん}</code> is a な-adjective, so its て-form is
  <code>{f|不安|ふあん}で</code>. Only い-adjectives take くて.

- ❌ {f|読|よ}め<b>ないで{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b> → ✅ {f|読|よ}め<b>なくて{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>
  ないで is the "without doing" form and carries no cause. A negative that feeds a
  judgement about cause takes なくて.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っ<b>て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b> → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>でしょう</b>
  Tomorrow's weather is not yet a fact, so there is nothing to call natural. This
  pattern judges; it does not forecast.

## Practice

**1.** {f|荷物|にもつ}をこんなに{f|持|も}たされたら、{f|重|おも}____{f|当然|とうぜん}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|重|おも}<b>くて{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>

<code>{f|重|おも}い</code> is an い-adjective, so the て-form drops い and adds くて.
The sentence says the weight is no surprise, given how much was loaded on.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|渋滞|じゅうたい}しているから、{f|彼|かれ}はもう{f|少|すこ}し{f|遅|おく}れる（て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ・はずだ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|遅|おく}れる<b>はずだ</b>

He has not arrived yet, so this is a prediction about an unconfirmed event, and
predictions take `はずだ`. Once he does turn up late, you could then say
<code>{f|遅|おく}れて{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> about it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "It was his first time cooking, so of course it burned."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|初|はじ}めての{f|料理|りょうり}だったのだから、{f|焦|こ}げ<b>て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>。

The premise goes in front with のだから, and the て-form of
<code>{f|焦|こ}げる</code> carries the verdict.
<code>{f|焦|こ}げたのも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> is equally correct and a shade more
explanatory in tone.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: あんな{f|練習|れんしゅう}をしていたら、けがをする{f|当然|とうぜん}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ あんな{f|練習|れんしゅう}をしていたら、けがをし<b>て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>。

<code>{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code> needs a て-form in front of it, never a dictionary
form. Nominalising also works: <code>けがをするのも{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|休|やす}みが{f|取|と}れなく<b>て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>。
(b) {f|休|やす}みが{f|取|と}れ<b>て{f|当然|とうぜん}だ</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) accepts the situation: given the workload or the season, nobody could have
expected leave to be granted. It is resigned, and often sympathetic.

(b) claims an entitlement: time off is something the speaker is owed, and being
refused it is unfair. Because the judgement is about the speaker's own advantage,
(b) easily sounds demanding - it is a sentence for arguing a point, not for small
talk.

</details>
