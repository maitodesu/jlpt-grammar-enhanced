# ～から言うと

**Meaning** · in terms of ~; judged on the scale of ~ - names the yardstick the speaker is measuring by

Read <code>から{f|言|い}うと</code>. The noun in front of it is a **criterion**:
price, safety, experience, the rules, the current state of things. The sentence
then delivers a verdict measured on exactly that scale, and says nothing about the
others. {f|値段|ねだん}から{f|言|い}えば{f|得|とく}だ concedes, without saying so,
that on some other scale it might not be.

## Formation

Three endings, all built on the same core.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (criterion) | Noun + から{f|言|い}うと | {f|値段|ねだん} → {f|値段|ねだん}から{f|言|い}うと |
| Same, conditional shade | Noun + から{f|言|い}えば | {f|経験|けいけん} → {f|経験|けいけん}から{f|言|い}えば |
| Same, more assertive | Noun + から{f|言|い}って | {f|常識|じょうしき} → {f|常識|じょうしき}から{f|言|い}って |
| Aspect spelled out | Noun + の{f|面|めん} + から{f|言|い}えば | {f|栄養|えいよう} → {f|栄養|えいよう}の{f|面|めん}から{f|言|い}えば |
| Fixed discourse opener | {f|結論|けつろん} + から{f|言|い}うと | - → {f|結論|けつろん}から{f|言|い}うと、… |
| Politeness | carried by the main clause | … → …から{f|言|い}うと{f|難|むずか}しいです |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the noun must be a measure or a body of information, not a person whose
opinion you are quoting. To say "if you ask me", Japanese uses
{f|私|わたし}に{f|言|い}わせれば or {f|私|わたし}としては.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|結論|けつろん}<b>から{f|言|い}うと</b>、この{f|案|あん}は{f|採用|さいよう}されなかった。
To give you the conclusion first: the proposal wasn't adopted.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}<b>から{f|言|い}えば</b>、こちらのほうがずっと{f|得|とく}だ。
In terms of price, this one is far better value.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|経験|けいけん}<b>から{f|言|い}うと</b>、{f|最初|さいしょ}の{f|三日|みっか}が{f|一番|いちばん}つらい。
Speaking from experience, the first three days are the worst.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|安全|あんぜん}{f|面|めん}<b>から{f|言|い}って</b>、この{f|設計|せっけい}は{f|認|みと}められない。
On safety grounds, this design can't be approved.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|栄養|えいよう}の{f|面|めん}<b>から{f|言|い}えば</b>、{f|朝|あさ}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|抜|ぬ}くのはよくない。
Nutritionally speaking, skipping breakfast is a bad idea.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|現時点|げんじてん}の{f|売|う}り{f|上|あ}げ<b>から{f|言|い}うと</b>、{f|目標|もくひょう}の{f|達成|たっせい}は{f|難|むずか}しそうだ。
Going by the sales figures as they stand, hitting the target looks unlikely.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|私|わたし}の{f|立場|たちば}<b>から{f|言|い}えば</b>、どちらの{f|味方|みかた}もできないのです。
Given the position I'm in, I can't take either side.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|過去|かこ}の{f|統計|とうけい}<b>から{f|言|い}って</b>、この{f|時期|じき}に{f|台風|たいふう}が{f|来|く}ることはめったにない。
On the historical figures, typhoons hardly ever arrive at this time of year.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a **written and formal-spoken** pattern. It belongs to reports, meetings,
editorials and any conversation where someone is being careful. In relaxed speech
you are far more likely to hear {f|値段|ねだん}で{f|言|い}うと or just
{f|値段|ねだん}なら.

The three endings shade differently:

- <code>から{f|言|い}うと</code> is the plain, neutral member.
- <code>から{f|言|い}えば</code> adds the `ば` conditional's "supposing we take
  that as the measure", which makes it slightly softer and more concessive.
- <code>から{f|言|い}って</code> is the most assertive. It very often precedes a
  refusal or a negative verdict - {f|常識|じょうしき}から{f|言|い}って
  {f|失礼|しつれい}だ - and it can sound blunt.

<code>{f|結論|けつろん}から{f|言|い}うと</code> deserves separate mention: it is a
set discourse opener meaning "cutting to the answer", and it is extremely useful.
{f|正直|しょうじき}に{f|言|い}うと ("honestly") looks similar but is a different
construction entirely - there the adverb modifies the *manner* of speaking, not
the scale of the judgement.

Because it names a scale, this pattern is naturally paired with contrast: one
sentence gives the verdict on price, the next on quality. If you use it and then
never mention the other scales, a Japanese listener may still hear the reservation.

## Contrast

### から{f|言|い}うと vs から{f|見|み}ると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>から{f|言|い}うと</code> names a **scale** you are
measuring on. <code>から{f|見|み}ると</code> names a **vantage point** you are
looking from.

</div>

<pre>
{f|広|ひろ}さ<b>から{f|言|い}うと</b>この{f|部屋|へや}で{f|十分|じゅうぶん}だ。
In terms of floor space this room is plenty.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|客|きゃく}<b>から{f|見|み}ると</b>この{f|部屋|へや}は{f|狭|せま}く{f|感|かん}じるだろう。
To a customer this room will feel cramped.
</pre>

The second sentence has a pair of eyes in it; the first has a tape measure. That
is why a person noun goes readily with <code>から{f|見|み}ると</code> and awkwardly
with <code>から{f|言|い}うと</code>.

### から{f|言|い}うと vs としては

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>から{f|言|い}うと</code> gives the **measure**;
<code>としては</code> gives the **capacity you are speaking in**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|規則|きそく}<b>から{f|言|い}うと</b>、それは{f|認|みと}められません。
By the rules, that isn't permitted.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|担当者|たんとうしゃ}<b>としては</b>、それは{f|認|みと}められません。
Speaking as the person in charge, I can't permit that.
</pre>

Same refusal, different authority behind it. The first hides the speaker behind a
standard; the second puts them in front of it, wearing a role. In a difficult
conversation that difference is the whole message.

### から{f|言|い}うと vs からすると

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>から{f|言|い}うと</code> **evaluates on a stated scale**;
<code>からすると</code> **infers from evidence**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|実力|じつりょく}<b>から{f|言|い}うと</b>、{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}して{f|当然|とうぜん}のチームだ。
On ability alone, this is a team that ought to win.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昨日|きのう}の{f|試合|しあい}<b>からすると</b>、{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}は{f|難|むずか}しそうだ。
Judging from yesterday's match, the title looks out of reach.
</pre>

The first weighs the team against a yardstick called "ability". The second reasons
forward from something that happened. Where the noun is straightforwardly
evidence, either can appear and the difference thins out; where the noun is a
criterion - {f|規則|きそく}, {f|値段|ねだん}, {f|安全|あんぜん} - only
<code>から{f|言|い}うと</code> is natural.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}<b>から{f|言|い}うと</b>、その{f|意見|いけん}には{f|賛成|さんせい}できない。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}<b>としては</b>、その{f|意見|いけん}には{f|賛成|さんせい}できない。（or {f|私|わたし}に{f|言|い}わせれば）
  A bare person is not a measuring scale. Add one - {f|私|わたし}の
  {f|経験|けいけん}から{f|言|い}うと - or switch pattern.

- ❌ {f|高|たか}い<b>から{f|言|い}うと</b>、{f|買|か}わないほうがいい。 → ✅ {f|値段|ねだん}<b>から{f|言|い}うと</b>、{f|買|か}わないほうがいい。
  Only a noun goes in front of it. An adjective clause cannot.

- ❌ {f|安全|あんぜん}{f|面|めん}<b>から{f|言|い}って</b>、{f|窓|まど}を{f|閉|し}めろ。 → ✅ {f|安全|あんぜん}{f|面|めん}<b>から{f|言|い}って</b>、{f|窓|まど}は{f|閉|し}めるべきだ。
  The main clause is a judgement made on the stated scale, not a shouted order.

- ❌ {f|結論|けつろん}から<b>{f|話|はな}すと</b>、この{f|案|あん}は{f|通|とお}りました。 → ✅ {f|結論|けつろん}から<b>{f|言|い}うと</b>、この{f|案|あん}は{f|通|とお}りました。
  The opener is fixed with {f|言|い}う. Substituting {f|話|はな}す loses the set
  phrase.

## Practice

**1.** {f|効率|こうりつ}____、この{f|方法|ほうほう}は{f|時間|じかん}がかかりすぎる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|効率|こうりつ}<b>から{f|言|い}うと</b>、この{f|方法|ほうほう}は{f|時間|じかん}がかかりすぎる。

Efficiency is the scale the method is being measured against, which is the core use
of the pattern. The sentence leaves open that on some other scale - accuracy, say -
the method might be fine.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|部下|ぶか}（から{f|言|い}うと・からすれば）、{f|急|きゅう}な{f|残業|ざんぎょう}は{f|迷惑|めいわく}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|部下|ぶか}<b>からすれば</b>、{f|急|きゅう}な{f|残業|ざんぎょう}は{f|迷惑|めいわく}だ。

{f|部下|ぶか} is a person whose position we are taking, not a measuring scale.
<code>から{f|言|い}うと</code> would need something like
{f|部下|ぶか}の{f|立場|たちば}から{f|言|い}うと to work.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Legally speaking, there's no problem, but I'm not sure it's the right thing to do."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|法律|ほうりつ}<b>から{f|言|い}えば</b>{f|問題|もんだい}はないが、それが{f|正|ただ}しいことなのかはわからない。

The English "legally speaking" is exactly this pattern's job: it fences the verdict
off to one scale, and the が clause then admits that another scale gives a different
answer.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: この{f|辺|へん}は{f|交通|こうつう}が{f|不便|ふべん}だから{f|言|い}うと、{f|家賃|やちん}が{f|安|やす}い。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ この{f|辺|へん}は{f|交通|こうつう}の{f|便|べん}<b>から{f|言|い}うと</b>{f|不便|ふべん}だが、{f|家賃|やちん}は{f|安|やす}い。

The original glues the pattern onto a `だから` clause. The pattern needs a bare
noun, so the criterion - {f|交通|こうつう}の{f|便|べん} - has to be pulled out and
the verdict put in the main clause.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|予算|よさん}<b>から{f|言|い}うと</b>{f|無理|むり}だ。
(b) この{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|予算|よさん}<b>から{f|言|い}って</b>{f|無理|むり}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both measure the plan against the budget and find it unworkable, so the propositions
are the same.

(b) is firmer. <code>から{f|言|い}って</code> presents the budget as settling the
matter, and often closes a discussion; in a meeting it can land as a flat refusal.
(a) is the neutral report of a verdict, and leaves more room for someone to answer
"but on other grounds...".

</details>
