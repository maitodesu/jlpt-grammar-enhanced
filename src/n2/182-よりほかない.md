# ～よりほかない

**Meaning** · there is nothing for it but to do this - every other road has been looked at and closed

Literally "there is nothing other than". The speaker is not choosing; they are
reporting the end of a search. `よりほかない` carries a note of resignation and a
faintly formal, written texture, which is what separates it from the everyday
`しかない`.

## Formation

The verb goes in the **dictionary form**. `ほか` is a noun ({f|他|ほか}), so the
particles that cluster around it - は, に, も - can all appear, with only a small
change in weight.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + よりほかない | {f|待|ま}つ → {f|待|ま}つよりほかない |
| Verb, with は | ～よりほかはない | {f|待|ま}つよりほかはない |
| Verb, with に | ～よりほかにない | {f|待|ま}つよりほかにない |
| Noun | Noun + よりほかない | {f|方法|ほうほう}はこれよりほかない |
| Polite | ～よりほかありません | {f|謝|あやま}るよりほかありません |
| Past | ～よりほかなかった | {f|断|ことわ}るよりほかなかった |
| Longer variant | ～よりほか{f|仕方|しかた}がない | {f|待|ま}つよりほか{f|仕方|しかた}がない |
| Short variant | ～ほかない ／ ～ほかはない | {f|待|ま}つほかない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - `より` here is not the `より` of comparison. It is the older "other
than", the same one you meet in {f|駅|えき}より{f|徒歩|とほ}{f|五分|ごふん} on an
estate agent's advertisement. Nothing is being compared;
<code>より</code> is marking what gets excluded.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|今|いま}は{f|待|ま}つ<b>よりほかない</b>。
For now there is nothing to do but wait.
</pre>

<pre>
こうなっては、{f|謝|あやま}る<b>よりほかありません</b>。
Now that it has come to this, all I can do is apologise.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|地図|ちず}もスマホもないので、{f|人|ひと}に{f|聞|き}く<b>よりほかない</b>。
I have neither a map nor my phone, so there is nothing for it but to ask someone.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|材料|ざいりょう}が{f|足|た}りないなら、{f|明日|あした}{f|買|か}い{f|足|た}す<b>よりほかはない</b>。
If we are short of ingredients, the only course left is to buy more tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事故|じこ}の{f|原因|げんいん}については、{f|調査|ちょうさ}の{f|結果|けっか}を{f|待|ま}つ<b>よりほかにない</b>。
As for the cause of the accident, we can do nothing but wait for the findings.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|抱|かか}えきれない{f|量|りょう}なら、{f|誰|だれ}かに{f|頼|たの}む<b>よりほかない</b>でしょう。
If it is more than one person can carry, there is really no option but to ask for help.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|息子|むすこ}が{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|決|き}めたことですから、{f|親|おや}としては{f|見守|みまも}る<b>よりほかありません</b>。
My son decided it for himself, so as his parent I can only stand by and watch.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|資金|しきん}が{f|尽|つ}きた{f|今|いま}、{f|事業|じぎょう}を{f|縮小|しゅくしょう}する<b>よりほかない</b>と{f|社長|しゃちょう}は{f|語|かた}った。
With the funds exhausted, the president said there was no alternative but to scale the business back.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`よりほかない` sits a step above ordinary conversation. You will meet it in
narration, in written accounts, in speeches and in the careful speech of an adult
explaining a difficult situation. It is not stiff in the way that
<code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> is - a colleague could say it to you - but nobody
uses it to decide where to have lunch.

What it always carries is **resignation**. The other roads were real, and they are
gone. A sentence with `よりほかない` almost always comes after a statement of what
went wrong: the train stopped, the money ran out, the letter never came. Without
that, it sounds like a shrug with nothing behind it.

The variants are close in meaning. `ほかはない` slightly emphasises the exclusion
("nothing *else* at all"), `ほかにない` puts the weight on the absence of any other
item, and bare `ほかない` - with `より` dropped - is the lightest and now the most
common in speech. The longest member,
<code>よりほか{f|仕方|しかた}がない</code>, adds a distinctly weary note; it is the
sentence of someone who has given up arguing.

Politeness works normally: <code>よりほかありません</code> in ます-speech,
<code>よりほかなかった</code> in the past. Avoid ❌<code>よりほかないです</code> in
writing - it mixes a literary frame with a casual ending, and
<code>ありません</code> is the settled polite form.

## Contrast

### ～よりほかない vs ～しかない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same meaning at two registers. `しかない` is what you say;
`よりほかない` is closer to what you write, and it sounds more resigned.

</div>

<pre>
{f|終|お}わってないなら、{f|徹夜|てつや}する<b>しかない</b>よ。
If it isn't finished, we'll just have to pull an all-nighter.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|納期|のうき}が{f|動|うご}かせない{f|以上|いじょう}、{f|徹夜|てつや}する<b>よりほかありません</b>。
Since the delivery date cannot be moved, there is nothing for it but to work through the night.
</pre>

There is one real difference under the register. `しかない` can be cheerful -
<code>こうなったら{f|楽|たの}しむしかない</code>, "we may as well enjoy it" - and
it can simply count things: <code>{f|席|せき}は{f|二|ふた}つしかない</code>.
`よりほかない` does neither. It reports a dead end, and it does not count.

### ～よりほかない vs ～ざるを{f|得|え}ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `よりほかない` says **no other option exists**.
<code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> says **I am being forced**, and puts the speaker's
unwillingness at the centre.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|時間|じかん}ではタクシーを{f|使|つか}う<b>よりほかない</b>。
At this hour there's no way of getting there but by taxi.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|上|うえ}からの{f|指示|しじ}なので、{f|方針|ほうしん}を{f|変|か}えざるを{f|得|え}ない。
It's an instruction from above, so we have no choice but to change course.
</pre>

The first is an observation about the world at one in the morning; the speaker may
not mind at all. The second is about pressure applied to the speaker, and it always
implies they would rather not. Notice too that `よりほかない` takes the dictionary
form and <code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> takes the ない-stem - a quick way to tell
them apart on the page.

### ～よりほかない vs ～ようがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ようがない` says there is **no method at all**; the action
cannot be performed. `よりほかない` says the method exists and it is the only one
left.

</div>

<pre>
{f|住所|じゅうしょ}も{f|電話番号|でんわばんごう}も{f|知|し}らないので、{f|連絡|れんらく}の<b>しようがない</b>。
I have neither an address nor a phone number, so there is no way to get in touch.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|住所|じゅうしょ}しか{f|分|わ}からないので、{f|手紙|てがみ}を{f|書|か}く<b>よりほかない</b>。
All I have is an address, so writing a letter is the only thing left to me.
</pre>

`ようがない` empties the shelf; `よりほかない` leaves exactly one item on it. That
is why they sound so different in practice: the first is helplessness, the second
is a decision the speaker did not want to make.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|待|ま}ち<b>よりほかない</b>。 → ✅ {f|待|ま}つ<b>よりほかない</b>。
  The verb takes its dictionary form. The ます-stem belongs to suffixes such as
  <code>かねる</code>, not to this one.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}なら、{f|中止|ちゅうし}<b>よりほかない</b>。 → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}なら、{f|中止|ちゅうし}する<b>よりほかない</b>。
  When the remaining option is an action, the verb has to appear. A bare
  する-noun leaves the sentence with nothing to do.

- ❌ {f|謝|あやま}るよりほか<b>ないでした</b>。 → ✅ {f|謝|あやま}るよりほか<b>ありませんでした</b>。
  <code>ない</code> is a real い-adjective ending here, so its polite past is
  <code>ありませんでした</code>.

- ❌ {f|合格|ごうかく}したので、{f|喜|よろこ}ぶ<b>よりほかない</b>。 → ✅ {f|合格|ごうかく}したので、{f|喜|よろこ}ばずにはいられない。
  The pattern reports a dead end, not an overflow of feeling. Applied to good
  news it sounds as though celebrating were a chore.

## Practice

**1.** バスも{f|電車|でんしゃ}も{f|止|と}まってしまった。ここから{f|歩|ある}いて{f|帰|かえ}る____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|歩|ある}いて{f|帰|かえ}る<b>よりほかない</b>（or よりほかありません・ほかない）

Both forms of transport are gone, so the sentence is reporting the last remaining
option - exactly what this pattern is for. The verb stays in the dictionary form.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|連絡先|れんらくさき}を{f|一|ひと}つも{f|知|し}らないので、{f|知|し}らせ（るよりほかない・ようがない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|知|し}らせ<b>ようがない</b>。

With no contact details at all there is no method left, and `ようがない` is the
pattern for a total absence of means. `よりほかない` would imply that one way of
telling them still exists.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The shop was already shut, so we had no choice but to give up and go home."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|店|みせ}はもう{f|閉|し}まっていたので、あきらめて{f|帰|かえ}る<b>よりほかなかった</b>。

The past is formed on <code>ない</code> as an い-adjective:
<code>よりほかなかった</code>. Note {f|閉|し}まる, the intransitive "to be shut" -
the shop closed on its own, so this is not {f|閉|し}める.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部品|ぶひん}が{f|届|とど}かないなら、{f|作業|さぎょう}を{f|止|と}めるよりしかない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部品|ぶひん}が{f|届|とど}かないなら、{f|作業|さぎょう}を{f|止|と}める<b>よりほかない</b>。（or {f|止|と}める<b>しかない</b>）
"If the parts aren't coming, there's nothing to do but stop work."

<code>より</code> belongs with <code>ほか</code>, and <code>しか</code> stands on
its own. Mixing the two produces a form that exists in neither pattern.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|今回|こんかい}は{f|見送|みおく}る<b>しかないね</b>。
(b) {f|今回|こんかい}は{f|見送|みおく}る<b>よりほかありません</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is conversational and light. The `ね` invites agreement, and the sentence could
be said between colleagues who are mildly disappointed and already moving on.

(b) is the written or formal version of the same conclusion. It sounds as though
the speaker has weighed the alternatives and is now recording the outcome - the
sentence of an announcement or a report rather than a chat, and noticeably more
resigned in tone.

</details>
