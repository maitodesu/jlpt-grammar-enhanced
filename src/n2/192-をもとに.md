# をもとに

**Meaning** · from; on the basis of; taking X as the starting material - X is what you worked *from*, and the result is a new thing

The noun inside is <code>{f|元|もと}</code> / <code>{f|基|もと}</code>, "source,
origin, base". `をもとに` names the **material**: a folk tale, an interview, a set
of figures, a sketch, somebody's advice. Out of that material something new is
built, and the new thing is free to add, cut and rearrange. That freedom is the
whole difference between this pattern and
<code>～に{f|基|もと}づいて</code>, which treats its noun as an authority you may not
depart from.

## Formation

The base is marked with `を`. Almost every sentence ends in a verb of *making* -
{f|作|つく}る, {f|書|か}く, {f|描|か}く, {f|考|かんが}える,
{f|開発|かいはつ}する, {f|編集|へんしゅう}する.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + をもとに | {f|昔話|むかしばなし} → {f|昔話|むかしばなし}をもとに |
| Noun (fuller, more verbal) | Noun + をもとにして | データ → データをもとにして |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + をもとにした + Noun | {f|実話|じつわ} → {f|実話|じつわ}をもとにした{f|物語|ものがたり} |
| Nominalised clause | ～という + Noun + をもとに | {f|噂|うわさ} → {f|事故|じこ}があったという{f|噂|うわさ}をもとに |
| Written in kanji (optional) | Noun + を{f|基|もと}に / を{f|元|もと}に | {f|資料|しりょう} → {f|資料|しりょう}を{f|基|もと}に |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the spelling. <code>{f|基|もと}</code> is the "foundation" kanji and
<code>{f|元|もと}</code> the "origin" one, and writers disagree about which fits here.
Modern practice is simply to write it in kana, which sidesteps the choice. Do that
and you will never be wrong.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|歌|うた}は{f|古|ふる}い{f|昔話|むかしばなし}<b>をもとに</b>{f|作|つく}られた。
This song was made from an old folk tale.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|写真|しゃしん}<b>をもとに</b>{f|絵|え}を{f|描|か}きます。
I paint from photographs.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|生徒|せいと}の{f|意見|いけん}<b>をもとにして</b>、{f|文化祭|ぶんかさい}の{f|企画|きかく}を{f|練|ね}った。
We worked the school-festival plan up out of what the students said.
</pre>

<pre>

Apparently a drama based on a true story will be broadcast next spring.
</pre>

<pre>

We set this year's purchasing volume from last year's sales figures.
</pre>

<pre>

Once I rewrote my CV on the strength of a senior colleague's advice, I started getting called to interviews.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|聞|き}き{f|取|と}り{f|調査|ちょうさ}で{f|集|あつ}めた{f|証言|しょうげん}<b>をもとに</b>、{f|当時|とうじ}の{f|村|むら}の{f|様子|ようす}を{f|再現|さいげん}した{f|地図|ちず}が{f|作|つく}られた。
From testimony gathered in interviews, a map was drawn up recreating the village as it stood at the time.
</pre>

<pre>

This dictionary was compiled over thirty years from a Meiji-era manuscript.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Formal and written, but a notch friendlier than
<code>に{f|基|もと}づいて</code>. It is entirely at home in a news report, a
presentation, a school newsletter or a piece of careful speech; what it is not is
chatty. In relaxed conversation people say
<code>～を{f|見|み}て</code> or <code>～から{f|考|かんが}えて</code> and leave `もとに`
for when they are being deliberate.

The three shapes divide up cleanly. `をもとに` is the default. `をもとにして` is the
same thing with the verb spelled out; it feels a touch more explicit about the
process and sits comfortably at the head of a long sentence. `をもとにした` is the
only one that can attach to a following noun - there is no
<code>{f|実話|じつわ}をもとに{f|映画|えいが}</code> without the `した`.

The base has to be **information or stuff**: a text, a recording, data, a memory,
an object. A person cannot be the base of this pattern. What you worked from is
their advice, their notes or their testimony, and Japanese makes you say so.

## Contrast

### をもとに vs ～に{f|基|もと}づいて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `をもとに` = **material**, and you may transform it.
<code>に{f|基|もと}づいて</code> = **authority**, and you may not depart from it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|漫画|まんが}<b>をもとに</b>{f|脚本|きゃくほん}を{f|書|か}いた。
I wrote the screenplay from the manga.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|契約書|けいやくしょ}<b>に{f|基|もと}づいて</b>{f|支払|しはら}いが{f|行|おこな}われる。
Payment is made in accordance with the contract.
</pre>

A screenplay that could not depart from the manga would be a transcription, and a
payment that treated the contract as a rough draft would be a breach. Each pattern
is doing the job the other cannot.

Both do appear with creative work, and there the choice is a claim about content.
<code>{f|小説|しょうせつ}をもとにした{f|映画|えいが}</code> says where the story came
from. <code>{f|事実|じじつ}に{f|基|もと}づいた{f|映画|えいが}</code> says the events
really happened. A studio that wants both will say both, in that order.

### をもとに vs を{f|参考|さんこう}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `をもとに`: X supplies the **substance** of the result.
<code>を{f|参考|さんこう}に</code>: X was merely **consulted**, and the result may owe
it very little.

</div>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|資料|しりょう}<b>をもとに</b>{f|今年|ことし}の{f|案|あん}を{f|作|つく}った。
I built this year's proposal out of last year's documents.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|去年|きょねん}の{f|資料|しりょう}を<b>{f|参考|さんこう}に</b>{f|今年|ことし}の{f|案|あん}を{f|作|つく}った。
I put this year's proposal together with last year's documents for reference.
</pre>

The first admits that most of the content is recycled. The second says you had a
look. That makes <code>を{f|参考|さんこう}に</code> the modest option, and it is
routinely chosen for exactly that reason - it is also what you say when you do not
want to claim someone else's work as your source.

### をもとに vs ～によって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `によって` names the **means** something was done by, or the
**agent** who did it. `をもとに` names the **source material** it was made out of.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|像|ぞう}は{f|有名|ゆうめい}な{f|彫刻家|ちょうこくか}<b>によって</b>{f|作|つく}られた。
This statue was made by a famous sculptor.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|像|ぞう}は{f|古|ふる}い{f|写真|しゃしん}{f|数枚|すうまい}<b>をもとに</b>{f|作|つく}られた。
This statue was made from a handful of old photographs.
</pre>

Both sentences end in <code>{f|作|つく}られた</code>, and English hides the
difference under one word, "by". `によって` has four jobs - cause, means, passive
agent, and the thing a result varies with - and *source material* is not among
them. That gap is precisely what `をもとに` fills, which is why the two so often
appear in the same sentence without competing.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|事実|じじつ}<b>にもとに</b>した{f|話|はなし} → ✅ {f|事実|じじつ}<b>をもとに</b>した{f|話|はなし}
  The base takes `を`. The `に` leaks in from <code>に{f|基|もと}づいて</code>, and the
  two patterns swap particles in learners' heads constantly.

- ❌ {f|実話|じつわ}<b>をもとに</b>{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}た。 → ✅ {f|実話|じつわ}<b>をもとにした</b>{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}た。
  To modify a noun you need the `した`. Without it the phrase tries to attach to the
  verb at the end, and the sentence says you watched the film *using a true story*.

- ❌ {f|聞|き}いた<b>をもとに</b>{f|記事|きじ}を{f|書|か}いた。 → ✅ {f|聞|き}いた{f|話|はなし}<b>をもとに</b>{f|記事|きじ}を{f|書|か}いた。
  A noun has to be there. A bare verb cannot attach, so supply the noun the clause
  was describing - {f|話|はなし}, {f|内容|ないよう}, {f|証言|しょうげん}.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}<b>をもとに</b>{f|作文|さくぶん}を{f|直|なお}した。 → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}のアドバイス<b>をもとに</b>{f|作文|さくぶん}を{f|直|なお}した。
  People are not material. Name the thing they gave you - the advice, the comments,
  the corrections - and the sentence works.

## Practice

**1.** {f|町|まち}に{f|伝|つた}わる{f|昔話|むかしばなし}____、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|絵本|えほん}を{f|作|つく}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昔話|むかしばなし}<b>をもとに</b>、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|絵本|えほん}を{f|作|つく}った。

A folk tale is material, and the picture book is a new thing built out of it - the
retelling is expected to change things. Note the particle `を`.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|校則|こうそく}（をもとに・に{f|基|もと}づいて）{f|処分|しょぶん}が{f|決|き}まった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|校則|こうそく}<b>に{f|基|もと}づいて</b>{f|処分|しょぶん}が{f|決|き}まった。

School rules are an authority the decision has to answer to, not raw material a
punishment is fashioned from. `をもとに` would suggest the school treated its own
rules as a rough draft.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "This play was written from a diary a soldier left behind."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

この{f|劇|げき}は、{f|兵士|へいし}が{f|残|のこ}した{f|日記|にっき}<b>をもとに</b>{f|書|か}かれました。

The diary is the source the playwright worked from, and a play necessarily invents
around it - textbook `をもとに`. The whole relative clause counts as a noun, so it
attaches with no extra machinery.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|実話|じつわ}をもとに{f|映画|えいが}が{f|話題|わだい}になっている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|実話|じつわ}<b>をもとにした</b>{f|映画|えいが}が{f|話題|わだい}になっている。

`をもとに` is adverbial - it modifies a verb. Here it is meant to modify the noun
<code>{f|映画|えいが}</code>, and that requires the `した` form.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|先輩|せんぱい}の{f|意見|いけん}をもとに{f|企画|きかく}を{f|立|た}てた。
(b) {f|先輩|せんぱい}の{f|意見|いけん}を{f|参考|さんこう}に{f|企画|きかく}を{f|立|た}てた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the senior colleague's opinion is what the plan is made of - the shape of
the proposal comes from them, and you are giving them credit for it.

(b) says you took their opinion into account. The plan is yours; theirs was one
input among several. This is the more modest phrasing, and the one you would use if
you did not in fact follow much of their advice.

</details>
