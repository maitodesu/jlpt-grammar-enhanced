# ～と考えられる

**Meaning** · it can be concluded that ～; it is reasonable to think that ～ - a conclusion stated with the person who reached it removed from the sentence

<code>{f|考|かんが}えられる</code> is {f|考|かんが}える with the -られる ending, and here it
carries the *impersonal* sense: not "I think", but "one can think". The pattern is how
Japanese states a judgement in a report or a paper. It sits a rung below
<code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> - the writer is not claiming certainty, only that the
evidence supports this reading - and it earns its authority precisely by not sounding
like a personal opinion.

## Formation

The clause in front is a **quotation**, so it takes the plain form and the `と` of quoted
speech. That means nouns and な-adjectives keep their `だ` - the opposite of what
<code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code> does.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + と{f|考|かんが}えられる | {f|増|ふ}える → {f|増|ふ}えると{f|考|かんが}えられる |
| Verb (past) | plain past + と{f|考|かんが}えられる | {f|起|お}きた → {f|起|お}きたと{f|考|かんが}えられる |
| い-adjective | plain form + と{f|考|かんが}えられる | {f|少|すく}ない → {f|少|すく}ないと{f|考|かんが}えられる |
| な-adjective | keep だ + と{f|考|かんが}えられる | {f|安全|あんぜん}だ → {f|安全|あんぜん}だと{f|考|かんが}えられる |
| Noun | keep だ + と{f|考|かんが}えられる | {f|原因|げんいん} → {f|原因|げんいん}だと{f|考|かんが}えられる |
| Polite | … + と{f|考|かんが}えられます | {f|難|むずか}しい → {f|難|むずか}しいと{f|考|かんが}えられます |
| Established view | … + と{f|考|かんが}えられている | {f|有効|ゆうこう}だ → {f|有効|ゆうこう}だと{f|考|かんが}えられている |
| Naming the conclusion | … + と{f|考|かんが}えられる + Noun | {f|要因|よういん} → {f|要因|よういん}だと{f|考|かんが}えられる{f|点|てん} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the same form also does duty as a straight potential: "such a thing is
conceivable". <code>その{f|可能性|かのうせい}も{f|考|かんが}えられる</code> means "that
possibility can also be entertained". Context separates the two easily, because the
conclusion use always has a quoted clause and `と` in front of it.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|結果|けっか}は{f|偶然|ぐうぜん}だ<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</b>。
This result can be put down to chance.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事故|じこ}の{f|原因|げんいん}は{f|整備|せいび}{f|不足|ぶそく}だ<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられます</b>。
The cause of the accident is believed to be poor maintenance.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|気温|きおん}の{f|上昇|じょうしょう}が{f|今年|ことし}の{f|収穫|しゅうかく}に{f|影響|えいきょう}した<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</b>。
The rise in temperature is thought to have affected this year's harvest.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|数字|すうじ}だけで{f|結論|けつろん}を{f|出|だ}すのは{f|難|むずか}しい<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられます</b>。
It would seem unwise to draw a conclusion from this figure alone.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|実験|じっけん}の{f|条件|じょうけん}が{f|十分|じゅうぶん}に{f|管理|かんり}されていなかった<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</b>。
The experimental conditions appear not to have been adequately controlled.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|若者|わかもの}の{f|車|くるま}{f|離|ばな}れの{f|背景|はいけい}には{f|都市部|としぶ}の{f|交通|こうつう}{f|事情|じじょう}がある<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</b>。
Behind young people's turn away from cars, one can see the transport situation in the cities.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|以上|いじょう}のことから、この{f|地域|ちいき}の{f|人口|じんこう}{f|減少|げんしょう}はしばらく{f|続|つづ}く<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられます</b>。
From all of the above, the decline in population in this region looks set to continue for some time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|不具合|ふぐあい}は、{f|複数|ふくすう}の{f|要因|よういん}が{f|重|かさ}なって{f|生|しょう}じたもの<b>だと{f|考|かんが}えられている</b>。
The current fault is held to have arisen from several factors coinciding.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Squarely **written and formal**: research papers, company reports, news analysis, expert
commentary, the discussion section of a lab write-up. The polite
<code>と{f|考|かんが}えられます</code> is also standard in presentations and in careful
business speech, where it makes a claim sound examined rather than felt.

The pattern's whole trick is *depersonalisation*. Japanese academic prose avoids
<code>{f|私|わたし}は … と{f|思|おも}う</code> for the same reason English academic prose
avoids "I reckon": the reader is being asked to accept the reasoning, not the writer.
Because the thinker has been deleted, though, you cannot put yourself back in.
<code>{f|私|わたし}はそう{f|考|かんが}えられます</code> is not a polite version of "I
think"; it reads as "I am capable of thinking that", which is not what anybody means.

**In conversation, say <code>と{f|思|おも}います</code>.** A friend asking whether the
restaurant is any good does not want a conclusion from the literature. Keep
<code>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</code> for the moments when you genuinely are summarising
evidence - a meeting, a written explanation, an exam essay.

One small caution about hedging. The pattern is already cautious, so piling more on top
of it - <code>と{f|考|かんが}えられると{f|思|おも}います</code> - reads as nervous rather
than careful. One hedge is enough.

## Contrast

### と{f|考|かんが}えられる vs と{f|思|おも}う

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>と{f|思|おも}う</code> names an opinion and its owner.
<code>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</code> presents a conclusion and hides its owner.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|案|あん}はうまくいく<b>と{f|思|おも}います</b>。
I think this plan will work.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|案|あん}は{f|一定|いってい}の{f|効果|こうか}をもたらす<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられます</b>。
This plan can be expected to produce a measurable effect.
</pre>

The first invites you to weigh the speaker; the second invites you to weigh the evidence.
That is also why the second is harder to argue with politely - and why overusing it in
conversation makes a person sound as though they are issuing findings rather than
chatting.

### と{f|考|かんが}えられる vs と{f|考|かんが}えられている

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - without ている, **the writer** is drawing the conclusion here and now.
With ている, it is **the accepted view** in the field, and the writer is reporting it.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|習慣|しゅうかん}は{f|江戸|えど}{f|時代|じだい}に{f|始|はじ}まった<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられている</b>。
This custom is generally held to have begun in the Edo period.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|今回|こんかい}の{f|調査|ちょうさ}から、その{f|説|せつ}には{f|無理|むり}がある<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</b>。
The present survey suggests that this theory is untenable.
</pre>

The ている form is close to <code>とされている</code> and
<code>と{f|言|い}われている</code>: all three hand the opinion to the scholarly community.
Use it for background, and drop the ている at the moment you start making your own case -
that switch is one of the clearest signals in a Japanese paper that the argument has
begun.

### と{f|考|かんが}えられる vs と{f|言|い}える

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>と{f|言|い}える</code> for what the data **shows**;
<code>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</code> for the interpretation you **build on top of** the
data.

</div>

<pre>
{f|数字|すうじ}を{f|見|み}る{f|限|かぎ}り、{f|効果|こうか}は{f|明|あき}らかだ<b>と{f|言|い}える</b>。
As far as the figures go, the effect can be said to be clear.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|効果|こうか}は{f|価格|かかく}の{f|変化|へんか}によるもの<b>だと{f|考|かんが}えられる</b>。
That effect is likely attributable to the change in price.
</pre>

The first is a claim you could defend by pointing at a table. The second goes beyond the
table to say *why*, and is therefore the weaker, more revisable statement of the two -
even though the grammar looks equally confident.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|重要|じゅうよう}<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</b>。 → ✅ この{f|問題|もんだい}は{f|重要|じゅうよう}<b>だと{f|考|かんが}えられる</b>。
  `と` is the quoting particle, so what precedes it must be a complete plain-form
  sentence. な-adjectives and nouns keep their `だ` here - the exact opposite of
  <code>に{f|違|ちが}いない</code>.

- ❌ {f|私|わたし}はこの{f|方法|ほうほう}がいい<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられます</b>。 → ✅ {f|私|わたし}はこの{f|方法|ほうほう}がいい<b>と{f|考|かんが}えています</b>。
  Putting {f|私|わたし}は back into a sentence whose whole purpose is to remove the thinker
  produces a potential reading: "I am able to think so." For a stated personal position,
  use <code>{f|考|かんが}えています</code>.

- ❌ この{f|店|みせ}のラーメンはおいしい<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられるよ</b>。 → ✅ この{f|店|みせ}のラーメンはおいしい<b>と{f|思|おも}うよ</b>。
  Grammatical, and comically over-dressed. Taste is an opinion, and a friend wants your
  opinion, not your findings.

- ❌ {f|原因|げんいん}は{f|人手|ひとで}{f|不足|ぶそく}だ<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられると{f|思|おも}います</b>。 → ✅ {f|原因|げんいん}は{f|人手|ひとで}{f|不足|ぶそく}だ<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられます</b>。
  Two hedges do not make a claim twice as careful. Pick one.

## Practice

**1.** {f|停電|ていでん}の{f|原因|げんいん}は{f|落雷|らくらい}____{f|考|かんが}えられます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|落雷|らくらい}<b>だと</b>{f|考|かんが}えられます。

{f|落雷|らくらい} is a noun, and `と` needs a finished sentence in front of it, so the `だ`
has to be there. Dropping it is the single most common slip with this pattern.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|映画|えいが}を{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}かない？　あの{f|監督|かんとく}の{f|新作|しんさく}、おもしろい（と{f|思|おも}うよ・と{f|考|かんが}えられるよ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

おもしろい<b>と{f|思|おも}うよ</b>。

An invitation to the cinema is exactly where a personal opinion belongs. The other
pattern strips the speaker out of the sentence, which in casual conversation sounds like
a press release.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, in the register of a report: "The decline in sales is thought to have been caused by the summer's unusual weather."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|売上|うりあげ}の{f|減少|げんしょう}は{f|夏|なつ}の{f|異常|いじょう}{f|気象|きしょう}によるもの<b>だと{f|考|かんが}えられます</b>。

<code>によるもの</code> gives you a noun to hang the `だ` on, which is the ordinary way to
say "caused by" in this register. English "is thought to" almost always maps to this
pattern in reports.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|調査|ちょうさ}の{f|結果|けっか}から、この{f|方法|ほうほう}は{f|安全|あんぜん}と{f|考|かんが}えられていると{f|思|おも}います。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|調査|ちょうさ}の{f|結果|けっか}から、この{f|方法|ほうほう}は{f|安全|あんぜん}<b>だと{f|考|かんが}えられます</b>。

Two repairs. {f|安全|あんぜん} is a な-adjective and needs `だ` before `と`; and the
sentence stacks three hedges at once. Since the conclusion comes from
<code>{f|調査|ちょうさ}の{f|結果|けっか}</code> - the writer's own survey - the plain
<code>と{f|考|かんが}えられます</code> is right, without ている and without
<code>と{f|思|おも}います</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}は{f|来年|らいねん}{f|実用化|じつようか}される<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられる</b>。
(b) この{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}は{f|来年|らいねん}{f|実用化|じつようか}される<b>と{f|考|かんが}えられている</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the writer's own reading of the evidence just presented. If a reader disagrees,
they are disagreeing with this author, and the author is expected to have grounds in the
preceding paragraph.

(b) reports what the field currently assumes. The writer takes no responsibility for it -
indeed (b) is very often the setup for a "however", with the next sentence arguing that
the accepted view is mistaken.

</details>
