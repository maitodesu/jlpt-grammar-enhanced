# ～くらいなら

**Meaning** · rather than put up with that, I'd sooner do this

`くらいなら` names something the speaker finds intolerable and then reaches for an
alternative - often an extreme one - as the lesser evil. Neither option is attractive.
The sentence is a choice made in exasperation, and it usually ends in
<code>ほうがましだ</code>, "that would be preferable", which itself means "less bad"
rather than "good".

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + くらいなら | {f|謝|あやま}る → {f|謝|あやま}るくらいなら |
| Verb (state) | ～ている + くらいなら | {f|待|ま}っている → {f|待|ま}っているくらいなら |
| Verb phrase, not a bare noun | ～する + くらいなら | バス → バスで{f|行|い}くくらいなら |
| Casual variant | ぐらいなら | {f|並|なら}ぶ → {f|並|なら}ぶぐらいなら |
| Main clause | the lesser evil | ～ほうがましだ / ～ほうがいい / ～たい / ～ない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>ましだ</code> is the pattern's signature ending and it is not a
compliment. It means "preferable to that", with the strong implication that the
preferred option is only marginally better. Substituting
<code>すばらしい</code> or <code>{f|最高|さいこう}だ</code> breaks the logic.

</div>

## Nuance & register

This is an emotional pattern. Choosing `くらいなら` announces that the first option is
not merely worse but *unacceptable*, and speakers reach for deliberately extreme
alternatives to make the point - quitting, walking an hour, never starting.
<code>{f|死|し}んだほうがましだ</code> is a real and common ending, and it is understood as
hyperbole, not as a statement about wanting to die.

`くらいなら` and `ぐらいなら` are the same thing; `ぐらい` is a little more casual and a
little more frequent in speech. The pattern itself is register-neutral and appears in
polite speech (<code>～ほうがましです</code>) and in editorials as readily as in
grumbling, though the grumbling is its home ground.

Two limits are worth holding on to. First, the alternative has to be a *choice between
bad options*: if you actually like the second thing, plain `より` or `ほうがいい` is what
you want. Second, `くらいなら` needs a clause in front of it, not a bare noun -
<code>バスくらいなら</code> means "if it's only as much as a bus", which is a different
pattern entirely. Put a verb in: <code>バスで{f|行|い}くくらいなら</code>.

## Contrast

### ～くらいなら vs ～より（は）

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `より` compares two options neutrally. `くらいなら` **rejects** the
first outright and settles for the second.

</div>

<pre>
バス<b>より</b>{f|電車|でんしゃ}のほうが{f|速|はや}いですよ。
The train is faster than the bus.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|渋滞|じゅうたい}したバスに{f|乗|の}る<b>くらいなら</b>、{f|駅|えき}まで{f|走|はし}ります。
Rather than sit on a bus stuck in traffic, I'll run to the station.
</pre>

`より` can compare two good things - <code>コーヒーよりお{f|茶|ちゃ}が{f|好|す}きだ</code> -
and carries no feeling. `くらいなら` cannot be neutral: the thing in front of it is
always something the speaker refuses.

### ～くらいなら vs ～のなら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `のなら` supposes the thing might happen and responds to it.
`くらいなら` treats it as unbearable and turns away.

</div>

<pre>
{f|本当|ほんとう}に{f|謝|あやま}る<b>のなら</b>、こちらも{f|水|みず}に{f|流|なが}しますよ。
If you're genuinely going to apologise, I'll let the matter drop.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|心|こころ}にもないことを{f|言|い}う<b>くらいなら</b>、{f|何|なに}も{f|言|い}わないでほしい。
Rather than say something you don't mean, I'd rather you said nothing.
</pre>

`のなら` leaves the door open and can be followed by an offer, an instruction or a
condition. `くらいなら` slams it, and can only be followed by the speaker's preferred
alternative.

### ～くらいなら vs the "extent" ～くらい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if what precedes is an **action you would hate**, the meaning is
"rather than". If it is a **degree or amount**, the meaning is "if it's only that
much".

</div>

<pre>
{f|少|すこ}し{f|高|たか}い<b>くらいなら</b>、{f|買|か}ってもいいかな。
If it's only a bit expensive, I might buy it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|定価|ていか}で{f|買|か}う<b>くらいなら</b>、セールまで{f|待|ま}ちます。
Rather than pay full price, I'll wait for the sale.
</pre>

Same four syllables, two patterns. The giveaway is what comes before: an adjective or a
quantity gives you the "only that much" reading, a volitional action gives you the
"rather than" one. The main clauses differ too - the first ends in permission, the
second in a rejection.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}く<b>くらいなら</b>、{f|海|うみ}がいいです。 → ✅ {f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}く<b>なら</b>、{f|海|うみ}がいいです。
  Travelling is not something the speaker is refusing, so there is nothing for
  `くらいなら` to reject. A neutral supposition takes `なら`.

- ❌ バス<b>くらいなら</b>、{f|歩|ある}きます。 → ✅ バスに{f|乗|の}る<b>くらいなら</b>、{f|歩|ある}きます。
  A bare noun in front of `くらい` gives the "about, only that much" reading. Supply a
  verb to get the "rather than" sense.

- ❌ {f|待|ま}たされる<b>くらいなら</b>、{f|時間|じかん}がかかります。 → ✅ {f|待|ま}たされる<b>くらいなら</b>、{f|予約|よやく}して{f|行|い}きます。
  The second half must be the alternative the speaker chooses. A plain description of
  the situation leaves the comparison unfinished.

- ❌ この{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|続|つづ}ける<b>くらいなら</b>、{f|転職|てんしょく}したほうがすばらしい。 → ✅ この{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|続|つづ}ける<b>くらいなら</b>、{f|転職|てんしょく}したほうがましだ。
  <code>ましだ</code> is the right word because the alternative is only *less bad*.
  Praising it outright contradicts the resignation the pattern carries.

## Practice

**1.** こんな{f|安|やす}い{f|値段|ねだん}で{f|売|う}る____、{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|使|つか}ったほうがいい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|売|う}る<b>くらいなら</b>

Selling at that price is being rejected as not worth doing, and the second half offers
the alternative the speaker prefers - the standard shape of this pattern.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|明日|あした}{f|来|こ}られる（なら・くらいなら）、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|用意|ようい}しておきます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|明日|あした}{f|来|こ}られる<b>なら</b>、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|用意|ようい}しておきます。

The speaker is arranging something helpfully, not refusing anything. `くらいなら` would
mean the visit was intolerable, which contradicts preparing documents for it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'd rather be told to my face than hear about it from someone else."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|他人|たにん}から{f|聞|き}かされる<b>くらいなら</b>、{f|直接|ちょくせつ}{f|言|い}ってもらったほうがましだ。

Hearing it secondhand is the rejected option, and <code>ほうがましだ</code> supplies
the "lesser evil" ending the pattern expects.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|残|のこ}すくらいなら、{f|最初|さいしょ}から{f|少|すく}なく{f|注文|ちゅうもん}してください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|残|のこ}すくらいなら、{f|最初|さいしょ}から{f|少|すく}なく{f|注文|ちゅうもん}した<b>ほうがいい</b>。

The problem is the request. `くらいなら` presents the speaker's own preference between
two options, so it is completed by a comparative judgement rather than by an
instruction.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|一時間|いちじかん}{f|待|ま}つ<b>より</b>、{f|歩|ある}いたほうが{f|早|はや}い。
(b) {f|一時間|いちじかん}も{f|待|ま}つ<b>くらいなら</b>、{f|歩|ある}いたほうがましだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a calm calculation: walking takes less time than waiting, and the speaker is
simply reporting which is quicker.

(b) is a refusal. An hour's wait is presented as unbearable, walking is grudgingly
accepted as the lesser evil, and <code>も</code> and <code>ましだ</code> both carry the
speaker's irritation.

</details>
