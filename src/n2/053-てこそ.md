# ～てこそ

**Meaning** · only by actually doing it - X is what makes Y true, and short of X there is no Y

`てこそ` sets a bar. It takes an action or a state, marks it with the emphatic `こそ`
you met at N3, and declares that **this and nothing less** is what brings the result
about. What follows is a verdict of worth: it counts, it means something, it becomes
possible. Socially the pattern is an exhortation - a person saying `てこそ` is
usually telling you, or telling the room, what the real standard is.

## Formation

て-form + `こそ`. Nouns and な-adjectives go through `である`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + こそ | {f|続|つづ}ける → {f|続|つづ}けてこそ |
| Verb (potential) | て-form + こそ | {f|説明|せつめい}できる → {f|説明|せつめい}できてこそ |
| Verb (passive) | て-form + こそ | {f|支|ささ}えられる → {f|支|ささ}えられてこそ |
| Noun | ～であってこそ | {f|健康|けんこう} → {f|健康|けんこう}であってこそ |
| な-adjective | ～であってこそ | {f|公平|こうへい}だ → {f|公平|こうへい}であってこそ |
| Usual endings | …{f|意味|いみ}がある／{f|価値|かち}がある／～と{f|言|い}える／～のだ | {f|本物|ほんもの}と{f|言|い}える |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the clause after `てこそ` is **positive**: something gains value,
becomes possible, or finally deserves its name. There is no negative counterpart -
"if you don't do X, Y won't happen" is <code>～なければ～ない</code>, never
❌<code>～なくてこそ</code>.

</div>

{f|自分|じぶん}でやっ<b>てこそ</b>、{f|身|み}につく。
You only really learn it by doing it yourself.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|使|つか}っ<b>てこそ</b>、{f|道具|どうぐ}は{f|生|い}きる。
A tool only comes into its own when it is used.
</pre>

</pre>

<pre>

<pre>

</pre>

I've come to see that health is what lets you throw yourself into what you love.
</pre>

<pre>

Only when you can say in your own words what went wrong can you carry the experience forward.
</pre>

<pre>

It is only because the neighbourhood has carried it that this festival has lasted a hundred years.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

`てこそ` is aphoristic. It turns up in mottoes, speeches, editorials, coaching talk
and advice from someone older, and it has a faint air of a maxim even in an ordinary
conversation. That is not a warning - it is used, and used often - but it does mean
a `てこそ` sentence sounds as though it is stating a principle rather than describing
Tuesday.

Politeness lives at the end of the sentence, not in `てこそ` itself:
<code>{f|続|つづ}けてこそ{f|意味|いみ}があります</code> is perfectly polite. The pattern
does not shift register the way `もんだから` or `ものですから` do.

Two limits are worth holding onto. First, the second clause has to be **favourable**
- worth, meaning, possibility, the real version of a thing. A sentence whose payoff
is a misfortune wants `から` or `せいで`. Second, the `てこそ` clause is normally
something achievable and, more often than not, something a person chooses to do or
to be. A purely accidental event in that slot reads oddly, because the pattern is
implicitly holding someone to a standard.

Finally, `てこそ` frequently pairs with {f|初|はじ}めて for emphasis -
<code>{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|払|はら}ってこそ{f|初|はじ}めて{f|物|もの}の{f|値段|ねだん}が{f|分|わ}かる</code> -
and the two reinforce each other without redundancy.

## Contrast

### ～てこそ vs ～てはじめて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>てはじめて</code> is about **when you found out**;
`てこそ` is about **what makes it count**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|暮|く}らし<b>てはじめて</b>、{f|親|おや}のありがたさが{f|分|わ}かった。
It was only after living alone that I understood what my parents had done for me.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|暮|く}らし<b>てこそ</b>、{f|生活|せいかつ}の{f|大変|たいへん}さが{f|身|み}にしみる。
It's living alone that really brings home how hard daily life is.
</pre>

<code>てはじめて</code> is a report, usually in the past, of a realisation that came
too late; it collocates with {f|分|わ}かる, {f|気|き}づく, {f|知|し}る. `てこそ` is a
general claim about value, usually in the non-past, and it collocates with
{f|意味|いみ}がある and {f|言|い}える. Where the sentence is your own experience, choose
<code>てはじめて</code>; where it is a principle you are recommending, choose `てこそ`.

### ～てこそ vs ～からこそ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `からこそ` explains something that **is already the case**.
`てこそ` states the condition on which something **would** be the case.

</div>

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}している<b>からこそ</b>、あの{f|安定|あんてい}した{f|演奏|えんそう}ができるのだ。
It's the daily practice that lets him play that steadily.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}し<b>てこそ</b>、{f|本番|ほんばん}で{f|力|ちから}が{f|出|だ}せる。
Only if you practise every day can you deliver on the night.
</pre>

The first looks at a musician who already plays well and identifies the reason. The
second looks at nobody in particular and lays down a rule. The forms differ too -
plain clause + `からこそ` against て-form + `こそ` - so the shape alone will usually
tell you which one a sentence is using.

### ～てこそ vs ～なければ～ない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - they are the positive and negative faces of one idea, and
Japanese only lets you build the negative face with `なければ`.

</div>

<pre>
{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|調|しら}べ<b>てこそ</b>、{f|記憶|きおく}に{f|残|のこ}る。
Looking it up yourself is what makes it stick.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|調|しら}べ<b>なければ</b>、{f|記憶|きおく}に{f|残|のこ}らない。
If you don't look it up yourself, it won't stick.
</pre>

The content is nearly the same; the effect is not. `てこそ` is encouraging and sets a
goal, `なければ～ない` is a warning. When you want the warning, do not try to force it
through `てこそ` - ❌<code>{f|調|しら}べなくてこそ</code> does not exist.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|練習|れんしゅう}する<b>こそ</b>、{f|上達|じょうたつ}する。 → ✅ {f|練習|れんしゅう}し<b>てこそ</b>、{f|上達|じょうたつ}する。
  `こそ` in this meaning attaches to the て-form, not the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|努力|どりょく}し<b>てこそ</b>、{f|成功|せいこう}できない。 → ✅ {f|努力|どりょく}し<b>なければ</b>、{f|成功|せいこう}できない。
  The clause after `てこそ` cannot be negative. Flip the whole sentence into
  <code>なければ～ない</code> instead.

- ❌ {f|健康|けんこう}<b>でこそ</b>、{f|何|なん}でもできる。 → ✅ {f|健康|けんこう}<b>であってこそ</b>、{f|何|なん}でもできる。
  A noun or な-adjective needs the full <code>である</code> before `こそ`.

- ❌ {f|寝坊|ねぼう}し<b>てこそ</b>、{f|遅刻|ちこく}した。 → ✅ {f|寝坊|ねぼう}した<b>から</b>、{f|遅刻|ちこく}した。
  `てこそ` announces that something gains value. A plain unwanted consequence has no
  business in that slot.

## Practice

**1.** {f|約束|やくそく}は、{f|守|まも}っ____{f|信頼|しんらい}が{f|生|う}まれる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|守|まも}っ<b>てこそ</b>{f|信頼|しんらい}が{f|生|う}まれる。

Keeping the promise is the condition on which trust becomes possible, and the payoff
is a positive one - the exact shape `てこそ` is for. Note the て-form: {f|守|まも}る → {f|守|まも}って.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|海外|かいがい}で{f|暮|く}らし（てこそ・てはじめて）、{f|日本|にほん}の{f|良|よ}さに{f|気|き}づきました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|海外|かいがい}で{f|暮|く}らし<b>てはじめて</b>、{f|日本|にほん}の{f|良|よ}さに{f|気|き}づきました。

This is a past realisation the speaker had personally, with {f|気|き}づく as the verb -
<code>てはじめて</code>'s home ground. `てこそ` would turn a memory into a piece of
advice for everyone, which the ました ending contradicts.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "A dictionary is only worth having if you actually use it."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|実際|じっさい}に{f|使|つか}っ<b>てこそ</b>{f|価値|かち}がある。

<code>{f|価値|かち}がある</code> is one of the standard endings for `てこそ`, and the
English "only… if" is precisely the bar-setting the pattern draws. A conditional with
`たら` would state a fact; `てこそ` states a principle.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|水|みず}をやってこそ、{f|花|はな}は{f|枯|か}れてしまう。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|水|みず}をやってこそ、{f|花|はな}は{f|元気|げんき}に{f|育|そだ}つ。
（or {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|水|みず}をやらなければ、{f|花|はな}は{f|枯|か}れてしまう。）

The original pairs `てこそ` with a bad outcome, which the pattern will not carry.
Either keep `てこそ` and give it a positive result, or keep the withering and switch
to <code>なければ</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|人|ひと}の{f|話|はなし}を{f|聞|き}い<b>てこそ</b>、いい{f|指導者|しどうしゃ}だ。
(b) {f|人|ひと}の{f|話|はなし}を{f|聞|き}く<b>から</b>、いい{f|指導者|しどうしゃ}だ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) defines the standard. It says that listening is the thing that qualifies someone
as a good leader at all, and by implication that a leader who does not listen is not
one. It is a claim about what the word ought to mean.

(b) simply explains an assessment already made of a particular person: he listens,
and that is why you rate him. No standard is being set, and nobody else is being
judged by it.

</details>
