# にて

**Meaning** · in; at; by means of - the formal, written counterpart of で

`にて` is `で` in a stiff collar. It marks the **place** something is held, the
**medium** through which it is done, and, in a couple of set phrases, the point at
which something ends. It is the particle of invitations, notices, ceremonies and
business correspondence; historically it is the ancestor of `で`, which is why the
two overlap so exactly. Nothing forces you to use it - every `にて` could be `で` -
but choosing it marks the whole sentence as formal.

## Formation

`にて` attaches to nouns only, and it never changes shape.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Place | Noun + にて | {f|市民|しみん}ホール → {f|市民|しみん}ホールにて |
| Means, medium | Noun + にて | メール → メールにて |
| Method of application | Noun + にて | {f|郵送|ゆうそう} → {f|郵送|ゆうそう}にて |
| Fixed closing phrase | これ + にて | - → これにて{f|終了|しゅうりょう} |
| Ceremonial farewell | これ + にて | - → これにて{f|失礼|しつれい}いたします |
| ❌ Verb or adjective | ❌{f|忙|いそが}しいにて | ✅{f|忙|いそが}しいので |
| ❌ Range of comparison | ❌{f|日本|にほん}にて{f|一番|いちばん}{f|高|たか}い{f|山|やま} | ✅{f|日本|にほん}で{f|一番|いちばん}{f|高|たか}い{f|山|やま} |

<div class="note">

**Note** - `にて` is a **particle**, so it takes no conjugation and no `の`. Where
you would say <code>{f|東京|とうきょう}で</code>, you may say
<code>{f|東京|とうきょう}にて</code>; where you would say
<code>{f|東京|とうきょう}の…</code>, `にて` has no place.

</div>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>
{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|市民|しみん}ホール<b>にて</b>、{f|開演|かいえん}は{f|六時|ろくじ}でございます。

</pre>

</pre>

<pre>
{f|結果|けっか}は{f|後日|ごじつ}、{f|書面|しょめん}<b>にて</b>お{f|知|し}らせいたします。
You will be informed of the result in writing at a later date.
</pre>

<pre>

## Nuance & register

`にて` is a register marker and almost nothing else. It belongs to printed notices,
wedding invitations, itineraries, station and shop announcements, and the polite
half of business email. Staff use it out loud to customers; a shop assistant saying
<code>{f|一階|いっかい}にて{f|承|うけたまわ}ります</code> is unremarkable. Between
friends it is not so much wrong as theatrical, and a learner who says
<code>カフェにて{f|待|ま}ってるね</code> will get a laugh.

Because it is a formality signal, it needs the rest of the sentence to match. `にて`
with a plain-form ending clashes badly: if you have reached for `にて`, the verb
should be です・ます at least, and more often the humble forms いたします,
{f|申|もう}し{f|上|あ}げます, させていただきます.

The one shape everyone meets first is <code>これにて</code>, which closes things:
<code>これにて{f|終了|しゅうりょう}</code> at the end of a programme,
<code>これにて{f|失礼|しつれい}いたします</code> when taking your leave. These are
frozen expressions, and they are worth learning as units.

A last historical note. The very formal use of `にて` for a cause - the sort of thing
you meet in old documents and stiff notices,
<code>{f|都合|つごう}にて{f|欠席|けっせき}</code> - is genuinely archaic and reads as
period flavour today. Recognise it; do not build sentences on it.

## Contrast

### にて vs で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same meaning, two registers. `で` is always available and always
safe. `にて` is `で` dressed for an occasion, and it obliges everything around it to
dress up too.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}の{f|打|う}ち{f|合|あ}わせ、{f|駅前|えきまえ}のカフェ<b>で</b>いい?
Shall we make it the cafe by the station for tomorrow's chat?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|明日|あす}の{f|打|う}ち{f|合|あ}わせは{f|駅前|えきまえ}の{f|支店|してん}<b>にて</b>{f|行|おこな}います。
Tomorrow's meeting will be held at the branch office by the station.
</pre>

The first is speech between colleagues; the second is a line in a calendar
invitation. Note that `で` covers jobs `にて` does not touch: the range of a
superlative (<code>クラスで{f|一番|いちばん}</code>), a cause
(<code>{f|風邪|かぜ}で{f|休|やす}む</code>) and a material
(<code>{f|木|き}で{f|作|つく}る</code>) all stay with `で` in modern Japanese.

### にて vs において

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are formal. `において` handles **abstract domains** as well
as places - fields, eras, situations. `にて` stays **concrete**: a venue, a medium, a
counter.

</div>

<pre>
{f|医療|いりょう}の{f|分野|ぶんや}<b>において</b>、AIの{f|利用|りよう}が{f|広|ひろ}がっている。
In the field of medicine, the use of AI is spreading.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|受付|うけつけ}カウンター<b>にて</b>{f|整理券|せいりけん}をお{f|配|くば}りします。
Numbered tickets will be handed out at the reception counter.
</pre>

Put `にて` in the first sentence and a field of study is turned into a room; put
`において` in the second and a counter is inflated into a domain. Where the noun
really is a venue, both work, and `において` is the more official of the two - it is
what a formal programme or a legal document prefers. `にて` also does something
`において` cannot: mark a **medium**. ❌<code>メールにおいてご{f|連絡|れんらく}します</code>
is not idiomatic.

### にて vs をもって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - for a means, <code>をもって</code> is heavier and more
legalistic than `にて`. For a **closing date**, <code>をもって</code> is the standard
expression and `にて` is not used.

</div>

<pre>
{f|合否|ごうひ}は{f|書面|しょめん}<b>にて</b>お{f|知|し}らせします。
You will be notified of the outcome in writing.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|当店|とうてん}は{f|三月|さんがつ}{f|末日|まつじつ}<b>をもって</b>{f|閉店|へいてん}いたします。
This shop will close its doors at the end of March.
</pre>

Both sentences are formal announcements, but only <code>をもって</code> can draw the
line under a period. The one place `にて` does mark an ending is the frozen
<code>これにて</code>, which refers to the present moment rather than to a date.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|友|とも}だちと{f|公園|こうえん}<b>にて</b>{f|遊|あそ}んだよ。 → ✅ {f|友|とも}だちと{f|公園|こうえん}<b>で</b>{f|遊|あそ}んだよ。
  A casual sentence with a plain ending cannot carry `にて`. The particle and the
  verb ending have to be at the same level of formality.

- ❌ {f|日本|にほん}<b>にて</b>{f|一番|いちばん}{f|人気|にんき}のある{f|漫画|まんが}です。 → ✅ {f|日本|にほん}<b>で</b>{f|一番|いちばん}{f|人気|にんき}のある{f|漫画|まんが}です。
  Marking the range of a comparison is a job `にて` never took over. That `で` stays
  `で` however formal the sentence.

- ❌ {f|事故|じこ}<b>にて</b>{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|遅|おく}れております。 → ✅ {f|事故|じこ}の<b>{f|影響|えいきょう}で</b>{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|遅|おく}れております。
  Cause with `にて` survives only as archaic flavour. Modern announcements use `で`
  or <code>のため</code>.

- ❌ {f|会場|かいじょう}<b>にてで</b>お{f|待|ま}ちください。 → ✅ {f|会場|かいじょう}<b>にて</b>お{f|待|ま}ちください。
  `にて` already contains the `で`. Stacking the two is a slip that comes from
  thinking of `にて` as に + で rather than as a single particle.

## Practice

**1.** {f|詳|くわ}しい{f|日程|にってい}は、{f|後日|ごじつ}メール____ご{f|案内|あんない}いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

メール<b>にて</b>

Email here is the medium through which the information is sent, which is one of the
two core jobs of this particle. The humble
<code>ご{f|案内|あんない}いたします</code> sets exactly the register `にて` expects.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|環境|かんきょう}{f|問題|もんだい}の{f|分野|ぶんや}（にて・において）、{f|日本|にほん}は{f|多|おお}くの{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}を{f|持|も}っている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|分野|ぶんや}<b>において</b>

A field of activity is an abstract domain, not a venue you can stand in. `にて`
handles concrete places and media only, so `において` is the only option here.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The ceremony will be held in the main hall on the second floor."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|式|しき}は{f|二階|にかい}の{f|大|おお}ホール<b>にて</b>{f|行|おこな}われます。

A named venue in an announcement is the archetypal use, and the passive
<code>{f|行|おこな}われます</code> keeps the sentence in the impersonal register that
notices are written in.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|三階|さんがい}にてやるから、{f|早|はや}めに{f|来|き}てね。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|三階|さんがい}<b>で</b>やるから、{f|早|はや}めに{f|来|き}てね。

The ending やるから…{f|来|き}てね is relaxed speech to a friend, and `にて` cannot
live in that sentence. Either lower the particle to `で`, as here, or raise the whole
sentence to <code>{f|三階|さんがい}にて{f|行|おこな}いますので…</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|本日|ほんじつ}の{f|受付|うけつけ}はこれ<b>にて</b>{f|終了|しゅうりょう}いたします。
(b) {f|本日|ほんじつ}の{f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|五時|ごじ}<b>をもって</b>{f|終了|しゅうりょう}いたします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) closes the desk **now**, at the moment of speaking.
<code>これにて</code> is a frozen phrase pointing at the present instant, and it is
what staff actually say as they put the sign up.

(b) names a **time** as the cut-off, and would be announced or printed in advance. It
is the more official of the two, and it is the form a written notice uses.

</details>
