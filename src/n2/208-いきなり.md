# いきなり

**Meaning** · out of nowhere, with no warning and no lead-up

`いきなり` is not just "suddenly". It says that the step which normally comes
first - the greeting, the explanation, the warm-up - was skipped entirely. That
missing run-up is why the word so often sounds like a complaint.

## Usage

`いきなり` is an adverb. It sits in front of the verb, or at the head of the
clause when it colours the whole event, and it never changes shape.

It attaches most naturally to things a *person* does without preparing you for
them:

- いきなり{f|大声|おおごえ}を{f|出|だ}す - to shout with no warning
- いきなり{f|本題|ほんだい}に{f|入|はい}る - to launch straight into the main point
- いきなり{f|立|た}ち{f|上|あ}がる - to jump to one's feet

Because the action is usually done *to* the speaker, the passive is extremely
common: いきなり{f|呼|よ}ばれる, いきなり{f|怒|おこ}られる,
いきなり{f|話|はな}しかけられる.

A second, calmer use has no surprise in it at all - it simply means *straight to
the last step, skipping the ones in between*: {f|初日|しょにち}にいきなり
{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|店|みせ}を{f|任|まか}された, "on day one they had me running the
shop by myself".

In casual speech it can also modify a noun with `の`: いきなりの{f|雨|あめ},
いきなりの{f|変更|へんこう}. That is colloquial; written Japanese prefers
<code>{f|突然|とつぜん}の</code>. There is no ❌いきなりな - the word is not a
な-adjective.

## Examples

<pre>
となりの{f|部屋|へや}のドアが<b>いきなり</b>{f|開|あ}いた。
The door of the next room flew open with no warning.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|後|うし}ろから<b>いきなり</b>{f|名前|なまえ}を{f|呼|よ}ばれて、びっくりした。
Someone called my name from behind out of nowhere and it startled me.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|準備運動|じゅんびうんどう}もせずに<b>いきなり</b>{f|走|はし}り{f|出|だ}すと、{f|足|あし}をいためますよ。
If you start running cold with no warm-up, you'll hurt your legs.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|前置|まえお}きもなく<b>いきなり</b>{f|数字|すうじ}の{f|話|はなし}を{f|始|はじ}められても、{f|何|なん}の{f|会議|かいぎ}か{f|分|わ}かりません。
When you open straight onto the figures with no preamble, nobody can tell what the meeting is even about.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Colloquial and very frequent. It is at home in conversation, chat messages and
casual writing; in a news report or a formal document the word used is
<code>{f|突然|とつぜん}</code> or <code>{f|急|きゅう}に</code> instead. It is not
marked for age or gender.

The attitude it carries is worth learning together with the meaning. Nine times
out of ten, `いきなり` is mildly aggrieved: something was done to the speaker in
the wrong order, and the sentence is a small protest about it. Reporting your own
action with it - いきなり{f|聞|き}いてすみません - is therefore a way of
apologising for the abruptness before anyone can object.

Because the complaint is about manners rather than about the event itself, the
word sits badly on grave news. For a death, an accident or an illness,
<code>{f|突然|とつぜん}</code> is the word; `いきなり` would sound flippant.

## Contrast

### いきなり vs {f|突然|とつぜん}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|突然|とつぜん}</code> says the **event** was
unexpected. `いきなり` says the **order** was wrong: the thing that should have
come first never happened.

</div>

<pre>
<b>{f|突然|とつぜん}</b>{f|停電|ていでん}になり、ビル{f|全体|ぜんたい}が{f|暗|くら}くなった。
The power went out all at once and the whole building went dark.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|上司|じょうし}は<b>いきなり</b>{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|出張|しゅっちょう}に{f|行|い}けと{f|言|い}ってきた。
My boss sprang it on me that I'm going on a business trip next week.
</pre>

A power cut has no manners to violate, so <code>{f|突然|とつぜん}</code> fits and
`いきなり` would be odd. The instruction from the boss could have been raised in
advance and was not, which is exactly what `いきなり` objects to.

<code>{f|突然|とつぜん}</code> is also the more written and more dramatic of the
two, and unlike `いきなり` it forms a noun modifier freely:
<code>{f|突然|とつぜん}の{f|訪問|ほうもん}</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|祖母|そぼ}がいきなり{f|亡|な}くなりました。 → ✅ {f|祖母|そぼ}が<b>{f|突然|とつぜん}</b>{f|亡|な}くなりました。
  `いきなり` complains about a missing run-up, which makes it sound casual about a
  death. Use <code>{f|突然|とつぜん}</code>.

- ❌ いきなりな{f|質問|しつもん}ですみません。 → ✅ <b>いきなりの</b>{f|質問|しつもん}ですみません。／<b>{f|突然|とつぜん}の</b>{f|質問|しつもん}ですみません。
  `いきなり` is an adverb, not a な-adjective. Before a noun it takes `の`, and in
  polite writing <code>{f|突然|とつぜん}の</code> is safer.

- ❌ すみませんが、いきなり{f|来|き}てください。 → ✅ すみませんが、<b>すぐに</b>{f|来|き}てください。
  `いきなり` is never "right away" as an instruction. Asking someone to arrive
  without warning is not what you meant.

## Practice

**1.** ドラマの{f|途中|とちゅう}で____{f|画面|がめん}が{f|真|ま}っ{f|黒|くろ}になって、{f|音|おと}も{f|消|き}えた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|途中|とちゅう}で<b>いきなり</b>{f|画面|がめん}が{f|真|ま}っ{f|黒|くろ}になって…

The complaint is that the programme gave no sign it was about to cut out.
<code>{f|突然|とつぜん}</code> would also be grammatical here; `いきなり` is the more
colloquial choice and adds the note of irritation.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: {f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|日|ひ}が（いきなり・{f|突然|とつぜん}）{f|変|か}わったと{f|部長|ぶちょう}にメールで{f|知|し}らされた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

Both are possible, but <b>いきなり</b> is the one a colleague would actually say:
the date could have been discussed first and was not, so the missing consultation
is the point. <code>{f|突然|とつぜん}</code> would frame the change as an event that
simply befell everyone, which is what you would write in a formal notice.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "He walked in without knocking and started talking about money."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ノックもせずに{f|入|はい}ってきて、<b>いきなり</b>お{f|金|かね}の{f|話|はなし}を{f|始|はじ}めた。

Two skipped preliminaries - the knock and any small talk - so `いきなり` is exactly
right. Note that it goes in front of the abrupt action itself, not at the very
head of the sentence.

</details>
