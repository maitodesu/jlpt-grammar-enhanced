# ～ずじまい

**Meaning** · ended up never doing it - you meant to, the chance went by, and now it is too late

`じまい` is <code>{f|仕舞|しま}い</code>, "the end", with the `し` voiced; `ず` is
the classical negative you already know from `ずに`. Put together they read almost
literally: *and that was the end of it, undone*. Three things have to be true for
the pattern to fit. You **intended** to do the thing. You **did not**. And the
opportunity is now **gone** - which is why the sentence always carries at least a
trace of regret.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ない-stem + ずじまい | {f|言|い}う → {f|言|い}わずじまい |
| Verb (る-verb) | ない-stem + ずじまい | {f|食|た}べる → {f|食|た}べずじまい |
| Verb (する) | **せずじまい** | {f|連絡|れんらく}する → {f|連絡|れんらく}せずじまい |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | ない-stem + ずじまい | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ずじまい |
| Potential verb | potential ない-stem + ずじまい | {f|渡|わた}せる → {f|渡|わた}せずじまい |
| Usual predicate | ～ずじまいだ ／ ～ずじまいだった | {f|会|あ}う → {f|会|あ}わずじまいだった |
| Ending up so | ～ずじまいになる ／ ～ずじまいに{f|終|お}わる | {f|使|つか}う → {f|使|つか}わずじまいに{f|終|お}わった |
| Modifying what follows | ～ずじまいの ／ ～ずじまいで | {f|出|だ}す → {f|出|だ}さずじまいの{f|手紙|てがみ} |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the negative here is the classical `ず`, so `する` gives
<code>せず</code>: ✅<code>{f|連絡|れんらく}せずじまい</code>,
❌<code>{f|連絡|れんらく}しずじまい</code>. And it is never
❌<code>{f|読|よ}まないじまい</code> - `ない` cannot take the place of `ず` in this
compound.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|結局|けっきょく}</code>, `とうとう` and `ついに` sit in front of this
pattern constantly. The sentence is a summing-up, so it likes an adverb that says
so.

</div>

## Nuance & register

The tone is retrospective and faintly literary. You will meet it in narration,
diaries, travel writing and reminiscence, and also in perfectly ordinary speech
whenever somebody is summing up a disappointment:
「{f|結局|けっきょく}{f|会|あ}わずじまいだったよ」. It is not formal exactly - it is
**wistful**, which is a different thing.

The **intention is not optional**. If buying a lottery ticket never crossed your
mind, <code>{f|宝|たから}くじは{f|買|か}わずじまいだった</code> is simply the wrong
sentence; you want plain <code>{f|買|か}わなかった</code>. The regret is baked in too, so where
there is genuinely no regret, the plain past negative is again what you want.

In practice the pattern is almost always **past**, because it reports an outcome.
But it does reach forward when the outcome is already effectively settled:
<code>このままでは{f|会|あ}わずじまいになりそうだ</code> - "at this rate we'll end
up never meeting" - is natural, and so is
<code>～ずじまいになってしまう</code>. What it cannot do is announce a decision. A
plan not to do something tomorrow is `～ないつもりだ`.

One structural point: `ずじまい` is verbal only. There is no noun row in the table
above because there is no noun form.

## Contrast

### ～ずじまい vs ～ないまま

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ないまま` describes a **state that persisted**: the thing was
simply not done. `ずじまい` adds that somebody **meant to** do it and the chance is
now gone.

</div>

<pre>
{f|理由|りゆう}が{f|分|わ}から<b>ないまま</b>、{f|話|はなし}は{f|終|お}わった。
The discussion ended with the reason still unclear.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|理由|りゆう}は{f|聞|き}か<b>ずじまい</b>だった。
I never did get round to asking why.
</pre>

`ないまま` is neutral. It will describe anything that stayed undone, including
things nobody had any intention of doing, and including states rather than actions
- <code>{f|分|わ}からないまま</code> is about knowledge, not about a missed task.
`ずじまい` always has a person in it who wanted to and did not.

### ～ずじまい vs the plain ～なかった

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なかった` reports the **bare fact**. `ずじまい` reports the same
fact as a **missed opportunity**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}では{f|一度|いちど}も{f|雪|ゆき}を{f|見|み}<b>なかった</b>。
I didn't see any snow at all in Hokkaido.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|楽|たの}しみにしていた{f|流氷|りゅうひょう}は、{f|見|み}<b>ずじまい</b>だった。
I never did get to see the drift ice I had been looking forward to.
</pre>

Take <code>{f|楽|たの}しみにしていた</code> out of the second sentence and it
weakens noticeably, because the pattern is leaning on it: something in the context
has to establish that the speaker wanted this. `なかった` needs no such support and
carries no feeling of its own.

### ～ずじまい vs ～そびれる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `そびれる` names the **moment of failing** - you missed your
chance to. `ずじまい` names the **outcome** - the whole affair ended undone.

</div>

<pre>
{f|話|はな}しかけようとしたが、{f|言|い}い<b>そびれて</b>しまった。
I was about to say something, but I missed my moment.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|結局|けっきょく}、{f|最後|さいご}まで{f|言|い}わ<b>ずじまい</b>だった。
In the end I never said it at all.
</pre>

`そびれる` - and its close relative <code>{f|損|そこ}なう</code>, as in
<code>{f|見|み}{f|損|そこ}なう</code> - can describe one blocked attempt in the
middle of a story, with more attempts still to come. `ずじまい` is the verdict at
the end of the story, and there is nothing after it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|連絡|れんらく}<b>しずじまい</b> → ✅ {f|連絡|れんらく}<b>せずじまい</b>
  As everywhere with `ず`, `する` becomes <code>せず</code>. There is no
  <code>しず</code> anywhere in Japanese.

- ❌ {f|読|よ}ま<b>ないじまい</b> → ✅ {f|読|よ}ま<b>ずじまい</b>
  The negative in this compound is the classical `ず`, not `ない`. The two are not
  interchangeable here.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|行|い}か<b>ずじまい</b>だ。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|行|い}か<b>ないつもりだ</b>。
  The pattern looks back at a chance already lost. It cannot announce a decision
  about tomorrow.

- ❌ {f|興味|きょうみ}がなかったので、{f|映画|えいが}は{f|見|み}<b>ずじまい</b>だった。 → ✅ {f|興味|きょうみ}がなかったので、{f|映画|えいが}は{f|見|み}<b>なかった</b>。
  No intention, no regret, no `ずじまい`. The first clause has removed the very
  thing the pattern needs.

## Practice

**1.** {f|温泉|おんせん}に{f|入|はい}ろうと{f|思|おも}っていたのに、{f|雨|あめ}で{f|入|はい}ら____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|入|はい}ら<b>ずじまいだった</b>。

<code>{f|入|はい}ろうと{f|思|おも}っていた</code> supplies the intention the pattern
requires, and the rain removed the opportunity. Without that first clause, plain
<code>{f|入|はい}らなかった</code> would be the natural sentence.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|名前|なまえ}を{f|聞|き}こうとしたが、{f|人|ひと}が{f|多|おお}くて（{f|聞|き}きそびれた・{f|聞|き}かずじまいだ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|人|ひと}が{f|多|おお}くて<b>{f|聞|き}きそびれた</b>。

This is one blocked attempt in the middle of an evening - the crowd got in the way
at that moment. `ずじまい` would be the verdict once the whole evening was over and
the chance had gone for good.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I carried my camera all the way up the mountain and never took a single photo."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

カメラを{f|山|やま}まで{f|持|も}って{f|行|い}ったのに、{f|一枚|いちまい}も{f|撮|と}ら<b>ずじまい</b>だった。

<code>のに</code> and the effort of carrying the camera up establish the intention.
<code>{f|一枚|いちまい}も</code> with a negative gives "not a single one", and the
`ずじまい` supplies the regret.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|準備|じゅんび}が{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わず、{f|発表|はっぴょう}はしずじまいになった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|準備|じゅんび}が{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わず、{f|発表|はっぴょう}は<b>せずじまい</b>になった。

`する` takes the irregular `せず`. Note that the same sentence already uses the
correct form earlier - <code>{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わず</code> - which is a useful
reminder that this `ず` is one thing wherever it appears.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}には{f|会|あ}わ<b>なかった</b>。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}には{f|会|あ}わ<b>ずじまい</b>だった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the bare fact, and it is neutral about why. It could even be deliberate -
you may have gone out of your way to avoid him.

(b) says you meant to see him, that circumstances kept getting in the way, and that
the visit is now not going to happen. The sentence sighs. It also implies a
finished period - a trip that has ended, a stay that is over - after which the
opportunity no longer exists.

</details>
