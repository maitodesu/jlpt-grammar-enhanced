# ～ことなく

**Meaning** · without ever doing it; without so much as doing it - the written, weighty way of saying "without"

`ことなく` says the same thing as `ずに`, at a different altitude. It belongs to
writing, and it carries a note of *not even once*: the runner never stopped, the
shop never changed its recipe, nobody ever found out. Use it where the absence is
remarkable. For "I went out without breakfast", it is far too grand.

## Formation

The verb goes in the **dictionary form**, and it stays affirmative - the negation
lives in `なく`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + ことなく | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}むことなく |
| Verb (する) | ～することなく | {f|変化|へんか}する → {f|変化|へんか}することなく |
| Passive | ～られることなく | {f|知|し}られる → {f|知|し}られることなく |
| Emphatic variant | ～ことなしに | {f|断|ことわ}ることなしに |
| "not without doing" | ～ことなしには～ない | {f|努力|どりょく}することなしには{f|続|つづ}かない |
| Often paired with | {f|一度|いちど}も／{f|最後|さいご}まで／{f|誰|だれ}にも | {f|一度|いちど}も{f|休|やす}むことなく |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the verb in front is **positive**.
❌<code>{f|休|やす}まないことなく</code> negates twice and means nothing. Compare
<code>{f|休|やす}まずに</code>, where the negation sits on the verb instead.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - `ことなく` takes verbs only. There is no
❌<code>{f|静|しず}かなことなく</code> and no ❌<code>{f|寒|さむ}いことなく</code>;
for adjectives and nouns you need a different construction altogether, such as
<code>～ではなく</code>.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|一度|いちど}も{f|休|やす}む<b>ことなく</b>{f|走|はし}った。
He ran the whole way without a single break.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|誰|だれ}にも{f|知|し}られる<b>ことなく</b>、{f|工事|こうじ}は{f|終|お}わった。
The work was completed without anyone finding out.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼女|かのじょ}は{f|迷|まよ}う<b>ことなく</b>、{f|一番|いちばん}{f|難|むずか}しい{f|道|みち}を{f|選|えら}んだ。
She chose the hardest road without a moment's hesitation.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}は30{f|年間|ねんかん}、{f|味|あじ}を{f|変|か}える<b>ことなく</b>{f|続|つづ}いている。
For thirty years this shop has carried on without ever changing its flavour.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}の{f|夜|よる}も、{f|工場|こうじょう}の{f|機械|きかい}は{f|止|と}まる<b>ことなく</b>{f|動|うご}き{f|続|つづ}けた。
Even on the night of the typhoon, the machines in the plant ran on without stopping.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|最後|さいご}まで{f|一言|ひとこと}も{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}う<b>ことなく</b>{f|作業|さぎょう}を{f|終|お}えた。
He finished the job without one word of complaint from beginning to end.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|争|あらそ}いが{f|再|ふたた}び{f|起|お}こる<b>ことなく</b>、{f|静|しず}かな{f|日々|ひび}が{f|続|つづ}いている。
Quiet days have continued, with the conflict never breaking out again.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|多|おお}くの{f|人|ひと}に{f|支|ささ}えられたからこそ、{f|途中|とちゅう}であきらめる<b>ことなく</b>ここまで{f|来|こ}られた。
It is only because so many people supported me that I got this far without giving up on the way.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is a **written** pattern, and a slightly ceremonial one. Its home is narration,
biography, company histories, reports, obituaries and the closing sentence of a
speech. In conversation it sounds like reading aloud; even a formal meeting will
usually reach for `ずに` instead.

Two consequences follow from that register, and both are practical.

First, `ことなく` prefers a **whole stretch of time**. It goes naturally with
{f|一度|いちど}も, {f|最後|さいご}まで, 30{f|年間|ねんかん},
{f|生涯|しょうがい} - anything that frames a long or complete span. A single
moment's action feels undersized: ❌<code>{f|窓|まど}を{f|閉|し}めることなく{f|出|で}かけた</code>
is not ungrammatical, but it dresses a small oversight in formal robes, and a
Japanese reader would find it faintly comic.

Second, the thing that did not happen must be **worth remarking on**. Something was
expected - a pause, an argument, an interruption, a discovery - and it never came.
That is what makes {f|止|と}まることなく or {f|知|し}られることなく carry weight,
and it is why the pattern so often appears alongside {f|続|つづ}ける,
{f|続|つづ}く and {f|終|お}える.

The variant `ことなしに` is a shade heavier again and is largely confined to
writing. Its most useful shape is the double negative
<code>～ことなしには～ない</code>, "you cannot Y without X":
{f|努力|どりょく}することなしには{f|上達|じょうたつ}しない. That frame is common
in essays and speeches, and it is worth recognising even if you never write one.

## Contrast

### ～ことなく vs ～ずに・～ないで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the same meaning, three registers. `ないで` is speech, `ずに` is
careful speech and writing, `ことなく` is writing with weight behind it.

</div>

<pre>
{f|鍵|かぎ}をかけ<b>ないで</b>{f|出|で}かけてしまった。
I went out without locking up.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会長|かいちょう}は{f|最後|さいご}まで{f|席|せき}を{f|立|た}つ<b>ことなく</b>{f|話|はなし}を{f|聞|き}いていた。
The chairman listened to the end without once leaving his seat.
</pre>

Swap them and both sentences fail on tone. A forgotten door lock does not deserve
`ことなく`, and a formal account of the chairman's attention would look thin with
`ないで`. Note also the conjugation difference, which is a fast way of telling them
apart: `ずに` and `ないで` sit on a **negative** stem, `ことなく` on the
**dictionary form**.

### ～ことなく vs ～ないまま

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ことなく` says something **never interrupted** what was going
on. `ないまま` says something is still **left undone**, and often that it should
not be.

</div>

<pre>
{f|理由|りゆう}を{f|聞|き}か<b>ないまま</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|去|さ}った。
He left the company with the reason still never asked.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|理由|りゆう}を{f|話|はな}す<b>ことなく</b>{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|去|さ}った。
He left the company without ever explaining why.
</pre>

`ないまま` leaves a loose end hanging, and the sentence usually feels unfinished on
purpose - something ought to have been settled and was not. `ことなく` simply
records that the action never occurred, and can be entirely neutral or even
admiring, as in {f|一度|いちど}も{f|休|やす}むことなく.

### ～ことなく vs ～ことなしに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ことなく` describes **how something happened**. `ことなしに`,
in its usual frame <code>～ことなしには～ない</code>, states a **condition**:
without this, that is impossible.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}は{f|振|ふ}り{f|返|かえ}る<b>ことなく</b>{f|歩|ある}いて{f|行|い}った。
He walked away without looking back.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|失敗|しっぱい}を{f|重|かさ}ねる<b>ことなしに</b>は、{f|本当|ほんとう}の{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}は{f|身|み}につかない。
Without a run of failures, no real skill is ever acquired.
</pre>

Used without the `は…ない` frame, `ことなしに` is simply a heavier `ことなく` and
the two are interchangeable. Used with it, the sentence becomes an argument rather
than a description, and `ことなく` cannot replace it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|休|やす}ま<b>ないことなく</b>{f|働|はたら}いた。 → ✅ {f|休|やす}む<b>ことなく</b>{f|働|はたら}いた。
  The negation is already in `なく`. The verb in front stays in the plain
  dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|休|やす}んだ<b>ことなく</b> → ✅ {f|休|やす}む<b>ことなく</b>
  Only the dictionary form appears here, whatever the tense of the main verb. The
  past sits at the end of the sentence.

- ❌ {f|朝|あさ}ごはんを{f|食|た}べる<b>ことなく</b>{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}た。 → ✅ {f|朝|あさ}ごはんを{f|食|た}べ<b>ずに</b>{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}た。
  Grammatical, but the register is absurdly high for skipping breakfast. Save
  `ことなく` for absences that are actually notable.

- ❌ {f|部屋|へや}が<b>{f|静|しず}かなことなく</b> → ✅ {f|部屋|へや}が{f|静|しず}かに<b>ならないまま</b>
  `ことなく` attaches to verbs only. Adjectives and nouns need another
  construction entirely.

## Practice

**1.** {f|雨|あめ}は{f|三日間|みっかかん}{f|降|ふ}り{f|続|つづ}き、{f|一度|いちど}も{f|止|と}む____{f|川|かわ}の{f|水位|すいい}が{f|上|あ}がっていった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|一度|いちど}も{f|止|と}む<b>ことなく</b>

<code>{f|一度|いちど}も</code> is the classic partner of this pattern, and three
days of unbroken rain is exactly the kind of long, complete span it wants. The verb
stays in the dictionary form {f|止|と}む.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|傘|かさ}を（{f|持|も}たずに・{f|持|も}つことなく）{f|出|で}かけて、びしょ{f|濡|ぬ}れになった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}た<b>ずに</b>{f|出|で}かけて、びしょ{f|濡|ぬ}れになった。

Forgetting an umbrella is a small, ordinary lapse, and `ずに` is the right size for
it. `ことなく` would give a rained-on afternoon the tone of a memorial address.
Note the form too: `ずに` needs the ない-stem {f|持|も}た.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "For fifty years she went on painting, without ever once holding an exhibition."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|彼女|かのじょ}は50{f|年間|ねんかん}、{f|一度|いちど}も{f|個展|こてん}を{f|開|ひら}く<b>ことなく</b>{f|描|か}き{f|続|つづ}けた。

A fifty-year span and the phrase "not once" are both signals for `ことなく`. Note
{f|開|ひら}く for holding an exhibition - the same kanji is {f|開|あ}ける when it
is a door being opened.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼|かれ}は{f|誰|だれ}にも{f|相談|そうだん}しないことなく、{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|決|き}めてしまった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼|かれ}は{f|誰|だれ}にも{f|相談|そうだん}する<b>ことなく</b>、{f|一人|ひとり}で{f|決|き}めてしまった。
"He decided it alone, without consulting anybody."

The `なく` already carries the negative, so the verb in front must be positive.
Writing both produces a double negative that a reader has to stop and untangle.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|返事|へんじ}をもらえ<b>ないまま</b>、{f|一年|いちねん}が{f|過|す}ぎた。
(b) {f|返事|へんじ}が{f|来|く}る<b>ことなく</b>、{f|一年|いちねん}が{f|過|す}ぎた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) leaves the matter open. The reply is still owed, and the sentence carries the
speaker's frustration at a thing that ought to have been settled.

(b) is more detached and more literary - a line of narration recording that no
reply arrived across the whole year. It closes the year off rather than leaving it
hanging, and it would fit a written account far better than a complaint to a
friend.

</details>
