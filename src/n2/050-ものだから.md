# ～ものだから

**Meaning** · because - the reason you put forward when you feel you owe the listener one

`ものだから` is a defence, not a neutral cause. You were late, you forgot, you did
something that needs accounting for, and this clause is the account: *the reason
was strong enough that anyone would have done the same*. N3 met the short reply
`もの／もん` ("because, that's why"); `ものだから` is that same instinct stretched
into a full subordinate clause, and it is one of the most useful things a learner
can have ready when apologising.

## Formation

Always written in kana. The clause in front is in plain form, and な-adjectives and
nouns link with `な` - `だ` is not possible.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + ものだから | {f|寝坊|ねぼう}した → {f|寝坊|ねぼう}したものだから |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + ものだから | {f|気|き}づかなかった → {f|気|き}づかなかったものだから |
| い-adjective | plain form + ものだから | {f|眠|ねむ}かった → {f|眠|ねむ}かったものだから |
| な-adjective | ～な + ものだから | {f|不安|ふあん}だ → {f|不安|ふあん}なものだから |
| Noun | ～な + ものだから | {f|初|はじ}めて → {f|初|はじ}めてなものだから |
| Casual | ～もんだから | {f|慌|あわ}てた → {f|慌|あわ}てたもんだから |
| Polite | ～ものですから | {f|急|きゅう}だ → {f|急|きゅう}なものですから |
| Left hanging | ～ものですから。 | {f|道|みち}が{f|混|こ}んでいたものですから。 |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the clause after `ものだから` reports a **fact**, normally one
that has already happened. It cannot be an order, a request, an invitation or a
statement of intention. Those all need plain `から`.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`ものだから` belongs to speech and to the spoken end of writing - a message to a
colleague, an explanation offered at the door. It is not used in reports, papers or
official notices, where <code>ため</code> does the same work without the emotional
colour.

The register ladder is worth memorising as a set: `もんだから` (friends),
`ものだから` (plain but not rough), `ものですから` (polite, and the standard shape of
a workplace apology). The polite one is often left hanging with nothing after it -
「{f|遅|おく}れてすみません。{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まっていたものですから。」 The
listener supplies the rest, and trailing off is part of the apology: you are not
going to insist on your excuse out loud.

What `ものだから` always adds is that the cause was **outsized** - too cold, too
sudden, too much. That is why it sits so comfortably with あまりに, とても and つい.
The flip side is that using it for a trivial or uncontroversial reason sounds
oddly defensive, as though you had expected to be told off and got in first.

One structural point trips people up. Because the clause is an account of something
that already stands, the main clause cannot be forward-looking: `ものだから` will not
introduce a request, an order, an invitation or a plan. If you want to *ask* for
something on the strength of a reason, use plain `から`, or the gentler
<code>もので</code>, which tolerates a set phrase like
<code>よろしくお{f|願|ねが}いします</code> after it.

## Contrast

### ～ものだから vs ～から

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `から` *states* a reason. `ものだから` *pleads* one.

</div>

<pre>
{f|熱|ねつ}があった<b>から</b>、{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|休|やす}みました。
I had a temperature, so I took the day off.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|熱|ねつ}があった<b>ものですから</b>、{f|会社|かいしゃ}をお{f|休|やす}みしました。
I had a temperature, so I'm afraid I took the day off.
</pre>

The first is information. The second is an apology with the information built in -
the speaker knows that being absent needed justifying. The other half of the
difference is grammatical: `から` will carry anything after it, including
{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けてください or {f|行|い}きましょう, and `ものだから` will not.

### ～ものだから vs ～もので

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - same job, different temperature. `もので` is deferential and
self-effacing; `ものだから` leans harder on the excuse.

</div>

<pre>
{f|不慣|ふな}れな<b>もので</b>、お{f|時間|じかん}をいただいてしまいました。
I'm new to this, so I've taken up more of your time than I should have.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|不慣|ふな}れな<b>ものだから</b>、{f|同|おな}じところで3{f|回|かい}も{f|間違|まちが}えた。
I'm new to it, so I made the same mistake three times over.
</pre>

`もので` and `ものですから` shade toward humility, which is why they survive in
customer-facing speech; `ものだから` keeps more of the "look, it really was like
that" energy. `もので` is also the more flexible of the two about what follows: a
polite formula after it is fine, where `ものだから` would clash.

### ～ものだから vs ～のだから

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ものだから` faces **inward** and excuses the speaker.
`のだから` faces **outward** and presses the listener: given this, obviously…

</div>

<pre>
{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|決|き}めた<b>のだから</b>、{f|最後|さいご}までやりなさい。
You decided this yourself, so see it through.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|決|き}めた<b>ものだから</b>、{f|誰|だれ}にも{f|相談|そうだん}しませんでした。
I'd decided it on my own, so I didn't consult anybody.
</pre>

`のだから` takes the premise as agreed between you and builds a demand or a firm
conclusion on it - which is exactly the sentence type `ものだから` refuses.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|暑|あつ}い<b>ものだから</b>、{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けてください。 → ✅ {f|暑|あつ}い<b>から</b>、{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}けてください。
  `ものだから` accounts for something already done. It cannot license a request.

- ❌ {f|不安|ふあん}<b>だものだから</b> → ✅ {f|不安|ふあん}<b>なものだから</b>
  な-adjectives and nouns link with `な`. <code>{f|学生|がくせい}だものだから</code> is
  wrong the same way; it has to be <code>{f|学生|がくせい}なものだから</code>.

- ❌ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>ものだから</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になるだろう。 → ✅ {f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}る<b>から</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になるだろう。
  The reason has to be an actual circumstance, not a forecast. `ものだから` cannot
  excuse something that has not happened yet.

- ❌ この{f|地域|ちいき}は{f|降水量|こうすいりょう}が{f|多|おお}い<b>ものだから</b>、{f|土砂|どしゃ}{f|災害|さいがい}が{f|起|お}こりやすい。 → ✅ …{f|多|おお}い<b>ため</b>、{f|土砂|どしゃ}{f|災害|さいがい}が{f|起|お}こりやすい。
  In a report or an article the personal, apologetic colour of `ものだから` is out of
  place. Written analysis uses <code>ため</code>.

## Practice

**1.** すみません、{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|遅|おく}れた____、{f|約束|やくそく}の{f|時間|じかん}に{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}いませんでした。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|遅|おく}れた<b>ものですから</b>

You are apologising, and the clause is your defence, so the excuse-flavoured
connector is what fits. The polite <code>ものですから</code> matches the ました ending
and the すみません in front of it.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|危|あぶ}ない（ものだから・から）、{f|手|て}すりにつかまってください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|危|あぶ}ない<b>から</b>、{f|手|て}すりにつかまってください。

The main clause is a request, and `ものだから` cannot introduce one. It only accounts
for a fact that already stands, so a following てください breaks the sentence.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "It's just that this is my first time doing it, so I got more nervous than I expected."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|初|はじ}めて<b>なものですから</b>、{f|思|おも}っていた{f|以上|いじょう}に{f|緊張|きんちょう}してしまいました。

A bare noun links to <code>もの</code> with `な`, never with `だ`. "It's just that"
is exactly the apologetic weight <code>ものですから</code> carries, and the polite
shape fits an explanation offered to someone you are not close to.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|忙|いそが}しかったものだから、{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|連絡|れんらく}するつもりです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|忙|いそが}しかったものだから、{f|連絡|れんらく}が{f|遅|おそ}くなってしまいました。
（or {f|忙|いそが}しい<b>ので</b>、{f|来週|らいしゅう}{f|連絡|れんらく}するつもりです。）

A statement of intention cannot follow `ものだから`. Either keep the excuse and put
an accomplished fact after it, or keep the plan and switch to `ので`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|子供|こども}が{f|生|う}まれた<b>ものですから</b>、{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}すことにしました。
(b) {f|子供|こども}が{f|生|う}まれた<b>のですから</b>、{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}したほうがいいですよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is the speaker explaining their own move to someone who might wonder about it -
"we had a baby, that's the reason." Nobody is being asked to do anything; the
sentence heads off the question before it is put.

(b) turns the same fact on the listener. `のですから` treats the birth as common
ground and pushes a recommendation off it: "you've had a baby, so you really ought
to move." Swap the two and both stop working - (a) cannot carry advice, and (b)
stops sounding like an account of the speaker's own situation.

</details>
