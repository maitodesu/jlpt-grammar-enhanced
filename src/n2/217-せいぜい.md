# せいぜい

**Meaning** · at most; at best - and the speaker thinks that is not much

`せいぜい` puts a ceiling on something. The number or the achievement that follows
is the *maximum*, and the word carries the speaker's judgement that the maximum is
unimpressive.

## Usage

`せいぜい` is an adverb. It stands in front of the quantity or the predicate it
caps, and it never changes shape.

Most often it caps an **estimate**, so it keeps company with the hedges that
estimates use - ぐらい, くらい, ほど, {f|程度|ていど}, だろう,
<code>しか～ない</code>:

- せいぜい{f|三人|さんにん}ぐらいだろう
- せいぜい{f|千円|せんえん}{f|程度|ていど}
- せいぜい{f|一週間|いっしゅうかん}しかもたない

It can also cap an outcome rather than a number:
<code>できるのはせいぜい{f|半分|はんぶん}だ</code>,
<code>せいぜい{f|時間|じかん}を{f|稼|かせ}ぐくらいのことしかできない</code>.

Because it names an upper limit, it cannot appear with expressions of *lower*
limit. ❌<code>せいぜい{f|三日|みっか}{f|以上|いじょう}</code> is a contradiction;
so is ❌<code>せいぜい{f|少|すく}なくとも</code>.

There is one more use to recognise. <code>せいぜい～てください</code> and
<code>せいぜい{f|頑張|がんば}りなさい</code> literally say "do it to the limit of
your ability", and older usage really was sincere encouragement - but in modern
Japanese it is heard as sarcasm: *knock yourself out, for all the good it will do*.
Learners meet it in dramas and take it for a kind word. It is not.

## Examples

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}は{f|狭|せま}くて、<b>せいぜい</b>{f|十人|じゅうにん}しか{f|入|はい}れない。
This place is small - ten people at the very most.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|直|なお}すのにかかるのは<b>せいぜい</b>{f|三十分|さんじゅっぷん}だろう。
Fixing it will take half an hour at the outside.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|給料|きゅうりょう}が{f|上|あ}がるといっても、<b>せいぜい</b>{f|数千円|すうせんえん}{f|程度|ていど}だ。
They say our pay is going up, but it'll be a few thousand yen at best.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|話|はなし}は{f|大|おお}きいけれど、あの{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|実際|じっさい}に{f|作|つく}れるのは<b>せいぜい</b>{f|試作品|しさくひん}までだろう。
They talk big, but the most that company can actually build is a prototype.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Conversational and neutral in politeness, but never neutral in attitude. `せいぜい`
always belittles a little: it says "no more than this, and this is not much". A
report that wants the same ceiling without the sneer writes
<code>{f|最大|さいだい}でも</code> or <code>{f|多|おお}くても</code>.

That edge matters when the ceiling is somebody else's.
<code>あの{f|人|ひと}にできるのはせいぜいそのくらいだ</code> is a put-down, not an
assessment. Applied to your own resources it is modest and perfectly safe:
<code>{f|出|だ}せるのはせいぜい{f|五千円|ごせんえん}です</code>.

The sarcastic imperative is the trap. Say
<code>せいぜい{f|楽|たの}しんでください</code> to someone leaving on holiday and you
have not wished them a good time - you have implied that the trip is a poor
consolation prize. The plain <code>{f|楽|たの}しんでください</code> is what you
wanted.

## Contrast

### せいぜい vs たかが

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `せいぜい` caps a **quantity**: at most this many.
`たかが` dismisses a **thing**: it's only a…, and it modifies a noun.

</div>

<pre>
{f|集|あつ}まったのは<b>せいぜい</b>{f|二十人|にじゅうにん}くらいだった。
Twenty people turned up, at the very most.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>たかが</b>ゲームじゃないか。そんなに{f|怒|おこ}るなよ。
It's only a game - don't get so worked up.
</pre>

The two share the dismissive tone, and they sit happily in the same breath -
<code>たかがメールだ。せいぜい{f|五分|ごふん}で{f|済|す}む</code>. But they are not
interchangeable. `たかが` takes a noun and belittles its
importance; `せいぜい` takes a measurement and caps it. `たかが` also has the fixed
frame <code>たかが～くらいで</code>, "just because of a mere…".

### せいぜい vs せめて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - opposite directions. `せいぜい` is an **upper** limit on what is
real; `せめて` is a **lower** limit on what the speaker wants.

</div>

<pre>
{f|参加者|さんかしゃ}は<b>せいぜい</b>{f|十人|じゅうにん}だろう。
There'll be ten participants at most.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>せめて</b>{f|十人|じゅうにん}は{f|集|あつ}めたい。
I want to get at least ten people together.
</pre>

The first is a prediction about reality and the speaker is unimpressed by it. The
second is a wish, scaled down from a bigger hope - and because it is a wish, it
needs a predicate of wanting, asking or intending. Swapping the two adverbs makes
both sentences ungrammatical rather than merely odd.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ せいぜい{f|一言|ひとこと}{f|謝|あやま}ってほしい。 → ✅ <b>せめて</b>{f|一言|ひとこと}{f|謝|あやま}ってほしい。
  A wish takes `せめて`. `せいぜい` estimates what will actually happen and cannot
  head a request.

- ❌ この{f|仕事|しごと}はせいぜい{f|三日|みっか}{f|以上|いじょう}かかる。 → ✅ この{f|仕事|しごと}は<b>せいぜい</b>{f|三日|みっか}で{f|終|お}わる。／この{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|少|すく}なくとも{f|三日|みっか}はかかる。
  <code>{f|以上|いじょう}</code> sets a floor and `せいぜい` sets a ceiling. One of
  the two has to go.

- ❌ {f|試験|しけん}、せいぜい{f|頑張|がんば}ってね。 → ✅ {f|試験|しけん}、{f|頑張|がんば}ってね。
  Meant as encouragement, heard as sarcasm. Leave the adverb out.

## Practice

**1.** あの{f|川|かわ}は{f|浅|あさ}くて、{f|深|ふか}いところでも____{f|一|いち}メートルぐらいだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|深|ふか}いところでも<b>せいぜい</b>{f|一|いち}メートルぐらいだ。

The sentence is capping a measurement and treating the figure as small, which is
exactly the job of `せいぜい`. Note the company it keeps: <code>でも</code> before
it and <code>ぐらい</code> after it, both typical of an estimate.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: {f|全部|ぜんぶ}は{f|無理|むり}でも、（せいぜい・せめて）{f|半分|はんぶん}は{f|終|お}わらせたい。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|全部|ぜんぶ}は{f|無理|むり}でも、<b>せめて</b>{f|半分|はんぶん}は{f|終|お}わらせたい。

<code>{f|終|お}わらせたい</code> is a wish, and the first clause has already given up
on the full amount. That is the `せめて` pattern: the ideal is out of reach, so the
speaker names a floor. `せいぜい` would turn the sentence into a gloomy forecast and
would clash with <code>たい</code>.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) この{f|仕事|しごと}は{f|三日|みっか}で{f|終|お}わる。
(b) この{f|仕事|しごと}はせいぜい{f|三日|みっか}で{f|終|お}わる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a flat estimate: three days.

(b) adds two things. It makes three days the *maximum* rather than the expected
figure, and it tells you what the speaker thinks of the job - small, quickly
disposed of, not worth the fuss someone is making about it. Said in front of the
person doing the work, that second layer can easily give offence.

</details>
