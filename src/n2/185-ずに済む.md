# ～ずに済む

**Meaning** · to get away without having to do it - the thing you were braced for turned out to be unnecessary

{f|済|す}む is "to be settled, to be done with". <code>ずに{f|済|す}む</code> therefore says that the
matter closed **without** the step you expected to have to take: no apology, no
surgery, no second trip. Relief is the whole point of the pattern. It reports an
outcome, not a permission.

## Formation

Take the ない-form, drop the `ない`, add <code>ずに{f|済|す}む</code>. The `ずに` behaves exactly as it
does on its own, irregular verbs included.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ～ない stem + ずに{f|済|す}む | {f|払|はら}わない → {f|払|はら}わずに{f|済|す}む |
| Verb (る-verb) | ～ない stem + ずに{f|済|す}む | {f|捨|す}てない → {f|捨|す}てずに{f|済|す}む |
| Verb (する) | **せずに{f|済|す}む** | {f|心配|しんぱい}する → {f|心配|しんぱい}せずに{f|済|す}む |
| Verb ({f|来|く}る) | <b>{f|来|こ}ずに{f|済|す}む</b> | {f|来|く}る → {f|来|こ}ずに{f|済|す}む |
| Everyday variants | ～ないで{f|済|す}む ／ ～なくて{f|済|す}む | {f|払|はら}わないで{f|済|す}む |
| Past - the usual shape | ～ずに{f|済|す}んだ | {f|払|はら}わずに{f|済|す}んだ |
| Noun | Noun + で{f|済|す}む | {f|電話|でんわ}で{f|済|す}む |
| Deliberately avoiding it | ～ずに{f|済|す}ませる ／ ～ずに{f|済|す}ます | {f|昼食|ちゅうしょく}を{f|取|と}らずに{f|済|す}ませた |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - ❌<code>しずに{f|済|す}む</code> is the error to watch for.
`する` takes the older stem `せ`, giving
<code>せずに{f|済|す}む</code> - the same `せ` as in
<code>せずに</code> and <code>せざるを{f|得|え}ない</code>.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|済|す}む</code> is intransitive: things settle themselves.
Its transitive partner <code>{f|済|す}ませる</code> means you settled them, on
purpose. That difference carries straight over into this pattern, and it is the one
place where learners produce a sentence that is grammatical but says the wrong
thing.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}を{f|買|か}わ<b>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</b>。
I got away without having to buy an umbrella.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|手術|しゅじゅつ}はせ<b>ずに{f|済|す}みました</b>。
In the end I didn't need surgery.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|早|はや}めに{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}たので、{f|走|はし}ら<b>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</b>。
I left the house early, so I didn't have to run after all.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|割引券|わりびきけん}があったから、{f|千円|せんえん}しか{f|払|はら}わ<b>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</b>。

</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}が{f|事情|じじょう}を{f|説明|せつめい}してくれて、{f|私|わたし}から{f|謝|あやま}ら<b>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</b>。

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

A new station opened nearby, so I no longer have to catch the bus every morning.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事前|じぜん}に{f|連絡|れんらく}しておいたおかげで、{f|受付|うけつけ}で{f|長|なが}く{f|待|ま}た<b>ずに{f|済|す}みました</b>。
Because I had got in touch beforehand, I didn't have to wait long at reception.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The three variants differ only in texture. <code>ずに{f|済|す}む</code> is the tightest and slightly
the most written; <code>ないで{f|済|す}む</code> is the everyday spoken form; <code>なくて{f|済|す}む</code> sits
between them and is very common. All three are ordinary Japanese and none is
formal in the way that <code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> is - you can say any of
them to a friend.

What the pattern always carries is **relief**. There was a cost - money, time,
embarrassment, a difficult conversation - hanging over the situation, and it did
not fall. That is why the past <code>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</code> is by far the
commonest form: you usually only notice the escape once it has happened. Used about
something the speaker wanted to do, the sentence becomes odd:
❌<code>{f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}かずに{f|済|す}んだ</code> tells your listener
the trip was a burden.

The relief can also be about **scale** rather than avoidance, and this is where the
noun pattern comes in. <code>{f|軽|かる}いけがで{f|済|す}んだ</code> - "it was only
a light injury" - and <code>{f|電話|でんわ}で{f|済|す}む{f|話|はなし}</code> -
"that could be settled with a phone call" - both say that the situation ended more
cheaply than it might have. <code>{f|済|す}む</code> is the same verb doing the
same job.

One useful frame for softening a request:
<code>～ずに{f|済|す}むように</code>, "so that you won't have to…". It is a kind way
of framing advice, because it puts the listener's convenience in the sentence:
{f|二度手間|にどでま}にならずに{f|済|す}むように、{f|先|さき}に{f|確認|かくにん}しておきます.

## Contrast

### ～ずに{f|済|す}む vs ～なくてもいい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なくてもいい` grants **permission in advance**: you don't have
to. <code>ずに{f|済|す}む</code> reports an **outcome**: as it turned out, I didn't
have to.

</div>

<pre>
{f|明日|あした}は{f|来|こ}<b>なくてもいい</b>ですよ。
You don't need to come in tomorrow.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|中止|ちゅうし}になって、{f|昨日|きのう}は{f|来|こ}<b>ずに{f|済|す}みました</b>。
The meeting was called off, so in the end I didn't have to come in yesterday.
</pre>

The first is somebody giving you permission, and it is what a manager says. The
second describes how things fell out, with a note of relief that permission has
nothing to do with. This is also why
<code>なくてもいい</code> works in the present about the future, while
<code>ずに{f|済|す}む</code> gravitates to the past.

### ～ずに{f|済|す}む vs ～ずにいる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>ずに{f|済|す}む</code> = the **need** went away.
`ずにいる` = the **action** has not happened, and often the speaker wishes it had.

</div>

<pre>
{f|忙|いそが}しくて、{f|三日|みっか}も{f|連絡|れんらく}せ<b>ずにいる</b>。
I've been so busy I've gone three days without getting in touch.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}が{f|伝|つた}えてくれたので、{f|連絡|れんらく}せ<b>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</b>。
He passed the message on, so I didn't have to make the call at all.
</pre>

The first is a state of not-doing that is still hanging over the speaker; the
obligation has not gone anywhere. The second closes the matter. If you can add
"thank goodness" to the English, you want <code>{f|済|す}む</code>.

### ～ずに{f|済|す}む vs ～ずに{f|済|す}ませる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|済|す}む</code> is intransitive: **it worked out**
that way. <code>{f|済|す}ませる</code> is transitive: **I arranged** for it not to
happen.

</div>

<pre>
{f|昼|ひる}は{f|時間|じかん}がなくて、パンだけで{f|済|す}ませた。
I had no time at lunch, so I made do with just some bread.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|昼|ひる}は{f|来客|らいきゃく}が{f|早|はや}く{f|帰|かえ}って、{f|外|そと}に{f|出|で}<b>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</b>。
The visitor left early, so I didn't have to go out at lunchtime.
</pre>

In the first the speaker made the choice, and the sentence often carries a hint of
cutting corners. In the second circumstances did the work, and the speaker is
simply glad. Choosing the wrong one flips who is responsible - a small difference
in the verb, a large one in what you are admitting to.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|心配|しんぱい}<b>しずに{f|済|す}んだ</b> → ✅ {f|心配|しんぱい}<b>せずに{f|済|す}んだ</b>
  `する` uses the stem `せ` in front of `ずに`. This is the same irregularity you
  already met in <code>せずに</code>.

- ❌ {f|払|はら}う<b>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</b> → ✅ {f|払|はら}わ<b>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</b>
  The base is the ない-stem, not the dictionary form.

- ❌ {f|来|き}<b>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</b> → ✅ {f|来|こ}<b>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</b>
  {f|来|く}る is irregular here, exactly as it is in {f|来|こ}ない.

- ❌ {f|楽|たの}しみにしていた{f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}か<b>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</b>。 → ✅ {f|楽|たの}しみにしていた{f|旅行|りょこう}に{f|行|い}けなくなった。
  The pattern means you were spared something. Applied to a treat, it says the trip
  was a chore you escaped.

- ❌ {f|夕食|ゆうしょく}はおにぎりで<b>{f|済|す}んだ</b>。（meaning "I made do with a rice ball"） → ✅ {f|夕食|ゆうしょく}はおにぎりで<b>{f|済|す}ませた</b>。
  If you chose it, the verb is transitive.

## Practice

**1.** {f|保険|ほけん}に{f|入|はい}っていたおかげで、{f|修理代|しゅうりだい}を{f|自分|じぶん}で{f|払|はら}わ____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|払|はら}わ<b>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</b>（or {f|払|はら}わずに{f|済|す}みました）

The insurance removed a cost the speaker had been expecting to bear, which is
exactly the relief this pattern reports. The past form is the natural one, because
the escape is already an accomplished fact.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|明日|あした}は{f|祝日|しゅくじつ}だから、{f|会社|かいしゃ}に（{f|行|い}かなくてもいい・{f|行|い}かずに{f|済|す}んだ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|会社|かいしゃ}に<b>{f|行|い}かなくてもいい</b>。

Tomorrow has not happened yet, so there is no outcome to report. The sentence is
stating that the obligation does not apply, which is
<code>なくてもいい</code>. <code>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</code> would need the day to be
over.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The rain stopped just in time, so we didn't have to cancel the barbecue."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ちょうど{f|雨|あめ}がやんだので、バーベキューを{f|中止|ちゅうし}せ<b>ずに{f|済|す}みました</b>。

{f|中止|ちゅうし}する is a する-verb, so the stem is <code>せ</code>. Note that the
relief here is about avoiding cancellation - the pattern attaches to the unwanted
action, not to the barbecue.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|傷|きず}は{f|浅|あさ}かったので、{f|病院|びょういん}に{f|行|い}くずに{f|済|す}ませました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|傷|きず}は{f|浅|あさ}かったので、{f|病院|びょういん}に{f|行|い}か<b>ずに{f|済|す}みました</b>。
"The cut was shallow, so I didn't have to go to hospital."

Two repairs. The base is the ない-stem {f|行|い}か, and the verb should be the
intransitive <code>{f|済|す}む</code> - the shallow cut decided the matter, not
the speaker.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|今日|きょう}は{f|残業|ざんぎょう}し<b>なくてもよかった</b>。
(b) {f|今日|きょう}は{f|残業|ざんぎょう}せ<b>ずに{f|済|す}んだ</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) says the obligation was not there - nobody required overtime today. It is a
statement about the rules of the day, and it can even carry a shade of regret, as
in "I needn't have stayed late after all."

(b) says the speaker got out of overtime they had been expecting, and it is warm
with relief. Something specific made it unnecessary - the work finished, a meeting
moved - and the listener will assume there was such a reason.

</details>
