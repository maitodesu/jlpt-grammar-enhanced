# そういえば

**Meaning** · come to think of it; that reminds me - flags that what somebody just
said has jogged something loose in your memory

Literally "if one says that", `そういえば` credits the previous remark with the
thought that follows. It is the conversational marker for an association: not a new
topic dropped in from nowhere, but one the last few seconds of talk pulled up.

## Usage

`そういえば` opens a sentence and never changes form. The `そう` is the ordinary
そ-series demonstrative pointing at what was just said, and the shape is the
conditional of <code>{f|言|い}う</code>, so it is occasionally written
<code>そう{f|言|い}えば</code>.

What follows it is usually one of three things:

- **a question** - そういえば、あの{f|件|けん}はどうなりましたか。
- **a remembered fact**, very often with `ね` or `な` - そういえば、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|燃|も}えるごみの{f|日|ひ}だったな。
- **a half-remembered one**, with `っけ` or `かな` - そういえば、{f|鍵|かぎ}は{f|閉|し}めたっけ。

There is a longer variant, <code>そういえば{f|話|はなし}は{f|変|か}わりますが</code>,
used when the association is loose enough that you want to apologise for it, and a
polite fixed opener <code>そういえば、{f|先日|せんじつ}は…</code> for raising
something from a previous meeting.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>そういえば</b>、{f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|来|こ}ないの?
Come to think of it, isn't Tanaka coming?
</pre>

<pre>
<b>そういえば</b>、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|会社|かいしゃ}に{f|忘|わす}れてきた。
That reminds me - I left my umbrella at the office.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|京都|きょうと}の{f|話|はなし}が{f|出|で}たが、<b>そういえば</b>{f|去年|きょねん}{f|一緒|いっしょ}に{f|行|い}った{f|店|みせ}はまだあるだろうか。
Kyoto came up, and it makes me wonder whether that place we went to last year is still there.
</pre>

<pre>
「{f|来週|らいしゅう}から{f|新|あたら}しい{f|方|かた}が{f|入|はい}ります」「<b>そういえば</b>、{f|面接|めんせつ}にいらしていた{f|方|かた}ですか」
"Someone new starts next week." "Ah - would that be the person who came for the interview?"
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Conversational and friendly, and usable at any politeness level: the word itself
does not change, and the sentence after it carries the register. With `ます` and
`です` around it, `そういえば` is perfectly acceptable with a manager or a client -
<code>そういえば、{f|資料|しりょう}の{f|件|けん}はいかがでしょうか</code> - and it is
a genuinely useful way to raise a small item without making a production of it.

Its social value is that it shares the credit. By saying "what you just said
reminded me", you make your new remark a continuation of theirs rather than an
interruption, which is why it softens what would otherwise be a change of subject.
Speakers use it that way slightly dishonestly all the time, attaching it to
something they had been meaning to bring up anyway.

The limit is that the association has to be *findable*. If the listener cannot see
any connection at all between the previous line and yours, `そういえば` misfires and
sounds either absent-minded or evasive. When there is genuinely no link, `ところで`
is the honest word.

In writing it appears in essays, blogs and letters - anywhere that imitates a
speaking voice - and not in reports or formal documents.

## Contrast

### そういえば vs そうだ・そうそう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `そういえば` means **you reminded me**. `そうだ` and `そうそう`
mean **I've just remembered**, with or without any prompt.

</div>

<pre>
<b>そういえば</b>、{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}すって{f|言|い}ってたよね。
Come to think of it, you said you were moving next month, didn't you.
</pre>

<pre>
あ、<b>そうだ</b>、{f|牛乳|ぎゅうにゅう}{f|買|か}わなきゃ。
Oh - that's right, I need to buy milk.
</pre>

The milk has nothing to do with anything anybody said; it simply surfaced. `そうだ`
and `そうそう` therefore work in silence, walking down the street alone, where
`そういえば` would have nothing to point at. Conversely, when the trigger *was* the
other person's remark, `そうだ` throws away the connection and can sound as though
you had stopped listening.

<code>そうそう</code> has a second job worth keeping separate: as a response it means
"yes, exactly", agreeing enthusiastically with what was just said.

### そういえば vs ところで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `そういえば` follows a **thread**. `ところで` cuts one and
starts a new one.

</div>

<pre>
<b>そういえば</b>、その{f|映画|えいが}の{f|監督|かんとく}、{f|新|あたら}しい{f|作品|さくひん}を{f|撮|と}ったそうですよ。
Speaking of which, I hear that film's director has made something new.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>ところで</b>、{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|件|けん}なんですが。
Anyway - about next week's meeting.
</pre>

`ところで` announces the break openly, which is honest and slightly abrupt; it is the
right word when you need to move business along. `そういえば` hides the break inside
an association, which is gentler but only works if the association exists. Using
`そういえば` for a genuinely unrelated topic is a small, transparent piece of theatre
and native speakers notice it.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ そういえば、はじめまして。{f|山田|やまだ}と{f|申|もう}します。 → ✅ はじめまして。{f|山田|やまだ}と{f|申|もう}します。
  Nothing has been said yet, so there is nothing for `そういえば` to point back to.
  It cannot open a conversation.

- ❌ そういえば、{f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}の{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りは{f|明日|あした}です。（as the main point of an email）→ ✅ {f|報告書|ほうこくしょ}の{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りは{f|明日|あした}です。
  `そういえば` frames what follows as an incidental thought. Attached to the actual
  purpose of the message it reads as either careless or passive-aggressive.

- ❌ そういえた、{f|明日|あした}は{f|休|やす}みだったね。 → ✅ <b>そういえば</b>、{f|明日|あした}は{f|休|やす}みだったね。
  It is a frozen phrase. The <code>{f|言|い}えば</code> inside it never becomes
  <code>{f|言|い}えた</code> or <code>{f|言|い}います</code>.

## Practice

**1.** 「{f|北海道|ほっかいどう}の{f|雪|ゆき}はすごいですよ」「____、{f|去年|きょねん}{f|札幌|さっぽろ}に{f|行|い}ったとき{f|大変|たいへん}でした」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>そういえば</b>、{f|去年|きょねん}{f|札幌|さっぽろ}に{f|行|い}ったとき{f|大変|たいへん}でした。

The other speaker's remark about Hokkaido snow is what pulled the memory up, and the
reply builds on it rather than replacing it. Note that the polite ending is
unaffected: `そういえば` sits comfortably in front of a です・ます sentence.

</details>

**2.** Choose: （そういえば・ところで）、{f|話|はなし}は{f|変|か}わりますが、{f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|予算|よさん}の{f|件|けん}はどうなりましたか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>ところで</b>、{f|話|はなし}は{f|変|か}わりますが…

The speaker openly announces a change of subject, and the budget has nothing to do
with what came before. `そういえば` would claim an association that the sentence
itself denies two words later.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) 「{f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}らしいよ」「<b>そういえば</b>、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|直|なお}してもらわないと」
(b) 「{f|明日|あした}は{f|雨|あめ}らしいよ」「あ、<b>そうだ</b>、{f|傘|かさ}を{f|直|なお}してもらわないと」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) presents the umbrella as something the rain forecast brought to mind. The two
remarks are linked, and the speaker is visibly following the other person's lead.

(b) presents it as a sudden recollection of the speaker's own. It may well have been
triggered by the same remark, but `そうだ` does not say so, and the effect is a
slightly more self-absorbed turn - the speaker jumping to their own errand rather
than continuing the conversation. Neither is rude; (a) is the more sociable of the
two.

</details>
