# ～にもかかわらず

**Meaning** · despite; in spite of; and yet - a fact is stated, and something that flatly contradicts it is stated next

`にもかかわらず` is the heavyweight of written concession. It literally says "without
this being involved": the first fact ought to have determined the outcome, and the
outcome ignored it. Because it is long, formal and unmistakable, it is what reports,
articles and speeches use when the mismatch is the point of the sentence.

On this batch's two axes - how much the speaker minds, and how formal the frame is - 
`にもかかわらず` is *pointedly aware that something is wrong* and *at the formal end*.
It is the pattern that criticises without raising its voice - and, in a fixed polite
formula, the pattern that thanks.

## Formation

It attaches to a plain form or directly to a noun. な-adjectives and nouns take
`である` when a clause reading is wanted.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + にもかかわらず | {f|雨|あめ} → {f|雨|あめ}にもかかわらず |
| Verb | plain form + にもかかわらず | {f|反対|はんたい}する → {f|反対|はんたい}するにもかかわらず |
| Verb (past) | plain past + にもかかわらず | {f|注意|ちゅうい}した → {f|注意|ちゅうい}したにもかかわらず |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + にもかかわらず | {f|届|とど}かない → {f|届|とど}かないにもかかわらず |
| い-adjective | plain form + にもかかわらず | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}いにもかかわらず |
| な-adjective | stem + である + にもかかわらず | {f|不利|ふり} → {f|不利|ふり}であるにもかかわらず |
| Noun, as a clause | Noun + である + にもかかわらず | {f|初日|しょにち} → {f|初日|しょにち}であるにもかかわらず |
| Sentence opener | にもかかわらず、… | - → にもかかわらず、{f|決定|けってい}は{f|覆|くつがえ}らなかった |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - one `も` decides everything. `にもかかわらず` = **despite the fact
that** (X is true, and Y happened anyway). `にかかわらず` = **regardless of whether**
(X may or may not be true; Y holds either way). They are not variants of each other.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>お{f|忙|いそが}しいにもかかわらず</code> is a set opening of thanks in
speeches, e-mails and ceremonies: "despite your busy schedule". It is one of the very
few places this pattern carries warmth rather than criticism, and it is worth learning
whole.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|大勢|おおぜい}の{f|人|ひと}が{f|集|あつ}まった。
A large crowd turned out despite the rain.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|努力|どりょく}した<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|結果|けっか}は{f|出|で}なかった。
For all the effort put in, there were no results.
</pre>

<pre>
お{f|忙|いそが}しい<b>にもかかわらず</b>、ご{f|出席|しゅっせき}いただきありがとうございます。
Thank you for attending despite your busy schedule.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}が{f|高|たか}い<b>にもかかわらず</b>、この{f|商品|しょうひん}はよく{f|売|う}れている。
This product sells well despite its high price.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|何度|なんど}も{f|注意|ちゅうい}した<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|同|おな}じミスがくり{f|返|かえ}された。
The same mistake was repeated despite repeated warnings.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|医師|いし}の{f|警告|けいこく}<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|彼|かれ}はたばこをやめようとしない。
He will not give up smoking, whatever his doctor tells him.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|初日|しょにち}である<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|会場|かいじょう}は{f|半分|はんぶん}も{f|埋|う}まっていなかった。

</pre>

<pre>

{f|事前|じぜん}に{f|連絡|れんらく}してあった<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|部屋|へや}の{f|準備|じゅんび}はまったくできていなかった。
We had let them know in advance, and yet the room had not been prepared at all.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

This is written Japanese and formal spoken Japanese. It appears in newspapers, annual
reports, academic writing, legal documents, complaint letters and the opening lines of
speeches. In relaxed conversation it is far too heavy - a friend describing a wet
festival says <code>{f|雨|あめ}なのに</code>, not
<code>{f|雨|あめ}にもかかわらず</code>.

Emotionally it is not neutral, although it is controlled. Because the pattern
foregrounds the mismatch, the reader is invited to find the mismatch improper: *warnings
were given, and the mistake happened anyway*. That makes it the natural vehicle for
formal criticism, and it is why so many `にもかかわらず` sentences appear in the passive
and name no culprit.

The exception is the thanks formula. <code>お{f|忙|いそが}しいところ</code> and
<code>お{f|忙|いそが}しいにもかかわらず</code> both acknowledge that the listener has
gone out of their way, and there the surprise is flattering rather than accusing.

Two structural facts worth remembering. First, it can open a sentence on its own, as a
conjunction meaning "nevertheless" - handy in written argument. Second, it cannot end
one: unlike `のに`, it has no dangling, reproachful use. If you want the sentence to
trail off in complaint, you want a different pattern.

## Contrast

### にもかかわらず vs にかかわらず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - with `も`: **despite** a fact that is true. Without `も`:
**regardless of** a variable that may go either way.

</div>

<pre>
{f|悪天候|あくてんこう}<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|行|おこな}われた。
The match was played despite the bad weather.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|天候|てんこう}<b>にかかわらず</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|行|おこな}われます。
The match will be played whatever the weather.
</pre>

The first reports what happened on one particular rainy day. The second announces a
policy that ignores the weather entirely, and the noun in front of it is the *variable*
(weather), not a *value* of it. That is the reliable test: if you can put "good or
bad", "large or small", "yes or no" after the noun, you want `にかかわらず`.

### にもかかわらず vs のに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical logic, opposite registers. `のに` is spoken and
emotional; `にもかかわらず` is written and formal.

</div>

<pre>
{f|予約|よやく}した<b>のに</b>、{f|席|せき}がなかったんですよ。
We'd booked, and there wasn't a table!
</pre>

<pre>
{f|予約|よやく}が{f|入|はい}っていた<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|席|せき}は{f|用意|ようい}されていなかった。
No table had been prepared, despite the reservation on record.
</pre>

The first is the story told to a friend; the second is the complaint sent to head
office. Note the second's passive and its impersonality - the register pulls the whole
sentence with it, not just the joint.

### にもかかわらず vs ものの

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both are written concessives. `にもかかわらず` points at a
**contradiction** and takes nouns. `ものの` reports a **shortfall** and needs a clause.

</div>

<pre>
{f|全員|ぜんいん}の{f|反対|はんたい}<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|実行|じっこう}された。
The plan was carried out in the teeth of unanimous opposition.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|計画|けいかく}は{f|実行|じっこう}された<b>ものの</b>、{f|成果|せいか}はほとんどなかった。
The plan was carried out, but it achieved next to nothing.
</pre>

`ものの` cannot take the bare noun <code>{f|反対|はんたい}</code> the way the first
sentence does, and `にもかかわらず` would sound overheated in the second, where nothing
is being condemned - only reported as disappointing.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}<b>だにもかかわらず</b> → ✅ {f|雨|あめ}<b>にもかかわらず</b>（or {f|雨|あめ}であるにもかかわらず）
  A noun attaches directly. `だ` never appears in front of this pattern.

- ❌ {f|年齢|ねんれい}<b>にもかかわらず</b>{f|応募|おうぼ}できます。 → ✅ {f|年齢|ねんれい}<b>にかかわらず</b>{f|応募|おうぼ}できます。
  Age here is the variable, not an obstacle that was overcome. Dropping the `も` is
  what turns "despite" into "regardless of".

- ❌ {f|不利|ふり}<b>にもかかわらず</b> → ✅ {f|不利|ふり}<b>であるにもかかわらず</b>
  な-adjectives need `である`. Without it the phrase reads as a bare noun and the
  sentence goes vague.

- ❌（chatting）{f|走|はし}った<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|遅|おく}れちゃった。 → ✅ {f|走|はし}った<b>のに</b>、{f|遅|おく}れちゃった。
  The contracted <code>{f|遅|おく}れちゃった</code> is casual, and `にもかかわらず` is
  not. Register has to match across the whole sentence.

## Practice

**1.** {f|厳|きび}しい{f|条件|じょうけん}____、{f|多|おお}くの{f|応募|おうぼ}があった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|厳|きび}しい{f|条件|じょうけん}<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|多|おお}くの{f|応募|おうぼ}があった。

The tough conditions should have deterred applicants and did not, so this is a
contradiction rather than an irrelevance. A noun attaches with no copula.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|経験|けいけん}の{f|有無|うむ}（にかかわらず・にもかかわらず）、どなたでも{f|参加|さんか}できます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|経験|けいけん}の{f|有無|うむ}<b>にかかわらず</b>、どなたでも{f|参加|さんか}できます。

<code>{f|有無|うむ}</code> means "whether or not there is", which is a variable with two
values - the textbook environment for `にかかわらず`. Nothing is being overcome here, so
the `も` would be wrong.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, formally: "Despite having received the report three weeks earlier, the department took no action."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|三週間|さんしゅうかん}{f|前|まえ}に{f|報告|ほうこく}を{f|受|う}けていた<b>にもかかわらず</b>、{f|担当|たんとう}{f|部署|ぶしょ}は{f|何|なん}の{f|対応|たいおう}も{f|取|と}らなかった。

A past-tense clause attaches directly, and the impersonal main clause keeps the whole
sentence in the register of a written finding. This is `にもかかわらず` doing its
commonest job: formal criticism.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|会員|かいいん}だにもかかわらず、{f|割引|わりびき}が{f|受|う}けられなかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|会員|かいいん}<b>であるにもかかわらず</b>、{f|割引|わりびき}が{f|受|う}けられなかった。
（also acceptable: {f|会員|かいいん}<b>にもかかわらず</b>）

`だ` cannot stand in front of `にもかかわらず`. Use `である` when you want the copula
audible, or attach the noun bare.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|国籍|こくせき}<b>にかかわらず</b>{f|応募|おうぼ}できます。
(b) {f|外国|がいこく}{f|籍|せき}である<b>にもかかわらず</b>{f|採用|さいよう}された。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) sets out a rule. Nationality is a variable, and the sentence says that whatever
value it takes, the door is open. There is no surprise and no obstacle - only a
declared irrelevance.

(b) reports one case, and treats the nationality as something that would normally have
stood in the way. The `も` carries the implication that this outcome was against
expectation, which is why (b) reads as a comment on an unusual event and (a) as a line
from a recruitment notice.

</details>
