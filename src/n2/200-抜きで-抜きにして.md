# ～抜きで / ～抜きにして

**Meaning** · without; leaving out; skipping - takes out something that would normally be part of the package

<code>{f|抜|ぬ}く</code> is "to pull out, to omit", and <code>{f|抜|ぬ}き</code> is its
noun. The word in front names a **component that is ordinarily there** - wasabi in
the sushi, breakfast in the morning, preamble before the business, the boss at the
drinks - and the pattern removes it on purpose. That word *ordinarily* is doing real
work: if the thing was never part of the arrangement, this is not the pattern for it.

## Formation

The noun attaches **bare** - no `を`, no `の` in front of
<code>{f|抜|ぬ}き</code> - and only a noun will do.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + {f|抜|ぬ}きで | わさび → わさび{f|抜|ぬ}きで |
| Noun (a proposal to skip it) | Noun + は{f|抜|ぬ}きにして | {f|冗談|じょうだん} → {f|冗談|じょうだん}は{f|抜|ぬ}きにして |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + {f|抜|ぬ}きの + Noun | {f|砂糖|さとう} → {f|砂糖|さとう}{f|抜|ぬ}きのコーヒー |
| As the predicate | Noun + {f|抜|ぬ}きだ | {f|昼食|ちゅうしょく} → {f|今日|きょう}は{f|昼食|ちゅうしょく}{f|抜|ぬ}きだ |
| "could not have, without it" | Noun + {f|抜|ぬ}きには / {f|抜|ぬ}きでは … ない | {f|協力|きょうりょく} → {f|協力|きょうりょく}{f|抜|ぬ}きには{f|成功|せいこう}しない |
| Bare compound (an order) | Noun + {f|抜|ぬ}き | ネギ → ネギ{f|抜|ぬ}き |

<div class="note">

**Note** - the last row is what you actually say across a counter.
<code>ネギ{f|抜|ぬ}きで</code> or just <code>ネギ{f|抜|ぬ}き</code> is a complete,
polite-enough request when it follows the name of the dish, and no Japanese speaker
will hear it as brusque.

</div>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

<pre>

</pre>

Without all of your help, this event would never have come together.
</pre>

The talk turned to going for a drink without the boss, and all of a sudden we had to find somewhere.
</pre>

<pre>

Handed the conclusion out of nowhere with no explanation, I have no idea what I am supposed to make of it.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Everyday and neutral. Ordering food is where most learners first meet it, and there
it is completely unmarked. Nothing about
<code>{f|抜|ぬ}き</code> is formal or literary.

<code>～は{f|抜|ぬ}きにして</code> is a **proposal**, and a brisk one: "let's drop
that and move on". <code>{f|前置|まえお}きは{f|抜|ぬ}きにして</code> and
<code>{f|堅|かた}い{f|話|はなし}は{f|抜|ぬ}きにして</code> are the standard ways of
cutting to the chase, and they are friendly rather than rude - the speaker is
proposing to save everybody time. <code>{f|冗談|じょうだん}{f|抜|ぬ}きで</code> as an
adverb is "seriously, no joke", and is very common in casual speech.

<code>～{f|抜|ぬ}きには…ない</code> pulls in the other direction: it is formal, and it
is the standard shape of thanks in a speech or an acknowledgement -
<code>{f|皆|みな}さまのお{f|力|ちから}{f|抜|ぬ}きには</code>. Note that it **requires a
negative** at the end; the frame is built to say that the thing would not have
happened.

One point of care. With a **person**, <code>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</code> means excluding them,
and it can land pointedly - <code>{f|課長|かちょう}{f|抜|ぬ}きで</code> tells the
listener that the exclusion was deliberate. That is often exactly what the speaker
means, but it is not a neutral way to describe who happened to be free.

## Contrast

### ～{f|抜|ぬ}きで vs ～なしで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `なしで` is plain **absence**: the thing simply is not there.
<code>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</code> is **removal**: the thing would normally be included and
somebody took it out.

</div>

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}<b>なしで</b>{f|出|で}かけたら、ひどい{f|目|め}にあった。
I went out without an umbrella and had a miserable time of it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|氷|こおり}<b>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</b>お{f|願|ねが}いします。
No ice, please.
</pre>

An umbrella is not a standard component of leaving the house, so `なしで` is right.
Ice is a standard component of the drink, so leaving it out is
<code>{f|抜|ぬ}き</code>. Where the thing genuinely is part of the package both are
possible, and <code>{f|抜|ぬ}き</code> then sounds like an instruction:
<code>{f|砂糖|さとう}なしのコーヒー</code> describes the cup, while
<code>{f|砂糖|さとう}{f|抜|ぬ}きのコーヒー</code> sounds like what you asked for.

`なしで` also takes nouns that nobody could remove -
<code>{f|許可|きょか}なしで{f|入|はい}る</code>, "enter without permission" - where
<code>{f|抜|ぬ}き</code> is impossible.

### ～{f|抜|ぬ}きで vs ～を{f|除|のぞ}いて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code> marks an **exception to a
statement**. <code>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</code> says the **activity was carried out** with the
item taken out.

</div>

<pre>
{f|日曜日|にちようび}<b>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</b>{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|開|あ}いています。
We are open every day except Sunday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|日曜日|にちようび}<b>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</b>{f|予定|よてい}を{f|組|く}んでもらえますか。
Could you draw up the schedule with Sunday left out of it?
</pre>

The first sentence is about the scope of a claim - which days the claim covers. The
second is about how a piece of work was done.
<code>を{f|除|のぞ}いて</code> is also stiffer, and it will not take
<code>{f|冗談|じょうだん}</code> or <code>{f|遠慮|えんりょ}</code>, because those are not
members of a list you are quantifying over.

### ～{f|抜|ぬ}きで vs ～ないで・～ずに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ないで` and `ずに` attach to a **verb**: you did not perform the
action. <code>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</code> attaches to a **noun**: the slot was left empty.

</div>

<pre>
{f|朝|あさ}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べ<b>ないで</b>{f|学校|がっこう}へ{f|行|い}った。
I went to school without eating breakfast.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|朝|あさ}ご{f|飯|はん}<b>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</b>{f|学校|がっこう}へ{f|行|い}った。
I went to school with no breakfast.
</pre>

Almost the same fact, two grammars. The first is about an action you did not carry
out; the second presents breakfast as a slot in the morning that was left empty, which
is why it fits so naturally into talk about dieting or oversleeping. And when there is
no action to negate at all - <code>{f|冗談|じょうだん}{f|抜|ぬ}きで</code>,
<code>{f|税|ぜい}{f|抜|ぬ}きで</code> - the noun pattern is the only one available.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|砂糖|さとう}<b>を{f|抜|ぬ}きで</b>お{f|願|ねが}いします。 → ✅ {f|砂糖|さとう}<b>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</b>お{f|願|ねが}いします。
  <code>{f|抜|ぬ}き</code> is a noun and forms a compound with the word in front. It
  takes no particle at all, even though the underlying verb would.

- ❌ {f|食|た}べる<b>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</b>{f|出|で}かけた。 → ✅ {f|食|た}べ<b>ないで</b>{f|出|で}かけた。
  A verb cannot attach. Either name the thing that was skipped -
  <code>{f|朝食|ちょうしょく}{f|抜|ぬ}きで</code> - or negate the verb instead.

- ❌ {f|雪|ゆき}<b>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</b>{f|冬|ふゆ}が{f|終|お}わった。 → ✅ {f|雪|ゆき}が{f|降|ふ}らないまま{f|冬|ふゆ}が{f|終|お}わった。
  Weather is not a component anyone leaves out. This pattern needs something that
  belongs to the package and that somebody could have chosen to include.

## Practice

**1.** すみません、ねぎ____お{f|願|ねが}いできますか。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

ねぎ<b>{f|抜|ぬ}きで</b>お{f|願|ねが}いできますか。

Spring onion is a standard part of the dish, so leaving it out is a removal. This is
the everyday restaurant request, and it needs no particle before
<code>{f|抜|ぬ}き</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|許可|きょか}（{f|抜|ぬ}きで・なしで）{f|中|なか}に{f|入|はい}ってはいけません。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|許可|きょか}<b>なしで</b>{f|中|なか}に{f|入|はい}ってはいけません。

Permission is not an ingredient anybody removes from anything - it is either present
or absent. <code>{f|抜|ぬ}き</code> needs a component that could have been included.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Without your advice, I could never have finished this."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

あなたのアドバイス<b>{f|抜|ぬ}きには</b>、これを{f|仕上|しあ}げることはできなかったでしょう。

This is the formal <code>{f|抜|ぬ}きには…ない</code> frame, and it is the standard
shape for thanking someone in a speech. The negative at the end is obligatory - the
whole point is what would *not* have happened.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|協力|きょうりょく}{f|抜|ぬ}きには、{f|完成|かんせい}できました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|彼女|かのじょ}の{f|協力|きょうりょく}<b>{f|抜|ぬ}きには</b>、{f|完成|かんせい}できませんでした。

<code>{f|抜|ぬ}きには</code> sets up a condition without which something fails, so it
has to be answered by a negative. As written, the sentence says her help was an
obstacle.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|今日|きょう}は{f|昼|ひる}ご{f|飯|はん}{f|抜|ぬ}きだ。
(b) {f|今日|きょう}は{f|昼|ひる}ご{f|飯|はん}を{f|食|た}べていない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) presents lunch as a slot in the day that has been struck out - because you are
dieting, because there is no time, or because somebody has decided you go without. It
sounds like a policy for the day.

(b) simply reports the state of affairs so far. It carries no suggestion that the
omission was planned, and it leaves the afternoon open, whereas (a) rather implies
lunch is not happening at all.

</details>
