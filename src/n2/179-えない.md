# ～えない

**Meaning** · cannot possibly; there is no way - the thing is inconceivable, not merely difficult

`～えない` is the negative of the possibility suffix written {f|得|え}る. It does
not report that someone failed or lacked the skill; it says the possibility itself
is closed. <code>あり{f|得|え}ない</code>, its most famous member, has escaped the
formal register entirely and become an everyday cry of disbelief.

## Formation

The suffix attaches to the **ます-stem**. Unlike the affirmative, which has the two
shapes `うる` and `える`, the negative has exactly one: `えない`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + えない | {f|止|と}める → {f|止|と}め{f|得|え}ない |
| する-verb | Noun + し + えない | {f|予想|よそう}する → {f|予想|よそう}し{f|得|え}ない |
| Past | ～えなかった | {f|想像|そうぞう}し{f|得|え}なかった |
| Polite | ～えません | あり{f|得|え}ません |
| Before a noun | ～えない + Noun | あり{f|得|え}ない{f|話|はなし} |
| Set expression | あり + えない | ある → あり{f|得|え}ない |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - there is no ❌<code>うない</code>. `うる` is a bare dictionary
form and nothing else; the instant a negative appears, the suffix is `える`.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - do not confuse this with <code>ざるを{f|得|え}ない</code>. The kanji is
the same and the reading is the same, but there `ざる` is an old negative, so
"cannot not do it" comes out as **must**. <code>{f|行|い}き{f|得|え}ない</code> =
"going is inconceivable"; <code>{f|行|い}かざるを{f|得|え}ない</code> = "I have no
choice but to go".

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|今|いま}さら{f|中止|ちゅうし}なんてあり<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>。
Calling it off at this stage is unthinkable.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}が{f|嘘|うそ}をつくなんてあり<b>{f|得|え}ません</b>。
There is no way he would lie.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|人間|にんげん}の{f|力|ちから}では{f|止|と}め<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>{f|流|なが}れだ。
It is a current that human strength cannot stop.
</pre>

<pre>
これは{f|素人|しろうと}には{f|判断|はんだん}し<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>{f|問題|もんだい}です。
This is a question that no layman is in a position to judge.
</pre>

<pre>
あの{f|状況|じょうきょう}で{f|冷静|れいせい}でいるなど、{f|私|わたし}にはあり<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>ことでした。
Staying calm in that situation was, for me, simply out of the question.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|時代|じだい}の{f|技術|ぎじゅつ}では{f|予測|よそく}し<b>{f|得|え}なかった</b>{f|規模|きぼ}の{f|変化|へんか}が{f|起|お}きた。
A change on a scale that the technology of the day could not have predicted took place.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試合|しあい}の{f|結果|けっか}は、{f|誰|だれ}にも{f|予想|よそう}し<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>ものだった。
The result of the match was something nobody could have predicted.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|信頼|しんらい}していた{f|部下|ぶか}に{f|裏切|うらぎ}られるとは、{f|当時|とうじ}の{f|私|わたし}には{f|想像|そうぞう}し<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>ことだった。
That I would be betrayed by a subordinate I trusted was beyond anything I could imagine at the time.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Two registers live inside one form, and they barely speak to each other.

With most verbs - {f|止|と}め{f|得|え}ない, {f|判断|はんだん}し{f|得|え}ない,
{f|予測|よそく}し{f|得|え}ない - the pattern is **formal and written**. It belongs
to editorials, court judgements, reports and speeches. Used in casual conversation
it sounds like a quotation from a document.

<code>あり{f|得|え}ない</code> is the exception, and it is now thoroughly
colloquial. It covers "impossible", "you're kidding" and, in a use that has spread
widely among younger speakers, plain outrage at something that has very obviously
happened: あの{f|態度|たいど}、あり{f|得|え}ない. That last use is emotional rather
than logical - nobody thinks the rudeness was literally impossible - and it is
casual enough that you would not aim it at a client. There is also the intensifier
<code>あり{f|得|え}ないほど</code>, "unbelievably".

In polite speech, prefer <code>あり{f|得|え}ません</code> to
<code>あり{f|得|え}ないです</code>; the ます form is the settled one in careful
Japanese, though the です form is heard.

One structural point worth holding on to: because this is a statement about the
world and not about a person, `～えない` almost never takes a personal subject with
は. You say <code>それはあり{f|得|え}ない</code>, not
❌<code>{f|私|わたし}はあり{f|得|え}ない</code>.

## Contrast

### ～えない vs the negative potential

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>～られない・～できない</code> says **this person can't
manage it**. `～えない` says **it cannot be so at all**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|暗|くら}くて{f|時計|とけい}の{f|文字|もじ}が{f|読|よ}めない。
It's too dark to read the figures on the clock.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|説明|せつめい}では、{f|誰|だれ}も{f|納得|なっとく}し<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>。
An explanation like that cannot satisfy anyone.
</pre>

The first is about one pair of eyes in one dark room. The second is a claim about
the explanation itself - no listener, anywhere, could be convinced by it. Swap them
and both sentences go wrong: ❌<code>{f|読|よ}み{f|得|え}ない</code> for a dark
room is nonsense, and <code>{f|納得|なっとく}できない</code> would shrink the
sentence to one person's private reaction.

### ～えない vs ～はずがない

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `はずがない` reasons from what you know about **this case**.
`～えない` denies the possibility **as such**, without needing evidence.

</div>

<pre>
{f|田中|たなか}さんが{f|遅|おく}れるはずがない。いつも{f|30分|さんじゅっぷん}{f|前|まえ}に{f|来|く}る{f|人|ひと}だ。
Tanaka can't be late - he's the sort who turns up half an hour early.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一晩|ひとばん}で{f|全|すべ}てが{f|変|か}わるなど、あり<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>。
Everything changing overnight is not a possibility.
</pre>

`はずがない` invites the follow-up "because…" and can be wrong when the evidence is
wrong. `～えない` does not argue; it rules the case out of the world. In speech
<code>あり{f|得|え}ない</code> is also the more forceful and more emotional of the
two.

### ～えない vs ～かねる

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `～えない` is about **what is possible**. `～かねる` is about
**what the speaker is willing or able to undertake**, and it is a way of declining.

</div>

<pre>
その{f|案|あん}には{f|賛成|さんせい}しかねます。
I am afraid I cannot support that proposal.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|案|あん}が{f|通|とお}ることはあり<b>{f|得|え}ません</b>。
There is no possibility of that proposal passing.
</pre>

The first is a refusal, softened; the speaker could support it and chooses not to.
The second is a forecast about the proposal's fate and carries no politeness at
all. Aim the second at a customer's request and you have told them their idea is
absurd.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ そんなことはあり**うない**。 → ✅ そんなことはあり<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>。
  `うる` has no negative. This error is common enough that it is worth drilling on
  its own.

- ❌ {f|漢字|かんじ}が{f|多|おお}すぎて、この{f|本|ほん}は{f|読|よ}み<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>。 → ✅ この{f|本|ほん}は{f|読|よ}めない。
  Your own inability to read the book is the negative potential, not a statement
  that reading it is inconceivable.

- ❌ {f|起|お}こる<b>{f|得|え}ない</b> → ✅ {f|起|お}こり<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>
  The base is the ます-stem.

- ❌ {f|参加|さんか}し<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>（meaning "I have no choice but to take part"） → ✅ {f|参加|さんか}せざるを<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>
  Same kanji, opposite meaning. Without `ざる`, {f|得|え}ない simply denies the
  possibility.

## Practice

**1.** これだけ{f|準備|じゅんび}したのだから、{f|失敗|しっぱい}などあり____。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|失敗|しっぱい}などあり<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>（or あり{f|得|え}ません）

The speaker is not saying that failure would be difficult - they are ruling it out.
Note that the affirmative <code>あり{f|得|う}る</code> has no negative shape of its
own; ❌あり**うない** does not exist.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|熱|ねつ}が38{f|度|ど}あるので、{f|明日|あした}は（{f|働|はたら}き{f|得|え}ない・{f|働|はたら}けない）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|明日|あした}は<b>{f|働|はたら}けない</b>。

A fever stops one particular body on one particular day, which is the negative
potential. <code>{f|働|はたら}き{f|得|え}ない</code> would claim that work is
inconceivable in principle - far too grand a statement for a temperature.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "A change on that scale is not something one person could have brought about."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

あれほどの{f|変化|へんか}は、{f|一人|ひとり}の{f|力|ちから}で{f|起|お}こし<b>{f|得|え}る</b>ものではない。（or {f|一人|ひとり}では{f|起|お}こし{f|得|え}ない{f|変化|へんか}だ）

Either shape works. The first negates the whole noun phrase and keeps
{f|得|え}る positive; the second negates the suffix directly. Both say that the
possibility is closed, rather than that somebody tried and failed.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|専門家|せんもんか}でも{f|説明|せつめい}するえない{f|現象|げんしょう}が{f|続|つづ}いている。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|専門家|せんもんか}でも{f|説明|せつめい}し<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>{f|現象|げんしょう}が{f|続|つづ}いている。
"Phenomena that not even the experts can explain are continuing."

A する-verb joins the suffix through its ます-stem <code>し</code>, giving
<code>{f|説明|せつめい}し{f|得|え}ない</code>. The dictionary form
<code>する</code> cannot sit in front of it.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|話|はなし}は{f|信|しん}じられない。
(b) {f|彼|かれ}の{f|話|はなし}はあり<b>{f|得|え}ない</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) reports the speaker's own reaction: I am unable to believe it. It leaves room
for the story to be true and for the speaker to come round later.

(b) makes a claim about the story itself - the events it describes could not have
occurred. In writing that is a flat verdict; said out loud in casual speech,
<code>あり{f|得|え}ない</code> also carries a strong note of indignation, closer to
"that is ridiculous" than to a calm judgement of probability.

</details>
