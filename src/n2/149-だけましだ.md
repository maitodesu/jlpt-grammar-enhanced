# ～だけましだ

**Meaning** · at least ～, which is something to be grateful for; it could have been worse

<code>まし</code> means *less bad* - preferable, given the alternatives. It is not
praise. <code>～だけましだ</code> therefore takes a situation that has already gone
wrong and points at the one part that did not, holding it up against the worse version
that almost happened. English "at least" catches it, as long as you hear the sigh in it.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + だけましだ | {f|残|のこ}っている → {f|残|のこ}っているだけましだ |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + だけましだ | {f|怪我|けが}をしない → {f|怪我|けが}をしなかっただけましだ |
| い-adjective | plain form + だけましだ | {f|安|やす}い → {f|安|やす}いだけましだ |
| な-adjective | ～な + だけましだ | {f|静|しず}かな → {f|静|しず}かなだけましだ |
| Noun | Noun + である／だった + だけましだ | {f|無事|ぶじ} → {f|無事|ぶじ}だっただけましだ |
| Polite | … + だけましです | {f|近|ちか}い → {f|近|ちか}いだけましです |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>まし</code> is written in kana in practice. The kanji
<code>{f|増|ま}し</code> exists but belongs to other words
(<code>{f|三割増|さんわりま}し</code>), and writing it here looks wrong to most readers.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|給料|きゅうりょう}は{f|安|やす}いが、{f|残業|ざんぎょう}がない<b>だけましだ</b>。
The pay is poor, but at least there's no overtime.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}に{f|降|ふ}られたけれど、{f|傘|かさ}があった<b>だけましだ</b>。
I got caught in the rain, but at least I had an umbrella.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|停電|ていでん}は{f|困|こま}るけれど、{f|水|みず}が{f|出|で}る<b>だけましです</b>よ。
A power cut is a nuisance, but at least the water's still running.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|試験|しけん}は{f|難|むずか}しかったが、{f|最後|さいご}まで{f|解|と}けた<b>だけましだ</b>。
The exam was hard, but at least I got to the end of it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}はせまいが、{f|駅|えき}から{f|近|ちか}い<b>だけましだ</b>と{f|自分|じぶん}に{f|言|い}い{f|聞|き}かせている。
The flat is cramped, but I keep telling myself at least it's near the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}で{f|屋根|やね}が{f|壊|こわ}れたが、{f|家族|かぞく}が{f|無事|ぶじ}だった<b>だけましだ</b>。
The typhoon wrecked the roof, but at least the family came through unhurt.
</pre>

<pre>

At least she still complains to me. Being left without a word would be harder.
</pre>

</pre>

I missed the last train and walked for an hour, but at least it wasn't raining.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Everything about this pattern presupposes that the overall situation is bad. It is a
consolation offered from inside a mess - to yourself, or to someone else who is having a
worse day than you. It pairs naturally with <code>まだ</code> in front
(<code>まだ{f|安|やす}いだけましだ</code>) and with a <code>が</code> or
<code>けれど</code> clause before it setting up the bad news.

The register is ordinary conversational Japanese, plain or polite as you like, and it is
not marked for age or gender. What needs care is **who you say it about**. Because
<code>まし</code> means "less bad", applying it to another person's work, cooking or
effort is faint praise at best and an insult at worst:
<code>{f|先生|せんせい}の{f|説明|せつめい}はましでした</code> would land badly. Keep it
for situations, weather, prices, damage - and for your own circumstances.

Said to someone in genuine distress it can also come out as dismissive, the Japanese
equivalent of "well, at least you've still got your health". The safe version is to apply
it to yourself and let the other person apply it to themselves if they want to.

## Contrast

### ～だけましだ vs ～ほうがましだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>だけましだ</code> evaluates **the situation you are in**.
<code>ほうがましだ</code> **chooses between two bad options**: "I'd rather".

</div>

<pre>
バスは{f|遅|おそ}いが、{f|座|すわ}れる<b>だけましだ</b>。
The bus is slow, but at least you get a seat.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|満員電車|まんいんでんしゃ}で{f|立|た}っているより、{f|歩|ある}いた<b>ほうがましだ</b>。
I'd rather walk than stand on a packed train.
</pre>

Both contain <code>まし</code> and both concede that nothing here is good. The first is
stuck with its situation and finds a redeeming feature; the second still has a choice and
picks the less painful branch.

### まし vs いい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>いい</code> means good. <code>まし</code> means **less bad**.
They are not degrees of the same thing.

</div>

<pre>
この{f|店|みせ}は{f|安|やす}くておいしいから、いつも<b>いい</b>と{f|思|おも}っている。
This place is cheap and good, so I always think well of it.
</pre>

<pre>
どの{f|店|みせ}もいまいちだが、ここが{f|一番|いちばん}<b>ましだ</b>。
None of the places are great, but this one is the least bad.
</pre>

Substituting one for the other changes a compliment into a complaint. If the thing you
are describing is genuinely good, <code>まし</code> is the wrong word no matter how
modest you are trying to sound.

### ～だけましだ vs ～だけでもありがたい

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>だけましだ</code> measures against **a worse outcome that was
avoided**. <code>だけでもありがたい</code> expresses **plain gratitude** for what you
did get.

</div>

<pre>
{f|返事|へんじ}が{f|遅|おそ}かったが、{f|連絡|れんらく}が{f|来|き}た<b>だけましだ</b>。
The reply was slow, but at least something came.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|忙|いそが}しいのに{f|来|き}てくれた<b>だけでもありがたい</b>。
I'm just grateful you came at all, busy as you are.
</pre>

The second is warm and directed at someone. The first is cooler and directed at fate.
Say <code>だけましだ</code> about a favour someone did you and it will sound as though
you were grading them, which is not what you meant.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ この{f|部屋|へや}は<b>{f|静|しず}かだだけましだ</b>。 → ✅ この{f|部屋|へや}は<b>{f|静|しず}かなだけましだ</b>。
  <code>だけ</code> follows a noun-modifying form, so a な-adjective links with
  <code>な</code>.

- ❌ この{f|旅館|りょかん}は{f|料理|りょうり}もお{f|風呂|ふろ}もすばらしくて、<b>ましだった</b>。 → ✅ …すばらしくて、<b>とてもよかった</b>。
  <code>まし</code> only makes sense when the surroundings are bad. Applied to
  something genuinely enjoyable it reads as damning with faint praise.

- ❌ 「{f|先生|せんせい}の{f|授業|じゅぎょう}はましでした」 → ✅ 「{f|先生|せんせい}の{f|授業|じゅぎょう}はわかりやすかったです」
  Grammatical, socially disastrous. Never grade a person with <code>まし</code> to
  their face.

- ❌ {f|合格|ごうかく}できた<b>だけましだ</b>。（about a result you are pleased with） → ✅ {f|合格|ごうかく}できて<b>よかった</b>。
  The pattern insists the news is bad overall. Attaching it to good news puzzles the
  listener, who will start looking for the disaster you must be referring to.

## Practice

**1.** {f|財布|さいふ}をなくしたが、{f|中|なか}に{f|現金|げんきん}が____だけましだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|中|なか}に{f|現金|げんきん}が<b>{f|入|はい}っていなかった</b>だけましだ。

The comparison is with the version where the wallet was full of cash. A plain form
attaches directly, and the sentence works because losing a wallet is unambiguously bad
news - the precondition this pattern requires.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|終電|しゅうでん}を{f|逃|のが}したら、タクシーで{f|帰|かえ}る（だけましだ・ほうがましだ）。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

タクシーで{f|帰|かえ}る<b>ほうがましだ</b>。

You still have a choice - taxi, or a night in an internet café - and you are picking the
less painful one. <code>だけましだ</code> would need you to be already stuck with the
taxi and finding a silver lining in it.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The flat's old, but at least the rent is cheap."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|部屋|へや}は{f|古|ふる}いが、{f|家賃|やちん}が{f|安|やす}い<b>だけましだ</b>。

The <code>が</code> clause puts the bad news first, which is where this pattern likes
it. An い-adjective attaches with no change.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|大雨|おおあめ}で{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まったが、{f|会社|かいしゃ}が{f|近|ちか}いだけよかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|大雨|おおあめ}で{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まったが、{f|会社|かいしゃ}が{f|近|ちか}い<b>だけましだ</b>。

<code>だけいい</code> is not the pattern, and it would claim the situation is good,
which it plainly is not. <code>まし</code> is what carries the "less bad than it might
have been" sense.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|熱|ねつ}は{f|下|さ}がらないが、{f|食|た}べられるだけましだ。
(b) {f|熱|ねつ}は{f|下|さ}がらないが、{f|食|た}べられてよかった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) frames eating as the floor of a bad situation. The speaker is comparing today with
the version where the patient could not keep anything down, and finding cold comfort.

(b) is simply relieved. It reports something positive without weighing it against a
worse scenario, so it sounds lighter and kinder - which is why (b) is what you would say
to the patient and (a) is what you would say about them.

</details>
