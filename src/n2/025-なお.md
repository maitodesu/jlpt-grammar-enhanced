# なお

**Meaning** · furthermore, please note; still, even now - appends a supplementary
remark to something already complete

`なお` has one main modern job and two secondary ones. The main job is the notice
board's: the announcement has been made, and `なお` adds the small print. The others
are adverbial - "still, even now" and "still more so" - and both belong to careful
written Japanese.

## Usage

`なお` never changes form. Which of its senses you are looking at is decided by
where it sits and what it is next to.

**1. Adding a proviso (conjunction).** It opens a new sentence, always after the
main information, and is followed by a comma. This is the sense you will meet most,
because it is the fixed furniture of notices, minutes, invitations and official
email:

- {f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|九時|くじ}からです。なお、{f|上|うわ}{f|履|ば}きをご{f|持参|じさん}ください。
- {f|会場|かいじょう}が{f|変更|へんこう}になりました。なお、{f|時間|じかん}に{f|変更|へんこう}はありません。

**2. Still; even now (adverb).** Usually with a time expression in front of it, and
very often in the fixed pairs <code>{f|今|いま}なお</code> and
<code>{f|今|いま}もなお</code>:

- {f|今|いま}なお{f|議論|ぎろん}が{f|続|つづ}いている。

**3. Still more; better still (adverb).** In front of an adjective or a comparison,
where English says "even":

- {f|説明|せつめい}を{f|加|くわ}えれば、なおよい。
- <code>なおさら</code> and <code>なお{f|一層|いっそう}</code> are the intensified
  forms and are commoner than bare `なお` in this sense.

## Examples

<pre>
<b>なお</b>、{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みは{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|金曜日|きんようび}までです。
Please note that applications close next Friday.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|事故|じこ}から{f|十年|じゅうねん}たった{f|今|いま}も<b>なお</b>、{f|原因|げんいん}は{f|分|わ}かっていない。
Ten years after the accident the cause is still unknown.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明会|せつめいかい}は{f|午後|ごご}{f|三時|さんじ}に{f|終|お}わります。<b>なお</b>、{f|当日|とうじつ}は{f|駐車場|ちゅうしゃじょう}をご{f|利用|りよう}いただけません。
The briefing ends at three. Please also note that the car park will be unavailable on the day.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|現状|げんじょう}でも{f|十分|じゅうぶん}{f|使|つか}えるが、{f|図|ず}を{f|加|くわ}えれば<b>なお</b>よい。
It is perfectly usable as it stands, but a diagram would make it better still.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Firmly written and formal. `なお` belongs to printed notices, company email, meeting
minutes, contracts and academic prose. It is not rude in speech - it simply sounds
as though you are reading aloud from a document, which is occasionally the effect a
speaker wants at the end of a formal announcement and almost never the effect anyone
wants in conversation. Among friends the same job is done by `あと`, `それと` or
`ちなみに`.

The proviso sense carries a structural signal that is worth internalising as a
reader: everything before `なお` is the message, and everything after it is
secondary. Japanese official writing relies on this heavily, and a reader who skims
past `なお` will miss exactly the sentence that says the deadline has moved. In your
own writing, the rule follows from the same logic - never put your main point after
`なお`, because you have just told the reader it is an afterthought.

The "still, even now" sense has a slightly elevated, almost elegiac feel and is
common in journalism and history writing:
<code>{f|寺|てら}は{f|今|いま}もなお{f|同|おな}じ{f|場所|ばしょ}に{f|立|た}っている</code>.
The everyday word for "still" is `まだ`, and `なお` is not its substitute in speech.

## Contrast

### なお vs ちなみに

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - both append. `なお` adds a **condition or caveat the reader
needs**; `ちなみに` adds a **side fact the reader may enjoy**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|申込書|もうしこみしょ}は{f|窓口|まどぐち}でお{f|受|う}け{f|取|と}りください。<b>なお</b>、{f|印鑑|いんかん}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。
Collect the application form at the counter. Please note that you will need your seal.
</pre>

<pre>
この{f|寺|てら}は{f|江戸|えど}{f|時代|じだい}の{f|建物|たてもの}です。<b>ちなみに</b>、{f|屋根|やね}は{f|一度|いちど}も{f|直|なお}していないそうです。
This temple dates from the Edo period. Incidentally, the roof has apparently never been repaired.
</pre>

Drop the `なお` sentence and somebody turns up without their seal; drop the
`ちなみに` sentence and nothing happens except that the conversation is a little less
interesting. `ちなみに` is also perfectly at home in speech, where `なお` is not - it
is the word to reach for when you want this move in conversation.

### なお vs また

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `また` adds a **second item of equal weight**. `なお` adds
something **subordinate** to what came before.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会費|かいひ}は{f|三千円|さんぜんえん}です。<b>また</b>、{f|懇親会|こんしんかい}は{f|別途|べっと}{f|二千円|にせんえん}かかります。
The fee is three thousand yen. There is also a separate two thousand for the reception.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会費|かいひ}は{f|三千円|さんぜんえん}です。<b>なお</b>、お{f|釣|つ}りのないようご{f|用意|ようい}ください。
The fee is three thousand yen. Please bring the exact amount if you can.
</pre>

The `また` sentence introduces a second charge - information of the same order as the
first. The `なお` sentence introduces a request about how to pay one, which only
exists because the first sentence does. Both words are equally formal; the choice is
purely about rank.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <b>なお</b>、{f|明日|あした}{f|遅|おく}れるかも。（text message to a friend）→ ✅ <b>あと</b>、{f|明日|あした}{f|遅|おく}れるかも。
  `なお` is document language. In a message to a friend it reads as though you had
  pasted in a paragraph from a company notice.

- ❌ {f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|送|おく}ります。<b>なお</b>、{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になりました。 → ✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|中止|ちゅうし}になりました。{f|資料|しりょう}は{f|参考|さんこう}までにお{f|送|おく}りします。
  Putting the important news after `なお` tells the reader to treat it as a footnote,
  and some of them will do exactly that. The main point goes first.

- ❌ {f|雨|あめ}が<b>なお</b>{f|降|ふ}っている。（spoken）→ ✅ {f|雨|あめ}が<b>まだ</b>{f|降|ふ}っている。
  In the "still" sense `なお` is written and normally anchored by
  <code>{f|今|いま}</code> or <code>{f|今|いま}も</code>. For an ordinary spoken
  observation, `まだ` is the word.

## Practice

**1.** {f|次回|じかい}の{f|練習|れんしゅう}は{f|土曜日|どようび}です。____、{f|体育館|たいいくかん}は{f|使|つか}えませんので{f|公園|こうえん}に{f|集合|しゅうごう}してください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>なお</b>、{f|体育館|たいいくかん}は{f|使|つか}えませんので…

The main announcement - the date - has already been made, and what follows is a
condition attached to it. That subordinate relationship, plus the polite written
register of the whole notice, is exactly `なお`'s territory.

</details>

**2.** Choose: この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|百年|ひゃくねん}{f|前|まえ}のものです。（なお・ちなみに）、{f|今|いま}でも{f|売|う}っているそうですよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>ちなみに</b>、{f|今|いま}でも{f|売|う}っているそうですよ。

The added remark is a piece of trivia offered in conversation - note the `そうですよ`
ending - and nothing depends on it. `なお` would clash with the spoken register and
would also promise a caveat the sentence does not deliver.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|五時|ごじ}までです。<b>また</b>、{f|土曜日|どようび}も{f|営業|えいぎょう}しております。
(b) {f|受付|うけつけ}は{f|五時|ごじ}までです。<b>なお</b>、{f|受付|うけつけ}{f|終了|しゅうりょう}{f|後|ご}のお{f|問|と}い{f|合|あ}わせはメールでお{f|願|ねが}いします。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) presents Saturday opening as a second, independent fact of the same weight -
two things the reader should know about when the desk is open.

(b) presents the email instruction as depending on the first sentence: it only
matters *because* the desk closes at five. `なお` grades the information, telling the
reader "this is the follow-up detail", and swapping in `また` would oddly promote a
fallback procedure to equal billing with the opening hours.

</details>
