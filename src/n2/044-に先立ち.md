# ～に先立ち / ～に先立って

**Meaning** · prior to; ahead of - the main clause happens in the run-up to a scheduled event, as part of getting there

<code>{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}つ</code> means "to go in front". The pattern names a
**planned event**, then reports something that happens **before it and because of
it**: the rehearsal before the ceremony, the briefing before the works, the anthem
before the match. It is announcement and news-report Japanese - the sentence shape
<code>～に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち、～が{f|行|おこな}われた</code> turns up in a bulletin
several times a night.

## Formation

The event goes in front, and it is normally a noun. <code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</code> is the stem form and
<code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って</code> the て-form; <code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}つ</code> is used when a noun follows.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Event noun | Noun + に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち | {f|開会|かいかい} → {f|開会|かいかい}に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち |
| Event noun (slightly lighter) | Noun + に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って | {f|試合|しあい} → {f|試合|しあい}に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って |
| Verb (dictionary form) | dictionary + に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち | {f|出発|しゅっぱつ}する → {f|出発|しゅっぱつ}するに{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち |
| Before a noun | ～に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}つ + Noun | {f|発売|はつばい} → {f|発売|はつばい}に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}つ{f|試写会|ししゃかい} |
| Marked as the topic | ～に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}っては | {f|審査|しんさ} → {f|審査|しんさ}に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}っては |
| ❌ Past form | ❌{f|閉|し}めたに{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って | ✅{f|閉|し}めるに{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って |
| ❌ As a main verb here | ❌{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}った、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|配|くば}った | ✅{f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|配|くば}った |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - two conditions. The event in front must be **scheduled**, and
the main clause must be **preparatory to it**. If either fails, you want
<code>{f|前|まえ}に</code>.

</div>

## Nuance & register

Written, official and above all journalistic. Newsreaders, programmes, timetables
and company notices are its home; nobody says it about brushing their teeth. If you
want to be understood as neutral and human, <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> is the word.

<code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</code> is the stiffer of the two shapes and dominates printed schedules and
formal writing. <code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って</code> is a little lighter and is what a chairperson might say
aloud. Neither has a casual form.

The pattern implies more than sequence: the earlier action **belongs to** the later
event. That is why the two clauses are almost always about the same undertaking - a
rehearsal and its concert, a survey and the construction it clears the way for.
Two unrelated things that merely happened in that order need
<code>{f|前|まえ}に</code>, which asks nothing of them.

One structural point that catches learners: the *event* takes `に`, and the *earlier
thing* is the subject of the main clause. English inverts this
("the anthem preceded the match"), so it is worth reading a few examples until the
Japanese order stops feeling backwards.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち vs ～{f|前|まえ}に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> is plain "before" and works for
anything at all. <code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</code> is formal and needs the two
events to be **parts of one arrangement**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|寝|ね}る<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>{f|歯|は}を{f|磨|みが}きなさい。
Brush your teeth before you go to bed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|出港|しゅっこう}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</b>、{f|船内|せんない}の{f|安全|あんぜん}{f|点検|てんけん}が{f|行|おこな}われる。
Before departure, a safety inspection is carried out on board.
</pre>

Teeth and bedtime are not a joint arrangement, and the register is domestic;
<code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</code> there would be comic. The inspection, by
contrast, exists **for** the sailing and appears on the same schedule.

### ～に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち vs ～にあたって

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</code> is **earlier than** the
event; `にあたって` is **at** it. Ask whether the event has begun yet.

</div>

<pre>
{f|改装|かいそう}{f|工事|こうじ}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</b>、{f|店内|てんない}の{f|商品|しょうひん}をすべて{f|運|はこ}び{f|出|だ}した。
Ahead of the refurbishment, we moved every item out of the shop.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|改装|かいそう}{f|工事|こうじ}を{f|始|はじ}める<b>にあたって</b>、{f|職人|しょくにん}に{f|注文|ちゅうもん}を{f|細|こま}かく{f|伝|つた}えた。
In embarking on the refurbishment, I gave the tradesmen detailed instructions.
</pre>

Both sentences describe preparation, and in cases like this the two really do
overlap. They separate at the edges: a greeting on the day can only be `にあたって`,
and something completed weeks earlier can only be
<code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</code>.

### ～に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち vs ～を{f|前|まえ}に(して)

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>を{f|前|まえ}に(して)</code> puts you **facing** something
that is coming, and often reports a feeling. <code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</code>
reports an **arrangement** completed in advance.

</div>

<pre>
{f|大会|たいかい}<b>を{f|前|まえ}に</b>、{f|選手|せんしゅ}たちは{f|緊張|きんちょう}を{f|隠|かく}せない{f|様子|ようす}だった。
With the tournament looming, the players could not hide their nerves.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|大会|たいかい}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</b>、{f|抽選|ちゅうせん}で{f|対戦|たいせん}{f|相手|あいて}が{f|決|き}められた。
Ahead of the tournament, the draw decided who would face whom.
</pre>

Nerves are not a preparation, so the first sentence resists
<code>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</code>. The draw is a scheduled preliminary, so it
resists <code>を{f|前|まえ}に</code>. Where the main clause is genuinely
preparatory, both are heard.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って</b>、{f|財布|さいふ}を{f|確認|かくにん}した。 → ✅ {f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}の<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>、{f|財布|さいふ}を{f|確認|かくにん}した。
  Everyday errands are not scheduled public events. The formal pattern makes a trip
  to the shops sound like a state visit.

- ❌ {f|会議|かいぎ}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}った</b>、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|配|くば}った。 → ✅ {f|会議|かいぎ}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</b>、{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|配|くば}った。
  The connective is <code>{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</code> or
  <code>{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って</code>. The form <code>{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}つ</code>
  appears only directly in front of a noun.

- ❌ {f|閉店|へいてん}した<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って</b>、セールを{f|行|おこな}った。 → ✅ {f|閉店|へいてん}する<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って</b>、セールを{f|行|おこな}った。
  A verb in front stays in the dictionary form, however firmly the event is in the
  past.

- ❌ {f|開会|かいかい}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</b>、{f|会|かい}が{f|開|ひら}かれた。 → ✅ {f|開会|かいかい}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</b>、リハーサルが{f|行|おこな}われた。
  The main clause must be a **different**, earlier action. Restating the same event
  produces a sentence that eats its own tail.

## Practice

**1.** {f|新|あたら}しい{f|路線|ろせん}の{f|開通|かいつう}____、{f|試運転|しうんてん}が{f|繰|く}り{f|返|かえ}された。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|開通|かいつう}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</b>（or に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って）

The test runs exist for the sake of the opening and are on the same schedule, which
is exactly the relationship this pattern encodes. A news report would use the stem
form.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}る（{f|前|まえ}に・に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って）、ガスを{f|消|け}したか{f|確|たし}かめた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|家|いえ}を{f|出|で}る<b>{f|前|まえ}に</b>

Leaving the house is not a scheduled event with a programme attached, and the
register is ordinary domestic speech. Plain <code>{f|前|まえ}に</code> is the only
natural choice here.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Ahead of the opening of the exhibition, the press were shown round."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|展覧会|てんらんかい}の{f|開幕|かいまく}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</b>、{f|報道|ほうどう}{f|関係者|かんけいしゃ}{f|向|む}けの{f|内覧会|ないらんかい}が{f|行|おこな}われた。

The press view is a scheduled preliminary belonging to the exhibition itself, and
the whole sentence is in report register - the natural home of this pattern.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|卒業式|そつぎょうしき}に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}つ、{f|練習|れんしゅう}が{f|三回|さんかい}{f|行|おこな}われた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|卒業式|そつぎょうしき}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</b>、{f|練習|れんしゅう}が{f|三回|さんかい}{f|行|おこな}われた。

<code>{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}つ</code> is the noun-modifying form and cannot end a
clause. To join two clauses you need the stem
<code>{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</code> or the て-form
<code>{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}って</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|開店|かいてん}<b>に{f|先|さき}{f|立|だ}ち</b>、{f|従業員|じゅうぎょういん}に{f|研修|けんしゅう}を{f|行|おこな}った。
(b) {f|開店|かいてん}<b>に{f|際|さい}して</b>、{f|従業員|じゅうぎょういん}に{f|研修|けんしゅう}を{f|行|おこな}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) places the training clearly **before** opening day and presents it as one of the
preparations that had to be finished first.

(b) attaches the training loosely to the opening as its occasion. It may have run
before the doors opened or in the first week; the sentence only says the opening was
what prompted it.

</details>
