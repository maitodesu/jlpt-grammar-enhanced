# ～ということは

**Meaning** · which means; so that means - takes a fact that has just been
established and states the inference that falls out of it

`ということは` picks something up - a remark, a thing you can see, a fact you have
just checked - packages it as a *statement* with <code>という</code>, and then makes
that statement the topic with `は`. The rest of the sentence is what the speaker
concludes from it. It is the sound of someone working something out in real time.

## Formation

<code>ということ</code> is a noun phrase, so whatever precedes it is a plain-form
clause. `は` then topicalises the whole package.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + ということは | {f|来|こ}ない → {f|来|こ}ないということは |
| い-adjective | plain form + ということは | {f|暗|くら}い → {f|暗|くら}いということは |
| な-adjective | ～だ + ということは | {f|静|しず}かだ → {f|静|しず}かだということは |
| Noun | ～だ + ということは | {f|休|やす}み → {f|休|やす}みだということは |
| Standalone sentence opener | ということは、… | - → ということは、{f|中止|ちゅうし}ですね |
| Polite | … + ということは + ですか／でしょうか | - → ということは、{f|明日|あした}でしょうか |

<div class="note">

**Note** - after a noun or a な-adjective the `だ` is regularly dropped in speech:
{f|休|やす}みということは… is heard as often as {f|休|やす}みだということは…. In
writing, keep the `だ`.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|電気|でんき}が{f|消|き}えている。<b>ということは</b>、もう{f|寝|ね}たんだ。
The lights are off. Which means he's already gone to bed.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部屋|へや}が{f|暗|くら}い<b>ということは</b>、まだ{f|帰|かえ}っていないんですね。
The room's dark, so that means she isn't back yet.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|名前|なまえ}がリストにない<b>ということは</b>、{f|申|もう}し{f|込|こ}みが{f|届|とど}いていないのかもしれません。
Your name isn't on the list, which may mean the application never arrived.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}が{f|九時|くじ}に{f|終|お}わった<b>ということは</b>、{f|昼|ひる}までに{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|直|なお}せるな。
The meeting finished at nine, so that means I can fix the handouts before lunch.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|三年|さんねん}{f|住|す}んでいた<b>ということは</b>、あの{f|辺|あた}りの{f|道|みち}には{f|詳|くわ}しいはずだ。
He lived there three years, so he must know those streets well.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値上|ねあ}げはしないと{f|言|い}っている。<b>ということは</b>、{f|量|りょう}を{f|減|へ}らすつもりなのだろうか。
They say there'll be no price rise. Does that mean they intend to shrink the portions?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|外国語|がいこくご}を{f|学|まな}ぶ<b>ということは</b>、その{f|国|くに}の{f|考|かんが}え{f|方|かた}を{f|学|まな}ぶことでもある。
To learn a foreign language is also to learn how that country thinks.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|母|はは}から{f|連絡|れんらく}が{f|来|こ}ない<b>ということは</b>、{f|手術|しゅじゅつ}は{f|無事|ぶじ}に{f|終|お}わったのだろう。
No word from my mother, which I take to mean the operation went fine.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and one of the most useful things you can carry into
conversation. In speech it is very often a sentence of its own, with a pause after
it: 「{f|定休日|ていきゅうび}か。ということは、{f|明日|あした}{f|出直|でなお}しだな」.
Said on a rising tone it becomes a check rather than a statement -
「ということは、{f|今日|きょう}はもう{f|来|こ}ないってこと?」 - and that is probably
the shape you will use most.

Notice what the second half tends to end in. Because the sentence is an inference
rather than a report, it lands on `んだ`, `のか`, `のだろう`, <code>わけだ</code>
or `ですね` far more often than on a bare `だ`. That ending is part of the pattern's
music; a flat <code>ということは、{f|中止|ちゅうし}だ</code> is grammatical but sounds
oddly clipped.

There is a small social edge to watch. Turning `ということは` on what somebody has
just told you is how you make them own an implication they did not spell out -
「ということは、{f|責任|せきにん}はこちらにあるということですか」. In a negotiation
that is a deliberate and effective move; over coffee with a friend it can feel like
cross-examination. The softeners are the usual ones: `でしょうか`, `かな`, or a
`ね` on the end.

Finally, the seventh example above uses a different sense that shares the same
shape. <code>{f|外国語|がいこくご}を{f|学|まな}ぶということは…</code> is not an
inference at all - it topicalises *the act itself* in order to say what that act
amounts to. This use is at home in essays and speeches, always with a generic,
definitional predicate after it. If the second half states a general truth rather
than a conclusion about the here and now, you are looking at this sense.

## Contrast

### ～ということは vs つまり

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `つまり` re-expresses the *same* content in different words.
`ということは` derives content that was **not** stated.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会員|かいいん}{f|以外|いがい}は{f|入|はい}れません。<b>つまり</b>、{f|受付|うけつけ}で{f|登録|とうろく}が{f|必要|ひつよう}です。
Non-members can't enter. That is, you need to register at reception.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会員|かいいん}{f|以外|いがい}は{f|入|はい}れない<b>ということは</b>、{f|今日|きょう}{f|来|き}た{f|友達|ともだち}は{f|外|そと}で{f|待|ま}つことになる。
Non-members can't get in, which means the friend I brought today will be waiting outside.
</pre>

The first sentence says the same thing twice; delete the second half and nothing is
lost. The second sentence adds a fact about today that the rule alone did not
contain. That is the test: if the second half is genuinely new information *derived*
from the first, `ということは` is the word.

The two also sit differently. `つまり` is an adverb dropped in front of a clause;
`ということは` is a whole clause plus a topic particle, which is why it can carry the
premise inside itself - <code>{f|暗|くら}いということは</code> - where `つまり` needs
the premise to have been a separate sentence.

### ～ということは vs ～わけだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ということは` **opens** the sentence with the premise;
<code>わけだ</code> **closes** it with the conclusion. They are the two ends of the
same move and frequently appear in one sentence.

</div>

<pre>
{f|靴|くつ}がない<b>ということは</b>、もう{f|出|で}かけたわけだ。
His shoes are gone, so he must already have left.
</pre>

Here the premise is marked at the front and the conclusion at the back, and neither
marker is redundant. Where they genuinely compete is when only one appears, and
then the difference is which half you want to spotlight. <code>どうりで{f|寒|さむ}いわけだ</code>
points at the conclusion and says "that explains it"; <code>{f|窓|まど}が{f|開|あ}いているということは</code>
points at the evidence and leaves the conclusion to follow.

One thing only `ということは` can do: stand alone as a sentence opener with the
premise left implicit, because it was the previous speaker's line. <code>わけだ</code>
cannot start a sentence at all.

### ～ということは vs ～ということだ

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - swap `は` for `だ` and the clause stops being the premise and
becomes the **conclusion**. `ということは` is the front half; `ということだ` is the
back half.

</div>

<pre>
{f|返信|へんしん}がない<b>ということは</b>、{f|断|ことわ}られたのだろう。
There's been no reply, so I suppose I've been turned down.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|返信|へんしん}がないのだから、{f|断|ことわ}られた<b>ということだ</b>。
There's been no reply, so it means I've been turned down.
</pre>

Same reasoning, opposite packaging. Note also that `ということだ` doubles as a
hearsay marker - "I hear that" - when a source is named, and `ということは` never
does. If the sentence reports somebody else's words rather than your own reasoning,
`は` is not available.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|静|しず}か<b>ということは</b>、{f|留守|るす}かな。（in writing）→ ✅ {f|静|しず}か<b>だということは</b>、{f|留守|るす}かな。
  な-adjectives and nouns link to <code>という</code> with `だ`. Dropping it is fine
  in speech and sloppy on the page.

- ❌ ということは、{f|今日|きょう}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るそうです。 → ✅ {f|今日|きょう}は{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るそうです。
  `ということは` has to conclude something from a premise. With nothing behind it and
  a piece of hearsay in front, there is no inference for it to introduce.

- ❌ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|来|こ}ないということは{f|残念|ざんねん}です。 → ✅ {f|彼|かれ}が{f|来|こ}ないのは{f|残念|ざんねん}です。
  To say that a fact is regrettable you want the plain nominaliser `の` or
  <code>こと</code>. <code>ということは</code> announces an inference, and "that he
  isn't coming is a shame" is not one.

- ❌ {f|田中|たなか}さんによると、{f|中止|ちゅうし}だ<b>ということは</b>。 → ✅ {f|田中|たなか}さんによると、{f|中止|ちゅうし}だ<b>ということです</b>。
  Reporting a source is the job of `ということだ`. `ということは` cannot end a
  sentence at all - something has to follow it.

## Practice

**1.** {f|窓|まど}に{f|明|あ}かりがついていない。____、まだ{f|誰|だれ}も{f|来|き}ていないんだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>ということは</b>、まだ{f|誰|だれ}も{f|来|き}ていないんだ。

The dark window is the evidence; the empty room is the conclusion drawn from it,
and it is information the first sentence did not state. Ending the second half in
`んだ` is what a spoken inference normally does.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|反対|はんたい}した（ということは・つまり）、この{f|案|あん}はもう{f|一度|いちど}{f|作|つく}り{f|直|なお}すことになる。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|全員|ぜんいん}が{f|反対|はんたい}した<b>ということは</b>、この{f|案|あん}はもう{f|一度|いちど}{f|作|つく}り{f|直|なお}すことになる。

Redrafting the proposal is a consequence, not a rewording of "everyone objected",
so this is derivation rather than restatement. `つまり` would also need the first
clause to be a finished sentence of its own; here it is a clause inside the
sentence, which is exactly the slot `ということは` fills.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The shop's closed on Mondays? So that means we can't go tomorrow."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

「あの{f|店|みせ}は{f|月曜|げつよう}が{f|休|やす}みなんですか。<b>ということは</b>、{f|明日|あした}は{f|行|い}けませんね」

The premise came from the other speaker, so `ということは` opens a fresh sentence
with the premise left implicit. `ね` at the end invites agreement, which is the
natural tone for an inference about a shared plan.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|部長|ぶちょう}のメールによると、{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}に{f|延|の}びたということは。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|部長|ぶちょう}のメールによると、{f|会議|かいぎ}は{f|来週|らいしゅう}に{f|延|の}びた<b>ということです</b>。

Reporting what a named source said calls for `ということだ／ということです`. The
`は` version topicalises a premise and therefore demands a second half; ending the
sentence on it leaves the listener waiting.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|試験|しけん}に{f|受|う}かった<b>ということは</b>、{f|四月|しがつ}から{f|通|かよ}えるんだね。
(b) {f|試験|しけん}に{f|受|う}かった<b>ということだ</b>。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) treats passing the exam as the known premise and works forward from it to
something new - being able to attend from April. The speaker is reasoning out loud
and expects the listener to follow.

(b) presents passing the exam as the endpoint: either a conclusion just reached
from other evidence, or a report of what somebody else said. Nothing follows it,
and depending on context it can be read as "so I hear", which (a) can never mean.

</details>
