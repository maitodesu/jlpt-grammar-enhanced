# 一応

**Meaning** · いちおう - for now; just to be safe; more or less

<code>{f|一応|いちおう}</code> marks something as done to the minimum acceptable standard, or
done as a precaution rather than out of conviction. It is one of the most useful
hedges in spoken Japanese and one of the easiest to overuse.

## Usage

<code>{f|一応|いちおう}</code> is an adverb. It goes in front of the predicate, or at the head
of the sentence when it is hedging the whole statement. It pulls in three
directions, and which one you get is decided entirely by context.

- **Just in case.** The action is a precaution: {f|一応|いちおう}{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っていく, {f|一応|いちおう}{f|確認|かくにん}しておきます. This sense
  pairs constantly with <code>～ておく</code>, which is itself the "do it in
  advance" form.
- **For the time being; provisionally.** The thing is settled, but not finally:
  {f|一応|いちおう}{f|予約|よやく}しました, {f|一応|いちおう}これで{f|完成|かんせい}です.
- **More or less; technically.** A hedge on a claim, very often about oneself:
  {f|一応|いちおう}{f|終|お}わりました, 「{f|一応|いちおう}{f|大学|だいがく}で{f|習|なら}いました」.

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|一応|いちおう}</code> always lowers the temperature of the claim
it modifies. Whatever the sentence asserts, it asserts a little less firmly.

</div>

It also works as a complete answer on its own. 「{f|終|お}わった？」「{f|一応|いちおう}。」means "yes - technically", and the listener will hear the
reservation.

The kanji is standard and normal in writing; the kana spelling いちおう appears in
casual text. There is no difference in meaning.

## Examples

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るかもしれないので、<b>{f|一応|いちおう}</b>{f|傘|かさ}を{f|持|も}っていきます。
It might rain, so I'll take an umbrella just in case.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|宿題|しゅくだい}は<b>{f|一応|いちおう}</b>{f|終|お}わりました。
I've finished the homework - more or less.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|出|で}かける{f|前|まえ}に、<b>{f|一応|いちおう}</b>{f|窓|まど}のかぎを{f|確|たし}かめておいた。
Before going out I checked the window locks, just to be safe.
</pre>

<pre>
まだ{f|決定|けってい}ではありませんが、<b>{f|一応|いちおう}</b>{f|来週|らいしゅう}の{f|金曜日|きんようび}ということでお{f|願|ねが}いします。
It isn't final yet, but let's provisionally say next Friday.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Thoroughly everyday, in speech and in writing, and not marked for age or gender.
Japanese speakers reach for it dozens of times a day.

The reservation it carries is real, and that has consequences at work. Reporting
「{f|一応|いちおう}やっておきました」tells your boss the job was done to the
minimum and invites them to check it; if you actually did it properly, say
「{f|終|お}わりました」and leave the hedge out. In the other direction, <code>{f|一応|いちおう}</code> is a
graceful way to be modest about your own ability -
「{f|一応|いちおう}{f|話|はな}せます」claims competence while declining to boast -
and it is standard practice in Japanese self-presentation.

Attaching it to someone else's work is where it turns rude:
「{f|一応|いちおう}できていますね」says the thing passes, barely. Applied to a
person's status it is worse - {f|一応|いちおう}{f|部長|ぶちょう}です about your
manager means "he's nominally the department head", which is an insult. About
yourself the same words are ordinary modesty.

## Contrast

### {f|一応|いちおう} vs とりあえず

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `とりあえず` is about **order**: this first, the rest later.
<code>{f|一応|いちおう}</code> is about **degree**: this much, and no more claimed.

</div>

<pre>
<b>とりあえず</b>ビールをお{f|願|ねが}いします。
We'll start with a beer, thanks.
</pre>

<pre>
<b>{f|一応|いちおう}</b>{f|全部|ぜんぶ}{f|読|よ}みましたが、よく{f|分|わ}かりませんでした。
I did read all of it, for what that's worth, but I didn't really follow.
</pre>

The beer is first, not minimal - nobody is saying it is a barely adequate beer.
The reading is complete but the speaker will not vouch for how well it was done.
The two overlap in sentences like {f|一応|いちおう}／とりあえず{f|連絡|れんらく}しておきます, where the contact is both the first step and a
precaution, and either word is natural. Where they differ, ask whether you mean
"before anything else" or "at least this much".

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|一応|いちおう}{f|課長|かちょう}です。 → ✅ {f|田中|たなか}さんは{f|課長|かちょう}です。
  Used of another person's rank, it means "he holds the title, technically". Save
  it for yourself.

- ❌ {f|一応|いちおう}{f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}だから、{f|絶対|ぜったい}{f|心配|しんぱい}しないで。 → ✅ {f|大丈夫|だいじょうぶ}だから、{f|心配|しんぱい}しないで。
  The hedge and the word {f|絶対|ぜったい} pull against each other. If you want to
  reassure someone, do not simultaneously tell them your assurance is provisional.

- ❌ {f|一応|いちおう}ビールをください。 → ✅ <b>とりあえず</b>ビールをください。
  Ordering "a beer to start with" is about sequence. <code>{f|一応|いちおう}</code> would
  suggest a beer will do, grudgingly.

- ❌ {f|一応|いちおう}な{f|返事|へんじ}をもらいました。 → ✅ <b>{f|一応|いちおう}</b>{f|返事|へんじ}をもらいました。
  It is an adverb and does not take `な` or `の` before a noun.

## Practice

**1.** {f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}は{f|分|わ}かっていますが、____{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}を{f|読|よ}んでおきます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>{f|一応|いちおう}</b>{f|説明書|せつめいしょ}を{f|読|よ}んでおきます。

The speaker already knows how to use it, so reading the manual is a precaution
rather than a need - and the `～ておく` ending is the form this sense of the
adverb most often travels with.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: {f|時間|じかん}がないので、（{f|一応|いちおう}・とりあえず）{f|急|きゅう}ぎの{f|分|ぶん}だけ{f|先|さき}に{f|送|おく}ります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

<b>とりあえず</b>{f|急|きゅう}ぎの{f|分|ぶん}だけ{f|先|さき}に{f|送|おく}ります。

The sentence is about what comes first, with the rest to follow. That is sequence,
which is `とりあえず`'s job, not a claim being hedged.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|資料|しりょう}を{f|作|つく}りました。
(b) {f|一応|いちおう}{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|作|つく}りました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a straightforward report: the document exists and the speaker stands behind
it. (b) hands it over with a reservation - it may be rough, it may need checking,
and the speaker is not claiming it is finished work. Said about your own output
(b) is modest and normal; said about a colleague's, the same hedge becomes a
criticism.

</details>
