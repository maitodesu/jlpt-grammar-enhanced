# ～てでも

**Meaning** · even if it takes doing that - I am going to get this done

`てでも` names a means the speaker would rather not use - borrowing money, staying up
all night, swallowing their pride - and says they will use it anyway. The て-clause is
the *method*, not a concession, and the sentence always ends in determination.

## Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + でも | {f|借|か}りる → {f|借|か}りてでも |
| Verb (て-form in で) | ～で + でも | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}んででも |
| する-verb | ～して + でも | {f|徹夜|てつや}する → {f|徹夜|てつや}してでも |
| Noun as instrument | Noun + で + でも | タクシー → タクシーででも |
| Set phrase | どんな{f|手|て}を{f|使|つか}ってでも | - |
| Main clause | will, wish or obligation | ～たい / ～つもりだ / ～なければならない |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|休|やす}んででも</code> looks like a typo and is not. The て-form of
<code>{f|休|やす}む</code> already ends in `で`, and `でも` is added whole, so two `で`
land side by side.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|徹夜|てつや}し<b>てでも</b>、{f|明日|あした}までに{f|仕|し}{f|上|あ}げます。
I'll finish it by tomorrow even if it means staying up all night.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|借金|しゃっきん}をし<b>てでも</b>、あの{f|絵|え}が{f|欲|ほ}しい。
I want that painting even if I have to borrow to get it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|休|やす}ん<b>ででも</b>、{f|息子|むすこ}の{f|試合|しあい}を{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}きたい。
I want to go and watch my son's match even if I have to take the day off work.
</pre>

<pre>
どんな{f|手|て}を{f|使|つか}っ<b>てでも</b>、{f|犯人|はんにん}を{f|見|み}つけ{f|出|だ}す。
I'll find the culprit by whatever means it takes.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|電車|でんしゃ}が{f|止|と}まっているなら、{f|歩|ある}い<b>てでも</b>{f|会場|かいじょう}に{f|向|む}かいます。
If the trains are down, I'll walk to the venue if I have to.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|親|おや}に{f|頭|あたま}を{f|下|さ}げ<b>てでも</b>、{f|留学|りゅうがく}の{f|費用|ひよう}を{f|出|だ}してもらうつもりだ。
I intend to get them to fund my study abroad, even if it means going cap in hand to my parents.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|多少|たしょう}{f|無理|むり}をし<b>てでも</b>、{f|今年|ことし}のうちに{f|資格|しかく}を{f|取|と}っておきたいと{f|考|かんが}えています。
I'd like to get the qualification before the year is out, even if I have to push myself a bit.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|値段|ねだん}を{f|下|さ}げ<b>てでも</b>、{f|在庫|ざいこ}を{f|年内|ねんない}に{f|売|う}り{f|切|き}らなければならない。
We have to clear the stock by the end of the year, even if that means cutting the price.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

The pattern is neutral in politeness - it takes whatever ending the sentence needs -
but it is emotionally loaded. Choosing `てでも` announces that you know the method is
costly and have decided it is worth it, so the sentence carries resolve, and sometimes
a hint of desperation. That makes it excellent in a job interview and slightly alarming
at a dinner table.

The costliness matters. A means that costs nothing sounds odd here: saying
<code>{f|歩|ある}いてでも{f|駅|えき}まで{f|行|い}く</code> only works if walking is a real
hardship in the situation - the trains are down, it is pouring, it is five kilometres.
Otherwise `て` alone will do.

The second half has to look forward: a wish, an intention, an obligation, an order.
Occasionally you meet a past-tense report of the same resolve
(<code>タクシーを{f|使|つか}ってでも{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わせた</code>), which is fine, but a
plain description of a state - "I was tired" - cannot close a `てでも` sentence, because
there is no will in it for the pattern to attach to.

## Contrast

### ～てでも vs ～ても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `てでも` presents the clause as the **means** you will use. `ても`
presents it as an **obstacle** you would put up with.

</div>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っ<b>ても</b>、{f|試合|しあい}は{f|行|おこな}われます。
The match will go ahead even if it rains.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|傘|かさ}を{f|買|か}っ<b>てでも</b>、{f|今日|きょう}{f|届|とど}けに{f|行|い}きます。
I'll go and deliver it today even if I have to buy an umbrella.
</pre>

Rain is not something you *do* in order to hold a match, so `ても` is right there. The
umbrella is a step the speaker will actively take, which is what `でも` adds. If the
clause is something the subject chooses to do, `てでも` is available; if it merely
happens to them, only `ても` is.

### ～てでも vs ～てまで

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `てでも` is the speaker's own resolve, and approves of it.
`てまで` measures how far someone went and usually **disapproves**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|列|れつ}に{f|三時間|さんじかん}{f|並|なら}ん<b>ででも</b>、この{f|店|みせ}のケーキを{f|買|か}いたい。
I'd queue three hours to buy a cake from this shop.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|列|れつ}に{f|三時間|さんじかん}も{f|並|なら}ん<b>でまで</b>、ケーキを{f|買|か}いたいとは{f|思|おも}わない。
I wouldn't want a cake badly enough to queue three hours for it.
</pre>

`てまで` characteristically appears with a negative or a rhetorical question - it asks
whether the goal was worth that price and implies not. Swapping the two reverses the
speaker's attitude, so this is a pair worth keeping straight.

### ～てでも vs Noun + でも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - after a noun, `でも` means "or something, for instance". Only after
a **て-form** does it mean "even by doing".

</div>

<pre>
{f|時間|じかん}があるなら、お{f|茶|ちゃ}<b>でも</b>{f|飲|の}みませんか。
If you've got time, shall we have some tea or something?
</pre>

<pre>
{f|自転車|じてんしゃ}を{f|飛|と}ばし<b>てでも</b>、{f|一番|いちばん}に{f|着|つ}きたかった。
I wanted to get there first, even if it meant tearing along on my bike.
</pre>

The first `でも` softens a suggestion; the second commits to a hard method. To make a
noun into a means you need a verb between it and `でも` - <code>タクシーを{f|使|つか}ってでも</code> -
or the instrumental particle, <code>タクシーででも</code>.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|走|はし}る<b>てでも</b>{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わせる。 → ✅ {f|走|はし}っ<b>てでも</b>{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わせる。
  `でも` attaches to the て-form, so the verb has to be conjugated first.

- ❌ タクシー<b>てでも</b>{f|行|い}きます。 → ✅ タクシーを{f|使|つか}っ<b>てでも</b>{f|行|い}きます。
  A bare noun cannot take `てでも`. Supply a verb, or use <code>タクシーででも</code>.

- ❌ {f|無理|むり}をし<b>てでも</b>、{f|疲|つか}れてしまった。 → ✅ {f|無理|むり}をし<b>て</b>、{f|疲|つか}れてしまった。
  The second half must express will or obligation. A plain report of how you ended up
  feeling has nothing for `でも` to reinforce.

- ❌ {f|少|すこ}し{f|待|ま}っ<b>てでも</b>、コーヒーが{f|飲|の}みたい。 → ✅ {f|少|すこ}し{f|待|ま}っ<b>ても</b>、コーヒーが{f|飲|の}みたい。
  Waiting a little is not a sacrifice. `てでも` needs a means the speaker genuinely
  wants to avoid.

## Practice

**1.** {f|会議|かいぎ}に{f|遅|おく}れそうなので、タクシーを{f|使|つか}っ____{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わせます。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|使|つか}っ<b>てでも</b>{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}わせます

The taxi is an expensive method the speaker would normally skip, and the ending states
a firm intention - both conditions `てでも` requires.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|嘘|うそ}をつい（てでも・てまで）{f|勝|か}ちたいとは{f|思|おも}わない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|嘘|うそ}をつい<b>てまで</b>{f|勝|か}ちたいとは{f|思|おも}わない。

The sentence rejects the method, and that judgement is `てまで`'s territory. `てでも`
would commit the speaker to lying, which contradicts the ending.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'll ask my old teacher for help if I have to, but I want to finish this research."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|昔|むかし}の{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|頼|たの}み{f|込|こ}ん<b>ででも</b>、この{f|研究|けんきゅう}をやり{f|遂|と}げたい。

<code>{f|頼|たの}み{f|込|こ}む</code> has a て-form ending in `で`, so `でも` follows it
directly and you get `ででも`. The closing <code>～たい</code> carries the resolve.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|頭|あたま}を{f|下|さ}げででも、{f|許|ゆる}してもらうつもりだ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|頭|あたま}を{f|下|さ}げ<b>てでも</b>、{f|許|ゆる}してもらうつもりだ。

<code>{f|下|さ}げる</code> is a る-verb, so its て-form is <code>{f|下|さ}げて</code>. The
doubled `ででも` only appears when the て-form itself ends in `で`, as with
<code>{f|休|やす}んで</code> or <code>{f|急|いそ}いで</code>.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|親|おや}に{f|反対|はんたい}され<b>ても</b>、{f|彼|かれ}と{f|結婚|けっこん}する。
(b) {f|親|おや}と{f|縁|えん}を{f|切|き}っ<b>てでも</b>、{f|彼|かれ}と{f|結婚|けっこん}する。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) concedes an obstacle: the parents may object, and the speaker will marry regardless.
Nothing is being chosen except to carry on.

(b) names a price the speaker is willing to pay - cutting ties with the family - as a
deliberate act. It is far more extreme, because the sacrifice is volunteered rather
than merely endured.

</details>
