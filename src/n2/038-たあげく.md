# ～たあげく

**Meaning** · after all that, it ended up like this - and the speaker is not pleased

<code>あげく</code> reports the last stop of a long, wearing process: dithering,
arguing, searching, being messed about. Two things are always true. The process
**dragged on**, and the outcome is one the speaker regards as a poor return for
it - often absurd, often a waste of everything that went before.

## Formation

<code>あげく</code> is the noun {f|挙|あ}げ{f|句|く}, "the closing line of a linked
poem", so it takes a noun-modifying clause: a **た-form** verb, or a noun with `の`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (た-form) | た-form + あげく | {f|悩|なや}む → {f|悩|なや}んだあげく |
| Verb (た-form) | た-form + あげくに | {f|迷|まよ}う → {f|迷|まよ}ったあげくに |
| Noun | Noun + の + あげく | {f|口論|こうろん} → {f|口論|こうろん}のあげく |
| Emphatic | あげくの{f|果|は}てに | - → あげくの{f|果|は}てに{f|泣|な}き{f|出|だ}した |
| ❌ Dictionary form | ❌{f|悩|なや}むあげく | ✅{f|悩|なや}んだあげく |
| ❌ Noun with no の | ❌{f|口論|こうろん}あげく | ✅{f|口論|こうろん}のあげく |
| ❌ Future or plan | ❌…あげく、{f|辞|や}めるつもりだ | ✅…あげく、{f|辞|や}めてしまった |
| ❌ An instant process | ❌ひと{f|目|め}{f|見|み}たあげく | ✅さんざん{f|見|み}{f|比|くら}べたあげく |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - three conditions, and all three must hold. The first clause is
**long and troublesome**, the main clause is **past and definite**, and the outcome
is **bad, wasteful or absurd**. Lose the third and you want
<code>{f|末|すえ}に</code>.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - normally written in kana. The kanji {f|挙|あ}げ{f|句|く} exists and is
occasionally used, but あげく is the ordinary spelling and the one to produce.

</div>

After weeks of sending it off for repair, I ended up having to buy a new one anyway.

## Nuance & register

Neutral in formality - equally at home in a novel, a complaint to a friend and a
newspaper column - but never neutral in **attitude**. Using
<code>あげく</code> is a small act of commentary: you are saying the effort was not
worth it. It pairs naturally with さんざん, {f|何度|なんど}も, {f|長|なが}い{f|間|あいだ},
{f|結局|けっきょく} and endings in `てしまう`, all of which push the same way.

Aimed at yourself it is rueful and rather likeable. Aimed at somebody else it is
close to an accusation, and <code>あげくの{f|果|は}てに</code> - "and to cap it all"
- is openly indignant: 「{f|文句|もんく}を{f|言|い}い{f|続|つづ}け、あげくの{f|果|は}てに{f|帰|かえ}ってしまった」.
Keep that one for situations where you mean it.

Because it delivers a verdict on something finished, the pattern cannot look
forward. No plans, no predictions, no requests. And it needs a stretch of time in
front of it - a decision made in three seconds has no
<code>あげく</code>, however bad the result.

## Contrast

### ～たあげく vs ～た{f|末|すえ}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical structure, opposite verdict.
<code>あげく</code> means the effort was **wasted**;
<code>{f|末|すえ}に</code> is **neutral** and often crowns a genuine effort with a
real result.

</div>

<pre>
{f|一年|いちねん}{f|考|かんが}え{f|続|つづ}け<b>た{f|末|すえ}に</b>、{f|留学|りゅうがく}を{f|決|き}めた。
After a year of thinking it through, I decided to study abroad.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|一年|いちねん}{f|迷|まよ}っ<b>たあげく</b>、{f|留学|りゅうがく}をあきらめた。
After dithering for a year, I gave up on studying abroad.
</pre>

Both took a year. The first presents that year as deliberation that led somewhere;
the second presents it as time thrown away. Swapping the two endings would make the
first sentence sneer at its own decision and the second sound oddly proud of
failing.

### ～たあげく vs ～た{f|結果|けっか}

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|結果|けっか}</code> states an outcome **objectively**
and explains what caused it. <code>あげく</code> adds duration and a sigh.

</div>

<code>た{f|結果|けっか}</code> is the N3 pattern <code>あげく</code> is closest to,
and the divergence is that <code>{f|結果|けっか}</code> has no feelings.

<pre>
{f|調査|ちょうさ}し<b>た{f|結果|けっか}</b>、{f|原因|げんいん}は{f|配線|はいせん}のミスだと{f|分|わ}かった。
Investigation showed the cause to be a wiring error.
</pre>

That sentence would be at home in an official report, where
<code>あげく</code> would be unusable - it would suggest the investigators had
wasted everyone's time. <code>{f|結果|けっか}</code> is also free of the duration
requirement: a single test can have a <code>{f|結果|けっか}</code>, but only a long
ordeal has an <code>あげく</code>.

### ～たあげく vs ～た{f|後|あと}で

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>{f|後|あと}で</code> merely puts two events in **order**.
<code>あげく</code> makes the first one the drawn-out cause of a disappointing
second.

</div>

<pre>
{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|片付|かたづ}け<b>た{f|後|あと}で</b>、{f|買|か}い{f|物|もの}に{f|行|い}きます。
I'll go shopping after I've put the luggage away.
</pre>

Neutral sequencing, and note that it can point at the future - something
<code>あげく</code> can never do.

<pre>
{f|荷物|にもつ}を{f|運|はこ}び{f|続|つづ}け<b>たあげく</b>、{f|腰|こし}を{f|痛|いた}めてしまった。
After hauling boxes about for hours on end, I ended up hurting my back.
</pre>

Here the first clause is not just earlier, it is what wore the speaker down. Drop
<code>あげく</code> for <code>{f|後|あと}で</code> and the sentence stops explaining
anything.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ さんざん{f|悩|なや}む<b>あげく</b>、{f|断|ことわ}った。 → ✅ さんざん{f|悩|なや}ん<b>だあげく</b>、{f|断|ことわ}った。
  <code>あげく</code> is a noun, so the clause in front is in noun-modifying shape -
  and specifically the た-form, because the process is over.

- ❌ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}し<b>たあげく</b>、{f|大会|たいかい}で{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}した。 → ✅ {f|毎日|まいにち}{f|練習|れんしゅう}し<b>た{f|末|すえ}に</b>、{f|大会|たいかい}で{f|優勝|ゆうしょう}した。
  A good outcome kills <code>あげく</code> outright. Winning is what
  <code>{f|末|すえ}に</code> is for.

- ❌ {f|迷|まよ}っ<b>たあげく</b>、{f|明日|あした}{f|決|き}めます。 → ✅ {f|迷|まよ}っ<b>たあげく</b>、{f|昨日|きのう}{f|決|き}めました。
  The verdict is passed on something already finished. There is no future
  <code>あげく</code>.

- ❌ {f|長|なが}い{f|議論|ぎろん}<b>あげく</b> → ✅ {f|長|なが}い{f|議論|ぎろん}<b>のあげく</b>
  A noun needs `の` in front of it, exactly as it would before any other noun.

## Practice

**1.** {f|何時間|なんじかん}も{f|列|れつ}に{f|並|なら}んだ____、{f|目当|めあ}ての{f|商品|しょうひん}は{f|売|う}り{f|切|き}れていた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|並|なら}んだ<b>あげく</b>

Hours of queueing followed by an empty shelf is the textbook shape: a long ordeal,
a past outcome, and the outcome makes the ordeal pointless.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|三年間|さんねんかん}{f|研究|けんきゅう}を{f|続|つづ}けた（あげく・{f|末|すえ}に）、ついに{f|新|あたら}しい{f|薬|くすり}を{f|完成|かんせい}させた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|三年間|さんねんかん}{f|研究|けんきゅう}を{f|続|つづ}けた<b>{f|末|すえ}に</b>、ついに{f|新|あたら}しい{f|薬|くすり}を{f|完成|かんせい}させた。

The outcome is a success, and ついに celebrates it.
<code>あげく</code> would turn three years of research into a sad story, which is
the opposite of what the sentence says.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "After keeping us waiting for two hours, he ended up not turning up at all."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|二時間|にじかん}も{f|待|ま}たせ<b>たあげく</b>、{f|彼|かれ}は{f|結局|けっきょく}{f|来|こ}なかった。

Long, trying, and a thoroughly annoying result - all three conditions met. Note
<code>{f|来|こ}なかった</code>: the negative of <code>{f|来|く}る</code> takes the
こ- stem.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|長|なが}い{f|相談|そうだん}あげく、{f|来月|らいげつ}{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}すつもりです。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|長|なが}い{f|相談|そうだん}<b>のあげく</b>、{f|引|ひ}っ{f|越|こ}すことになってしまった。

Two faults. A noun takes `の` before <code>あげく</code>; and the main clause cannot
be a plan, because <code>あげく</code> passes judgement on a process that has already
run its course.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|散々|さんざん}{f|迷|まよ}っ<b>たあげく</b>、{f|一番|いちばん}{f|安|やす}いものを{f|買|か}った。
(b) {f|散々|さんざん}{f|迷|まよ}っ<b>た{f|末|すえ}に</b>、{f|一番|いちばん}{f|安|やす}いものを{f|買|か}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is self-mocking. All that agonising, and the speaker went for the cheapest item
anyway - the buying up front makes the deliberation look silly.

(b) treats the same deliberation as work that produced a considered choice. The
cheapest item was what the thinking arrived at, not what the thinking was wasted
on.

</details>
