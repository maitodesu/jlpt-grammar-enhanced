# ～に向かって

**Meaning** · towards; facing; heading for - points the subject at a target, whether that target is a place, a person or a goal

<code>{f|向|む}かう</code> is "to face, to head for". <code>～に{f|向|む}かって</code>
gives the **orientation** of whatever the subject is doing: a body walking that way,
a face turned that way, a voice thrown that way, a situation trending that way. What
it does not do is deliver anything to the target. It describes the heading, not the
arrival - which is why arrival verbs refuse it.

## Formation

<code>{f|向|む}かう</code> is intransitive, so the target takes `に` (or `へ`), never
`を`. A **noun only**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (place or direction) | Noun + に{f|向|む}かって | {f|駅|えき} → {f|駅|えき}に{f|向|む}かって |
| Noun (stiffer, written) | Noun + に{f|向|む}かい | {f|北|きた} → {f|北|きた}に{f|向|む}かい |
| Modifying a following noun | Noun + に{f|向|む}かう + Noun | {f|山頂|さんちょう} → {f|山頂|さんちょう}に{f|向|む}かう{f|道|みち} |
| Person, before a verb of speech | Person + に{f|向|む}かって | {f|上司|じょうし} → {f|上司|じょうし}に{f|向|む}かって |
| A state trending somewhere | Noun + に{f|向|む}かっている | {f|回復|かいふく} → {f|回復|かいふく}に{f|向|む}かっている |
| With `へ` instead | Noun + へ{f|向|む}かって | {f|南|みなみ} → {f|南|みなみ}へ{f|向|む}かって |

<div class="note">

**Note** - <code>{f|机|つくえ}に{f|向|む}かう</code> has hardened into an idiom meaning
"to sit down to work" - homework, writing, study. It is not really about the
furniture: {f|一日中|いちにちじゅう}{f|机|つくえ}に{f|向|む}かっていた means you spent
the day working, wherever you were sitting.

</div>

## Examples

<pre>
{f|駅|えき}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>{f|歩|ある}いています。
I'm walking towards the station.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|鏡|かがみ}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>スピーチの{f|練習|れんしゅう}をした。
I practised my speech in front of the mirror.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|台風|たいふう}は{f|北|きた}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>{f|進|すす}んでいる。
The typhoon is moving northwards.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|目標|もくひょう}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>{f|一歩|いっぽ}ずつ{f|進|すす}むしかない。
There is nothing for it but to move towards the goal one step at a time.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|祖母|そぼ}の{f|体調|たいちょう}は、ゆっくりですが{f|回復|かいふく}<b>に{f|向|む}かっています</b>。
My grandmother's health is slowly turning towards recovery.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|部長|ぶちょう}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>そんな{f|口|くち}のきき{f|方|かた}をするものではない。
That is no way to talk to the department head.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|毎晩|まいばん}{f|机|つくえ}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>いるのに{f|考|かんが}えがまとまらず、{f|一行|いちぎょう}も{f|書|か}けない{f|日|ひ}が{f|続|つづ}いている。
I sit down to it every evening, but my thoughts will not come together and I have gone days without writing a line.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|窓|まど}の{f|外|そと}では、{f|夕日|ゆうひ}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>{f|鳥|とり}の{f|群|む}れが{f|飛|と}んでいくのが{f|見|み}えた。
Outside the window I could see a flock of birds flying off towards the setting sun.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

In its physical sense the pattern is completely register-neutral and belongs to
ordinary speech as much as to writing. Weather forecasts, directions, narration -
all of it.

The use with a **person** is the one to handle with care. Putting somebody in front
of <code>に{f|向|む}かって</code> and then a verb of speech frames you as squared up
to them, and the sentence usually turns out to be about rudeness or defiance:
<code>{f|先生|せんせい}に{f|向|む}かって{f|何|なん}だ、その{f|態度|たいど}は</code>. Neutral
"say to somebody" is plain `に`. Use <code>に{f|向|む}かって</code> deliberately, when
the facing-up-to is the point.

The **figurative** use - <code>{f|解決|かいけつ}に{f|向|む}かう</code>,
<code>{f|回復|かいふく}に{f|向|む}かう</code>,
<code>{f|悪化|あっか}に{f|向|む}かう</code> - reads as measured and slightly formal, and
is common in news and in medical or economic commentary. It nearly always appears in
the ている form, because it describes a trend in progress.

<code>に{f|向|む}かい</code> is the written connective. `へ` in place of `に` is
slightly more literary and stresses the direction over the destination; both are
correct.

## Contrast

### ～に{f|向|む}かって vs bare ～へ・～に

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `へ` and `に` name a **destination**.
<code>に{f|向|む}かって</code> names the **heading**, and says nothing about getting
there.

</div>

<pre>
{f|来週|らいしゅう}、{f|京都|きょうと}<b>へ</b>{f|行|い}きます。
I'm going to Kyoto next week.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|車|くるま}は{f|京都|きょうと}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>{f|走|はし}り{f|続|つづ}けた。
The car kept driving in the direction of Kyoto.
</pre>

The first is a trip, arrival included; the second is a stretch of road with the
front of the car pointed a certain way, and it is perfectly compatible with never
arriving. That gap is also why <code>{f|山|やま}に{f|向|む}かって{f|叫|さけ}ぶ</code>
works and <code>{f|山|やま}へ{f|叫|さけ}ぶ</code> does not: a shout has a direction but
no destination.

The corollary is that arrival verbs - <code>{f|着|つ}く</code>,
<code>{f|入|はい}る</code>, <code>{f|到着|とうちゃく}する</code> - take plain `に` and
reject this pattern outright.

### ～に{f|向|む}かって vs ～に{f|向|む}けて

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|向|む}かって</code>: the subject **itself** faces or
moves that way. <code>に{f|向|む}けて</code>: something is **aimed** at the target, or
work is being done with a **future date** in view.

</div>

<pre>
{f|海|うみ}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>{f|大声|おおごえ}で{f|叫|さけ}んだ。
I shouted at the top of my voice out towards the sea.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|来年|らいねん}の{f|大会|たいかい}<b>に{f|向|む}けて</b>{f|練習|れんしゅう}を{f|始|はじ}めた。
We have started training with next year's tournament in view.
</pre>

The split is reliable. A physical target you could point at takes
<code>に{f|向|む}かって</code>. A scheduled event you are preparing for takes
<code>に{f|向|む}けて</code> - <code>{f|開催|かいさい}に{f|向|む}けて</code>,
<code>{f|実現|じつげん}に{f|向|む}けて</code>, <code>{f|試験|しけん}に{f|向|む}けて</code> -
and this is one of the most common phrases in Japanese news writing.

### ～に{f|向|む}かって vs ～に{f|対|たい}して

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - <code>に{f|向|む}かって</code> is **physical facing**, and with a
person it tends to flag confrontation. <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> is **abstract
targeting**, and is neutral.

</div>

<pre>
お{f|客|きゃく}さま<b>に{f|対|たい}して</b>{f|失礼|しつれい}な{f|態度|たいど}を{f|取|と}ってはいけません。
You must not behave rudely towards customers.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|親|おや}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>{f|大|おお}きな{f|声|こえ}を{f|出|だ}すな。
Don't raise your voice at your parents.
</pre>

The first states a policy about a category of people; the second is about a person
standing in front of another person. <code>に{f|対|たい}して</code> will happily take
institutions, abstractions and even numbers -
<code>{f|昨年|さくねん}に{f|対|たい}して{f|一割|いちわり}{f|増|ぞう}</code>, "up ten per
cent on last year". <code>に{f|向|む}かって</code> needs something you could physically
turn and face.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|空港|くうこう}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>{f|着|つ}いた。 → ✅ {f|空港|くうこう}<b>に</b>{f|着|つ}いた。
  Arrival verbs take plain `に`. This pattern describes the journey's heading, and a
  heading cannot arrive.

- ❌ {f|試験|しけん}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|勉強|べんきょう}しています。 → ✅ {f|試験|しけん}<b>に{f|向|む}けて</b>{f|毎日|まいにち}{f|勉強|べんきょう}しています。
  A scheduled event you are preparing for takes
  <code>に{f|向|む}けて</code>. You are not walking at the exam.

- ❌ {f|先生|せんせい}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>{f|質問|しつもん}しました。 → ✅ {f|先生|せんせい}<b>に</b>{f|質問|しつもん}しました。
  An ordinary question to somebody takes plain `に`. With
  <code>に{f|向|む}かって</code> the sentence suggests you rounded on the teacher, which
  is presumably not what happened.

## Practice

**1.** {f|船|ふね}は{f|小|ちい}さな{f|島|しま}____ゆっくりと{f|進|すす}んでいった。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|島|しま}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>ゆっくりと{f|進|すす}んでいった。

The island is where the bow is pointed. Nothing in the sentence says the boat got
there, and that is exactly the space this pattern occupies.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|発表|はっぴょう}（に{f|向|む}かって・に{f|向|む}けて）{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|集|あつ}めています。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|来月|らいげつ}の{f|発表|はっぴょう}<b>に{f|向|む}けて</b>{f|資料|しりょう}を{f|集|あつ}めています。

A presentation is a date in the calendar, not a place you can face, so preparation
for it takes <code>に{f|向|む}けて</code>. This is the standard phrasing for working
towards a deadline.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The situation is finally moving towards a settlement."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|事態|じたい}はようやく{f|解決|かいけつ}<b>に{f|向|む}かっています</b>。

The figurative sense - a state of affairs trending somewhere - is standard here, and
it wants the ている form because the movement is still under way. This is the
register a news bulletin uses.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|目標|もくひょう}を{f|向|む}かって{f|一年|いちねん}{f|頑張|がんば}ります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|目標|もくひょう}<b>に{f|向|む}かって</b>{f|一年|いちねん}{f|頑張|がんば}ります。

<code>{f|向|む}かう</code> is intransitive: nothing is being acted on, so `を` has no
role. The target of a heading is always `に`, or occasionally `へ`.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|課長|かちょう}に{f|向|む}かって「それは{f|違|ちが}います」と{f|言|い}った。
(b) {f|課長|かちょう}に「それは{f|違|ちが}います」と{f|言|い}った。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) stages the moment: you turned to face your section head and contradicted him. The
pattern foregrounds the confrontation, and in most contexts the sentence is being told
as a story about nerve, or about bad manners.

(b) simply reports who was told what. It is the neutral option, and it is what you
would use if the point of the sentence were the content of the correction rather than
the act of making it.

</details>
