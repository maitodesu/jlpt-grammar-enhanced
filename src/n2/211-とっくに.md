# とっくに

**Meaning** · already, long since - and a good deal earlier than you seem to think

`とっくに` is `もう` with an attitude. It does not just report that something has
happened; it corrects an assumption that it has not, and it insists that the event
is far back in the past.

## Usage

`とっくに` is an adverb and goes in front of the predicate. `もう` often precedes
it for emphasis: <code>もうとっくに{f|終|お}わっている</code>.

The shape of the predicate is the thing to learn. `とっくに` looks **backwards**,
so it takes

- a plain past - とっくに{f|帰|かえ}った
- a resultative <code>～ている</code> - とっくに{f|過|す}ぎている, とっくに{f|着|つ}いている
- <code>～てしまった</code>・<code>～ちゃった</code> - とっくに{f|売|う}り{f|切|き}れてしまった

It cannot take a plain future: ❌とっくに{f|始|はじ}まります is not Japanese.

Negatives are possible where the negative itself describes a long-standing state:
<code>あの{f|店|みせ}はとっくにないよ</code>, "that shop closed ages ago".

Two variants you will meet: <code>とっくの{f|昔|むかし}に</code>, an emphatic "ages
and ages ago", and `とうに`, the same word in a more literary key.

## Examples

<pre>
バスは<b>とっくに</b>{f|行|い}ってしまった。
The bus left ages ago.
</pre>

<pre>
その{f|話|はなし}なら<b>とっくに</b>{f|知|し}っているよ。
I've known about that for ages.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|締|し}め{f|切|き}りは<b>とっくに</b>{f|過|す}ぎていますが、まだ{f|半分|はんぶん}しか{f|書|か}けていません。
The deadline went by long ago and I've still only written half of it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|心配|しんぱい}しなくていいよ。{f|息子|むすこ}は<b>とっくに</b>{f|家|いえ}に{f|着|つ}いて、ごはんまで{f|食|た}べ{f|終|お}わってる。
You don't need to worry - our son got home long ago and has even finished his dinner.
</pre>

## Nuance & register

Colloquial. `とっくに` belongs to speech and to casual writing; a report or a
business email uses <code>{f|既|すで}に</code>.

It is also faintly blunt. Because the word implies "obviously, and long before
now", replying <code>とっくに{f|終|お}わっています</code> to a manager who has just
asked about your work can land as impatient - "why are you only asking now?" With
<code>もう{f|終|お}わっています</code> the same fact carries no edge at all. Among
friends that edge is often the whole point, and `とっくに` is a standard way of
brushing off a warning you did not need.

The gap it implies must be a real one. Something that finished five minutes ago is
not とっくに; the word wants an interval long enough that the listener's picture of
the world has gone stale.

## Contrast

### とっくに vs {f|既|すで}に・もう

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - all three mean "already". `もう` is the neutral default,
<code>{f|既|すで}に</code> is its formal written twin, and `とっくに` adds **"far
earlier than you think"** plus the speaker's attitude.

</div>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}は<b>もう</b>{f|始|はじ}まっています。
The meeting has already started.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|該当|がいとう}の{f|商品|しょうひん}は<b>{f|既|すで}に</b>{f|販売|はんばい}を{f|終了|しゅうりょう}しております。
Sales of the item in question have already ended.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|会議|かいぎ}なら<b>とっくに</b>{f|終|お}わって、みんな{f|帰|かえ}ったよ。
The meeting finished ages ago - everyone's gone home.
</pre>

The first two state a fact. The third also comments on the listener: you are
behind. Note too that `もう` has a second life as "soon"
（<code>もう{f|着|つ}きます</code>）and a third as "any more"
（<code>もう{f|要|い}りません</code>）, neither of which `とっくに` shares.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|映画|えいが}はとっくに{f|始|はじ}まります。 → ✅ {f|映画|えいが}は<b>とっくに</b>{f|始|はじ}まっています。
  The word points into the past, so the predicate has to be past or resultative. A
  plain future contradicts it.

- ❌ ご{f|連絡|れんらく}はとっくに{f|受|う}け{f|取|と}っております。 → ✅ ご{f|連絡|れんらく}は<b>{f|既|すで}に</b>{f|受|う}け{f|取|と}っております。
  Humble ます-form and a colloquial complaint do not belong in the same sentence.
  To a customer, use <code>{f|既|すで}に</code>.

- ❌ {f|五分|ごふん}{f|前|まえ}にとっくに{f|出|で}ました。 → ✅ {f|五分|ごふん}{f|前|まえ}に{f|出|で}ました。
  Five minutes is not "long since". Naming a short interval cancels the one thing
  `とっくに` is for.

## Practice

**1.** 「{f|宿題|しゅくだい}、そろそろやったら?」「____やったよ。」

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

「<b>とっくに</b>やったよ。」

The reply is not simply reporting that the homework is done - it is pushing back
on the reminder. `とっくに` says the parent's information is out of date, which is
exactly the tone the exchange calls for.

</details>

**2.** Choose the natural word: {f|申|もう}し{f|訳|わけ}ございません、その{f|商品|しょうひん}は（とっくに・{f|既|すで}に）{f|完売|かんばい}いたしました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

その{f|商品|しょうひん}は<b>{f|既|すで}に</b>{f|完売|かんばい}いたしました。

Serving a customer, you want the neutral formal word. `とっくに` would imply the
customer should have known better, which is the opposite of an apology.

</details>

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) もう{f|気|き}づいていました。
(b) とっくに{f|気|き}づいていました。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a plain statement: by the time it came up, the speaker had noticed.

(b) puts a distance behind the noticing. It says the speaker worked it out long
before the other person got round to mentioning it, and depending on the voice it
can be reassuring （"don't worry, I was ahead of you"）or cutting （"you're the
last to know"）.

</details>
