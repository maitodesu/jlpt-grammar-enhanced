# ～てまで

**Meaning** · going so far as to; even at the cost of - an extreme means is named, and the speaker doubts it was worth it

`てまで` measures how far somebody was willing to go. The `まで` is the ordinary "as far
as": the て-clause marks the outer limit of what was done, and the main clause then
asks, or denies, whether the goal justified going that far. *Lying to win. Borrowing
money to travel. Wrecking your health to keep working.*

On this batch's two axes - how much the speaker minds, and how formal the frame is - 
`てまで` is *the one that minds most openly*, and it is *register-neutral*. It is not a
concession made to be polite; it is a concession made in order to disapprove.

## Formation

The verb takes the て-form. A noun naming the extreme act uses `までして` instead.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + まで | {f|借|か}りる → {f|借|か}りてまで |
| Verb (て → で) | で-form + まで | {f|並|なら}ぶ → {f|並|なら}んでまで |
| Verb, emphatic | て-form + までも | {f|傷|きず}つける → {f|傷|きず}つけてまでも |
| する-verb | ～してまで | {f|徹夜|てつや}する → {f|徹夜|てつや}してまで |
| Noun | Noun + までして | {f|借金|しゃっきん} → {f|借金|しゃっきん}までして |
| Typical main clause | ～たくない／～{f|必要|ひつよう}はない／～ことはない | - → うそをついてまで{f|勝|か}ちたくない |
| Typical main clause | ～だろうか／～{f|価値|かち}があるのか | - → そこまでする{f|価値|かち}があるのか |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - the main clause must **judge**. A want, a refusal, a "there's no
need", or a question. ❌<code>{f|走|はし}ってまで{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}った</code> is not a
sentence, because nothing is being weighed - that is just
<code>{f|走|はし}って{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}った</code>.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - the mirror image of this pattern is `～てでも`, "even if I have to". The two
share almost the same picture and point in opposite directions: `てでも` is the
speaker's own determination, `てまで` is usually somebody else's excess.

</div>

## Nuance & register

`てまで` is neutral as to formality - it works in conversation, in essays and in
newspaper columns alike - but it is not neutral as to attitude. The default reading is
critical. Naming an extreme act and then attaching a want, a need or a question invites
the listener to find the act excessive, and that is how a Japanese listener will take
it unless something else in the sentence says otherwise.

Two things can shift it. First, a straightforwardly positive main clause turns it into
admiration or at least neutral report:
<code>{f|会社|かいしゃ}を{f|辞|や}めてまで{f|夢|ゆめ}を{f|追|お}った</code> can be told
either way, and the tone of voice decides. Second, in the first person it can express
your own limit: <code>そこまでしてほしくない</code> - I would not want anyone to go to
that trouble for me.

The noun frame `までして` is worth having as well.
<code>{f|借金|しゃっきん}までして</code>, <code>{f|徹夜|てつや}までして</code>,
<code>{f|手術|しゅじゅつ}までして</code> - the noun names the extreme act directly, and
the meaning is identical. Choose it when the extremity is a *thing* rather than an
action you want to spell out with a verb.

Because the pattern is a judgement, it can be sharp. Saying
<code>そんなことをしてまで{f|勝|か}ちたいの?</code> to somebody's face is a rebuke, not a
question. Used about yourself it is safe; used about the person you are talking to,
handle it as you would any criticism.

## Contrast

### てまで vs てでも

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `てでも` = "even if I have to", and the speaker **will**. `てまで` =
"going so far as to", and the speaker thinks somebody **shouldn't have**.

</div>

<pre>
タクシーを{f|使|つか}っ<b>てでも</b>{f|開演|かいえん}までに{f|着|つ}きたい。
I want to get there before curtain-up even if I have to take a taxi.
</pre>

<pre>
タクシーを{f|使|つか}っ<b>てまで</b>{f|見|み}に{f|行|い}く{f|映画|えいが}ではない。
It isn't a film worth taking a taxi to get to.
</pre>

The same means, the opposite verdict. `てでも` looks forward and expresses resolve, so
it pairs with <code>～たい</code>, <code>～つもりだ</code> and volitional endings;
`てまで` looks at the price and finds it too high, so it pairs with negatives and
rhetorical questions. Swapping them reverses what you meant.

### てまで vs ても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `ても` concedes a **condition**. `てまで` measures the **cost of the
means**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|高|たか}く<b>ても</b>この{f|辞書|じしょ}は{f|買|か}うつもりだ。
I'm going to buy this dictionary even if it's expensive.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|生活|せいかつ}を{f|切|き}りつめ<b>てまで</b>{f|買|か}うような{f|辞書|じしょ}ではない。
It isn't the kind of dictionary you cut back on living expenses to buy.
</pre>

`ても` accepts a circumstance the speaker does not control - the price is what it is.
`てまで` names something the speaker chooses to do, and asks whether the choice makes
sense. If the clause is an action taken deliberately in pursuit of the goal, you want
`てまで`.

### てまで vs plain まで "until"

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - dictionary form + `まで` = **until**. て-form + `まで` = **to the
extent of**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}が{f|止|や}む<b>まで</b>ここで{f|待|ま}ちましょう。
Let's wait here until the rain stops.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|雨|あめ}に{f|濡|ぬ}れ<b>てまで</b>{f|写真|しゃしん}を{f|撮|と}りたくはない。
I don't want a photograph badly enough to get soaked for it.
</pre>

The particle is the same word in both - `まで` marks a limit - but the limit is
temporal in the first and a limit of effort in the second, and the verb form is what
tells them apart. Getting the form wrong turns "going so far as to get wet" into "until
I get wet", which is a different sentence entirely.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|徹夜|てつや}する<b>まで</b>{f|準備|じゅんび}した。 → ✅ {f|徹夜|てつや}し<b>てまで</b>{f|準備|じゅんび}した。
  The dictionary form gives "until", not "to the extent of". This pattern needs the
  て-form.

- ❌ {f|走|はし}っ<b>てまで</b>{f|電車|でんしゃ}に{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}った。 → ✅ {f|走|はし}って{f|電車|でんしゃ}に{f|間|ま}に{f|合|あ}った。
  Nothing is being weighed here, so the pattern has no work to do. `てまで` needs a
  main clause that judges the cost.

- ❌ {f|無理|むり}をし<b>てまで</b>{f|今日|きょう}{f|中|じゅう}に{f|仕上|しあ}げます。 → ✅ {f|無理|むり}をし<b>てでも</b>{f|今日|きょう}{f|中|じゅう}に{f|仕上|しあ}げます。
  Announcing your own determination is `てでも`. `てまで` would have you criticising
  the very thing you are promising to do.

- ❌ {f|借金|しゃっきん}まで<b>してまで</b>{f|車|くるま}を{f|買|か}った。 → ✅ {f|借金|しゃっきん}<b>までして</b>{f|車|くるま}を{f|買|か}った。（or {f|借金|しゃっきん}をし<b>てまで</b>）
  Pick one frame. `までして` after the noun and `てまで` after the verb do the same job,
  and using both at once doubles the particle.

## Practice

**1.** {f|順番|じゅんばん}を{f|抜|ぬ}かし____{f|乗|の}りたい{f|電車|でんしゃ}ではない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|順番|じゅんばん}を{f|抜|ぬ}かし<b>てまで</b>{f|乗|の}りたい{f|電車|でんしゃ}ではない。

Queue-jumping is the extreme means, and the negative main clause delivers the verdict:
not worth it. The て-form plus `まで` is the frame.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|終電|しゅうでん}を{f|逃|のが}したら、タクシーを{f|使|つか}っ（てまで・てでも）{f|帰|かえ}ります。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

タクシーを{f|使|つか}っ<b>てでも</b>{f|帰|かえ}ります。

The speaker is stating their own intention, and `てでも` is the determined one. `てまで`
would mean the speaker considers taking a taxi excessive - which contradicts the
promise to get home.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I wouldn't go so far as to skip work to see a concert."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|仕事|しごと}を{f|休|やす}ん<b>でまで</b>コンサートに{f|行|い}こうとは{f|思|おも}わない。

<code>{f|休|やす}む</code> gives the で-form, so the joint is
<code>{f|休|やす}んでまで</code>. The main clause is a refusal, which is the natural
company this pattern keeps.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|家|いえ}を{f|売|う}るまで、{f|息子|むすこ}を{f|留学|りゅうがく}させた。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|家|いえ}を{f|売|う}っ<b>てまで</b>、{f|息子|むすこ}を{f|留学|りゅうがく}させた。

With the dictionary form, `まで` means "until the house is sold", which is not the
intended sense. The て-form turns it into "went as far as selling the house".

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|睡眠|すいみん}{f|時間|じかん}を{f|削|けず}っ<b>てまで</b>{f|練習|れんしゅう}している。
(b) {f|睡眠|すいみん}{f|時間|じかん}を{f|削|けず}っ<b>ても</b>{f|練習|れんしゅう}している。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) presents cutting back on sleep as the extreme lengths the person has gone to, and
invites a judgement on it - usually "that's too far", occasionally "that's real
dedication", depending on the rest of the sentence and the speaker's tone.

(b) is awkward, and that awkwardness is instructive. `ても` concedes a condition the
subject does not control, but losing sleep here is the very choice being described, so
there is nothing to concede. Where the clause names a deliberate sacrifice made in
pursuit of the goal, `てまで` is the pattern that fits.

</details>
