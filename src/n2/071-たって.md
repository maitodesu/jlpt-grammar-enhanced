# ～たって

**Meaning** · even if; even though; no matter how - the spoken, shrugging version of `～ても`

`たって` says exactly what `ても` says and says it in a T-shirt. It concedes a
condition and dismisses it in the same breath: *fine, suppose it rains - we're playing
anyway.* Because it only lives in casual speech, it carries the attitude of casual
speech with it, which is usually defiance, impatience, or a wave of the hand.

On this batch's two axes - how much the speaker minds, and how formal the frame is - 
`たって` is *audibly impatient* and *as informal as the group gets*. `とも`, later in
this batch, means the same thing at the opposite end of both scales.

## Formation

Take the plain **past** form and swap the final `た` for `たって`. A verb whose past
ends in `だ` gives `だって`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (た) | past た → たって | {f|走|はし}った → {f|走|はし}ったって |
| Verb (だ) | past だ → だって | {f|呼|よ}んだ → {f|呼|よ}んだって |
| Verb (negative) | ～なく + たって | {f|行|い}かない → {f|行|い}かなくたって |
| い-adjective | ～く + たって | {f|高|たか}い → {f|高|たか}くたって |
| い-adjective (negative) | ～くなく + たって | {f|安|やす}くない → {f|安|やす}くなくたって |
| な-adjective | stem + だって | {f|下手|へた} → {f|下手|へた}だって |
| Noun | Noun + だって | {f|子|こ}ども → {f|子|こ}どもだって |
| Permission | ～たって + いい | {f|休|やす}む → {f|休|やす}んだっていい |
| With an intensifier | いくら／どんなに + ～たって | - → いくら{f|言|い}ったって |

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - build it from the **past tense**, not the dictionary form:
✅<code>{f|飲|の}んだって</code>, ❌<code>{f|飲|の}むたって</code>. If you can say the past
tense, you can say the `たって`.

</div>

<div class="note">

**Note** - nouns and な-adjectives come out as `だって`, which is the same shape as the
"even X" particle in <code>{f|子|こ}どもだって{f|知|し}っている</code>. That is not a
coincidence and not a problem: they are the casual counterpart of `でも`, and Japanese
speakers do not keep them apart either.

</div>

</pre>

## Nuance & register

`たって` is spoken Japanese and nothing else. It belongs in conversation, in chat
messages, in dialogue in a novel and in the chattier kind of blog post. It does not
belong in a report, an examination answer, a business e-mail or a news article, where
`ても` is the neutral form and `としても` or `にせよ` the formal ones. Putting `たって`
into a written assignment is the single most likely way to misuse it.

Within speech it is register-neutral as to age and gender - everybody says it - but it
is not tone-neutral. It leans towards brushing something off, so it pairs naturally
with `いくら`, `どんなに`, `なんか`, `よ` and `さ`, and with rhetorical questions. Said
to a superior it can sound flippant even when the content is polite, which is why
<code>{f|急|いそ}いだって{f|無理|むり}です</code> sounds worse than
<code>{f|急|いそ}いでも{f|無理|むり}です</code> despite meaning the same.

Two very common fixed shapes are worth learning whole. <code>～たっていい</code> is the
casual <code>～てもいい</code>: <code>{f|先|さき}に{f|帰|かえ}ったっていいよ</code>,
"you can go home first if you like." And `なんたって`, a worn-down
<code>{f|何|なん}と{f|言|い}ったって</code>, means "more than anything" -
<code>なんたって{f|安|やす}いのが{f|一番|いちばん}だ</code>.

Do not confuse the register with the strength. `たって` is casual, but a casual
concession can be very firm: <code>{f|何|なに}を{f|言|い}われたってやめない</code> is
not a soft sentence.

## Contrast

### たって vs ても

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - identical meaning; `ても` is neutral and works everywhere, `たって`
is casual and works only in speech.

</div>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}を{f|読|よ}ん<b>でも</b>、{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}が{f|分|わ}かりませんでした。
Even after reading the instructions I couldn't work out how to use it.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|説明|せつめい}{f|読|よ}ん<b>だって</b>、{f|使|つか}い{f|方|かた}なんか{f|分|わ}かんないよ。
Reading the instructions isn't going to tell you how to use it.
</pre>

Notice what travels with the choice: the second sentence has also dropped its particle
and contracted <code>{f|分|わ}からない</code>. Casual grammar arrives in convoys. A
`たって` sitting inside an otherwise polite sentence is the seam that gives a learner
away.

### たって vs the quotative って after a past tense

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - if the sentence **stops** there, or <code>{f|言|い}う</code>／<code>{f|聞|き}く</code> follows, it is
hearsay. If a **second clause** follows, it is the concession.

</div>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}、もう{f|出|で}かけ<b>たって</b>。
He says he's already gone out.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|彼|かれ}がもう{f|出|で}かけ<b>たって</b>、{f|待|ま}っていれば{f|戻|もど}ってくるよ。
Even if he's already gone out, he'll come back if you wait.
</pre>

The syllables are identical; only the shape of the sentence tells them apart. The
hearsay one is <code>～と{f|言|い}っていた</code> worn down to `って`, and it ends the
sentence with a falling tone. The concessive one is a joint and demands a main clause
after it.

### たって vs たら

<div class="rule">

**Rule of thumb** - `たら` says the result **follows**; `たって` says it **doesn't**.

</div>

<pre>
{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}け<b>たら</b>、{f|涼|すず}しくなった。
I opened the window and it got cooler.
</pre>

<pre>
{f|窓|まど}を{f|開|あ}け<b>たって</b>、{f|涼|すず}しくならないよ。
Opening the window isn't going to cool it down.
</pre>

Both are built on the past tense, and beginners who have just learned `たら` regularly
hear `たって` as a variant of it. They are opposites: `たら` is a conditional that
delivers, `たって` is a conditional that is refused.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ {f|行|い}く<b>たって</b> → ✅ {f|行|い}っ<b>たって</b>
  Build it from the past tense. The dictionary form never takes `たって`.

- ❌ {f|高|たか}い<b>たって</b> → ✅ {f|高|たか}く<b>たって</b>
  い-adjectives go through the `く` form first, exactly as they do for `ても`
  (<code>{f|高|たか}くても</code>).

- ❌ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>でたって</b> → ✅ {f|学生|がくせい}<b>だって</b>
  Nouns and な-adjectives take `だって` directly; there is no `で` in front of it.

- ❌ (in a report) {f|多少|たしょう}{f|費用|ひよう}がかかっ<b>たって</b>、{f|安全|あんぜん}を{f|優先|ゆうせん}すべきである。 → ✅ {f|多少|たしょう}{f|費用|ひよう}がかかっ<b>ても</b>、{f|安全|あんぜん}を{f|優先|ゆうせん}すべきである。
  The sentence is written in `である` style, and `たって` cannot appear there. Register
  has to match end to end.

## Practice

**1.** いくら{f|待|ま}っ____、バスは{f|来|こ}ないよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

いくら{f|待|ま}っ<b>たって</b>、バスは{f|来|こ}ないよ。

<code>いくら</code> sets up a "no matter how much", and in casual speech that is
completed by `たって`. The past-tense stem is <code>{f|待|ま}った</code>, so the joint
is <code>{f|待|ま}ったって</code>.

</details>

**2.** Choose: {f|会議|かいぎ}の{f|資料|しりょう}は、{f|多少|たしょう}{f|遅|おく}れ（たって・ても）{f|必|かなら}ず{f|提出|ていしゅつ}してください。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|多少|たしょう}{f|遅|おく}れ<b>ても</b>{f|必|かなら}ず{f|提出|ていしゅつ}してください。

The sentence ends in <code>ください</code>, so it is polite, and a polite sentence
takes `ても`. `たって` would clash with the request form and sound offhand about
somebody else's deadline.

</details>

**3.** Translate into Japanese, casually: "You don't have to come early - I'll be here."

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

{f|早|はや}く{f|来|こ}なく<b>たって</b>いいよ。{f|僕|ぼく}がここにいるから。

The negative goes through <code>～なく</code> before `たって`, and
<code>～たっていい</code> is the casual <code>～てもいい</code> that grants permission.
Note <code>{f|来|こ}ない</code>: <code>{f|来|く}る</code> takes こ- in the negative.

</details>

**4.** Find and fix the error: {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}るたって、{f|傘|かさ}はいらない。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

✅ {f|雨|あめ}が{f|降|ふ}っ<b>たって</b>、{f|傘|かさ}はいらない。

<code>{f|降|ふ}る</code> is a う-verb whose past is <code>{f|降|ふ}った</code>, so the
concessive is <code>{f|降|ふ}ったって</code>. Attaching `たって` to the dictionary form
is the commonest error with this pattern.

</details>

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance:
(a) {f|試|ため}し<b>ても</b>{f|同|おな}じだと{f|思|おも}います。
(b) {f|試|ため}し<b>たって</b>{f|同|おな}じだよ。

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

(a) is a neutral, polite opinion. `ても` carries no attitude of its own, and
<code>と{f|思|おも}います</code> softens it further - this is something you could say to
a client.

(b) is the same content thrown away over your shoulder. `たって` plus the plain ending
and `よ` makes it dismissive: don't waste your time. Between friends that is friendly
bluntness; said upwards at work it would sound as though you could not be bothered.

</details>
