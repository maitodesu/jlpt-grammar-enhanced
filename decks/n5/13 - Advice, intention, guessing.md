# N5 - Advice, intention, guessing

- ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to
- ～つもり - plan to; intend to
- だろう・でしょう - probably; … right?
- んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation

---

# ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to

**Meaning** · you'd better do it - doing this is the better of the two options

`ほう` means "side" or "alternative", so `～ほうがいい` literally weighs one choice against another: *the doing-it side is better*. That comparison never fully goes away, and it is what gives the pattern its bite. Saying it means you have judged the listener's situation and found their current course wanting.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Formation

For advice, the verb goes in the **た-form**. This is the form you will use ninety percent of the time, and it is not a past tense.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (advice) | た-form + ほうがいい | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだほうがいい |
| Verb (negative advice) | ない-form + ほうがいい | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないほうがいい |
| Verb (general comparison) | dictionary form + ほうがいい | <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>くほうがいい |
| い-adjective | plain form + ほうがいい | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いほうがいい |
| な-adjective | な + ほうがいい | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなほうがいい |
| Noun | の + ほうがいい | バス → バスのほうがいい |
| Polite | いい → いいです | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだほうがいいです |
| Past ("should have") | いい → よかった | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだほうがよかった |

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - affirmative advice takes `た`; negative advice takes `ない`. There is no ❌`～なかったほうがいい`. The two halves are not symmetrical, and this is the single most common error with the pattern.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Examples

<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>たほうがいい**よ。<br>*You should get to bed early.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないほうがいい**です。<br>*It'd be better not to go today.*

<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだほうがいい**ですよ。<br>*You ought to take some medicine.*

かさを**<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていったほうがいい**よ。<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>から<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>だって。<br>*Take an umbrella - apparently it's going to rain this afternoon.*

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Examples (cont.)

あの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いから、こっちで**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ったほうがいい**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う。<br>*That shop's expensive, so I think you're better off buying it here.*

<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby><ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>くにこの<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>らないほうがいい**です。<ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>くて<ruby>危<rt>あぶ</rt></ruby>ないですから。<br>*You'd better not take this road late at night - it's dark and it's dangerous.*

<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>があるなら、<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んで<ruby>病院<rt>びょういん</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったほうがいい**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いますよ。<br>*If you've got a fever, I really think you should take the day off and see a doctor.*

<ruby>上司<rt>じょうし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>してから**<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めたほうがいい**んじゃないでしょうか。<br>*Wouldn't it be better to decide after you've talked it over with your manager?*

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Nuance & register

`ほうがいい` is often taught as a gentle "should", and that is the source of a lot of accidental rudeness. It is a **recommendation with weight behind it**. The comparison built into `ほう` means that when you recommend option A, you are simultaneously saying option B - the one the listener is currently on - is worse. English "you'd better" carries the same faint edge, and for the same reason.

Some practical consequences:

**Do not aim it upward.** <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ったほうがいいですよ is grammatical, polite in form, and still presumptuous, because you are telling a superior what to do. To a superior, drop the directive framing entirely: `～てはいかがでしょうか`, <code>～てもよろしいかと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code>, or just describe the situation and let them decide.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Nuance & register (cont.)

**`よ` is doing real work.** In speech, `～たほうがいいよ` reads as friendly and concerned; bare `～たほうがいい` can land flat, like a verdict. `～たほうがいいですよ` is the everyday polite advice form you will actually hear.

**Hedges are normal, not weakness.** <code>～と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code>, `～んじゃないですか`, `～かもしれません` all take the edge off without changing the advice. Native speakers use them constantly.

**`～ないほうがいい` is blunter than the affirmative.** Telling someone *not* to do something presupposes they were about to. Reserve it for cases where you mean it - warnings, danger, genuine bad ideas.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two spelling and shape notes. `ほう` is very often written <code><ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby></code>, and you should expect <code><ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>がいい</code> in print. And `いい` swaps freely for its politer relatives: <code>～たほうがよろしいかと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code> is standard business Japanese, while `～たほうがよかった` shifts the whole thing into regret - "I should have."

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · ～たほうがいい vs ～るほうがいい

> **Rule of thumb** - た-form gives advice about *this* situation. Dictionary form compares two options in the abstract.

This is the distinction the pattern is built on, and it is why the "past tense" form is the normal one for advice.

<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだほうがいい**です。<br>*You should take some medicine.*

The た-form presents the action as a completed package - "having taken it" - and holds that up against the listener's actual, present situation. It points at *you*, *now*.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · ～たほうがいい vs ～るほうがいい (cont.)

<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>むほうがいい**です。<br>*Taking medicine is the better approach.*

The dictionary form floats free of anyone in particular. It reads as a general policy statement, which is why using it on a sick friend sounds oddly detached, as though you were reading from a leaflet.

Dictionary form comes into its own when the two options are actually spelled out:

バスで<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くより、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くほうがいい**ですよ。<br>*Going by train is better than going by bus.*

There the sentence really is a comparison, and `より` makes the other side explicit. That is the same `ほうがいい` you meet as the comparison pattern.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · ～ないほうがいい vs ～なくてもいい

> **Rule of thumb** - `～ないほうがいい` = *don't*. `～なくてもいい` = *you don't have to*. One forbids, the other releases.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないほうがいい**です。<br>*You'd better not come tomorrow. (something is wrong with coming)*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいい**です。<br>*You don't have to come tomorrow. (it's optional; come if you like)*

Mixing these up produces one of the more embarrassing N5 errors, because the two sentences are almost opposite in social effect. `～なくてもいい` grants permission; `～ないほうがいい` withdraws it.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · ～ほうがいい vs ～たらどうですか

> **Rule of thumb** - `～ほうがいい` says the alternative is worse. `～たらどうですか` merely puts an idea on the table.

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いたほうがいい**ですよ。<br>*You should ask the teacher.*

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いたらどうですか。<br>*How about asking the teacher?*

The second leaves the decision visibly with the listener, which makes it the safer choice when you are not sure the advice is wanted, or when the person outranks you. It is not always softer - said sharply, `～たらどうですか` can sound impatient - but its default is more open.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · ～ほうがいい vs ～たらどうですか (cont.)

Further up the scale sit `～なければなりません` ("must") and `～べきです` ("ought to", moral weight). `ほうがいい` sits between "how about" and "must": stronger than a suggestion, weaker than an obligation.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>か**なかった**ほうがいいです。 → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>か**ない**ほうがいいです。<br>*Negative advice uses the plain `ない`-form. The た of `～たほうがいい` has no negative counterpart in this pattern.*

- ❌ <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>ですか。<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>**むほうがいい**ですよ。 → ✅ <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>**んだほうがいい**ですよ。<br>*For advice aimed at the person in front of you, use the た-form. The dictionary form turns it into a detached general statement.*

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、もっと<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ったほうがいいですよ。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、そろそろお<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りになってはいかがでしょうか。<br>*The grammar is fine; the direction is not. `ほうがいい` implies the listener is currently choosing wrong, which you do not say upward.*

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないほうがいいです。（intending "you don't have to come"）→ ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいいです。<br>*`～ないほうがいい` tells someone not to do it. Optionality is `～なくてもいい`.*

- ❌ この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んだほうが**いいでした**。 → ✅ この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んだほうが**よかった**です。<br>*`いい` is an い-adjective. Its past is `よかった`, never ❌`いいでした`.*

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>ですね。<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>____ほうがいいですよ。

{reveal}
<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>**んだ**ほうがいいですよ。

{reveal}
Affirmative advice takes the た-form. <code><ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む</code> is a う-verb ending in む, so む → んだ. The た-form here is not past tense - it is what makes the advice land on this person, in this situation.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>危<rt>あぶ</rt></ruby>ないですから、あの<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>は（<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>らない・<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>らなかった）ほうがいいです。

{reveal}
あの<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>らない**ほうがいいです。

{reveal}
Affirmative and negative are not symmetrical here. Affirmative advice uses た, but negative advice uses the plain `ない`-form. ❌<code><ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>らなかったほうがいい</code> is not a form of this pattern at all.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "You'd better take an umbrella."

{reveal}
かさを**<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていったほうがいい**ですよ。 （also fine: かさを<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ったほうがいいですよ。）

{reveal}
た-form because this is advice for the person in front of you. The `ですよ` is worth adding - `よ` frames it as helpful information rather than a verdict, which is how this advice is normally delivered in speech.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the problem: <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、その<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>したほうがいいですよ。

{reveal}
The grammar is correct - the problem is the register.

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、その<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>なさってはいかがでしょうか。 （or: …<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>していただいたほうがよろしいかと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。）

{reveal}
`ほうがいい` carries the implication that the listener's current course is the worse option, so aiming it at a superior is presumptuous however polite the ending is. Reframe as a question or a hedged opinion.

---

## ～ほうがいい - it'd be better to · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) もっと<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたほうがいいです。 (b) もっと<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べるほうがいいです。

{reveal}
(a) is advice directed at the listener: "you should eat more vegetables." The た-form ties the recommendation to this particular person's situation, and implies the speaker has looked at how they currently eat.

{reveal}
(b) is a general comparison: "eating more vegetables is the better option." It floats free of any individual and reads like a statement of policy - fine in a health article, oddly impersonal said to a friend over dinner.

# ～つもり - plan to; intend to

**Meaning** · I intend to; I'm planning to - a decision already made in the speaker's head

`つもり` is a noun meaning something like "intention", so a sentence with it literally says *my intention is to do X*. That is why it feels more settled than "I think I'll" and less fixed than a diary entry: the plan exists, it belongs to the speaker, and it could still change.

---

## ～つもり - plan to; intend to · Formation

`つもり` is a noun, so it needs `だ` or `です` after it, and things attach to it the way they attach to any noun.

---

## ～つもり - plan to; intend to · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + つもりだ | <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>うつもりだ |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + つもりだ | <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>わないつもりだ |
| Polite | つもりだ → つもりです | <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>うつもりです |
| Denying the intention | つもりはない | <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>うつもりはない |
| Intention that changed | つもりだった | <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>うつもりだった |
| Question | つもりですか | <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>うつもりですか |
| Noun | noun + の + つもり | <ruby>冗談<rt>じょうだん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>冗談<rt>じょうだん</rt></ruby>のつもり |
| Verb (た-form) | た-form + つもり = "under the impression" | <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>った → <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったつもり |

---

## ～つもり - plan to; intend to · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the verb before `つもり` stays **plain**. ❌<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますつもりです</code> is one of the most recognisable learner errors in the language.

---

## ～つもり - plan to; intend to · Examples

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>るつもりです**。<br>*I'm planning to go to bed early tonight.*

<ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby>、<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>へ**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くつもりです**。<br>*I intend to go to Japan next year.*

<ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>こそたばこを**やめるつもりだ**。<br>*This year I really am going to quit smoking.*

<ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>みはどう**するつもり**ですか。<br>*What are you thinking of doing over the summer?*

---

## ～つもり - plan to; intend to · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>甘<rt>あま</rt></ruby>いものは、しばらく**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べないつもりです**。<br>*I'm planning to stay off sweet things for a while.*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいパソコンを**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>うつもりだった**が、お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>が<ruby>足<rt>た</rt></ruby>りなかった。<br>*I'd meant to buy a new computer, but I didn't have enough money.*

<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たら<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>って、<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えるつもりです**。<br>*After I finish university I intend to go home and teach Japanese.*

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>にはもう**<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>うつもりはありません**。<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>もしないでください。<br>*I have no intention of seeing him again. Please don't contact me either.*

---

## ～つもり - plan to; intend to · Nuance & register

`つもり` is **volitional and personal**. It reports a decision the speaker has taken about their own behaviour, which puts two hard limits on it.

**No intending what you cannot control.** ❌<code><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るつもりです</code> is impossible - the rain has no plans. The same rule blocks `つもり` with verbs like `できる`, <code><ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かる</code> or `ある`. For predictions, `でしょう` or <code>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code> does the job.

**Careful with other people's intentions.** <code><ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るつもりです</code> claims direct access to Tanaka's mind. Add evidence: <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るつもりらしいです</code>, <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るつもりだと<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っていました</code>.

---

## ～つもり - plan to; intend to · Nuance & register (cont.)

**`どうするつもりですか` can be an accusation.** Asked flatly, "what do you intend to do?" carries the tone of a parent facing a broken window. Aimed at a superior it is worse - <code><ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>はいつ<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>るつもりですか</code> sounds like a challenge. Upward, ask about their <code>ご<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby></code> instead: <code><ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>のご<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>はいかがでしょうか</code>.

**`つもりはない` is a door slamming.** It denies that the intention exists at all, and in an argument it is very cold. `～ないつもりです` is the mild version.

**`つもりだった` almost always implies the plan failed or changed.** Nobody says <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くつもりだった</code> about a trip they took; it sets up a "but".

---

## ～つもり - plan to; intend to · Nuance & register (cont.)

**A second life as a plain noun.** <code><ruby>冗談<rt>じょうだん</rt></ruby>のつもりだった</code> ("I meant it as a joke"), <code><ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったつもりだったんだけど</code> ("I was sure I'd told you"). Here `つもり` is about what you *believed*, not what you planned, and the belief is usually wrong. Register-wise `つもり` itself is neutral; `つもりです` is fine in a job interview, and `つもり` bare is fine with friends.

---

## ～つもり - plan to; intend to · ～つもりです vs ～予定です

> **Rule of thumb** - `つもり` is what you have decided. <code><ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby></code> is what is on the calendar. Intention lives in your head; a schedule exists outside it.

<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ**<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>すつもりです**。<br>*I'm planning to move next month. (I've decided; nothing is booked yet)*

<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>す<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>です。<br>*I'm due to move next month. (it's arranged - date set, movers booked)*

Because <code><ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby></code> points at an arrangement rather than a state of mind, it is also the polite way to ask about someone senior, where `つもり` would be intrusive.

---

## ～つもり - plan to; intend to · ～つもりです vs ～(よ)うと思います

> **Rule of thumb** - `つもり` reports a decision already taken. <code>～(よ)うと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code> presents the thought as forming now, which makes it softer.

<ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>へ**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くつもりです**。<br>*I'm planning to go to the sea at the weekend.*

<ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こうと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。<br>*I think I'll head to the sea at the weekend.*

The second is the safer answer to a casual question, because it does not commit you. The first can sound slightly firm, even stubborn, if the listener was hoping to change your mind.

---

## ～ないつもりです vs ～つもりはありません

> **Rule of thumb** - `～ないつもり` = I plan not to. `～つもりはない` = I have no such plan, and I resent the suggestion.

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>はお<ruby>酒<rt>さけ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まないつもりです**。<br>*I'm planning not to drink today.*

お<ruby>酒<rt>さけ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>むつもりはありません**。<br>*I have no intention of drinking.*

The first describes a resolution you have made for yourself. The second rejects the whole idea, and depending on the tone it can be a refusal, a defence, or a rebuke. It is a useful sentence to understand and a risky one to use.

---

## ～つもり - plan to; intend to · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ます**つもりです。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>**く**つもりです。<br>*`つもり` follows a plain form. The politeness goes on `です` at the end of the sentence, once.*

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るつもりです。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るでしょう。<br>*Only a person with a choice can have an intention. Weather takes `でしょう`.*

- ❌ <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないつもりです。 → ✅ <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないつもりらしいです。<br>*Asserting someone else's intentions flatly claims to read their mind. Mark the evidence.*

- ❌ これは<ruby>冗談<rt>じょうだん</rt></ruby>つもりでした。 → ✅ これは<ruby>冗談<rt>じょうだん</rt></ruby>のつもりでした。<br>*`つもり` is a noun, so another noun joins it with `の`.*

---

## ～つもり - plan to; intend to · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>、いつ<ruby>海外<rt>かいがい</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くつもりですか。 → ✅ <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>海外<rt>かいがい</rt></ruby>へはいつ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かれるご<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>ですか。<br>*The grammar is sound; asking a superior about their `つもり` sounds like an interrogation.*

# だろう・でしょう - probably; … right?

**Meaning** · probably - or, said with a rising tone, "…don't you think?"

`だろう` and `でしょう` are the same word in two politeness levels, and that word does one job: it stops a sentence being a flat assertion. Whether it comes out as a guess or as an appeal for agreement is decided almost entirely by **intonation** - falling for "probably", rising for "right?". Learners are usually taught only the first half.

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Formation

`でしょう` attaches to **plain forms**, and the `だ` of nouns and な-adjectives disappears in front of it.

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + でしょう | <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るでしょう |
| Verb (past) | た-form + でしょう | <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>った → <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ったでしょう |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + でしょう | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないでしょう |
| い-adjective | plain form + でしょう | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いでしょう |
| な-adjective | drop だ + でしょう | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>でしょう |
| Noun | noun + でしょう | <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>でしょう |
| Plain / written style | でしょう → だろう | <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るだろう |
| Casual spoken | でしょう → でしょ／だろ | <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るでしょ |
| Common partners | たぶん, きっと, もしかしたら | たぶん<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るでしょう |

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - no `だ` and no `ます` in front. ❌<code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だでしょう</code>, ❌<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますでしょう</code>. The politeness of the sentence is already carried by `でしょう` itself.

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Examples

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>**でしょう**。<br>*It will probably rain tomorrow.*

たぶん<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないでしょう**。<br>*Tanaka probably won't come.*

この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>、**<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しいでしょう**？<br>*This question's hard, isn't it?*

<ruby>今夜<rt>こんや</rt></ruby>はかなり**<ruby>冷<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>えるだろう**。<br>*It'll get pretty cold tonight.*

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>空港<rt>くうこう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いているでしょう**。<br>*He's probably already arrived at the airport.*

ほら、<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>った**でしょう**？だから<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていけばよかったのに。<br>*See? I told you. You should have taken an umbrella.*

<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>から<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>くなるでしょう**。おでかけの<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>はご<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>ください。<br>*The wind will strengthen from the afternoon. Those going out should take care.*

その<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>、もう**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たでしょう**？どうだった？<br>*You've already seen that film, haven't you? What did you think?*

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Nuance & register

**The two senses are one form.** Falling intonation gives conjecture: the speaker is guessing and knows they are guessing. Rising intonation turns the sentence outward and asks the listener to agree. In writing there is no intonation, so a `？` marks the second sense - and a `でしょう` at the end of a written forecast is always the first.

**`だろう` is plain, and in speech it leans male.** In writing - essays, novels, news commentary - `だろう` is simply the plain-style equivalent of `でしょう` and carries no gender at all. In conversation, bare <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るだろう</code> and the clipped `だろ？` sound distinctly masculine and rather rough; women's casual speech prefers `でしょ？` or `～よね？`. `でしょ` is the everyday spoken shortening for everyone.

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Weather forecasts live on `でしょう`.** Japanese meteorologists do not assert; they say <code><ruby>晴<rt>は</rt></ruby>れるでしょう</code>, <code><ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>になるでしょう</code>. Hearing that register helps fix the falling, conjectural sense in your ear.

**The rising `でしょう？` has an edge.** It presses the listener toward your view. <code>だから<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったでしょう</code> is a genuine "I told you so", and used upward it is bad news: <code><ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、これでいいでしょう？</code> is telling your boss what to think. The upward version is a question - `これでよろしいでしょうか` - where `でしょう` has become deference rather than pressure.

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Do not guess about your own choices.** ❌<code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くでしょう</code> is strange for the same reason "I will probably go, I suppose" is strange about a decision that is yours to make. Use <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます</code> or <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くつもりです</code>. `でしょう` about yourself is only natural for things outside your control: <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>も<ruby>太<rt>ふと</rt></ruby>るでしょうね</code>.

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · だろう vs でしょう

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different level. `でしょう` is the ます／です-style form; `だろう` is the plain-style form and, in speech, noticeably blunt.

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>には<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる**でしょう**。<br>*It'll probably be finished by next week.*

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>には<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる**だろう**。<br>*It'll probably be finished by next week.*

The English is identical; the Japanese is not interchangeable. Use `でしょう` with anyone you would use `です` with. Use `だろう` in writing, in a diary, or with close friends - and be aware that a man and a woman saying `だろう` out loud will be heard differently, fairly or not.

---

## Falling でしょう vs rising でしょう

> **Rule of thumb** - falling = *I think so, but I'm not sure*. Rising = *you know this too, don't you?* One looks inward at the speaker's uncertainty; the other reaches out to the listener.

<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんはこのニュースを**<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないでしょう**。<br>*Yamada probably doesn't know about this news. (falling - a guess)*

<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんもこのニュース、**<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っているでしょう**？<br>*Yamada knows about this news too, right? (rising - checking with you)*

The rising version assumes the listener is in a position to confirm, so it is used about things they can be expected to know - shared experience, obvious facts, or something you told them earlier. Using it about something they could not possibly know sounds like an interrogation.

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · でしょう vs かもしれません

> **Rule of thumb** - a rough scale of confidence: `かもしれません` (maybe, 50/50) → `でしょう` (probably) → `はずです` (it should be, by reasoning) → <code>に<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>いありません</code> (it must be).

<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>から<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>になる**でしょう**。<br>*It'll probably turn to snow this afternoon.*

<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>から<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>になるかもしれません。<br>*It might turn to snow this afternoon.*

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · でしょう vs かもしれません (cont.)

<code>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code> sits alongside these but does something different: it labels the statement as the speaker's personal opinion rather than measuring likelihood. <code><ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないでしょう</code> is a forecast about the world; <code><ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code> is what *I* think - which is why the second is often the politer choice when you might be wrong.

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**だ**でしょう。 → ✅ <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>でしょう。<br>*Nouns and な-adjectives lose their `だ` before `でしょう`.*

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り**ます**でしょう。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るでしょう。<br>*`でしょう` follows plain forms. Doubling the politeness sounds like a bad announcement voice.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby><ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>るでしょう。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby><ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>るつもりです。<br>*You do not speculate about your own decisions.*

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>でいいでしょう？ → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>でよろしいでしょうか。<br>*Rising `でしょう？` pushes the listener to agree. Upward, ask.*

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**だろう**でしょう。 → ✅ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かでしょう。<br>*`だろう` and `でしょう` are the same word; they never stack.*

---

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby><ruby>予報<rt>よほう</rt></ruby>によると、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>____。

{reveal}

{reveal}

## だろう・でしょう - probably; … right? · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>ったんだから、この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>める（でしょう？・かもしれません）。

{reveal}

{reveal}

# んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation

**Meaning** · here's the background - or, as a question, so what's the story?

`んです` does not change what a sentence says. It changes what the sentence is *for*. Adding it tells the listener that this statement is tied to the situation you are both in: it explains something, or asks the other person to explain. `んです` is the spoken form, `のです` the written one, and the `ん` is simply `の` worn down by speech.

---

## んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Formation

`の` is a nominaliser, so everything attaches to it the way it attaches to a noun - which is why nouns and な-adjectives need `な` in between.

---

## んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + んです | <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れる → <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れるんです |
| Verb (past) | た-form + んです | <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れた → <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れたんです |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + んです | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないんです |
| い-adjective | plain form + んです | <ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>いんです |
| な-adjective | drop だ + **な** + んです | <ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>なんです |
| Noun | noun + **な** + んです | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>なんです |
| Plain style | んです → んだ | <ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>いんだ |
| Written / formal | んです → のです | <ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>いのです |
| Question | んですか | どうした → どうしたんですか |

---

## んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Preface, softener | んですが／んですけど | <ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きたいんですが |

> **Rule of thumb** - `な` after nouns and な-adjectives, nothing after verbs and い-adjectives. ❌<code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だんです</code> is the classic slip; <code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>なんです</code> is what you want.

---

## んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Examples

どうした**んです**か。<br>*What's the matter?*

<ruby>頭<rt>あたま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>い**んです**。<br>*I've got a headache. (that's why I'm asking to go home)*

すみません、<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からない**んですが**…<br>*Excuse me - I'm a bit lost…*

<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>はフランス<ruby>人<rt>じん</rt></ruby>**なんです**。だからワインにくわしいんですよ。<br>*She's French. That's why she knows so much about wine.*

## んです・のです - explaining, or asking for an explanation · Examples (cont.)

この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>、なんて<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む**んです**か。<br>*How do you read this kanji?*

「どうして<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れたんですか。」「<ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まった**んです**。」<br>*"Why were you late?" "The trains were stopped because of an accident."*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ませていただけませんか。<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>から<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る**んです**。<br>*Could I take tomorrow off? My mother is coming over from home.*

<ruby>実<rt>じつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>す**のです**。<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>い<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>お<ruby>世話<rt>せわ</rt></ruby>になりました。<br>*The fact is, I'm moving next month. Thank you for everything over the years.*

---

## Nuance & register

**What `んです` adds is *connection*.** <code><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っています</code> reports the weather. <code><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っているんです</code> offers it as the reason for something - why I'm wet, why the match is off, why I'd rather not go out. Nothing about the rain has changed; the sentence has been plugged into the conversation.

**That is also its danger.** A learner who ends every sentence with `んです` sounds as if they are permanently explaining themselves, or making excuses. In neutral self-introduction and plain description, use plain `です`／`ます`. <code><ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます</code> is a fact about you; <code><ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいるんです</code> answers a question that was asked, out loud or in the air.

---

## Nuance & register (cont.)

**`んですか` can accuse.** Because the question demands an explanation, tone decides everything. <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>をしているんですか</code> asked warmly is "oh, what are you up to?"; asked flatly it is "what do you think you're doing?" Similarly <code>どうして<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかったんですか</code> presses for a justification in a way that <code><ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>られませんでしたか</code> does not. When you only want information and not a defence, drop the `ん`.

**`～んですが` is one of the most useful shapes in the language.** It sets up a request by explaining the situation first and then trailing off, leaving the listener to offer: <code>ちょっとお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いがあるんですが…</code>, <code>すみません、この<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>、<ruby>空<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いてるんですけど…</code>. The unfinished ending is the politeness.

---

## Nuance & register (cont.)

**Register.** `んです` is spoken and polite; `のです` is written and formal, at home in essays, reports and public announcements, and it sounds stiff in conversation. Plain style gives `んだ`／`のだ` (<code><ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいんだ</code>) and, very commonly, bare `の` for questions and soft statements: `どうしたの？` is neutral for everyone, while `～のよ` and `～のね` are female-leaning and `～んだよ` male-leaning. The same `の` turns up as the casual reason connector `んで` (a worn-down `ので`): <code>ちょっと<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いでるんで</code>.

---

## んです vs です

> **Rule of thumb** - `です` states. `んです` explains. Ask yourself whether the sentence is answering a question, spoken or unspoken. If it is not, you probably do not need `ん`.

かぜを<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I've caught a cold. (a report - new information)*

かぜを<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>いた**んです**。<br>*I've caught a cold. (that's why I look terrible / why I'm not coming)*

The second sentence needs a context to lean on. Said out of nowhere with nothing to explain, it makes the listener hunt for the missing connection.

---

## ～んですか vs ～ですか

> **Rule of thumb** - `～ですか` asks for a fact. `～んですか` asks for the story behind something you have just noticed.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますか。<br>*Are you going to Osaka tomorrow? (simple question)*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**んです**か。<br>*Oh, you're going to Osaka tomorrow? (I've seen the ticket / heard something)*

The `ん` version presupposes evidence. That makes it warm and interested with a friend, and prickly with a stranger or a subordinate, because you are asking them to account for something. Learners over-produce `んですか` in exactly the situations where it stings.

---

## のです vs んです vs んだ

> **Rule of thumb** - one grammar point, three registers: `のです` on paper, `んです` in polite speech, `んだ`／`の` among friends.

この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>では<ruby>解決<rt>かいけつ</rt></ruby>できない**のです**。<br>*This problem cannot be solved alone. (written, formal)*

<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>じゃ<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>な**んだ**。<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ってくれない？<br>*I can't manage on my own. Could you give me a hand?*

Note that the plain form of the noun／な-adjective pattern is `なんだ`, not ❌`だんだ` - the `な` survives at every register.

---

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>**だんです**。 → ✅ <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>**なんです**。<br>*Nouns join `の` with `な`. The same goes for な-adjectives: ❌<code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだんです</code> → ✅<code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなんです</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ます**んです。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くんです。<br>*`んです` follows plain forms; the politeness is already in the `です` at the end.*

- ❌ はじめまして。<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>なんです。 → ✅ はじめまして。<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>です。<br>*An introduction explains nothing, so there is no `ん`. Using it here sounds like you are correcting a misunderstanding.*

---

## Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ (to a colleague, neutrally) どうして<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかったんですか。 → ✅ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>られませんでしたか。<br>*`んですか` demands an explanation. If you do not want to sound like you are taking someone to task, ask the plain question.*
