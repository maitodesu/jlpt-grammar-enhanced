# N5 - Amount and degree

- くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately
- だけ
- ～すぎる

---

# くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately

**Meaning** · about, roughly - stuck onto an amount to say you are not being exact

`くらい` takes a number and loosens it. <ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>ぐらい is "about half an hour", <ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>ぐらい is "a thousand yen or so", これくらい is "about this much". Whatever it attaches to has to be a **quantity or an extent** - a length of time, a price, a headcount, a size - never a point on the clock or the calendar.

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Formation

`くらい` and `ぐらい` are the same word, and nothing about what comes in front decides which one you say. It is a suffix: it goes straight onto the amount, with nothing in between.

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Number + counter | Amount + くらい／ぐらい | <ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>ぐらい |
| People | Number + <ruby>人<rt>にん</rt></ruby> + ぐらい | <ruby>十五人<rt>じゅうごにん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>十五人<rt>じゅうごにん</rt></ruby>ぐらい |
| Money | Price + ぐらい | <ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>ぐらい |
| Amount word | Noun + くらい | <ruby>半分<rt>はんぶん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>半分<rt>はんぶん</rt></ruby>くらい |
| Demonstrative | これ／それ／あれ + くらい | これ → これくらい |
| Modifying a noun | ～くらいの + Noun | これ → これぐらいの<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きさ |
| Asking an amount | どのくらい／どれくらい | どのくらいかかりますか |
| Followed by a particle | ～くらい + で／から／まで | <ruby>五分<rt>ごふん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>五分<rt>ごふん</rt></ruby>ぐらいで |

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `くらい` answers **how much? how long? how many?** If the phrase answers **when?**, you need `ごろ` instead: <code><ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby>ぐらい</code> ("about three hours") but <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>ごろ</code> ("around three o'clock").

> **Note** - a quantity with `くらい` on it usually needs no particle at all. You say <code>ビールを<ruby>三本<rt>さんぼん</rt></ruby>ぐらい<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みました</code>, not ❌<code>ビールを<ruby>三本<rt>さんぼん</rt></ruby>ぐらいを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みました</code> - the amount sits in front of the verb as an adverb, and `を` is already doing its work on ビール.

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Examples

コーヒーを<ruby>三杯<rt>さんばい</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みました。<br>*I drank about three cups of coffee.*

この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**です。<br>*This book is about a thousand yen.*

<ruby>教室<rt>きょうしつ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>十五人<rt>じゅうごにん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**います。<br>*There are about fifteen students in the classroom.*

<ruby>空港<rt>くうこう</rt></ruby>まで、どの**くらい**かかりますか。<br>*How long does it take to get to the airport?*

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Examples (cont.)

これ**ぐらい**の<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きさの<ruby>箱<rt>はこ</rt></ruby>はありますか。<br>*Do you have a box about this big?*

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>は<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>二時間<rt>にじかん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**ピアノを<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>します。<br>*My older sister practises the piano for about two hours a day.*

すみません、あと<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**で<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きますので、<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>しておいてください。<br>*Sorry - I'll be there in about ten minutes, so go ahead and order without me.*

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>のラーメンは、<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くと<ruby>二十分<rt>にじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>並<rt>なら</rt></ruby>ばなければなりません。<br>*If you go to this ramen place at lunchtime you have to queue for twenty minutes or so.*

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Nuance & register

`くらい` is completely register-neutral. It is the same in casual speech and in a business email, it is not marked as male or female, and adding it to a number never makes you sound less serious - if anything the opposite, because Japanese conversation prefers a hedged number to a bare one. Answering `どのくらいかかりますか` with a flat <code><ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>です</code> claims a precision you probably do not have; <code><ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>ぐらいです</code> is what a native speaker says.

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Nuance & register (cont.)

**`くらい` and `ぐらい` are the same word.** They mean the same thing, both are correct everywhere, and no listener will notice which one you chose. The variation is distributional rather than semantic: `ぐらい` is somewhat more common in speech and especially after the demonstratives - これぐらい, どれぐらい - while `くらい` turns up a little more often in careful writing. Those are tendencies, not rules. Pick whichever comes out of your mouth, and stay consistent inside a sentence.

In writing, and in announcements read aloud, you will also meet <code><ruby>約<rt>やく</rt></ruby></code>, which does the same job from the other side of the number. It is treated in the Contrast section below.

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Nuance & register (cont.)

One thing to expect later: the same word has a second job, measuring a **degree** rather than an amount - <code><ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>きたいくらいうれしい</code>, "so happy I could cry" - along with a dismissive use that belittles a small task. That sense is a separate grammar point at N3. If you meet a `くらい` that is not sitting on a number, it is that other job, not this one.

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · くらい／ぐらい vs ごろ

> **Rule of thumb** - `ごろ` blurs a **point in time**; `くらい` blurs an **amount**. One character in the counter decides it: <code><ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby></code> is a when, <code><ruby>六時間<rt>ろくじかん</rt></ruby></code> is a how-long.

<ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby><ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>**ごろ**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*I'll be home around six in the evening.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>六時間<rt>ろくじかん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*I slept about six hours last night.*

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · くらい／ぐらい vs ごろ (cont.)

Dates and days of the week are points in time too, so they take `ごろ`: <code><ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>ごろ</code>, <code><ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby>ごろ</code>. Anything that is counted rather than located - money, distance, weight, people, pages - can only take `くらい`: ❌<code><ruby>五人<rt>ごにん</rt></ruby>ごろ</code> is not Japanese.

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · くらい vs 約

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, opposite position, different register. <code><ruby>約<rt>やく</rt></ruby></code> is a prefix and belongs to **written** Japanese; `ぐらい` is a suffix and belongs to **speech**. Do not put both on one number.

<ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>まで**<ruby>約<rt>やく</rt></ruby>**<ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>かかります。<br>*It is approximately thirty minutes to the venue.*

<ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**かかります。<br>*It takes about half an hour to get to the venue.*

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · くらい vs 約 (cont.)

The first belongs on a printed leaflet or in a station announcement; the second is what you say to a friend on the phone. <code><ruby>約<rt>やく</rt></ruby></code> only ever attaches to a number, so there is no ❌<code><ruby>約<rt>やく</rt></ruby>これ</code> to match これぐらい, and stacking the two - ❌<code><ruby>約<rt>やく</rt></ruby><ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>ぐらい</code> - hedges the same number twice.

---

## Amount くらい vs degree くらい

> **Rule of thumb** - if `くらい` sits on a **number or an amount**, it means "about". If it sits on a **verb or an adjective**, it is measuring a degree, and "about" is the wrong translation.

<ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちました。<br>*I waited about thirty minutes.*

<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>てない**ぐらい**<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れました。<br>*I got so tired I couldn't stand up.*

---

## Amount くらい vs degree くらい (cont.)

The second sentence has no number in it at all: the clause <code><ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>てない</code> is being used as a yardstick for how tired the speaker was. That degree use, together with a dismissive "it's only ～" reading, is a separate grammar point at N3. Here you only need to recognise the shape and not translate it as "approximately".

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>二週間<rt>にしゅうかん</rt></ruby>**ごろ**<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みます。 → ✅ <ruby>二週間<rt>にしゅうかん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*<code><ruby>二週間<rt>にしゅうかん</rt></ruby></code> is a length of time, not a date. Only a point in time takes `ごろ`.*

- ❌ **ぐらい**<ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>かかります。 → ✅ <ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**かかります。<br>*English puts "about" in front of the number; Japanese puts `ぐらい` behind it. `くらい` is a suffix and can never lead.*

- ❌ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby>ぐらい**に**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しました。 → ✅ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*A duration goes in front of the verb with no particle. The `に` of <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>に</code> marks a point in time, and a span has no point to mark.*

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>約<rt>やく</rt></ruby><ruby>五千円<rt>ごせんえん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**でした。 → ✅ <ruby>約<rt>やく</rt></ruby><ruby>五千円<rt>ごせんえん</rt></ruby>でした。／<ruby>五千円<rt>ごせんえん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**でした。<br>*Both words already mean "about". Use one of them, and let the register decide which.*

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Practice 1

**1.** 「<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>から<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>まで、どの____かかりますか。」「<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いて<ruby>十五分<rt>じゅうごふん</rt></ruby>____です。」

{reveal}
「どの**くらい**かかりますか。」「<ruby>十五分<rt>じゅうごふん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**です。」

{reveal}
Both blanks are about a **length** of time, so both take `くらい`／`ぐらい`. Note how the hedged answer is the natural one: a bare <code><ruby>十五分<rt>じゅうごふん</rt></ruby>です</code> would sound as though you had timed it.

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>（ごろ・ぐらい）<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いて、<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>（ごろ・ぐらい）<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>をします。

{reveal}
<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>**ごろ**<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いて、<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>をします。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby></code> is a clock time - a point - so it takes `ごろ`. <code><ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby></code> is a span of time, so it takes `ぐらい`. The two counters differ by one character and choose different words.

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "About twenty people came to the party."

{reveal}
パーティーに<ruby>二十人<rt>にじゅうにん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。

{reveal}
The amount goes in front of the verb with `ぐらい` attached and no particle after it. <code><ruby>二十人<rt>にじゅうにん</rt></ruby>くらい</code> is equally correct - the two forms are the same word.

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>ぐらいに<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>をします。<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>ごろで<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わると<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>**ごろ**<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>をします。<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**で<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わると<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
The two words have swapped places. A day of the week is a point in time and needs `ごろ`; ten minutes is a span and needs `ぐらい`. Note that `に` also drops away once `ごろ` is there.

---

## くらい / ぐらい - about; approximately · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference: (a) この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます。 (b) この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>十年前<rt>じゅうねんまえ</rt></ruby>**ごろ**<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>してきました。

{reveal}
(a) measures **how long** the speaker has lived here: roughly ten years, and they are still here. <code><ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby></code> is a duration, so `ぐらい` is the only choice.

{reveal}
(b) locates **when** they arrived: at some point around ten years ago, as a single completed event. <code><ruby>十年前<rt>じゅうねんまえ</rt></ruby></code> is a moment on the calendar, which is `ごろ` territory. The same number, doing two different jobs.

# だけ

**Meaning** · only; just - this much and nothing more

`だけ` draws a line around something and says that everything outside the line is excluded. It is a neutral limiter: it reports the boundary without complaining about it and without apologising for it.

---

## だけ · Formation

`だけ` is a particle-like suffix. It goes **after** the word it limits, and the word keeps its own plain form.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + だけ | <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby> → <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>だけ |
| Number / counter | Counter + だけ | <ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>だけ |
| Verb | plain form + だけ | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>るだけ |
| い-adjective | plain form + だけ | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いだけ |
| な-adjective | stem + な + だけ | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなだけ |
| After a particle | に／で／と + だけ | <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちにだけ／<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちだけに |
| Sentence-final | だけ + です／だ | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>るだけです |

---

## だけ · Formation (cont.)

`が` and `を` are **dropped** after だけ, or moved behind it （<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>だけ<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む／<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>だけを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む）. `に`, `で`, `と`, `へ` are kept, on either side of だけ.

---

## だけ · Examples

コーヒーを<ruby>一杯<rt>いっぱい</rt></ruby>**だけ**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みました。<br>*I had just one cup of coffee.*

うちには<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一匹<rt>いっぴき</rt></ruby>**だけ**います。<br>*We have only one cat.*

<ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>**だけ**アルバイトをしています。<br>*I only work my part-time job on Saturdays.*

<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し**だけ**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*Please wait just a moment.*

---

## だけ · Examples (cont.)

これは<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る**だけ**で、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いません。<br>*I'm only looking - I'm not going to buy anything.*

<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>をここに<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>く**だけ**でいいですよ。<br>*All you have to do is write your name here.*

その<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い**だけ**で、あまりおいしくありませんでした。<br>*That place was just expensive - it wasn't even very good.*

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>っていたのは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**だけ**でしたから、<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>して<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*I was the only one left in the room, so I turned off the lights and went home.*

---

## だけ · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. `だけです` in polite speech, `だけだ` or a bare `だけ` in plain speech, and nothing about it is marked for age or gender.

The important feeling is that `だけ` is **factual, not emotional**. It states the limit and stops. If you want the limit to sound *insufficient* - "only three people came, unfortunately" - Japanese reaches for `しか～ない` instead, which is the same fact wrapped in disappointment.

Two very common frames are worth memorising whole:

---

## だけ · Nuance & register (cont.)

- `～だけで` - "just with / merely by". <ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>くだけでいいです<br>*means the writing is all that is required.*
- `これだけ`／`それだけ` - depending on the sentence these mean either<br>*"only this" or, with rising emphasis, "*this* much". これだけ<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>したのに is "after studying *this* much…". Context decides.*

In speech `だけ` often carries a small dismissive shrug when it follows an adjective: <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いだけ, <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きいだけ - "expensive and that's all it has going for it".

---

## だけ · だけ vs しか～ない

> **Rule of thumb** - same fact, opposite framing. `だけ` is neutral; `しか～ない` says the amount is *less than you'd want*, and forces the verb negative.

<ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>**だけ**<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*I have a thousand yen. (that's the amount, stated flatly)*

<ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていません。<br>*I've only got a thousand yen. (and it isn't going to be enough)*

Note the verb: `だけ` leaves it positive, `しか` requires the negative. Mixing them up - ❌<ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っています - is the single most common error with this pair.

---

## だけ · だけ vs ばかり

> **Rule of thumb** - `だけ` counts; `ばかり` complains. `ばかり` means "nothing but", and usually implies the speaker thinks that is too much of one thing.

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>はゲーム**だけ**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*Games are the one thing my little brother likes.*

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>はゲームばかりしています。<br>*My little brother does nothing but play games.*

`ばかり` (an N4 pattern) does not really restrict the set - other things may happen - it says one thing dominates, and it usually sounds critical.

---

## だけ · だけ vs のみ

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register. `のみ` is the written, formal twin of `だけ` and sounds stiff in conversation.

このドアは<ruby>関係者<rt>かんけいしゃ</rt></ruby>**だけ**<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>えます。<br>*Only staff can use this door.*

<ruby>関係者<rt>かんけいしゃ</rt></ruby>のみ<ruby>入場<rt>にゅうじょう</rt></ruby>できます。<br>*Staff only. (as printed on a sign)*

You will meet `のみ` on notices, in contracts and in announcements. Say `だけ` when you are speaking to a person.

---

## だけ · Common mistakes

- ❌ りんごを**だけ**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。 → ✅ りんご**だけ**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。／りんご**だけを**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*`を` cannot sit in front of `だけ`. Either drop it or move it behind. The same goes for `が`.*

- ❌ <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>**だけ**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みません。（meaning "I only drink water"）→ ✅ <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>**だけ**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。／<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みません。<br>*`だけ` plus a negative negates the limit itself: "it isn't only water that I drink." If you want the restriction to stand, keep the verb positive or switch to `しか`.*

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ**だけ**の<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**な**だけの<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby><br>*な-adjectives take `な` before `だけ`, exactly as they do before a noun.*

---

## だけ · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ テレビを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ます**だけ**です。 → ✅ テレビを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る**だけ**です。<br>*Politeness is carried by the final `です`. The verb in front of `だけ` stays in its plain dictionary form.*

---

## だけ · Practice 1

**1.** ケーキを<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つ____<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました。

{reveal}
ケーキを<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つ**だけ**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました。

{reveal}
`だけ` follows the counter directly, and the `を` on ケーキ is unaffected because it belongs to a different word. "I ate just one piece of cake."

---

## だけ · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>（だけ・しか）ありません。

{reveal}
<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**しか**ありません。

{reveal}
The verb is already negative (`ありません`), and only `しか` licenses that. With `だけ` you would have to write <ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>だけあります - same ten minutes, but reported as a plain fact rather than as "only ten minutes left".

## だけ · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I only studied for thirty minutes yesterday."

{reveal}

{reveal}
The limiter goes on the amount of time, not on the verb. If you want it to sound like an admission - *only* thirty minutes, which isn't enough - <ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しませんでした does that job.

{reveal}
`を` may not precede `だけ`. The natural fix is to delete it; <ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>だけを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます is also grammatical and puts a little extra weight on the restriction.

## だけ · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>だけ<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。 (b) <ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ませんでした。

# ～すぎる

**Meaning** · too much; excessively - past the point where it is still all right

`～すぎる` is **not** an intensifier. <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>すぎる does not mean "very expensive"; it means the price has crossed a line and become a problem. Whenever you attach it, you are passing judgement: this is more than it should be.

---

## ～すぎる · Formation

Cut the word back to its stem, then add `すぎる`. `すぎる` itself is a regular る-verb: すぎます, すぎない, すぎた, すぎて.

---

## ～すぎる · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + すぎる | <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み → <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みすぎる |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + すぎる | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べすぎる |
| Verb (する／<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | し／<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby> + すぎる | <ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>しすぎる |
| Verb (negative) | ない → なさ + すぎる | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べない → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べなさすぎる |
| い-adjective | drop い + すぎる | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>すぎる |
| な-adjective | stem + すぎる | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かすぎる |
| いい／よい | よ + すぎる（irregular） | いい → よすぎる |
| ない | な**さ** + すぎる | ない → なさすぎる |
| Noun form | すぎ | <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みすぎ, <ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>きすぎ |

---

## ～すぎる · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - whatever goes in front of `すぎる` is the same shape you would use in front of `ます` (for verbs) or in front of `～くない` (for adjectives, minus the く). If you can still see an い or a だ, you have cut too little.

---

## ～すぎる · Examples

このかばんは**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>すぎます**。<br>*This bag is too expensive.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>のパーティーで**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みすぎました**。<br>*I drank too much at the party yesterday.*

このスープは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>には**からすぎる**。<br>*This soup is too spicy for me.*

ごはんを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べすぎて**、おなかが<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*I ate too much and now my stomach hurts.*

---

## ～すぎる · Examples (cont.)

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かすぎて**、なんだかおちつきません。<br>*This room is too quiet - it makes me uneasy somehow.*

このシャツは<ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>には<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し**<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きすぎました**。<br>*This shirt turned out a bit too big for my son.*

テレビを**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>すぎる**と、<ruby>目<rt>め</rt></ruby>が<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>くなりますよ。<br>*If you watch too much television, your eyes will suffer.*

この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一年生<rt>いちねんせい</rt></ruby>には**<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しすぎる**ので、もっとやさしいものに<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>えましょう。<br>*This question is too hard for first-years, so let's swap it for an easier one.*

---

## ～すぎる · Nuance & register

Register-neutral in itself - <code><ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>すぎます</code> is as polite as the `ます` on the end makes it - but the *content* is always a criticism, so it can land harder than you intend. Softening it with `ちょっと` or <code><ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し</code> is extremely common: ちょっと<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>すぎますね sounds like a negotiation, while <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>すぎます sounds like a verdict.

The `すぎ` noun form appears constantly in everyday health talk: <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みすぎ, <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べすぎ, <ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>きすぎ, <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>すぎ - often on its own as a diagnosis. <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みすぎだ。

---

## ～すぎる · Nuance & register (cont.)

One modern exception to the "always negative" rule: in casual speech, especially among younger speakers, `すぎる` is used as pure hyperbole for praise - かわいすぎる, おいしすぎる, すごすぎる. This is slangy, it is written with a lot of exclamation, and it does not belong in a polite conversation or in writing. Learn to recognise it; do not build sentences on it yet.

Note also that `すぎる` is a real verb in its own right meaning "to pass by" （<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>過<rt>す</rt></ruby>ぎる, <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>り<ruby>過<rt>す</rt></ruby>ぎる）. Compounds are usually written in kana, the standalone verb in kanji.

---

## ～すぎる · ～すぎる vs とても／すごく

> **Rule of thumb** - `とても` says the quality is high. `すぎる` says the quality has gone wrong. Praise never uses `すぎる`.

このコーヒーはとても<ruby>熱<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*This coffee is very hot. (nice and hot)*

このコーヒーは**<ruby>熱<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>すぎます**。<br>*This coffee is too hot. (I can't drink it)*

---

## ～すぎる · ～すぎる vs とても／すごく (cont.)

So a compliment must not take `すぎる` in careful speech: if you tell a cook ❌ この<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>はおいしすぎます in a formal setting, the literal reading is that the dish is unreasonably rich. Say とてもおいしいです.

---

## ～すぎる · ～すぎる vs ～すぎ

> **Rule of thumb** - `すぎる` is a verb and conjugates; `すぎ` is a noun and takes `だ／です`.

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>きすぎました**。<br>*I worked too much yesterday.*

<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>きすぎですよ。<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んでください。<br>*You're overworking. Take a break.*

The noun form is what you use to label a situation rather than narrate an action, and it is the form that appears in set warnings like <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べすぎに<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby> - "don't overeat".

---

## ～すぎる · ～すぎる vs the standalone verb すぎる

> **Rule of thumb** - attached to a stem, it means "too much". Standing alone with its own particle, the same verb means "to go past".

<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>過<rt>す</rt></ruby>ぎました。<br>*It's gone three o'clock.*

おかしを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べすぎました**。<br>*I ate too many sweets.*

---

## ～すぎる · ～すぎる vs the standalone verb すぎる (cont.)

In the first sentence `を` marks the point being passed and the verb is the main verb of the sentence. In the second, `すぎる` has no object of its own - it is riding on <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ. If you can find a `を` in front of it, it is the "pass by" verb.

---

## ～すぎる · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べるすぎる → ✅ **<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べすぎる**<br>*Verbs attach at the ます-stem, never at the dictionary form. Ask yourself what goes before `ます`: <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ.*

- ❌ <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いすぎる → ✅ **<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>すぎる**<br>*い-adjectives drop the final い first. The same cut you make for <code><ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない</code> or <code><ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>かった</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなすぎる → ✅ **<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かすぎる**<br>*な-adjectives use the bare stem. No `な`, and no `だ`.*

- ❌ いいすぎる（meaning "too good"）→ ✅ **よすぎる**<br>*`いい` becomes `よ-` in every conjugation, and this one is no exception. Careful: <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いすぎる is a real and different word - "to say too much, to go too far".*

---

## ～すぎる · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べないすぎる → ✅ <ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べなさすぎる**<br>*Negatives insert `さ` before `すぎる`, because they behave like adjectives - the same `さ` you see in なさすぎる.*

---

## ～すぎる · Practice 1

**1.** この<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>____すぎます。

{reveal}
この<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>**さ**すぎます。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さい</code> is an い-adjective, so you remove only the final い and keep the さ: <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さい → <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さ → <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さすぎます. Learners often cut back to <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby> by mistake.

---

## ～すぎる · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>は（とてもおもしろかった・おもしろすぎた）です。 You loved it and want to recommend it.

{reveal}
<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>は**とてもおもしろかった**です。

{reveal}
`すぎる` is a complaint. おもしろすぎた would suggest the film was somehow excessive - overwhelming, more than you wanted. To recommend something, use `とても` or `すごく`.

---

## ～すぎる · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I got up too late this morning."

{reveal}
<ruby>今朝<rt>けさ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きるのが<ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>すぎました**。 （or simply: <ruby>今朝<rt>けさ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>すぎました**。）

{reveal}
Either the adjective takes `すぎる`（<ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>すぎる）or you put it on the verb that caused the problem. <code><ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>すぎる</code> - "to oversleep" - is what a Japanese speaker would actually say.

---

## ～すぎる · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: このお<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>はあついいすぎます。

{reveal}
✅ このお<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>は**あつすぎます**。

{reveal}
The writer has attached `すぎる` without cutting the adjective, and doubled the い along the way. あつい → あつ → あつすぎます. Only the final い goes.

---

## ～すぎる · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>はとてもしずかです。 (b) この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>はしずかすぎます。

{reveal}
(a) is a compliment, or at worst a neutral description. Quiet is being presented as a feature of the town.

{reveal}
(b) says the quiet has become a problem - nothing is open, nobody is about, the speaker is unsettled by it. The `すぎる` is what converts a pleasant quality into a complaint, and that conversion happens whatever the adjective is.

# ～と同じ

**Meaning** · the same as ～ - identical to something else, not merely similar

<code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ</code> is the word for sameness, and `と` is the particle that names what the sameness is measured against. English says "the same **as**"; Japanese says "**と** the same". Get that particle wrong and the sentence falls apart.

---

## ～と同じ · Formation

<code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ</code> is a strange word: it behaves like a な-adjective everywhere *except* directly in front of a noun, where the `な` disappears.

---

## ～と同じ · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (predicate) | Noun + と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ + です／だ | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じです |
| Noun (modifying) | Noun + と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ + Noun | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じクラス |
| Negative | と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ + じゃない／ではありません | <ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じじゃありません |
| Past | と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ + でした／だった | <ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じでした |
| Degree | Noun + と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じくらい + adjective | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じくらい<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い |
| Manner | Noun + と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じように + verb | <ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じように<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>る |
| Two things listed | AとBは<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ | このかばんとあのかばんは<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じです |

---

## ～と同じ · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - no `な` before a noun. ✅<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby> ／ ❌<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じな<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>. This one word is the exception that every learner trips over, because <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かな<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby> and <ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>な<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby> behave normally.

---

## ～と同じ · Examples

このかばんは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の**と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**です。<br>*This bag is the same as mine.*

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**<ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>です。<br>*Tanaka is the same age as me.*

この<ruby>色<rt>いろ</rt></ruby>はあの<ruby>色<rt>いろ</rt></ruby>**と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**じゃありません。<br>*This colour isn't the same as that one.*

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>**と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>通<rt>かよ</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*My younger brother goes to the same school as my older brother.*

---

## ～と同じ · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**くらい<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*My little sister is about as tall as I am.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>**と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**ホテルに<ruby>泊<rt>と</rt></ruby>まりました。<br>*We stayed at the same hotel as last year.*

<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>**と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>って<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*Every morning I take the same train as my father to the office.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby><ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>したこと**と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**ことを、もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いておきます。<br>*I'll write down the same thing I explained yesterday, one more time.*

---

## ～と同じ · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and just as common in speech as in writing.

The `と` may be dropped when the two items are listed together as a pair: このペンとそのペンは<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じです。 Here the `と` you can see is the "and" particle joining the two pens, and <code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ</code> stands alone as the predicate. Both patterns are correct - what is *not* correct is having neither.

Watch the `の` when you compare possessions. <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ</code> compares the other thing to **me**, the person. <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のと<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ</code> compares it to **mine**, the object. English hides this distinction; Japanese does not, and getting it wrong produces sentences like "this bag is the same as me."

Two fixed extensions are worth learning as units:

---

## ～と同じ · Nuance & register (cont.)

- <code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じくらい</code> - "about the same amount / to the same degree". This is<br>*how you say "as … as" for adjectives.*
- <code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じように</code> - "in the same way". It modifies a verb, not a noun.

In writing you may also meet <code><ruby>同様<rt>どうよう</rt></ruby>に</code> and <code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じく</code>, both stiffer equivalents. Neither belongs in conversation at this level.

---

## ～と同じ · 同じ + noun vs 同じ<b>な</b> + noun

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ</code> is the one な-adjective that drops its `な` in front of a noun.

<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんと**<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>です。<br>*I'm of the same opinion as Yamada.*

❌ <ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じな<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby> is simply wrong. But `な` **does** come back before `ので` and `のに`: <ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じなので, <ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じなのに. So the rule is narrower than "no `な` ever" - it is "no `な` before a noun".

---

## ～と同じ · ～と同じ vs ～に似ている

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ</code> means identical. <code><ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ている</code> means resembling. And note the particle flips: `と` for same, `に` for similar.

この<ruby>時計<rt>とけい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>の**と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**です。<br>*This watch is the same one my brother has.*

この<ruby>時計<rt>とけい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>のに<ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ています。<br>*This watch looks like my brother's.*

---

## ～と同じ · ～と同じ vs ～に似ている (cont.)

The first says you could swap them and nobody would notice. The second says they merely look alike. For faces, <code><ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ている</code> is the only option - <ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>に<ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ています, never <ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じです unless you mean something very odd.

---

## ～と同じ · ～と同じ vs ～と違う

> **Rule of thumb** - the same frame, negated. Both take `と`, and both put the comparison target in front of it.

この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>はあの<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>**と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>です。<br>*This kanji has the same reading as that one.*

この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>はあの<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>と<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>が<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>います。<br>*This kanji is read differently from that one.*

Note that <code><ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う</code> is a **verb**, so it takes ます／ました and never です: ❌<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>うです → ✅<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>います. <code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ</code>, being adjectival, does the opposite and needs です.

---

## ～と同じ · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じです。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じです。<br>*The comparison target is marked with `と`. `に` belongs to <code><ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ている</code>, not here.*

- ❌ <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんと<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**な**<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんと<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>です。<br>*No `な` in front of a noun. This is the single most frequent error with the word.*

- ❌ このかばんは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じです。（meaning "the same as mine"）→ ✅ このかばんは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**の**と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じです。<br>*Without `の` you are comparing a bag to a person. `の` turns <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby></code> into "mine".*

- ❌ <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**に**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りました。 → ✅ <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**ように**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*To modify a verb you need `ように`. Bare <code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じに</code> is not idiomatic here.*

---

## ～と同じ · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**くらいと**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです。 → ✅ <ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>は<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じくらい<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*<code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じくらい</code> sits directly in front of the adjective, with no particle between them.*

---

## ～と同じ · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>林<rt>はやし</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>____<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じアパートに<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます。

{reveal}
<ruby>林<rt>はやし</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じアパートに<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます。

{reveal}
The thing being matched always takes `と`. Note also that <code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ</code> attaches straight to アパート with no `な` in between.

## ～と同じ · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: この<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>（と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ・に<ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ている）です。 You mean the person resembles your mother.

{reveal}
この<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ています**。

{reveal}
Resemblance takes <code>に<ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ている</code>, with `に`. <ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じです would claim the two are the same person - or the same *thing*, which is stranger still. Note the answer ends in ています, not です, because <code><ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>る</code> is a verb.

## ～と同じ · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I ordered the same thing as you."

{reveal}
あなた**と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ**ものを<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>しました。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ</code> modifies もの directly - no `な`. In real conversation the `あなた` would usually be replaced by the person's name, or dropped entirely: <ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じものをください。

## ～と同じ · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: この<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>はあなたと<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じなです。

{reveal}
✅ この<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>はあなた**の**と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じです。

{reveal}
Two problems. `な` cannot stand before `です` - <code><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じです</code> is the predicate form. And the umbrella should be compared with *yours*, not with *you*, which is what the added `の` does.

## ～と同じ · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>と<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>です。 (b) <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ている<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>です。

{reveal}
(a) claims identity: it is literally that shop, the one you went to last year, still in the same place under the same name.

{reveal}
(b) claims resemblance only: a different shop that reminds you of the old one - similar food, similar atmosphere. Japanese keeps these two claims strictly apart, where English "like last year's place" can be read either way.
