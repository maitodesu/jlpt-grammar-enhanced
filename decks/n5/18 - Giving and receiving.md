# N5 - Giving and receiving

- あげる・くれる・もらう
- お・ご

---

# あげる・くれる・もらう

**Meaning** · give (away) / give (to me) / receive

English has one verb "give" and lets context sort out the direction. Japanese does not: it has **two** verbs for giving, and which one you use depends on whether the thing is moving toward you or away from you. Choosing wrongly is not a stylistic slip - it makes the sentence unintelligible.

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Formation

Each verb decides who the subject is and which way the object travels. All three are regular る-verbs (あげます, くれます - but note くださる → くださいます).

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| あげる（away from me） | Aが Bに Nを あげる | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>をあげる |
| くれる（toward me） | Aが（<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>に）Nを くれる | <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちが<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>をくれる |
| もらう（I receive） | Bが Aに／から Nを もらう | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちに<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>をもらう |
| Polite | あげます／くれます／もらいます | <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>をもらいました |
| I give upward（humble） | さしあげる | <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>にさしあげる |
| A superior gives to me（honorific） | くださる → くださいます | <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>がくださる |
| I receive from above（humble） | いただく | <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>にいただく |
| Giving down the scale | やる | <ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>に<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>をやる |

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - ask where the thing ends up. If it ends up with **me or my side**, the giving verb is `くれる`. If it ends up with **anyone else**, it is `あげる`. `もらう` describes the same event from the receiver's chair.

The particles are worth memorising as a set: the **giver** is `が` (or `は`) with あげる and くれる, and `に`／`から` with もらう. The **thing** is always `を`.

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Examples

<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>にプレゼントを**あげました**。<br>*I gave my little sister a present.*

<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちが<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>にチョコレートを**くれました**。<br>*A friend gave me some chocolate.*

<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>時計<rt>とけい</rt></ruby>を**もらいました**。<br>*I got a watch from my father.*

<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんに<ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>を**あげました**。<br>*Yamada gave Tanaka some flowers.*

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>にごはんを**やって**から<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*Every morning I feed the dog before leaving for work.*

となりの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもたちにおかしを**くれました**。<br>*The neighbour gave my children some sweets.*

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>を**くださいました**。<br>*The teacher kindly gave my younger brother a dictionary.*

<ruby>誕生日<rt>たんじょうび</rt></ruby>に<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>からきれいなカップを**もらいました**。<br>*I got a lovely cup from my older sister for my birthday.*

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>をやめるとき、<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>部<rt>ぶ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>たちが<ruby>花束<rt>はなたば</rt></ruby>を**くれました**。<br>*When I left the company, the people in my department gave me a bouquet.*

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Nuance & register

**The in-group is what really matters.** `くれる` is not only for gifts to *me*. It is for gifts to my **side** - what Japanese calls うち, as opposed to そと, everyone else. My family, and depending on the conversation my close friends, my colleagues, my company: if the thing lands with any of them, `くれる` is the verb.

That is why 「<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>をくださいました」 uses a giving-toward-me verb even though the book went to my brother. My brother is inside my circle, so the book came toward my side of the line. Say ❌<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>をあげました and you have pushed your own brother out of your family.

The line moves with the situation. Talking to a customer, everyone at my company is うち; talking to a colleague, only my own team might be.

**The politeness axis.** Each verb has a version for when a status gap is in play:

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Nuance & register (cont.)

- `さしあげる` - humble, for when *I* give to someone above me.
- `くださる` - honorific, for when someone above me gives to me or mine.<br>*The ます-form is irregular: **くださいます**, not ❌くださります. `ください`, the word you already know, is its imperative.*
- `いただく` - humble, for when *I* receive from someone above me.

Of these, `くださる` and `いただく` are safe and extremely common. `さしあげる` needs care: announcing that you have bestowed something on your superior can sound self-important, so people often avoid it and say <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>にお<ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>ししました or simply <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>しました instead.

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Nuance & register (cont.)

**やる****, and how rough it sounds.** `やる` is the downward version of `あげる`, used for plants, animals and - in older or rougher speech - for children and younger siblings. It is standard for <ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>に<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>をやる and <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>にえさをやる. Used of people it now sounds blunt and is male-marked; younger speakers say <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>にごはんをあげる without a second thought, and some older speakers find that overly precious. Either is acceptable; know that both exist.

**Two smaller points.** `もらう` takes `に` or `から` for the source. `から` is preferred when the giver is an organisation rather than a person: <ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>をもらいました.

The plain command `くれ`（<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>をくれ）is very rough - men's speech, brusque. Use ください.

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, these three verbs also work as helpers on the て-form - <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ってあげる, <ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてくれる, <ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ってもらう - where the same direction logic applies to *actions* rather than objects. That construction is taught at N4, and it is unusable until the direction rule on this page is solid.

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · あげる vs くれる

> **Rule of thumb** - both mean "give", and both take the giver as subject. `あげる` sends the thing away from the speaker; `くれる` brings it toward the speaker's side.

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんに<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を**あげました**。<br>*I gave Tanaka an umbrella.*

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を**くれました**。<br>*Tanaka gave me an umbrella.*

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · あげる vs くれる (cont.)

The two sentences describe opposite events, and the only substantive difference is the verb. Note that `くれる` may **never** have the speaker as giver: ❌<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんにくれました is impossible in any context.

In `くれる` sentences the <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>に is usually dropped, because the verb has already said it: <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>をくれました is the natural form.

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · くれる vs もらう

> **Rule of thumb** - one event, two camera angles. `くれる` puts the giver in the subject slot; `もらう` puts the receiver there.

<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>が<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>にセーターを**くれました**。<br>*My mother gave me a jumper.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>にセーターを**もらいました**。<br>*I got a jumper from my mother.*

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · くれる vs もらう (cont.)

Both are correct and both are common. `くれる` feels slightly warmer - it presents the other person as acting kindly toward you - while `もらう` is more neutral and focuses on the fact that you now have the thing. Note that the particle on <ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby> flips: `が` when she is the subject, `に` when she is the source.

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう vs さしあげる・くださる・いただく

> **Rule of thumb** - the plain three are for equals and for people below you. The polite three encode a status gap, and each one specifies which direction the gap runs in.

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**くださいました**。<br>*The section chief gave me the documents.*

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**いただきました**。<br>*I received the documents from the section chief.*

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう vs さしあげる・くださる・いただく (cont.)

Both are polite and both are safe. What you must not do is use the plain verbs of a superior's giving in a formal setting - <ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>をくれました is grammatical but sounds disrespectful in front of the section chief, though it is perfectly normal when grumbling to a friend.

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちが<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>にプレゼントを**あげました**。 → ✅ <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちが<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>にプレゼントを**くれました**。<br>*When the thing arrives at the speaker, the verb must be `くれる`. This is the single most frequent error learners make, because English uses "gave" for both directions.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんに<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を**くれました**。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんに<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を**あげました**。<br>*`くれる` can never have the speaker as the giver. Nothing can move toward you when you are the one letting go of it.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>**が**お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>をもらいました。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>**に**お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>をもらいました。<br>*With `もらう` the source takes `に` or `から`. `が` there would make your brother the one doing the receiving.*

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>を**くださります**。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>を**くださいます**。<br>*`くださる` is one of a small group of honorific verbs with an irregular ます-form - like おっしゃる → おっしゃいます.*

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>を**あげました**。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>を**さしあげました**。／<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>をお<ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>ししました。<br>*Using plain `あげる` about a gift to a teacher sounds as though you were doing them a favour from above. Even the humble form is often sidestepped in favour of a neutral verb.*

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>を____。

{reveal}
<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>を**くれました**。

{reveal}
The map ends up with the speaker, so the verb has to be `くれる`. In real speech the <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>に would usually be left out, since `くれる` already tells you where the map went.

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>にお<ruby>土産<rt>みやげ</rt></ruby>を（もらいました・いただきました）。 You are speaking at work, in front of colleagues.

{reveal}
<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>にお<ruby>土産<rt>みやげ</rt></ruby>を**いただきました**。

{reveal}
`いただく` is the humble verb for receiving from someone above you, and a department head qualifies. `もらいました` is not wrong grammatically, but in a workplace setting it sounds too flat about a superior's kindness.

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "My grandmother gave my son a book."

{reveal}
<ruby>祖母<rt>そぼ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を**くれました**。

{reveal}
Your son is inside your family, so the book has travelled toward your side and `くれる` is required - even though the speaker is not the recipient. Using あげました here would sound as though the boy were somebody else's child.

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちにおかしをくれました。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちにおかしを**あげました**。

{reveal}
The sweets are leaving the speaker, so the verb must be `あげる`. `くれる` with <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby> as the giver is not merely unnatural - it is impossible, because the verb's whole job is to mark movement toward the speaker.

---

## あげる・くれる・もらう · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>をくれました。 (b) <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>をもらいました。

{reveal}
Both report the same bicycle changing hands, and both are perfectly natural.

{reveal}
(a) makes the father the subject and presents the act as something he did for the speaker; there is a faint warmth of gratitude in it. (b) makes the speaker the subject and simply records the acquisition - closer to "I got a bike from my dad". The choice is about where you point the camera, not about the facts.

# お・ご

**Meaning** · the politeness prefix - attached to a noun to make it respectful, humble, or simply more refined

`お` and `ご` are the same prefix wearing two coats. Which one a word takes is decided by the word's **origin**, not by how polite you are feeling: native Japanese vocabulary takes `お`, vocabulary borrowed from Chinese takes `ご`. The choice is fixed per word and has to be learnt with the word.

---

## お・ご · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Native noun (kun reading) | **お** + noun | <ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby> → お<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby> |
| Native noun (kun reading) | **お** + noun | <ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby> → お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby> |
| Sino-Japanese noun (on reading) | **ご** + noun | <ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby> → ご<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby> |
| Sino-Japanese noun (on reading) | **ご** + noun | <ruby>住所<rt>じゅうしょ</rt></ruby> → ご<ruby>住所<rt>じゅうしょ</rt></ruby> |
| Sino-Japanese exceptions | **お** + noun | <ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby> → お<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby> |
| な-adjective | お／ご + adjective | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby> → お<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>／<ruby>親切<rt>しんせつ</rt></ruby> → ご<ruby>親切<rt>しんせつ</rt></ruby> |
| Verb, honorific | お + ます-stem + になる | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む → お<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みになる |
| Verb, humble | お／ご + stem + する | <ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>する → お<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>します |
| Fossilised | prefix is part of the word | ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>、お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>、おなか |

---

## お・ご · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Loanwords | normally nothing | コーヒー → ❌おコーヒー |

> **Rule of thumb** - read the word aloud. If it sounds like plain old Japanese （<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>, <ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>）, use `お`. If it is a compact two-character compound read with Chinese-derived sounds （<ruby>住所<rt>じゅうしょ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>, <ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>）, use `ご`. Then learn the handful of exceptions by heart.

---

## お・ご · Formation (cont.)

The common Sino-Japanese words that take `お` anyway are worth memorising as a list: お<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>, お<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>, お<ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby>, お<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>, お<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>, お<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>, お<ruby>礼<rt>れい</rt></ruby>, お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>, お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>さん. These have been in everyday Japanese so long that they stopped feeling foreign.

---

## お・ご · Examples

**お<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>**を<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてください。<br>*Could you tell me your name?*

**ご<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>**は<ruby>何人<rt>なんにん</rt></ruby>ですか。<br>*How many people are there in your family?*

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>、**お<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>**ですか。<br>*How are you, sensei?*

すみません、**お<ruby>手洗<rt>てあら</rt></ruby>い**はどこですか。<br>*Excuse me, where is the toilet?*

---

## お・ご · Examples (cont.)

**ご<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>**はお<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>まりですか。<br>*Are you ready to order?*

あとで<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>から**お<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>します**。<br>*I'll call you from the office later.*

**ご<ruby>住所<rt>じゅうしょ</rt></ruby>**と<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby><ruby>番号<rt>ばんごう</rt></ruby>をこちらに<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください。<br>*Please write your address and telephone number here.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は**ご<ruby>親切<rt>しんせつ</rt></ruby>**にありがとうございました。<br>*Thank you for your kindness yesterday.*

---

## お・ご · Nuance & register

The prefix is doing one of three quite different jobs, and telling them apart is the whole skill.

**Respect.** Attached to something belonging to the listener - お<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>, ご<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>, お<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby> - it raises *them*. This is the use that must never be turned on yourself: ❌<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のお<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>は… is the classic beginner's error, and it sounds like bowing to your own reflection.

**Humility.** In お + stem + する（お<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>します, お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちしています）the prefix belongs to *your* action, lowering you rather than raising anyone. The `お` is attached to a verb here, not a noun.

---

## お・ご · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Beautification** - <ruby>美化語<rt>びかご</rt></ruby>. お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>, ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>, お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>, お<ruby>菓子<rt>かし</rt></ruby>, お<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby> have nothing to do with respect at all. Everyone says them about everyone's tea and everyone's money, and dropping the prefix from some of these（<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>）sounds coarse. A few have fused completely: おなか and おかし are simply the words, and no one hears a prefix in them any more.

Register notes worth having:

---

## お・ご · Nuance & register (cont.)

- Heavy use of <ruby>美化語<rt>びかご</rt></ruby> is traditionally associated with women's speech and<br>*with a refined register. Layering it everywhere - おビール, おズボン - sounds affected, though shops and restaurants do exactly that.*
- Katakana loanwords normally take nothing. The one you will genuinely hear is<br>*おトイレ, which has become ordinary polite speech.*
- A small set of い-adjectives takes `お` in fixed courtesies:<br>*お<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいところすみません, お<ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>いですね.*
- Some words take either prefix - お<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby> and ご<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby> are both<br>*used, with お the more common today.*

---

## お・ご · Nuance & register (cont.)

You cannot attach the prefix wherever you like. There is no ❌お<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>, no ❌おテレビ, no ❌ご<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby><ruby>部屋<rt>べや</rt></ruby>. The set of words that take it is closed, and it is learnt word by word.

---

## お・ご · お vs ご

> **Rule of thumb** - `お` for <ruby>和語<rt>わご</rt></ruby> (native words, kun readings), `ご` for <ruby>漢語<rt>かんご</rt></ruby> (Chinese-derived words, on readings).

**お<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>**をありがとうございました。<br>*Thank you for your letter.*

**ご<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>**をお<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>かせください。<br>*Please let us hear your opinion.*

---

## お・ご · お vs ご (cont.)

<code><ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby></code> is read with native Japanese sounds, so `お`. <code><ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby></code> is a two-character on-reading compound, so `ご`. The test is not infallible - <ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby> is an on-reading compound and still prefers `お` - but it is right far more often than it is wrong.

---

## お・ご · Respectful お vs beautifying お

> **Rule of thumb** - if removing the prefix would be rude to a *person*, it is respect. If it would just sound rough, it is beautification.

**お<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>**は<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>ですか。<br>*What do you do for a living?*

**お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>**でも<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みませんか。<br>*Shall we have some tea?*

---

## お・ご · Respectful お vs beautifying お (cont.)

The first `お` honours the listener, and you would drop it when talking about your own job: <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>教師<rt>きょうし</rt></ruby>です. The second belongs to the tea and stays put whoever the tea is for - you say お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby> about your own cup without a second thought.

---

## お・ご · お + stem + になる vs お + stem + する

> **Rule of thumb** - `になる` raises the other person; `する` lowers you. Both are polite; they point in opposite directions.

<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>はもう**お<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りになりました**。<br>*The company president has already gone home.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、こちらから**お<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>けします**。<br>*We'll deliver it to you tomorrow.*

---

## お・ご · お + stem + になる vs お + stem + する (cont.)

Use the first about somebody else's action, the second about your own. Applying the honorific form to yourself - ❌<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>がお<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りになります - is a serious error, and the mirror-image mistake of using <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のお<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby></code>. Both are covered properly at N4; what matters now is knowing that the prefix alone does not make a phrase polite in the right direction.

---

## お・ご · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の**お**<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>です。<br>*The respectful prefix never goes on your own possessions. Ask the listener for お<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>, then answer with a bare <ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>.*

- ❌ **ご**<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>ありがとうございます。 → ✅ **お**<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>ありがとうございます。<br>*<code><ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby></code> looks like a `ご` word - two characters, on readings - but it is one of the fixed exceptions that take `お`.*

- ❌ **お**<ruby>住所<rt>じゅうしょ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください。 → ✅ **ご**<ruby>住所<rt>じゅうしょ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください。<br>*The mirror error. <code><ruby>住所<rt>じゅうしょ</rt></ruby></code> is straightforwardly <ruby>漢語<rt>かんご</rt></ruby> and takes `ご`, as do ご<ruby>職業<rt>しょくぎょう</rt></ruby> and ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby> on the same form.*

- ❌ **お**コーヒーをどうぞ。 → ✅ コーヒーをどうぞ。<br>*Loanwords do not take the prefix. Shop staff sometimes say it anyway; a learner saying it sounds like a mistake rather than like service language.*

---

## お・ご · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました。 → ✅ <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました。<br>*In ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby> the prefix has fused into the word. Removing it does not make the sentence casual - it makes it wrong.*

## お・ご · Practice 1

**1.** すみません、____<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>とおっしゃいますか。

{reveal}
すみません、**お**<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>とおっしゃいますか。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby></code> is a native Japanese word, so the prefix is `お`. It is required here because you are asking about the listener's name, and a bare <ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby> in a question to a stranger sounds abrupt.

## お・ご · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: （お・ご）<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>によろしくお<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えください。

{reveal}
**ご**<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>によろしくお<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えください。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby></code> is a two-character on-reading compound - <ruby>漢語<rt>かんご</rt></ruby> - so it takes `ご`. Note that お<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えください in the same sentence takes `お`, because <code><ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>える</code> is a native verb.

---

## お・ご · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Thank you for your reply."

{reveal}
**お<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>**ありがとうございます。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby></code> is one of the on-reading words that nevertheless prefers `お`. You will also see ご<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>, especially in older writing, but お<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby> is the safer choice today.

## お・ご · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のお<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>です。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>教師<rt>きょうし</rt></ruby>です。

{reveal}
Two things. The honorific `お` cannot describe your own job, so it must go. And <code><ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby></code> is itself a respectful word - use <code><ruby>教師<rt>きょうし</rt></ruby></code> about yourself.

## お・ご · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がありますか。 (b) お<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>、よろしいですか。

{reveal}
(a) is a plain question about availability. Between friends it is perfectly normal; addressed to a customer or a superior it sounds blunt.

{reveal}
(b) treats the listener's time as something belonging to them and worth honouring, which is why the `お` appears. Combined with よろしいですか it becomes the standard way to ask a busy person for a moment - the prefix is carrying the politeness that the grammar alone does not.
