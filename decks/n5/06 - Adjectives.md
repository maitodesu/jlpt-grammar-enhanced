# い-adjective conjugation

**Meaning** · how い-adjectives change for negative, past, and linking - くない, かった, くなかった, くて

An い-adjective is a word like <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い, <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい or <ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しい: it ends in い in its dictionary form, and - this is the point - it **conjugates by itself**. It does not lean on the copula the way a noun does. <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い carries its own past tense, <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>かった, with no `でした` anywhere in sight. Learners who treat an い-adjective like a noun produce ❌<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いでした, and it is the single most recognisable beginner error in the language.

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Formation

Chop the final い off and replace it. Everything below is one operation on that stem.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective (plain non-past) | ～い | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い |
| い-adjective (polite non-past) | ～い + です | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いです |
| い-adjective (negative) | い → くない | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くない |
| い-adjective (past) | い → かった | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>かった |
| い-adjective (past negative) | い → くなかった | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くなかった |
| い-adjective (て-form) | い → くて | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くて |
| い-adjective (adverb) | い → く | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>く<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>う |
| い-adjective (before a noun) | ～い + Noun | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby> |

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Formation (cont.)

Politeness is added afterwards, by putting `です` on the finished form - it never changes the form itself:

| Plain | Polite (everyday) | Polite (formal) |
|---|---|---|
| <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いです | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いです |
| <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くない | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くないです | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くありません |
| <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>かった | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>かったです | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>かったです |
| <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなかった | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなかったです | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くありませんでした |

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the past tense lives **inside the adjective**, never on `です`. <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>かったです is correct; ❌<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いでした is not. `です` here is a politeness marker only, and it does not inflect.

**いい is irregular.** It is the one い-adjective that does not conjugate on its own stem: every form except the dictionary form is built on its older shape **よい**.

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Formation (cont.)

| Form | いい / よい |
|---|---|
| Plain non-past | いい（よい） |
| Negative | よくない |
| Past | よかった |
| Past negative | よくなかった |
| て-form | よくて |
| Adverb | よく |
| Polite past | よかったです |

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Formation (cont.)

There is no ❌いくない and no ❌いかった. The irregularity spreads to every compound built on いい: かっこいい → かっこよかった, ちょうどいい → ちょうどよくない, <ruby>都合<rt>つごう</rt></ruby>がいい → <ruby>都合<rt>つごう</rt></ruby>がよかった.

> **Note** - a handful of words end in い but are **not** い-adjectives. きれい, <ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>, <ruby>嫌<rt>きら</rt></ruby>い are な-adjectives; the negative of きれい is きれいじゃない, not ❌きれくない. One partial test: a genuine い-adjective never ends in **えい**, so きれい and <ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby> are ruled out on sight. <ruby>嫌<rt>きら</rt></ruby>い ends in あい and passes the test, so that one simply has to be memorised. Going the other way, <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい and <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さい really are い-adjectives, though they also have the special prenominal forms <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きな and <ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな.

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Examples

このスープは**<ruby>熱<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>くない**。<br>*This soup isn't hot.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>面白<rt>おもしろ</rt></ruby>かった**。<br>*Yesterday's film was good.*

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>のパンは**<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くて**おいしいです。<br>*The bread at this shop is cheap and tasty.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>より<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>く**なりました。<br>*Vegetables have got more expensive than last year.*

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>が**よくなかった**ので、<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>りませんでした。<br>*The weather wasn't good, so we didn't climb the mountain.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>から**<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しくて**、<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>もなかった。<br>*I've been busy since the morning and didn't even have time for lunch.*

<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っていたほど**<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しくなかった**です。<br>*The exam wasn't as hard as I'd been expecting.*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どものころは<ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>みが**<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>かった**のに、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>は1<ruby>週間<rt>しゅうかん</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>めない。<br>*The summer holidays were so long when I was a child, and now I only get a week off.*

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Nuance & register

い-adjectives are register-neutral in themselves; the register is set by what you put after them. Plain <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い to a friend, <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いです to anyone else.

The negative has a genuine register split that learners are rarely told about. `～くないです` and `～くありません` mean the same thing, but `～くありません` sounds noticeably more formal and a little stiff - it belongs to announcements, written notices, and careful speech. In ordinary conversation <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くないです is what people actually say. The past is more lopsided still: <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くなかったです is normal speech, while <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くありませんでした sounds like a customer-service script.

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Nuance & register (cont.)

A bare い-adjective is a complete sentence, and Japanese uses them that way constantly: <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い! <ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>い! おいしい! Adding `です` to these exclamations makes them oddly formal - nobody yelps ❌<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>いです when they stub a toe.

Two things to be careful of:

- `です` after an い-adjective is **not** the copula doing its usual work; it is<br>*purely a politeness ending. That is why it cannot inflect for tense, and why ❌<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いでした fails.*
- Adjectives describing another person's inner state - <ruby>寂<rt>さび</rt></ruby>しい,<br>*<ruby>欲<rt>ほ</rt></ruby>しい, <ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>い - cannot be asserted flatly about a third person. You need そう, ようだ, or `～がる`: <ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>寂<rt>さび</rt></ruby>しがっている.*

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, `～く` is the adverb, and it does far more work than English learners expect: <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きる "get up early", <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きく<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>く "write it big", よく<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>た "slept well".

---

## い-adjective conjugation · い-adjective vs な-adjective

> **Rule of thumb** - an い-adjective inflects **itself**; a な-adjective leaves the work to the copula. So the tense marker moves: <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>かった against <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだった.

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>のアパートは**<ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>かった**。<br>*Last year's flat was cramped.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>のアパートは**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだった**。<br>*Last year's flat was quiet.*

---

## い-adjective conjugation · い-adjective vs な-adjective (cont.)

Both sentences are past tense; the machinery differs completely. In the first, い became かった inside the word. In the second, the adjective did not change at all and `だ` became `だった`. This is why the negatives are also <ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>くない against <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かじゃない - and why mixing them up gives ❌<ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>いじゃない or ❌<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かくない.

---

## い-adjective conjugation · ～く vs ～に

> **Rule of thumb** - both make an adverb, but the ending follows the adjective's class: い-adjectives take **く**, な-adjectives take **に**.

<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>をもっと**<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きく**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください。<br>*Please write the characters bigger.*

もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かに**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Please speak a bit more quietly.*

---

## い-adjective conjugation · ～く vs ～に (cont.)

The same split covers "becoming": <ruby>暖<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かくなる against <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>になる, and "making": <ruby>短<rt>みじか</rt></ruby>くする against きれいにする. Get into the habit of storing every new adjective together with its adverb form - it removes the guesswork permanently.

---

## い-adjective conjugation · ～くないです vs ～くありません

> **Rule of thumb** - identical meaning. `くないです` is conversational, `くありません` is formal and a bit starchy. Neither is wrong; the mismatch is with the situation, not the grammar.

この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>はそんなに**<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しくないです**。<br>*This question isn't all that hard. (talking to a classmate)*

<ruby>当店<rt>とうてん</rt></ruby>のサービスは<ruby>決<rt>けっ</rt></ruby>して**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くありません**。<br>*Our service is by no means expensive. (a shop's notice)*

The rule extends to the past: `くなかったです` in speech, `くありませんでした` in formal contexts. If you only want to remember one column, remember the `です` one - it is never wrong, only occasionally casual.

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いでした → ✅ **<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>かったです**<br>*The past tense goes inside the adjective. `です` never inflects after an い-adjective.*

- ❌ <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いじゃない → ✅ **<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くない**<br>*`じゃない` is the negative for nouns and な-adjectives. い-adjectives swap い for くない.*

- ❌ いくない／いかった → ✅ **よくない**／**よかった**<br>*いい is irregular: everything except the dictionary form is built on よ. The same applies to かっこいい → かっこよかった.*

- ❌ <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいの<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby> → ✅ **<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>**<br>*An い-adjective attaches straight to the noun. No `の`, and no `な` either.*

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いくない → ✅ **<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない**<br>*Remove the い before adding くない. Keeping both is the same slip as ❌<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べるない with verbs.*

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>のパーティーはとても<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>し____です。

{reveal}
<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>**しかった**です

{reveal}
<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby> puts the sentence in the past, and an い-adjective carries its own past tense: い → かった. `です` is then added unchanged, purely for politeness.

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Practice 2

**2.** この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は（きれくない・きれいじゃない）。

{reveal}
この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は**きれいじゃない**。

{reveal}
きれい ends in い but is a な-adjective, so it negates with `じゃない`. The giveaway is that it ends in **えい**, a shape no genuine い-adjective has.

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The film wasn't interesting, but the music was good."

{reveal}
<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>面白<rt>おもしろ</rt></ruby>くなかった**けど、<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>は**よかった**です。

{reveal}
Two い-adjectives, both carrying their own tense. Note that いい becomes よかった - never ❌いかった - and that only the last clause takes `です`.

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>、あまり<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きいじゃなかったです。

{reveal}
✅ この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>、あまり**<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなかった**です。

{reveal}
<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい is an い-adjective despite looking like it might be a な-adjective, so the past negative is built by stacking い-adjective endings: い → くない → くなかった.

---

## い-adjective conjugation · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>、あまり<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るくないです。 (b) この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>、あまり<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るくありません。

{reveal}
The meaning is the same: neither area is well lit. What differs is how formal the speaker is being. (a) is the everyday polite negative - what you would say to a neighbour or a colleague.

{reveal}
(b) is the older, more formal construction. It sounds appropriate in a written notice, a news report, or an official explanation, and slightly starchy in casual conversation.

# な-adjective conjugation

**Meaning** · how な-adjectives work - the な before a noun, and じゃない, だった, で

A な-adjective is a word like <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か, <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>, <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き or きれい. The name comes from the one place it differs visibly from a noun: put it in front of a noun and it needs a な: **<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かな<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>** "a quiet town".

The important structural fact is what な-adjectives *don't* do: they do not conjugate. The word itself never changes. All the tense and negation happens on the copula behind it - <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだった → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かじゃない. If you can handle <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だった, you can already handle <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだった, because they are the same machinery.

---

## な-adjective conjugation · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| な-adjective (before a noun) | ～な + Noun | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>な<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby> |
| な-adjective (plain non-past) | ～だ | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>だ |
| な-adjective (polite non-past) | ～です | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>です |
| な-adjective (negative) | ～じゃない／ではない | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>じゃない |
| な-adjective (past) | ～だった | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>だった |
| な-adjective (past negative) | ～じゃなかった | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>じゃなかった |
| な-adjective (て-form) | ～で | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>で |
| な-adjective (adverb) | ～に | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>になる |

## な-adjective conjugation · Formation (cont.)

| Plain | Polite (everyday) | Polite (formal) |
|---|---|---|
| <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だ | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>です | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>です |
| <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>じゃない | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>じゃないです | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>ではありません |
| <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だった | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>でした | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>でした |
| <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>じゃなかった | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>じゃなかったです | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>ではありませんでした |

> **Rule of thumb** - `な` appears **only** directly before a noun. Everywhere else it is not there. So <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かな<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby> but <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かです - never ❌<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなです.

---

## な-adjective conjugation · Formation (cont.)

Set the two adjective classes side by side and the whole system falls out of one question: does the word inflect, or does the copula do it for you?

| | い-adjective (<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い) | な-adjective (<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か) |
|---|---|---|
| Before a noun | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby> | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かな<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby> |
| Plain non-past | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ |
| Negative | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かじゃない |
| Past | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>かった | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだった |
| Past negative | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くなかった | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かじゃなかった |
| て-form | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くて | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かで |
| Adverb | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かに |

---

## な-adjective conjugation · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - な-adjectives behave almost exactly like nouns, and historically that is what many of them are. <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>, <ruby>親切<rt>しんせつ</rt></ruby> and <ruby>健康<rt>けんこう</rt></ruby> can all appear as nouns in their own right. The **only** systematic difference is what goes in front of a noun: a な-adjective takes `な`, a noun takes `の`. <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> "a sick person" (noun), <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>な<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> "a lively person" (な-adjective). Everything else - だ, だった, じゃない, で, でした - is shared.

## な-adjective conjugation · Formation (cont.)

- Ending in い but な-adjectives: きれい, <ruby>嫌<rt>きら</rt></ruby>い, <ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>,<br>*<ruby>嫌<rt>いや</rt></ruby>, <ruby>得意<rt>とくい</rt></ruby>. きれいじゃない, not ❌きれくない.*
- <ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ is a な-adjective that drops the な before a noun:<br>*<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>, never ❌<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じな<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>. But the な comes back before ので and のに: <ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じなので.*
- <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き, <ruby>嫌<rt>きら</rt></ruby>い, <ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>, <ruby>下手<rt>へた</rt></ruby> are adjectives in<br>*Japanese although they translate as English verbs, and their object takes `が`: <ruby>魚<rt>さかな</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです.*

## な-adjective conjugation · Examples

この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>はとても**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かです**。<br>*This town is very quiet.*

わたしは<ruby>魚<rt>さかな</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きじゃない**。<br>*I don't like fish.*

ここは<ruby>交通<rt>こうつう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>な**<ruby>場所<rt>ばしょ</rt></ruby>です。<br>*This is a spot with good transport links.*

<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>は**にぎやかだった**。<br>*This area used to be lively.*

---

## な-adjective conjugation · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>全然<rt>ぜんぜん</rt></ruby>**<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>じゃなかった**。<br>*The day before the exam I wasn't well at all.*

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>は<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>で**、いつもおいしいケーキを<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ってくれる。<br>*My sister is a good cook and always makes me lovely cakes.*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>事務所<rt>じむしょ</rt></ruby>は**きれいで**<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るいですが、<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>から<ruby>遠<rt>とお</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*The new office is bright and clean, but it's a long way from the station.*

<ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さいころ<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>嫌<rt>きら</rt></ruby>いでした**が、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます。<br>*My son hated vegetables when he was small, but now he'll eat anything.*

---

## な-adjective conjugation · Nuance & register

The word itself is register-neutral; the copula after it is where register lives. `だ` is plain and slightly assertive, `です` is polite, and in casual speech `だ` is very often dropped altogether: この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か - a complete, natural sentence. Women's casual speech in particular tends to omit `だ`; keeping it can sound blunt or declamatory.

The negative comes in three shapes, and they form a clear ladder:

- **じゃない** - casual, spoken. A contraction of ではない.
- **じゃないです／じゃありません** - polite, everyday.
- **ではありません／ではございません** - formal, written, and service Japanese.

---

## な-adjective conjugation · Nuance & register (cont.)

`ではない` in speech sounds like a lecture or a debate; `じゃない` in a written report looks careless. That mismatch, rather than any grammatical error, is what makes learners' Japanese sound off.

One more thing worth knowing early: `じゃない` with rising intonation is a tag question, not a negative - きれいじゃない？ means "isn't it pretty?", the opposite of what the words look like. Context and intonation carry it.

Some な-adjectives are almost never used about oneself. Saying わたしは<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>です about your own Japanese is boastful; the modest answer is まだまだです. And <ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby> has drifted in modern speech into a soft refusal - offered a plastic bag, <ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>です means "no thanks", not "that's fine".

---

## な-adjective vs い-adjective - telling them apart

> **Rule of thumb** - ignore the last letter and look at the **negative**. If it takes じゃない, it is a な-adjective. If it takes くない, it is an い-adjective. The dictionary form lies; the negative never does.

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は**きれいじゃない**。<br>*This room isn't clean.*

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>汚<rt>きたな</rt></ruby>くない**。<br>*This room isn't dirty.*

---

## な-adjective vs い-adjective - telling them apart (cont.)

きれい and <ruby>汚<rt>きたな</rt></ruby>い both end in い, and they belong to different classes. The reliable signal is the syllable before the final い: an い-adjective never ends in **えい**, so きれい (kirei) and <ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby> (yuumei) give themselves away. <ruby>嫌<rt>きら</rt></ruby>い does not, and just has to be learned. When you meet a new adjective, learn it with its な attached - きれいな, <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな - and the problem disappears.

---

## な-adjective conjugation · な vs の before a noun

> **Rule of thumb** - な-adjectives take `な`; nouns take `の`. Everything after the noun is identical, so this one particle is the whole visible difference between the two word classes.

<ruby>親切<rt>しんせつ</rt></ruby>な**<ruby>店員<rt>てんいん</rt></ruby>**さんに<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてもらった。<br>*A kind shop assistant gave me directions.*

<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I wrote a letter to my Japanese teacher.*

---

## な-adjective conjugation · な vs の before a noun (cont.)

A few words sit in both camps and change meaning accordingly. <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> is "a person with an illness" (noun +の); <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>な<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> is "a lively person" (な-adjective). If you are unsure which class a word is in, `の` is the safer guess for anything that names a thing, and `な` for anything that describes a quality.

---

## な-adjective conjugation · ～な vs ～に

> **Rule of thumb** - `な` modifies a **noun**; `に` modifies a **verb**. Same adjective, two different jobs.

**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かな**<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*I like quiet people.*

ドアを**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かに**<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めてください。<br>*Please close the door quietly.*

---

## な-adjective conjugation · ～な vs ～に (cont.)

`に` is also the form used with なる and する: <ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>になる "get good at something", きれいにする "make something clean". This `に` is the な-adjective's equivalent of the い-adjective's `く`, and the two never cross over: ❌<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かくする, ❌<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>にする.

---

## な-adjective conjugation · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かの<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby> → ✅ **<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かな<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>**<br>*<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か is a な-adjective, so it takes `な` before a noun. `の` is for nouns modifying nouns.*

- ❌ きれいくない → ✅ **きれいじゃない**<br>*きれい ends in い but is a な-adjective. It negates with `じゃない`, like a noun.*

- ❌ <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>なです → ✅ **<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>です**<br>*The `な` only appears immediately before a noun. Before `です` there is nothing.*

- ❌ <ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>かった → ✅ **<ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>でした**／**<ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>だった**<br>*な-adjectives have no past tense of their own; the copula supplies it. `かった` belongs to い-adjectives.*

---

## な-adjective conjugation · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>に<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> → ✅ **<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>な<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>**<br>*`に` goes with verbs, `な` with nouns. <ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>に<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す is fine; <ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>な<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> is the noun version.*

---

## な-adjective conjugation · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは____<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>です。（しんせつ）

{reveal}

{reveal}
<ruby>親切<rt>しんせつ</rt></ruby> is a な-adjective, and it is standing directly in front of the noun <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>, which is exactly the environment that requires `な`.

## な-adjective conjugation · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The hotel was clean and quiet, but it wasn't cheap."

{reveal}

Two な-adjectives linked by the て-form, which for a な-adjective is simply `で`. Only the last one carries tense. The final adjective <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い is an い-adjective, so it makes its own past negative.

## な-adjective conjugation · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>のコンサートはとてもすてきかったです。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>のコンサートはとても**すてきでした**。

{reveal}
すてき is a な-adjective, and な-adjectives never take `かった`. The past is carried by the copula: だった in plain style, でした in polite style.

## な-adjective conjugation · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はギターが<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>です。 (b) <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はギターを<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>に<ruby>弾<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>きます。

{reveal}
(a) uses <ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby> as an adjective describing him, so the guitar is marked with `が` - it is a statement about what kind of player he is.

{reveal}
(b) uses the adverbial `に` form to describe how he performs the action of playing, so the guitar returns to being an ordinary object with `を`. (a) is a judgement about ability; (b) is a description of a performance.
