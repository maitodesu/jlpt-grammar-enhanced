# N5 - Permission and prohibition

- ～てもいい - it's okay to
- ～てはいけない - must not
- な - don't (blunt prohibitive)
- ～なくてはいけない・～なくてはならない - must; have to

---

# ～てもいい - it's okay to

**Meaning** · it's all right to do it; you may do it

`～てもいい` grants permission, or asks for it. Literally it says "even if you do it, it's fine" - `も` is the "even" of concession and `いい` is the verdict. That shape is worth holding on to: the pattern never pushes anyone to act. It only opens a door and leaves it open.

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Formation

`いい` is an い-adjective, so everything you can do to `いい` you can do here: `いいです`, `よかった`, `よろしいでしょうか`.

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + もいい | <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>ってもいい |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form → なくて + もいい | <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>わなくてもいい |
| い-adjective | drop い + くて + もいい | <ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>くてもいい |
| な-adjective | drop だ + でもいい | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かでもいい |
| Noun | noun + でもいい | お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby> → お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>でもいい |
| Polite | いい → いいです | <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>ってもいいです |
| Asking permission | ～てもいいですか | <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>ってもいいですか |
| Softer / politer | いい → かまいません, よろしいでしょうか | <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>ってもよろしいでしょうか |

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - verbs join with `て`, everything else joins with `で`. The `で` in <code>お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>でもいい</code> is the て-form of `です`, not the particle `で`.

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Examples

ここに**<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>ってもいい**ですか。<br>*May I sit here?*

その<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>、**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでもいい**よ。<br>*Go ahead and read that book.*

<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いので、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けてもいい**ですか。<br>*It's hot, so is it all right if I open the window?*

ペンを<ruby>一本<rt>いっぽん</rt></ruby>**<ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>りてもいい**？<br>*Can I borrow a pen?*

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>支払<rt>しはら</rt></ruby>いはカード**でもいい**ですか。<br>*Is it okay to pay by card?*

<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったら、<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってもいい**ですよ。<br>*Once you've finished, you're welcome to head home ahead of me.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>には**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいい**ですよ。<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>だけ<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*You don't have to come to tomorrow's meeting - just send me the documents.*

この<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>、ブログに**<ruby>載<rt>の</rt></ruby>せてもいい**でしょうか。<br>*Would it be all right if I put this photo up on my blog?*

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Nuance & register

Permission has a direction, and `～てもいい` is sensitive to it. **Asking** goes in any direction; **granting** normally goes downward, from the person with authority to the person without it. That asymmetry causes more trouble for learners than the grammar does.

**Asking upward.** `～てもいいですか` is perfectly polite to a classmate, a colleague or a shop assistant. To a customer, a client or a senior manager it is a little bare, and Japanese reaches for something longer: `～てもよろしいでしょうか`, or the humble `～させていただけますか`. The extra length *is* the politeness.

**Granting upward is the mistake.** <code><ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、もう<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってもいいですよ</code> is flawless grammar and socially impossible - you have just given your boss permission to leave. When someone senior asks *you*, agree instead of authorising: `どうぞ`, `もちろんです`, <code>はい、<ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>です</code>.

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Nuance & register (cont.)

**In casual speech `も` disappears.** <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ていい？</code>, <code><ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っていい？</code> - the shortened form is what you will actually hear between friends, and it is not sloppy, just spoken. `～たっていい` is another casual variant (<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたっていいよ</code>), slightly more argumentative in tone: "you *could* eat it, you know."

**Refusals almost never use `いけません`.** Answering <code><ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってもいいですか</code> with `いいえ、いけません` sounds like a teacher scolding a child. Adults say `すみません、ちょっと…`, or state the rule impersonally: <code><ruby>館内<rt>かんない</rt></ruby>は<ruby>撮影<rt>さつえい</rt></ruby><ruby>禁止<rt>きんし</rt></ruby>となっております</code>.

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Nuance & register (cont.)

**A second, quieter use: offering.** <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってもいいよ</code> is not permission at all - it means "I'm willing to go" / "I don't mind going." Said flatly it can sound grudging, as though you are doing everyone a favour, so it usually comes with `よ` and a warm tone.

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · ～てもいい vs ～てはいけない

> **Rule of thumb** - `～てもいい` opens the door; `～てはいけない` shuts it. They are the two ends of the same axis, but they are not used with the same freedom: permission is offered constantly, prohibition mostly by rules and by adults talking to children.

ここで<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってもいい**ですか。<br>*May I take photographs here?*

ここで<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってはいけません**。<br>*You must not take photographs here.*

The second sentence is what a sign says, or what a guard says. Between equals it lands hard, which is why a refused request usually gets softened into an apology instead.

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · ～てもいい vs ～なくてもいい

> **Rule of thumb** - `～てもいい` permits the **doing**. `～なくてもいい` permits the **not doing**. Both are permission; they release you from opposite things.

<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby>**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてもいい**ですよ。<br>*You can eat all of it. (help yourself)*

<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby>**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べなくてもいい**ですよ。<br>*You don't have to eat all of it. (stop whenever you like)*

These two complete the square with obligation: `～なくてはいけない` says you must, `～なくてもいい` says you needn't, `～てはいけない` says you mustn't, `～てもいい` says you may.

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · ～てもいいですか vs ～てくれませんか

> **Rule of thumb** - `～てもいいですか` asks about **my** action. `～てくれませんか` asks **you** to act. English "can I / can you" blurs this; Japanese does not.

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めてもいい**ですか。<br>*May I close the window? (I will close it)*

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めてくれませんか。<br>*Could you close the window? (you close it)*

Getting these backwards produces a genuinely confusing sentence, because the listener cannot tell whose hand is meant to move. If you are reaching for the handle yourself, it is always `～てもいいですか`.

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Common mistakes

- ❌ この<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>**るもいい**ですか。 → ✅ この<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>**ってもいい**ですか。<br>*`もいい` attaches to the て-form, never to the dictionary form.*

- ❌ ペンはこれ**もいい**ですか。 → ✅ ペンはこれ**でもいい**ですか。<br>*Nouns and な-adjectives need `で` before `もいい`. Only verbs and い-adjectives supply their own て/くて.*

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、もう<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってもいいですよ。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、あとは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>がやりますので、どうぞお<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に。<br>*The grammar is fine and the direction is not. You do not grant a superior permission; you offer to take over.*

- ❌ ここで<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>っても**よくないです**。 → ✅ ここで<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>っては**いけません**。<br>*Negating `いい` does not produce prohibition. "May not" has its own pattern.*

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Practice 1

**1.** すみません、この<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>____いいですか。

{reveal}
この<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>**ってもいい**ですか。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>る</code> is an う-verb in る, so its て-form is <code><ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>って</code>, and `もいい` attaches to that. Asking permission about your own action is exactly what this pattern is for.

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みですから、（<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てもいい・<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいい）ですよ。 - meaning "you don't need to come".

{reveal}
<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みですから、**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいい**ですよ。

{reveal}
`～てもいい` would give them permission to come, which is the opposite of the intended message. Releasing someone from an obligation needs the negative stem: <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいい</code>. Note the reading change - こ, not き.

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Can I ask one more question?"

{reveal}
もう<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つ**<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>してもいい**ですか。 （politer: もう<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>してもよろしいでしょうか。）

{reveal}
The action is yours, so permission is the right frame rather than a request. In a meeting or to a customer, stretch it to `よろしいでしょうか` - the longer ending is what carries the deference.

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Practice 4

**4.** A student says to their teacher: <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>、もう<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってもいいですよ。 They mean "may I go home now?" Fix it.

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>、そろそろ<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってもよろしいでしょうか。

{reveal}
Two problems. `よ` turns the sentence into a statement that *grants* permission, so the student has dismissed the teacher. And a plain question needs to be a question: `～てもいいですか`, or upward, `～てもよろしいでしょうか`.

---

## ～てもいい - it's okay to · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいいです。 (b) <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないでください。

{reveal}
(a) releases the listener from an obligation: coming is now optional, and if they turn up anyway nothing is wrong.

{reveal}
(b) asks them not to come. It is a request with a reason behind it - the office is closed, the room is being used - and turning up anyway would go against what was asked. (a) is generous; (b) is restrictive.

---

# ～てはいけない - must not

**Meaning** · you must not do it; doing it is not allowed

`～てはいけない` is prohibition. Broken apart it says "as for doing it - that won't do": `は` puts the action up as a topic and `いけない` (literally "it cannot go") passes judgement on it. Because the judgement comes from a rule or from someone with authority, the pattern always sounds like it is being handed down from above.

---

## ～てはいけない - must not · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + はいけない | <ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってはいけない |
| い-adjective | drop い + くて + はいけない | <ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>くてはいけない |
| な-adjective | drop だ + で + はいけない | <ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby>ではいけない |
| Noun | noun + で + はいけない | うそ → うそではいけない |
| Polite | いけない → いけません | <ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってはいけません |
| Casual contraction | ては → ちゃ | <ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っちゃいけない |
| Casual contraction (で-form) | では → じゃ | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んじゃいけない |
| Written, rules and law | いけない → ならない | <ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってはならない |
| Blunt spoken | いけない → だめ | <ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っちゃだめ |

---

## ～てはいけない - must not · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the て/で of the verb decides the contraction. <code><ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いて</code> → <code><ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いちゃ</code>, but <code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んで</code> → <code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んじゃ</code>. There is no ❌<code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでちゃ</code>.

---

## ～てはいけない - must not · Examples

ここでたばこを**<ruby>吸<rt>す</rt></ruby>ってはいけません**。<br>*You must not smoke here.*

<ruby>授業中<rt>じゅぎょうちゅう</rt></ruby>にスマホを**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てはいけません**。<br>*You mustn't look at your phone during class.*

そんなことを**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っちゃいけない**よ。<br>*You shouldn't say things like that.*

<ruby>芝生<rt>しばふ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってはいけません**。<br>*Keep off the grass.*

---

## ～てはいけない - must not · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>では、<ruby>二十歳<rt>はたち</rt></ruby>になるまでお<ruby>酒<rt>さけ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んではいけません**。<br>*In Japan you may not drink alcohol until you turn twenty.*

<ruby>試験中<rt>しけんちゅう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>ってはいけません**。<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>下<rt>した</rt></ruby>に<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>いてください。<br>*You may not use a dictionary during the exam - please put it under your desk.*

<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れてはいけない**ので、カレンダーに<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いておきました。<br>*It wouldn't do to forget the appointment, so I wrote it on the calendar.*

この<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きな<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してはならない**。<br>*No loud voices are permitted inside this building.*

---

## ～てはいけない - must not · Nuance & register

`～てはいけない` is **strong and top-down**. It is the voice of a rule, a teacher, a parent, a sign, a manual. That is not a matter of politeness level - putting it in `ません` form makes it polite prohibition, not gentle prohibition.

**Who says it to whom.** Adults say it to children, teachers to students, staff to visitors. Between colleagues and friends it is jarring, and upward it is unthinkable. If you need to stop a superior or a customer doing something, the language goes impersonal and passive-aggressive on purpose: <code><ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>ございませんが、こちらは<ruby>禁煙<rt>きんえん</rt></ruby>となっております</code>, or <code>ご<ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>ください</code> ("please refrain"). Nobody is *told* anything; a rule simply exists.

---

## ～てはいけない - must not · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Contractions belong to speech.** <code><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っちゃだめ</code> is what a parent actually shouts in a car park; `～てはいけません` is what the sign on the wall says. `ちゃ` and `じゃ` are casual but not rude - they are the everyday shape of this pattern among family and friends. `だめ` for `いけない` is blunter still and very common with children.

**`～てはならない` is written.** You meet it in laws, contracts, school rules and formal notices, rarely in conversation. `いけない` covers the individual case; `ならない` states a standing prohibition.

---

## ～てはいけない - must not · Nuance & register (cont.)

**It can point at yourself.** <code><ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れてはいけないので、<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>めに<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ます</code> - "since it would be bad to be late, I'll leave early." Here nobody is forbidding anything; the speaker is judging an outcome as unacceptable. This use is common and often missed by learners who only learn the pattern as "must not".

---

## ～てはいけない - must not · ～てはいけない vs ～ないでください

> **Rule of thumb** - `～てはいけない` states a **rule**. `～ないでください` makes a **request**. One says the thing is forbidden; the other says please don't.

ここに<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>めてはいけません**。<br>*You may not park here.*

ここに<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>めないでください。<br>*Please don't park here.*

The first cites an authority the speaker did not invent; the second is the speaker asking. In everyday interaction - a neighbour, a colleague, a stranger - `～ないでください` is almost always the right choice, and learners under-use it badly because textbooks teach `～てはいけない` first.

---

## ～てはいけない - must not · ～てはいけない vs ～な

> **Rule of thumb** - same content, different force. `～てはいけない` is a rule delivered in words. `～な` is a bark.

<ruby>廊下<rt>ろうか</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ってはいけません**。<br>*You mustn't run in the corridor.*

<ruby>廊下<rt>ろうか</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>るな**。<br>*Don't run in the corridor!*

The bare `な` prohibitive is abrupt and male-coded, at home in orders, warnings and signs. `～てはいけません` is the neutral written and spoken standard, and the form you should default to until you have a very good ear for register.

---

## ～てはいけない - must not · ～てはいけない vs ～なくてはいけない

> **Rule of thumb** - `～ては` forbids; `～なくては` compels. One `なく` flips the sentence to its opposite, and it is easy to lose in a long sentence.

この<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んではいけません**。<br>*You must not take this medicine.*

この<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まなくてはいけません**。<br>*You must take this medicine.*

Both end in `いけません`, so the ending tells you nothing. Read the middle: a て-form means prohibition, a `なくて` means obligation. Together with `～てもいい` (may) and `～なくてもいい` (needn't) these four cover the whole permission grid.

---

## ～てはいけない - must not · Common mistakes

- ❌ ここに<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>**るはいけません**。 → ✅ ここに<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>**ってはいけません**。<br>*`はいけない` attaches to the て-form. The dictionary form never takes it.*

- ❌ <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ん**でちゃ**いけない。 → ✅ <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ん**じゃ**いけない。<br>*Verbs whose て-form is `で` contract to `じゃ`, not `ちゃ`.*

- ❌ <ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>ん**では**いけませんよ。（intending "you have to take your medicine"）→ ✅ <ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>ま**なくては**いけませんよ。<br>*Prohibition and obligation differ by one `なく`. This mix-up can be dangerous, not merely wrong.*

---

## ～てはいけない - must not · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、ここでたばこを<ruby>吸<rt>す</rt></ruby>ってはいけません。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、こちらは<ruby>禁煙<rt>きんえん</rt></ruby>となっておりますので…<br>*Grammatically fine, socially impossible. Upward, you point at the rule rather than at the person.*

---

## ～てはいけない - must not · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>美術館<rt>びじゅつかん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>____いけません。

{reveal}

# な - don't (blunt prohibitive)

**Meaning** · don't do it - a flat, unsoftened order not to do something

`な` is the shortest prohibition in the language: one syllable stuck onto the dictionary form. It carries no politeness, no reason and no room for discussion, which is exactly why it survives in the places where speed matters - warnings, orders, sports, signs, and the mouths of angry fathers in films.

---

## な - don't (blunt prohibitive) · Formation

`な` attaches to the **dictionary form**, and to nothing else. It does not conjugate and it cannot be made polite.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| う-verb | dictionary form + な | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くな |
| る-verb | dictionary form + な | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>るな |
| する | する + な | <ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>するな |
| <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る | くる + な | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るな |
| Softened among friends | + よ | <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>うなよ |
| Shouted / on a sign | + ！ | <ruby>触<rt>さわ</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>触<rt>さわ</rt></ruby>るな！ |

---

## な - don't (blunt prohibitive) · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - dictionary form, always. There is no ❌<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないな</code> and no ❌<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きな</code> meaning "don't go" - the second is a *positive* command, and the trap is covered below.

い-adjectives, な-adjectives and nouns cannot take prohibitive `な` at all. When you see `な` after <code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い</code> or <code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ</code>, it is a different word entirely.

---

## な - don't (blunt prohibitive) · Examples

それに**<ruby>触<rt>さわ</rt></ruby>るな**。<br>*Don't touch that.*

<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>**するな**。<br>*Don't worry.*

そんなに**<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぐな**よ。<br>*Don't rush so much.*

<ruby>危<rt>あぶ</rt></ruby>ない。<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>るな**。<br>*Danger. Do not enter.*

---

## な - don't (blunt prohibitive) · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>冗談<rt>じょうだん</rt></ruby>だよ。そんなに**<ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>るな**よ。<br>*I'm joking. Don't get so angry.*

<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>最後<rt>さいご</rt></ruby>まで**あきらめるな**。<br>*Don't give up until the match is over.*

まだ**<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るな**。<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>の<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>ができていないんだ。<br>*Don't come in yet - the room isn't ready.*

この<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>うな**よ。<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>発表<rt>はっぴょう</rt></ruby>するから。<br>*Don't tell anyone about this - we're announcing it next week.*

---

## な - don't (blunt prohibitive) · Nuance & register

This is the one N5 pattern where register is not a footnote but the entire point.

**It is male-coded and harsh.** Prohibitive `な` belongs to men's speech, especially older men, and to relationships with a clear hierarchy: a coach to a player, a father to a child, a boss to a subordinate he is annoyed with, one close male friend to another. A woman using it sounds deliberately rough, which is sometimes exactly the intended effect and never a neutral choice. Learners should be able to **recognise** `な` everywhere and should be very cautious about **producing** it.

**Where you will actually meet it.** Signs and warnings (<code><ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>るな</code>, <code><ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>すな</code>), manga and film dialogue, song lyrics, sports slogans (<code><ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けるな</code>, `あきらめるな`), and advertising copy that wants to sound urgent. In those written contexts the harshness reads as brevity rather than rudeness - a sign has no register.

---

## な - don't (blunt prohibitive) · Nuance & register (cont.)

**`よ` changes it more than you would expect.** <code><ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>くな</code> is an order; <code><ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>くなよ</code> is closer to "hey, don't cry" and can be affectionate. It stays casual and still leans male, but the edge comes off.

**Never aim it upward, and rarely sideways.** To a superior, a customer, a stranger or anyone you use です／ます with, the choices are `～ないでください`, <code>ご<ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>ください</code>, or `～てはいけません` if you are stating a rule. There is no polite version of `な`; the archaic `～ますな` (<code>お<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れなさいますな</code>) survives only in period drama.

**It is not the same `な` as `～なさい`.** Nor is it the sentence-final particle you hear in <code>いい<ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>だな</code>. Both look identical on the page. The next section separates them.

---

## な - don't (blunt prohibitive) · な vs ～ないで・～ないでください

> **Rule of thumb** - same instruction, opposite social weight. `な` orders; `～ないでください` asks. If you are not certain which you want, you want `～ないでください`.

ここに<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>めるな**。<br>*Don't park here.*

ここに<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>めないでください。<br>*Please don't park here.*

---

## な - don't (blunt prohibitive) · な vs ～ないで・～ないでください (cont.)

Between the two sits plain `～ないで` (<code><ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>めないで</code>), which is casual but not rough, and is the everyday negative request among friends and family - including for speakers who would never say `な`. Think of it as the neutral option that `な` is the shouted version of.

---

## な - don't (blunt prohibitive) · 行くな vs 行きな

> **Rule of thumb** - dictionary form + `な` = **don't**. ます-stem + `な` = **do**. One syllable of difference reverses the sentence.

<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くな**。<br>*Don't go on ahead.*

<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きな**。<br>*Go on ahead.*

---

## な - don't (blunt prohibitive) · 行くな vs 行きな (cont.)

<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きな</code> is `～なさい` with the tail bitten off, so it is a soft command - warm, casual, common from mothers and shopkeepers, and much less aggressive than prohibitive `な`. <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べるな</code> means don't eat; <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べな</code> means go ahead and eat. Reading the stem correctly is the whole game here.

---

## Prohibitive な vs the sentence-final particle な

> **Rule of thumb** - if it follows an い-adjective, a な-adjective or a noun, it can **only** be the emotive particle. Only after a dictionary-form verb is prohibition even possible.

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い**な**。<br>*It's cold today, isn't it.*

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けるな**。<br>*Don't open the window.*

---

## Prohibitive な vs the sentence-final particle な (cont.)

After a plain verb both readings exist, and three things separate them: the emotive particle is usually drawn out (`なあ`), it is said with a falling, musing tone rather than a bark, and it attaches happily to non-volitional predicates - <code>よく<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るな</code> can only mean "it really is raining," because you cannot order the sky. Prohibition needs an action the listener could choose to stop.

---

## な - don't (blunt prohibitive) · Common mistakes

- ❌ そこに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>か**ないな**。 → ✅ そこに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>**くな**。<br>*`な` goes on the dictionary form. Adding it to the ない-form produces something that is not a prohibition at all.*

- ❌ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**な**。（intending "don't eat it"）→ ✅ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**るな**。<br>*Without `る` you have said the opposite: "go on, eat." Keep the dictionary form intact.*

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、そこに<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>るな。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、そちらの<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>はご<ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>ください。<br>*There is no polite `な`. Upward, switch pattern entirely.*

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ**な**。（intending "don't be quiet"）→ ✅ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かに**するな**。<br>*`な` after a な-adjective is the emotive particle - you have said "how quiet it is." Prohibition needs a verb.*

---

## な - don't (blunt prohibitive) · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>り**ますな**。 → ✅ <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>**るな**。／<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>らないでください。<br>*`な` cannot be attached to a polite form. `～ますな` exists but is archaic and will sound like a samurai drama.*

# ～なくてはいけない・～なくてはならない - must; have to

**Meaning** · you have to do it - not doing it is not an option

Japanese builds obligation out of two negatives. `～なくては` means "if you don't", `いけない`／`ならない` means "that won't do", so the whole thing is *not doing it won't do*. Nothing in the pattern says the speaker wants you to act - the pressure comes from a rule, a schedule or the situation itself, and the speaker is merely reporting it.

---

## ～なくてはいけない・～なくてはならない - must; have to · Formation

Everything hinges on the negative stem. Make the ない-form first, drop the `い`, add `くては`.

---

---

## Nuance & register

The four endings are not interchangeable in feel, and the differences are the kind a textbook table hides.

**`いけない` vs `ならない`.** `いけない` handles the case in front of you: *I* have to do this, *today*. `ならない` states a standing requirement and sounds heavier and more written - laws, regulations, company rules, formal writing. <code><ruby>税金<rt>ぜいきん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>わなければならない</code> reads like a statute, while <code><ruby>税金<rt>ぜいきん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>わなくちゃ</code> is a person groaning at a form.

**`なくては` vs `なければ`.** `なければ` is the tidier, more formal conditional and dominates in writing and careful speech. `なくては` is a shade more conversational. Paired up, `なければならない` is the most formal of the four and `なくてはいけない` the most everyday.

---

## Nuance & register (cont.)

**Contractions are everywhere in speech.** `なくちゃ`, `なきゃ`, and very often with the ending simply dropped: <code>あ、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>しなきゃ</code>, <code>もう<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>なくちゃ</code>. Learners rarely produce these and hear them constantly. Note that they are casual, not sloppy - but they do not belong in a report or a speech.

**`～ないと` is the most common spoken form of all.** <code><ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>がないと</code>, <code><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>わないといけない</code>. If you listen to two friends planning a weekend, `ないと` will outnumber the textbook forms considerably.

---

## Nuance & register (cont.)

**Direction of use.** Describing your own obligations is safe at any politeness level. Telling someone else what they must do is strong - to a superior, replace it with a question (<code>ご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>いただけますでしょうか</code>) or state the rule without an addressee. Between friends it is fine, and often sympathetic: the speaker is on your side against the requirement.

---

## ～なくてはいけない vs ～なくてもいい

> **Rule of thumb** - one syllable apart, opposite meanings. `～なくてはいけない` = must; `～なくてもいい` = needn't. The **は** closes the option; the **も** opens it.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>七時<rt>しちじ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてはいけません**。<br>*You have to be here at seven tomorrow.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>七時<rt>しちじ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいいです**。<br>*You don't have to be here at seven tomorrow.*

Because they differ by a single particle, they are easy to mishear and easy to mistype. When you want to release someone from a duty, check that you wrote `も`.

---

## ～なくてはいけない vs ～てはいけない

> **Rule of thumb** - `～なくては` compels, `～ては` forbids. The `いけない` at the end is shared and tells you nothing; the information is in the middle of the word.

この<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>かなくてはいけません**。<br>*You have to write your name on this form.*

この<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてはいけません**。<br>*You must not write your name on this form.*

Laid out, the four corners of the grid are: `～てもいい` may, `～てはいけない` must not, `～なくてはいけない` must, `～なくてもいい` needn't. Permission and prohibition sit on the て-form; obligation and exemption sit on the negative stem.

---

## いけない vs ならない vs ないと

> **Rule of thumb** - `ならない` for rules on paper, `いけない` for what you personally have to do, `ないと` for what you are saying out loud to a friend.

<ruby>申込書<rt>もうしこみしょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さ**なければならない**。<br>*Applications must be submitted by March.*

<ruby>申込書<rt>もうしこみしょ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さ**ないと**。<br>*I need to get the application in by March.*

The first is the wording of a notice; the second is a person remembering a deadline in the kitchen. Same requirement, completely different voice - and the trailing `ないと` with nothing after it is entirely normal Japanese, not an unfinished sentence.

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**なくては**いけません。 → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>か**なくては**いけません。<br>*Build the ない-form first (<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない</code>), then swap `い` for `くては`. The dictionary form never takes it.*

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**だなくては**いけません。 → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**でなくては**いけません。<br>*Nouns and な-adjectives negate with `で(は)ない`, so the join is `でなくては`.*

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>な**くては**いいです。（intending "you don't have to come"）→ ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>な**くても**いいです。<br>*`は` and `も` are doing all the work. `なくてはいい` is not a pattern.*

---

## Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>しなくてはいけませんよ。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、こちらの<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>をご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>いただけますでしょうか。<br>*Stating someone's obligations to them is strong even in polite form. Upward, ask rather than require.*

- ❌ <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>が**なきゃなりません**。 → ✅ <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>が**なければなりません**。／<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>が**なきゃ**。<br>*Mixing registers inside one word is jarring: `なきゃ` is the casual contraction and does not take the formal `なりません` tail.*

## ～なくてはいけない・～なくてはならない - must; have to · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>き____いけません。

{reveal}
<ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>き**なくては**いけません。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きる</code> → <code><ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きない</code> → <code><ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きなくては</code>. The obligation comes from outside the speaker - an early train, a meeting - which is exactly what this pattern reports.

---

## ～なくてはいけない・～なくてはならない - must; have to · Practice 2

**2.** パーティーですから、ネクタイをし（なくては・なくても）いけません。

{reveal}
ネクタイをし**なくては**いけません。

{reveal}
`なくても` plus `いけない` is not a pattern - `なくても` pairs with `いい`. If the tie is required, use `なくてはいけません`; if it is optional, the whole ending changes to `しなくてもいいです`.

---

## ～なくてはいけない・～なくてはならない - must; have to · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese, casually, as if muttering to yourself: "I have to do the laundry today."

{reveal}
<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>洗濯<rt>せんたく</rt></ruby>しなきゃ。 （also fine: <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>洗濯<rt>せんたく</rt></ruby>しなくちゃ。）

{reveal}
Muttered self-obligation is the natural home of the contractions, and the `いけない` tail is normally left off entirely. Writing out <code><ruby>洗濯<rt>せんたく</rt></ruby>しなければなりません</code> here would sound like a regulation about laundry.

---

## ～なくてはいけない・～なくてはならない - must; have to · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the problem: <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>までにレポートを<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>すなくてはならないです。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>までにレポートを<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さなくてはなりません。

{reveal}
Two errors. `なくては` attaches to the negative stem, so <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> becomes <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さない</code> → <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さなくては</code>. And `ならない` is a verb, not an adjective: its polite form is `なりません`, never ❌`ならないです`.

---

## ～なくてはいけない・～なくてはならない - must; have to · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>会員<rt>かいいん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>毎年<rt>まいとし</rt></ruby><ruby>会費<rt>かいひ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>わなければならない。 (b) <ruby>会費<rt>かいひ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>わなくちゃ。

{reveal}
(a) is the wording of a rule. `なければならない` states a standing requirement that applies to a class of people, and the sentence would sit comfortably in a membership agreement.

{reveal}
(b) is one member remembering it. The contraction, the dropped ending and the comma-pause all mark it as spoken and personal - the obligation has stopped being a regulation and become a chore.
