# N5 - Comparing

- いちばん
- ～のなかで～がいちばん
- ～より～のほうが～

---

# いちばん

**Meaning** · the most; the best; number one

Japanese adjectives have no `-est` ending. `いちばん` supplies it: put this one word in front of an adjective and it becomes a superlative. Literally "number one", and it is written either as `いちばん` or with the kanji <ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby>.

---

## いちばん · Usage

`いちばん` is an adverb, so it goes **directly in front of the word it makes superlative** - an adjective, a な-adjective, or an adverb - and nothing comes between them.

- Before an い-adjective: いちばん<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い, いちばん<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい
- Before a な-adjective: いちばん<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か（な）, いちばん<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>（な）
- Before an adverb: いちばん<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました, "arrived earliest"
- Before <code><ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き</code>, which behaves as a な-adjective:<br>*いちばん<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな<ruby>色<rt>いろ</rt></ruby>*

It can also stand alone as a noun meaning "the top one" - <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby>になりたい, "I want to be number one in Japan" - and it feeds the ordinal <ruby>一番目<rt>いちばんめ</rt></ruby>, "the first".

---

## いちばん · Usage (cont.)

The set of things being compared is usually left unsaid and understood from context. When you do want to state it explicitly, Japanese wraps `いちばん` in the frame <code>～の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で～がいちばん…</code>, which has its own page.

---

## いちばん · Examples

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>のケーキが**いちばん**おいしいです。<br>*The cake at this shop is the best.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>が**いちばん**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*Summer is my favourite season.*

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>までは、この<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>が**いちばん**<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>いですよ。<br>*This road is the shortest way to the station.*

<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>は、よく<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>るのが**いちばん**<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>だと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。<br>*Before an exam, I think sleeping well is the most important thing of all.*

---

## いちばん · Nuance & register

Register-neutral and extremely frequent in ordinary speech. Nothing about it marks age, gender or formality.

In kanji it is <ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby>, and both spellings are normal; textbooks and signage tend to use the kanji, casual writing the kana. The reading never changes.

The formal written equivalent is <code><ruby>最<rt>もっと</rt></ruby>も</code>. A newspaper writes <ruby>最<rt>もっと</rt></ruby>も<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>; a person talking to you says いちばん<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>. Using <code><ruby>最<rt>もっと</rt></ruby>も</code> in conversation sounds like you are reading from a report.

---

## いちばん · Nuance & register (cont.)

Do not confuse `いちばん` with `もっと`. `もっと` means "more" - a comparative, not a superlative - so もっと<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby> is "a cheaper shop" while いちばん<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby> is "the cheapest shop". The two are one syllable apart and constantly mixed up.

Spoken Japanese also uses `いちばん` loosely as a rough intensifier of preference - 「やっぱりこれがいちばん」, "this really is the best" - where no real ranking has been carried out. That usage is casual and warm, and perfectly standard.

---

## いちばん · Common mistakes

- ❌ この<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いいちばんです。 → ✅ この<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>が**いちばん<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い**です。<br>*`いちばん` goes *in front of* the adjective, never after it. Word order carries the whole meaning here.*

- ❌ <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>より**いちばん**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。 → ✅ <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>より<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*`より` compares exactly two things, and a superlative needs three or more. The two patterns cannot be combined in one sentence.*

- ❌ いちばん**の**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby> → ✅ **いちばん<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby><br>*No `の` between `いちばん` and the adjective. `いちばん` is an adverb here, not a noun.*

- ❌ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>がいちばん<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなです。 → ✅ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が**いちばん<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**です。<br>*The `な` of a な-adjective appears only before a noun. `いちばん` changes nothing about how the adjective itself is conjugated.*

---

## いちばん · Practice 1

**1.** クラスで<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんが____<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>いです。

{reveal}
クラスで<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんが**いちばん**<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>いです。

{reveal}
`いちばん` marks the top of the group. It sits in front of the part being ranked, and the person holding that rank is marked with `が`, not `は`.

---

## いちばん · Practice 2

**2.** Find and fix the error: この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>はいちばんの<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>です。

{reveal}
✅ この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>が**いちばん<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>**です。

{reveal}
Delete the `の`: `いちばん` attaches straight onto the adjective. Changing `は` to `が` is also natural, because picking out the top item of a set is exactly the job `が` does.

---

## いちばん · Practice 3

**3.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) もっと<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きいかばんがほしいです。 (b) いちばん<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きいかばんがほしいです。

{reveal}
(a) is a comparative: the speaker has seen a bag and wants a bigger one than that. No ranking of the whole shop is implied.

{reveal}
(b) is a superlative: out of everything available, the speaker wants the single biggest one. `もっと` moves you one step up the scale; `いちばん` takes you to the top of it.

---

# ～のなかで～がいちばん

**Meaning** · of all ～, ～ is the most ～ - naming the top of a stated group

This is the frame that makes a superlative explicit. The first slot names the field of comparison, the second names the winner. Japanese marks that winner with `が`, never `は`, because the sentence exists precisely to single one item out.

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Formation

Two halves that go together: <code>～の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で</code> sets the field, `～がいちばん + adjective` announces the winner.

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Group noun | Noun + の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で | クラス → クラスの<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で |
| Category noun | Noun + の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で | スポーツ → スポーツの<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で |
| Listed items | A と B と C + の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で | <ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>と<ruby>青<rt>あお</rt></ruby>と<ruby>黒<rt>くろ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で |
| Place or period | Noun + で（no <ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>） | <ruby>世界<rt>せかい</rt></ruby>で／<ruby>一年<rt>いちねん</rt></ruby>で |
| Winner | Noun + が + いちばん + adjective | <ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>がいちばん<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いです |
| Question (people) | だれが いちばん …か | だれがいちばん<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>いですか |
| Question (closed set) | どれが いちばん …か | どれがいちばん<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いですか |
| Question (place / time) | どこが／いつが いちばん …か | どこがいちばん<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いですか |

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the winner takes `が`. Using `は` there （❌<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>はいちばん<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いです）turns the sentence into a topic statement and loses the "out of all of them" force. `が` selects; `は` merely comments.

The question word matches the field: **だれ** for people, **どれ** for a closed set of listed things, **なに** for an open category, **どこ** for places, **いつ** for times.

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Examples

<ruby>果物<rt>くだもの</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で**、りんご**がいちばん**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*Of all fruit, I like apples best.*

クラス**の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>林<rt>はやし</rt></ruby>さん**がいちばん**<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*Hayashi is the tallest in the class.*

<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で**、だれ**がいちばん**<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きますか。<br>*Who gets up earliest in your family?*

<ruby>一年<rt>いちねん</rt></ruby>**で**、<ruby>八月<rt>はちがつ</rt></ruby>**がいちばん**<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*August is the hottest month of the year.*

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Examples (cont.)

この<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つ**の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で**、どれ**がいちばん**<ruby>軽<rt>かる</rt></ruby>いですか。<br>*Which of these three is the lightest?*

<ruby>兄弟<rt>きょうだい</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>**がいちばん**ピアノが<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>です。<br>*Of us siblings, my little sister is the best at the piano.*

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>のメニュー**の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で**、この<ruby>定食<rt>ていしょく</rt></ruby>**がいちばん**<ruby>人気<rt>にんき</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*Of everything on this restaurant's menu, this set meal is the most popular.*

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>た<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で**、あの<ruby>作品<rt>さくひん</rt></ruby>**がいちばん**おもしろかったです。<br>*Of all the films I've seen, that one was the most interesting.*

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. The whole frame works unchanged in plain speech - just drop `です` - and it is one of the first things a learner is asked in conversation practice: <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>がいちばん<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。

The <code><ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby></code> is often left out when the field is a **place or a period of time**, because those are already containers: <ruby>世界<rt>せかい</rt></ruby>でいちばん<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>, <ruby>一年<rt>いちねん</rt></ruby>でいちばん<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>, クラスでいちばん<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>な<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>. Saying <ruby>世界<rt>せかい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で is not wrong, but it is heavier than a native speaker would usually bother with. With a category noun - fruit, sports, films - the <code>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で</code> is the normal choice.

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Nuance & register (cont.)

In writing you will see <code><ruby>最<rt>もっと</rt></ruby>も</code> in place of `いちばん`, and `～のうちで` in place of <code>～の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で</code>. Both are stiffer. Neither changes the grammar.

Answering such a question, you normally repeat the frame only if it is not already obvious. Asked <ruby>季節<rt>きせつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>でいつがいちばん<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか, you answer simply <ruby>秋<rt>あき</rt></ruby>がいちばん<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです - or just <ruby>秋<rt>あき</rt></ruby>です.

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · ～の中で～がいちばん vs ～より～のほうが

> **Rule of thumb** - count the candidates. Two → `より`. Three or more → `いちばん`.

スポーツ**の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で**、サッカー**がいちばん**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*Of all sports, I like football best.*

サッカーより<ruby>野球<rt>やきゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*I like baseball more than football.*

Mixing the two produces sentences that are simply broken: ❌ サッカーよりいちばん<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです. If a `より` phrase is present, the comparison is between two named things and there is no top of any ranking to point at.

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · が vs は in the winner slot

> **Rule of thumb** - this pattern answers "which one?", and answers to "which one?" always take `が`.

<ruby>四人<rt>よにん</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん**がいちばん**<ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*Tanaka is the youngest of the four.*

Replace `が` with `は` and the sentence stops selecting: it would be read as "as for Tanaka, he is the youngest", which is a comment about Tanaka rather than the result of comparing four people. The same logic governs the question - the question word `だれ` cannot take `は` at all.

`は` does appear one step earlier in the sentence, on the field, when the field is being contrasted: <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>では…, "in Japan at least, …".

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · の中で vs で

> **Rule of thumb** - with a **group**, keep <code>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で</code>. With a **place or a stretch of time**, the plain `で` is enough.

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>富士山<rt>ふじさん</rt></ruby>**がいちばん**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*Mt. Fuji is the highest mountain in Japan.*

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>富士山<rt>ふじさん</rt></ruby>**がいちばん**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*Of all the mountains in Japan, Mt. Fuji is the highest.*

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · の中で vs で (cont.)

Both are correct. The first treats Japan as a location; the second explicitly assembles the set of Japanese mountains and ranks it. What you must not do is drop the particle altogether: ❌<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby><ruby>富士山<rt>ふじさん</rt></ruby>がいちばん<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです.

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>果物<rt>くだもの</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で、みかん**は**いちばん<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いです。 → ✅ <ruby>果物<rt>くだもの</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で、みかん**が**いちばん<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*The item being crowned takes `が`. This is the error learners make most often with the pattern, because English "oranges are the cheapest" looks like a topic sentence.*

- ❌ スポーツの<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>**に**、テニスがいちばん<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。 → ✅ スポーツの<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>**で**、テニスがいちばん<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*`で` marks the domain within which the comparison is carried out. `に` would mark a location of existence, which is not what is going on.*

- ❌ <ruby>世界<rt>せかい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>でいちばん<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>世界<rt>せかい</rt></ruby>でいちばん<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby><br>*Not ungrammatical, but a place name plus <code>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で</code> is clumsy. Places and time spans take the bare `で`.*

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ この<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つの<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で、**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>**がいちばんいいですか。 → ✅ この<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つの<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で、**どれ**がいちばんいいですか。<br>*When the candidates have been listed, the question word is `どれ`. `なに` asks about an open field with no stated members.*

- ❌ <ruby>兄弟<rt>きょうだい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>がいちばん<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>**な**です。 → ✅ <ruby>兄弟<rt>きょうだい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>がいちばん<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>です。<br>*`いちばん` does not change how the adjective ends. A な-adjective keeps its `な` only in front of a noun.*

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で、お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>____いちばん<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。

{reveal}
<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で、お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>**が**いちばん<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。

{reveal}
`が` marks the item selected out of the group. It is also the particle <code><ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き</code> normally takes for the thing liked, so the two requirements agree here.

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>と<ruby>秋<rt>あき</rt></ruby>と（どちら・どれ）がいちばん<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。

{reveal}
<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>と<ruby>秋<rt>あき</rt></ruby>と**どちら**が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。

{reveal}
With exactly two candidates you use `どちら` - and then `いちばん` has to go, since there is no ranking of three or more to head. `どれ` needs at least three listed options.

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Which season do you like best?"

{reveal}
<ruby>季節<rt>きせつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で、**いつがいちばん**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。 （also natural: どの<ruby>季節<rt>きせつ</rt></ruby>がいちばん<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。）

{reveal}
Seasons are times, so `いつ` fits; <code>どの + <ruby>季節<rt>きせつ</rt></ruby></code> is the other standard way to ask. Either way the question word carries `が`, never `は`.

---

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>はいちばんきれいだと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>**で**、<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>**が**いちばんきれいだと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
Two particles are wrong. The field of comparison takes `で`, not `に`, and the selected item takes `が`, not `は`.

## ～のなかで～がいちばん · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>のパンはおいしいです。 (b) この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で、この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>のパンがいちばんおいしいです。

{reveal}
(a) is an unranked judgement - the bread here is good, and nothing is said about anywhere else.

{reveal}
(b) sets up a field (the shops around here), compares them, and awards first place. The listener now knows the speaker has tried the others. That explicit field is exactly what the pattern adds over a bare adjective.

# ～より～のほうが～

**Meaning** · ～ is more ～ than ～ - comparing exactly two things

Japanese adjectives have no comparative form: <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い covers both "tall" and "taller". The comparison is carried entirely by the particles. `より` marks the loser, <code>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が</code> marks the winner, and the adjective stays exactly as it is.

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (winner) | Noun + の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が | <ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が |
| Noun (loser) | Noun + より | <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>より |
| Verb | plain form + <ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が（no の） | <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が |
| い-adjective | plain form + <ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が |
| な-adjective | stem + な + <ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かな<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が |
| Question | AとBと、どちらが + adjective + か | どちらが<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いですか |
| Answer | Bの<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が + adjective + です | Bの<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いです |
| Short form | Aより + adjective | <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>より<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いです |

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the order is free. AよりBの<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです and Bの<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>がAより<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです say the same thing. What is fixed is which particle goes on which noun: `より` on the one that loses, <code>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が</code> on the one that wins. Swap the particles and you reverse the sentence.

Either half can also stand alone. <code><ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>より<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いです</code> ("colder than last year") and <code>こちらの<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いです</code> ("this one is cheaper") are both complete, ordinary sentences.

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · Examples

<ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>**より**<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*I like cats more than dogs.*

バス**より**<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*The train is faster than the bus.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>**より**<ruby>暖<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かいですね。<br>*It's warmer today than yesterday, isn't it.*

<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>と<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>と、どちら**の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>いですか。<br>*Which has more people, Tokyo or Osaka?*

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>肉<rt>にく</rt></ruby>**より**<ruby>魚<rt>さかな</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>を**よく<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます。<br>*I eat fish more often than meat.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>**より**<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*I'm slightly taller than my older sister.*

<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**より**、<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>いですよ。<br>*It's quicker to walk than to go by car.*

<ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っていた**より**ずっと<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>かったです。<br>*Hokkaido in winter was far colder than I had expected.*

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. In casual speech <code>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が</code> is often reduced to <code>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby></code> with the `が` dropped - こっちの<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いよ - and the whole `より` half is regularly left out when it is obvious what the comparison is against.

Degree adverbs slot in before the adjective and are very common: `ずっと` (far, by a long way), `もっと` (more), <code><ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し</code> and `ちょっと` (a little). <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>よりずっと<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいです.

The question form is `AとBと、どちらが…`. Note three things about it: `どちら` not `どれ` (two candidates, not three); `が` not `は`; and the polite `どちら` has a casual counterpart `どっち` that you will hear constantly in conversation - <ruby>紅茶<rt>こうちゃ</rt></ruby>とコーヒー、どっちがいい?

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · Nuance & register (cont.)

<code>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby></code> literally means "side, direction", and that image is worth keeping: you are pointing at one side of a pair. It is why the pattern feels wrong with three or more items - there is no "other side" to point away from.

Finally, `より` has a second life in formal writing and announcements where it means "from": <ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>より<ruby>開<rt>ひら</rt></ruby>きます, "opening from nine". Same character, unrelated job.

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · ～より～の方が vs ～の中で～がいちばん

> **Rule of thumb** - two candidates take `より`; three or more take `いちばん`. They never appear in the same clause.

コーヒー**より**<ruby>紅茶<rt>こうちゃ</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*I prefer tea to coffee.*

<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で<ruby>紅茶<rt>こうちゃ</rt></ruby>がいちばん<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*Of all drinks, tea is my favourite.*

The first ranks two named things and says nothing about anything else - the speaker may dislike both. The second crowns a winner over a whole field.

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · comparative `より` vs "from" `より`

> **Rule of thumb** - if `より` is followed by an adjective, it is comparing. If it is followed by a verb of starting, opening or beginning, it means "from" and is a formal replacement for `から`.

この<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>は<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>作品<rt>さくひん</rt></ruby>**より**<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*This film is longer than the previous one.*

<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>十時<rt>じゅうじ</rt></ruby>より<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まります。<br>*Reception opens from ten o'clock.*

The second belongs to notices and announcements. In speech you would say <ruby>十時<rt>じゅうじ</rt></ruby>から.

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · ～の方が vs ～方がいい

> **Rule of thumb** - the same word <code><ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby></code>, two different jobs. With an adjective it compares; with `いい` and a verb it advises.

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいパソコン**の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>軽<rt>かる</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*The new laptop is lighter.*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいパソコンを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>がいいですよ。<br>*You'd better buy a new laptop.*

The advice pattern normally takes the た-form of the verb and ends in `いい`. If you find yourself saying <code><ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>がいい</code> when you only meant "more", you have accidentally told someone what to do.

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>**が**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。 → ✅ <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>**より**<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*The losing item needs `より`. Without it there is nothing to compare against and the sentence has two competing `が`.*

- ❌ <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く**の**<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>いです。 → ✅ <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*Nouns take `の` before <code><ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby></code>; verbs and い-adjectives attach directly. Only な-adjectives insert anything, and what they insert is `な`.*

- ❌ <ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby><ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。 → ✅ <ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>**の**<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*The mirror-image error. After a noun the `の` is compulsory.*

- ❌ コーヒーと<ruby>紅茶<rt>こうちゃ</rt></ruby>と、どちらが**いちばん**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。 → ✅ コーヒーと<ruby>紅茶<rt>こうちゃ</rt></ruby>と、どちらが<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。<br>*`いちばん` needs three or more candidates. With two, `どちら` alone already asks the question.*

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>より**もっと<ruby>暖<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かいの<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が**です。 → ✅ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>より<ruby>暖<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かいです。<br>*The adjective itself never changes shape, and the two halves of the pattern do not both attach to it. Comparison lives in the particles, not in the adjective.*

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>飛行機<rt>ひこうき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>新幹線<rt>しんかんせん</rt></ruby>____<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>いです。

{reveal}
<ruby>飛行機<rt>ひこうき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>新幹線<rt>しんかんせん</rt></ruby>**より**<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>いです。

{reveal}
`より` attaches to the thing being beaten. The winner here is already the topic, marked with `は`, so <code>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が</code> is not needed - though <ruby>新幹線<rt>しんかんせん</rt></ruby>より<ruby>飛行機<rt>ひこうき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>いです is equally correct.

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>と<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>と、（どちら・どれ）が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。

{reveal}
<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>と<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>と、**どちら**が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。

{reveal}
Two options means `どちら` (casually `どっち`). `どれ` requires a set of at least three, and choosing it here would leave the listener looking for a third season.

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "This room is quieter than that one."

{reveal}
あの<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>**より**この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かです。

{reveal}
Both halves are nouns, so both take their own particle, and <code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かです</code> stays in its ordinary form - there is no comparative ending to add. Reversing the two halves is also fine.

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**より**<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです。

{reveal}
As written, `は` and <code>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が</code> are fighting over which noun is the subject. Marking the speaker with `より` makes it clear who is being compared with whom - and, incidentally, who is taller.

---

## ～より～のほうが～ · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>です。 (b) この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>った<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>がいいです。

{reveal}
(a) is a comparison. Some other dictionary is in play, and this one is the more useful of the two. The speaker is describing, not instructing.

{reveal}
(b) is advice: you should use this dictionary. The た-form plus <code><ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>がいい</code> is a recommendation, and depending on tone it can sound quite firm. Same noun <code><ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby></code>, entirely different social act.
