---

# だ / です

**Meaning** · the copula - "X is Y"

`だ` and `です` are the word that glues a noun to what it *is*. They are the same word at two politeness levels: `です` is polite, `だ` is plain. Japanese has no verb "to be" in the English sense - this is what stands in for it.

---

## だ / です · Formation

`だ` / `です` come at the very end of the sentence, after the noun or な-adjective they attach to. Nothing changes inside the noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + だ / です | <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>だ / <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>です |
| な-adjective | stem (no な) + だ / です | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かな → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ / <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かです |
| い-adjective | plain adjective + です (never だ) | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです |
| の-phrase (possession) | Noun + の + だ / です | <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>の → <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>のです |
| Question (polite) | Noun + ですか | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>ですか |
| Question (plain) | Noun + ? - the だ drops | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>? |
| Verb | does **not** take です - use ます | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます |

---

## だ / です · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - `です` after an い-adjective is doing a different job. It is not linking anything; the adjective already is the predicate, and `です` is bolted on purely to make the sentence polite. That is why <code><ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです</code> is fine but ❌<code><ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いだ</code> is not: `だ` has no politeness to add.

---

## だ / です · Examples

<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**です**。<br>*Yamada-san is a teacher.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>み**だ**。<br>*Today's a day off.*

わたしの<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**です**。<br>*My room is quiet.*

その<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>の**です**。<br>*That bicycle is my older brother's.*

---

## だ / です · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>**ですか**。<br>*Is it going to rain tomorrow?*

この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい**です**。<br>*This dictionary is new.*

<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わせは<ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>**です**。<br>*We're meeting at six in front of the station.*

「ご<ruby>出身<rt>しゅっしん</rt></ruby>はどちら**ですか**。」「<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>**です**。」<br>*"Where are you from?" "Osaka."*

---

## だ / です · Nuance & register

`です` is the default polite ending and it is safe everywhere: with strangers, at work, in class, in a shop. If you are unsure which form to use, use `です`. It is neither stiff nor warm - it is simply neutral-polite, and nobody will read anything into it.

`だ` is the plain form, used with friends, family, and in your own head. It is also the form used in writing that has no listener - notes, diaries, newspapers, essays - where politeness would be meaningless. What makes `だ` tricky is that in actual speech it is very often **dropped**:

- 「これ、だれのかさ?」「わたしの。」 - the `だ` is simply not said.
- 「あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>、<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>?」 - a plain question does not keep `だ`.

---

## だ / です · Nuance & register (cont.)

Spoken `だ` that is *kept* sounds assertive and a little blunt, and it is more common in men's casual speech. Women's casual speech drops it more often, or softens it with `よ` / `ね` (「そうよ」「そうだね」). A learner who ends every casual sentence with a hard `だ` sounds like a cartoon character; leaving it off is safer.

Two further forms you will meet but do not need to produce yet. `である` is the copula of formal written argument - reports, papers, legal text; it never appears in ordinary conversation. `でございます` is the humble-polite copula of shop staff and announcements: 「こちらが<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいメニューでございます」. Recognising them is enough for now.

---

## だ / です · だ vs です

> **Rule of thumb** - same word, two registers. `です` for anyone you are being polite to; `だ` for people you are close to, and for writing with no reader in mind.

The choice says nothing about meaning and everything about the relationship.

こちらが<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>**です**。<br>*This is my desk.*

そこ、ぼくの<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>**だ**よ。<br>*Hey, that's my seat.*

The second sentence is not ruder in content - it is simply aimed at a friend. Said to a stranger, it would land as abrupt.

---

## だ / です · だ vs です (cont.)

Politeness in Japanese is decided at the **end** of the sentence, so this one word sets the tone for everything that came before it. Do not mix levels inside a single sentence without a reason.

---

## だ / です · い-adjective + です vs な-adjective + だ / です

> **Rule of thumb** - い-adjectives are already complete predicates: add `です` for politeness, and never `だ`. な-adjectives and nouns need the copula to stand up at all.

この<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い**です**。<br>*This luggage is heavy.*

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**です**。<br>*This room is quiet.*

Both end in `です`, but for different reasons - and the difference surfaces the moment you go plain. <code><ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い</code> stands alone as a whole sentence; <code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か</code> cannot, and needs `だ`:

---

## だ / です · い-adjective + です vs な-adjective + だ / です (cont.)

この<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>、**<ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い**。<br>*This luggage is heavy.*

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>、<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**だ**。<br>*This room is quiet.*

So the test is: strip the politeness away. If what is left needs propping up, it takes `だ`.

---

## だ / です · です vs ます

> **Rule of thumb** - `です` makes a **noun or adjective** sentence polite; `ます` makes a **verb** sentence polite. You never need both.

English hides "is" inside every tense, so learners reach for `です` when a verb is already doing the work. Japanese verbs carry their own politeness.

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>**です**。<br>*Tanaka-san is a doctor.*

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>り**ます**。<br>*Tanaka-san runs every day.*

---

## だ / です · です vs ます (cont.)

❌<code><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>るです</code> and ❌<code><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>りますです</code> are both broken. The one place a verb does meet `です` is through a nominaliser - <code><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>るのが<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです</code> - and even there `です` is attached to <code><ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き</code>, a な-adjective, not to the verb.

---

## だ / です · Common mistakes

- ❌ この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いだ。 → ✅ この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い。<br>*An い-adjective is already a predicate. It takes `です` for politeness, but never `だ`.*

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだです。 → ✅ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かです。<br>*`だ` and `です` are one word at two levels. You choose one; you never stack them.*

- ❌ <ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby>コーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>むです。 → ✅ <ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby>コーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*Verbs are made polite with `ます`, not with `です`.*

- ❌ あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だですか。 → ✅ あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>ですか。<br>*In a polite question the whole copula is `です`, and `か` follows it directly.*

---

## だ / です · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ これは<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなです。 → ✅ これは<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かです。<br>*A な-adjective keeps its `な` only in front of a noun (<code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かな<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby></code>). Before the copula the `な` drops.*

---

## だ / です · Practice 1

**1.** あの<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>は<ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>____。（polite）

{reveal}
あの<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>は<ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>**です**。

{reveal}
A noun predicate needs the copula, and the polite copula is `です`. Nothing changes inside the noun itself.

---

## だ / です · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しい（だ・です・nothing）。

{reveal}
<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しい**です** (polite), or <ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しい on its own (plain).

{reveal}
<code><ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しい</code> is an い-adjective, so it is already a complete sentence by itself. Add `です` if you want to be polite, but ❌<code><ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しいだ</code> is never possible.

---

## だ / です · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese, politely: "That umbrella is my mother's."

{reveal}
そのかさは<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>の**です**。

{reveal}
The `の` carries the possession, and the noun it stands for ("umbrella") is simply left out, so `です` attaches straight onto `の`.

---

## だ / です · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るですか。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ますか。

{reveal}
The predicate is a verb, and verbs take `ます` for politeness - `です` has no work to do here. The question particle `か` then follows `ます`.

---

## だ / です · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in feel: (a) <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みです。 (b) <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みだ。

{reveal}
The information is identical: tomorrow is a day off. Only the relationship differs.

{reveal}
(a) is aimed at someone you are being polite to - a colleague, a customer, a teacher. (b) is aimed at a friend, or at nobody at all: it is also exactly how the sentence would be written in a diary or a memo. Said out loud to a stranger, (b) sounds abrupt.

---

# じゃない・ではない

**Meaning** · the negative copula - "X is not Y"

`じゃない` and `ではない` are the negative of `だ` / `です`. They deny that one thing is the other. `ではない` is the full form; `じゃない` is its everyday contraction, and it is what you will hear almost all the time.

---

## じゃない・ではない · Formation

`では` contracts to `じゃ` in speech, which is the whole story behind the two shapes. Everything below comes in pairs for that reason.

---

## じゃない・ではない · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (plain) | Noun + じゃない / ではない | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>じゃない |
| Noun (polite) | Noun + じゃないです / じゃありません | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>じゃありません |
| Noun (formal) | Noun + ではありません | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>ではありません |
| な-adjective | stem (no な) + じゃない | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かな → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かじゃない |
| い-adjective | ～い → ～くない (never じゃない) | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない |
| Verb | ～ない form (never じゃない) | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない |
| Past | じゃなかった / じゃありませんでした | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>じゃなかった |
| Joining on | じゃなくて (て-form) | Aじゃなくて、B |

---

## じゃない・ではない · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the politeness ladder runs `じゃない` → `じゃないです` → `じゃありません` → `ではありません` → `ではございません`, from most casual to most formal. The middle three are all "polite"; they differ only in how starched they sound.

---

## じゃない・ではない · Examples

これは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のかさ**じゃない**。<br>*This isn't my umbrella.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>木曜日<rt>もくようび</rt></ruby>**じゃありません**。<br>*Today isn't Thursday.*

その<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>**じゃないです**よ。<br>*That story isn't true.*

<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>**じゃなくて**、<ruby>再来週<rt>さらいしゅう</rt></ruby>です。<br>*The exam isn't next week - it's the week after.*

---

## じゃない・ではない · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>冗談<rt>じょうだん</rt></ruby>**じゃない**よ。<ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*I'm not joking. It really happened.*

<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>のとき、わたしは<ruby>真面目<rt>まじめ</rt></ruby>な<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**じゃなかった**。<br>*I wasn't a serious student back in university.*

この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>あまり<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**じゃない**ので、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めて<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ています。<br>*It isn't very quiet around here at night, so I sleep with the window shut.*

<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>ありませんが、これは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>**ではありません**ので、<ruby>係<rt>かかり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>を<ruby>呼<rt>よ</rt></ruby>びます。<br>*I'm very sorry, but this isn't my area - I'll fetch the person in charge.*

---

## じゃない・ではない · Nuance & register

The split between `じゃ` and `では` is one of pronunciation, not of meaning. `ではない` is what you write; `じゃない` is what you say. Reading `ではありません` aloud in a friendly conversation sounds like reading from a form.

Within the polite forms there is a further, smaller split. `じゃありません` is the textbook polite negative and is perfectly normal in speech. `じゃないです` is a shade more conversational - it is built by taking the plain `じゃない` and adding `です`, the same trick い-adjectives use - and it is extremely common in modern spoken Japanese. `ではありません` belongs to announcements, written notices and stiff business speech, where nobody is trying to sound warm.

---

## じゃない・ではない · Nuance & register (cont.)

One more thing about `じゃない` catches learners badly. With **falling** intonation it negates: 「<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんじゃない。」 = "That's not Yamada." With **rising** intonation it does the opposite - it becomes a tag question asserting that something *is* so: 「<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんじゃない?」 = "Isn't that Yamada?" This second use can attach to things the negative copula normally cannot, including い-adjectives and verbs (「<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いじゃない!」 = "That's expensive!"). If you meet `じゃない` attached to an い-adjective, it is this tag-question use, not negation.

In casual speech `じゃない` shortens further to `じゃん`: 「いいじゃん」 = "that's fine, isn't it". It is friendly, young-sounding, and out of place at work.

---

## じゃない・ではない · じゃない vs ～くない

> **Rule of thumb** - `じゃない` negates **nouns and な-adjectives**. い-adjectives negate themselves by changing `～い` to `～くない`.

The two look interchangeable in English ("is not X") but they are not.

この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>**じゃない**。<br>*This problem isn't easy.*

この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>し**くない**。<br>*This problem isn't hard.*

---

## じゃない・ではない · じゃない vs ～くない (cont.)

<code><ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby></code> is a な-adjective, so it needs the copula and takes `じゃない`. <code><ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しい</code> is an い-adjective, so it conjugates on its own. The same test as with `だ`: if the word needs the copula to stand up, it negates with `じゃない`.

`いい` is the irregular one worth learning now: it negates as `よくない`, never ❌`いくない`.

---

## じゃない・ではない · じゃありません vs じゃないです vs ではありません

> **Rule of thumb** - all three are polite. Pick by how formal the room is, not by what you mean.

それは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のかばん**じゃないです**。<br>*That's not my bag.*

それは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のかばん**じゃありません**。<br>*That's not my bag.*

<ruby>当店<rt>とうてん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby><ruby>営業<rt>えいぎょう</rt></ruby>**ではありません**。<br>*Our shop is not open for business today.*

---

## じゃない・ではない · じゃありません vs じゃないです vs ではありません (cont.)

The first is what you say to a coworker; the second is what a textbook or a careful speaker says; the third is what a sign says. A learner who only ever produces `じゃありません` will never sound wrong, which makes it the safe default.

---

## じゃない・ではない · じゃない vs 違う

> **Rule of thumb** - `じゃない` denies an equation ("A is not B"); <code><ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う</code> is a verb meaning "to differ / to be mistaken", and it is what you say to correct someone.

これは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>**じゃない**です。<br>*This isn't my umbrella.*

「<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんですか。」「いいえ、**<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>います**。」<br>*"Are you Tanaka-san?" "No, I'm not."*

---

## じゃない・ではない · じゃない vs 違う (cont.)

Both are heard as "no", but they work differently. <code><ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>います</code> is a complete answer on its own and needs no noun in front of it, which is exactly why it is the standard way to say "that's not right". Notice that answering a question with a bare 「<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>じゃありません」 sounds blunter than 「<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>います」 - the verb is the softer, more idiomatic correction.

---

## じゃない・ではない · Common mistakes

- ❌ この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いじゃない。（meaning "this book isn't expensive"） → ✅ この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない。<br>*い-adjectives negate with `～くない`. The string <code><ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いじゃない</code> does exist, but only as the rising tag question "it's expensive, isn't it!" - the opposite of what you meant.*

- ❌ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだじゃない。 → ✅ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かじゃない。<br>*`じゃない` already contains the copula. Never leave `だ` in front of it.*

- ❌ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>じゃないでした。 → ✅ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>じゃありませんでした。<br>*The polite negative past is built on `ありません`, not on `です`. In plain form: <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>じゃなかった。*

---

## じゃない・ではない · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ わたしはコーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>むじゃない。 → ✅ わたしはコーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まない。<br>*Verbs have their own negative. `じゃない` never attaches to a verb to negate it.*

---

## じゃない・ではない · Practice 1

**1.** あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>____。（plain, negative）

{reveal}
あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**じゃない**。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby></code> is a noun, so it needs the copula, and the plain negative copula is `じゃない`. The fuller `ではない` would be the written equivalent.

---

## じゃない・ではない · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は（<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くない・<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いじゃない）。 - "This shop isn't cheap."

{reveal}
この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くない**。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い</code> is an い-adjective, so it negates by changing `い` to `くない`. The other option would be heard as a tag question meaning "it's cheap, isn't it!", which is the opposite claim.

## じゃない・ではない · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese, politely: "It isn't Monday today - it's Tuesday."

{reveal}
<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>月曜日<rt>げつようび</rt></ruby>**じゃなくて**、<ruby>火曜日<rt>かようび</rt></ruby>です。

{reveal}
The て-form `じゃなくて` links the correction to what follows, so you get one smooth sentence instead of two. 「<ruby>月曜日<rt>げつようび</rt></ruby>じゃありません。<ruby>火曜日<rt>かようび</rt></ruby>です。」 also works and is a little more emphatic.

## じゃない・ではない · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>のアパートは<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>だじゃありませんでした。

{reveal}

{reveal}
<code><ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby></code> is a な-adjective, and the negative form already carries the copula, so the extra `だ` has to go. Only the stem is left in front of `じゃ`.

## じゃない・ではない · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんじゃない。 (b) <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんじゃない?

{reveal}
(a), said with falling intonation, is straightforward negation: "that isn't Yamada."

{reveal}

# だった・でした

**Meaning** · the past copula - "X was Y"

`だった` and `でした` put the copula into the past. `だった` is plain, `でした` is polite. They are how you say what something *was*, where it *was*, and how it *felt* - for nouns and な-adjectives.

---

## だった・でした · Formation

The past copula replaces `だ` / `です` entirely; nothing else in the sentence moves.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + だった / でした | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だった / <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>でした |
| な-adjective | stem (no な) + だった / でした | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>な → <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だった / <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>でした |
| い-adjective | ～い → ～かった (+ です) | <ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しかった / <ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しかったです |
| Negative past | じゃなかった / じゃありませんでした | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>じゃなかった |
| Question | Noun + でしたか / plain + ? | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>でしたか |
| Verb | ～た / ～ました (never でした) | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った / <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました |
| Before a noun | だった + Noun (でした cannot) | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だった<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> |

---

## だった・でした · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the last row is worth pausing on. Inside a sentence, modifying a noun, only the plain `だった` is available: <code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だった<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby></code> = "a person who was a student". The politeness of the whole sentence is still decided at the very end.

---

## だった・でした · Examples

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>のテストは<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>**だった**。<br>*Yesterday's test was easy.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**でした**。<br>*I was a student until last year.*

あの<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>、どう**だった**?<br>*How was that movie?*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どものとき、<ruby>野球<rt>やきゅう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き**でした**。<br>*I liked baseball when I was a child.*

---

## だった・でした · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>水曜日<rt>すいようび</rt></ruby>**じゃなかった**。<ruby>火曜日<rt>かようび</rt></ruby>だったよ。<br>*The meeting wasn't Wednesday. It was Tuesday.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>のパーティーはにぎやか**でしたか**。<br>*Was yesterday's party lively?*

<ruby>十年前<rt>じゅうねんまえ</rt></ruby>、ここは<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>工場<rt>こうじょう</rt></ruby>**でした**が、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>になっています。<br>*Ten years ago this was a big factory, but now it's a park.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>ばかりで<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>**でした**が、とても<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しかったです。<br>*Last year's trip was nothing but rain and pretty rough, but it was a lot of fun.*

---

## だった・でした · Nuance & register

The register split is the same one `だ` and `です` already set up: `でした` for people you are being polite to, `だった` for friends, family and writing with no reader. Nothing about the past tense changes that calculation.

Unlike plain `だ`, plain `だった` is **not** dropped in casual speech. 「テスト、 <ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>?」 works as a present-tense question with no copula at all, but to put it in the past you must say 「<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>だった?」 - there is no other way to carry the tense. This is the first place where beginners who have learned to drop `だ` get stuck.

---

## だった・でした · Nuance & register (cont.)

`でした` also has a quiet second life as part of the polite negative past for adjectives and verbs: `～ませんでした`, `～くありませんでした`. In those it is not the copula any more, just the polite past ending being reused. That is exactly why ❌<code><ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しいでした</code> feels tempting and is wrong: い-adjectives make their own past with `～かった`, and `です` may only be added afterwards.

One nuance about `だった` in speech: with a falling, drawn-out delivery (「そうだった～」) it often marks a sudden recollection - "oh, right, that's how it was." Learners hear this a lot and mistake it for ordinary past tense.

---

## だった・でした · だった vs でした

> **Rule of thumb** - identical meaning, different politeness. `でした` in polite speech, `だった` with friends and in plain-style writing.

<ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>**でした**。<br>*Last week's match was a disappointment.*

<ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>、<ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>**だった**な。<br>*Shame about last week's match.*

There is one asymmetry worth knowing. `だった` can sit **inside** a sentence, modifying a noun or leading into <code>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code>; `でした` cannot, because polite forms live only at the end.

---

## だった・でした · だった vs でした (cont.)

<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>、ここは<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>**だった**と<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I heard this used to be the sea.*

The sentence is polite - but the politeness is carried by <code><ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました</code> at the end, and the embedded copula stays plain.

---

## だった・でした · い-adjective past vs でした

> **Rule of thumb** - い-adjectives have their own past, `～かった`. They never take `でした`.

This is the single most common past-tense error at N5, because English puts "was" in front of both kinds of word.

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>**かったです**。<br>*It was cold yesterday.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>暇<rt>ひま</rt></ruby>**でした**。<br>*I was free yesterday.*

---

## だった・でした · い-adjective past vs でした (cont.)

<code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い</code> is an い-adjective, so the past is built inside the word and `です` is added only for politeness. <code><ruby>暇<rt>ひま</rt></ruby></code> is a な-adjective, so it has no past of its own and hands the job to the copula. ❌<code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いでした</code> and ❌<code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>かったでした</code> are both wrong; ❌<code><ruby>暇<rt>ひま</rt></ruby>かった</code> is wrong for the same reason in reverse.

`いい` is irregular here too: the past is `よかった`, never ❌`いかった`.

---

## だった・でした · でした vs ました

> **Rule of thumb** - `でした` is the past of a **noun / な-adjective** sentence; `ました` is the past of a **verb** sentence.

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>に**いました**。<br>*Last summer I was in Hokkaido.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>に**いた**。<br>*Last summer I was in Hokkaido.*

---

## だった・でした · でした vs ました (cont.)

Note that English "was" here is not the copula at all - it is existence, which Japanese handles with the verbs `いる` and `ある`, and therefore with `ました` / `た`. Saying ❌<code><ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>にでした</code> is not possible.

The negative past follows the same division: `～ませんでした` for verbs, `じゃありませんでした` for nouns.

---

## だった・でした · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>は<ruby>面白<rt>おもしろ</rt></ruby>いでした。 → ✅ <ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>は<ruby>面白<rt>おもしろ</rt></ruby>かったです。<br>*い-adjectives carry their own past in `～かった`. `でした` belongs to nouns and な-adjectives.*

- ❌ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しかったでした。 → ✅ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しかったです。<br>*Once the adjective is already past, only `です` is added - the past is not marked twice.*

- ❌ <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だでした。 → ✅ <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>でした。<br>*`でした` already is the copula. There is no `だ` in front of it.*

- ❌ <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちと<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>るでした。 → ✅ <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちと<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*A verb sentence goes past with `ました`, never with `でした`.*

---

## だった・でした · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だったです。 → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>でした。<br>*<code><ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しかったです</code> works because `です` is attaching to an adjective. After the copula `だった` it is not standard - use `でした` instead.*

---

## だった・でした · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>____。（polite）

{reveal}
<ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>**でした**。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby></code> is a noun, so the past tense has to be carried by the copula, and the polite past copula is `でした`.

## だった・でした · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>は（<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しいでした・<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しかったです）。

<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しかったです**。

<code><ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しい</code> is an い-adjective, so it forms its own past by changing `い` to `かった`; `です` is then added purely for politeness. The first option marks the past in the wrong place.

## だった・でした · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese, plain form: "When I was little, my father was a teacher."

{reveal}
Both halves are past in English, but only the main clause needs marking: the `とき` clause takes its tense from the adjective, and the noun predicate <code><ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby></code> takes the plain past copula.

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>のコンサートはとてもよかったでした。

## だった・でした · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>でした。 (b) <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>だったんです。

{reveal}

# は

**Meaning** · marks the topic - "as for X, …"

`は` does not mean anything by itself. It lifts a noun out of the sentence and hangs it up as the thing being talked *about*; everything after it is the comment. It is written with the kana は but **pronounced わ**.

---

## は · Formation

`は` attaches to a noun or a noun phrase with no change to the noun. What matters is what it does to the particle that was already there.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + は | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby> → <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は |
| A subject marked が | が is **replaced** | <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き → <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き |
| An object marked を | を is **replaced** | ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → ごはんは<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる |
| に・で・と・から・まで | は is **added after** them | <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>に → <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>には |
| Time / adverb phrase | phrase + は | <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は |
| Negative sentences | は is the default marker | <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>はありません |
| Pronunciation | written は, said わ | - |

---

## は · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - that third and fourth row are the whole mechanical rule, and it is worth memorising as a pair. `が` and `を` **disappear** under `は`; every other particle **survives** and `は` stacks on top. There is no ❌`がは` or ❌`をは`, but `には`, `では`, `とは`, `からは` are all normal.

---

## は · Examples

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いですね。<br>*It's cold today, isn't it.*

この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>面白<rt>おもしろ</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*This book is interesting.*

<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですが、<ruby>肉<rt>にく</rt></ruby>**は**あまり<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きじゃありません。<br>*I like vegetables, but I'm not so keen on meat.*

<ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>に**は**<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>がありません。<br>*There are no meetings on Saturdays.*

---

## は · Examples (cont.)

きのう**は**ありがとうございました。<br>*Thank you for yesterday.*

あの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>のラーメン**は**<ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>においしいですよ。<br>*The ramen at that place is genuinely good.*

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>くて、<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>で**は**<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>も<ruby>気温<rt>きおん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がりません。<br>*Summer in Japan is hot, and in Tokyo the temperature doesn't even drop at night.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますが、<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*I'm going to Osaka next week, but my younger sister is staying home.*

---

## は · Nuance & register

`は` is register-neutral. It is the same in plain speech, polite speech, and writing, and choosing it tells the listener nothing about how formal you are being.

Two habits will make you sound much more natural. The first is **leaving it out**. Once a topic is established, Japanese simply keeps talking about it - you do not restate it. A conversation that begins 「<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>ですか」 continues 「はい、<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>です」, with no topic at all. Beginners who start every sentence with <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は</code> sound like a textbook, and after a while faintly self-absorbed; native speakers say it once, if at all, and then drop it.

---

## は · Nuance & register (cont.)

The second is that in casual speech `は` is often just not pronounced - 「これ、おいしい」 - or replaced by `って`, which is the conversational topic marker: 「<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん**って**、<ruby>面白<rt>おもしろ</rt></ruby>いね」 = "Tanaka's funny, isn't he." `って` is friendly and slightly gossipy, and it is out of place in a report.

Finally, `は` always carries a faint edge of **contrast**, even when you do not intend one. Saying 「<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいです」 quietly implies "today in particular - other days, not so much." That side effect is very useful for softening refusals (「<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>はちょっと…」), and it is also why piling `は` onto something can sound pointed: 「あなたはいいですね」 can land as "well, it's all right for *you*."

---

## は · は vs が

> **Rule of thumb** - the new information goes **after** `は`, but **before** `が`.

This is the single hardest thing at N5, and the confusion starts from a bad label: `は` is not a subject marker. `が` marks the **subject** - the thing that does the verb. `は` marks the **topic** - the thing under discussion. They often land in the same slot in the sentence, which makes them look like alternatives, but they are answering different needs.

Watch where the news sits:

<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>にいます。<br>*The cat is outside.*

We both know which cat; the news is *where it is*, and it comes after `は`. This answers "where's the cat?"

---

## は · は vs が (cont.)

<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>**が**<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>にいます。<br>*There's a cat outside.*

Here the news is *the cat itself* - presented fresh, before `が`. This answers "what's out there?", or it is simply you noticing.

Two consequences follow directly. First, a question word is by definition the unknown, so it must sit on the `が` side and can never take `は`: 「どれ**が**<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんのかばんですか」. Second, a brand-new thing is introduced with `が` and then talked about with `は` - which is why stories start one way and continue the other.

---

## は · は vs が (cont.)

`は` also reaches further than `が`. A topic scopes over everything to the end of the sentence, while `が` only marks the subject of its own clause. That is why `は` cannot appear inside a clause that is merely modifying a noun:

これは<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>**が**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ったケーキです。<br>*This is a cake my older sister made.*

<code><ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>が<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>った</code> is decorating `ケーキ`; the outer topic is `これ`. Putting `は` on <code><ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby></code> would try to make her the topic of the whole sentence, and it breaks.

And both can live in one sentence, doing their separate jobs:

---

## は · は vs が (cont.)

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>**が**<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>です。<br>*Japan has convenient trains. (As for Japan - the trains are convenient.)*

If you take one thing from this section: ask yourself which word in your sentence is the *news*. If the news is the noun in front of the particle, use `が`. If the news is what comes after it, use `は`.

---

## は · は vs も

> **Rule of thumb** - `は` says "as for this one"; `も` says "this one as well". Both replace `が` and `を`, and only one can be there at a time.

They occupy the same slot, so choosing between them is choosing what you imply about everything else.

<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>大学生<rt>だいがくせい</rt></ruby>です。<br>*My older brother is a university student.*

<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>**も**<ruby>大学生<rt>だいがくせい</rt></ruby>です。<br>*My older brother is a university student too.*

---

## は · は vs も (cont.)

The `も` version only makes sense if somebody else has already been named as a student. The `は` version, by contrast, faintly suggests a comparison going the other way - the brother, as opposed to whoever else we were discussing.

In negative sentences the pair splits cleanly: `は` gives "not this one (whatever about the others)", `も` gives "not this one either".

コーヒー**は**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みません。<br>*I don't drink coffee. (tea might be another matter)*

コーヒー**も**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みません。<br>*I don't drink coffee either.*

---

## は · は with other particles: には・では・とは

> **Rule of thumb** - `は` swallows `が` and `を`, but it rides on the back of every other particle.

The particle that shows the *role* of the noun cannot be thrown away, or the sentence stops making sense. So it stays, and `は` is added behind it.

<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>に**は**<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I've been to Kyoto once. (Kyoto, at least - other places, who knows)*

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>で**は**カードが<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>えません。<br>*You can't use a card at this shop.*

---

## は · は with other particles: には・では・とは (cont.)

Dropping the inner particle changes or destroys the meaning: ❌<code><ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました</code> loses the "to", and ❌<code>この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は…</code> would make the shop itself the topic rather than the place of the action. The order is always **particle first, `は` second** - never ❌`はに`, ❌`はで`.

---

## は · Common mistakes

- ❌ だれ**は**この<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けましたか。 → ✅ だれ**が**この<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けましたか。<br>*A question word is the unknown, and an unknown cannot be a topic. Question words take `が`.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**がは**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*`は` replaces `が`; it never stacks with it. The same goes for `を`.*

- ❌ <ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます。 → ✅ <ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます。（or <ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>**には**…）<br>*<code><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>む</code> needs `に` to show "in". `は` may be added on top of `に`, but it cannot take its place.*

- ❌ これ**は**<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>った<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ これ**は**<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>**が**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>った<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>です。<br>*Inside a clause that modifies a noun, the subject takes `が`. A topic cannot be trapped in there.*

---

## は · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ Pronouncing は as *ha* when it is the particle. It is always **wa**. (The same<br>*kana is *ha* inside ordinary words, as in はな.)*

# も

**Meaning** · also; too; as well

`も` adds its noun to something already on the table. Whatever was just said about A is now said about B as well. In negative sentences the same particle gives you "not … either", and with a number it turns into an expression of surprise: "as many as".

---

## も · Formation

`も` sits exactly where `は` would sit, and follows the same replacement rule.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + も | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby> → <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>も |
| A subject marked が | が is **replaced** | <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>る |
| An object marked を | を is **replaced** | ケーキを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → ケーキも<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる |
| A topic marked は | は is **replaced** | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は → <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>も |
| に・で・と・から・まで | も is **added after** them | <ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>に → <ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>にも |
| A も B も | both A and B (or, with a negative, neither) | <ruby>英語<rt>えいご</rt></ruby>も<ruby>中国語<rt>ちゅうごくご</rt></ruby>も |
| Question word + も | + negative = not any | <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も／だれも／どこも |
| Number + counter + も | as many as … | <ruby>三杯<rt>さんばい</rt></ruby>も |

---

## も · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the replacement rule matters more here than anywhere else, because English lets you tack "too" onto the end of a finished sentence. Japanese does not: `も` goes on the noun that is being added, and it pushes `が` / `を` / `は` out of the way.

---

## も · Examples

わたし**も**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*I'll go too.*

これ**も**ください。<br>*I'll take this one as well, please.*

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>は<ruby>英語<rt>えいご</rt></ruby>**も**<ruby>中国語<rt>ちゅうごくご</rt></ruby>**も**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>します。<br>*My older sister speaks both English and Chinese.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>**も**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いませんでした。<br>*I didn't buy anything yesterday.*

---

## も · Examples (cont.)

「わたし、<ruby>甘<rt>あま</rt></ruby>いものが<ruby>苦手<rt>にがて</rt></ruby>なんだ。」「え、わたし**も**。」<br>*"I'm not big on sweet things." "Oh - me neither."*

きのうは<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>を<ruby>二<rt>に</rt></ruby>リットル**も**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだ。<br>*I drank a whole two litres of water yesterday.*

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん**も**<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>っていて、<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>でびっくりしました。<br>*Tanaka was on the same train as well, and I got a surprise at the station.*

あの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くて、<ruby>店員<rt>てんいん</rt></ruby>さん**も**<ruby>親切<rt>しんせつ</rt></ruby>なので、よく<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*That shop is cheap and the staff are friendly too, so I go there often.*

---

## も · Nuance & register

`も` is register-neutral: identical in plain and polite speech, in writing, and in anyone's mouth. Nothing about it is casual or formal.

What it *is* sensitive to is context. `も` only works if the listener can supply the "as well as what". 「わたしも」 as a one-word reply - "me too" - is perfectly natural precisely because the other half was just said out loud. Dropped into a conversation with nothing to attach to, `も` sounds like the beginning of a sentence you forgot to finish.

Beyond plain addition, two extensions are worth having now.

With a **number**, `も` stops meaning "also" and starts meaning "as many as" - it marks the amount as more than expected: 「<ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>った」 = "I waited a full three hours." With a *small* number plus a negative it flips to "not even": 「<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがない」 = "I've never been, not even once."

---

## も · Nuance & register (cont.)

With a **question word**, `も` plus a negative sweeps the whole category away: だれも<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない = "nobody came", <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>もない = "there's nothing", どこにも<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない = "I'm not going anywhere". This construction is fixed - the negative is not optional, and this is where beginners most often build a sentence that means the opposite of what they wanted.

`も` also has a softening, generous feel when offering things: 「コーヒーもいかがですか」 sounds like an addition rather than a demand, and shopkeepers use it constantly.

---

## も · も vs は

> **Rule of thumb** - `は` sets its noun **apart** from the others; `も` puts it **alongside** them.

They fight for the same slot in the sentence, so the choice is always meaningful.

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>**は**ピアノを<ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*My little brother takes piano lessons.*

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>**も**ピアノを<ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*My little brother takes piano lessons too.*

The second sentence only stands up if somebody else was already named as a piano student. The first quietly does the reverse: it hints that the brother is the exception among the people under discussion.

---

## も · も vs は (cont.)

This distinction is sharpest in refusals and admissions. 「<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は <ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>です」 leaves other days open; 「<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>も<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>です」 admits today is one more in a run of no's.

---

## も · も vs と

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` glues nouns into one phrase ("A and B"); `も` marks each noun separately as an addition ("A as well, B as well").

English "and" covers both, which is why this goes wrong so often.

パン**と**たまごを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*I bought bread and eggs.*

パン**も**たまご**も**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*I bought bread *and* eggs - both of them.*

---

## も · も vs と (cont.)

The `と` version is a neutral shopping list. The `も…も` version stresses the completeness of it, and it is the only one that can turn into "neither" simply by negating the verb: 「パンもたまごも<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いませんでした」 = "I bought neither."

Note also that `と` joins **nouns only**. To link two whole sentences, `と` is not available at all.

---

## も · だれも / 何も vs だれか / 何か

> **Rule of thumb** - question word + `も` needs a **negative** and means "none"; question word + `か` is **positive** and means "some".

The two look like near-twins and mean opposite things.

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>にだれ**も**いません。<br>*There's nobody in the room.*

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>にだれ**か**いますか。<br>*Is somebody in the room?*

---

## も · だれも / 何も vs だれか / 何か (cont.)

Mixing them up produces sentences that are not just unnatural but ungrammatical: ❌<code>だれも<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました</code> cannot mean "somebody came" - `だれも` demands a negative verb, so the sentence simply breaks. Use <code>だれか<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました</code> for "somebody came."

---

## も · Common mistakes

- ❌ わたし**はも**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。 → ✅ わたし**も**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*`も` replaces `は`, `が` and `を`. It never stacks with them.*

- ❌ ケーキ**をも**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました。 → ✅ ケーキ**も**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました。<br>*Same rule for the object marker: `を` disappears under `も`.*

- ❌ <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だち**もと**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。 → ✅ <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だち**とも**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*With the particles that survive (`に`, `で`, `と`, `から`), the order is always particle first, `も` second.*

- ❌ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>**も**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました。 → ✅ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>**か**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました。<br>*<code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も</code> can only be followed by a negative. For "I ate something", use <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か</code>.*

---

Yamada has already been established as coming, so Tanaka is being added to that group. `も` replaces the `が` that would otherwise mark the subject.

The verb is negative, and question word + `も` + negative is the fixed way to say "nothing at all". <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か</code> would need a positive verb: <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>かありました</code>.

## も · Practice 3

The `も` does the work of "either" - Japanese uses the same particle for "too" and "either", and the negative verb supplies the difference. Note that `も` replaces the `は` that marked the first speaker's topic.

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>、<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>もへ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。

# か - or

**Meaning** · joins alternatives - "A or B"

Placed **between** two nouns, `か` offers a choice: either one will do. This is the everyday Japanese "or", and it is the same syllable as the question particle without being the same word - position tells them apart.

---

## か - or · Formation

`か` goes between the items, not after the last one. Any case particle the phrase needs (`を`, `に`, `で`…) is attached to the **whole group**, once, at the end.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Two nouns | A か B | コーヒー か お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby> |
| Three or more | A か B か C | <ruby>月<rt>げつ</rt></ruby> か <ruby>火<rt>か</rt></ruby> か <ruby>水<rt>すい</rt></ruby> |
| With a case particle | (A か B) + を・に・で | コーヒーかお<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む |
| Both items marked | A か B か（、）… | バスか<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>か、どちらか |
| Numbers / dates | approximation: "…or…" | <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>か<ruby>四日<rt>よっか</rt></ruby> |
| Two clauses | plain form + か、plain form | <ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>るか、<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む |

---

## か - or · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the case particle rule is the mechanical trap. ❌<code>コーヒーをかお<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>を</code> is wrong; the `を` is not distributed over the alternatives. Build the choice first, then mark it: <code>コーヒーかお<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます</code>.

---

## か - or · Examples

コーヒー**か**お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*I'll have coffee or tea.*

ペン**か**えんぴつ、ありますか。<br>*Do you have a pen or a pencil?*

<ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>**か**<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いましょう。<br>*Let's meet on Saturday or Sunday.*

バス**か**<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>**か**、どちらで<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますか。<br>*Bus or train - which will you take?*

---

## か - or · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>**か**<ruby>四日<rt>よっか</rt></ruby>で<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わると<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。<br>*I think it'll be finished in three or four days.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>なら、<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る**か**、<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*If it rains tomorrow, I'll watch a film or read at home.*

<ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>みは<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>**か**<ruby>沖縄<rt>おきなわ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きたいと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*For the summer holiday I'm thinking of going to Hokkaido or Okinawa.*

すみません、<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一階<rt>いっかい</rt></ruby>**か**<ruby>二階<rt>にかい</rt></ruby>か、どちらでしょうか。<br>*Excuse me - is reception on the first floor or the second?*

---

## か - or · Nuance & register

`か` is register-neutral. It is the same word in a text message and in a contract, and nobody will hear it as either casual or stiff.

Marking **both** items - 「AかBか」 - changes the feel slightly. It slows the sentence down and presents the choice as a genuine, deliberate fork: 「<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くか、 <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないか」 = "whether to go or not". A single `か` between two nouns is the lighter, more offhand version, and it is what you will say most of the time.

---

## か - or · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two related shapes to recognise. In relaxed speech, `とか` does the same job with a looser, "…or something like that" flavour: 「お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>とかコーヒーとか」. And in written or formal Japanese, `または` replaces `か` in instructions and forms: 「ペンまたはえんぴつでご<ruby>記入<rt>きにゅう</rt></ruby>ください」.

Note one limit. `か` does not translate every English "or". English uses "or" to threaten ("hurry, **or** we'll be late") and to correct ("Tuesday - **or** rather, Wednesday"); Japanese uses other constructions for both, and reaching for `か` there will not work.

Finally, do not read the choice as exclusive by default. 「コーヒーかお<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>」 offers two options; it does not insist you may not have both. If exclusivity matters, say so - 「どちらか<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つ」.

---

## か - or · か vs と

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` means **and** (both), `か` means **or** (either). They sit in exactly the same place, so a single syllable flips the meaning.

パン**と**<ruby>牛乳<rt>ぎゅうにゅう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います。<br>*I'll buy bread and milk.*

パン**か**<ruby>牛乳<rt>ぎゅうにゅう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います。<br>*I'll buy bread or milk.*

The first commits you to two items; the second to one, not yet decided. Both take a single `を` at the end, because in both cases the two nouns form one phrase together.

---

## か - or · か vs それとも

> **Rule of thumb** - `か` joins **nouns inside one sentence**; `それとも` joins **two complete questions**.

`それとも` is a conjunction, not a particle. It starts a new clause and cannot sit between bare nouns.

お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>**か**コーヒーはいかがですか。<br>*Would you like tea or coffee?*

お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>にしますか。**それとも**コーヒーにしますか。<br>*Will you have tea? Or would you rather have coffee?*

---

## か - or · か vs それとも (cont.)

The `それとも` version is more deliberate - it lays out two full alternatives and asks the listener to pick between them, which makes it useful when you want to be clearly polite about offering a real choice. ❌<code>お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>それともコーヒー</code> is not possible.

---

## か - or · か (or) vs か (question particle)

> **Rule of thumb** - `か` **between** nouns means "or"; `か` at the **end** of a sentence makes a question. Position decides, every time.

Learners see the same kana and assume ambiguity. There is almost none, because the two uses never occupy the same slot.

<ruby>紅茶<rt>こうちゃ</rt></ruby>**か**コーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*I'll have black tea or coffee.*

<ruby>紅茶<rt>こうちゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます**か**。<br>*Will you have some black tea?*

---

## か - or · か (or) vs か (question particle) (cont.)

And both can appear in one sentence without any clash, each doing its own job: 「<ruby>紅茶<rt>こうちゃ</rt></ruby>**か**コーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます**か**。」 - "Will you have tea or coffee?" The first `か` offers the choice; the last one turns the whole thing into a question.

---

## か - or · Common mistakes

- ❌ コーヒーをかお<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。 → ✅ コーヒーかお<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*The case particle marks the whole choice once, at the end. It is not repeated on each alternative.*

- ❌ パンかたまごを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。（when you bought both） → ✅ パンとたまごを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*`か` is "or". If you took both items home, the word you want is `と`.*

- ❌ お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>それともコーヒーがいいです。 → ✅ お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>かコーヒーがいいです。<br>*`それとも` links two whole questions, not two nouns.*

---

## か - or · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>か<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>か<ruby>明後日<rt>あさって</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*`か` needs something on **both** sides. A single item with a trailing `か` at the end of a noun phrase is not an alternative - it will be read as a question particle or as "something like".*

---

## か - or · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんはうどん____そばにします。

{reveal}
うどん**か**そばにします。

{reveal}
Two nouns offered as alternatives take `か` between them, and the following particle - here `に` - attaches once, after the whole pair.

---

## か - or · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>月曜日<rt>げつようび</rt></ruby>（と・か）<ruby>火曜日<rt>かようび</rt></ruby>です。まだ<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>まっていません。

{reveal}
<ruby>月曜日<rt>げつようび</rt></ruby>**か**<ruby>火曜日<rt>かようび</rt></ruby>です。

{reveal}
The second sentence says it hasn't been decided, so only one of the two days will happen. That is "or", which is `か`. `と` would claim the meeting runs on both days.

---

## か - or · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Please write your name in pen or pencil."

{reveal}
ペン**か**えんぴつでお<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください。

{reveal}
The choice is built first, then marked once with `で` for the instrument. In a printed form you would meet the written equivalent, `または`, in the same slot.

---

## か - or · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>はビールをかワインをお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>はビールかワインをお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします。

{reveal}
`を` cannot be attached to each alternative. Join the nouns with `か` first, and let the single `を` cover the resulting phrase.

---

## か - or · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) バスか<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。 (b) バスか<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>か、どちらかで<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。

{reveal}
Both say you will travel by one of the two. (a) is light and offhand - you have not thought about it much, and either is fine.

{reveal}
(b) marks both options and then names the choice explicitly with `どちらか`, which slows the sentence down and presents the decision as a real, still-open fork. It is what you would say when the choice actually matters, or when you are being careful in front of someone.

---

# か - question particle

**Meaning** · turns a statement into a question

Put `か` at the end of a sentence and it becomes a question. Nothing else moves - no word order changes, no auxiliary appears. This is the same syllable as the "or" particle, but at the end of the sentence rather than between two nouns.

---

## か - question particle · Formation

`か` is the last thing in the sentence, after the predicate is already complete.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ます-form verb | ～ます + か | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますか |
| Noun + です | Noun + ですか | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>です → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>ですか |
| い-adjective + です | ～いです + か | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです → <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いですか |
| Past / negative | ～ましたか・～ませんか | <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました → <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ましたか |
| Plain form | plain + か (blunt or written) | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くか |
| Question word sentences | question word … + か | どこ → どこですか |
| Inside a sentence | clause + か + わかりません etc. | <ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るか、わかりません |
| Punctuation | normally 。 - the か already asks | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますか。 |

---

## か - question particle · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - after a noun in the **plain** form, the `だ` drops before `か`: <code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>か</code>, never ❌<code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だか</code>. In the polite form there is no such problem, because `です` stays: <code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>ですか</code>.

---

## か - question particle · Examples

お<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>です**か**。<br>*What's your name?*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます**か**。<br>*Are you coming tomorrow?*

すみません、コピー<ruby>機<rt>き</rt></ruby>はどこです**か**。<br>*Excuse me, where's the photocopier?*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ました**か**。<br>*Did you watch yesterday's match?*

---

## か - question particle · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>にお<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べません**か**。<br>*Would you like to have lunch with me?*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる**か**、まだわかりません。<br>*I still don't know what time the meeting ends.*

すみません、この<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>ってもいいです**か**。<br>*Excuse me, may I sit here?*

<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っています**か**。<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>から<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るそうですよ。<br>*Do you have an umbrella with you? Apparently it's going to rain from the afternoon.*

---

## か - question particle · Nuance & register

`か` belongs with polite Japanese. Attached to `です` / `ます` it is simply the normal way to ask anything, and it carries no attitude at all.

Attached to a **plain** form it is a different animal. 「<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くか」 sounds blunt, brusque, and distinctly masculine - a boss barking at a subordinate, or a character in a film. Ordinary casual questions do not use `か` at all: you take the plain form and raise your pitch at the end.

- 「<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く?」 - "You going?"
- 「これ、<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる?」 - "You want this?"

---

## か - question particle · Nuance & register (cont.)

That is the single most useful register fact on this page. A learner who says 「<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べるか?」 to a friend sounds aggressive; 「<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる?」 is warm and normal. In writing, plain form + `か` is fine and neutral - it is the everyday shape of a question in a book or a report, where nobody is being addressed.

---

## か - question particle · Nuance & register (cont.)

Plain `か` also survives in a set of self-directed expressions that everybody uses regardless of gender: 「そうか」("ah, I see"), 「<ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>か」, and the polite 「そうですか」. Watch the intonation on that last one. Rising 「そうですか?」 is a real question - "is that so?". Falling 「そうですか。」 is not a question at all; it is "I see", an acknowledgement. Beginners produce the falling version by accident and puzzle their listener.

---

## か - question particle · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two more things worth knowing. `か` is normally followed by `。`, not `?`, in careful writing - the particle already marks the question. And a **negative** question, `～ませんか`, is not really a question about ability or fact: it is an invitation. 「<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ませんか」 = "won't you watch it with me?", not "do you not watch it?".

---

## か - question particle · か vs rising intonation

> **Rule of thumb** - polite speech asks with `か`. Casual speech asks by raising the pitch and adding nothing.

コーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます**か**。<br>*Would you like some coffee?*

コーヒー、<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む**?**<br>*Want some coffee?*

---

## か - question particle · か vs rising intonation (cont.)

Both are questions and neither is wrong, but they are for different people. The first is what you say to a client; the second to a friend. Fusing them - ❌「コーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>むか?」 - produces the rough, confrontational register mentioned above, which is very rarely what a learner intends.

---

## か - question particle · ～ますか vs ～ませんか

> **Rule of thumb** - `～ますか` asks for information. `～ませんか` offers an invitation.

<ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ます**か**。<br>*Do you watch films at the weekend?*

<ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ません**か**。<br>*How about seeing a film at the weekend?*

---

## か - question particle · ～ますか vs ～ませんか (cont.)

The negative shape is doing politeness work, not negation: by phrasing the offer so that "no" is the easy answer, you leave the listener room to refuse. English does the same thing with "you wouldn't want to…, would you?" A step further along is `～ましょうか`, which offers to do something *for* the listener: 「<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>いましょうか」 = "shall I give you a hand?"

---

## か - question particle · か vs の

> **Rule of thumb** - `か` is the neutral polite question. Sentence-final `の` is the casual question that asks for an *explanation*.

どうして<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ませんでした**か**。<br>*Why didn't you come?*

どうして<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかった**の**?<br>*How come you didn't come?*

---

## か - question particle · か vs の (cont.)

`の` builds on the explanatory `のだ` / `んです`, so it asks about the reason or the situation behind something rather than the bare fact. It is soft and personal - used a great deal with children and between friends, and slightly more by women than by men in its bare form. Its polite counterpart is `んですか`: 「どうして<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかったんですか」, which is gentler than a flat `ませんでしたか` and is often the better choice when a direct `か` question would feel like an interrogation.

---

## か - question particle · Common mistakes

- ❌ あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だか。 → ✅ あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>ですか。／<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>か。<br>*In the polite form the copula is `です`; in the plain form the `だ` drops out entirely before `か`.*

- ❌ 「<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>ですか。」「はい、そうですか。」 → ✅ 「はい、そうです。」<br>*Falling 「そうですか」 means "I see" - it is what the *listener* says. Answering with it tells your questioner you have just learned your own occupation.*

- ❌ どこか<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますか。（meaning "where are you going?"） → ✅ どこへ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますか。<br>*`どこか` is one word meaning "somewhere". The question word needs its ordinary particle instead.*

---

## か - question particle · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちに「<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べるか?」と<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました。 → ✅ 「<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる?」と<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*Plain form + `か` addressed at a person is rough. Casual questions drop the particle and rely on intonation.*

---

## か - question particle · Practice 1

**1.** すみません、<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>はどちら____。

{reveal}
<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>はどちらです**か**。

{reveal}
The sentence ends in a noun predicate, so the polite copula `です` comes first and `か` follows it to make the question. Asking a stranger, the polite form is the only sensible choice.

## か - question particle · Practice 2

**2.** Choose the more inviting version: <ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に（<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りますか・<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りませんか）。

{reveal}
<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りませんか**。

{reveal}
The negative question is Japanese's standard invitation. It leaves refusing easy, so it sounds considerate rather than pushy. <code><ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りますか</code> would just be asking whether the person intends to go home.

{reveal}

## か - question particle · Practice 4

✅ <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちに「<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る?」と<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました。

## か - question particle · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) どうして<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みましたか。 (b) どうして<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだんですか。

{reveal}
Both are polite and both ask why the person missed work. (a) is a bare request for a fact, and coming from a superior it can feel like being called in for questioning.

{reveal}
(b) uses the explanatory `んです`, which frames the question as "what's the story behind it?" - it acknowledges there is a situation and invites the person to explain it. That makes it warmer and more usual in real conversation, especially when the answer might be personal.
