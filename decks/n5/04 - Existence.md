# N5 - Existence

- ～がある
- ～がいる

---

# ～がある

**Meaning** · there is / there are - something inanimate exists, or is available

`ある` is the existence verb for things that do not move under their own will: objects, places, plants, and abstractions like time, money and plans. The same verb covers "I have" (<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がある) and "there's a scheduled event" (<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>がある) - in Japanese those are all just *something existing*.

---

## ～がある · Formation

The thing that exists takes `が`. The place it exists in takes `に`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + が + ある | <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>がある |
| Noun + place | Place + に + Noun + が + ある | <ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>がある |
| Polite | ある → あります | <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>があります |
| Negative | ある → **ない** | <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>がない / <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>がありません |
| Past | ある → あった | <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>があった / <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>がありました |
| Negative past | ある → なかった | <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>がなかった / <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>がありませんでした |
| て-form | ある → あって | <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>があって… |

---

## ～がある · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `ある` has one irregularity, and it is the negative. The plain negative is `ない`, not ❌`あらない`. Everything else conjugates like a normal う-verb.

---

## ～がある · Examples

<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>**があります**。<br>*There's a book on the desk.*

<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>**がありません**。<br>*I don't have time.*

この<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>くにコンビニ**がある**?<br>*Is there a convenience store around here?*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>、<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>**があります**。<br>*There's a meeting tomorrow afternoon.*

---

## ～がある · Examples (cont.)

かばんの<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>**があります**か。<br>*What's in the bag?*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>まで、ここに<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>**がありました**。<br>*Until last year there was a big tree here.*

すみません、ちょっとお<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>**があるん**ですが、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>よろしいですか。<br>*Sorry - there's something I'd like to talk to you about. Do you have a moment?*

この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>にはスーパー**がない**ので、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>のときは<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*There's no supermarket around here, so when I shop I walk all the way to the station.*

---

## ～がある · Nuance & register

`ある` is register-neutral. `あります` is the ordinary polite form; `ある` is plain. In formal service or business Japanese it climbs to `ございます`: <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>はございますか - "Are there any questions?" You will hear that from shop staff and in speeches, and you are not expected to produce it at N5.

Two things learners get blindsided by:

**`が` drops in casual speech.** <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>ある?／お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>ない／コンビニあるかな。 This is extremely common - much more common than the full form in conversation. Keep `が` in writing and in ます-speech.

---

## ～がある · Nuance & register (cont.)

**`ある` covers far more than physical objects.** Events (<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>がある), appointments (<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>がある), experiences (<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがある), abilities and resources (<ruby>自信<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>がある). If you find yourself reaching for "to have" in English, `ある` is usually the right verb.

One historical note: <code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもがある</code> for "to have children" does exist, and you will meet it in older or formal writing, where it frames children as a fact about someone's life rather than as people in the room. In modern speech use <code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもがいる</code>.

---

## ～がある · ～がある vs ～がいる

> **Rule of thumb** - `いる` is for things that move under their own will; `ある` is for everything else. The line is *animacy*, not *life*.

People and animals take `いる`. Plants - alive, but rooted - take `ある`. So do cars, buildings, books, and ideas.

<ruby>庭<rt>にわ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>**があります**。<br>*There's a big tree in the garden.*

<ruby>庭<rt>にわ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>が**います**。<br>*There's a cat in the garden.*

---

## ～がある · ～がある vs ～がいる (cont.)

The animacy test is about how the speaker frames the thing, which is why the same noun can go either way. A fish swimming in a tank is <ruby>魚<rt>さかな</rt></ruby>が**いる**; a fish laid out at the shop is <ruby>魚<rt>さかな</rt></ruby>が**ある**. Ghosts, being willful, take `いる`.

> **Note** - vehicles are the classic grey area. `タクシーがある` means taxis are *available* here; `タクシーがいる` pictures one sitting there with a driver in it. Both are heard. When in doubt with a vehicle, `ある` is safe.

---

## ～がある · ～がある vs ～を持っている

> **Rule of thumb** - `ある` says a thing exists and is at your disposal. <code><ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っている</code> says you own it or are physically carrying it.

Both translate as "have", and English gives you no help choosing.

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>**がありません**。<br>*I don't have any money right now.*

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていません。<br>*I'm not carrying any money on me right now.*

---

## ～がある · ～がある vs ～を持っている (cont.)

The useful hard rule: **<code><ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っている</code> needs something you can hold or own.** Abstract nouns cannot take it, so time, plans, meetings and experience are always `ある`.

- ✅ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>があります　❌ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っています
- ✅ <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>があります　❌ <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っています
- ✅ <ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>があります／<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っています（both fine - "there's a car I can use" vs "I own a car"）

---

## ～がある · ～に～がある vs ～は～にある

> **Rule of thumb** - put the **new** information late. If the *thing* is new, lead with the place. If the *place* is new, lead with the thing and mark it `は`.

Both sentences below are correct, and they answer different questions.

<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>**があります**。<br>*There's a book on the desk.*

That answers "what's on the desk?" - or describes a scene from scratch. The book is the news, so it stays on the `が` side.

<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>にあります。<br>*The book is on the desk.*

---

## ～がある · ～に～がある vs ～は～にある (cont.)

That answers "where's the book?" We both already know which book; the news is the location, and news comes after `は`.

---

## ～がある · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**あらない**。 → ✅ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**ない**。<br>*`ある` is the one verb whose plain negative is suppletive. `ない` - and past `なかった`, polite `ありません`.*

- ❌ <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>が**あります**。 → ✅ <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>が**います**。<br>*Animals move under their own will, so they take `いる`.*

- ❌ ペン**を**あります。 → ✅ ペン**が**あります。<br>*`ある` is intransitive. Nothing is being acted on, so `を` has no job here.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていません。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がありません。<br>*<code><ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っている</code> needs a concrete possession. Time is not one.*

---

## ～がある · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ ここ**で**<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>があります。 → ✅ ここ**に**<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*`で` marks where an action happens. Existence is not an action - it takes `に`.*

---

## ～がある · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぎましょう。もう<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が____。

{reveal}
もう<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**ありません**（casual: **ない**）。 "Let's hurry - we're out of time."

{reveal}
The negative of `ある` is suppletive: `ない` in plain form, `ありません` in polite form. ❌`あらない` and ❌`あらありません` do not exist.

---

## ～がある · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>い<ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>が（あります・います）。

{reveal}
<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>い<ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>が**あります**。

{reveal}
Trees are alive, but the `ある`／`いる` split is not about life - it's about moving under your own will. Plants stay put, so they pattern with objects and take `ある`.

---

## ～がある · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Is there a post office near the station?"

{reveal}
<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>くに<ruby>郵便局<rt>ゆうびんきょく</rt></ruby>が**ありますか**。

{reveal}
The location takes `に` (not `で` - nothing is happening there), the thing that exists takes `が`, and a post office is inanimate, so `ある`. In casual speech you would drop the particle: <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>く、<ruby>郵便局<rt>ゆうびんきょく</rt></ruby>ある?

---

## ～がある · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていますから、<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>します。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>が**あります**から、<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>します。 "I have an exam next week, so I'm going to study."

{reveal}
English "have an exam" pulls you toward <code><ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>つ</code>, but <code><ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っている</code> requires something you can physically hold or legally own. Scheduled events exist - they use `ある`.

---

## ～がある · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) つくえの<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>があります。 (b) <ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>はつくえの<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>にあります。

{reveal}
(a) introduces the dictionary as new information - "there's a dictionary on the desk." You would say it describing a room, or answering "what's on the desk?"

{reveal}
(b) treats the dictionary as already under discussion (`は` marks it as the topic) and delivers the *location* as the news - "the dictionary is on the desk." That's the answer to "where's the dictionary?" Same facts, opposite information flow.

---

# ～がいる

**Meaning** · there is / there are - a living thing that moves under its own will exists, or someone has such a thing

`いる` is the existence verb Japanese reserves for beings that can decide to go somewhere: people, animals, insects, fish in the water. It also does the work of English "have" when what you have is a person or an animal - <code><ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>がいる</code> is "I have an older brother", and <code><ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>がいる</code> can be either "there's a cat" or "I have a cat", decided entirely by context.

---

## ～がいる · Formation

The being that exists takes `が`. The place it is in takes `に`. `いる` is a regular る-verb, so every form is predictable - which makes it the easy half of the `ある`／`いる` pair.

---

## ～がいる · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + が + いる | <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>がいる |
| Noun + place | Place + に + Noun + が + いる | <ruby>庭<rt>にわ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>がいる |
| Polite | いる → います | <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>がいます |
| Negative | いる → いない | <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>がいない／いません |
| Past | いる → いた | <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>がいた／いました |
| Negative past | いる → いなかった | いなかった／いませんでした |
| て-form | いる → いて | <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちがいて… |
| Honorific / humble | いる → いらっしゃる／おる | <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>がいらっしゃいます |
| Counting | Place + に + Noun + が + counter + いる | <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>います |

---

## ～がいる · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `いる` conjugates with no surprises at all. Its partner `ある` is the irregular one: the plain negative of `ある` is `ない`, whereas `いる` gives a perfectly ordinary `いない`.

---

## ～がいる · Examples

<ruby>池<rt>いけ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>鳥<rt>とり</rt></ruby>が**います**。<br>*There are birds on the pond.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>には<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>が<ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>**います**。<br>*I have two older brothers.*

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>が**いました**。<br>*There was a big dog in front of the station.*

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>に**いません**。<br>*He isn't in Osaka at the moment.*

---

## ～がいる · Examples (cont.)

すみません、<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは**いらっしゃいますか**。<br>*Excuse me - is Mr Tanaka in?*

さっきまでここに<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもが<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>**いた**のに、<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになった。<br>*There were three children here a moment ago, and then it suddenly went quiet.*

<ruby>海外<rt>かいがい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいる<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちが**いる**ので、<ruby>時々<rt>ときどき</rt></ruby><ruby>英語<rt>えいご</rt></ruby>で<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>します。<br>*I have a friend living abroad, so I sometimes speak English.*

この<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>には<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せる<ruby>社員<rt>しゃいん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>何人<rt>なんにん</rt></ruby>**いますか**。<br>*How many employees who can speak Japanese are there at this company?*

---

## ～がいる · Nuance & register

Plain `いる` and polite `います` are the ordinary pair, and neither carries any special colour. What does need care is the honorific layer and the casual shortening.

**Honorific and humble forms are compulsory at work.** Talking *about* someone you respect, `いる` climbs to `いらっしゃる`: <code><ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>会議室<rt>かいぎしつ</rt></ruby>にいらっしゃいます</code>. Talking about yourself or your own company to an outsider, it drops to `おる`: <code><ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby><ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>におります</code>. Using `います` about a customer is not rude exactly, but it marks you as untrained.

**`が` drops in casual speech**, exactly as it does with `ある`: 「<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>かいる?」「いないよ」. Keep `が` in writing and in ます-speech.

---

## ～がいる · Nuance & register (cont.)

**`いる` is also how you say you have a partner.** <code><ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>がいる</code> and <code><ruby>彼氏<rt>かれし</rt></ruby>がいる</code> are the standard phrasings, and by extension <code><ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>している<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>がいる</code>. There is no verb "to have" involved anywhere.

**Do not confuse the existence verb with the auxiliary.** The `いる` in <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べている</code> is the same word historically, but grammatically it is a suffix on a て-form, not a predicate of its own. That is why <code><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っています</code> means "I am waiting", not "there is a waiting".

**One homophone worth flagging early**: <code><ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>る</code>, "to need", is also pronounced いる, is also used with `が`, and appears at exactly the same stage of study. It is a completely different verb - see the contrast below.

---

## ～がいる · ～がいる vs ～がある

> **Rule of thumb** - `いる` for anything that can move itself; `ある` for everything else. The dividing line is **animacy as the speaker frames it**, not biological life.

People, animals, birds, insects and fish take `いる`. Plants, buildings, tools, books and abstractions take `ある`, even though a plant is unambiguously alive.

<ruby>水槽<rt>すいそう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>金魚<rt>きんぎょ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>五<rt>ご</rt></ruby><ruby>匹<rt>ひき</rt></ruby>**います**。<br>*There are five goldfish in the tank.*

<ruby>台所<rt>だいどころ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*There's a new fridge in the kitchen.*

---

## ～がいる · ～がいる vs ～がある (cont.)

The framing matters more than the noun. A goldfish swimming in the tank is `いる`; the same fish sold frozen in a bag is `ある`. A robot that walks and answers you may well get `いる`, and so does a character in a story. The `ある` page treats this boundary from the other side and gives the vehicle cases; the two accounts agree.

---

## ～がいる · ～がいる vs ～が要る

> **Rule of thumb** - same pronunciation, same particle, unrelated meanings. `いる` = exists. <code><ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>る</code> = is needed. They are told apart by their conjugation: negative `いない` versus <code><ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>らない</code>.

This trips people up because both are N5, both take `が`, and the second is very often written in kana.

<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が**いません**。<br>*There's nobody at the reception desk.*

<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>では<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>りません。<br>*You don't need a reservation at reception.*

---

## ～がいる · ～がいる vs ～が要る (cont.)

The conjugation is decisive. <code><ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>る</code> is a う-verb, so it goes <code><ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>らない</code>, <code><ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>ります</code>, <code><ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>った</code>. The existence verb is a る-verb: `いない`, `います`, `いた`. If you meet `いらない` it is never the existence verb, and if you meet `いない` it never means "unnecessary".

---

## ～がいる · ～にいる vs ～ている

> **Rule of thumb** - `～にいる` states *where someone is*. `～ている` states *what someone is doing*, or the state that resulted from doing it. Only the first is the existence verb.

Because both end in `いる`, learners routinely produce one when they mean the other.

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>に**います**。<br>*My older sister is in Kyoto right now.*

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>は<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます。<br>*My older sister lives in Kyoto.*

---

## ～がいる · ～にいる vs ～ている (cont.)

The first is about location at this moment and answers 「どこにいますか」. The second is about an ongoing situation and answers 「どこに<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいますか」. Saying <code><ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>にいます</code> when you mean you live there sounds as though you are on a trip, and it is one of the most common small errors in self-introductions.

---

## ～がいる · Common mistakes

- ❌ この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>病院<rt>びょういん</rt></ruby>が**います**。 → ✅ この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>病院<rt>びょういん</rt></ruby>が**あります**。<br>*A hospital is a building. Nothing that cannot walk away takes `いる`.*

- ❌ <ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>を**います**。 → ✅ <ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>が**います**。<br>*`いる` is intransitive: nothing is being acted upon, so `を` has no role. English "I have a brother" makes <ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby> look like an object; in Japanese it is the subject.*

- ❌ <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>がいます。 → ✅ <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>がいます。<br>*`で` marks the place an action happens. Existing is not an action.*

## ～がいる · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>が**いらない**。（meaning "there is no dog"）→ ✅ <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>が**いない**。<br>*`いらない` is the negative of <code><ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>る</code>, "to need". As written, the sentence says you do not want a dog.*

- ❌ お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>さまは<ruby>会議室<rt>かいぎしつ</rt></ruby>に**います**。 → ✅ お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>さまは<ruby>会議室<rt>かいぎしつ</rt></ruby>に**いらっしゃいます**。<br>*Grammatical, but in front of a customer or a superior the plain `います` is flat-footed. Use `いらっしゃる` for them and `おる` for yourself.*

## ～がいる · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>が____。

{reveal}
<ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>が**います**。（casual: **いる**）

{reveal}
A cat can climb down whenever it likes, so it is animate and takes `いる`. Note the tree in the same sentence would take `ある` - the choice follows the noun marked by `が`, not the scenery.

---

## ～がいる · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>が（います・あります）。

{reveal}
<ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>が**あります**。

{reveal}
Vegetables are alive in a botanical sense and completely inert in a grammatical one. The test is whether the thing moves of its own accord, and a carrot does not.

## ～がいる · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Is anyone from the sales team still in the office?"

{reveal}
<ruby>営業<rt>えいぎょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>はまだ<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に**いますか**。

{reveal}
People take `いる`, and the location takes `に`. In a call to another company you would raise it to `いらっしゃいますか`, and refer to your own colleagues with `おります`.

---

## ～がいる · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: すみません、この<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>にはんこがいますか。

{reveal}
✅ すみません、この<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>にはんこが**<ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>りますか**。 "Excuse me, does this document need a seal?"

{reveal}
A はんこ is an object, so the existence verb would have to be `ある` - but the intended meaning is "is it needed", which is the separate verb <code><ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>る</code>. Its ます-form is <code><ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>ります</code>, never `います`.

## ～がいる · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>に**います**。 (b) <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。

{reveal}
(a) uses the existence verb and reports where your father physically is - possibly for the next hour, possibly permanently. It answers "where is he?"

{reveal}
(b) uses <code><ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>く</code> plus the `～ている` auxiliary, which happens to contain the same syllables but is a suffix, not a predicate. It describes an ongoing activity rather than a position, and the place therefore takes `で` instead of `に`.
