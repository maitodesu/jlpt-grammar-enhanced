# に

**Meaning** · marks a point - where something ends up, where it sits, when it happens, or who receives it

`に` names a **point**, never a stretch or an area. The place a movement finishes, the spot a thing occupies, the moment on the clock, the person a thing lands with: all of those are single points, and `に` is how Japanese pins them down. Nearly every use below is a version of that one idea.

---

## に · Formation

`に` is a particle. It attaches straight onto a noun, a time word, or a ます-stem, and nothing changes shape.

---

## に · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun - destination | Noun + に + motion verb | <ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く |
| Noun - where something exists | Noun + に + ある／いる | <ruby>台所<rt>だいどころ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>台所<rt>だいどころ</rt></ruby>にある |
| Noun - person who receives | Person + に + giving or telling verb | <ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby> → <ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>に<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す |
| Time word with a number or a name | Time + に | <ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きる |
| Verb ます-stem - purpose | stem + に + <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く／<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る／<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る | <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います → <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く |
| Noun - thing entered, boarded, sat on | Noun + に + <ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>る／<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>る／<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>る | いす → いすに<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>る |
| Period - rate or frequency | Period + に + number | <ruby>週<rt>しゅう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>週<rt>しゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>三回<rt>さんかい</rt></ruby> |
| Noun or な-adjective - result of a change | Noun + に + なる | <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>になる |

---

## に · Formation (cont.)

> **Note - which time words take `に`?** If the time word is a number or a fixed name on the calendar, it takes `に`: <ruby>七時<rt>しちじ</rt></ruby>に, <ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby>に, <ruby>月曜日<rt>げつようび</rt></ruby>に, <ruby>二十日<rt>はつか</rt></ruby>に, <ruby>誕生日<rt>たんじょうび</rt></ruby>に. If it is positioned relative to *now*, or if it is a frequency word, it takes **nothing at all**: <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>, <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>, <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>, <ruby>今朝<rt>けさ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>今晩<rt>こんばん</rt></ruby>, <ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby>, <ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby>, <ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby>, いつ. Adding `に` to those is one of the most persistent N5 errors. <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby> and <ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby> sit in between - both forms are heard, and the bare one is more common in speech.

---

## に · Examples

<ruby>七時<rt>しちじ</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*I get up at seven.*

<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>下<rt>した</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>がいます。<br>*There's a cat under the desk.*

ゆうべ<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*I phoned my mother last night.*

ちょっとパンを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い**に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってきます。<br>*I'm just popping out to buy some bread.*

---

## に · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>水曜日<rt>すいようび</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>で<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*There's a meeting in Osaka next Wednesday.*

<ruby>週<rt>しゅう</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>三回<rt>さんかい</rt></ruby>ジムで<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>いでいます。<br>*I swim at the gym three times a week.*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どものころ、この<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>**に**よく<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>び**に**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*When I was a kid I often came to this park to play.*

すみません、この<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん**に**<ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>してもらえますか。<br>*Sorry - could you pass this document to Tanaka?*

---

## に · Nuance & register

`に` is completely register-neutral. It is the same in plain speech, in ます-form, in business email and in newspapers. Choosing `に` never makes you sound stiff or casual.

What varies is whether you say it. In quick casual speech the **destination** `に` is routinely dropped:

- どこ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くの? - "Where are you off to?"
- ちょっとコンビニ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってくる。 - "Just nipping to the convenience store."

That dropping is limited to destinations. The `に` of **existence**, the `に` of a **recipient**, and the `に` of a **clock time** are not dropped - remove them and the sentence stops parsing rather than sounding casual.

---

## に · Nuance & register (cont.)

`に` is phonologically weightless. It never carries stress, and it attaches so tightly to the preceding noun that Japanese speakers hear <code><ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>に</code> as one word. Do not pause before it.

One more use worth knowing early, because it turns up constantly: `に` marks the **result of a change**, with なる and する. <ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>は<ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>になりました。 <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>をきれい**に**してください。 It is the same "landing point" idea - the thing ends up *at* that state.

---

## に · に vs で

This is the single most common particle error at N5, and it is worth over-learning.

> **Rule of thumb** - `に` marks where something **is**; `で` marks where something **happens**.

If the verb is ある, いる, <ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>む, <ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>く, <ruby>泊<rt>と</rt></ruby>まる - verbs about a thing occupying a spot - use `に`. If the verb is an activity that could happen anywhere, use `で`.

<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>**に**ベンチがあります。<br>*There's a bench in the park.*

<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>**で**お<ruby>弁当<rt>べんとう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました。<br>*We ate our packed lunch in the park.*

---

## に · に vs で (cont.)

Same park; different job. The bench *is located* there, so `に`. The eating *took place* there, so `で`.

The trap that catches nearly everyone is <ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>む. In English "live in Tokyo" feels like an activity, but Japanese treats it as being located somewhere:

<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます。<br>*My older brother lives in Tokyo.*

❌<code><ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます</code> is wrong. Learn <code><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>む</code> as a に-verb the way you learn a verb's conjugation.

---

## に · に vs へ

> **Rule of thumb** - for a destination they are interchangeable. Everywhere else, only `に` works.

<ruby>空港<rt>くうこう</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I've arrived at the airport.*

Swap that `に` for `へ` and the sentence is odd, because <ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>く is about the arrival *point*, which is `に` territory. Where the verb is plain movement - <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く, <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る - either particle is fine and the difference is a shade of emphasis: `に` on the endpoint, `へ` on the heading.

---

## に · に vs へ (cont.)

The clean division is what `へ` **cannot** do. It cannot mark a time (❌<code><ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>へ</code>), it cannot mark existence (❌<code><ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>へいます</code>), and it cannot mark the person you tell or ask something (❌<code><ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました</code>). All three are `に`.

---

## に · に vs を with verbs of movement

> **Rule of thumb** - go **in**, get **on**, sit **down**: `に`. Go **out**, get **off**, pass **through**: `を`.

English uses a preposition for both directions, so learners reach for one particle for all of them. Japanese splits them. Entering, boarding and attaching take `に`, because they end at a point. Leaving and traversing take `を`, because the noun is the thing being *departed* or *crossed*.

<ruby>次<rt>つぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>急行<rt>きゅうこう</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*I'll get on the express at the next station.*

<ruby>次<rt>つぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>降<rt>お</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*I'll get off the train at the next station.*

---

## に · に vs を with verbs of movement (cont.)

The same pairing runs through <ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>る／<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>る and <ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>る／<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>る.

---

## に · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*Time words that float relative to now - <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>, <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>, <ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby>, <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby> - take no particle at all.*

- ❌ <ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>**で**ベンチがあります。 → ✅ <ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>**に**ベンチがあります。<br>*ある and いる report where a thing *exists*, and existence is always `に`.*

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*The person you ask is a recipient, not an object. <code><ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>く</code> takes `を` for the *question* and `に` for the *person*.*

- ❌ パンを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>う**に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。 → ✅ パンを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い**に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*Purpose `に` attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form. With a する-noun you can drop the verb entirely: <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く.*

---

## に · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます。 → ✅ <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます。<br>*<code><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>む</code> is a location-of-existence verb in Japanese, however active "live" feels in English.*

---

## に · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>____<ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
<ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby> is a named day on the calendar, so it takes `に`. Note that <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby> on its own would take nothing - it is the <ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby> inside the phrase that licenses the particle.

---

## に · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>（に・で）<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>があります。

{reveal}
<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>があります。

{reveal}
The verb is ある, which reports where a thing exists, and existence takes `に`. `で` would need an action happening on the desk, which a dictionary is not doing.

---

## に · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I go to the gym twice a week."

{reveal}
<ruby>週<rt>しゅう</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>二回<rt>にかい</rt></ruby>ジム**に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。

{reveal}
Two different jobs for the same particle. The first `に` is rate - a number of times per period. The second is destination. Saying <ruby>週<rt>しゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>二回<rt>にかい</rt></ruby> without `に` is possible in casual speech but the full form is safer.

---

## に · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby><ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きて、<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きて、<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。 "I get up at six every morning and go to work."

{reveal}
The two particles are swapped. <ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby> is a frequency word and takes nothing; <ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby> is a clock time and needs `に`.

---

## に · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) パンを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い**に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。 (b) パンを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>って<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。

{reveal}
(a) is purpose: buying bread is the *reason* for going. You set off, and the errand is what the trip is for. Nothing is said about where you go afterwards.

{reveal}
(b) is sequence: you buy bread and then continue on your way. The going is already planned, and the bread is picked up en route - this is what you would say about bringing something to a friend's house.

# へ - direction, toward

**Meaning** · points movement in a direction - toward, off in the direction of

`へ` orients. It says which way something is heading, and unlike `に` it says nothing about arriving. That is why `へ` works for compass directions and for the address on an envelope, and why it is useless for times, for existence, and for people you talk to.

> **Note - `へ` is read *e*.** When it is a particle, the hiragana へ is pronounced **え**, not *he*. <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>へ is *Tōkyō e*. It is written へ for historical reasons only, exactly like the topic particle は being read *wa*.

---

## へ - direction, toward · Formation

`へ` is a particle and attaches directly to a noun. The noun does not change.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun - place you head for | Place + へ + motion verb | <ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby> → <ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く |
| Noun - compass or relative direction | Direction + へ | <ruby>右<rt>みぎ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>右<rt>みぎ</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>曲<rt>ま</rt></ruby>がる |
| Noun - addressee of a letter or gift | Person + へ | <ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby> → <ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>へ |
| Noun modifying another noun | Noun + への + Noun | <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>への<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby> |
| Fixed greeting | ようこそ + Place + へ | <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby> → ようこそ<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>へ |

---

## へ - direction, toward · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the fourth row is the one grammatical thing `へ` can do that `に` cannot. `へ` takes `の` and modifies a following noun; `に` does not. <ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>へのプレゼント is natural, ❌<code><ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>にのプレゼント</code> is not Japanese.

---

## へ - direction, toward · Examples

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>**へ**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*I'm going to the station.*

どこ**へ**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますか。<br>*Where are you going?*

あの<ruby>角<rt>かど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>右<rt>みぎ</rt></ruby>**へ**<ruby>曲<rt>ま</rt></ruby>がってください。<br>*Please turn right at that corner.*

<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>**へ**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*I'm going back to my home country next month.*

---

## へ - direction, toward · Examples (cont.)

ようこそ<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>**へ**。<br>*Welcome to Japan.*

<ruby>祖母<rt>そぼ</rt></ruby>**へ**<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I wrote a letter to my grandmother.*

<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>**への**<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>てしまいました。<br>*The train to Tokyo has already left.*

<ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>北<rt>きた</rt></ruby>**へ**<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>んでいるので、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>飛行機<rt>ひこうき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>ばないかもしれません。<br>*The typhoon is moving north, so tomorrow's flight may not take off.*

---

## へ - direction, toward · Nuance & register

`へ` is neutral in politeness - it is not more formal than `に` in the way learners are sometimes told. What is true is that `へ` is **more common in writing** and in slightly composed speech, and `に` dominates ordinary conversation. Announcements, timetables, travel writing and envelopes lean `へ`.

Two places where `へ` is essentially fixed and swapping in `に` would sound wrong:

- **Envelopes and dedications.** <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>へ on the front of a card,<br>*<ruby>両親<rt>りょうしん</rt></ruby>へ at the top of a letter. The `へ` here is "to, in the direction of you" rather than "handed to you".*
- **ようこそ～へ.** <ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>へようこそ, ようこそわが<ruby>社<rt>しゃ</rt></ruby>へ. A set frame.

---

## へ - direction, toward · Nuance & register (cont.)

In fast casual speech `へ` is dropped just as `に` is - どこ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くの? - so the choice disappears entirely at the most informal level.

One last register point: because `へ` emphasises the heading rather than the arrival, it carries a faint flavour of a journey being undertaken. <ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>へ is a touch more evocative than <ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>に. Song titles and book titles exploit this constantly. You do not need to reproduce the effect, but recognise it when you meet it.

---

## へ - direction, toward · へ vs に

> **Rule of thumb** - `へ` is the **heading**; `に` is the **landing point**. For plain <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く／<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る／<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る they overlap almost completely, so pick either. Outside destinations, only `に` exists.

<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>**へ**<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いていきました。<br>*He walked off in the direction of the mountains.*

<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*We reached the top of the mountain.*

The first says nothing about whether he got anywhere; `へ` only gives the bearing. The second is entirely about arrival, and that is a point, so `に`.

---

## へ - direction, toward · へ vs に (cont.)

Because of this, verbs that focus on the endpoint prefer `に`: <ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>く, <ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>く. Verbs of open-ended motion happily take `へ`: <ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>かう, <ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む, <ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>ぶ, <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く.

And the hard boundary is worth restating. `へ` cannot mark a **time** (❌<code><ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>へ</code>), cannot mark **existence** (❌<code><ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>がいます</code>), and cannot mark the **person you tell or ask** (❌<code><ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>しました</code>). Those are `に` jobs, all three.

---

## へ - direction, toward · へ vs まで

> **Rule of thumb** - `へ` gives the direction of travel; `まで` gives the far end of the stretch you cover.

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>**へ**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*I ran to the station.*

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>**まで**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*I ran as far as the station.*

---

## へ - direction, toward · へ vs まで (cont.)

The first is about where you were headed. The second measures the running: the station is the point where it stopped, and the sentence implies the whole distance was covered on foot. That is why `まで` pairs with `から` and `へ` does not: <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで - a span with two ends.

---

## へ - direction, toward · へ vs を

> **Rule of thumb** - `へ` marks the place you are heading for; `を` marks the space you move **through**.

English "go along the river" and "go to the river" both look like one preposition slot, so learners over-use `へ`. When the noun is the route rather than the goal, Japanese uses `を` - with <ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く, <ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>する, <ruby>曲<rt>ま</rt></ruby>がる.

この<ruby>橋<rt>はし</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>ると、<ruby>郵便局<rt>ゆうびんきょく</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*Cross this bridge and there's a post office.*

---

## へ - direction, toward · へ vs を (cont.)

あの<ruby>橋<rt>はし</rt></ruby>**へ**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう。<br>*Let's go over to that bridge.*

Note that <ruby>曲<rt>ま</rt></ruby>がる takes both, in different slots: <ruby>角<rt>かど</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>右<rt>みぎ</rt></ruby>**へ**<ruby>曲<rt>ま</rt></ruby>がる - you turn *at* the corner, *toward* the right.

---

## へ - direction, toward · Common mistakes

- ❌ pronouncing <ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>へ as "gakkō **he**" → ✅ "gakkō **e**"<br>*The particle へ is read え. Learners who first meet it in writing often carry the wrong pronunciation for years.*

- ❌ <ruby>七時<rt>しちじ</rt></ruby>**へ**<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てください。 → ✅ <ruby>七時<rt>しちじ</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てください。<br>*`へ` cannot mark a time. Only the destination slot is available to it.*

- ❌ <ruby>教室<rt>きょうしつ</rt></ruby>**へ**<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>がいます。 → ✅ <ruby>教室<rt>きょうしつ</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>がいます。<br>*Existence with ある／いる is always `に`. `へ` needs movement.*

- ❌ <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>**にの**<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>**への**<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby><br>*Only `へ` combines with `の` to modify a following noun.*

---

## へ - direction, toward · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**へ**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*A person you question is a recipient, not a direction. `へ` marks a person only when the thing sent is physical - a letter, a parcel, a present.*

# で - location of an action

**Meaning** · at, in - the place where something is done

`で` marks the **stage the action is played out on**. Not where a thing sits, not where a movement ends: where the doing happens. If you can put "at" or "in" in front of an English verb phrase and it still describes an activity, Japanese almost certainly wants `で`.

---

## で - location of an action · Formation

`で` is a particle and attaches straight onto a noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun - place of an action | Place + で + action verb | <ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>する |
| Noun - organisation or institution | Org + で + verb | <ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>く |
| Noun - event, as its venue | Event は + Place + である | パーティー → パーティーはホールである |
| Noun - the field a statement ranges over | Scope + で（いちばん…） | <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>でいちばん<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い |
| Question word | どこ + で | どこ → どこで<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いますか |

---

## で - location of an action · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - one apparent exception that is really the rule. An **event** is something that *takes place*, so it uses `で` even with ある: <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>であります。 Compare a *thing*, which uses `に`: <ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>があります。 The particle tells you whether ある means "is held" or "is located".

---

## で - location of an action · Examples

<ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>します。<br>*I study at the library.*

どこ**で**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いましたか。<br>*Where did you buy it?*

<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>**で**サッカーをしました。<br>*We played soccer in the park.*

<ruby>台所<rt>だいどころ</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>がご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*My mother is making dinner in the kitchen.*

---

## で - location of an action · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>**で**いちばん<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>富士山<rt>ふじさん</rt></ruby>です。<br>*The highest mountain in Japan is Mt Fuji.*

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>のパーティーはこのホール**で**あります。<br>*Next week's party is being held in this hall.*

<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>くのカフェ**で**<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べることが<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*I usually have lunch at a café near the office.*

<ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>、<ruby>駅前<rt>えきまえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>映画館<rt>えいがかん</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>話題<rt>わだい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*Last Sunday I saw that much-talked-about film at the cinema by the station.*

---

## で - location of an action · Nuance & register

`で` is register-neutral. It behaves identically in casual speech, in ます-form and in writing, and it is never dropped the way a destination `に` can be - the sentence would become ambiguous, so speakers keep it.

Two habits of real speech are worth noting. First, `で` is very often followed by `は` for contrast: <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>**では**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かです - "at home (as opposed to elsewhere) he's quiet." Second, in a topic-first sentence the place can be lifted to the front with `では`: <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>では<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>脱<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ぎます。 That `では` is this same `で`, just wearing a topic marker.

---

## で - location of an action · Nuance & register (cont.)

The **scope** use - <code><ruby>世界<rt>せかい</rt></ruby>でいちばん</code>, <code>クラスで<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby></code> - feels like a different word to learners, but it is the same idea stretched: the comparison is *carried out* within that field. It appears constantly in N5 superlative sentences, so learn it as part of this particle rather than as a separate item.

Finally, note that `で` is homophonous with the て-form of です／だ and with the て-form of verbs like <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んで. Context separates them instantly, but a beginner reading aloud can be thrown. If a noun of place sits immediately before it, it is this particle.

---

## で - location of an action · で vs に

> **Rule of thumb** - `で` is where something **happens**; `に` is where something **is**.

Ask what the verb is doing. An activity - <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる, <ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>く, <ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>ぶ, <ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う, <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>う, <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐ - takes `で`. A verb of existence or position - ある, いる, <ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>む, <ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>く - takes `に`.

<ruby>駅前<rt>えきまえ</rt></ruby>のカフェ**で**<ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*I'm waiting for my friend at the café by the station.*

<ruby>駅前<rt>えきまえ</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいカフェができました。<br>*A new café has opened up by the station.*

Waiting is an activity; the café is its stage, so `で`. Coming into existence is not an activity in this sense - the café now *is* there - so `に`.

---

## で - location of an action · で vs に (cont.)

The event/thing split under ある is where this rule earns its keep:

<ruby>三階<rt>さんがい</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>会議室<rt>かいぎしつ</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*There's a meeting room on the third floor.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三階<rt>さんがい</rt></ruby>**で**あります。<br>*The meeting is on the third floor.*

Same building, same verb, opposite particles - because a room *is located* and a meeting *is held*.

---

## で - location of an action · で vs を

> **Rule of thumb** - `で` is the place an action is set in; `を` is the space the action moves **across**.

A handful of motion verbs - <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く, <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>ぶ, <ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>する - can take the place as `を`, and the meaning shifts.

<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*He's out running in the park.*

<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*He's running through the park.*

---

## で - location of an action · で vs を (cont.)

The first frames the park as the venue for a jog. The second traces a path across it - he entered one side and is heading for the other. With any verb that is not motion, `を` is simply unavailable for a place.

---

## で - location of an action · で as place vs で as means

> **Rule of thumb** - ask what kind of noun it is. A place gives you "at"; a tool, vehicle, language or material gives you "by / with".

The two `で` are the same particle historically, and Japanese speakers do not experience them as ambiguous, because the noun settles it instantly.

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*I made it in this room.*

この<ruby>機械<rt>きかい</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*I made it with this machine.*

---

## で - location of an action · で as place vs で as means (cont.)

Identical grammar, and nobody is confused: a room is somewhere you can stand, a machine is something you can use. When both appear at once they simply stack, place first: <ruby>台所<rt>だいどころ</rt></ruby>でナイフ**で**<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>る is grammatical though clumsy - a native speaker would rephrase.

---

## で - location of an action · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>**で**ビールがあります。 → ✅ <ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>**に**ビールがあります。<br>*ある reporting where a **thing** sits is `に`. Only an *event* takes であります.*

- ❌ <ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>します。 → ✅ <ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>します。<br>*Studying is an activity, and activities are staged with `で`. `に` here would try to make the school a destination or a location of existence.*

- ❌ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>**に**いちばん<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>**で**いちばん<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby><br>*Superlatives set a field of comparison, and that field is marked `で`.*

- ❌ コンサートはこのホール**に**あります。 → ✅ コンサートはこのホール**で**あります。<br>*A concert is held, not located. This is the one place ある takes `で`.*

---

## で - location of an action · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ この<ruby>川<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>いではいけません。 → ✅ この<ruby>川<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>いではいけません。<br>*<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐ in the sense "go swimming" treats the river as a venue. `を` would mean swimming *across* it, which is not what a warning sign is saying.*

# で - means, instrument, cause

**Meaning** · with, by, in, out of - the tool, vehicle, language, material or cause that makes the action possible

`で` names **what you used**. A knife, a train, English, wood, a hundred yen, ten minutes, a typhoon - anything the action was carried out *by means of*, or *because of*. It is the same particle as the one that marks the place of an action, and it works the same way: it supplies the circumstance the verb depends on.

---

## で - means, instrument, cause · Formation

`で` attaches directly to a noun. Nothing changes shape, and it never follows a verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun - tool or instrument | Tool + で + verb | はし → はしで<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる |
| Noun - vehicle | Vehicle + で + motion verb | <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く |
| Noun - language or medium | Language + で + verb | <ruby>英語<rt>えいご</rt></ruby> → <ruby>英語<rt>えいご</rt></ruby>で<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す |
| Noun - material you can still see | Material + で + <ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>る | <ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby> → <ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>で<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>る |
| Noun - cause or reason | Cause + で + verb | <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む |
| Noun - amount, price or elapsed time | Quantity + で | <ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>える |
| Number of people | Number + で | <ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby> → <ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く |

---

## で - means, instrument, cause · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - "on foot" breaks the pattern. <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く is a verb, not a tool, so you say <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いて<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます, never ❌<code><ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いてで</code> and never ❌<code><ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます</code>. Learn <code><ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いて</code> as the one member of the transport list that is not a noun + で.

---

## で - means, instrument, cause · Examples

はし**で**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます。<br>*I eat with chopsticks.*

---

## で - means, instrument, cause · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みました。<br>*I stayed off school with a cold.*

## で - means, instrument, cause · Nuance & register

`で` is register-neutral and is not dropped in casual speech - unlike a destination particle, it carries information nothing else supplies. はし<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる is not casual Japanese, it is broken Japanese.

A few things worth knowing about how it behaves in real sentences:

---

## で - means, instrument, cause · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で, <ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>で, みんなで.** This is the standard way to say "alone",<br>*"the two of us", "all together". It is very frequent and learners reach for `と` instead, which does not work with a number.*
- **Cause `で` is neutral and slightly detached.** <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れました states<br>*the cause flatly and is perfect for an apology at work, because it does not argue. Compare the reason particle `から`, which sounds like you are making a case.*
- **Amounts.** <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>で<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わります - "it'll be done in three days" - and<br>*<ruby>五百円<rt>ごひゃくえん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました - "I got it for five hundred yen." Here `で` marks the quantity the action was completed *within* or *for*.*
- **`で` is often followed by contrastive `は`.** <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>**では**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>けません

---

## で - means, instrument, cause · Nuance & register (cont.)

- "you can't get there by train (whatever else you might manage)."

Because the tool `で` and the place `で` look identical, Japanese avoids stacking two of them in one clause. If you need both, most speakers restructure the sentence rather than write two `で` in a row.

---

## で - means, instrument, cause · で vs に with vehicles

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>る</code> takes `に`; every other verb takes `で`.

This is the highest-frequency `で` error at N5, because English uses "on" for both.

<ruby>東京駅<rt>とうきょうえき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>新幹線<rt>しんかんせん</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*I'll board the shinkansen at Tokyo Station.*

<ruby>新幹線<rt>しんかんせん</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*I'll go to Osaka by shinkansen.*

<code><ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>る</code> is "get on", a movement that lands on a point, and points are `に`. <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く, <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>通<rt>かよ</rt></ruby>う all describe travel *using* the vehicle, so they take `で`. The opposite of <code><ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>る</code>, <code><ruby>降<rt>お</rt></ruby>りる</code>, takes `を`.

---

## で - means, instrument, cause · で vs から for material

> **Rule of thumb** - if you can still see what it was made of, use `で`. If the material was chemically transformed and has disappeared, use `から`.

このかばんは<ruby>紙<rt>かみ</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*I made this bag out of paper.*

ワインはぶどう**から**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*Wine is made from grapes.*

---

## で - means, instrument, cause · で vs から for material (cont.)

The bag is still visibly paper. The wine is not visibly grapes - the raw material went in and something else came out. The same split gives <ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>で<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>った<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby> (you can see the wood) but <ruby>米<rt>こめ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>る<ruby>日本酒<rt>にほんしゅ</rt></ruby> (you cannot see the rice). In practice `で` is far more common; `から` is reserved for genuine transformations, and native speakers will not object to `で` in borderline cases.

---

## で - means, instrument, cause · で as means vs で as place

> **Rule of thumb** - the noun decides. Somewhere you could stand is a place; something you could pick up, speak, or blame is a means.

Japanese does not experience these as two words, and neither should you after a little practice - the noun disambiguates before the verb arrives.

<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*I came by bicycle.*

<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby><ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>き<ruby>場<rt>ば</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*I'm waiting at the bike park.*

---

## で - means, instrument, cause · で as means vs で as place (cont.)

The tricky residue is nouns that could be either. <ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby> is a means (<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す, "talk on the phone"); <ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby> is a place. When a noun is genuinely ambiguous, Japanese relies on the verb: <ruby>新聞<rt>しんぶん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る is "find out from the newspaper" (means), because <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る is not something you do *at* a newspaper.

---

## で - means, instrument, cause · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>ります。 → ✅ <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*<code><ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>る</code> is the one transport verb that takes `に`. Everything else in the travel vocabulary takes `で`.*

- ❌ <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いて**で**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りました。 → ✅ <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いて<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*`で` attaches to nouns only. The て-form already carries the "by doing" sense.*

- ❌ この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>英語<rt>えいご</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてあります。 → ✅ この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>英語<rt>えいご</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてあります。<br>*A language used as a medium is a means. `を` would try to make English the thing that was written.*

- ❌ <ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>った<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>った<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby><br>*The wood is still visible in the desk, so it is `で`. Save `から` for materials that have been transformed out of sight.*

---

## で - means, instrument, cause · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>と<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。 → ✅ <ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>と<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*A number of people is a circumstance, not a companion. Counted groups take `で`.*

# から - because, since

**Meaning** · because - gives the reason for what is said in the other half of the sentence

`から` follows a **whole clause** and turns it into a reason. The reason comes first, the consequence second: 「reason + から、result」. It is the reason particle a speaker uses when they are willing to stand behind the reason - which is why it sits so naturally in front of a suggestion, an invitation or a command.

---

## から - because, since · Formation

`から` attaches to a finished clause. Whatever ends that clause has to be a complete predicate, so nouns and な-adjectives need `だ` or `です` first.

---

## から - because, since · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb, plain | plain form + から | <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るから |
| Verb, polite | ます-form + から | <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ります → <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>りますから |
| い-adjective | plain form + から | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いから |
| い-adjective, polite | ～いです + から | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いです → <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いですから |
| な-adjective | ～だ + から | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだから |
| な-adjective, polite | ～です + から | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かですから |
| Noun | ～だ + から | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>み → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みだから |
| Noun, polite | ～です + から | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>み → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みですから |
| Past | past form + から | <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れた → <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れたから |

---

## から - because, since · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the politeness of the clause before `から` is free. Both <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いから<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めます and <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いですから<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めます are correct; the plain form before `から` is more common even in polite speech, and the ですから version sounds a little more careful. What matters for politeness is the **end** of the sentence.

---

## から - because, since · Examples

<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い**から**、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めます。<br>*It's cold, so I'll shut the window.*

<ruby>危<rt>あぶ</rt></ruby>ない**から**、<ruby>触<rt>さわ</rt></ruby>らないでください。<br>*It's dangerous, so please don't touch it.*

<ruby>頭<rt>あたま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>い**から**、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*My head hurts, so I'm going home early today.*

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い**から**、よく<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*This shop is cheap, so I come here a lot.*

---

## から - because, since · Examples (cont.)

もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってください。すぐ<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わります**から**。<br>*Please wait a little longer - I'll be finished in a moment.*

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>りそうです**から**、<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていきましょう。<br>*It looks like rain, so let's take an umbrella.*

<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>は<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む**から**、<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>いうちに<ruby>出発<rt>しゅっぱつ</rt></ruby>したほうがいいですよ。<br>*The roads get busy on Sundays, so you'd do well to set off early in the morning.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>給料日<rt>きゅうりょうび</rt></ruby>だ**から**、みんなで<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こうよ。<br>*It's payday, so let's all go out for a drink.*

---

## から - because, since · Nuance & register

`から` is neutral in politeness by itself - it appears in the most casual speech and in formal writing - but it is **not** neutral in attitude. `から` presents the reason as *yours*: something you assert, and are prepared to defend. That has three practical consequences.

**It is the natural reason particle in front of a push.** Invitations, suggestions, requests and commands almost always take `から` rather than `ので`: <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がないから<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いで。 The reason exists to justify what you are asking for.

**It can sound blunt in an apology.** If you are explaining to a customer or a boss why something went wrong, `から` can read as defensive - "because X, that's why." `ので` is softer and is the safe default there.

---

## から - because, since · Nuance & register (cont.)

**It stands alone at the end of a sentence.** ～から。 with nothing after it is extremely common in speech and leaves the consequence implied: <ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>がやるから。 - "It's fine, I'll do it." This has a warm, reassuring feel, and is worth copying.

Two more forms you will meet immediately. **～からです** answers a どうして question as a complete sentence: 「どうして<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだんですか。」「<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>があったからです。」 And **だから** at the head of a sentence is this same から promoted to a conjunction: <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>だ。だから、<ruby>今夜<rt>こんや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>する。 In polite speech that becomes ですから.

---

## から - because, since · から vs ので

> **Rule of thumb** - `から` is *my* reason and pushes; `ので` is *the* reason and explains. When in doubt with a superior, use `ので`.

Both mean "because". The difference is stance. `から` presents a judgement; `ので` presents a circumstance and lets the listener draw their own conclusion, which makes it sound more deferential.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みだ**から**、ゆっくりしよう。<br>*Tomorrow's a day off, so let's take it easy.*

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れました**ので**、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>遅刻<rt>ちこく</rt></ruby>します。<br>*My train was delayed, so I'll be a little late.*

---

## から - because, since · から vs ので (cont.)

There is a hard grammatical difference too, and it is the one exams test. After a noun or a な-adjective, `から` takes **だ** and `ので` takes **な**:

- <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>**だ**から ／ <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>**な**ので
- <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**だ**から ／ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**な**ので

Finally, a command, a request or a volitional in the second half strongly prefers `から`. <ruby>危<rt>あぶ</rt></ruby>ないから<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がって! is natural; the `ので` version sounds oddly formal for a shout.

---

## から - because, since · から vs the causal て-form

> **Rule of thumb** - the て-form can give a cause, but only when the result is something that *happened to you*, never something you decide.

<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しく**て**、<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れました。<br>*Work was hectic, and so my reply was late.*

<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい**から**、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>断<rt>ことわ</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*Work is hectic, so I'm going to turn it down today.*

---

## から - because, since · から vs the causal て-form (cont.)

The first result is involuntary - the lateness simply followed. The second is a decision, and a decision needs `から`. This is why ❌<code><ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しくて、<ruby>断<rt>ことわ</rt></ruby>りましょう</code> is wrong: you cannot hang a volitional, a request or a command off a causal て-form.

---

## から - because, since · から as reason vs から as "from"

> **Rule of thumb** - look at what is in front of it. A **clause** before `から` means "because"; a bare **noun** before `から` means "from".

The two are the same word historically - "starting from this fact" - and Japanese speakers never confuse them, because the test is mechanical.

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*I came from Japan.*

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きだ**から**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*I came because I love Japan.*

---

## から - because, since · から as reason vs から as "from" (cont.)

<code><ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby></code> alone is a noun, so the first is a starting point. Add a predicate and the same から flips to a reason. The one place beginners genuinely trip is answering どうして with a bare noun - see the mistakes below.

---

## から - because, since · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>から、<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いです。 → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**だ**から、<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*A noun cannot sit directly in front of reason `から`; it needs だ or です. Without it the sentence reads "from a student".*

- ❌ <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>**な**から、よく<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>います。 → ✅ <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>**だ**から、よく<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>います。<br>*な belongs to `ので`. `から` takes だ.*

- ❌ 「どうして<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだの?」「<ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>から。」 → ✅ 「<ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>**だ**から。」<br>*The bare noun version says "from a cold" and sounds like a starting point. The same fix in polite speech: <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>だからです／<ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>でしたから.*

---

## から - because, since · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ (to a client) <ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby><ruby>者<rt>しゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んでいますから、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>します。 → ✅ <ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby><ruby>者<rt>しゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んでおりますので、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>します。<br>*Grammatically fine, socially blunt. In an apology or an explanation upward, `ので` removes the note of "and that's your answer".*

- ❌ <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くて、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めてください。 → ✅ <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いから、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めてください。<br>*A request cannot follow a causal て-form. Use `から` whenever the second half is something you want the listener to do.*

---

## から - because, since · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>____、<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みです。

{reveal}
<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>**だから**、<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みです。

{reveal}
A noun in front of reason `から` must be propped up with だ (or です). Leaving it out would turn the phrase into "from Sunday", which is a starting point, not a reason.

---

## から - because, since · Practice 2

**2.** すみません、<ruby>用事<rt>ようじ</rt></ruby>がある（から・ので）、お<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>します。

{reveal}
すみません、<ruby>用事<rt>ようじ</rt></ruby>がある**ので**、お<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>します。

{reveal}
You are excusing yourself to people who are still working, so you want the deferential particle. `から` would be understood, but it presents your errand as a justification and sounds a touch self-assured.

---

## から - because, since · Practice 3

{reveal}

{reveal}

# から - from

**Meaning** · from - the starting point of a distance, a stretch of time, or a flow of something

`から` marks **where something begins**. A place a journey starts from, a clock time an event starts at, the person a letter came from, the raw material a product started life as. Its natural partner is `まで`, which marks the other end.

---

## から - from · Formation

`から` attaches directly to a noun, and it is the one place particle that also attaches to a て-form.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun - place of origin | Place + から | <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby> → <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>から<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く |
| Noun - starting time | Time + から | <ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まる |
| Noun - person or source | Source + から + もらう／<ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>りる／<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>く | <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>から<ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>りる |
| Noun - raw material | Material + から + <ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>る | <ruby>米<rt>こめ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>米<rt>こめ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>る |
| Verb て-form - "after doing" | て-form + から | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べて → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてから |
| Paired with まで | A から B まで | <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>から<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>まで |

---

## から - from · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - `～てから` is a fixed pattern meaning "after doing X". The verb must be in the て-form, and the tense of the whole sentence is carried by the **final** verb only: <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってから<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>しました. Never conjugate the て-form for past.

---

## から - from · Examples

<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まります。<br>*It starts at nine.*

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いて<ruby>五分<rt>ごふん</rt></ruby>です。<br>*It's a five-minute walk from the station.*

<ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>**から**メールが<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*I got an email from a friend.*

この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*I borrowed this dictionary from my teacher.*

---

## から - from · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>って**から**ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べなさい。<br>*Wash your hands before you eat.*

<ruby>日本酒<rt>にほんしゅ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>米<rt>こめ</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*Sake is made from rice.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>二時<rt>にじ</rt></ruby>**から**ですが、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>めに<ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*The meeting is from two, but please come into the hall a little early.*

<ruby>北<rt>きた</rt></ruby>の<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>冷<rt>つめ</rt></ruby>たい<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってくるので、カーテンを<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めました。<br>*A cold draught was coming in through the north window, so I closed the curtain.*

---

## から - from · Nuance & register

`から` is register-neutral and is never dropped - it carries information no other part of the sentence supplies.

A few behaviours worth having ready:

---

## から - from · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **から alone can be the predicate.** <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>からです。 -<br>*"The meeting is from three." No verb is needed, and the question form works the same way: <ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>からですか。*
- **からの modifies a noun**, just as `への` does. <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんからの<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby> -<br>*"a letter from Tanaka." This is one of the very few particles that takes `の` directly.*
- **Passage through, not just origin.** <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>から<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>る,<br>*<ruby>裏口<rt>うらぐち</rt></ruby>から<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>る. `から` marks the opening something passes through, and English usually picks "through" rather than "from" here.*
- **Range expressions.** <ruby>一時<rt>いちじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>まで, <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>から<br>*<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>まで. The pair is so tightly bound that hearing `から` makes a Japanese listener expect `まで`.*

---

## から - from · Nuance & register (cont.)

`から` also has an entirely separate life as the reason particle meaning "because". The test is what precedes it: a bare noun gives you "from", a finished clause gives you "because". There is no ambiguity in practice.

---

## から - from · から vs まで

> **Rule of thumb** - `から` is the near end, `まで` is the far end. They are a pair, and either can appear alone.

<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*The bank is open from nine until three.*

Use `から` alone when only the start matters (<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>きます), and `まで` alone when only the end does. What you cannot do is use one where the other belongs:

<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>十五分<rt>じゅうごふん</rt></ruby>かかります。<br>*It takes fifteen minutes from home to the station by bike.*

---

## から - from · から vs まで (cont.)

Note that `まで`, not `へ`, is what pairs with `から`. A `から～へ` sentence is not idiomatic Japanese.

---

## から - from · から vs に for receiving

> **Rule of thumb** - with もらう, <ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>りる, <ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>く and <ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>う, both work for a person, but only `から` works for an organisation - and neither works with あげる, which is always `に`.

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>**から**まんがを<ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*I borrowed some manga from my sister.*

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>**に**まんがを<ruby>貸<rt>か</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*I lent some manga to my sister.*

---

## から - from · から vs に for receiving (cont.)

When the verb is one of *receiving*, `に` and `から` are both possible for a person, with `から` making the source a little more explicit and `に` a little more personal. As soon as the source is a company, a school or a shop, `から` is the only natural choice: <ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>がありました. When the verb is one of *giving* - あげる, <ruby>貸<rt>か</rt></ruby>す, <ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>える - the other party is a recipient and takes `に` only.

---

## から - from · ～てから vs ～たあとで

> **Rule of thumb** - `～てから` says the first action is a **precondition** for the second. `～たあとで` just puts them in order.

くすりを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んで**から**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*I take my medicine and then go to bed.*

<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たあとで、ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました。<br>*After we saw the film, we had dinner.*

---

## から - from · ～てから vs ～たあとで (cont.)

The medicine has to come first for the sentence to make sense - that is `てから`. The film and the dinner are simply two events in sequence, which is `あとで`. Because `てから` implies dependency, it is what instructions and rules use: <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ってから, <ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>してから. Also note `てから` cannot take past tense on the first verb, while `あとで` requires it.

---

## から - from · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**から**プレゼントをあげました。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**に**プレゼントをあげました。<br>*あげる is giving, so the other party is a recipient. Only *receiving* verbs allow `から`.*

- ❌ ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる**から**、くすりを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。 → ✅ ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べて**から**、くすりを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*The wrong version is grammatical but means "because I'm going to eat". "After eating" needs the て-form.*

- ❌ <ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>から**に**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まります。 → ✅ <ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まります。<br>*`から` already marks the time; stacking `に` on top of it is not possible. Choose one: <ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まります means "starts at nine" with no sense of a span.*

---

## から - from · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ 「どうして<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れたの?」「<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>から。」 → ✅ 「<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れた**から**。」<br>*A bare noun before `から` gives the "from" reading - "from the train". A reason needs a full clause in front.*

- ❌ <ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>**へ**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>します。 → ✅ <ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>**まで**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>します。<br>*The far end of a span is `まで`. `から` never pairs with `へ`.*

---

## から - from · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>____<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>までです。

{reveal}
<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>までです。

{reveal}
`まで` at the far end makes `から` almost obligatory at the near end - the two form a fixed pair. Note that the sentence ends in です - a `から～まで` span needs no verb.

---

## から - from · Practice 2

**2.** この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>（から・に）<ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>りました。

{reveal}
この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>りました。

{reveal}
With a person, both particles are possible. With an institution - a library, a company, a school - only `から` is natural, because there is no individual to hand it to you.

---

## から - from · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I'll call you after the meeting ends."

{reveal}
<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わって**から**<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>します。

{reveal}
`～てから` because the call depends on the meeting being over. The て-form stays uninflected; the tense of the sentence sits on <ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>します at the end.

## から - from · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けるから、<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>涼<rt>すず</rt></ruby>しくなりました。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けて**から**、<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>涼<rt>すず</rt></ruby>しくなりました。 "The room got cooler after I opened the window."

{reveal}
With the dictionary form, `から` reads as "because I'm going to open the window", which clashes with the past tense at the end. The て-form gives the intended "after".

## から - from · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん**に**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました。 (b) <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん**から**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました。

# まで

**Meaning** · until, up to, as far as - and, in one special use, "even"

`まで` marks the **far end**. Something continues, and `まで` says where it stops: an hour on the clock, a station, the moment a condition changes. Whatever it attaches to is *included* - <ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>まで covers five o'clock itself. Its partner is `から`, which marks the near end.

---

## まで · Formation

`まで` attaches directly to a noun or to a plain non-past verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun - place | Place + まで | <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby> → <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く |
| Noun - time | Time + まで | <ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>く |
| Verb - plain non-past | dictionary form + まで | <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる → <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わるまで<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ |
| Paired with から | A から B まで | <ruby>月曜日<rt>げつようび</rt></ruby> → <ruby>月曜日<rt>げつようび</rt></ruby>から<ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>まで |
| Deadline | Noun／Verb + までに | <ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby> → <ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す |
| Noun - "even" | Noun + まで | <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども → <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもまで<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている |

---

## まで · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the verb before `まで` is always **plain and non-past**, no matter how the sentence ends. <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わるまで<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちました is correct; ❌<code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったまで</code> and ❌<code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりますまで</code> are not. The final verb carries all the tense and all the politeness.

---

## まで · Examples

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>**まで**<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*I'll walk as far as the station.*

<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>**まで**<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*I work until five.*

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>がやむ**まで**ここで<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちましょう。<br>*Let's wait here until the rain stops.*

この<ruby>美術館<rt>びじゅつかん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>月曜日<rt>げつようび</rt></ruby>**まで**<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みです。<br>*This museum is closed until Monday.*

---

## まで · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>から<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>**まで**<ruby>新幹線<rt>しんかんせん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I took the shinkansen from Tokyo to Osaka.*

そんなことは<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**まで**<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っていますよ。<br>*Even children know that.*

<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる**まで**だれも<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>たなかった。<br>*Nobody left their seat until the match was over.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらなくて、<ruby>夜中<rt>よなか</rt></ruby>の<ruby>二時<rt>にじ</rt></ruby>**まで**<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>っていました。<br>*I couldn't get finished yesterday and stayed at the office until two in the morning.*

---

## まで · Nuance & register

`まで` is register-neutral. It is identical in plain and polite speech and in writing, and it is never dropped.

Three things about how it feels in use:

---

## まで · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **It is inclusive.** <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>まで means the third is still covered. If you need<br>*"before, not including", Japanese uses <ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に rather than `まで`.*
- **The "even" use is emphatic and slightly loaded.** <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>笑<rt>わら</rt></ruby>った -<br>*"even the teacher laughed" - presents the teacher as the least likely member of the set. It always carries a note of surprise, and it can sound accusatory if the sentence is a complaint: <ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>反対<rt>はんたい</rt></ruby>するのか。 Use it deliberately.*
- **までも and までは.** <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>までには<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わります adds "by tomorrow at the<br>*latest"; そこまでしなくてもいい means "you don't have to go that far". These build on the same core idea of a limit and are worth recognising early.*

---

## まで · Nuance & register (cont.)

`まで` also survives on its own as a sentence-final marker in signs and notices: <ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>まで。 Nothing is missing - the reader supplies です.

---

## まで · まで vs までに

> **Rule of thumb** - `まで` = "**until**", for something that continues. `までに` = "**by**", for something that happens once, at any point before the deadline.

This is the highest-value distinction on the page, and it is tested at every level.

<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>**まで**<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>にいます。<br>*I'll be at the office until five.*

<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>**までに**<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>戻<rt>もど</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*I'll be back at the office by five.*

---

## まで · まで vs までに (cont.)

The first stretches across the whole period; the second is a single event that lands somewhere inside it. So the test is the verb. **Durative** verbs - いる, <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ, <ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>く, <ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける, <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ている - take `まで`. **Punctual** verbs - <ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>く, <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す, <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらせる - take `までに`.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>**までに**この<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Please hand this document in by tomorrow morning.*

Say まで there and you are asking someone to keep submitting the document all night.

---

## まで · まで vs から

> **Rule of thumb** - `から` opens the span, `まで` closes it. Either can stand alone; when both appear, `から` always comes first.

<ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>みは<ruby>七月<rt>しちがつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>八月<rt>はちがつ</rt></ruby>**まで**です。<br>*The summer holiday runs from July to August.*

Alone, each simply leaves the other end open. <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>から<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいです says nothing about when the busy period ends; <ruby>水曜日<rt>すいようび</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいです says nothing about when it started. The pairing is so strong that a `から` early in a sentence sets up an expectation of `まで` - leaving it unfilled is a stylistic choice, not an error.

---

## まで · まで vs に and へ

> **Rule of thumb** - `に` and `へ` give the goal of a movement. `まで` measures the stretch covered and says where it ended.

<ruby>空港<rt>くうこう</rt></ruby>**まで**<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*I'll take you as far as the airport.*

<ruby>空港<rt>くうこう</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I've arrived at the airport.*

---

## まで · まで vs に and へ (cont.)

The first is about the journey - the whole distance is covered and the airport is where your involvement stops; it quietly implies you are not going further. The second is about the endpoint alone. This is why <ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く and <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>る take `まで` so naturally, and why <ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>く cannot: arriving has no stretch to measure.

---

## まで · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>**まで**レポートを<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください。 → ✅ <ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>**までに**レポートを<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Handing something in happens once, so it needs the deadline form. The まで version asks for continuous submission.*

- ❌ <ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>**までに**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しました。 → ✅ <ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>**まで**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*Studying is durative - it filled the period - so it takes plain `まで`.*

- ❌ <ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わり**ますまで**<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>たないでください。 → ✅ <ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる**まで**<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>たないでください。<br>*The verb in front of `まで` is always plain non-past. Politeness lives at the end of the sentence.*

---

## まで · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>**までに**<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ります。 → ✅ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>**まで**<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*With a place, `までに` would mean "by the time we reach the station". If you mean "as far as", it is plain `まで`.*

- ❌ <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください（meaning "before the 3rd"） → ✅ <ruby>二日<rt>ふつか</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください<br>*`まで` and `までに` **include** the point they name, so the first sentence allows the 3rd itself. To exclude it, name the day before, or say <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に.*

---

# と - and, joining a complete list

**Meaning** · and - joins nouns into a list, and claims the list is complete

`と` glues nouns together: A と B is "A and B". The part learners are not told is that `と` is **exhaustive**. A と B と C says those three are all of them. If you only want to name a couple of examples out of many, Japanese has a different particle, `や`, and using `と` where you meant `や` makes a claim you did not intend.

---

## と - and, joining a complete list · Formation

`と` sits between the nouns. The last noun takes whatever particle the sentence needs; the `と`-group behaves as a single noun phrase.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Two nouns | Noun と Noun | パン → パンと<ruby>卵<rt>たまご</rt></ruby> |
| Three or more | Noun と Noun と Noun | <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>と<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>と<ruby>鳥<rt>とり</rt></ruby> |
| The group takes a particle | (N と N) + が／を／は | <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>・<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby> → <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>と<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます |
| Choosing between two | N と N、どちらが… | コーヒー → コーヒーと<ruby>紅茶<rt>こうちゃ</rt></ruby>、どちらが |

---

## と - and, joining a complete list · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - `と` joins **nouns only**. It cannot join verbs, adjectives or clauses. For those, Japanese uses the て-form (<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くて<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>) or そして (<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>をしました。そして、<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りました). The `と` you meet at the end of a clause - <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>になると<ruby>暖<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かくなる - is a different word entirely, a conditional.

---

## と - and, joining a complete list · Examples

パン**と**<ruby>牛乳<rt>ぎゅうにゅう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*I bought bread and milk.*

<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>にいます。<br>*My father and mother are in Osaka.*

<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>卵<rt>たまご</rt></ruby>**と**ごはんです。<br>*Breakfast is eggs and rice.*

すみません、コーヒー**と**ケーキをください。<br>*Excuse me - a coffee and a cake, please.*

---

## と - and, joining a complete list · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>英語<rt>えいご</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>中国語<rt>ちゅうごくご</rt></ruby>**と**フランス<ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せます。<br>*I can speak English, Chinese and French.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>るのは<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>だけです。<br>*The only two attending the meeting are the manager and me.*

この<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>には<ruby>塩<rt>しお</rt></ruby>**と**こしょう**と**オリーブオイルしか<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>っていません。<br>*This dish uses nothing but salt, pepper and olive oil.*

<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていくのはパスポート**と**カメラ**と**<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>ぐらいです。<br>*About all I'm taking on the trip is my passport, a camera and some medicine.*

---

## と - and, joining a complete list · Nuance & register

`と` is register-neutral and is never dropped. In a list of three or more it is repeated between every pair - Japanese has no equivalent of the English "A, B and C" where the first joins are commas. A comma-only list (<ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>鳥<rt>とり</rt></ruby>) does exist, but it reads as a written enumeration, like items on a form, rather than as speech.

A trailing `と` after the last item - <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>と、ノートと。 - is heard when someone is still counting things off out loud, and it signals "…and there may be more coming". Once the list is finished, drop it. In a written sentence a trailing `と` before a particle is simply wrong: ❌<code><ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>とノートとが</code>.

---

## と - and, joining a complete list · Nuance & register (cont.)

The exhaustiveness of `と` is what makes it useful in shops, orders and instructions, where you want to be precise. <ruby>材料<rt>ざいりょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>小麦粉<rt>こむぎこ</rt></ruby>と<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>と<ruby>塩<rt>しお</rt></ruby>です means those are the ingredients, full stop. In relaxed conversation about vaguer things, `や` is often the more natural choice, and over-using `と` can make you sound like you are reading from a list.

One more thing `と` does that looks unrelated but is the same idea: it sets up a **two-way comparison**. コーヒーと<ruby>紅茶<rt>こうちゃ</rt></ruby>、どちらがいいですか。 The two items are being presented as a closed set to choose between - exhaustive again.

---

## と - and, joining a complete list · と vs や

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` says "these and no others". `や` says "these among others".

かばんの<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>には<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>鍵<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*There's a wallet and keys in my bag.*

かばんの<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>には<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>**や**<ruby>鍵<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*There's a wallet, keys and so on in my bag.*

The first is a complete inventory - that bag holds two things. The second offers a sample; there is clearly other stuff in there. Because `や` is a sample, it very often finishes with など: <ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>や<ruby>鍵<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>など. That など is only available to `や`; ❌<code><ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>と<ruby>鍵<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>など</code> is contradictory.

---

## と - and, joining a complete list · と vs や (cont.)

Practical consequence: if someone asks what is in a place and you are not sure you have listed everything, `や` protects you. If you are giving an order or a specification, use `と`.

---

## と - and, joining a complete list · と vs the て-form and そして

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` is for nouns. To link adjectives use the て-form; to link actions use the て-form or そして.

This is the most frequent `と` error, because English "and" does all three jobs.

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>く**て**<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るいです。<br>*This room is spacious and bright.*

<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**て**、<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I had breakfast and went to work.*

Both would be ungrammatical with `と`. The rule has no exceptions worth learning at N5: if what you are joining ends in い, な or a verb, `と` is not the tool.

---

## と - and, joining a complete list · と as "and" vs と as "with"

> **Rule of thumb** - if the と-phrase is one of the things the verb is *about*, it is "and". If it names a person the subject acts *together with*, it is "with".

The same two words can go either way, and only the sentence shape decides.

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん**と**<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*Tanaka and Yamada came.*

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さん**と**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*Tanaka came with Yamada.*

---

## と - and, joining a complete list · と as "and" vs と as "with" (cont.)

In the first, `が` follows the whole group, so the group is one subject: two people arrived. In the second, `が` has already marked Tanaka as the subject, so <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さん can only be a companion. Watch where the subject particle lands and the ambiguity disappears. Adding <ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に forces the "with" reading outright.

---

## と - and, joining a complete list · Common mistakes

- ❌ この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**と**きれいです。 → ✅ この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かで、きれいです。<br>*`と` cannot join adjectives. な-adjectives link with で, い-adjectives with the て-form.*

- ❌ <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた**と**、<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけました。 → ✅ <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べて、<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけました。<br>*Two actions in sequence are linked with the て-form. `と` after a clause makes a conditional, not a list.*

- ❌ りんご**と**バナナ**と**など<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。 → ✅ りんご**や**バナナなどが<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*など marks an open-ended sample, which contradicts the "complete list" meaning of `と`. など belongs with `や`.*

- ❌ <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんと<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さん**と**が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。 → ✅ <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんと<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*No `と` after the final item in a written sentence. The group takes its particle directly.*

---

# と - with, accompaniment

**Meaning** · with - names the person you do something together with, or the other party in a two-sided action

`と` marks a **partner**. Sometimes the partner is optional - you could have gone to the film alone - and sometimes the verb cannot exist without one: you cannot marry, argue or shake hands by yourself. Both are the same `と`.

---

## と - with, accompaniment · Formation

`と` attaches directly to a noun, almost always a person or an animal, and the phrase then modifies the verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun - companion | Person + と + verb | <ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby> → <ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>と<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く |
| Noun - companion, emphasised | Person + と<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に + verb | <ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby> → <ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>と<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる |
| Noun - the other party in a mutual act | Person + と + <ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>する／けんかする／<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う | <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>と<ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>する |
| Question word | だれ + と | だれ → だれと<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ましたか |
| Combined with a count | Person + と + number + で | <ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby> → <ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>と<ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>で |

---

## と - with, accompaniment · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - <ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に is optional but common. It adds nothing grammatically; it just makes the togetherness explicit and slightly warmer. The `に` is not optional: ❌<code><ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>と<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く</code>.

---

## と - with, accompaniment · Examples

<ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*I saw a film with a friend.*

だれ**と**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ましたか。<br>*Who did you come with?*

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*I'm going shopping with my older sister.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>、<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>先輩<rt>せんぱい</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*Last year I married a senior from my university.*

---

## と - with, accompaniment · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>をしました。<br>*I've arranged to speak with my teacher tomorrow.*

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>**と**けんかして、<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>に<ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>られました。<br>*I had a fight with my little brother and got told off by my mother.*

<ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>川<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>って、バーベキューをする<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>です。<br>*At the weekend I'm going to the river with my family for a barbecue.*

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>してから、お<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>します。<br>*I'll get back to you once I've talked it over with my section chief.*

---

## と - with, accompaniment · Nuance & register

`と` is register-neutral and is not dropped, even in fast speech - remove it and the sentence loses the only thing marking the companion.

The set it belongs to is worth naming, because it decides the particle for a whole class of verbs. **Mutual verbs** need two parties, and the second one is always `と`: <ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>する, <ruby>離婚<rt>りこん</rt></ruby>する, けんかする, <ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>する, <ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>する, <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す, <ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>る - with the exception noted below. Learn these as と-verbs and a large slice of N4 grammar comes for free.

---

## と - with, accompaniment · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two frequency notes. **だれと** is the standard way to ask "who with?", and it comes up constantly in small talk about the weekend. And **と<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に** is the polite, slightly fuller version you use when the companionship is the point: <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>と<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>います。

Finally, keep this `と` separate from the quotation `と`, which follows a whole clause and precedes <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う, <ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う or <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>く: <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くと<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いました。 If a full sentence sits in front of `と`, it is quotation, not accompaniment. And if a bare noun sits in front and the group is what the verb is about, it is the "and" `と` instead.

---

## と - with, accompaniment · と vs で

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` needs a **named companion**. A **number of people** takes `で`.

This pair trips up almost everyone, because English says "with two people" and "with a friend" the same way.

<ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I went with a friend.*

<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*The three of us went.*

You cannot say ❌<code><ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>と<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました</code>, because a bare number names nobody. The same logic gives <ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で for "alone" and みんなで for "all together".

---

## と - with, accompaniment · と vs で (cont.)

The two combine happily when you want both facts at once:

<ruby>同僚<rt>どうりょう</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>四人<rt>よにん</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I went out drinking with colleagues, four of us in all.*

---

## と - with, accompaniment · と vs に with mutual verbs

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` presents the action as **two-sided**; `に` presents it as **one-directional**, aimed at the other person.

Several verbs take either particle, and the choice changes the picture.

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*I ran into my teacher at the station.*

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*I met up with my teacher at the station.*

---

## と - with, accompaniment · と vs に with mutual verbs (cont.)

With `に`, you did the meeting - it can be an accident, and often is. With `と`, both parties met, which implies it was arranged. The same split runs through <ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>する (<ruby>上司<rt>じょうし</rt></ruby>に = you take the problem to them; <ruby>上司<rt>じょうし</rt></ruby>と = you thrash it out together) and <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す.

Some verbs allow only one. <ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>する and けんかする are `と` only, because they are unavoidably two-sided. <ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>る is `に` only - <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ています, "I take after my father" - even though resemblance sounds mutual in English.

---

## と - with, accompaniment · と as "with" vs と as "and"

> **Rule of thumb** - find the subject marker. If `が` or `は` follows the whole と-group, it is "and". If the subject has already been named elsewhere, the と-phrase is "with".

<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>は<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます。<br>*My brother and sister live in Tokyo.*

<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます。<br>*My brother lives in Tokyo with my sister.*

In the first, `は` scopes over both people, so they are one subject: two residents. In the second, `は` has already claimed <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>, so <ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>と can only be accompaniment.

---

## と - with, accompaniment · と as "with" vs と as "and" (cont.)

When there is no subject particle at all - a bare <ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>と<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました - the reading is "with", because a list of things needs a slot in the sentence and this one has none. Adding <ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に removes any doubt.

---

## と - with, accompaniment · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。 → ✅ <ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*A count of people is a circumstance, not a companion. Numbers take `で`.*

- ❌ <ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>と<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。 → ✅ <ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>と<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*The first `と` names the companions; the number that follows still needs `で`.*

- ❌ <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さん**に**<ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>しました。 → ✅ <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さん**と**<ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*Marrying is unavoidably two-sided, so it is `と` only. The `に` version is a very common learner error and sounds strange to a native ear.*

---

## と - with, accompaniment · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんと<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。 → ✅ <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんと<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に is a fixed adverbial phrase and cannot lose its `に`.*

- ❌ <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ています。 → ✅ <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ています。<br>*<ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>る is the mutual-looking verb that takes `に`. Resemblance in Japanese is modelled as one thing pointing at another.*

---

## と - with, accompaniment · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じクラスの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>____<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しました。

{reveal}
<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じクラスの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しました。

{reveal}
A named person doing the activity alongside you is a companion, which is `と`. `で` would be wrong here because <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> is not a count.

---

## と - with, accompaniment · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>には<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>（と・で）<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。

{reveal}
<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>には<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。

{reveal}
<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby> is a number of people, not a person, so it takes `で`. There is no companion to mark - that is the whole point of the sentence.

---

## と - with, accompaniment · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "There's something I'd like to discuss with the manager."

{reveal}
<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>したいことがあります。

{reveal}
<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>する with `と` frames it as a two-sided discussion, which is what "discuss with" means. <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>する is also correct Japanese, but it means taking a problem *to* him for advice.

## と - with, accompaniment · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>、<ruby>高校<rt>こうこう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>しました。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>、<ruby>高校<rt>こうこう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>友達<rt>ともだち</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>しました。 "Last year I married a friend from high school."

{reveal}
<ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>する belongs to the mutual-verb set, where the other party must be `と`. `に` would try to make the friend a recipient, which makes no sense for a marriage.

## と - with, accompaniment · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しました。 (b) <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しました。

{reveal}
(a) is one-directional: you told him something. The information went from you to him, and the sentence says nothing about whether he replied - this is what you say after reporting a problem upward.

{reveal}
(b) is two-sided: the two of you had a conversation. Both people spoke, and the sentence implies some exchange took place. Choosing `と` with a superior can also sound a shade more equal, so `に` is the safer default when you are simply reporting.
