---

# や

**Meaning** · and - but only naming *some* of the things, not all of them

`や` joins nouns into a list the speaker is not finishing. You name two or three representative items and leave the rest implied, the way English does with "eggs, cheese, that sort of thing". If you want the list to be complete, `や` is the wrong particle.

---

## や · Formation

`や` sits **between** nouns. It never attaches to a verb, an adjective or a clause, and the particle that governs the whole list (`が`, `を`, `に`…) goes after the **last** noun only.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (two items) | Noun や Noun | <ruby>卵<rt>たまご</rt></ruby> + チーズ → <ruby>卵<rt>たまご</rt></ruby>やチーズ |
| Noun (three or more) | N や N や N | ペンやノートや<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>しゴム |
| Rounded off with など | N や N など | <ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>や<ruby>果物<rt>くだもの</rt></ruby>など |
| Followed by a case particle | N や N + particle | <ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>や<ruby>椅子<rt>いす</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>運<rt>はこ</rt></ruby>ぶ |
| ❌ Verbs | not possible - use ～たり～たり | ❌<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>むや<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>く |
| ❌ い-adjectives | not possible - use ～くて | ❌<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いやおいしい |

---

## や · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `や` is a *sampler*. Two items on the page, more items in the world. `など` at the end makes that explicit and is very common with `や`.

---

## や · Examples

<ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>卵<rt>たまご</rt></ruby>**や**チーズがあります。<br>*There are eggs, cheese and so on in the fridge.*

<ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>洗濯<rt>せんたく</rt></ruby>**や**<ruby>掃除<rt>そうじ</rt></ruby>をします。<br>*On weekends I do laundry, cleaning, that kind of thing.*

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しの<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>にはさみ**や**のりが<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*There are scissors, glue and suchlike in the drawer.*

<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>**や**<ruby>奈良<rt>なら</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってみたいです。<br>*I'd like to visit Kyoto, Nara and places like that.*

---

## や · Examples (cont.)

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>では<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>**や**<ruby>果物<rt>くだもの</rt></ruby>が<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>く<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>えます。<br>*At this shop you can buy vegetables, fruit and the like cheaply.*

<ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>みには<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>**や**<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かける<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*Over the summer holidays a lot of people head to the sea, the mountains and so on.*

<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんはおにぎり**や**サンドイッチで<ruby>済<rt>す</rt></ruby>ませることが<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い。<br>*I usually make do with a rice ball or a sandwich or something for lunch.*

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しのとき、<ruby>本棚<rt>ほんだな</rt></ruby>**や**<ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>いソファなどの<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>家具<rt>かぐ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>捨<rt>す</rt></ruby>てました。<br>*When I moved I threw out all the big furniture - the bookshelf, the old sofa and so on.*

---

## や · Nuance & register

`や` is register-neutral. It appears in newspapers, in textbooks and in ordinary conversation, and it does not sound stiff or casual either way.

What it *does* carry is a small amount of politeness-by-vagueness. Because `や` declines to close the list, it sounds less like a bureaucratic inventory. Asked what you did on holiday, <ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>や<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました sounds relaxed; the same sentence with `と` sounds like you are filing a report.

**In speech, `とか` does the same job and is much more common.** <ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>とか<ruby>果物<rt>くだもの</rt></ruby>とか<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ってきて - that is what you would actually text a housemate. `とか` is casual, can repeat after the final item, and can (unlike `や`) also hang off verbs. In writing, prefer `や`.

---

## や · Nuance & register (cont.)

**`など` is the natural finisher.** <code><ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>や<ruby>果物<rt>くだもの</rt></ruby>など</code> makes the open-endedness explicit and is standard in written Japanese. In formal documents the same word is often written in kanji as <code><ruby>等<rt>など</rt></ruby></code>; write it in kana at N5.

---

## や · や vs と

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` closes the list; `や` leaves it open. `と` = "A and B, that's everything". `や` = "A and B, among other things".

This is the only difference, and it is a real one - a listener hears it.

かばんの<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>には、さいふ**と**かぎがあります。<br>*In my bag there's a wallet and a key. (Those two things. Nothing else.)*

かばんの<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>には、さいふ**や**かぎがあります。<br>*In my bag there's a wallet, a key, stuff like that. (And other things besides.)*

---

## や · や vs と (cont.)

Two consequences worth remembering. First, when the set really is fixed, `や` is odd: <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>島<rt>しま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>と<ruby>本州<rt>ほんしゅう</rt></ruby>と… - you are enumerating, so use `と`. Second, `や` needs at least two items; ❌<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>やください is not a sentence.

---

## や · や vs とか

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register. `や` is neutral-to-written; `とか` is spoken.

`とか` also repeats after the last item, which `や` cannot do.

コーヒー**とか**お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>**とか**、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む?<br>*Coffee, tea, something - want a drink?*

And `とか` will attach to verbs, where `や` is simply ungrammatical: <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>るとか<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐとか、<ruby>運動<rt>うんどう</rt></ruby>をしたほうがいいよ。

---

## や · や vs ～たり～たり

> **Rule of thumb** - `や` lists **nouns**. To give a non-exhaustive list of **actions**, you need `～たり～たり`.

Learners reach for `や` here because English uses "and" for both. Japanese does not.

<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>は<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>**や**<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*On Sundays I like music, films, that sort of thing. (nouns - や is right)*

<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>は<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>い**たり**<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>**たり**します。<br>*On Sundays I listen to music, watch films, and so on. (actions - たり is required)*

---

## や · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む**や**<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きます。 → ✅ <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ん**だり**<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>い**たり**します。<br>*`や` joins nouns only. A list of activities needs `～たり～たり`.*

- ❌ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るい**や**<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>いです。 → ✅ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>る**くて**<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*Adjectives chain with `くて`／`で`, never with `や`.*

- ❌ ペン**や**ノート**や**を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。 → ✅ ペン**や**ノート**を**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*`や` goes *between* nouns. Nothing follows the last item except the case particle (or `など`).*

- ❌ <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>**や**<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>は<ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>とも<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>は<ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>とも<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>です。<br>*<code><ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>とも</code> says the list is exactly two people, which contradicts the open-endedness of `や`.*

---

## や · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>**など**や<ruby>果物<rt>くだもの</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います。 → ✅ <ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>**や**<ruby>果物<rt>くだもの</rt></ruby>**など**を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います。<br>*`など` closes the whole list, so it comes at the end - not in the middle.*

---

## や · Practice 1

**1.** テーブルの<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>にコップ____おさらがあります。

{reveal}
テーブルの<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>にコップ**や**おさらがあります。

{reveal}
Either `や` or `と` is grammatical here, and they say different things. With `や` the cups and plates are examples of what is on the table; with `と` they are the complete contents. If you can imagine a fork sitting there too, use `や`.

---

## や · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>（や・と）<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>と<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>です。

{reveal}
<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>**と**<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>と<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>です。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>です</code> states the exact size of the set, so the list has to be exhaustive. `や` would promise that there are other family members you have not mentioned, which flatly contradicts the rest of the sentence.

---

## や · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I bought stamps, envelopes and things like that at the post office."

{reveal}
<ruby>郵便局<rt>ゆうびんきょく</rt></ruby>で<ruby>切手<rt>きって</rt></ruby>**や**<ruby>封筒<rt>ふうとう</rt></ruby>（**など**）を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。

{reveal}
"and things like that" is exactly what `や` encodes, so no extra word is needed - though `など` after the final noun makes it unmistakable. Note that `を` attaches once, to the end of the whole list.

---

## や · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>はパンを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べるや<ruby>新聞<rt>しんぶん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みます。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>はパンを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**たり**<ruby>新聞<rt>しんぶん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ん**だり**します。 "In the morning I eat toast, read the paper, and so on."

{reveal}
The two items being listed are *actions*, not nouns, so `や` cannot join them. The non-exhaustive list of actions is `～たり～たり`, and the sentence then ends in `します`.

---

## や · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>はそうじと<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>をしました。 (b) <ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>はそうじや<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>をしました。

{reveal}
(a) presents cleaning and shopping as the whole of Saturday - those two jobs, and that is the account. It sounds like an answer to "what exactly did you do?"

{reveal}
(b) offers them as samples of a busier day: cleaning, shopping, and other errands you are not bothering to list. Japanese speakers reach for (b) far more often in small talk, because pinning the day down to precisely two activities sounds strangely precise.

# ので

**Meaning** · because - presenting the reason as a plain fact, not as your opinion

`ので` links a cause to its result. The half before `ので` is the reason; the half after it is what follows from that reason. It frames the connection as something anyone would accept, which is why it is the polite way to explain yourself when you are asking for indulgence.

---

## ので · Formation

`ので` attaches to a **clause**, not to a word. The trap is nouns and な-adjectives, which need `な` in front of it.

---

## ので · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain) | plain form + ので | <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るので |
| Verb (past) | た-form + ので | <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れた → <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れたので |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + ので | わからない → わからないので |
| い-adjective | plain form + ので | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いので |
| い-adjective (past) | かった + ので | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>かったので |
| な-adjective | ～**な** + ので | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**な**ので |
| Noun | Noun + **な** + ので | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みだ → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>み**な**ので |
| Noun (past) | ～だった + ので | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みだったので |
| Polite (formal) | ます-form + ので | <ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>ります → <ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>りますので |

---

## ので · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - ❌`だので` does not exist. A noun or な-adjective takes `な` before `ので`, and `な` only. The past forms `だったので` are fine, because there the `だ` has already become `だった`.

Politeness lives on the **final** verb of the sentence, not on the `ので` clause: <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いので、まどを<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>め**ます**. The ます-form before `ので` shown in the last row is a separate, extra-formal register - announcements and business email.

---

## ので · Examples

<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い**ので**、コートを<ruby>着<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*It's cold, so I'll put a coat on.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>み**なので**、ゆっくり<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*Tomorrow's a day off, so I'm going to sleep in.*

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れた**ので**、<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いませんでした。<br>*The train was delayed, so I didn't make the meeting.*

<ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**なので**、よく<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*The library is quiet, so I often go there to study.*

---

## ので · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>頭<rt>あたま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>い**ので**、<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>します。<br>*I have a headache, so I'll head off before the rest of you.*

まだ<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>じゃない**ので**、ゆっくり<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>していただけませんか。<br>*My Japanese still isn't very good, so could you speak slowly?*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもがまだ<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さい**ので**、<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>の<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まりには<ruby>参加<rt>さんか</rt></ruby>できません。<br>*My children are still small, so I can't come to evening gatherings.*

<ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>づいております**ので**、<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>めに<ruby>閉店<rt>へいてん</rt></ruby>させていただきます。<br>*A typhoon is approaching, so we will be closing early today.*

---

## ので · Nuance & register

`ので` is polite-leaning but not itself a polite form - you will hear plain <ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>いので<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>る among friends. What it does is present the reason as **objective**: this is how things are, and the conclusion follows by itself.

That objectivity is why `ので` is the excuse-making particle. Cancelling on someone, arriving late, asking to leave early - <ruby>急用<rt>きゅうよう</rt></ruby>ができました**ので** sounds like circumstances have decided, not like you have. Saying the same thing with `から` puts your own will in the frame and can land as "because I say so".

---

## ので · Nuance & register (cont.)

**The contraction is `んで`.** <ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>いんで<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>るね。 This is very common in casual speech and completely normal among friends, but it is markedly informal - it sounds rough in the mouth of a shop assistant and you should not write it. Learners often meet `んで` in dramas long before anyone tells them it is just `ので`.

At the other end, `ますので` (as in the last example above) is service and business register. It is not more grammatical, just more deferential, and it belongs to announcements, apologies and formal email.

---

## ので · Nuance & register (cont.)

One more habit worth copying: `ので` frequently ends a sentence on its own, with the consequence left unsaid. <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>が<ruby>離<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せないので…。 The listener supplies "…so I can't right now", and nobody has had to refuse anything out loud.

---

## ので · ので vs から

> **Rule of thumb** - `から` is *my* reason; `ので` is *the* reason. `から` pushes, `ので` explains.

Both mean "because" and both are correct in most sentences. The difference is whose judgement the reason belongs to.

<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>がある**ので**、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ませていただきます。<br>*I have a fever, so I'll be taking today off. (Circumstances decide; I'm reporting them.)*

<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>がある**から**、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む。<br>*I've got a fever, so I'm taking today off. (My call, stated plainly.)*

Practical consequences:

---

## ので · ので vs から (cont.)

- Apologising or asking a favour of someone senior → `ので`. `から` can sound like<br>*you are pressing your case.*
- Strong personal statements, warnings, invitations, imperatives → `から`.<br>*<ruby>危<rt>あぶ</rt></ruby>ない**から**、<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るな! An imperative with `ので` sounds mismatched, though polite requests with `ので` are completely normal and extremely common: <ruby>危<rt>あぶ</rt></ruby>ないので、<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がってください。*
- Answering the question どうして? → `からです`. ❌`のでです` is not a form.

---

## ので · ので vs のに

> **Rule of thumb** - identical attachment, opposite meaning. `ので` = the result follows; `のに` = the result *fails* to follow, and the speaker is put out about it.

They are one syllable apart and both take `な` after a noun, so they are easy to mix up in production.

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っている**ので**、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>です。<br>*It's raining, so the match is cancelled.*

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っている**のに**、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になりませんでした。<br>*It's raining, and yet the match wasn't cancelled.*

`のに` always carries feeling - surprise, complaint, disappointment. `ので` is emotionally flat.

---

## ので · ので vs the plain て-form

> **Rule of thumb** - the て-form can imply cause, but only when the result is something outside your control. If the second clause is a decision, a request or an invitation, you need `ので`.

<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいた**ので**、タクシーで<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*The roads were busy, so I went by taxi. (a choice - ので)*

<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んで**いて**、なかなか<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>めませんでした。<br>*The roads were busy and we could hardly move. (no choice involved - て is fine)*

So ❌<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くて、まどを<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めてください is wrong; closing a window is a request. ✅<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いので、まどを<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めてください。

---

## ので · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>**だ**ので、<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけません。 → ✅ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>**な**ので、<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけません。<br>*A noun takes `な` before `ので`. The same goes for な-adjectives: ❌<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>だので → ✅<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>なので.*

- ❌ どうして<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかったんですか。 -  - <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しかった**のでです**。 → ✅ <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しかった**からです**。<br>*`ので` connects two clauses; it cannot stand as a predicate. The stand-alone answer form is `からです`.*

- ❌ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がありません**ので**<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>げ! → ✅ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がない**から**<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>げ!<br>*A blunt imperative wants the subjective `から`. `ので` with a barked order sounds like two different people talking.*

---

## ので · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いませんでした**ので**<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れました。 → ✅ <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れました**ので**、<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いませんでした。<br>*The reason comes **first**. Japanese never puts `ので` in front of the cause the way English puts "because" in front of it.*

- ❌ <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>かった**なので**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。 → ✅ <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>かった**ので**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*The `な` belongs only to nouns and な-adjectives in their non-past form. Once a predicate is already `かった` or `だった`, `ので` attaches bare.*

---

## ので · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>____、<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いていません。

{reveal}
<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>**なので**、<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いていません。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby></code> is a noun, so `ので` needs `な` in front of it. ❌<code><ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>だので</code> is the single most common `ので` error, and it comes from thinking of `ので` as attaching to a finished sentence.

---

## ので · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さん、<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>ありません。<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>用事<rt>ようじ</rt></ruby>ができました（ので・から）、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>のパーティーは<ruby>欠席<rt>けっせき</rt></ruby>させていただきます。

{reveal}
…<ruby>用事<rt>ようじ</rt></ruby>ができました**ので**、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>のパーティーは<ruby>欠席<rt>けっせき</rt></ruby>させていただきます。

{reveal}
You are apologising to someone and asking to be excused, so you want the reason to look like circumstance rather than preference. `から` here would foreground your own decision and sound faintly self-justifying.

---

## ので · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The room was noisy, so I couldn't sleep well."

{reveal}
<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>がうるさかった**ので**、よく<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>られませんでした。

{reveal}
うるさい is an い-adjective, so its past form `うるさかった` takes `ので` directly - no `な`. Note also that the whole sentence's tense sits on the final verb; you do not need to mark past twice in any special way.

---

## ので · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さいので、もっと<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きく<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>け。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さい**から**、もっと<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きく<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>け。 Or, keeping `ので`: <ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さいので、もっと<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きく<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください。

{reveal}
The grammar of the `ので` clause is fine; the mismatch is in register. A bare imperative is maximally subjective, and it pairs with the subjective `から`. Swap either half and the sentence settles.

## ので · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がないから、また<ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>にしましょう。 (b) <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がないので、また<ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>にしましょう。

{reveal}
Both propose postponing, and both are perfectly natural. (a) puts the shortage of time forward as the speaker's own read on the situation - it is direct, it is what you say to a friend, and among strangers it can sound slightly brusque.

{reveal}
(b) presents the lack of time as an external fact that has made the decision for both of you. It is the softer, more deferential version, and it is what you would say to a client or a teacher. Nothing about the facts differs; only who is seen to be deciding.

# けど / けれども

**Meaning** · but; although - and, very often, just a soft way to start talking

`けど` joins two clauses where the second is not what the first led you to expect. It is also, in everyday Japanese, the ordinary way to preface a request or a piece of news without landing on it too hard.

---

## けど / けれども · Formation

`けど` clips onto the **end of a finished clause**, plain or polite. Nouns and な-adjectives need `だ` (plain) or `です` (polite) first.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain) | plain form + けど | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くけど |
| Verb (polite) | ます-form + けど | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますけど |
| Verb (past) | た-form + けど | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったけど |
| い-adjective | plain form + けど | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いけど |
| な-adjective | ～**だ** + けど | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>**だ**けど／<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>**です**けど |
| Noun | Noun + **だ** + けど | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**だ**けど／<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**です**けど |
| Negative | ない-form + けど | <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないけど |

---

## けど / けれども · Formation (cont.)

The four spellings are one word at four levels of formality:

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Casual speech | けど | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いけど |
| Slightly softer | けども | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いですけども |
| Neutral / written | けれど | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いけれど |
| Formal / written | けれども | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いけれども |

> **Rule of thumb** - longer means more formal. `けど` in conversation, `けれども` in a written report. `が` is one step more formal again.

---

## けど / けれども · Examples

このケーキは<ruby>甘<rt>あま</rt></ruby>い**けど**、おいしくない。<br>*This cake is sweet but it isn't good.*

<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>は<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しい**けど**、<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しいです。<br>*Japanese is hard, but it's fun.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby><ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>した**けど**、<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なかったね。<br>*I called you yesterday, but you didn't pick up.*

すみません、<ruby>中村<rt>なかむら</rt></ruby>**ですけど**、<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>はいらっしゃいますか。<br>*Excuse me, this is Nakamura - is the department head in?*

---

## けど / けれども · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き**だけど**、<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>すぎるのはちょっとね。<br>*I love the sea in summer, but the crowds are a bit much.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>かった**けれども**、ひと<ruby>月<rt>つき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>壊<rt>こわ</rt></ruby>れてしまった。<br>*The bike I bought last year was cheap, but it broke within a month.*

<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>メールを<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りました**けど**、まだお<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>をいただいていません。<br>*I did send an email once, but I haven't had a reply yet.*

<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>ありません**けれども**、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>欠席<rt>けっせき</rt></ruby>させていただきます。<br>*I'm very sorry, but I'll have to miss tomorrow's meeting.*

---

## けど / けれども · Nuance & register

`けど` is one of the most-used words in spoken Japanese, and most of the time it is not really contrastive at all.

**The trailing `けど`.** A sentence that stops on `けど` is a complete, polite utterance. <ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>いたいことがあるんですけど…。 Nothing has been left out by accident; the `けど` hands the floor to the listener instead of stating your business flatly. Requests, complaints, refusals and first approaches to a stranger nearly all arrive this way, and a learner who never uses trailing `けど` sounds blunt without knowing why.

---

## けど / けれども · Nuance & register (cont.)

**The preface `けど`.** <ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>なんですけど、<ruby>今週<rt>こんしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか。 Here `けど` marks the first clause as background - "about the film" - with no contrast whatsoever. Translating it as "but" gives nonsense; it is closer to English "so,".

**Register.** `けど` is casual but not rude, and it is fine after `です`／`ます`: <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いですけど、いいですか。 is perfectly polite. `けれども` in speech sounds careful and slightly formal; in writing it is unremarkable. `けども` is a spoken middle ground you will hear a lot from shop staff.

`けど` is not gender- or age-marked. What *is* marked is stacking: two `けど` clauses in one sentence sounds rambling, and three sounds like you have lost the thread.

---

## けど / けれども · けど vs が

> **Rule of thumb** - same grammar, different formality. `が` is the written and formal member of the pair; `けど` is the spoken one.

They are interchangeable in structure - both attach to a finished clause, both can be contrastive or merely prefatory, both can trail off at the end.

<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです**が**、<ruby>品質<rt>ひんしつ</rt></ruby>はたしかです。<br>*It is expensive, but the quality is dependable. (a written product description)*

<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い**けど**、ものはいいよ。<br>*Pricey, but it's good stuff. (the same claim, said to a friend)*

---

## けど / けれども · けど vs が (cont.)

Two practical notes. `が` after a plain form (<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いが) reads as literary, not casual - plain form plus `が` belongs to essays, not chat. And in a speech or a formal email, `が` is the safe default.

---

## けど / けれども · けど vs でも

> **Rule of thumb** - `けど` goes at the **end** of the first clause. `でも` goes at the **start** of the second sentence. They are never interchangeable in position.

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>い**けど**、<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るいです。<br>*This room is small, but it's bright. (one sentence)*

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>いです。**でも**、<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るいです。<br>*This room is small. But it's bright. (two sentences)*

---

## けど / けれども · けど vs でも (cont.)

This is the mistake English speakers make most, because English "but" sits between the two halves and so *looks* like `でも`. It is not: ❌<ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>いでも is ungrammatical. If the word comes after the first clause, it must be `けど`.

---

## けど / けれども · けど vs のに

> **Rule of thumb** - `のに` is `けど` plus a grievance. Use `のに` only when the outcome genuinely disappoints or surprises the speaker.

<ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>った**けど**、<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかった。<br>*I waited three hours, but he didn't come.*

<ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>った**のに**、<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかった。<br>*I waited three whole hours and he still didn't come! (and I'm annoyed)*

---

## けど / けれども · けど vs のに (cont.)

Because `のに` carries emotion, it is wrong where you are simply setting two facts side by side: ❌<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>低<rt>ひく</rt></ruby>いのに、<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです。 Nobody's expectation has been thwarted by how tall someone's brother is, so this needs `けど`.

---

## けど / けれども · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>けど、<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。 → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**だ**けど、<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*A noun cannot sit bare in front of `けど`. Insert `だ` (plain) or `です` (polite). Same for な-adjectives: ❌<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かけど → ✅<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**だ**けど.*

- ❌ <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いでも、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います。 → ✅ <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い**けど**、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います。<br>*`でも` cannot follow a clause. It opens a new sentence: <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いです。**でも**、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います。*

- ❌ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っているけど、<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っているけど、ぬれました。 → ✅ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っていて<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていたけど、ぬれました。<br>*One `けど` per sentence. Chaining them reads as unfinished thinking.*

---

## けど / けれども · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いけれども。 as a full written sentence → ✅ <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いけれども、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いませんでした。<br>*Trailing off is a *spoken* move. In writing, finish the sentence - a dangling `けれども` on the page just looks truncated.*

- ❌ けど、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>けません。 (at the start of a sentence) → ✅ でも、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>けません。<br>*`けど` is a clause-final connective. To begin a sentence with "but", use `でも`, `しかし`, or `ですが`.*

---

## けど / けれども · Practice 1

**1.** この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>____、<ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いです。

{reveal}
この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>**だけど**（polite: **ですけど**）、<ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いです。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby></code> is a な-adjective, so it cannot touch `けど` directly - you need `だ` or `です` between them. ❌<code><ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>けど</code> and ❌<code><ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>なけど</code> are both wrong.

---

## けど / けれども · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がなかったです。（けど・でも）、<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりました。

{reveal}
<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がなかったです。**でも**、<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりました。

{reveal}
The first clause has already been closed with `です` and a full stop, so the connector is starting a new sentence - that is `でも`'s job. `けど` would have had to attach inside the first sentence: <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がなかったけど、<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりました。

---

## けど / けれども · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I've read the book, but I don't really understand it."

{reveal}
その<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みました**けど**、あまりわかりません。

{reveal}
Polite `ました` plus `けど` is a completely normal combination - `けど` does not drag the register down. In casual speech: <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んだ**けど**、あまりわからない。

---

## けど / けれども · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: すみません、<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きたいでも、いいですか。

{reveal}
✅ すみません、<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きたいん**ですけど**、いいですか。

{reveal}
`でも` cannot follow a clause, and here the speaker is not contrasting anything anyway - this is the prefatory `けど` that softens an approach to a stranger. The `んですけど` shape is the standard opening for asking a favour.

---

## けど / けれども · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>したけど、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けました。 (b) <ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>したのに、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けました。

{reveal}

{reveal}

# ～てから

**Meaning** · after doing X - and X has to happen first for Y to be possible

`～てから` puts two actions in order and says the first one *clears the way* for the second. It is not a neutral timestamp: the reason you mention washing your hands before eating is that eating comes after washing, and should.

---

## ～てから · Formation

`から` clips onto the plain **て-form**. It never conjugates and never carries tense - the tense of the whole sentence sits on the final verb.

---

## ～てから · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + から | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてから |
| Verb (voiced て-form) | で-form + から | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ん**で**から |
| する | して + から | <ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>してから |
| <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る | <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>て + から | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てから |
| Tense of the sentence | on the **final** verb only | <ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ってから<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**ました** |
| Politeness | on the **final** verb only | <ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ってから<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**ます** |
| ❌ Noun | not possible - use Noun + のあとで | ❌<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>のてから |
| ❌ Negative | ❌ないでから does not exist - rephrase | ❌<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ないでから |

---

## ～てから · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the verb in front of `から` is *always* bare て-form. ❌<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りますてから, ❌<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ったから and ❌<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>るから all mean something else or nothing at all.

> **Note** - `～てから` has a second, closely related job: measuring elapsed time from a starting point. <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てから３<ruby>年<rt>ねん</rt></ruby>になります - "it's been three years since I came to Japan." Same form, and the logic is the same (everything after that point rests on it), but here the second clause is a duration rather than an action.

---

## ～てから · Examples

<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>っ**てから**、ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます。<br>*I'll wash my hands and then eat.*

<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>をし**てから**、テレビを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>なさい。<br>*Do your homework first, then watch television.*

お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れ**てから**、ボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Put the money in first, then press the button.*

<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>脱<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>い**でから**、<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がってください。<br>*Please take your shoes off before you come up.*

## ～てから · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>**てから**、３<ruby>年<rt>ねん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>経<rt>た</rt></ruby>ちました。<br>*Three years have passed since I came to Japan.*

<ruby>説明書<rt>せつめいしょ</rt></ruby>をよく<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ん**でから**、<ruby>組<rt>く</rt></ruby>み<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ててください。<br>*Read the instructions carefully before you assemble it.*

<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>し**てから**、ずっと<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*I've worked at the same company ever since I graduated.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>していただい**てから**、<ruby>皆<rt>みな</rt></ruby>さんにお<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りします。<br>*I'll send the meeting materials round once the department head has checked them.*

## ～てから · Nuance & register

`～てから` is register-neutral. It has no casual contraction and no formal variant; the politeness of the sentence is decided entirely by the verb at the end.

What it does carry is a faint sense of **order being observed**. That makes it the natural form for instructions, rules and procedures - machine displays, recipes, safety notices, and parents talking to children all use it. ボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>してから、レバーを<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>げてください reads as a step-by-step procedure in a way that the plain て-form does not.

---

## ～てから · Nuance & register (cont.)

The flip side: because `てから` insists on the ordering, using it for two things that merely happened to follow one another sounds fussy. <ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きてから<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けて、<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ってから<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>着<rt>き</rt></ruby>て… - nobody narrates a morning like that. **One `てから` per sentence** is the practical limit; when you need a longer chain, use plain て-forms and reserve `てから` for the one step that genuinely gates the rest.

For the elapsed-time use (<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てから３<ruby>年<rt>ねん</rt></ruby>), the second half is very often `なります`, <code><ruby>経<rt>た</rt></ruby>ちます</code> or a state - not an action.

---

## ～てから · ～てから vs ～たあとで

> **Rule of thumb** - `てから` says the first action is a **precondition**; `たあとで` just says it came **earlier**. Both translate as "after", and only `てから` claims a connection between the two.

くすりを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>ん**でから**、<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*I took the medicine and then went to bed. (I went to bed on the strength of having taken it.)*

くすりを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>ん**だあとで**、<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*I went to bed after taking the medicine. (Simple sequence; the two are not necessarily linked.)*

Three consequences you can act on:

---

## ～てから · ～てから vs ～たあとで (cont.)

- If the second action **depends** on the first - permission, readiness,<br>*eligibility - `てから` is the right one: <ruby>十八歳<rt>じゅうはっさい</rt></ruby>になっ**てから**<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>できます。*
- If you want a bare timeline, or the two events have nothing to do with each<br>*other, `たあとで` is safer.*
- Only `てから` measures elapsed time. ❌<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たあとで３<ruby>年<rt>ねん</rt></ruby>になります is wrong;<br>*<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てから３<ruby>年<rt>ねん</rt></ruby>になります is right.*

Note that the forms differ too: `てから` takes the **て-form**, `たあとで` takes the **た-form**.

---

## ～てから · ～てから vs the plain て-form

> **Rule of thumb** - plain `～て` says "and then", loosely. `～てから` says "and not until then".

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>い**て**、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*I walked to the station and got on the train. (ordinary narration)*

きっぷを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>っ**てから**、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*Buy a ticket before boarding the train. (you may not board until you have)*

Both are correct Japanese in most contexts, so the choice is about emphasis. Where the て-form is genuinely wrong is when the gating matters and the listener might miss it - a rule, a warning, an instruction.

---

## ～てから · ～てから vs ～前に

> **Rule of thumb** - same two events, opposite anchor. `AてからB` puts A first and looks forward; `BまえにA` puts B first and looks back. Watch the form: `てから` wants the **て-form**, <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> wants the **dictionary form**.

<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>っ**てから**、<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めます。<br>*I'll wash my hands, and then start cooking.*

<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>います。<br>*Before I start cooking, I'll wash my hands.*

---

## ～てから · ～てから vs ～前に (cont.)

The facts are identical. What differs is which action is the topic of the sentence: with `てから` you are talking about the washing, with <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> you are talking about the cooking.

---

## ～てから · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる**から**、<ruby>歯<rt>は</rt></ruby>をみがきます。 → ✅ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**てから**、<ruby>歯<rt>は</rt></ruby>をみがきます。<br>*Dictionary form + `から` is "because", not "after". The sequence meaning needs the て-form, and dropping the `て` changes the sentence entirely.*

- ❌ うちへ<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>り**ますてから**、<ruby>晩<rt>ばん</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ります。 → ✅ うちへ<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>っ**てから**、<ruby>晩<rt>ばん</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*Politeness belongs to the last verb only. The `てから` clause is always plain.*

- ❌ <ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>**のてから**、<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。 → ✅ <ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>**のあとで**、<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*`てから` attaches to verbs, never to nouns. For a noun you need `のあとで`.*

---

## ～てから · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>**ないでから**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。 → ✅ <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>**ないで**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*There is no negative `てから`. "Without doing X" is `～ないで`; "not until X" is handled by rephrasing in the positive.*

- ❌ <ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きてから<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ってから<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>着<rt>き</rt></ruby>てから<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけました。 → ✅ <ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きて<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>って、<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>着<rt>き</rt></ruby>てから<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけました。<br>*Chained `てから` sounds mechanical. Use plain て-forms for the run of events and keep `てから` for the one step that actually gates what follows.*

{reveal}

{reveal}

## ～てから · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: シャワーを<ruby>浴<rt>あ</rt></ruby>びるてから、<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ます。

## ～てから · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) お<ruby>風呂<rt>ふろ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってから、ビールを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。 (b) お<ruby>風呂<rt>ふろ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ったあとで、ビールを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。

# ～前に

**Meaning** · before doing X - X hasn't happened yet at the moment Y takes place

<code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> puts one event in front of another on the timeline. The whole sentence can be past, present or future; the clause in front of <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> is always in the **dictionary form**, because from the viewpoint of the main clause that event still lies ahead.

---

| Verb | **dictionary form** + <ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に | <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>る<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に |
| Verb (in a past sentence) | still **dictionary form** | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>い**ました** |
| Verb (in a future sentence) | still **dictionary form** | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>い**ます** |
| Noun | Noun + **の** + <ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に | <ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby>**の**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に |
| Duration ("ago") | Quantity + <ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に - **no** の | <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>一週間<rt>いっしゅうかん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に |

## ～前に · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> takes the **dictionary form**, and its mirror image `～たあとで` takes the **past form**. That asymmetry is not a quirk of memorisation: "before eating" is a moment when the eating has not happened yet, "after eating" is a moment when it has. Japanese marks that literally. ❌<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に and ❌<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べるあとで are both wrong, and for the same reason.

> **Note** - the `に` can drop when the phrase is doing adverbial duty at the head of a sentence: <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>る<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みます。 Keep the `に` while you are learning; it is never wrong.

## ～前に · Nuance & register

<code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> is register-neutral and has no contracted form. It appears in signs, in manuals and in ordinary chat without changing its shape.

The one thing to internalise is that **the dictionary form here carries no tense**. Look at the fifth example again: the studying is finished and gone, the main verb is <code><ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>いました</code>, and yet the clause still reads <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に. A learner who translates "before I came" word for word produces ❌<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に every time. The dictionary form is not "present tense" - it is simply the unmarked form the construction demands.

Two related patterns worth recognising when you meet them:

---

## ～前に · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **A bare <ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby> after a quantity means "ago".** <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>しました - "I called three days ago." No `の`, and the sentence is<br>*about the past, not about an ordering.*
- **<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby> also means "in front of" in space**, and then it takes `で` or `に`<br>*according to the usual place rules: <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っています。 Same kanji, entirely different job.*

In more formal writing you will meet <code><ruby>以前<rt>いぜん</rt></ruby>に</code> and <code>～に<ruby>先立<rt>さきだ</rt></ruby>ち</code> doing similar work. Neither is expected at N5.

---

## ～前に · ～前に vs ～たあとで

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> demands the **dictionary form**; `あとで` demands the **た-form**. Neither one ever changes to match the tense of the sentence.

This is the single most useful table on the page, so here it is as a pair:

ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みました。<br>*I took the medicine before eating.*

ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**たあとで**、<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みました。<br>*I took the medicine after eating.*

---

## ～前に · ～前に vs ～たあとで (cont.)

Both main clauses are past. Only the first clause differs, and it differs in form, not in tense: <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> versus <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたあとで</code>. Learners routinely write ❌<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> by copying the English past tense across. Japanese does not.

---

## ～前に · 前に vs 前で

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> is **time**; <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>で</code> is **place**, and it means physically in front of something.

<ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*I asked a question before the lesson.*

<ruby>教室<rt>きょうしつ</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*I asked a question at the front of the classroom.*

---

## ～前に · 前に vs 前で (cont.)

The two are told apart by the noun as much as by the particle: a *time* noun (<ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>) makes it temporal, a *place* noun (<ruby>教室<rt>きょうしつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>、<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>) makes it spatial. With a place noun and a verb of existence you get <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> again, but locationally: <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>があります。

---

## ～前に · ～前に vs ～までに

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> orders two events; `までに` sets a **deadline**. If English would say "by", you want `までに`.

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まる**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>配<rt>くば</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*I'll hand out the documents before the meeting starts.*

<ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>**までに**、<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Please submit the documents by Friday.*

---

## ～前に · ～前に vs ～までに (cont.)

The difference bites with a fixed time. <ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ります sounds like you are arranging your afternoon around five o'clock; <ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ります is a promise to be back no later than five. For appointments and obligations, `までに`.

---

## ～前に · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>**た**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>しました。 → ✅ <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>**る**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*Always the dictionary form, no matter how far in the past the sentence is. The English "before I went to bed" is what leads you astray.*

- ❌ <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>**の**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きました。 → ✅ <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*A duration takes no `の`. The `の` appears only when <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> follows an ordinary noun: <ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に.*

- ❌ <ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>います。 → ✅ <ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby>**の**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>います。<br>*The mirror error: an ordinary noun does need `の`. (Some fixed compounds such as <ruby>食前<rt>しょくぜん</rt></ruby> exist, but they are separate words with their own readings.)*

---

## ～前に · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っています。 → ✅ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*Waiting is an action, and actions happen `で`. `に` in front of <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby></code> marks location only with existence verbs.*

- ❌ <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れない<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に、メモしておきます。 → ✅ <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れない**うちに**、メモしておきます。<br>*There is no negative <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code>. "While it hasn't yet happened" is `～ないうちに`.*

# ～たあとで

**Meaning** · after X - plain chronological order, with no claim that X caused Y

`～たあとで` places one event later than another on the timeline and says nothing else about them. It is the neutral "after": useful precisely because it does not imply that the first thing had to happen for the second to be possible.

---

## ～たあとで · Formation

The verb in front of `あとで` is in the **た-form**, and it stays in the た-form whether the sentence is about the past, the present or the future.

---

## ～たあとで · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | **た-form** + あとで | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたあとで |
| Verb (voiced た-form) | だ-form + あとで | <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>ん**だ**あとで |
| Verb (in a future sentence) | still **た-form** | <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったあとで<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ます** |
| Noun | Noun + **の** + あとで | <ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby> → <ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>**の**あとで |
| Without で | た-form + あと | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたあと、コーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む |
| In kanji | あと = <ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby> | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で |
| Politeness | on the **final** verb only | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたあとで<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み**ます** |
| ❌ Negative | ❌なかったあとで does not exist | ❌<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>なかったあとで |

---

## ～たあとで · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `あとで` takes the **た-form**; its mirror image <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> takes the **dictionary form**. ❌<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べるあとで and ❌<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に are the two halves of the same mistake. The logic: after eating, the eating is done (た); before eating, it is not (dictionary).

> **Note** - the `で` is optional and drops freely, especially in writing. <ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったあと、<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>しました is completely natural. There is also `あとに`, but at N5 treat it as a different word: it is about a thing being left *in the place of* another, not about time.

---

## ～たあとで · Examples

ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**たあとで**、コーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*I have coffee after eating.*

<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>**のあとで**、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*I'm going shopping after work.*

<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>**たあとで**、みんなで<ruby>感想<rt>かんそう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*After the film we all talked about what we thought of it.*

<ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**たあと**、<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*I asked the teacher a question after the lesson finished.*

---

## ～たあとで · Examples (cont.)

シャワーを<ruby>浴<rt>あ</rt></ruby>び**たあとで**、すぐに<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てしまった。<br>*I fell straight asleep after taking a shower.*

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>がやん**だあとで**、<ruby>空<rt>そら</rt></ruby>に<ruby>虹<rt>にじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*A rainbow appeared in the sky after the rain stopped.*

<ruby>夕<rt>ゆう</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>っ**たあとで**、<ruby>台所<rt>だいどころ</rt></ruby>をきれいに<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けた。<br>*After making dinner I tidied the kitchen up properly.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**たあとで**、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しお<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>をいただけませんか。<br>*Could I have a few minutes of your time once the meeting is over?*

---

## ～たあとで · Nuance & register

`たあとで` is register-neutral and does not contract. Whether the sentence is polite is settled entirely by the final verb, exactly as with <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code>.

Its defining quality is **neutrality**. It reports a sequence and stops there. If you want to say that the first event made the second possible, or that you were waiting for it, `たあとで` is too flat and you want `てから` instead. This is why `たあとで` feels slightly odd in instructions: a sign that read <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ったあとで<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてください is grammatical but sounds like it is describing your habits rather than telling you what to do.

Two smaller habits:

---

## ～たあとで · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby> written in kanji is common in adult writing**, and the reading is<br>*あと, not うしろ. Same character, two words: <ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で = "later, afterwards"; <ruby>後<rt>うし</rt></ruby>ろ = "behind". A learner who reads them the wrong way round produces sentences about standing behind a meeting.*
- **`あとで` also stands alone as an adverb meaning "later"**, with no<br>*clause in front of it at all: あとで<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>するね。 That usage is extremely common in speech and is not the same grammar point, though it is obviously the same word.*

---

## ～たあとで · ～たあとで vs ～てから

> **Rule of thumb** - `たあとで` reports the order; `てから` claims the first event was a **precondition** for the second. If you could insert "and only then" in English, use `てから`.

<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>をし**たあとで**、<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちに<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*I phoned my friend after doing my homework. (What I did, in order.)*

<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>をし**てから**、<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちに<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*I did my homework first, and then phoned my friend. (The homework had to come first.)*

Three practical splits:

---

## ～たあとで · ～たあとで vs ～てから (cont.)

- Rules, instructions and permissions want `てから`, because the whole point is<br>*the gate: お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>を<ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>ってから<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってください。*
- Elapsed time takes `てから` only: <ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>してから<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby>になります。<br>*❌<ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>したあとで<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby>になります is wrong.*
- A noun can only take `のあとで`. There is no ❌<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>のてから.

And do not miss the form difference: `てから` needs the **て-form**, `たあとで` the **た-form**.

---

## ～たあとで · ～たあとで vs ～前に

> **Rule of thumb** - mirror images, and the mirror includes the verb form. `たあとで` = **た-form**; <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> = **dictionary form**.

Put the two side by side on the same pair of events and the pattern is obvious.

<ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**たあとで**、<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>をとりました。<br>*We took photos after the party ended.*

<ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>をとりました。<br>*We took photos before the party ended.*

---

## ～たあとで · ～たあとで vs ～前に (cont.)

Both main clauses are past, and neither first clause is marked for tense at all - <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わった</code> and <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> are chosen by the *construction*, not by when things happened. English speakers reliably write ❌<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わった<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に because English says "before it ended". Japanese never does.

There is one more asymmetry worth noticing: a noun takes `の` with both (<code><ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>のあとで</code>, <code><ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code>), so the noun pattern gives you no clue about which verb form to use. Learn the verb forms as a pair.

---

## ～たあとで · <code>あとで</code> on its own vs ～たあとで

> **Rule of thumb** - with a clause in front, `あとで` means "after [that event]". With nothing in front, it means simply "later on".

<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**たあとで**、<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>します。<br>*I'll reply after I finish the phone call.*

**あとで**<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>します。<br>*I'll reply later.*

---

## ～たあとで · <code>あとで</code> on its own vs ～たあとで (cont.)

The second sentence names no reference point at all - it just pushes the action into the unspecified future, which is why it is a favourite for putting things off. Note that <ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>でね。 on its own ("see you later", "in a bit") is a complete utterance in casual speech.

---

## ～たあとで · Common mistakes

- ❌ ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**る**あとで、<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。 → ✅ ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**た**あとで、<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*`あとで` always takes the た-form, even when the whole sentence is about the future. Nothing here is being said about *when* - only about order.*

- ❌ <ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>あとで<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>します。 → ✅ <ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>**の**あとで<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>します。<br>*A noun needs `の` before `あとで`. Only a verb can attach directly, and only in the た-form.*

- ❌ <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>後<rt>うし</rt></ruby>ろで<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しましょう。 → ✅ <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しましょう。<br>*<code><ruby>後<rt>うし</rt></ruby>ろ</code> is a place word meaning "behind". The time word is <code><ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby></code>, and it is written without the ろ.*

---

## ～たあとで · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだあとで、<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>してもいいです。 → ✅ <ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>ん**でから**、<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>してもいいです。<br>*Not a grammar error, a nuance error. Permission depends on the first event, so the sentence wants the precondition marker `てから`.*

- ❌ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たあとで、３<ruby>年<rt>ねん</rt></ruby>になります。 → ✅ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>**てから**、３<ruby>年<rt>ねん</rt></ruby>になります。<br>*Only `てから` can measure elapsed time from a starting point. `たあとで` needs a second *event*, not a duration.*

## ～たあとで · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ったあとで、パンを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた。 (b) <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ってから、パンを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた。

{reveal}
(a) is a report: two things happened, in that order. It would be a fine sentence in a diary, and it carries no suggestion that the hand-washing mattered.

{reveal}
(b) says the washing came first *because it should* - the bread was not going to be touched until the hands were clean. Parents, food-safety notices and anyone explaining a rule use (b). Same events, but (b) adds a reason for the order that (a) leaves out entirely.
