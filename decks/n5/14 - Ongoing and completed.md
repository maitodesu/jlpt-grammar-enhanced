# N5 - Ongoing and completed

- ～ている - is doing; is in a state
- まだ - still; not yet
- まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet
- もう - already; anymore
- ～たことがある

---

# ～ている - is doing; is in a state

**Meaning** · the action is going on now - or its result is still with us

`～ている` is not the English present progressive, and treating it as one is the single most expensive mistake in beginner Japanese. It says that something connected to the verb is **true right now**. Whether that something is the action still running, or the state left behind after the action finished, is decided by the verb itself - not by `ている`.

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · Formation

`いる` is a る-verb, so it conjugates normally after the て-form.

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| う-verb (て) | て-form + いる | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ → <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っている |
| う-verb (で) | で-form + いる | <ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>ぶ → <ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>んでいる |
| る-verb | て-form + いる | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べている |
| する | している | <ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>している |
| <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る | きている | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ている |
| Polite | ています | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べています |
| Negative | ていない／ていません | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べていません |
| Past | ていた／ていました | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べていました |
| Casual contraction | ている → てる | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてる |

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Honorific / humble | ていらっしゃる／ております | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っていらっしゃる |

> **Rule of thumb** - verbs that take time (<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる</code>, <code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む</code>, <code><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>る</code>) give **ongoing action**. Verbs that happen in an instant and change something (<code><ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>する</code>, <code><ruby>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>ぬ</code>, <code><ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>く</code>, <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る</code>) give **resulting state**. Same `ている`, two readings, and the verb decides.

There is a third, quieter use - **habitual action** (<code><ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っています</code>) - and a fourth for accumulated record (<code><ruby>三回<rt>さんかい</rt></ruby><ruby>優勝<rt>ゆうしょう</rt></ruby>している</code>, "has won three times"). Both are common; the first is essential at N5.

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもたちが<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>んでいる**。<br>*The children are playing in the park.*

あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っています**か。<br>*Do you know that person's name?*

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんはめがねを**かけています**。<br>*Tanaka wears glasses.*

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いています**よ。<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めましょうか。<br>*The window's open. Shall I shut it?*

<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きて、<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>くの<ruby>川<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>まで**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っています**。<br>*Every morning I get up at six and run down to the river nearby.*

<ruby>庭<rt>にわ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>虫<rt>むし</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>んでいた**ので、<ruby>土<rt>つち</rt></ruby>にうめてやった。<br>*There was a dead insect in the garden, so I buried it.*

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>混<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいる**から、<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>くのが<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>くなりそうです。<br>*The roads are busy, so I'll probably arrive a little late.*

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · Nuance & register

**`ている` is where Japanese talks about the present.** The dictionary form does *not* mean "now": <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる</code> is "will eat" or "eats (habitually)", never "is eating". If something is happening as you speak, `ている` is not optional.

**`てる` is the normal spoken shape.** <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>してるの？</code>, <code><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってるよ</code>, <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>ってた？</code> - the `い` drops in casual speech, including in `ていた` → `てた`. It is informal but extremely common; keep the full form in writing, in `ます` speech and in the exam.

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · Nuance & register (cont.)

**States you would never think of as verbs.** Japanese uses `ている` for a large class of descriptions that English handles with adjectives or the simple present: <code><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます</code> (live), <code><ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っています</code> (have), <code><ruby>勤<rt>つと</rt></ruby>めています</code> (work for), <code><ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ている</code> (resemble), <code><ruby>太<rt>ふと</rt></ruby>っている</code> (be heavy-set), <code><ruby>曲<rt>ま</rt></ruby>がっている</code> (be bent). Several of these are *only* natural in `ている` form - you cannot describe a present resemblance with ❌<code><ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>る</code>.

**Politeness.** Honorific speech gives `～ていらっしゃる` and `～ておられる`; humble speech gives `～ております` (<code><ruby>承知<rt>しょうち</rt></ruby>しております</code>). These are N4 and beyond, but you will hear `ております` on the phone from day one.

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · Nuance & register (cont.)

**The negative is where the special cases hide.** <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べていません</code> means "hasn't eaten (yet)" - the action has not started or is not underway. But some state verbs refuse the negative `ている` altogether, and <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る</code> is the famous one: see below.

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · Ongoing action vs resulting state

> **Rule of thumb** - ask how long the verb takes. If the action itself has duration, `ている` catches it mid-flow. If the action is over in an instant and leaves a changed world, `ている` describes the change that is still there.

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いています**。<br>*My little brother is writing a letter right now. (still writing)*

おじいさんは<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>**<ruby>亡<rt>な</rt></ruby>くなっています**。<br>*My grandfather passed away last year. (and is dead now)*

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · Ongoing action vs resulting state (cont.)

The second is not "is dying". <code><ruby>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>ぬ</code> and <code><ruby>亡<rt>な</rt></ruby>くなる</code> take no time at all, so there is no flow for `ている` to catch - only the state afterwards. The same logic explains <code><ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>している</code> (is married, not "is getting married"), <code><ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ている</code> (is here, having come), <code>ドアが<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>まっている</code> (the door is shut) and <code><ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えている</code> (remembers).

<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>が**ついています**。<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>かいるのかもしれません。<br>*The light is on. Someone might be in.*

A useful test: if the English translation is an adjective or "is/has been", you are looking at a resulting state.

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · ～ている vs the dictionary form

> **Rule of thumb** - `ている` = now. Dictionary form = will, or generally does. The overlap English speakers expect between "I eat" and "I'm eating" does not exist here.

<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>新聞<rt>しんぶん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*I read the newspaper every day. (habit - plain present is fine)*

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、<ruby>新聞<rt>しんぶん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいます**。<br>*I'm reading the newspaper right now.*

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · ～ている vs the dictionary form (cont.)

Habitual actions can take either form, with a difference of framing: `ています` presents the habit as a current, ongoing arrangement of your life - <code><ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています</code> is how you say what you do for a living, and <code><ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>きます</code> would sound like a plan for tomorrow.

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · 知っている vs 知らない

> **Rule of thumb** - "I know" is <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っています</code>. "I don't know" is <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>りません</code>. There is no ❌<code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っていません</code> in ordinary use.

その<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>なら**<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っています**。<br>*Yes, I know about that.*

すみません、<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>りません。<br>*Sorry, I don't know.*

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · 知っている vs 知らない (cont.)

<code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る</code> means "to come to know", so <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている</code> is the state of having come to know - and its opposite is simply never having done so, which is <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない</code>. Answering a question with ❌<code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っていません</code> marks you immediately as a learner; answering with a flat <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>りません</code> can sound curt, so soften it: <code>ちょっと<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません</code>, <code>すみません、<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないんです</code>.

<code><ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かる</code> behaves differently again: it is already a state, so <code><ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かります</code>／<code><ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません</code> need no `ている` at all.

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · Common mistakes

- ❌ その<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っていません。 → ✅ その<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>りません。<br>*The negative of <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている</code> is <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない</code>. This one is worth memorising as a pair rather than as a rule.*

- ❌ <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます。（intending "I'm eating lunch now"）→ ✅ <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べています。<br>*The plain present is future or habitual. It cannot describe what is happening as you speak.*

- ❌ <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>します。（intending "my brother is married"）→ ✅ <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>しています。<br>*Without `ている` you have announced a wedding that has not happened yet.*

- ❌ <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>みます。 → ✅ <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいます。<br>*Living somewhere is a state. The dictionary form would mean you are about to move there.*

---

## ～ている - is doing; is in a state · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちています。 → ✅ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*<code><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ</code> is a `つ` verb, so its て-form is <code><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>って</code>. `ている` never attaches to the ます-stem.*

# まだ - still; not yet

**Meaning** · still (it hasn't changed) - or, with a negative, not yet (it hasn't happened)

`まだ` marks a situation as **unchanged**. If the sentence is positive, what has not changed is a state that continues: *still*. If the sentence is negative, what has not changed is an event everyone is waiting for: *not yet*. One word, one idea, two English translations.

---

## まだ - still; not yet · Usage

`まだ` is an adverb. It does not conjugate and it attaches to nothing - you simply put it before the part of the sentence it applies to, usually near the front.

With an **affirmative** predicate it means *still*: <code>まだ<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っています</code> (still raining), <code>まだ<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもです</code> (still a child), <code>まだ<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい</code> (still new). Verbs, い-adjectives, な-adjectives and nouns all take it equally.

With a **negative** predicate it means *not yet*: <code>まだ<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>まっていません</code> (not decided yet), <code>まだ<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ません</code> (hasn't come yet). The most important pattern of this kind is `まだ～ていません`, which has a page of its own.

---

## まだ - still; not yet · Usage (cont.)

`まだ` also stands alone as a complete answer. To <code>もう<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べましたか</code> you can reply simply `まだです`, or casually `まだ`. And doubled, `まだまだ` means "nowhere near yet": <code><ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>はまだまだです</code>.

One extra use worth knowing: before a quantity or a time, `まだ` means *only* - <code>まだ<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>ですよ</code> ("it's only three"), <code>まだ<ruby>半分<rt>はんぶん</rt></ruby><ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>っている</code> ("there's still half left"). The nuance is that there is more of it than the listener assumed.

---

## まだ - still; not yet · Examples

**まだ**<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がありますよ。<br>*There's still time.*

<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>は**まだ**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ています。<br>*My father is still asleep.*

バスが**まだ**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ません。<br>*The bus still hasn't come.*

<ruby>駅前<rt>えきまえ</rt></ruby>のパン<ruby>屋<rt>や</rt></ruby>、**まだ**やっているかな。もう<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>だけど。<br>*I wonder if the bakery by the station is still open - it's gone nine, though.*

---

## まだ - still; not yet · Nuance & register

`まだ` is register-neutral. It sits comfortably in a business email and in a text message to a friend, and it carries no politeness of its own - the ending of the sentence does that work.

What it does carry is a faint sense of **expectation not yet met**, and that is where tone creeps in. A bare `まだ？` to someone who is running late is impatient in any language. The polite answer `まだです` is neutral, but as a *question* - `まだですか` - it presses. Aimed upward, replace it with something that does not ask the person to account for the delay: <code><ruby>恐<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>りますが、いかがでしょうか</code>.

In the other direction, `まだまだ` is a standard humility formula. Told that your Japanese is good, `いえいえ、まだまだです` is the expected reply - literally "not by a long way yet", and understood as modesty rather than self-criticism.

---

## まだ - still; not yet · まだ vs もう

> **Rule of thumb** - `まだ` says nothing has changed; `もう` says the change has already happened. They divide the timeline at the moment of change, and they are each other's natural opposite in both positive and negative sentences.

<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>は**まだ**<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るいですね。<br>*It's still light outside.*

<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>いですね。<br>*It's already dark outside.*

The negative pairing is the one to watch, because the English flips too:

---

## まだ - still; not yet · まだ vs もう (cont.)

<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は**まだ**<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いていません。<br>*My brother isn't working yet. (he will - he's still studying)*

<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いていません。<br>*My brother isn't working any more. (he has retired or quit)*

`まだ` + negative looks forward to something expected; `もう` + negative looks back at something finished. Choosing the wrong one does not produce a slightly odd sentence - it produces the opposite fact.

---

## まだ - still; not yet · Common mistakes

- ❌ **まだ**<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べませんでした。 → ✅ **まだ**<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べていません。<br>*`ませんでした` closes the door on the event: it did not happen and that is that. `まだ` means it is still coming, so it needs the `ていません` form.*

- ❌ レポートは**まだ**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりました。 → ✅ レポートは**もう**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりました。<br>*`まだ` cannot introduce a completed event. Once something is done, the adverb you want is `もう`.*

- ❌ もう**まだ**<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いていません。 → ✅ **まだ**<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いていません。<br>*`もう` and `まだ` are opposites and do not stack. Pick one.*

---

## まだ - still; not yet · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、あの<ruby>件<rt>けん</rt></ruby>、**まだ**ですか。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、あの<ruby>件<rt>けん</rt></ruby>はいかがでしょうか。<br>*Grammatical, but it asks a superior why they have not finished. Ask about the status instead of the delay.*

---

{reveal}

## まだ - still; not yet · Practice 3

{reveal}

# まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet

**Meaning** · I haven't done it yet - and it is still on the list

This is the standard answer to `もう～ましたか`. The two halves each do a job: `まだ` says the situation is unchanged, and `～ていません` says the action has not reached completion. Together they describe something that has not happened **and is still expected to** - which is why "yet" is such a good translation and "didn't" is such a bad one.

---

## まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | まだ + て-form + いません | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → まだ<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べていません |
| Plain style | まだ + て-form + いない | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → まだ<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べていない |
| Casual contraction | ていない → てない | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → まだ<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてない |
| する verbs | まだ + していません | <ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>する → まだ<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>していません |
| <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る | まだ + きていません | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → まだ<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ていません |
| Short answer | まだです | 「もう<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みましたか。」「いいえ、まだです。」 |
| The question it answers | もう～ましたか | もう<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みましたか |

---

## まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the negative goes on `いる`, not on the main verb. It is <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べて + いません</code>, never ❌<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べない + います</code> and never ❌<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べませんでした</code>.

---

## まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Examples

いいえ、**まだ<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいません**。<br>*No, I haven't read it yet.*

<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>は**まだ<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっていません**。<br>*I haven't finished my homework yet.*

ホテルは**まだ<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>していません**。<br>*I haven't booked the hotel yet.*

## まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Examples (cont.)

そのドラマ、**まだ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てない**から<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わないで。<br>*I haven't watched that show yet, so don't tell me.*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>住所<rt>じゅうしょ</rt></ruby>は**まだ<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えていません**。<br>*I haven't given anyone my new address yet.*

<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>のことは、<ruby>両親<rt>りょうしん</rt></ruby>に**まだ<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>していない**んです。<br>*I haven't told my parents about next month's trip yet.*

<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>した<ruby>品物<rt>しなもの</rt></ruby>が**まだ<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>いていない**のですが、<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>していただけますか。<br>*The item I ordered still hasn't arrived - could you look into it?*

---

## まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Nuance & register

**It promises the action is coming.** <code>まだ<ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>っていません</code> means the bill is unpaid *and* that paying is still on the table. That is why it makes such a good excuse and such a good apology: it admits the gap and implies you will close it. If the thing genuinely will never happen, this pattern is the wrong choice.

**It is softer than it looks.** Compared with a flat `いいえ`, answering `まだです` or <code>まだ<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めていません</code> leaves the door open and buys time. In business Japanese this is a standard hedge: <code><ruby>社内<rt>しゃない</rt></ruby>でまだ<ruby>検討<rt>けんとう</rt></ruby>しております</code> - "we are still considering it internally" - says no without saying no.

---

## まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Register is carried by the ending, not by `まだ`.** `まだ～ていません` polite, `まだ～ていない` plain, `まだ～てない` casual and extremely common in speech. `まだ？` on its own is blunt and impatient; `まだですか` presses; and neither should be aimed at a superior.

**Watch the stative verbs.** A handful of verbs describe states already and do not take `ている` in the negative: it is <code>まだ<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません</code> ("I don't know yet"), <code>まだ<ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>りません</code> ("I don't need it yet"), and `まだありません`. The most notorious is <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る</code> - "I don't know yet" is <code>まだ<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>りません</code>, never ❌<code>まだ<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っていません</code>.

---

## まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · まだ～ていません vs ～ませんでした

> **Rule of thumb** - `～ませんでした` closes the book: it didn't happen, end of story. `まだ～ていません` leaves it open: it hasn't happened *so far*.

お<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>は**まだ<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べていません**。<br>*I haven't had lunch yet. (and I intend to)*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>はお<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べませんでした。<br>*I didn't have lunch yesterday. (I skipped it - that day is over)*

English uses "haven't" and "didn't" for this distinction and Japanese uses the aspect of the verb, so the mapping is clean once you see it. If you can add "yet" to the English, you need `まだ～ていません`.

---

## まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · まだ～ていません vs もう～ました

> **Rule of thumb** - these two are the answer pair to the same question. `もう～ました` = done. `まだ～ていません` = not done. Nothing else belongs in that slot.

「もう<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みましたか。」「はい、もう<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みました。」<br>*"Have you taken your medicine?" "Yes, I've taken it."*

「もう<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みましたか。」「いいえ、**まだ<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでいません**。」<br>*"Have you taken your medicine?" "No, not yet."*

---

## まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · まだ～ていません vs もう～ました (cont.)

Note the asymmetry that trips everyone up: the affirmative answer uses the plain past, but the negative answer cannot. ❌<code>いいえ、<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みませんでした</code> would mean you skipped it altogether - a different, and in a hospital a dangerous, answer.

---

## まだ～ていません vs ～たことがありません

> **Rule of thumb** - "not yet" implies you expect to. "Never" makes no such promise. `まだ～ていません` is about a pending task; `～たことがありません` is about an empty life-experience column.

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>は、**まだ<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>っていません**。<br>*I haven't made the materials for next week's meeting yet.*

<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>へは<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがありません。<br>*I've never been to Hokkaido.*

---

## まだ～ていません vs ～たことがありません (cont.)

Saying <code><ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>へはまだ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>っていません</code> is not wrong, but it means something narrower: a trip is planned, or expected of you, and it has not happened *yet*. Use it when there is a plan; use `～たことがありません` when there is only an absence.

---

## まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Common mistakes

- ❌ まだレポートを<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**ませんでした**。 → ✅ まだレポートを<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**ていません**。<br>*`まだ` cannot sit with a plain past negative. The two contradict each other: one says "still pending", the other says "over and done with".*

- ❌ **もう**<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>いていません。 → ✅ **まだ**<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>いていません。<br>*`もう～ていません` means "not any more". With a parcel that never came, you want `まだ`.*

- ❌ まだ<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っていません。 → ✅ まだ<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>りません。<br>*<code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る</code> has no negative `ている` form. This exception survives inside the `まだ` pattern too.*

---

## まだ～ていません - haven't ~ yet · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ まだ<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**ないです**。 → ✅ まだ<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**ていません**。<br>*<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べない</code> means "won't eat" - a refusal, not a delay. The negative belongs on `いる`.*

- ❌ お<ruby>寿司<rt>すし</rt></ruby>はまだ<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べていません。（meaning "I've never eaten sushi"）→ ✅ お<ruby>寿司<rt>すし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたことがありません。<br>*`まだ` promises that it is going to happen. For a plain absence of experience, use `～たことがありません`.*

# もう - already; anymore

**Meaning** · the change has happened - already, by now, or (with a negative) not any more

`もう` marks a threshold as crossed. Whatever the situation used to be, it is not that any longer: the meal is eaten, the shop is shut, the pain has stopped. Point it at a positive sentence and you get *already*; point it at a negative one and you get *not any more*.

---

## もう - already; anymore · Usage

`もう` is an adverb and never changes shape. It normally comes early in the sentence, though it can sit right before the word it modifies.

With a **past or completed** predicate it means *already*: <code>もう<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりました</code>, <code>もう<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた</code>, <code>もう<ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>です</code>. In a question it is the standard way to ask whether something is done: <code>もう<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ましたか</code>.

With a **negative** predicate it means *no longer*: <code>もう<ruby>吸<rt>す</rt></ruby>いません</code> (I don't smoke any more), <code>もう<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもじゃない</code> (not a child any more). The change has already happened and there is no going back.

---

## もう - already; anymore · Usage (cont.)

Before a **number or quantity** it means *another*, *more*: <code>もう<ruby>一杯<rt>いっぱい</rt></ruby></code> (one more glass), <code>もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby></code> (once more), <code>もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し</code> (a bit more), `もうちょっと`. This use has nothing to do with time, and learners often meet it before they meet the other two.

Before a **time expression** it means the moment has arrived: <code>もうすぐ<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きます</code> (arriving shortly), <code>もう<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>だ</code> (time to go home). And on its own, `もう` is an interjection of exasperation - see below.

---

## もう - already; anymore · Examples

**もう**<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べましたか。<br>*Have you had lunch already?*

**もう**お<ruby>酒<rt>さけ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みません。<br>*I don't drink any more.*

コーヒーを**もう**<ruby>一杯<rt>いっぱい</rt></ruby>いかがですか。<br>*Would you like another cup of coffee?*

**もう**こんな<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>。そろそろ<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>します。<br>*Look at the time already. I ought to be going.*

---

## もう - already; anymore · Nuance & register

`もう` itself is neutral - it belongs equally to a text message and to a meeting. What varies is what surrounds it, and `もう` has a few spoken lives worth knowing.

**`もう？` is surprise.** Said with a rising tone in reply to news, it means "that soon?" - <code>え、もう<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったの？</code>. It is friendly, and it is one of the easiest natural-sounding reactions to learn.

**`もう！` is exhaustion.** Alone, drawn out, it expresses irritation or fond exasperation: <code>もう、<ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>いよ！</code>. This interjection leans female and very casual, and it is the `もう` you will hear most often in drama.

---

## もう - already; anymore · Nuance & register (cont.)

**`もういい` needs care.** It means "that's enough" and lands anywhere between "I'm full, thank you" and "forget it, I'm done with this," depending entirely on tone. `もういいです` said flatly to a shop assistant sounds like giving up on them; <code><ruby>結構<rt>けっこう</rt></ruby>です</code> is the safe refusal.

**Formal writing prefers `すでに`.** Reports and notices use <code>すでに<ruby>完了<rt>かんりょう</rt></ruby>しております</code> rather than `もう`. `もう` is not rude, just conversational.

---

## もう - already; anymore · もう vs まだ

> **Rule of thumb** - `もう` is on the far side of the change; `まだ` is on the near side. Every `もう` sentence has a `まだ` mirror image, and the mirror flips in the negative as well as the positive.

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は**もう**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まりました。<br>*The meeting has already started.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>はまだ<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まっていません。<br>*The meeting hasn't started yet.*

Now the negatives, which is where English gives you two completely different words:

<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>は**もう**<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>くありません。<br>*My leg doesn't hurt any more. (it did; it stopped)*

---

## もう - already; anymore · もう vs まだ (cont.)

<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>はまだ<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*My leg still hurts. (nothing has changed)*

Keep hold of the direction rather than the English: `もう` + negative looks back at something that has ended, `まだ` + negative looks forward to something that has not begun. <code>もう<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いていません</code> is a retirement; <code>まだ<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いていません</code> is a student.

---

## もう - already; anymore · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>は**もう**しています。（intending "I've already done it"）→ ✅ <ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>は**もう**しました。<br>*`もう～ている` says you are already in the middle of it. Completion in the past takes the plain past: `もう～ました`.*

- ❌ バスが**もう**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ていません。（intending "the bus hasn't come yet"）→ ✅ バスが**まだ**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ていません。<br>*With a negative, `もう` means "not any more" - you would be saying the bus service has stopped running.*

- ❌ コーヒーを**もう**<ruby>一<rt>いち</rt></ruby>ください。 → ✅ コーヒーを**もう**<ruby>一杯<rt>いっぱい</rt></ruby>ください。<br>*`もう` needs a full counter phrase after it: <code><ruby>一杯<rt>いっぱい</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つ</code>, <code><ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し</code>.*

---

## もう - already; anymore · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ （in a written report）<ruby>作業<rt>さぎょう</rt></ruby>は**もう**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりました。 → ✅ <ruby>作業<rt>さぎょう</rt></ruby>はすでに<ruby>完了<rt>かんりょう</rt></ruby>しております。<br>*Not ungrammatical, just conversational. Formal writing reaches for `すでに`.*

## もう - already; anymore · Practice 3

# ～たことがある

**Meaning** · to have done something before - an experience you carry with you

`～たことがある` is not a past tense. It says that somewhere in your life there exists an instance of doing this thing. Literally: "there is a *time-when-I-did-it*." That is why it uses `ある` - the experience is treated as something you possess, not as an event on a calendar.

---

## ～たことがある · Formation

Take the **た-form**, then add `ことがある`.

---

## ～たことがある · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | た-form + ことがある | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがある |
| Verb (る-verb) | た-form + ことがある | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたことがある |
| Verb (する／<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | した／<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た + ことがある | する → したことがある |
| い-adjective | かった + ことがある | <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しかったことがある |
| な-adjective | だった + ことがある | <ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>だったことがある |
| Noun | だった + ことがある | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だったことがある |
| Polite | ある → あります | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがあります |
| Negative | ある → ない／ありません | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがない |
| Past | ある → あった／ありました | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがありました |

---

## ～たことがある · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the `た` is compulsory and is **not** the past tense. It is the form the pattern is built on, the way `～ておく` is built on the て-form. A dictionary form here gives you a different pattern entirely (see Contrast).

---

## ～たことがある · Examples

おすしを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたことがあります**か。<br>*Have you ever eaten sushi?*

<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがあります**。<br>*I've been to Hokkaido.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>馬<rt>うま</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>ったことがない**。<br>*I've never ridden a horse.*

<ruby>富士山<rt>ふじさん</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>ったことある**?<br>*Have you ever climbed Mt. Fuji?*

---

## ～たことがある · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>のとき、パン<ruby>屋<rt>や</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いたことがあります**。<br>*When I was a student I worked at a bakery for a while.*

<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>だけ、テレビで<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>た<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ったことがある**。<br>*Just once, I've run into someone on the street that I'd seen on TV.*

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいましたが、<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>には<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがありません**。<br>*I lived in Japan for three years, but I've never once been to Kyoto.*

こんなに<ruby>美<rt>うつく</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>景色<rt>けしき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>まで**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たことがない**。<br>*I have never seen scenery this beautiful in my life.*

## ～たことがある · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. `あります` polite, `ある` plain, and nothing about the pattern marks age or gender.

Two contractions dominate spoken Japanese and will ambush you:

- **`が` drops.** <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことある? - this is the ordinary spoken form of the<br>*question. So is <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たことない, for the negative.*
- **`が` becomes `は` for emphasis.** <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たこと**は**ない - "I've certainly<br>*never *seen* it (whatever else I may have done)." The `は` adds the contrast that `は` always adds.*

---

## ～たことがある · Nuance & register (cont.)

The pattern carries a built-in sense of **distance**. The experience has to feel like part of your history, not like something from this morning. That is why a specific recent time and `～たことがある` fight each other: ❌ きのう、おすしを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたことがあります. Say <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました. Vague time expressions are fine, and in fact common: <ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に, <ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>, <ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>, <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どものとき, <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>まで.

It also implies the experience is **worth remarking on**. Everyday, everyone-does-it actions sound strange: ❌ ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたことがあります is odd, because of course you have. The exception is when you are genuinely drawing a line - <ruby>納豆<rt>なっとう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたことがありません is fine, because plenty of people haven't.

---

## ～たことがある · Nuance & register (cont.)

<code><ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も</code> + negative is the fixed way to say "not even once", and the two must agree: <ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことが**ありません**.

---

## ～たことがある · ～たことがある vs the plain past

> **Rule of thumb** - plain past puts the event on a timeline. `～たことがある` puts it on your résumé.

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>、<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I went to Hokkaido last year.*

That is a report of one occasion, anchored to a date. Which occasion matters.

<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがあります**。<br>*I've been to Hokkaido.*

That is a fact about *me*. When I went, or how often, is beside the point - what is being claimed is that Hokkaido is inside my experience.

---

## ～たことがある · ～たことがある vs the plain past (cont.)

This is why a definite time expression forces the plain past. <code><ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby></code>, `きのう`, <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>に</code> all pin the event down, and pinning it down is exactly what `～たことがある` refuses to do. English "I have been to Hokkaido" behaves the same way - you cannot say "I have been there yesterday" either.

---

## ～たことがある · ～たことがある vs ～たことがない

> **Rule of thumb** - the negation lands on `ある`, never on the verb.

<ruby>海外<rt>かいがい</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがありません**。<br>*I've never been abroad.*

Learners reach for the verb and produce ❌<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなかったことがあります</code>. That is not a negative - it means "there have been occasions when I didn't go", which is a real but very different sentence.

The negative is used far more than the affirmative in practice, and it has its own set of companions: <code><ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も</code> (not once), `まだ` (not yet), <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>まで</code> (up to now).

---

## ～たことがある · ～たことがある vs ～たことがない (cont.)

まだ<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがないんです。<br>*I haven't been to the new place yet.*

Past tense on `ある` gives you "hadn't, at that point": <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>飛行機<rt>ひこうき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>ったことが**ありませんでした** - "until last year I'd never flown."

---

## ～たことがある · ～たことがある vs ～ることがある

> **Rule of thumb** - た-form = "I have done it". Dictionary form = "it sometimes happens".

Same three words, and swapping the verb form changes the meaning completely.

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れたことがあります**。<br>*The train has been late before. (I've experienced it)*

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れることがあります**。<br>*The train is sometimes late. (it happens from time to time)*

The dictionary-form version is about frequency in the present, so it works with `ときどき`, `たまに`, and with negatives: <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べないことがあります - "there are times when I skip breakfast."

---

## ～たことがある · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くことがあります**。（meaning "I've been"）→ ✅ <ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがあります**。<br>*Dictionary form gives you "I sometimes go there", which is a different claim. Experience needs the た-form.*

- ❌ きのう、おすしを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたことがあります。 → ✅ きのう、おすしを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました**。<br>*A specific recent time pins the event to the calendar, and `～たことがある` is precisely the form that refuses to be pinned. Use the plain past.*

- ❌ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったこと**を**あります。 → ✅ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったこと**が**あります。<br>*`ある` is intransitive and never takes `を`. The `が` here is doing its normal job: marking what exists.*

---

## ～たことがある · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たことが**あります**。 → ✅ <ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たことが**ありません**。<br>*<code><ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も</code> requires a negative to complete it, the way English "not once" does.*

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>ことがあります。 → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**だった**ことがあります。<br>*Nouns and な-adjectives need `だった` in front of `ことがある`. The pattern always wants a plain past form before it, whatever the word class.*

## ～たことがある · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>馬<rt>うま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>____ことがありますか。

{reveal}
<ruby>馬<rt>うま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>**った**ことがありますか。

{reveal}
The pattern is built on the た-form. <code><ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>る</code> is a う-verb ending in る, so る → った: <ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>った.

## ～たことがある · Practice 2

**2.** "Trains around here are sometimes delayed." この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>は（<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れる・<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れた）ことがあります。

{reveal}
この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れる**ことがあります。

{reveal}
Dictionary form + `ことがある` means "it happens from time to time" - a statement about how often something occurs. The た-form would have said "the trains have been late before", which is about experience rather than frequency.

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I've never been to Kyoto."

{reveal}
<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがありません**。 （casual: <ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>、<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことない。）

{reveal}
The negation goes on `ある`, not on the verb - the た-form stays fixed. Adding <code><ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も</code> gives "never once": <ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>には<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがありません。

## ～たことがある · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby>、あの<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たことがあります。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby>、あの<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ました**。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby></code> locates the event on a specific occasion, and `～たことがある` cannot take a definite recent time - it deliberately leaves *when* open. Drop the time word and <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たことがあります</code> becomes fine again.

## ～たことがある · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>沖縄<rt>おきなわ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。 (b) <ruby>沖縄<rt>おきなわ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことがあります。

{reveal}
(a) reports a particular trip. It belongs in a narrative - the listener will expect to hear when, or who with, or what happened there. On its own it can sound like the opening of a story.

{reveal}
(b) makes no claim about any particular trip. It says only that Okinawa is inside my experience, which is what you offer when someone asks "have you been?" or mentions Okinawa in passing. It is a fact about the speaker, not about a day.
