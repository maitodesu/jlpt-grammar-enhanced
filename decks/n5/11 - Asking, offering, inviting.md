---

# ～てください

**Meaning** · please do ~ - a request, and often an instruction

`～てください` attaches to the て-form and asks the listener to perform an action. It is polite in *form* - `ください` is the imperative of the honorific verb <code><ruby>下<rt>くだ</rt></ruby>さる</code> - but it is not a soft or deferential way to ask. Teachers, doctors, station staff and forms all use it, and that is the flavour it carries: a clear directive, delivered courteously.

---

## ～てください · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | て-form + ください | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ → <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってください |
| Verb (る-verb) | て-form + ください | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てください |
| Verb (する) | して + ください | <ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>してください |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>て + ください | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てください |
| Negative request | ない-form + で + ください | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないでください |
| Casual | て-form on its own | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>って／<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってね |
| More polite | ～てくださいませんか／～ていただけませんか | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っていただけませんか |

To ask for a **thing** rather than an action, `ください` follows a noun with `を` instead - that pattern has its own page in this book.

---

## ～てください · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `ください` attaches to the て-form, never the dictionary form. Get the て-form right and the rest is automatic: <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す → <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>して, <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぐ → <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いで, <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んで, <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>って.

---

## ～てください · Examples

ちょっと**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってください**。<br>*Hang on a moment, please.*

ここに<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください**。<br>*Please write your name here.*

もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってください**。<br>*Could you say that again, please.*

どうぞ**<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>ってください**。<br>*Please, have a seat.*

---

## ～てください · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたら**<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>してください**。<br>*Give me a call when you get to the station.*

<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いので、ドアを**<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めてください**。<br>*It's cold, so please shut the door.*

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を、<ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>までにメールで**<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ってください**。<br>*Please email me the material for next week's meeting by Friday.*

<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>ありませんが、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>て**くださいませんか**。<br>*I'm terribly sorry, but could you possibly come in half an hour early tomorrow?*

---

## ～てください · Nuance & register

The single most useful thing to know about `～てください` is that **it is not the polite form of a request**. It is the *neutral* one. Because it tells rather than asks, it belongs to situations where the speaker has a right to direct: a teacher to a class, a doctor to a patient, an announcement to passengers, a form to whoever is filling it in. Used sideways or upward it can land as bossy - <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、これをコピーしてください is grammatically impeccable and would raise eyebrows.

The ladder, roughly from most casual to most deferential:

---

## ～てください · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>って** - friends, family. て-form alone.
- **<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってくれる？／<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってくれない？** - casual, but genuinely asking.
- **<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってください** - the neutral polite directive on this page.
- **<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってくださいませんか** - a real question, so noticeably softer.
- **<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っていただけませんか／<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っていただけますか** - humble; the workplace default upward.
- **お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちください** - honorific, used *to* customers by staff.

---

## ～てください · Nuance & register (cont.)

`くださる` and `いただく` describe the same event from opposite ends: `くださる` is "you kindly give", `いただく` is "I humbly receive". Both are polite; the `いただく` versions are the ones you reach for when you want to sound careful.

Small softeners matter more than learners expect. Front the request with すみませんが or <ruby>恐<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>りますが, add ちょっと, and finish with ね (<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>をつけてくださいね) and the same sentence stops sounding like an order.

Note one friendly use that runs against all of this: with どうぞ, `～てください` is an *offer*, not a command. <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてください at a dinner table means "please, help yourself".

---

## ～てください · Nuance & register (cont.)

In casual speech the bare て-form does all this work - <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>て！, <ruby>貸<rt>か</rt></ruby>して - and ちょうだい (<ruby>貸<rt>か</rt></ruby>してちょうだい) is a softer, childish or motherly variant.

---

## ～てください · ～てください vs ～ていただけませんか

> **Rule of thumb** - `～てください` *tells*; `～ていただけませんか` *asks*. Upward, and for anything that is a favour rather than a duty, ask.

この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてください**。<br>*Tell me how to read this kanji.*

この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えていただけませんか**。<br>*Would you mind telling me how to read this kanji?*

The second is longer for a reason. It is a negative question, which leaves the listener room to say no, and it uses the humble `いただく`, which frames you as the one receiving a kindness. The pair `～てくださいませんか` works the same way with `くださる` and is a shade less humble.

---

## ～てください · ～てください vs ～ていただけませんか (cont.)

A useful test: if a "no" would be perfectly reasonable, use the question form. If you are describing a step in a procedure - <ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>をぬいでください at the door - `～てください` is exactly right and the long version would sound strange.

---

## ～てください · ～てください vs the plain て-form

> **Rule of thumb** - drop `ください` and you have the casual request. Drop it with the wrong person and you have an order.

ちょっと**<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>って**。<br>*Give me a hand a sec.*

ちょっと**<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ってください**。<br>*Could you give me a hand, please.*

---

## ～てください · ～てください vs the plain て-form (cont.)

The bare て-form is everywhere in speech between friends, within a family, and from older to younger. It is not rude in itself - it is simply unmarked for politeness, which means the relationship has to carry it. Adding ね or よ softens it (<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いでね); adding よ alone can sharpen it.

`～てくれる？` and `～てくれない？` sit slightly above the bare form: still casual, but phrased as a question, so they ask rather than tell.

---

## ～てください · ～てください vs ～をください

> **Rule of thumb** - `ください` after a **て-form** asks for an action; `ください` after a **noun + を** asks for a thing.

<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>を**ください**。<br>*Some water, please.*

<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ってきてください**。<br>*Please bring me some water.*

---

## ～てください · ～てください vs ～をください (cont.)

Both are natural; they differ in what you are asking for. The first hands over an object, the second requests the act of fetching. The mistake to avoid is trying to force a noun into the verb slot - ❌<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>いをしてください is possible but stilted, and ✅<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ってください is what a speaker actually says.

---

## ～てください · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる**てください** → ✅ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>**べてください**<br>*`ください` attaches to the て-form, not the dictionary form.*

- ❌ <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ**てください** → ✅ <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っ**てください**<br>*`つ`-verbs make their て-form with a small `っ`: <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ → <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>って.*

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない**てください** → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない**でください**<br>*The negative request uses `ないで`, never `ないて`.*

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、これを**コピーしてください**。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、これを**コピーしていただけませんか**。<br>*Grammatical, but you are giving an instruction to someone senior. Ask instead.*

---

## ～てください · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ ペンを**してください**。 → ✅ ペンを**ください**。<br>*Asking for an object needs no verb at all.*

---

## ～てください · Practice 1

**1.** すみません、この<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めません。ゆっくり<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>____。

{reveal}
ゆっくり<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>**してください**。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す</code> is a `す`-verb, so its て-form is <code><ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>して</code>, and `ください` attaches directly to that. Adding すみませんが at the front, as the question does, already softens the request.

---

## ～てください · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>に：「この<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>を（<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てください・<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ていただけませんか）。」

{reveal}
この<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ていただけませんか**。

{reveal}
Looking over your document is a favour, not part of the president's duties, and he outranks you. The humble question form leaves him room to decline, which is exactly what politeness upward is made of.

---

## ～てください · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Please turn off the light when you leave the room."

{reveal}
<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>るとき、<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>してください**。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>す</code> → <code><ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>して</code> + ください. This is the instructional use of the pattern - a house rule, not a personal favour - so plain `～てください` is right.

---

## ～てください · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: あぶないですから、そこに<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>つてください。

{reveal}
✅ あぶないですから、そこに**<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>たないでください**。

{reveal}
Two things are wrong. The て-form of <code><ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>つ</code> is <code><ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>って</code>, not ❌<code><ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>つて</code>; and the sense demands a negative - "it's dangerous, so please *don't* stand there" - which is built with `ないで`.

---

## ～てください · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けてください**。 (b) <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けてくださいませんか**。

{reveal}
(a) is a plain polite directive. In a classroom, or between people who know each other well, it is entirely normal - but it assumes compliance and does not invite a refusal.

{reveal}
(b) is phrased as a negative question, so it genuinely asks. It is longer, more deferential, and the natural choice when you are imposing on someone, addressing a stranger, or speaking to someone senior to you.

# ～をください

**Meaning** · please give me ~ - asking for an object

`～をください` names a **thing** and asks for it. It is the sentence you use in a shop, at a ticket window, at a counter, in a restaurant - anywhere the other person is there to hand you something. `ください` is the imperative of the honorific verb <code><ruby>下<rt>くだ</rt></ruby>さる</code> "to give (to me)", which is why a form that is literally a command still counts as polite.

---

## ～をください · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + を + ください | コーヒー → コーヒーをください |
| Noun + quantity | Noun + を + counter + ください | りんご → りんごを<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つください |
| Quantity (spoken, `を` dropped) | Noun + counter + ください | コーヒー<ruby>二<rt>ふた</rt></ruby>つください |
| Verb (an action, not a thing) | て-form + ください | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ → <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってください |
| Politer | ～をお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします | コーヒーをお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします |
| Politer still | ～をいただけますか | メニューをいただけますか |
| Casual | ～ちょうだい | コーヒーちょうだい |

---

## ～をください · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the thing takes `を`, never `が`. `ください` is a verb of giving, so what is given is its direct object - the opposite of `ほしい`, which is an adjective and takes `が`.

---

## ～をください · Examples

コーヒーを**ください**。<br>*A coffee, please.*

すみません、メニューを**ください**。<br>*Excuse me, could I have a menu.*

この<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>いりんごを<ruby>五<rt>いつ</rt></ruby>つ**ください**。<br>*Five of these red apples, please.*

<ruby>切符<rt>きっぷ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby><ruby>二<rt>に</rt></ruby>まい、<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども<ruby>一<rt>いち</rt></ruby>まい**ください**。<br>*Two adult tickets and one child's, please.*

---

## ～をください · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>袋<rt>ふくろ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>りませんので、レシートだけ**ください**。<br>*I don't need a bag - just the receipt, please.*

もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>を**ください**。<br>*Please give me a little more time.*

すみません、<ruby>温<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かいお<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>と、<ruby>砂糖<rt>さとう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し**ください**。<br>*Excuse me - a hot tea, and a little sugar, please.*

<ruby>白<rt>しろ</rt></ruby>いシャツの<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きいサイズがあれば、それを**ください**。<br>*If you have that white shirt in a large, I'll take it.*

---

## ～をください · Nuance & register

`～をください` is the customer's sentence. Between a customer and staff the balance of the situation makes it perfectly polite, and it is what Japanese speakers actually say at a counter - no hedging required.

Turn it around and the picture changes. Because `ください` is an imperative, asking a **superior** for something with it sounds like an instruction: <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、ペンをください is the sort of thing that gets noticed. Upward you switch to a question - ペンをお<ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>りできますか, <ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>をいただけますか - or to お<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします.

---

## ～をください · Nuance & register (cont.)

Quantities behave loosely in speech. コーヒーを<ruby>二<rt>ふた</rt></ruby>つください and コーヒー<ruby>二<rt>ふた</rt></ruby>つください are both normal, and the second is more common at a counter; what you should not do is mark the number with `を` as well (❌コーヒーを<ruby>二<rt>ふた</rt></ruby>つをください). Politeness particles cluster at the front and back instead: すみません、～ください and ～ください**ね** are both very common.

`ちょうだい` is the casual replacement - それちょうだい - and reads as childish or warmly familiar depending on the speaker; it is more common from women and from adults talking to children. In writing, ください survives in fixed instructions (ご<ruby>記入<rt>きにゅう</rt></ruby>ください, <ruby>少々<rt>しょうしょう</rt></ruby>お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちください), which are honorific forms rather than this pattern.

---

## ～をください · Nuance & register (cont.)

`ください` is not limited to physical objects. Abstract nouns work fine, and this is where the pattern turns up outside shops: <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>をください, <ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>をください, もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>チャンスをください.

---

## ～をください · ～をください vs ～をお願いします

> **Rule of thumb** - `ください` asks for a **thing you can hand over**; お<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします asks for that *or* for a service, and sounds a touch more deferential.

<ruby>生<rt>なま</rt></ruby>ビールを**ください**。<br>*A draught beer, please.*

<ruby>生<rt>なま</rt></ruby>ビールを**お<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします**。<br>*A draught beer, please. (slightly more polite)*

---

## ～をください · ～をください vs ～をお願いします (cont.)

Where they come apart is scope. お<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします covers requests that have no object at all - <ruby>会計<rt>かいけい</rt></ruby>をお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします "the bill, please", <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんをお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします "could I speak to Yamada" - where ください would be wrong or comic. As a rule, if you are asking someone to *do* something on your behalf, お<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします; if you are asking them to *give* you something, either works.

---

## ～をください · ～をください vs ～てください

> **Rule of thumb** - noun + `を` + ください for an **object**; verb て-form + ください for an **action**.

<ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>を**ください**。<br>*Could I have a map.*

<ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください**。<br>*Please draw me a map.*

---

## ～をください · ～をください vs ～てください (cont.)

They share the word `ください` and nothing else. English blurs them - "give me a hand" looks like a noun request but is really an action - so the trap is real: ✅<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ってください, not ❌<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>いをください.

When you want an object *brought* to you rather than simply handed over, the two combine: <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ってきてください.

---

## ～をください · ～をください vs ～がほしいです

> **Rule of thumb** - `ほしい` describes your inner state; `ください` performs the request. To a shop assistant, only the second one is a sentence that does anything.

この<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>黒<rt>くろ</rt></ruby>を**ください**。<br>*I'll take these shoes in black.*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>が**ほしい**です。<br>*I want new shoes.*

The second is a fine thing to say to a friend and an odd thing to say to a salesperson - you have announced a feeling and left them to guess what to do about it. Note the particles as well: `ほしい` is an adjective and takes `が`, while `ください` is a verb and takes `を`. Mixing them up (❌これがください) is the single most common error with this pattern.

---

## ～をください · Common mistakes

- ❌ これ**が**ください。 → ✅ これ**を**ください。<br>*`ください` is a verb of giving; the thing given is its object.*

- ❌ （レストランで）お<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>が**ほしいです**。 → ✅ お<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>を**ください**。<br>*Naming your desire at a server does not place an order.*

- ❌ <ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>いを**ください**。 → ✅ **<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ってください**。<br>*Help is an action, not an object. Use the て-form.*

- ❌ コーヒーを<ruby>二<rt>ふた</rt></ruby>つ**を**ください。 → ✅ コーヒーを<ruby>二<rt>ふた</rt></ruby>つ**ください**。<br>*One `を` per object. The counter does not take a particle of its own.*

---

## ～をください · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>、はんこを**ください**。 → ✅ <ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>、はんこを**いただけますか**。<br>*`ください` is an imperative. Pointed upward it reads as an instruction.*

---

## ～をください · Practice 1

**1.** すみません、この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>____ください。

{reveal}
この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>**を**ください。

{reveal}
`ください` comes from the verb <code><ruby>下<rt>くだ</rt></ruby>さる</code>, so the thing you are asking for is its direct object and takes `を`. The `が` that goes with `ほしい` does not transfer.

---

## ～をください · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>で：「そのかばんを（ください・ほしいです）。」

{reveal}
そのかばんを**ください**。

{reveal}
In a shop you are placing a request, and `ください` is what performs it. `ほしいです` only reports how you feel, which leaves the assistant to work out what you actually want.

---

## ～をください · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Two coffees and one orange juice, please."

{reveal}
コーヒーを<ruby>二<rt>ふた</rt></ruby>つと、オレンジジュースを<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つ**ください**。

{reveal}
Counters go between the noun and `ください`, and only one `を` per item. In speech the `を` is often dropped altogether: コーヒー<ruby>二<rt>ふた</rt></ruby>つとオレンジジュース<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つください.

---

## ～をください · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: すみません、ちょっと<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>いをください。

{reveal}
✅ すみません、ちょっと**<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ってください**。

{reveal}
What you want is an action, not an object, so it belongs in the て-form pattern. English "give me a hand" hides a verb inside a noun, and that is where the mistake comes from.

---

## ～をください · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) タクシーを**ください**。 (b) タクシーを**お<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします**。

{reveal}
(a) asks for a taxi as though it were an object to be handed across a counter, and it sounds odd for that reason - nobody gives you a taxi.

{reveal}
(b) is what a hotel guest actually says. お<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします asks the listener to *arrange* something on your behalf, which covers services, phone calls and errands. That wider scope is the main practical reason to learn it alongside `ください`.

# ～ないでください

**Meaning** · please don't do ~ - the negative request

`～ないでください` is the negative counterpart of `～てください`. Take the ない-form, add `で`, add `ください`. The `で` is not decoration: `ないで` is the **negative て-form** of a verb, and it does the same joining work elsewhere in the language - <ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>たないで<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた「I went out without an umbrella」.

---

## ～ないでください · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ない-form + で + ください | <ruby>吸<rt>す</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>吸<rt>す</rt></ruby>わないでください |
| Verb (る-verb) | ない-form + で + ください | <ruby>捨<rt>す</rt></ruby>てる → <ruby>捨<rt>す</rt></ruby>てないでください |
| Verb (する) | しないで + ください | <ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>しないでください |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないで + ください | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないでください |
| Casual | ない-form + で | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ないで／<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ないでね |
| More polite | ～ないでいただけませんか | <ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>らないでいただけませんか |
| Notices and signs | ご<ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>ください／おやめください | <ruby>喫煙<rt>きつえん</rt></ruby>はご<ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>ください |

---

## ～ないでください · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - build the ない-form first, then swap `ない` for `ないで`. The ない-form is the hard part: <ruby>吸<rt>す</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>吸<rt>す</rt></ruby>**わ**ない (not ❌<ruby>吸<rt>す</rt></ruby>あない), する → しない, <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない (read **こ**ない, never ❌きない).

> **Note** - `ないで` on its own is a full form of the verb, not just a piece of this pattern. It joins clauses in the sense *without doing* (<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わないで<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>て<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った), and it feeds other endings: <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れないでね, <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないでほしい, <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ないでくれる？

---

## ～ないでください · Examples

ここでたばこを**<ruby>吸<rt>す</rt></ruby>わないでください**。<br>*Please don't smoke here.*

まだ**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>らないでください**。<br>*Please don't leave yet.*

**<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>しないでください**。<br>*Please don't worry.*

<ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>はスマホを**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ないでください**。<br>*Please don't look at your phone during class.*

---

## ～ないでください · Examples (cont.)

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>には**<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>らないでください**。<br>*Please don't go into this room.*

<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>な<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>ですから、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れないでください**ね。<br>*Tomorrow's an important day, so please don't be late.*

すみませんが、この<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>はまだ<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わないでください**。<br>*Sorry, but please don't tell anyone about this yet.*

<ruby>恐<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>りますが、<ruby>館内<rt>かんない</rt></ruby>では<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>らないでいただけますか**。<br>*I'm very sorry, but could I ask you not to take photographs inside the building?*

---

## ～ないでください · Nuance & register

Everything true of `～てください` is true here: it is polite in form and directive in function. A negative directive is inherently more confronting than a positive one, though, because you are telling someone to stop or not to start - so the softeners matter more.

The usual ones are すみませんが / <ruby>恐<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>りますが at the front, ちょっと in the middle, and ね at the end. <ruby>大声<rt>おおごえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さないでくださいね is noticeably gentler than the same sentence without it.

Public notices avoid the bare form, because a sign that tells thousands of people what not to do is at risk of sounding hectoring. Japanese signage prefers ご<ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>ください「we ask that you refrain」, おやめください「please stop」, or a plain noun + <ruby>禁止<rt>きんし</rt></ruby>「prohibited」 on the sternest notices. If you learn to read those, you have covered most stations and museums.

---

## ～ないでください · Nuance & register (cont.)

Casually, `ください` drops off and the ない-form does the whole job: <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ないで！, <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わないで. Add ね to soften, よ to press. `～ないでくれる？` and `～ないでほしい` are common alternatives among friends - the first asks, the second reports your wish.

One thing `～ないでください` is *not*: a statement of prohibition. It is a request. If you need to say something is forbidden, `～てはいけません` is the pattern, and it carries an authority that this one does not.

---

## ～ないでください · ～ないでください vs ～ないでいただけませんか

> **Rule of thumb** - the negative request, then the negative *question*. Upward, and to strangers, ask rather than tell.

ここに<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>を**とめないでください**。<br>*Please don't park here.*

ここに<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>を**とめないでいただけませんか**。<br>*Would you mind not parking here?*

---

## ～ないでください · ～ないでください vs ～ないでいただけませんか (cont.)

The second is what a neighbour says to a neighbour; the first is what the sign says. `～ないでくださいませんか` sits between them, and the fully honorific ご<ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>ください is the institutional voice - a shop, a train company, a museum.

The relationship decides. Telling a stranger not to do something is delicate in any language, and Japanese solves it by turning the request into a question and letting the listener seem to choose.

---

## ～ないでください · ～ないで vs ～なくて

> **Rule of thumb** - Japanese has **two** negative て-forms. `ないで` means *without doing* and is the one used in requests; `なくて` means *and so, because … not*.

<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べないで**<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I went to school without eating breakfast.*

バスが**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくて**、<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れました。<br>*The bus didn't come, and so I was late.*

The first describes *how* the going happened; the second gives the *reason* for being late. Swap them and the sentences stop making sense.

---

## ～ないでください · ～ないで vs ～なくて (cont.)

Two consequences worth memorising. First, requests always use `ないで` - there is no ❌<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなくてください. Second, `なくて` is the form that adjectives and nouns take when they are negated and joined (<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くなくて<ruby>安心<rt>あんしん</rt></ruby>しました, <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>じゃなくてよかった), so `ないで` is verb-only territory.

---

## ～ないでください · ～ないでください vs ～てはいけません

> **Rule of thumb** - `～ないでください` **asks**; `～てはいけません` **forbids**.

<ruby>教室<rt>きょうしつ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ら**ないでください**。<br>*Please don't run in the classroom.*

<ruby>教室<rt>きょうしつ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っ**てはいけません**。<br>*You must not run in the classroom.*

The first is a request, however firmly meant; the second states a rule, and the speaker has to be in a position to state it - a teacher, a parent, a notice. Used by an equal it sounds like a scolding.

---

## ～ないでください · ～ないでください vs ～てはいけません (cont.)

There is a third, gentler option that learners rarely meet early enough: `～ないようにしてください`「please try not to…」, which frames the same content as advice. <ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby><ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>く<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>しないようにしてください is the version that survives being said to almost anyone.

---

## ～ないでください · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ない**てください** → ✅ <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ない**でください**<br>*The negative て-form is `ないで`. There is no form ❌`ないて`.*

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>か**なくてください** → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>か**ないでください**<br>*`なくて` gives a reason; it cannot carry a request.*

- ❌ **するないでください** → ✅ **しないでください**<br>*する is irregular: its ない-form is しない, so the request is しないでください.*

- ❌ たばこを**<ruby>吸<rt>す</rt></ruby>うないでください** → ✅ たばこを**<ruby>吸<rt>す</rt></ruby>わないでください**<br>*`ないで` attaches to the ない-form, not the dictionary form. For う-verbs ending in `う`, the ない-form takes `わ`.*

---

## ～ないでください · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ないでください → ✅ <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないでください<br>*Same kanji, different reading. The ない-form of <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る is read **こ**ない.*

---

<code><ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す</code> → ない-form <code><ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>さない</code> → swap `ない` for `ないで`. A library is exactly the setting where the plain polite request is appropriate.

---

## ～ないでください · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を（<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けなくてください・<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けないでください）。

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けないでください**。

Japanese has two negative て-forms and only one of them can carry a request. `なくて` is for causes and consequences - <code><ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けなくて<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>かった</code> - so it cannot be followed by `ください`.

The ない-form of <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る</code> is <code><ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない</code> - the kanji stays put and the reading changes to こ. Writing きない is the classic slip.

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>をするないでください。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>を**しないでください**。

{reveal}

## ～ないでください · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) ここに<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>か**ないでください**。 (b) ここに<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>い**てはいけません**。

# ～ましょう

**Meaning** · let's - a proposal made on the assumption you already agree

`～ましょう` is the polite volitional. It turns a verb into "let's do it", and its defining feature is **confidence**: the speaker takes agreement as read. That makes it the right form for a plan already settled, for a leader moving a group along, and for a public slogan - and the wrong form for a genuine, open invitation, where `～ませんか` is what you want.

A second use hides inside the first. With <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が</code>, `～ましょう` is not "let's" at all but "I'll do it" - the speaker volunteering.

---

## ～ましょう · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + ましょう | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + ましょう | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べましょう |
| Verb (する) | し + ましょう | します → しましょう |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | き + ましょう | <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます → <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ましょう |
| Softened into a question | ましょう + か | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょうか |
| Casual equivalent | plain volitional | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こう／<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べよう |

---

## ～ましょう · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `ましょう` replaces `ます`, so it builds off the same stem. <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み**ます** → <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み**ましょう**. It never attaches to the dictionary form: ❌<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>むましょう.

> **Note** - there is no negative `ましょう`. ❌<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんしょう does not exist. To propose *not* doing something, negate the content instead: <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>うのはやめましょう or <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>わないことにしましょう.

---

## ～ましょう · Examples

じゃあ、**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう**。<br>*Right - let's go.*

<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べましょう**。<br>*Let's have lunch together.*

<ruby>七<rt>しち</rt></ruby><ruby>時<rt>じ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いましょう**。<br>*Let's meet in front of the station at seven.*

そろそろ**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めましょう**。<br>*Let's get started.*

---

## ～ましょう · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>はここまでに**しましょう**。<br>*Let's call it a day there.*

はい、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>しましょう**。<br>*All right - I'll drive.*

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>りそうですから、<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>って**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう**。<br>*It looks like rain, so let's take umbrellas.*

<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がありませんから、<ruby>細<rt>こま</rt></ruby>かいことは<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しましょう**。<br>*We're out of time, so let's go through the details afterwards.*

---

## ～ましょう · Nuance & register

`ましょう` is polite in form but *assertive in function*. You are announcing what the group is going to do. That is fine when the plan is agreed, when you are the one running the meeting, or when the proposal is obviously welcome (<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いですね、<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>りましょう). It is presumptuous when the other person has not yet agreed to anything, and doubly so upward: <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう helps itself to the boss's evening. <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか or いかがですか leaves the decision where it belongs.

The same assertiveness is why `ましょう` is the voice of public exhortation. Handwashing posters, road-safety signs and school announcements are full of it: <ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>いましょう, <ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>をつけましょう, <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>守<rt>まも</rt></ruby>りましょう. Grammatically these are invitations; in practice they are instructions with a smile.

---

## ～ましょう · Nuance & register (cont.)

As an answer, `ましょう` is the standard *yes*. To <ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか you reply ええ、<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう - the invitation was open, and `ましょう` closes it.

The <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が</code> use is worth practising: <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちましょう「I'll carry that」. It is a shade more take-charge than <ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちましょうか, which asks first, and noticeably more formal than <ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちます. In service Japanese it is often replaced by the humble お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちしましょう.

Casually, the whole thing collapses to the plain volitional: <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こう, <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べよう, しよう, <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>よう. Between friends `ましょう` sounds oddly ceremonious, like proposing a motion.

---

## ～ましょう · ～ましょう vs ～ませんか

> **Rule of thumb** - `ましょう` **assumes** you'll agree; `ませんか` **asks** whether you will. If the other person could reasonably say no, use `ませんか`.

<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう**。<br>*Let's go and see a film.*

<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか**。<br>*Would you like to go and see a film?*

The first works when you have already decided to spend the afternoon together and are choosing what to do. The second works when you are asking someone out - it is a question, it is negative, and both of those give the listener room.

---

## ～ましょう · ～ましょう vs ～ませんか (cont.)

This is why the pair so often appears in sequence: one person invites with `ませんか`, the other accepts with `ましょう`. Reversing them is the classic learner error, and it is the difference between "would you like to?" and "we're going".

---

## ～ましょう · ～ましょう vs ～ましょうか

> **Rule of thumb** - the `か` turns a decision into a question. `ましょう` announces; `ましょうか` checks, or offers to do something *for* the listener.

そろそろ**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ましょう**。<br>*Let's head off.*

そろそろ**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ましょうか**。<br>*Shall we head off?*

---

## ～ましょう · ～ましょう vs ～ましょうか (cont.)

Adding `か` costs nothing and softens a great deal, so `ましょうか` is the safer default whenever you are not certain the other person is ready. It also unlocks a sense `ましょう` does not have: with the speaker as the doer, it becomes an offer - <ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちましょうか「shall I take your bag?」 where <ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちましょう is you announcing that you will.

---

## ～ましょう · ～ましょう vs the plain volitional

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different politeness. `ましょう` is the ます-level form; <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こう and <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べよう are the plain-level ones.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>八<rt>はち</rt></ruby><ruby>時<rt>じ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まりましょう**。<br>*Let's gather at eight tomorrow.*

おなかすいた。なんか**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べよう**。<br>*I'm hungry. Let's get something to eat.*

---

## ～ましょう · ～ましょう vs the plain volitional (cont.)

The plain forms are built like this: う-verbs change the final `-u` to `-ou` (<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こう, <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>もう), る-verbs swap `る` for `よう` (<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>よう), and the two irregulars are する → しよう, <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>よう.

Register maps straight across: use the plain volitional with friends, family and anyone you would speak to in plain form, and `ましょう` everywhere you are using `ます`. Note that the plain form can also take `か` - <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こうか is the casual equivalent of <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょうか.

---

## ～ましょう · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む**ましょう** → ✅ <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み**ましょう**<br>*`ましょう` replaces `ます` on the stem, not the dictionary form.*

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ましょうですか** → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ましょうか**<br>*`ましょう` already contains the copula. Just add `か`.*

- ❌ <ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>り**ましょう**。 → ✅ <ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>り**ませんか**。<br>*Deciding a superior's movements for them. Ask instead of proposing.*

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り**ましょう**。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>る**でしょう**。<br>*`ましょう` is a proposal, not a prediction. Guessing about the weather is `でしょう`.*

---

## ～ましょう · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ませんしょう**。 → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くのは**やめましょう**。<br>*There is no negative `ましょう`; negate the content instead.*

# ～ましょうか

**Meaning** · shall I? / shall we? - offering to do something, or proposing tentatively

`～ましょうか` is `～ましょう` with a question mark built in, and that small `か` does two different jobs.

- **Shall I?** - you offer to do something *for* the listener.<br>*<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けましょうか。*
- **Shall we?** - you propose something for both of you, but check first instead of<br>*announcing. そろそろ<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りましょうか。*

---

## ～ましょうか · Overview

Which one a listener hears is decided by the situation, not the grammar: if the action benefits them and only you can do it, it is an offer; if it is something you would do together, it is a proposal.

---

## ～ましょうか · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + ましょうか | <ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちます → <ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちましょうか |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + ましょうか | <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けます → <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けましょうか |
| Verb (する) | し + ましょうか | します → しましょうか |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | き + ましょうか | <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます → <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ましょうか |
| Humble offer (service Japanese) | お + ます-stem + しましょうか | お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちしましょうか |
| Casual | plain volitional + か | <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けようか／<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>おうか |

---

## ～ましょうか · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - mark the doer and the ambiguity disappears. <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>運<rt>はこ</rt></ruby>びましょうか is unmistakably an offer; <ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>運<rt>はこ</rt></ruby>びましょうか is unmistakably a proposal.

---

## ～ましょうか · Examples

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けましょうか**。<br>*Shall I open the window?*

**<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>いましょうか**。<br>*Shall I give you a hand?*

<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みましょうか**。<br>*What shall we have to drink?*

<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いですね。エアコンを**つけましょうか**。<br>*It's hot, isn't it. Shall I put the air conditioning on?*

---

## ～ましょうか · Examples (cont.)

どこで**<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いましょうか**。<br>*Where shall we meet?*

<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>りましょうか**。<br>*Shall I take a photo for you?*

<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>混<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいますね。<ruby>次<rt>つぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>角<rt>かど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>右<rt>みぎ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>曲<rt>ま</rt></ruby>がりましょうか**。<br>*The traffic's heavy. Shall we turn right at the next corner?*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>人数分<rt>にんずうぶん</rt></ruby>**コピーしておきましょうか**。<br>*Shall I run off enough copies of the meeting handouts for everyone?*

---

## ～ましょうか · Nuance & register

`ましょうか` is the workhorse of everyday helpfulness. It is what you say when you notice someone struggling with a door, a suitcase, a printer. It is polite without being stiff, it commits you to nothing until they answer, and - crucially - it does not put the listener under obligation, because you are volunteering rather than asking.

Learn the replies as a set, because you will hear them constantly:

- **お<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします** - yes, please.
- **すみません、ありがとうございます** - yes, with thanks and apology at once.
- **あ、<ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>です** - no, I'm fine. The everyday polite refusal.
- **いえ、<ruby>結構<rt>けっこう</rt></ruby>です** - no thank you. Firmer, and cooler.

---

## ～ましょうか · Nuance & register (cont.)

Upward, `ましょうか` is acceptable but plain. In service Japanese and towards superiors the humble frame is standard: お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちしましょうか, お<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べしましょうか, ご<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>しましょうか. These raise the listener by lowering the action, and they are what hotel staff and careful employees use.

Casually the whole thing shortens to the plain volitional plus `か`: <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けようか, <ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>おうか, <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ってこようか. Note that in casual speech the `か` is often dropped entirely and carried by intonation - <ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>おう？

One warning about intonation and reading: `ましょうか` is a question, and it takes a rising tone. Written down, it is often followed by `。` rather than `？` in Japanese prose, which does not make it any less of a question.

---

## ～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～ましょう

> **Rule of thumb** - `ましょう` **announces**; `ましょうか` **checks**. The `か` hands the decision back to the listener.

<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>を**つけましょう**。<br>*Let's put the light on.*

<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>を**つけましょうか**。<br>*Shall I put the light on?*

The first version assumes; the second asks. That is why the `か` version doubles as an offer and the bare version does not: an offer only makes sense if the other person can decline it.

---

## ～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～ましょう (cont.)

Between the two, `ましょうか` is almost always the safer choice with people you do not know well. Use bare `ましょう` when the plan is already agreed and you are simply moving it along - <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう as you both stand up.

---

## ～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～ませんか

> **Rule of thumb** - both ask, but `ませんか` **invites** the listener to join you; `ましょうか` proposes a next step in something already underway, or offers to act on their behalf.

<ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>の<ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>、<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>りませんか**。<br>*How about climbing a mountain this Saturday?*

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ってきましたね。<ruby>下<rt>お</rt></ruby>り**ましょうか**。<br>*It's started raining. Shall we head back down?*

`ませんか` opens something new and can be refused without awkwardness - it is the form for asking someone out. `ましょうか` takes the shared activity as given and asks about the *next move* within it.

---

## ～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～ませんか (cont.)

There is also a distribution difference that catches learners. For an **offer**, `ませんか` does not work: ❌<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちませんか sounds like you are inviting the other person to carry luggage, not offering to carry theirs. Offers are `ましょうか` territory.

---

## ～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～てあげましょうか

> **Rule of thumb** - plain `ましょうか` for offers. `～てあげましょうか` announces that you are doing the listener a kindness, and that is not yours to announce.

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りましょうか**。<br>*Shall I drive you to the station?*

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ってあげましょうか**。<br>*Shall I do you the favour of driving you to the station?*

The second is grammatical and is used - among close friends, or lightly to a child - but `あげる` explicitly frames the act as a benefit conferred, and offering that to an equal or a superior sounds self-congratulatory. The neutral offer leaves the gratitude to the other person.

---

## ～ましょうか · ～ましょうか vs ～てあげましょうか (cont.)

The polite move goes the other way, towards humility: お<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りしましょうか lowers your own action, and is the form used in service and business. `～てあげる` and `お～する` are the two ends of the same axis, and the safe default at N5 is plain `ましょうか` in the middle.

---

## ～ましょうか · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ける**ましょうか** → ✅ <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け**ましょうか**<br>*Built on the ます-stem: <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けます → <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け + ましょうか.*

- ❌ <ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちませんか**。（as an offer） → ✅ <ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちましょうか**。<br>*`ませんか` invites the listener to do it. To offer your own help, use `ましょうか`.*

- ❌ <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けましょうか**。（meaning "please open the window"） → ✅ <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けてください**。<br>*`ましょうか` puts the action on *you*, not on the listener. It can never be a request.*

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、コピーして**あげましょうか**。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、コピー**しましょうか**。／お<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>い**しましょうか**。<br>*`てあげる` advertises your own kindness. Upward, drop it or go humble.*

---

## ～ましょうか · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ましょうか、ですか**。 → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ましょうか**。<br>*`ましょうか` is already a complete question.*

# ～ませんか

**Meaning** · won't you…? how about it? - a genuine, open invitation

`～ませんか` invites. It is built as a **negative question**, and that is the whole point: a negative question presupposes nothing, so it leaves the listener free to decline without embarrassment. Where `～ましょう` announces a plan, `～ませんか` asks whether you would like one - which is why it is the polite way to ask someone to do something with you.

---

## ～ませんか · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + ませんか | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + ませんか | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べませんか |
| Verb (する) | し + ませんか | します → しませんか |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | き + ませんか | <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます → <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ませんか |
| Casual | ない-form, rising tone | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない？／<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べない？ |
| Politer | ～ませんでしょうか | いらっしゃいませんでしょうか |
| Accepting | ええ、～ましょう | ええ、<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう |

---

## ～ませんか · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `ませんか` is just the polite negative `ません` plus the question particle `か`, so it is built on the ます-stem like everything else in that family: <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し**ます** → <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し**ません** → <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し**ませんか**.

---

## ～ませんか · Examples

---

## ～ませんか · Examples (cont.)

この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>、よかったら**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでみませんか**。<br>*Would you like to give this book a read?*

<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby><ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めます。<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんも**<ruby>参加<rt>さんか</rt></ruby>しませんか**。<br>*We're starting a morning study group next month. Would you like to join, Tanaka?*

もしお<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>があれば、<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>くにできた<ruby>美術館<rt>びじゅつかん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってみませんか**。<br>*If you have time, would you like to go with me to the new museum near the station?*

---

## ～ませんか · Nuance & register

`ませんか` is the polite invitation, full stop. It works upward, sideways and to strangers, and it is the form to reach for whenever the answer could genuinely be no. The politeness comes from the shape of the question: by asking in the negative, you build the refusal into the sentence and remove any pressure to accept.

Because it is a real question, it comes with a real conversational script. **Accepting** is almost never a bare はい:

- ええ、<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう。 - yes, let's.
- いいですね。ぜひ。 - sounds good, I'd love to.

**Declining** almost never uses いいえ either. The standard refusal trails off and is never completed:

---

## ～ませんか · Nuance & register (cont.)

- すみません、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>はちょっと… - sorry, tomorrow is a bit…
- ありがとうございます。でも、その<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>用事<rt>ようじ</rt></ruby>があって… - thank you, but I have something on that day.

The unfinished ちょっと is doing real work. Saying the refusal out loud would be blunt; leaving it hanging lets both sides treat it as understood.

Casually, `ませんか` becomes the plain negative with a rising tone: <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない？, <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べない？, <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない？ These are extremely common among friends and carry exactly the same softness.

---

## ～ませんか · Nuance & register (cont.)

One caution: `～ませんか` is also, literally, an ordinary negative question. Out of context, <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べませんか could mean "aren't you eating?" Intonation and situation sort it out, and the invitation reading dominates when the sentence names a shared activity or includes <ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に.

Upward, `ませんか` is already good; いかがですか is the softer noun-based version, and in formal invitations you will meet ～ませんでしょうか and ご<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>しませんか.

---

## ～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ましょう

> **Rule of thumb** - `ませんか` **asks**, `ましょう` **assumes**. Invite with the first; accept with the second.

<ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>、テニスを**しませんか**。<br>*Would you like to play tennis at the weekend?*

いいですね、**しましょう**。<br>*Sounds good - let's.*

That two-turn exchange is the natural home of both patterns, and it shows why they are not interchangeable. Opening with `ましょう` treats the other person's weekend as already spent; replying with `ませんか` would be asking them a question they have just asked you.

---

## ～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ましょう (cont.)

`ましょう` is fine as an opener only when agreement is safe - when you are already out together, or when you have the standing to direct the group.

---

## ～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ましょうか

> **Rule of thumb** - `ませんか` invites the listener **to join you**; `ましょうか` offers **your own** action, or checks the next step of something already agreed.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>美術館<rt>びじゅつかん</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか**。<br>*How about going to the art museum tomorrow?*

<ruby>切符<rt>きっぷ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>っておきましょうか**。<br>*Shall I get the tickets in advance?*

---

## ～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ましょうか (cont.)

The first opens a plan; the second handles a detail inside a plan that already exists. And for offers of help the division is strict: <ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>いましょうか offers your hands, while ❌<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>いませんか asks the listener to come and help with something - possibly the opposite of what you meant.

---

## ～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ますか

> **Rule of thumb** - `ますか` **asks a question** about what the listener will do; `ませんか` **invites**. The negative is what turns information-gathering into an invitation.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>のパーティーに**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますか**。<br>*Are you going to the party tomorrow?*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>のパーティーに**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか**。<br>*Would you like to come to the party tomorrow?*

The first is neutral enquiry - you want to know her plans, and you may not be going yourself. The second includes you, and asks her to come along.

---

## ～ませんか · ～ませんか vs ～ますか (cont.)

Using `ますか` where you meant to invite is a common learner slip. It is not rude, it simply does not invite: <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べますか offers information-gathering where <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べませんか offers company. The affirmative question can also feel a touch demanding, because it presupposes that going is on the table.

---

## ～ませんか · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**ませんか** → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ませんか**<br>*Built on the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.*

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ませんですか** → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ませんか**<br>*`ません` is already polite. Do not add another `です`.*

- ❌ <ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちませんか**。（offering to carry it） → ✅ <ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちましょうか**。<br>*`ませんか` invites the other person to do the action. For your own offer, use `ましょうか`.*

- ❌ 「コーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みませんか。」「はい、<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みません。」 → ✅ 「ええ、いただきます。」／「ありがとうございます。」<br>*Do not answer the negative literally. Accepting an invitation is ええ plus a positive verb.*

---

## ～ませんか · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べに**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう**。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べに**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか**。<br>*Upward, invite rather than announce.*

---

# ～はどうですか

**Meaning** · how about ~? / how is ~? - putting an option on the table, or asking for an impression

`～はどうですか` does two related jobs, and Japanese does not distinguish them grammatically:

- **Suggesting** - you offer something and ask what the listener makes of it.<br>*<ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>はどうですか。「How about Saturday?」*
- **Asking for an evaluation** - you ask how something is, or was.<br>*<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>はどうですか。「How's the new job?」*

---

## ～はどうですか · Overview

Underneath, both are the same question: *what is your assessment of this?* Tense and context decide which one the listener hears.

---

## ～はどうですか · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + はどうですか | コーヒー → コーヒーはどうですか |
| Verb (proposing an action) | Dictionary + のは + どうですか | <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いて<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くのはどうですか |
| Verb (advising the listener) | た-form + らどうですか | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだらどうですか |
| Past (a finished event) | ～はどうでしたか | <ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>はどうでしたか |
| Politer | ～はいかがですか | お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>はいかがですか |
| Softer, more tentative | ～はどうでしょうか | <ruby>水曜日<rt>すいようび</rt></ruby>はどうでしょうか |
| Casual | ～はどう？ | これはどう？ |

---

## ～はどうですか · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `どう` asks *how / in what state*. It is not `どんな`, which asks *what kind of* and must be followed by a noun. ✅<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>はどうでしたか。 ✅どんな<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>でしたか。 ❌<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>はどんなでしたか。

---

## ～はどうですか · Examples

コーヒー**はどうですか**。<br>*How about a coffee?*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>**はどうですか**。<br>*How's the new job?*

<ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>の<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>**はどうですか**。<br>*How about Saturday afternoon?*

<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>**はどうでしたか**。<br>*How was the trip to Kyoto?*

---

## ～はどうですか · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>調子<rt>ちょうし</rt></ruby>**はどうですか**。<br>*How are you feeling?*

この<ruby>色<rt>いろ</rt></ruby>**はどうですか**。もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るいのもありますよ。<br>*How about this colour? We have a slightly brighter one as well.*

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>くに<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいそば<ruby>屋<rt>や</rt></ruby>ができたそうですが、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>はそこ**はどうですか**。<br>*Apparently a new soba place has opened near the station - how about there for lunch today?*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>ですが、<ruby>水曜日<rt>すいようび</rt></ruby>の<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby><ruby>三<rt>さん</rt></ruby><ruby>時<rt>じ</rt></ruby>から**というのはどうでしょうか**。<br>*About the time for the meeting - how would Wednesday from three in the afternoon be?*

---

## ～はどうですか · Nuance & register

`どうですか` is polite and completely ordinary, but it is *plain* polite - the same level as `です／ます` and no higher. Two situations expose that.

The first is **offering something to a superior or a customer**. <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、コーヒーはどうですか is not rude, exactly, but it is the register you would use with a colleague. お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>はいかがですか is what waiters, hosts and careful employees say, and it costs nothing to learn.

The second is **asking someone senior how something is going**. <ruby>論文<rt>ろんぶん</rt></ruby>はどうですか to your supervisor can sound like you are checking up on them. いかがですか, or <ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>んでいますか, avoids the edge.

---

## ～はどうですか · Nuance & register (cont.)

For proposals, `どうですか` is genuinely soft, because it asks for an opinion rather than a commitment. It is the standard way to float a time, a place or an option in a conversation, and <ruby>水曜日<rt>すいようび</rt></ruby>はどうでしょうか is softer still - `でしょうか` adds a layer of tentativeness that is very common in business email.

Watch the tense. A finished event takes `どうでしたか`; using `どうですか` about last night's party is a real mistake, not a stylistic one.

---

## ～はどうですか · Nuance & register (cont.)

Casually the copula drops: これ、どう？, <ruby>味<rt>あじ</rt></ruby>はどう？ Among friends this is the default, and it can be blunt in the sense of demanding a verdict - asking どう？ about someone's cooking while they watch you eat is as pointed in Japanese as in English.

---

## ～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～はいかがですか

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different altitude. `いかがですか` is the polite form of `どうですか`, and it is the default when offering something to a guest, customer or superior.

デザート**はどうですか**。<br>*How about dessert?*

デザート**はいかがですか**。<br>*Would you care for a dessert?*

`いかが` is not a different word class or a different construction - everything you can do with `どう`, you can do with `いかが`: いかがでしたか, いかがでしょうか. What changes is who you are speaking to.

---

## ～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～はいかがですか (cont.)

The one place `どうですか` is preferred is when you genuinely want a *judgement* between equals: この<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>はどうですか to a colleague asks for their honest read. `いかがですか` in the same slot can sound as though you are serving them something.

---

## ～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～ませんか

> **Rule of thumb** - `はどうですか` proposes an **option** and asks what you think of it; `ませんか` invites the **person** to do something with you.

<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんは、そばとカレーとどちらがいいですか。ここ**はどうですか**。<br>*Soba or curry for lunch? How about here?*

<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べませんか**。<br>*Would you like to have lunch together?*

The second commits you both to an activity; the first only puts a candidate forward inside a plan you are already making. That is why `はどうですか` is so useful in arranging things - dates, venues, options - while `ませんか` is what opens the arrangement in the first place.

---

## ～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～ませんか (cont.)

They stack naturally in one conversation: <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか opens it, <ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>はどうですか settles the detail.

---

## ～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～たらどうですか

> **Rule of thumb** - `はどうですか` offers an option; `たらどうですか` tells the listener what *they* should do. The second is advice, and it can sting.

この<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>**はどうですか**。<br>*How about this medicine?*

<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し**<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだらどうですか**。<br>*Why don't you take a bit of a rest?*

`～たらどうですか` is built on the conditional た-form and means "why don't you…". Said warmly it is caring; said flatly it implies the listener has failed to think of something obvious, which is why it carries a faint edge of impatience. Upward it is best avoided altogether - ～てはいかがでしょうか is the safe version.

---

## ～はどうですか · ～はどうですか vs ～たらどうですか (cont.)

Casual speech shortens it to `～たら？` - <ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったら？ - and the trailing, unfinished shape is exactly what makes it sound offhand.

---

## ～はどうですか · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>のパーティー**はどうですか**。 → ✅ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>のパーティー**はどうでしたか**。<br>*A finished event takes the past form.*

- ❌ <ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**はどうですか**。 → ✅ <ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**のはどうですか**。<br>*A verb needs the nominaliser `の` before `は`.*

- ❌ <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は**どんなですか**。 → ✅ <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は**どうですか**。／**どんな<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>ですか**。<br>*`どんな` cannot stand alone; it must attach to a noun.*

- ❌ <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>、お<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>は**どうですか**。 → ✅ <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>、お<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>は**いかがですか**。<br>*Offering to someone senior calls for `いかが`.*

---

## ～はどうですか · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、もっと<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ったらどうですか**。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、もっと<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**お<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りになってはいかがでしょうか**。<br>*Advice aimed upward needs a much softer frame.*

---

## ～はどうですか · Practice 1

{reveal}

{reveal}

## ～はどうですか · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "How was the exam yesterday?"

{reveal}
<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>**はどうでしたか**。

{reveal}
The exam is over, so the question has to be in the past: `どうでしたか`. `どうですか` would be asking about an exam that is still going on.

## ～はどうですか · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいアパートはどんなですか。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいアパートは**どうですか**。

{reveal}
`どんな` is an attributive - it has to sit in front of a noun, as in `どんなアパートですか`. To ask how something is, on its own, the word is `どう`.

## ～はどうですか · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>**はどうですか**。 (b) <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>い**ませんか**。

{reveal}
(a) puts a date forward as a candidate. It assumes you are already arranging to meet and asks the other person to evaluate the option - it is about the *day*, not about whether to meet at all.

{reveal}
(b) is the invitation itself. It asks whether the listener wants to meet you, and it is the sentence that opens the arrangement rather than filling in its details.
