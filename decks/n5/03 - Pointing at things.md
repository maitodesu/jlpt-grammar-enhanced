- これ・それ・あれ・どれ

- Question words
- 何か・誰か・どこか

# これ・それ・あれ・どれ

**Meaning** · this one / that one / that one over there / which one - the four words that stand in for a *thing* without naming it

These four are **pronouns**. Each one *is* a noun: it takes particles, it sits where a noun sits, and it can be the entire answer to a question. Which one you pick depends on whose territory the thing is in - yours (`これ`), your listener's (`それ`), or neither of yours (`あれ`) - and `どれ` asks the listener to choose one out of several.

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Formation

Nothing attaches to these words; they *are* the noun. The table shows what each one picks out and how it slots into a sentence.

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing - これ is itself a noun | これ = a thing in the speaker's reach | これ、おいしいですね |
| Nothing | それ = a thing in the listener's reach, or one just mentioned | それを<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つください |
| Nothing | あれ = a thing away from both of you | あれは<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>ですか |
| Nothing | どれ = which one, out of **three or more** | どれが<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いですか |
| Particles | これ／それ／あれ／どれ + は・が・を・に・で・と | それと<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じものをください |
| A following noun | ❌ これ + Noun → use この + Noun | ❌ これかばん → ✅ このかばん |
| Two choices only | どれ → どちら（casual どっち） | コーヒーとお<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>、どちらにしますか |
| Polite equivalent | これ／それ／あれ／どれ → こちら／そちら／あちら／どちら | こちらが<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいメニューです |

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - if a noun follows, you need `この・その・あの・どの`. If nothing follows, you need `これ・それ・あれ・どれ`. A word in this series is never sitting directly in front of the thing it points at.

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Examples

**これ**は<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>のかさです。<br>*This is my older sister's umbrella.*

**それ**、ちょっと<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>せてください。<br>*Could you let me see that for a second?*

**あれ**は<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>のビルです。<br>*That building over there is the station.*

メニューの<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で**どれ**が<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いですか。<br>*Which is the cheapest thing on the menu?*

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Examples (cont.)

**それ**はいい<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えですね。<br>*That's a good idea.*

すみません、**これ**と**それ**をください。<br>*Excuse me - I'll take this one and that one.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby><ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>で<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ったカップ、**あれ**はどこにしまいましたか。<br>*That cup we bought in Kyoto last year - where did you put it away?*

<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりましたが、**それ**が<ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>かどうかは<ruby>別<rt>べつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>です。<br>*I follow the explanation, but whether it is true is another matter.*

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Nuance & register

The series itself is register-neutral. `これは…です` is the first full sentence most learners ever build, and it stays usable for life.

Three things are worth knowing before you aim one of these words at a person.

**Never point at a human with これ／それ／あれ.** They are for objects. For people, use <code>この<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby></code> at the blunt end and `こちら` when you are being polite - <code>こちらは<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんです</code> is the standard way to introduce someone. `こいつ`／`そいつ`／`あいつ` also exist for people and they are rough: tolerable between close friends about an absent third party, insulting anywhere else.

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Nuance & register (cont.)

**こちら・そちら・あちら・どちら are the polite upgrade**, and they double as the two-item version of `どれ`. In a shop you will hear `こちら` where a friend would say `これ` - `こちらでよろしいでしょうか`, "will this one be all right?"

**`あれ` on its own is also an interjection** meaning "huh?" - the noise you make when something is not where you left it: `あれ、かぎがない`. Same word on the page, completely different job, and context makes it obvious.

In speech the particle is routinely dropped: `これ、いくら?` rather than `これはいくらですか`. Keep the particle in writing and in ます-speech.

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ vs この・その・あの・どの

> **Rule of thumb** - the れ-series replaces the noun; the の-series introduces it. `これ` = "this one". `この` = "this ___", and the blank is compulsory.

This is the commonest error in the whole topic, because English uses one word - "this" - for both jobs.

**これ**は<ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*This one is heavy.*

**この**はこは<ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*This box is heavy.*

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ vs この・その・あの・どの (cont.)

You cannot mix them: ❌`これはこ` and ❌<code>このは<ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いです</code> are both broken. And unlike the place words, the れ-series does not take `の` in front of a noun either - ❌<code>これの<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby></code> is not how you say "this book"; <code>この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby></code> is.

---

## それ vs あれ - pointing back at the conversation

> **Rule of thumb** - for something *said* rather than seen: `それ` picks up what one of you has just mentioned; `あれ` picks up something you both already know.

Once nothing is physically in the room, distance stops being about metres. `それ` handles information that has only just entered the conversation, from one side or the other.

「<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>があるよ。」「えっ、**それ**、<ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>?」<br>*"There's an exam next week." "Wait - is that true?"*

`あれ` reaches instead for shared memory: something you have both experienced, which is why it can turn up with no antecedent in the sentence at all.

---

## それ vs あれ - pointing back at the conversation (cont.)

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby><ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>で<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>た<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>、**あれ**はよかったね。<br>*That film the two of us saw last year - that was a good one, wasn't it.*

Use `あれ` when the listener does *not* share the memory and you will sound as if you are talking to yourself.

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · どれ vs どちら・どっち

> **Rule of thumb** - `どれ` needs **three or more** candidates. With exactly two, Japanese switches to `どちら`, or `どっち` in casual speech.

English "which" does not care how many there are, so this has to be learned on purpose.

この<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つのうち、**どれ**にしますか。<br>*Which of these three will you have?*

<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>と<ruby>青<rt>あお</rt></ruby>、**どちら**がいいですか。<br>*Red or blue - which would you prefer?*

`どちら` is also simply the polite form of `どれ`, so a shop assistant may use it even facing a whole shelf of options. `どっち` is casual and extremely frequent in speech; keep it out of email.

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Common mistakes

- ❌ **これ**かばんは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のです。 → ✅ **この**かばんは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のです。<br>*A noun follows, so the の-series is required. `これ` can only stand alone.*

- ❌ **これの**<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みました。 → ✅ **この**<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みました。<br>*`の` does not rescue it. Unlike <code>ここの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby></code>, which is perfectly normal, <code>これの<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby></code> is not a thing.*

- ❌ <ruby>紅茶<rt>こうちゃ</rt></ruby>とコーヒー、**どれ**がいいですか。 → ✅ <ruby>紅茶<rt>こうちゃ</rt></ruby>とコーヒー、**どちら**がいいですか。<br>*Two candidates means `どちら`／`どっち`. `どれ` starts at three.*

- ❌ **あれ**は<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんです。 → ✅ **あちら**は<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんです。<br>*`あれ` is for objects. Aimed at a person it is genuinely rude; `あちら`*

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Common mistakes (cont.)

- or <code>あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby></code> among friends - is what you want.

- ❌ 「これは<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>ですか。」「はい、**これ**はペンです。」 → ✅ 「…」「**それ**はペンです。」<br>*Once the object is in your *listener's* hands it has moved into their sphere, so the answer flips to `それ`. Replying with `これ` claims you are the one holding it.*

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Practice 1

**1.** すみません、____をください。（you are pointing at a cake inside the display case, out of reach of both of you）

{reveal}
すみません、**あれ**をください。

{reveal}
The cake is behind glass, away from both you and the shop assistant, so it sits in neither person's sphere - which is exactly what `あれ` is for. `こちら` would be the staff's word, not yours.

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>四<rt>よん</rt></ruby><ruby>足<rt>そく</rt></ruby><ruby>並<rt>なら</rt></ruby>んでいます。（どれ・どちら）が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。

{reveal}
**どれ**が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。

{reveal}
Four pairs means three or more candidates, so `どれ`. `どちら` would tell the listener you are offering a choice between exactly two - or that you are being markedly polite.

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Is that yours? - No, this one is mine."

{reveal}
**それ**はあなたのですか。 - いいえ、**これ**が<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のです。

{reveal}
The thing asked about is near the listener, hence `それ`; the speaker then points at the one in their own reach, hence `これ`. The answer uses `が` because "this one" is being singled out as the correct one among several.

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: これのシャツは<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さいですから、<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きいのをください。

{reveal}
✅ **この**シャツは<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さいですから、<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きいのをください。 "This shirt is too small, so please give me a bigger one."

{reveal}
`これの` is not a possible modifier. When a noun follows, switch to the の-series. The `の` later in the sentence is a different `の` - the pronoun "one", standing in for シャツ.

---

## これ・それ・あれ・どれ · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>、**それ**はもう<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりました。 (b) <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>、**あれ**はもう<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりました。

{reveal}
(a) treats yesterday's matter as something that has just been raised in this conversation - typically the listener brought it up and you are answering their words. It is the neutral, businesslike choice.

{reveal}
(b) treats it as a file you both already have open: shared history you do not need to re-explain. It sounds warmer and more familiar, and it would be strange if the listener had no idea what you were referring to.

# この・その・あの・どの

**Meaning** · this ~ / that ~ / that ~ over there / which ~ - pointing words that modify a noun instead of replacing it

`この` and its siblings are **adnominals**: they do nothing on their own and must be followed immediately by a noun. <code>この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby></code> is "this book", never "this". The こ／そ／あ／ど split is the same one the rest of the series uses - the speaker's side, the listener's side, away from both, and the question.

---

## この・その・あの・どの · Formation

Nothing attaches *to* these words. They attach to the noun on their right, with nothing allowed in between except an adjective.

---

## この・その・あの・どの · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | この + Noun - in the speaker's territory | この<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby> |
| Noun | その + Noun - in the listener's territory, or just mentioned | その<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby> |
| Noun | あの + Noun - away from both, or shared memory | あの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby> |
| Noun | どの + Noun - which ~, out of **three or more** | どの<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby> |
| Adjective + Noun | この + Adj + Noun | この<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>いくつ |
| The particle | この + Noun **+ particle** - never この + particle | この<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のです |
| Nothing | ❌ この on its own → use これ | ❌ このは<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい → ✅ これは<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい |
| Two choices only | どの → どちらの（casual どっちの） | どちらの<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>がいいですか |
| Polite equivalent | この／その／あの → こちらの／そちらの／あちらの | そちらの<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>にお<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きください |

---

## この・その・あの・どの · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `この` is a hook with nothing on it. Something must hang from it, and that something is a noun. If you have no noun to supply, you wanted `これ・それ・あれ・どれ` instead.

---

## この・その・あの・どの · Examples

**この**ペンは<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>のです。<br>*This pen is my father's.*

**その**いすに<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*Please sit on that chair.*

**あの**<ruby>白<rt>しろ</rt></ruby>い<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>は<ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>です。<br>*That white building over there is the library.*

**どの**バスが<ruby>空港<rt>くうこう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますか。<br>*Which bus goes to the airport?*

---

## この・その・あの・どの · Examples (cont.)

**その**<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I have already heard that story.*

**あの**とき、もっと<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ればよかった。<br>*I should have left earlier that time.*

**この**<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>になると<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かですから、<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>するのにいいですよ。<br>*This area goes quiet after dark, so it's a good place to study.*

<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>は**あの**<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>店員<rt>てんいん</rt></ruby>さんにお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いしました。<br>*I gave my order to that tall member of staff over there.*

---

## この・その・あの・どの · Nuance & register

Neutral in itself, and unavoidable - you cannot get through a paragraph of Japanese without it.

**`あの` is also the standard hesitation noise**, usually stretched to `あのう`. It opens a request to a stranger the way "excuse me…" does in English: `あのう、すみません、ちょっとよろしいですか`. Beginners hear it constantly and assume it is the demonstrative; it is not, and it points at nothing.

**`その` is the workhorse of written and business Japanese.** In an email, <code>その<ruby>件<rt>けん</rt></ruby>について</code> ("regarding that matter") refers back to whatever the previous sentence or the previous email established. Anaphoric `その` is far more common in writing than the physical "near you" sense you learn first.

---

## この・その・あの・どの · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Politeness rises through `こちらの`.** <code>あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby></code> is fine among friends but too blunt about a customer or a superior; <code>あちらの<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby></code> is the safe formal version. `あいつ` and friends are the rough end of the same scale and take a `の` too: `あいつの` - avoid in polite company.

**Casual speech shortens `どの` to `どっちの` when there are two**, and drops the noun altogether when it can be guessed: 「どっち?」rather than <code>どちらの<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>がいいですか</code>.

---

## この・その・あの・どの vs これ・それ・あれ・どれ

> **Rule of thumb** - `この` modifies, `これ` replaces. `この` must be followed by a noun; `これ` must **not** be.

They are the same four distances packaged for two different grammatical slots, and mixing them up is the single most frequent mistake in this topic.

**その**<ruby>時計<rt>とけい</rt></ruby>はいくらですか。<br>*How much is that watch?*

それはいくらですか。<br>*How much is that one?*

---

## この・その・あの・どの vs これ・それ・あれ・どれ (cont.)

Both are correct; the first names the object, the second assumes you can see which object is meant. What you may never write is ❌<code>それ<ruby>時計<rt>とけい</rt></ruby></code> or ❌`この、いくらですか`.

There is one asymmetry worth memorising: the place words *do* take `の` before a noun (<code>ここの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby></code>, "the shop here"), but the thing pronouns do not. ❌<code>これの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby></code> is wrong; <code>この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby></code> is right.

---

## この・その・あの・どの · その vs あの

> **Rule of thumb** - `その` = it is on your listener's side, or it entered the conversation a moment ago. `あの` = it is on nobody's side, or we both already know it.

Physically the split is easy. In conversation, where nothing is visible, it is about who owns the information.

「<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいカフェができたよ。」「へえ、**その**カフェ、どこ?」<br>*"A new café has opened." "Oh - where is that café?"*

The speaker has never seen it. It exists for them only because the listener just said so, so `その`.

<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>のとき、よく<ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったよね。**あの**カフェ、まだあるかな。<br>*We used to go together as students, didn't we. I wonder if that café is still there.*

---

## この・その・あの・どの · その vs あの (cont.)

Here the café is in both memories, so `あの`. Using `あの` for something the listener has never heard of sounds as though you are muttering to yourself - which is precisely why writers use it for that effect.

---

## この・その・あの・どの · どの vs どんな

> **Rule of thumb** - `どの` picks one out of a known set. `どんな` asks what kind of thing it is, with no set in front of you.

Both sit in front of a noun and both come out as "what/which" in English, so they collapse together unless you separate them deliberately.

<ruby>棚<rt>たな</rt></ruby>の<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>のうち、**どの**<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby><ruby>役<rt>やく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ちますか。<br>*Of the books on the shelf, which book is the most useful?*

**どんな**<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>をよく<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みますか。<br>*What kind of books do you usually read?*

---

## この・その・あの・どの · どの vs どんな (cont.)

Answer the first with a book you can point at; answer the second with a category - <ruby>歴史<rt>れきし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>, <ruby>小説<rt>しょうせつ</rt></ruby>. A quick test: if the English is "which of these", it is `どの`; if it is "what sort of", it is `どんな`.

---

## この・その・あの・どの · Common mistakes

- ❌ **この**は<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のかばんです。 → ✅ **これ**は<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のかばんです。<br>*`この` cannot stand alone. Either supply a noun (<code>このかばんは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のです</code>) or switch to `これ`.*

- ❌ **それ**<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってください。 → ✅ **その**<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*A noun follows, so the の-series is required. `それ` never sits in front of a noun.*

- ❌ **あのの**<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ **あの**<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>です。<br>*`この・その・あの・どの` already end in the `の` they need. Adding a second one is doubling up.*

- ❌ <ruby>肉<rt>にく</rt></ruby>と<ruby>魚<rt>さかな</rt></ruby>、**どの**<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>にしますか。 → ✅ <ruby>肉<rt>にく</rt></ruby>と<ruby>魚<rt>さかな</rt></ruby>、**どちらの**<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>にしますか。<br>*Two options means `どちらの`. `どの` needs three or more to choose between.*

---

## この・その・あの・どの · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ **どの**<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。（meaning "what kind of music do you like?"）→ ✅ **どんな**<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。<br>*`どの` would be asking the listener to point at one specific piece of music from a list in front of them. For a genre, you want `どんな`.*

---

## この・その・あの・どの · Practice 1

**1.** ____<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>は<ruby>百年<rt>ひゃくねん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>建<rt>た</rt></ruby>てられました。（the building is a long way off, visible from where you both stand）

{reveal}

# ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ

**Meaning** · here / there / over there / where - the four words that name a *place* without naming it

These are place pronouns. Like `これ` they stand alone and take particles, but they denote locations, which changes which particles they attract: `に` for existence and destination, `で` for where something happens, `から`／`まで` for the ends of a route. Note the shape of the third one - it is `あそこ`, not `あこ`. That is the one irregularity in the whole こそあど system.

---

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Formation

Nothing attaches to these words; they *are* the place noun. What follows is a particle.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing - ここ is itself a noun | ここ = where the speaker is | ここで<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちます |
| Nothing | そこ = where the listener is, or a place just mentioned | そこに<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>いてください |
| Nothing | あそこ = a place away from both of you | あそこまで<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>きましょう |
| Nothing | どこ = where | どこに<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいますか |
| Particle に | Place + に - existence or destination | ここに<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きます |
| Particle で | Place + で - where an action happens | あそこで<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いましょう |
| から／まで | Place + から／まで - route ends | ここから<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby> |
| **Noun modification** | ここ／そこ／あそこ／どこ **+ の +** Noun | ここの<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>はおいしい |

---

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Polite equivalent | ここ／そこ／あそこ／どこ → こちら／そちら／あちら／どちら | どちらにお<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>まいですか |

> **Rule of thumb** - `に` puts something *at* the place, `で` makes something *happen* at the place. <code>ここに<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>がある</code> versus <code>ここで<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む</code>. Getting this pair wrong is the single most audible error learners make with place words.

---

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Examples

かばんは**ここ**に<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*I'll put my bag here.*

<ruby>出口<rt>でぐち</rt></ruby>は**どこ**ですか。<br>*Where is the exit?*

**そこ**、ちょっと<ruby>危<rt>あぶ</rt></ruby>ないですよ。<br>*It's a bit dangerous where you're standing.*

**あそこ**の<ruby>角<rt>かど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>右<rt>みぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>曲<rt>ま</rt></ruby>がってください。<br>*Turn right at that corner over there.*

---

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Examples (cont.)

**ここ**のパンは<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby><ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>き<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がります。<br>*The bread here comes out of the oven at six in the morning.*

<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>、**どこ**でとったんですか。<br>*Where did you take these holiday photos?*

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たら、**そこ**から<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>目<rt>め</rt></ruby>の<ruby>信号<rt>しんごう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>左<rt>ひだり</rt></ruby>に<ruby>曲<rt>ま</rt></ruby>がってください。<br>*Once you leave the station, turn left at the third set of lights from there.*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どものころ<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいた<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>戻<rt>もど</rt></ruby>ったが、**あそこ**はもうすっかり<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わってしまった。<br>*I went back to the town I lived in as a child, but the place has changed completely.*

---

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Nuance & register

Neutral, and used constantly. Three extensions are worth knowing.

**They point at abstract "places" too.** In an explanation or an argument, `ここ` means "this point in what I am saying" and `そこ` means "that point you just made": <code>ここが<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>です</code> ("this is the important bit"), <code>そこが<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>なんです</code> ("that is exactly the problem"). This use is extremely common and completely idiomatic; a learner who only knows the physical sense will misread it.

**`ここ` also stretches over time.** <code>ここ<ruby>一週間<rt>いっしゅうかん</rt></ruby></code> means "these last/next seven days", and <code>ここ<ruby>数年<rt>すうねん</rt></ruby></code> "these past few years". `そこ` and `あそこ` do not do this.

---

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Nuance & register (cont.)

**`こちら`・`そちら`・`あちら`・`どちら` are the polite register**, and in service or business Japanese they replace the plain words wholesale. Asked for your address you will hear <code>どちらにお<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>まいですか</code>, never <code>どこに<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいますか</code>. `どちら` is also how you ask a customer where they are from. Meanwhile `ここら`／`そこら` ("round about here / there") is the casual end of the scale.

One caution on `そこ` with people: <code>そこの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby></code>, shouted, is the Japanese equivalent of "oi, you there" - accurate grammar, terrible manners.

---

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ vs これ・それ・あれ and この・その・あの

> **Rule of thumb** - three parallel series, three different jobs. `これ` is a *thing* standing alone, `この` modifies a following noun, and `ここ` is a *place* standing alone.

Everything in the こそあど system shares the same four-way distance split; the series you pick is decided by what grammatical slot you need to fill.

**ここ**は<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>です。<br>*This place is a bank. (You are standing in it.)*

これは<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>のかぎです。<br>*This is the key to the bank. (An object in your hand.)*

---

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ vs これ・それ・あれ and この・その・あの (cont.)

The place words have one privilege the thing pronouns lack: **they take `の` before a noun.** <code>ここの<ruby>店員<rt>てんいん</rt></ruby></code> ("the staff here") and <code>どこの<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby></code> ("which company / a company from where") are both normal, whereas ❌<code>これの<ruby>店員<rt>てんいん</rt></ruby></code> is impossible - you would have to say <code>この<ruby>店員<rt>てんいん</rt></ruby></code>. So `ここの` is a genitive, and `この` is a demonstrative; they are not interchangeable.

---

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · ここに vs ここで

> **Rule of thumb** - `に` marks the place a thing *is* or *ends up*; `で` marks the stage on which an action is performed.

English "here" and "there" carry no such distinction, so this must be chosen consciously every time.

**ここに**<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*There is a bicycle here.*

**ここで**<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>します。<br>*I fix bicycles here.*

---

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · ここに vs ここで (cont.)

Existence verbs and verbs of arriving or putting take `に`; everything active takes `で`. The test that catches most cases: if you can rephrase the English as "there is / it goes onto", use `に`; if it is "the activity takes place at", use `で`.

---

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · どこ vs どちら

> **Rule of thumb** - same question, two registers. `どこ` is neutral, `どちら` is polite, and `どちら` additionally means "which of two".

トイレは**どこ**ですか。<br>*Where's the toilet?*

お<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>は**どちら**ですか。<br>*Where are you from? (polite)*

Asking a customer, a client or an older stranger `どこですか` is not ungrammatical, just noticeably plain. In the other direction, `どっち` is the casual contraction and belongs with friends. Note that `どちら` doing double duty - "where" and "which of two" - is resolved by context alone; nobody finds it ambiguous in practice.

---

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Common mistakes

- ❌ **あこ**に<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>があります。 → ✅ **あそこ**に<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*The あ-form of the place series is irregular. `これ→あれ` and `この→あの` drop the そ, but `ここ→あそこ` keeps it.*

- ❌ **ここで**<ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>があります。 → ✅ **ここに**<ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*`で` marks the setting of an action. Existing is not an action, so it takes `に`.*

- ❌ **ここに**<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。 → ✅ **ここで**<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*The mirror image: working *is* an action, so the place takes `で`.*

- ❌ **これ**は<ruby>入口<rt>いりぐち</rt></ruby>です。（pointing at a doorway you are standing in）→ ✅ **ここ**が<ruby>入口<rt>いりぐち</rt></ruby>です。<br>*A location needs the place series, not the thing series. `これ` would mean you were holding the entrance.*

---

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ レストランが<ruby>三<rt>さん</rt></ruby><ruby>軒<rt>げん</rt></ruby>あります。**どこの**<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>にしますか。 → ✅ **どの**<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>にしますか。<br>*<code>どこの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby></code> asks which *area or chain* a shop belongs to. To pick one out of a set in front of you, use `どの`.*

---

{reveal}

{reveal}
<code><ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>く</code> is a verb of putting, so the landing place takes `に`, not `で`. The question word for a place is `どこ`; in a formal setting you could raise it to `どちら`.

**2.** <ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>（に・で）<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>をします。

{reveal}
<ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>をします。

{reveal}
Doing homework is an activity, and the library is the stage it happens on, so `で`. You would use `に` only for existence or arrival - for example <code><ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます</code>.

{reveal}

{reveal}
Place words take `の` to modify a following noun, which is how you say "the coffee of that place". `あそこ` because the shop is away from both speaker and listener.

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: パスポートはあこにありますか。

{reveal}

{reveal}

## ここ・そこ・あそこ・どこ · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) **そこ**が<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby><ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しいところです。 (b) **ここ**が<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby><ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しいところです。

{reveal}
Neither has to involve a physical location. (a) picks up the point the *listener* has just raised - "that, what you just mentioned, is the hard part" - so it acknowledges their contribution.

{reveal}

# Question words

**Meaning** · the words that ask what, who, when, where, how, why, what kind and how much

Japanese does not rearrange a sentence to ask a question. The question word simply occupies the slot the answer will occupy, the word order stays exactly as it was, and `か` goes on the end. <code><ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます</code> becomes <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ますか</code> - one word swapped, nothing moved.

---

## Question words · Formation

One row per word. Each sits in the slot its answer would fill.

---

## Question words · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun slot | <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>／<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby> - what | これは<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>ですか |
| Noun slot | <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby> - who（polite どなた） | <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ますか |
| Noun slot | いつ - when（never takes `に`） | <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>はいつですか |
| Noun slot | どこ - where（polite どちら） | <ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>はどこですか |
| Adverb slot | どう - how, in what state | コーヒーはどうですか |
| Adverb slot | どうして - why（neutral, spoken and written） | どうして<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れたんですか |
| Adverb slot | なぜ - why（formal, mostly written） | なぜ<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>くのか |
| **Before a noun** | どんな + Noun - what kind of | どんな<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか |
| Noun slot | いくら - how much（price, amount） | これはいくらですか |

---

## Question words · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - a question word that is the subject takes `が`, never `は`. `は` marks something already known, and the whole point of a question word is that it is not known. ✅<code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りましたか</code> ／ ❌<code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りましたか</code>. The reply keeps the `が` too: <code><ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>が<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りました</code>.

> **Reading rule for <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>** - it is read **なん** before a sound in the た／だ／な rows (で・と・に・だ・です・の) and before every counter; it is read **なに** everywhere else. So <ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>ですか, <ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>, <ruby>何人<rt>なんにん</rt></ruby>, <ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby> - but <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>が, <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>を, <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か, <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も.

---

## Question words · Examples

このマークは**<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>**ですか。<br>*What is this symbol?*

**<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>**がピアノを<ruby>弾<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>きますか。<br>*Who is going to play the piano?*

**いつ**<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ましたか。<br>*When did you come to Japan?*

この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>は**どう**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みますか。<br>*How do you read this kanji?*

---

## Question words · Examples (cont.)

**どんな**<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>をよく<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ますか。<br>*What sort of films do you watch a lot?*

<ruby>地下鉄<rt>ちかてつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>換<rt>か</rt></ruby>えは**どこ**でしますか。<br>*Where do you change subway lines?*

**どうして**<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby><ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだんですか。<br>*Why were you off school yesterday?*

<ruby>送料<rt>そうりょう</rt></ruby>も<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れると、<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby>で**いくら**になりますか。<br>*Including postage, how much does it come to altogether?*

---

## Question words · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>報告書<rt>ほうこくしょ</rt></ruby>には、**なぜ**この<ruby>方法<rt>ほうほう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>選<rt>えら</rt></ruby>んだのかを<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください。<br>*In the report, please write why you chose this method.*

---

## Question words · Nuance & register

The words themselves are neutral; what varies is politeness and how blunt the question sounds.

**Politeness ladder.** <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby></code> → `どなた` and `どこ` → `どちら` are the two upgrades you will actually need. 「<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>ですが、どなたですか」is how you ask a stranger who they are; <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>ですか</code> at the same moment is abrupt. `いくら` has a formal partner too - `おいくらですか`, and in shops `おいくらになりますか`.

---

## Question words · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Softening with ん／の.** A bare <code>どうして<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかったんですか</code> is softer than <code>どうして<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ませんでしたか</code>, because the `ん` frames the question as asking for an explanation you are ready to accept rather than demanding one. Without it, "why" questions can land as accusations in Japanese just as in English.

**Three words for "why".** `なんで` is the casual spoken one, `どうして` the everyday neutral one, `なぜ` the formal and written one. `なんで` from a subordinate to a boss sounds childish; `なぜ` between friends sounds like a lecture.

---

## Question words · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Rising intonation replaces `か` in casual speech**, and the question word carries the stress: 「どこ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くの?」「<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる?」 Adding `か` to a plain form - ❌<code>どこ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くか?</code> - sounds rough or interrogational, so leave `か` for the ます-form.

Finally, note that all of these words also serve as the building blocks for indefinites: add `か` and <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby></code> becomes "something", add `も` with a negative and it becomes "nothing". Those are separate patterns with their own pages.

---

## Question words · どうして vs なぜ vs なんで

> **Rule of thumb** - all three mean "why". `なんで` is casual speech, `どうして` is the neutral default, `なぜ` is formal and belongs mainly to writing.

There is no difference in meaning, only in register, which is why choosing wrongly is so noticeable.

**なんで**<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れたの?<br>*How come you're late?*

**なぜ**<ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きたのか、まだ<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かっていません。<br>*It is still not understood why the accident occurred.*

---

## Question words · どうして vs なぜ vs なんで (cont.)

`どうして` sits comfortably in both of those situations and is the safe choice if you are unsure. One extra wrinkle: `どうして` can also mean "by what means, how" in older or set expressions, and `なんで` written in kanji as <code><ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>で</code> is ambiguous with "by what means" - see the reading section below.

---

## Question words · どう vs どんな

> **Rule of thumb** - `どう` asks about a state or a manner and stands alone; `どんな` asks what *kind* of thing and must be followed by a noun.

Both come out as "how" or "what" in English and both are asking for a description, so they blur together fast.

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は**どう**ですか。<br>*How's the new job?*

**どんな**<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>ですか。<br>*What kind of job is it?*

---

## Question words · どう vs どんな (cont.)

The first invites an evaluation - busy, fun, tiring. The second asks for the category - sales, teaching, night shifts. Grammatically, ❌<code>どう<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby></code> is impossible, because `どう` is an adverb and cannot modify a noun; and ❌<code><ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>はどんなですか</code> is impossible, because `どんな` cannot stand alone. If you want the adjective-like form on its own, it is <code>どんな<ruby>感<rt>かん</rt></ruby>じ</code>.

---

## Question words · 何 vs 何 - reading the same kanji two ways

> **Rule of thumb** - **なん** before で・と・に・だ・です・の and before any counter; **なに** everywhere else, including on its own.

The kanji never changes, so this is invisible on the page and audible the moment you speak.

<ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べましたか。<br>*What did you eat, and at what time?*

Both readings in one sentence: `なんじ` because a counter follows, `なに` because `を` follows. Further examples: <ruby>何人<rt>なんにん</rt></ruby>, <ruby>何回<rt>なんかい</rt></ruby>, <ruby>何日<rt>なんにち</rt></ruby>, <ruby>何曜日<rt>なんようび</rt></ruby> all take なん - but <ruby>何色<rt>なにいろ</rt></ruby> ("what colour") and <ruby>何語<rt>なにご</rt></ruby> ("what language") take なに, because those are not counters.

---

## Question words · 何 vs 何 - reading the same kanji two ways (cont.)

The pair that actually causes trouble is <code><ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>で</code> versus <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>で</code>.

**<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>をやめたんですか。<br>*Why did you quit the company?*

**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ましたか。<br>*What did you come to the office by?*

Same three characters, two readings, two meanings: なんで is "why", なにで is "by what means". In speech people avoid the ambiguity by saying `どうして` for the first and <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>って</code> for the second.

---

## Question words · 何 vs 何 - reading the same kanji two ways (cont.)

> **Note** - <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>に</code> is the one place the rule wobbles. Following the な-row rule it should be なんに, and 「<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>になさいますか」("what will you have?") is indeed なん. But 「<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>に<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>うの?」("what's it for?") is normally なに. Both readings are heard; treat this cell as genuinely variable rather than as a rule you have got wrong.

---

## Question words · Common mistakes

- ❌ これは**なに**ですか。 → ✅ これは**なん**ですか。<br>*`です` begins with a だ-row sound, so the reading is なん. The kanji is identical, which is why this survives for years unnoticed.*

- ❌ <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>**は**この<ruby>絵<rt>え</rt></ruby>をかきましたか。 → ✅ <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>**が**この<ruby>絵<rt>え</rt></ruby>をかきましたか。<br>*`は` presents known information. A question word is by definition unknown, so it takes `が`.*

- ❌ **どう**<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。 → ✅ **どんな**<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きですか。<br>*`どう` is an adverb and cannot sit in front of a noun. To ask "what kind of", you need `どんな`.*

---

## Question words · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで**いくら**かかりますか。（asking about *time*）→ ✅ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで**どのくらい**かかりますか。<br>*`いくら` asks about money or quantity. For duration and distance the word is `どのくらい`／`どれくらい`. With `かかる` both are possible, so state which you mean.*

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、**なんで**この<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>にしたんですか。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、**どうして**この<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>にされたんですか。<br>*Not a grammar error but a register one: `なんで` to a superior sounds like a child demanding an explanation.*

---

## Question words · Practice 1

**1.** ____がこのケーキを<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りましたか。

{reveal}
**<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>**がこのケーキを<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りましたか。 "Who made this cake?"

{reveal}
The missing element is the subject, so the question word goes straight into the subject slot with `が` - not `は`, because an unknown person cannot be a known topic.

## Question words · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>は（どう・どんな）でしたか。

{reveal}
<ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>は**どう**でしたか。 "How was your weekend?"

{reveal}
`どんな` cannot stand alone in front of `です`; it always needs a noun after it. To use `どんな` here you would have to say <code>どんな<ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>でしたか</code>, which asks what sort of weekend it was rather than how it went.

---

## Question words · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese, formally, for a written report: "Why did the number of customers fall?"

{reveal}
**なぜ**お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>さんの<ruby>数<rt>かず</rt></ruby>が<ruby>減<rt>へ</rt></ruby>ったのでしょうか。

{reveal}
`なぜ` is the register the task asks for - `どうして` would be acceptable but plainer, and `なんで` would be wrong in a report. `のでしょうか` keeps the tone measured rather than accusatory.

## Question words · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: すみません、この<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>ですか。<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れましたか。

{reveal}
✅ すみません、この<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>**ですか。**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>**を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れましたか。 "Excuse me, what is this parcel? What did you put in it?"

{reveal}
The two readings are swapped. `です` triggers なん; `を` triggers なに. The written sentence looks identical either way, so only reading it aloud exposes the error.

## Question words · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) **<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますか。 (b) **<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますか。

{reveal}
They are the same three characters and differ only in reading. (a), read なんで, asks *why* you are going to Tokyo - it is the casual equivalent of `どうして`.

{reveal}

# 何か・誰か・どこか

**Meaning** · something / someone / somewhere - an unspecified one of a kind, rather than a request to identify it

Add `か` to a question word and it stops asking and starts referring. <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby></code> demands "what?"; <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か</code> says "some thing or other, and I am not saying which - possibly because I do not know, possibly because it does not matter". The reading is **なにか**, not なんか.

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · Formation

The `か` fuses onto the question word. What comes after it is an ordinary predicate.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby> | <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby> + か → <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か - something, anything | <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる? |
| <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby> | <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby> + か → <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>か - someone（polite どなたか） | <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>か<ruby>呼<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでください |
| どこ | どこ + か → どこか - somewhere（polite どちらか） | どこかで<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みましょう |
| いつ | いつ + か → いつか - sometime, one day | いつか<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>します |
| どれ | どれ + か → どれか - one of them（three or more） | どれか<ruby>選<rt>えら</rt></ruby>んでください |
| どちら | どちら + か → どちらか - one of the two | どちらかに<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めてください |
| Particles が・を・は | **dropped** | ❌<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>かを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>う → ✅<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>う |
| Particles に・で・へ・と・から | **kept, after the か** | <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>かに<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>く／どこかで<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った |

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - a `か`-word makes the sentence a **yes/no** question, not a content question. <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いましたか</code> is answered はい or いいえ. <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いましたか</code> demands a list of purchases. Same English word "what", opposite conversations.

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · Examples

**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みますか。<br>*Would you like something to drink?*

**<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>か**いますか。<br>*Is anybody there?*

<ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か**<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>っていますか。<br>*Is there anything left in the fridge?*

<ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>、**どこか**へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか。<br>*Shall we go somewhere at the weekend?*

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · Examples (cont.)

**<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>か**に<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いたほうがいいですね。<br>*We had better ask somebody for directions.*

**いつか**<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>馬<rt>うま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>ってみたい。<br>*One day I want to try riding a horse in Hokkaido.*

さっきから**どこか**で<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>鳴<rt>な</rt></ruby>っているが、<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ない。<br>*A phone has been ringing somewhere for a while now, but nobody is picking up.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か**<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>しておきたいことはありますか。<br>*Is there anything you would like to check before the meeting?*

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and very high frequency in speech, largely because Japanese prefers a vague offer to a specific one.

**The particle drop is the natural form.** <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>かを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる</code> is not exactly ungrammatical, but it is stiff and rarely said; <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる</code> is what people actually produce. The same goes for `が` - <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>か<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たよ</code>, not <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>かが<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たよ</code> in ordinary speech, although the `が` version does appear in writing when the subject needs emphasis. `は` never attaches at all, because `は` announces a known topic and a `か`-word is by definition unknown.

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Vagueness is politeness.** Offering <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みますか</code> lets the guest steer, whereas naming a drink pushes them into accepting or refusing that drink. The same softening drives <code>どこか<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きたいところある?</code> when planning a day out.

**`なんか` is a different word.** Written the same way in kanji, but `なんか` functions as "or something / like / kind of": `コーヒーなんかどうですか` ("how about a coffee or something?"), or as pure filler - <code>なんか、<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れた</code>. It is casual and, used as filler, slightly juvenile. Do not read <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か</code> as なんか.

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · Nuance & register (cont.)

**<code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か</code> can also mean "somehow, somewhat"** in front of an adjective: <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>かおかしい</code>, "something's not right". No noun is being referred to at all.

**Polite upgrades** exist and are worth having: `どなたか` for <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>か</code>, and `どちらか` for `どこか` when you mean a place. On the phone: `どなたかいらっしゃいますか`.

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · 何か・誰か・どこか vs 何も・誰も・どこも

> **Rule of thumb** - `か` is the positive indefinite and works with any predicate; `も` is the negative one and **requires** a negative verb. They are a matched pair, and a question in `か` is usually answered in `も`.

This is the pairing to drill, because the two sets are one syllable apart and mean opposite things.

<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはん、**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べましたか。<br>*Did you eat anything for lunch?*

いいえ、いそがしくて<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べませんでした。<br>*No - I was busy and didn't eat anything.*

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · 何か・誰か・どこか vs 何も・誰も・どこも (cont.)

Note that the も-answer cannot be positive: ❌<code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました</code> is broken Japanese. And the か-question cannot be negated into a "nothing": ❌<code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>か<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ませんでした</code> does not mean "nobody came" - for that you need <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ませんでした</code>.

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · 何か食べましたか vs 何を食べましたか

> **Rule of thumb** - with `か` you are asking **whether**; with `を` you are asking **what**. The first expects はい／いいえ, the second expects a noun.

English "did you eat something / anything" versus "what did you eat" makes the same split, but learners import the `を` version by default and end up interrogating people.

<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>、**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みましたか。<br>*Did you have anything to drink this morning?*

<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みましたか。<br>*What did you drink this morning?*

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · 何か食べましたか vs 何を食べましたか (cont.)

The second presupposes that you drank; it is fine between friends and pushy from a stranger. Use the `か` form when you do not yet know whether the event happened at all.

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · 何か vs 何でも

> **Rule of thumb** - `か` means "some one thing, unspecified". `でも` means "any at all, take your pick". `か` narrows to one; `でも` opens the whole set.

Both land on English "anything", which hides the difference completely.

**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か**<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>はありますか。<br>*Are there any questions?*

<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてください。<br>*Ask me anything you like.*

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · 何か vs 何でも (cont.)

The first asks whether at least one question exists. The second grants permission across the entire range. So <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>か<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>って</code> means "could someone help", while <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>える</code> means "anyone at all is able to help".

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · Common mistakes

- ❌ reading <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か</code> as **なんか** → ✅ **なにか**<br>*`なんか` is a separate colloquial word meaning "or something / kind of". The indefinite pronoun is なにか, always.*

- ❌ **<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>か**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ませんでした。 → ✅ **<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ませんでした。<br>*Negating a `か`-word does not give you "nobody". The か-sentence would mean something closer to "it wasn't the case that someone came", which is not what you want; switch to the も-series.*

- ❌ **<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>かは**この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めますか。 → ✅ **<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>か**この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めますか。<br>*`は` marks a known topic and cannot attach to an indefinite. `が` and `を` are also dropped after a か-word.*

---

## 何か・誰か・どこか · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ **いつかに**<ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby>でもしましょう。 → ✅ **いつか**<ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby>でもしましょう。<br>*`いつか` is already adverbial, like `いつ` itself. Time words of this kind take no `に`.*

---

# 何も・誰も・どこも

**Meaning** · nothing / no one / nowhere - a question word plus `も`, finished off with a **negative** verb

Japanese builds "nothing" out of two halves. <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も</code> supplies the "any", and the negative ending on the verb supplies the "not". Neither half works alone: <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べません</code> is "I don't eat anything", while ❌<code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます</code> is not a sentence. English puts the negation in the pronoun; Japanese puts it in the verb, every time.

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · Formation

The negative sits on the predicate at the end of the clause, however far away that is.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby> | <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby> + も + **negative** - nothing | <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えない |
| <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby> | <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby> + も + **negative** - no one | <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない |
| どこ | どこ + も + **negative** - nowhere | どこも<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いていない |
| どれ | どれ + も + **negative** - none of them | どれも<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>らない |
| Particles が・を・は | **dropped** | ❌<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>もを → ✅<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も |
| Particles に・で・へ・と | **inserted before the も** | <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>**に**も／どこ**へ**も／<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>**と**も |
| Emphatic | <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も → <ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>にも（なんにも） | <ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>っていない |
| Positive predicate | どこも／どれも + affirmative = **every** | どこも<ruby>混<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいる |

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the particle goes **inside**, not outside. It is <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わない</code>, not ❌<code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>もに<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わない</code>. Think of it as `question word + particle + も`, with `が`, `を` and `は` simply deleted at that first slot.

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · Examples (cont.)

この<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わないでください。<br>*Please don't tell anyone about this.*

<ruby>三<rt>さん</rt></ruby><ruby>着<rt>ちゃく</rt></ruby><ruby>試<rt>ため</rt></ruby>してみたが、**どれも**サイズが<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わなかった。<br>*I tried three of them on, but none of them fitted.*

**<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げなかったので、<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>った<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby>をしていた。<br>*Nobody put their hand up, so the teacher looked stuck.*

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>してきたばかりで、この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>には<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>り<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いが**<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も**いない。<br>*I've only just moved here, so I don't know a soul in this town.*

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · Nuance & register

Neutral in register; what varies is force.

**`なんにも` is the emphatic version.** Written <code><ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>にも</code>, it is <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も</code> with feeling - <code><ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>にもない</code> means the fridge is *completely* empty. Spoken, slightly whiny, very common. Its counterpart for people is <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby><ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>…ない</code>, which is the *formal* intensifier: <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby><ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby><ruby>反対<rt>はんたい</rt></ruby>しなかった</code>, "not a single person objected".

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Not every も-word is negative.** This is the trap. `いつも` means "always" and takes a positive verb. `どこも` and `どれも` swing both ways: with a negative they mean "nowhere" and "none", with a positive they mean "everywhere" and "every one of them" - `どれもおいしかった`, "they were all delicious". Only <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も</code> is reliably negative-only. <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も</code> is negative in ordinary sentences, but <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>もが</code>, with `が` added, flips to "everyone" and belongs to formal writing: <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>もが<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby></code>.

**It is the standard answer to a `か`-question.** 「<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った?」 「ううん、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も」 - the negative verb can even be left off entirely when the context supplies it, and trailing off after <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も</code> is completely natural in speech.

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Word order is flexible but the negative is not.** You can move <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも</code> around the sentence freely; what you cannot do is let the verb come out positive.

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · 何も・誰も・どこも vs 何か・誰か・どこか

> **Rule of thumb** - `か` is the positive indefinite, `も` the negative one. `か` sentences end in a positive verb; `も` sentences end in a negative one.

They are the same nine question words wearing opposite hats, and a `か`-question is normally answered with a `も`-sentence.

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>かいますか。<br>*Is there anybody in the room?*

いいえ、**<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も**いません。<br>*No, there's no one.*

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · 何も・誰も・どこも vs 何か・誰か・どこか (cont.)

The two cannot be crossed. ❌<code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>かいません</code> does not mean "there is nobody" - it is at best a strange way of denying that someone is present. And ❌<code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>もいます</code> is simply ungrammatical.

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · 何も vs 何でも

> **Rule of thumb** - `も` with a negative = "not any". `でも` with a positive = "any at all, whichever". One closes the set, the other throws it open.

English "anything" covers both, so the two get swapped constantly.

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も**しませんでした。<br>*I did nothing yesterday.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>なら<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>でもできます。<br>*Tomorrow I can do anything you like.*

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · 何も vs 何でも (cont.)

The same split runs through the whole set: <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない</code> ("nobody comes") versus <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>られる</code> ("anybody can come"); <code>どこにも<ruby>売<rt>う</rt></ruby>っていない</code> ("it isn't sold anywhere") versus <code>どこでも<ruby>売<rt>う</rt></ruby>っている</code> ("it's sold everywhere").

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · どこも with a negative vs どこも with a positive

> **Rule of thumb** - `どこも` and `どれも` take their meaning from the verb. Negative verb → "nowhere / none". Positive verb → "everywhere / all of them".

This is the one place where the "も means negative" shortcut misleads you, and the resulting misreadings are total reversals of meaning.

<ruby>正月<rt>しょうがつ</rt></ruby>は**どこも**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いていなかった。<br>*Nothing was open over New Year.*

<ruby>正月<rt>しょうがつ</rt></ruby>は**どこも**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだった。<br>*Everywhere was quiet over New Year.*

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · どこも with a negative vs どこも with a positive (cont.)

<code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も</code> does not behave this way - it stays negative - and <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も</code> only turns positive in the fixed written pattern <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>もが</code>. So the swing is a property of `どこも` and `どれも` specifically. Read to the end of the sentence before deciding what a も-word means.

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました。 → ✅ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べませんでした。<br>*<code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も</code> carries no negation of its own. The verb has to supply it.*

- ❌ パーティーで**<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>もと**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>さなかった。 → ✅ パーティーで**<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>とも**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>さなかった。<br>*The particle goes between the question word and `も`, not after it. Likewise <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも</code>, `どこへも`, `どこでも`.*

- ❌ <ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>みは**どこも**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんでした。 → ✅ <ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>みは**どこへも**（or **どこにも**）<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんでした。<br>*With a verb of motion the destination particle is required. Bare `どこも` before <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く</code> is heard casually but reads as sloppy, and it collides with the "everywhere" sense.*

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ お<ruby>皿<rt>さら</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>もを**<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>さないでください。 → ✅ お<ruby>皿<rt>さら</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>さないでください。<br>*`を`, `が` and `は` are all deleted after a も-word. Only `に`, `で`, `へ` and `と` survive, and they go inside.*

- ❌ reading <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も</code> as **なんも** → ✅ **なにも**<br>*`も` is not in the た／だ／な rows, so <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby></code> keeps its なに reading. `なんも` exists in some regional speech but is not standard.*

## 何も・誰も・どこも · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けたが、<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>には____<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っていなかった。

{reveal}
<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>には**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っていなかった。 "I opened my wallet, but there was nothing in it."

{reveal}
The verb is already negative, so the slot needs the も-series, not the か-series. <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か</code> here would produce a strange denial rather than "nothing".

---

## 何も・誰も・どこも · Practice 2

**2.** すみません、この<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>について（<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>か・<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も）<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>はいませんか。

{reveal}
**<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>か**<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>はいませんか。

{reveal}
Although the sentence ends in a negative, that negative belongs to `いませんか`, which is a polite *invitation* - "isn't there anyone who…?" You are hoping the answer is yes, so the positive indefinite `か` is correct. <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も</code> would flatly assert that no such person exists.

## 何も・誰も・どこも · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Don't say anything to anyone."

{reveal}
**<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わないでください。

{reveal}
Two も-words can share one negative verb without any conflict - Japanese has no double-negative rule. `に` sits inside <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも</code> because it marks the person spoken to, while <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も</code> drops its `を`.

## 何も・誰も・どこも · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>連休<rt>れんきゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>温泉<rt>おんせん</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったが、どれもホテルが<ruby>空<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いていました。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>連休<rt>れんきゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>温泉<rt>おんせん</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったが、**どのホテルも**<ruby>空<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いていませんでした。 "I went to a hot spring over the long weekend, but none of the hotels had rooms."

{reveal}
Two problems. `どれも` cannot be followed by the noun it refers to - attach `も` to the noun instead: `どのホテルも`. And the intended meaning is "none", so the verb must be negative; as written, the sentence claimed every hotel was free.

## 何も・誰も・どこも · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は**どこも**<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいた。 (b) <ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は**どこも**<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいなかった。

{reveal}
(a) has a positive verb, so `どこも` reads as "every one of them": every shop was crowded. (b) has a negative verb, so the same `どこも` reads as "not any of them": no shop was crowded anywhere.

{reveal}
The two sentences are identical up to the ending, which is why you must read a も-word all the way to the verb before deciding what it means. With <code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も</code> this ambiguity never arises - it is negative-only.
