# ～おかげで

**Meaning** · thanks to; because of - a good result, with the credit handed to whatever made it possible

`おかげで` does two things at once: it names a cause, and it thanks it. The second half of the sentence has to be something the speaker is glad about, and the first half gets the credit for it. That built-in gratitude is also what makes the pattern usable as sarcasm - put a plainly awful result after it and you are not thanking anybody.

---

## ～おかげで · Formation

`おかげ` is a noun, so whatever comes in front of it attaches the way it would attach to any noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + の + おかげで | <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>のおかげで |
| Verb (past) | た-form + おかげで | <ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ってくれた → <ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ってくれたおかげで |
| Verb (non-past) | plain form + おかげで | <ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいる → <ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいるおかげで |
| い-adjective | plain form + おかげで | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いおかげで |
| な-adjective | ～**な** + おかげで | <ruby>丈夫<rt>じょうぶ</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>丈夫<rt>じょうぶ</rt></ruby>なおかげで |
| Sentence-final | ～おかげだ／おかげです | <ruby>皆<rt>みな</rt></ruby>さんのおかげです |
| Fixed courtesy | おかげさまで + clause | おかげさまで<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>です |

---

## ～おかげで · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - there is no ❌<code><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>だおかげで</code> and no ❌<code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだおかげで</code>. A noun takes `の`, a な-adjective takes `な`. The past forms are fine as they stand: <code><ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みだったおかげで</code>.

`おかげで` can also open a sentence on its own once the cause is already on the table: <ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んで<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>た。おかげで<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>にはすっかり<ruby>良<rt>よ</rt></ruby>くなっていた。

---

## ～おかげで · Examples

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>の**おかげで**<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合格<rt>ごうかく</rt></ruby>できました。<br>*Thanks to you, sir, I passed the exam.*

<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>の**おかげで**<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がった。<br>*The medicine brought my fever down.*

<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>空<rt>す</rt></ruby>いていた**おかげで**、<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>より<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*The roads were clear, so we got there earlier than planned.*

アプリが<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>いやすい**おかげで**、<ruby>祖母<rt>そぼ</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>できます。<br>*Because the app is easy to use, even my grandmother can book on her own.*

---

## ～おかげで · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>が<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してくれた**おかげで**、<ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>運<rt>はこ</rt></ruby>ばずにすんだ。<br>*My brother drove us, so I got out of hauling the heavy bags.*

**おかげさまで**、<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>で<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby>になります。<br>*Thanks to everyone's support, the shop turns ten this year.*

<ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>余計<rt>よけい</rt></ruby>なことを<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってくれた**おかげで**、<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>がすっかりこじれたよ。<br>*Thanks to you sticking your oar in, the whole thing has come apart.*

<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>職場<rt>しょくば</rt></ruby>で<ruby>数字<rt>すうじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>厳<rt>きび</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>上司<rt>じょうし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>鍛<rt>きた</rt></ruby>えられた**おかげで**、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>決算<rt>けっさん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>時期<rt>じき</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いていられます。<br>*A boss who was strict about the figures put me through my paces at my last job, and thanks to that I can keep my head even at year-end close.*

---

## ～おかげで · Nuance & register

`おかげで` itself is register-neutral. It sits happily in plain speech and in a formal address alike - <ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>かったよ、<ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>のおかげ on one side, <ruby>皆様<rt>みなさま</rt></ruby>のおかげをもちまして on the other - and nothing about choosing it makes you sound stiff or casual. It is normally written in kana; the kanji spelling お<ruby>陰<rt>かげ</rt></ruby>で turns up in older or deliberately literary text.

`おかげさまで` is a separate, fixed courtesy. It thanks a vague, unnamed "everyone" and needs no stated cause, which is why it is the standard answer to お<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>ですか and the standard opener when reporting good news to someone outside your circle. It is warm and slightly formal. Bare `おかげで` cannot take that slot: 「はい、おかげで」 sounds like a sentence someone abandoned halfway.

---

## ～おかげで · Nuance & register (cont.)

**The sarcastic use.** This is the part learners cannot parse when they first meet it. Because `おかげで` builds gratitude into the grammar, pairing it with a result that is obviously unwanted produces irony - thanks you clearly do not mean:

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>三回<rt>さんかい</rt></ruby>も<ruby>延<rt>の</rt></ruby>びた**おかげで**、<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>びました。<br>*Three postponements of the meeting, and my whole summer schedule is gone.*

---

## ～おかげで · Nuance & register (cont.)

Three things flag it. The result is plainly bad; the cause is often a person, and often the person you are talking to; and `～てくれた` frequently sits in front of it, since a benefactive "did me the favour of" is exactly the form you twist. Aimed at someone, it is not a gentle jab - it bites harder than `せいで`, because `せいで` only blames, while ironic `おかげで` also needles the other person for thinking they helped. Aimed at yourself it is merely rueful: <ruby>寝坊<rt>ねぼう</rt></ruby>した**おかげで**、<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>バタバタだった。

---

## ～おかげで · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two smaller tendencies. Speakers normally credit other people or circumstances rather than themselves, so <ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>のおかげ, while grammatical, tends to come across as crowing. And when the result is bad and you are *not* being ironic, `おかげで` is simply the wrong word - you want `せいで`.

---

## ～おかげで · ～おかげで vs ～せいで

> **Rule of thumb** - same slot, opposite verdict. `おかげで` says the outcome was good and hands out credit; `せいで` says it was bad and hands out blame. The grammar is identical, so the choice is pure evaluation.

<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>もった**おかげで**、<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもたちは<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>よろこびだった。<br>*The snow settled, and the children were over the moon.*

<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>もった**せいで**、<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まった。<br>*The snow settled, and it stopped every train that morning.*

The cause is one and the same event. What decides the word is whether the speaker is pleased, and a listener hears that verdict immediately - which is why the sarcastic use lands at all.

---

## ～おかげで · ～おかげで vs ～から・～ので

> **Rule of thumb** - `から` and `ので` merely connect a cause to a result. `おかげで` connects them *and* passes judgement. If you have nothing to be grateful for, use the N5 conjunctions.

<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>っていた**ので**、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>っておきました。<br>*There were cheap seats left, so I went ahead and bought them.*

<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>っていた**おかげで**、<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby><ruby>全員<rt>ぜんいん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>けることになりました。<br>*Cheap seats were still going, and thanks to that the whole family can come.*

---

## ～おかげで · ～おかげで vs ～から・～ので (cont.)

There is also a hard structural difference. `から` and `ので` can be followed by a request, an invitation or a command; `おかげで` cannot, because you cannot be grateful in advance for something that has not happened yet. ❌<code><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>や</rt></ruby>んだおかげで、<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけましょう</code> - use `ので`.

---

## ～おかげで · ～おかげで vs ～ため（に）

> **Rule of thumb** - `ため` is the flat, formal cause with no opinion in it. `おかげで` is warm. Announcements and reports take `ため` precisely because it declines to evaluate.

<ruby>補助金<rt>ほじょきん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>た**ため**、<ruby>設備<rt>せつび</rt></ruby>を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>替<rt>か</rt></ruby>えることができた。<br>*A subsidy came through, and the equipment could be replaced.*

<ruby>補助金<rt>ほじょきん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>た**おかげで**、<ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>い<ruby>機械<rt>きかい</rt></ruby>をやっと<ruby>手放<rt>てばな</rt></ruby>せました。<br>*The subsidy came through, and thanks to it we could finally get rid of the old machines.*

The first is a line from a report. The second has a person in it who is relieved. Put `ため` into a thank-you speech and it sounds like minutes of a meeting.

---

## ～おかげで · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>の**おかげで**<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になりました。 → ✅ <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>の**せいで**<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になりました。<br>*A cancelled trip is not good news, and a typhoon cannot be teased. With no target for the irony, this just reads as a mistake.*

- ❌ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>だおかげで → ✅ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>**の**おかげで<br>*`おかげ` is a noun. A noun in front of it links with `の`, never with `だ`.*

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだおかげで → ✅ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**な**おかげで<br>*な-adjectives take `な` before it, exactly as they do before `ので`.*

---

## ～おかげで · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ 「お<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>ですか。」「はい、おかげで。」 → ✅ 「はい、おかげさまで。」<br>*The fixed reply is `おかげさまで`. Bare `おかげで` in that slot sounds like an unfinished sentence.*

- ❌ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>ができた**おかげで**、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んでください。 → ✅ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>ができた**ので**、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んでください。<br>*A request cannot follow `おかげで`; what comes after it has to be something that already turned out well.*

---

## ～おかげで · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>先輩<rt>せんぱい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>付<rt>つ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ってくれた____、<ruby>大会<rt>たいかい</rt></ruby>で<ruby>勝<rt>か</rt></ruby>てました。

{reveal}
<ruby>付<rt>つ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ってくれた**おかげで**、<ruby>大会<rt>たいかい</rt></ruby>で<ruby>勝<rt>か</rt></ruby>てました。

{reveal}
The result is a win, and someone else made it possible, so this is exactly the slot `おかげで` exists for. `ので` would be grammatical, but it would throw the thanks away.

---

## ～おかげで · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>電池<rt>でんち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>れた（おかげで・せいで）、<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一枚<rt>いちまい</rt></ruby>も<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>れなかった。

{reveal}
<ruby>電池<rt>でんち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>れた**せいで**、<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一枚<rt>いちまい</rt></ruby>も<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>れなかった。

{reveal}
Losing all your photos is a bad outcome, so the blaming word is the right one. `おかげで` would only work here as heavy sarcasm aimed at whoever forgot to charge the battery, and with no such person in the sentence it simply reads as an error.

---

## ～おかげで · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Thanks to the map you drew for me, I didn't get lost."

{reveal}
<ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてくれた**おかげで**、<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>迷<rt>まよ</rt></ruby>わずにすみました。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてくれた</code> already marks the favour, and `おかげで` picks it up as the reason for the good outcome. <code><ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>のおかげで</code> is also fine and a little shorter; keeping `てくれた` makes the thanks more personal.

---

## ～おかげで · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>丈夫<rt>じょうぶ</rt></ruby>だおかげで、<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>入院<rt>にゅういん</rt></ruby>したことがありません。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>丈夫<rt>じょうぶ</rt></ruby>**な**おかげで、<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>入院<rt>にゅういん</rt></ruby>したことがありません。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>丈夫<rt>じょうぶ</rt></ruby></code> is a な-adjective and `おかげ` is a noun, so the two are joined with `な`. The `だ` survives only in the past form: <code><ruby>丈夫<rt>じょうぶ</rt></ruby>だったおかげで</code>.

---

## ～おかげで · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>がアドバイスしてくれた**おかげで**、<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby>やり<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>すことになったよ。 (b) <ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>のアドバイスの**おかげで**、うまくいったよ。

{reveal}
(b) is the straightforward use: the advice helped, the speaker is grateful, and `おかげで` says so.

{reveal}
(a) is sarcasm. Having to redo everything is plainly not a good outcome, so the gratitude built into `おかげで` cannot be sincere - the speaker is complaining, and `してくれた` sharpens it into "you did me the *favour* of advising me." Said to a close friend it is a rueful jab; said to a colleague it is a real reproach.

# ～せいで

**Meaning** · because of; owing to - a bad result, with the blame pinned on what caused it

`せい` is a noun meaning *fault*. `せいで` therefore does not just report a cause; it points a finger at it. Everything after `せいで` is something the speaker wishes had not happened, and everything before it is what they hold responsible.

---

## ～せいで · Formation

Structurally `せいで` is the mirror image of `おかげで` - `せい` is a noun, so the same attachment rules apply.

---

## ～せいで · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + の + せいで | <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>のせいで |
| Verb (past) | た-form + せいで | <ruby>寝坊<rt>ねぼう</rt></ruby>した → <ruby>寝坊<rt>ねぼう</rt></ruby>したせいで |
| Verb (non-past) | plain form + せいで | <ruby>混<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいる → <ruby>混<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいるせいで |
| い-adjective | plain form + せいで | うるさい → うるさいせいで |
| な-adjective | ～**な** + せいで | <ruby>複雑<rt>ふくざつ</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>複雑<rt>ふくざつ</rt></ruby>なせいで |
| Sentence-final | ～せいだ／せいです | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>判断<rt>はんだん</rt></ruby>のせいです |
| Hedged cause | ～せいか | <ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>のせいか |
| Assigning blame | Noun + の + せいにする | <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>のせいにする |

---

## ～せいで · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - `せいか` swaps certainty for a guess: "perhaps because ～". It is how you name a cause you cannot prove, and it is very common with age, weather and mood: <ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>のせいか、<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>りが<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>い。 The frozen phrase <ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>のせい means "it's only your imagination" and never takes `で`.

---

## ～せいで · Examples

<ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>の**せいで**<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ません。<br>*I've lost my voice because of a cold.*

<ruby>寝坊<rt>ねぼう</rt></ruby>した**せいで**、バスに<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れた。<br>*I overslept and missed the bus because of it.*

<ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>がうるさい**せいで**、<ruby>全然<rt>ぜんぜん</rt></ruby><ruby>集中<rt>しゅうちゅう</rt></ruby>できない。<br>*The noise from the roadworks is so loud I can't concentrate at all.*

<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>複雑<rt>ふくざつ</rt></ruby>な**せいで**、<ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>所<rt>ところ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>戻<rt>もど</rt></ruby>ってしまった。<br>*The streets are so tangled that I kept ending up back in the same place.*

---

## ～せいで · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>の**せいか**、<ruby>夜中<rt>よなか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>目<rt>め</rt></ruby>が<ruby>覚<rt>さ</rt></ruby>めるようになった。<br>*Maybe it's my age, but I've started waking up several times a night.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の**せいで**<ruby>皆<rt>みな</rt></ruby>さんにご<ruby>迷惑<rt>めいわく</rt></ruby>をおかけして、<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>ありません。<br>*I'm very sorry to have caused you all this trouble on my account.*

<ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けたのは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>判断<rt>はんだん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>かった**せいです**。<br>*We lost because I was too slow to make the call.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby><ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>な<ruby>計画<rt>けいかく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>けた**せいで**、チーム<ruby>全体<rt>ぜんたい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>半年<rt>はんとし</rt></ruby><ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みなしで<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>くことになってしまった。<br>*Taking on an impossible schedule last year has left the whole team working six months without a break.*

---

## ～せいで · Nuance & register

Grammatically `せいで` is neutral - it appears in plain speech and in ます-form without changing shape, and neither men nor women lean on it more. What is not neutral is what it *does*. Naming someone's `せい` is an accusation, and Japanese tends to make accusations obliquely, so the word carries more weight than a learner expects from its plain English translation "because of".

Three practical consequences.

**Aimed at a person, it is strong.** <ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>のせいだ is close to "this is on you", and it is not a phrase you use with a superior or a customer. Between friends it is normal; in a workplace it usually gets softened to <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby><ruby>不足<rt>ぶそく</rt></ruby>でした or to a `ため` clause with nobody in it.

---

## ～せいで · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Aimed at yourself, it is an apology.** <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のせいで is how you take responsibility, and it is completely at home in polite speech - the sentence around it simply goes into keigo.

**Public language avoids it entirely.** Station announcements, notices and press releases use `ため`, because an organisation reporting a delay is describing a situation, not assigning fault. A `せいで` in that register would sound like the railway company complaining about its own accident.

The related verb `～のせいにする` means "blame it on ～", and <code><ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>のせいにする</code> - "blame other people" - is a stock criticism of character. `せいか`, by contrast, is soft and slightly musing; it is the one member of the family that does not accuse anyone.

---

## ～せいで · ～せいで vs ～おかげで

> **Rule of thumb** - identical grammar, opposite verdict. `せいで` says "and that was bad"; `おかげで` says "and thank goodness". Neither is available for a neutral statement of cause.

<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になった**せいで**、<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby><ruby>無駄<rt>むだ</rt></ruby>にした。<br>*The match was called off, and it cost me a whole day.*

<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になった**おかげで**、<ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>しぶりに<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>と<ruby>過<rt>す</rt></ruby>ごせた。<br>*The match was called off, and thanks to that I got a rare day with my family.*

Same event, two speakers, two verdicts. Note that `おかげで` can be turned ironic and used for a bad outcome, but the reverse does not work: `せいで` with a genuinely happy result is not sarcasm, just an error.

---

## ～せいで · ～せいで vs ～ため

> **Rule of thumb** - `ため` reports; `せいで` complains. Anything written on behalf of an organisation takes `ため`.

<ruby>人身<rt>じんしん</rt></ruby><ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>の**ため**、<ruby>上<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>線<rt>せん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れが<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ております。<br>*Owing to an accident involving a passenger, northbound services are running late.*

<ruby>人身<rt>じんしん</rt></ruby><ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>の**せいで**、<ruby>大事<rt>だいじ</rt></ruby>な<ruby>面接<rt>めんせつ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れそうだ。<br>*Because of some accident on the line I'm going to be late for an important interview.*

The first is the station announcement; the second is the passenger swearing at it. The facts are the same, and only the speaker's stake in them has changed. This is the single most useful register test in this family of patterns: if the sentence belongs on a sign, it takes `ため`.

---

## ～せいで · ～せいで vs ～ばかりに

> **Rule of thumb** - `せいで` blames the cause; `ばかりに` regrets it. `ばかりに` means "just because of that one small thing", and the speaker is usually the one who has to live with the consequence.

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>一言<rt>ひとこと</rt></ruby>の**せいで**、<ruby>場<rt>ば</rt></ruby>の<ruby>空気<rt>くうき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>くなった。<br>*One remark from him soured the whole mood of the room.*

<ruby>一言<rt>ひとこと</rt></ruby><ruby>余計<rt>よけい</rt></ruby>なことを<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>った**ばかりに**、<ruby>長年<rt>ながねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>友人<rt>ゆうじん</rt></ruby>をなくしてしまった。<br>*Just because I said one thing too many, I lost a friend of many years.*

`せいで` points outward at an ordinary-sized cause. `ばかりに` insists that the cause was trivial and the price enormous, and it is characteristically self-directed.

---

## ～せいで · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>の**せいで**<ruby>合格<rt>ごうかく</rt></ruby>できました。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>の**おかげで**<ruby>合格<rt>ごうかく</rt></ruby>できました。<br>*Passing is a good outcome, so this reads as if you were blaming your teacher for your own success. The evaluation, not the grammar, is what is wrong.*

- ❌ <ruby>大雪<rt>おおゆき</rt></ruby>の**せいで**<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休業<rt>きゅうぎょう</rt></ruby>させていただきます。 → ✅ <ruby>大雪<rt>おおゆき</rt></ruby>の**ため**<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休業<rt>きゅうぎょう</rt></ruby>させていただきます。<br>*A notice on a shop door is written for the public, and public language does not blame. Use `ため`.*

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだせいで → ✅ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**な**せいで<br>*`せい` is a noun, so a な-adjective needs `な` and a noun needs `の`.*

---

## ～せいで · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>い**せいで**、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けてください。 → ✅ <ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>い**ので**、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けてください。<br>*A request cannot follow `せいで`; the second clause has to be a result that has already gone wrong.*

- ❌ それは<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>の**せいで**ですよ。 → ✅ それは<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>の**せい**ですよ。<br>*<code><ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>のせい</code> ("you're imagining it") is a fixed noun phrase. It takes `だ`／`です`, never `で`.*

## ～せいで · Practice 4

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>不十分<rt>ふじゅうぶん</rt></ruby>**だった**せいで、<ruby>参加者<rt>さんかしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>えてしまった。

## ～せいで · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>の**せいで**、この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>も<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かじゃない。 (b) <ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>の**せいか**、この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>も<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かじゃない。

{reveal}

{reveal}
(b) offers the cause as a guess - "maybe it's the roadworks". The speaker has noticed the noise, has a suspicion, and is not committing to it. `せいか` also softens the accusation considerably, which is why it is the safer choice when you are speculating about something a person did.

# ～ために - purpose

**Meaning** · in order to; for the sake of - the aim you are acting towards

`ために` states a **goal** and then the action taken to reach it. The goal comes first, the action second, and the two are joined by the speaker's intention: I want that, therefore I do this. With a noun in front, it shifts slightly to "for the benefit of" - the person or thing the effort is aimed at.

---

## ～ために - purpose · Formation

`ため` is a noun, so it takes `の` after another noun and the plain attributive form after a verb or adjective.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain non-past) | dictionary form + ために | <ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>える → <ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えるために |
| Noun | Noun + の + ために | <ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby> → <ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>のために |
| Modifying a noun | ～ため + **の** + Noun | <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>のための<ruby>参考書<rt>さんこうしょ</rt></ruby> |
| Setting up a condition | ～ため**には** | <ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける → <ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けるためには |
| Sentence-final | ～ためだ／ためです | <ruby>貯金<rt>ちょきん</rt></ruby>するためです |
| Written style | ～ため（`に` dropped） | <ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>するため、<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>しました |

---

## ～ために - purpose · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the verb in front of purpose `ために` must be **non-past** and must be something you can decide to do. Past tense in that slot switches the whole sentence to the *cause* meaning, and a verb nobody can will - <ruby>治<rt>なお</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>喜<rt>よろこ</rt></ruby>ぶ, potential forms, negatives - normally calls for `ように` instead.

Both clauses normally share a subject: the person with the goal is the person who acts. The `Noun + のために` version escapes that restriction, which is why you can do something <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どものために without the child doing anything at all.

---

## ～ために - purpose · Examples

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>く**ために**、<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>を<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しています。<br>*I'm studying Japanese in order to work in Japan.*

<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>の**ために**、<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby>お<ruby>弁当<rt>べんとう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*I make packed lunches every morning for my family.*

<ruby>健康<rt>けんこう</rt></ruby>の**ために**、エレベーターを<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>わないようにしている。<br>*For my health, I make a point of not using the lift.*

<ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>する**ために**、<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>かけてお<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>をためた。<br>*I spent three years saving up so that I could study abroad.*

---

## ～ために - purpose · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby><ruby>暮<rt>ぐ</rt></ruby>らしを<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける**ためには**、もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>収入<rt>しゅうにゅう</rt></ruby>がいる。<br>*If I'm going to keep living on my own, I need a bit more income.*

これは<ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>現場<rt>げんば</rt></ruby>に<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れる**ための**<ruby>研修<rt>けんしゅう</rt></ruby>です。<br>*This is training designed to get new staff used to the site.*

<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いでいるのは、<ruby>終電<rt>しゅうでん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わせる**ためです**。<br>*The reason I'm rushing is to make the last train.*

<ruby>地域<rt>ちいき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>たちの<ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>守<rt>まも</rt></ruby>る**ために**、<ruby>毎年<rt>まいとし</rt></ruby><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>時期<rt>じき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>避難<rt>ひなん</rt></ruby><ruby>訓練<rt>くんれん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>行<rt>おこな</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*To keep the people of the area safe, we run an evacuation drill at the same time every year.*

---

## ～ために - purpose · Nuance & register

`ために` sits a step above casual conversation. It is entirely normal in speech, but it has a slightly deliberate, purposeful ring, and friends chatting are more likely to say <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>きたいから<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>してる than to build a purpose clause. Written Japanese goes the other way and often drops the `に` altogether: <ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby><ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>のため、<ruby>一時<rt>いちじ</rt></ruby><ruby>停止<rt>ていし</rt></ruby>します。

`ためには` is worth learning as a unit. The `は` turns the goal into a premise - "if the aim is X, then…" - and it is the standard opening for advice, requirements and analysis: <ruby>上達<rt>じょうたつ</rt></ruby>するためには、<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しずつ <ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けることです。 `ためだけに` adds "solely, and for nothing else", often with a hint that the effort was excessive.

---

## ～ために - purpose · Nuance & register (cont.)

One social note. <code><ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>のために<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っているんだ</code> - "I'm saying this for your own good" - is a stock line of someone overstepping, and Japanese speakers hear it that way. `のために` aimed at a listener claims to know what is good for them, so in a request or a piece of advice it is often safer to leave the phrase out and let the kindness be implied.

---

## ～ために - purpose · ～ために vs ～ように

> **Rule of thumb** - `ために` for goals you act on directly; `ように` for outcomes you can only arrange for. If the verb in front is a potential form, a negative, or something that happens to someone else, you want `ように`.

This is the split that decides most sentences, and it is mechanical enough to test.

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>える**ために**、<ruby>毎晩<rt>まいばん</rt></ruby><ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>で<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*I write kanji out by hand every night in order to learn them.*

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れない**ように**、<ruby>毎晩<rt>まいばん</rt></ruby><ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>で<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*I write kanji out by hand every night so that I don't forget them.*

---

## ～ために - purpose · ～ために vs ～ように (cont.)

Learning is something you set out to do; not forgetting is a state you can only make likely. The same test explains the subject rule:

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもが<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けられる**ように**、<ruby>棚<rt>たな</rt></ruby>を<ruby>低<rt>ひく</rt></ruby>くしました。<br>*I lowered the shelves so the children can tidy up by themselves.*

The person acting is the parent; the person doing the tidying is the child, and <code><ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けられる</code> is a potential form. Both point to `ように`. Replace it with `ために` and the sentence claims the parent is going to do the tidying.

---

## ～ために - purpose · ～ために for purpose vs ～ため for cause

> **Rule of thumb** - check the tense and the verb type. Purpose: non-past, willed, pointing forward. Cause: past tense, adjectives, or events nobody chose, pointing backward.

<ruby>資格<rt>しかく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る**ために**、<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>一週間<rt>いっしゅうかん</rt></ruby><ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みました。<br>*I took a week off work in order to get the qualification.*

<ruby>資格<rt>しかく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>れなかった**ため**、<ruby>昇進<rt>しょうしん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一年<rt>いちねん</rt></ruby><ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れました。<br>*Because I failed to get the qualification, my promotion was put back a year.*

---

## ～ために - purpose · ～ために for purpose vs ～ため for cause (cont.)

Nothing but the form of the first verb has changed. `～る + ために` is read as an aim; `～た + ため` and `～ない + ため` are read as a reason. The cause use also accepts adjectives and nouns freely - <code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いため</code>, <code><ruby>大雨<rt>おおあめ</rt></ruby>のため</code> - while the purpose use cannot, because an adjective is not something you do on purpose.

---

## ～ために - purpose · ～ために vs ～のに

> **Rule of thumb** - `ために` names your aim. The `のに` of purpose comments on whether something is *suited* to a task: convenient, necessary, expensive, time-consuming.

<ruby>缶<rt>かん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ける**ために**、<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>道具<rt>どうぐ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*I bought a new tool in order to open the tins.*

この<ruby>道具<rt>どうぐ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>缶<rt>かん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ける**のに**<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>です。<br>*This tool is handy for opening tins.*

---

## ～ために - purpose · ～ために vs ～のに (cont.)

The tell is the predicate that follows. If it is an ordinary action verb, use `ために`; if it is <code><ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>だ</code>, <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>だ</code>, <code><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がかかる</code>, `いくらかかる` or the like, `のに` is the natural partner. Note that this is the *purpose* `のに`, not the "although" `のに` learners meet first; the two are told apart by exactly this test.

---

## ～ために - purpose · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>ために<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。 → ✅ <ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>**の**ために<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*`ため` is a noun, so another noun links to it with `の`.*

- ❌ <ruby>健康<rt>けんこう</rt></ruby>になったために<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っています。 → ✅ <ruby>健康<rt>けんこう</rt></ruby>になるために<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*Purpose `ために` never takes past tense. With `なった` the sentence flips to a cause reading - "because I got healthy" - which is not what you meant.*

- ❌ <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>かない**ために**、<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ます。 → ✅ <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>かない**ように**、<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*Catching a cold is not something you decide, so the negative goal takes `ように`.*

---

## ～ために - purpose · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもが<ruby>喜<rt>よろこ</rt></ruby>ぶ**ために**、ケーキを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。 → ✅ <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもが<ruby>喜<rt>よろこ</rt></ruby>ぶ**ように**、ケーキを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*Two different subjects and a verb the speaker cannot perform. ✅<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの**ために**ケーキを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました is the other repair - with a noun, `のために` is free of the same-subject rule.*

- ❌ これは<ruby>外国人<rt>がいこくじん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>生活<rt>せいかつ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れるために<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ …<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れる**ための**<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>です。<br>*In front of a noun, `ため` links with `の`. `ために` can only modify a predicate.*

---

## ～ために - purpose · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>つ____、<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby><ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いてお<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>をためました。

{reveal}

# ～ために - cause

**Meaning** · because of; owing to - the flat, formal statement of a cause, with no opinion attached

This is the same `ため` as the purpose one, doing the opposite job. Here it looks backwards: something happened, and this clause names what brought it about. Its defining quality is that it **refuses to evaluate**. It neither thanks nor blames, which is exactly why signs, announcements, reports and business email are full of it.

---

## ～ために - cause · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (past) | た-form + ため | <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れた → <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れたため |
| Verb (non-past) | plain form + ため | <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>かない → <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>かないため |
| い-adjective | plain form + ため | <ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>いため |
| い-adjective (past) | ～かった + ため | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>かった → <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>かったため |
| な-adjective | ～**な** + ため | <ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby>なため |
| Noun | Noun + **の** + ため | <ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>のため |
| Noun (past) | ～だった + ため | <ruby>休日<rt>きゅうじつ</rt></ruby>だった → <ruby>休日<rt>きゅうじつ</rt></ruby>だったため |
| Sentence-final | ～ためです | <ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になったのは<ruby>大雨<rt>おおあめ</rt></ruby>のためです |

---

## ～ために - cause · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - in the cause sense the `に` is very often dropped. `～ため、` with a comma is the standard shape on a notice; `～ために` is equally correct and sounds a shade less clipped. The purpose sense, by contrast, keeps its `に` most of the time. That is a tendency and not a test - the tense of the verb in front is the reliable signal.

---

## ～ために - cause · Examples

<ruby>大雨<rt>おおあめ</rt></ruby>の**ため**、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>延期<rt>えんき</rt></ruby>されました。<br>*Owing to heavy rain, the match has been postponed.*

<ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>の**ため**、<ruby>道路<rt>どうろ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいます。<br>*The roads are congested because of an accident.*

<ruby>部品<rt>ぶひん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>かない**ため**、<ruby>出荷<rt>しゅっか</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>になります。<br>*As the parts have not arrived, shipping will be next week.*

<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>が<ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>い**ため**、この<ruby>棟<rt>とう</rt></ruby>にはエレベーターがありません。<br>*This wing has no lift, the building being an old one.*

---

## ～ために - cause · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>交通<rt>こうつう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby>な**ため**、この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>家賃<rt>やちん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い。<br>*Rents are low around here because the transport links are poor.*

<ruby>停電<rt>ていでん</rt></ruby>になった**ために**、<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby><ruby>作業<rt>さぎょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まってしまった。<br>*The power went out, and work was at a standstill for a whole day.*

お<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>みが<ruby>定員<rt>ていいん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>達<rt>たっ</rt></ruby>した**ため**、<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>終了<rt>しゅうりょう</rt></ruby>いたしました。<br>*Applications having reached the limit, registration is now closed.*

<ruby>担当者<rt>たんとうしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入院<rt>にゅういん</rt></ruby>した**ため**、<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>説明会<rt>せつめいかい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>日程<rt>にってい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>改<rt>あらた</rt></ruby>めてご<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>いたします。<br>*As the member of staff in charge has been taken into hospital, we will write again with new dates for next month's briefing.*

---

## ～ために - cause · Nuance & register

`ため` is the written cause. It belongs to notices, station announcements, news copy, minutes, academic prose and any email where you are speaking as a company rather than as yourself. In relaxed conversation it sounds starched: a friend who says <ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>かったため<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れたよ is either joking or reading from a form. The everyday equivalents are `から` and `ので`.

What makes it worth the stiffness is its neutrality. `せいで` blames and `おかげで` thanks; `ため` does neither, so a railway can announce <ruby>人身<rt>じんしん</rt></ruby><ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>のため without implying anything about anybody. When you need to explain a problem to a customer or a superior and want no accusation audible in the sentence, `ため` is the safe word.

---

## ～ために - cause · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two structural habits follow from the register. First, what comes after a cause `ため` is normally a **statement**, not a request or an invitation; when a notice does end in お<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします, the `ため` in front of it is usually the purpose one （<ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby><ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>のため、…）. If you need "because X, please do Y", reach for `ので`. Second, `ため` prefers reasons that are impersonal and verifiable - weather, accidents, rules, numbers - over reasons that are matters of feeling. <ruby>寂<rt>さび</rt></ruby>しかったため<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>した is grammatical and slightly absurd.

---

## ～ために - cause · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, be aware that `ため` is genuinely ambiguous in some sentences. <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>のために<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>辞<rt>や</rt></ruby>めた can mean "I quit for my father's sake" or "I quit because of my father", and only context decides. When it matters, make the cause reading explicit with a verb: <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>倒<rt>たお</rt></ruby>れたため<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>辞<rt>や</rt></ruby>めた。

---

## ～ために - cause · ～ため vs ～から・～ので

> **Rule of thumb** - same relationship, different register. `から` is personal and pushes; `ので` is polite and explains; `ため` is institutional and reports.

<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>もっている**から**、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>をやめよう。<br>*The snow's piled up, so let's not take the car today.*

<ruby>積雪<rt>せきせつ</rt></ruby>の**ため**、<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>路線<rt>ろせん</rt></ruby>バスを<ruby>運休<rt>うんきゅう</rt></ruby>といたします。<br>*Owing to snowfall, bus services are suspended today.*

---

## ～ために - cause · ～ため vs ～から・～ので (cont.)

The second sentence is not more polite than the first in any simple sense - it is *less personal*. Notice what else changed: the vocabulary went Sino-Japanese （<ruby>積雪<rt>せきせつ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>運休<rt>うんきゅう</rt></ruby>）and the ending went humble. `ため` travels with that whole register, and dropping it into casual speech drags the register along with it.

---

## ～ために - cause · ～ため for cause vs ～ために for purpose

> **Rule of thumb** - read the verb in front. Past tense, a negative, an adjective or a noun means cause. A non-past verb that names something you can decide to do means purpose.

<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>凍<rt>こお</rt></ruby>っていた**ため**、<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>かかった。<br>*The roads were frozen, so it took an hour to get to the office.*

<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>節約<rt>せつやく</rt></ruby>する**ために**、<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>くに<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいる。<br>*I live near the office in order to save time.*

---

## ～ために - cause · ～ため for cause vs ～ために for purpose (cont.)

Adjectives and nouns settle the question on their own: nobody acts *in order to be* cold, so <code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いため</code> and <code><ruby>大雨<rt>おおあめ</rt></ruby>のため</code> can only be causes. The one shape that stays genuinely ambiguous is `Noun + のために` with a person in it, which is why the note above suggests spelling the cause out with a verb.

---

## ～ために - cause · ～ため vs ～せいで・～おかげで

> **Rule of thumb** - all three name a cause. `せいで` adds blame, `おかげで` adds thanks, `ため` adds nothing. Choosing `ため` is choosing not to have an opinion on the record.

システムの<ruby>不具合<rt>ふぐあい</rt></ruby>の**ため**、ご<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れております。<br>*Owing to a system fault, confirmation of your order has been delayed.*

システムの<ruby>不具合<rt>ふぐあい</rt></ruby>の**せいで**、<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>がまだ<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>できない。<br>*Thanks to some system fault I still can't confirm my order.*

The first is what the shop writes; the second is what the customer says. Note that `ため` is also the escape hatch when the real cause is somebody's mistake and you do not want to say so - this is why company apologies so often name a system, a process or the weather in a `ため` clause.

---

## ～ために - cause · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>だため<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になりました。 → ✅ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>**の**ため<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になりました。<br>*`ため` is a noun; a noun in front of it links with `の`.*

- ❌ <ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby>だため<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まらない。 → ✅ <ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby>**な**ため<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まらない。<br>*な-adjectives take `な`, exactly as they do before `ので`.*

- ❌ お<ruby>腹<rt>なか</rt></ruby>がすいた**ため**、コンビニに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こう。 → ✅ お<ruby>腹<rt>なか</rt></ruby>がすいた**から**、コンビニに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こう。<br>*A personal impulse and a volitional ending are the opposite of what `ため` is for. It sounds like a hunger incident report.*

---

## ～ために - cause · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い**ため**、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めてください。 → ✅ <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い**ので**、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めてください。<br>*A cause `ため` clause is normally followed by a statement. Requests take `ので`.*

- ❌ <ruby>海外<rt>かいがい</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったため、パスポートを<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>りました。 → ✅ <ruby>海外<rt>かいがい</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**ために**、パスポートを<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*With the past tense the sentence claims you went abroad first and got the passport afterwards. Purpose needs the non-past form.*

# ～もの - because

**Meaning** · because - the reason you give when you are defending yourself

`もの` is the excuse-maker's "because". It comes in two shapes. At the end of a sentence, `もの` (almost always shortened to `もん`) answers back: *because, that's why*. In front of a clause, `ものだから` and `もので` explain away something that went wrong: *it's just that…*. Both belong to speech, and both put the speaker's feelings on display in a way `から` and `ので` do not.

---

## ～もの - because · Formation

Always written in kana in this use. Note the split: the sentence-final form takes `だ` after a noun or な-adjective, while `ものだから` takes `な`.

---

## ～もの - because · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb, sentence-final | plain form + もの／もん | <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない → <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないもん |
| い-adjective, sentence-final | plain form + もの／もん | <ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>いもん |
| な-adj・Noun, sentence-final | ～**だ** + もの／もん | <ruby>嫌<rt>いや</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>嫌<rt>いや</rt></ruby>だもん |
| After ～んだ | ～んだ + もの／もん | <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しかったんだ → <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しかったんだもん |
| Verb・い-adj, excuse clause | plain form + ものだから | <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いでいた → <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いでいたものだから |
| な-adj・Noun, excuse clause | ～**な** + ものだから | <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>な → <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>なものだから |
| Polite excuse | plain form + もので／ものですから | <ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れていない → <ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れていないもので |
| Casual contractions | もん／もんだから／もんで | <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れた → <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れたもんだから |

---

## ～もの - because · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the sentence-final `もの` is very often introduced by `だって` at the head of the reply. だって…もん is a set frame, and hearing `だって` should make you expect a `もん` at the other end.

---

## ～もの - because · Examples

## ～もの - because · Nuance & register

**Sentence-final `もん`.** This is spoken Japanese only. It never appears in an essay, a report or an email, and it cannot be aimed upward at a teacher, a client or a boss - even in ます-form, ❌<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しかったんですもん to a manager will land badly. What it does is *push back*. It is the reply of somebody who has been criticised, questioned or told they were wrong, and it insists that their reason was good enough. That defensive edge is why it so easily sounds petulant.

---

## ～もの - because · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register tendencies, and they are only tendencies: drawn out with a soft, coaxing delivery - ～だも～ん - it is associated with small children and with a cute, wheedling style that leans young and feminine. Said flatly and quickly it is simply blunt casual speech and is used by everyone; the clipped <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないもん of an irritated adult has nothing coaxing about it. What is constant is the defensiveness, not the gender.

---

## ～もの - because · Nuance & register (cont.)

**`ものだから` and `もので`.** These make excuses, and they do it by framing the reason as something that happened *to* the speaker. That is the whole point: the speaker is not owning the outcome, they are showing you the circumstance that overtook them. `ものだから` is the more emphatic and more spoken; `もので` and its fuller `ものですから` are softer and combine happily with keigo, which makes <code><ruby>不慣<rt>ふな</rt></ruby>れなもので</code> and <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>いでおりましたもので</code> stock apologies in service and office Japanese.

---

## ～もの - because · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two cautions. Used too often, they mark you as somebody who always has an excuse - Japanese speakers notice this exactly as English speakers notice a run of "it's just that…". And the excuse works only when the situation really was outside your control; <ruby>面倒<rt>めんどう</rt></ruby>だったものだから ("because I couldn't be bothered") is grammatical and makes things worse.

The casual `もんだから` / `もんで` are extremely common in ordinary speech and should be recognised on sight, but `もんで` in particular is quite blunt and is better kept for people you are relaxed with.

---

## ～もの - because · sentence-final ～もん vs ～から

> **Rule of thumb** - `から` states a reason. `もん` *defends* one. If nothing is being challenged, `もん` has nothing to do.

「なんで<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>したの?」「<ruby>苦<rt>にが</rt></ruby>い**もん**。」<br>*"Why did you leave it?" "Because it's bitter."*

<ruby>苦<rt>にが</rt></ruby>い**から**、<ruby>砂糖<rt>さとう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れて<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでいます。<br>*It's bitter, so I drink it with sugar.*

The first is an answer given under mild attack; the speaker is justifying themselves, and a small note of "leave me alone about it" comes with the `もん`. The second is a flat piece of information and could not take `もん` at all - there is nobody to argue with. The same test explains why `もん` almost always ends the utterance: a defence is the last word, not the first half of a sentence.

---

## ～もの - because · ～ものだから・～もので vs ～ので

> **Rule of thumb** - `ので` gives a reason. `ものだから` gives an excuse, and adds "…so it wasn't really my doing".

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まった**ので**、<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby><ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>らせます。<br>*The trains have stopped, so we'll push the meeting back half an hour.*

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まってしまった**ものですから**、<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いませんでした。<br>*The trains went and stopped, I'm afraid, so I didn't make the meeting.*

`ので` is compatible with a decision, an instruction or a plan; `ものだから` is not. What follows an excuse clause is almost always a **past-tense account of what went wrong**, often with `てしまった` reinforcing it. If your second clause is a request or a proposal, you need `ので`.

---

## ～もの - because · sentence-final ～もの vs ～ものだ

> **Rule of thumb** - same three syllables, unrelated meanings. Reason `もの` follows a plain clause and ends the utterance. `ものだ` is a predicate in its own right, and states a general truth, a piece of advice, or a fond memory.

<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>はよくこの<ruby>川<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>いだ**ものだ**。<br>*We used to swim in this river all the time.*

<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>はこの<ruby>川<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>げた**もん**。<br>*We *could* swim in this river back then, you know.*

---

## ～もの - because · sentence-final ～もの vs ～ものだ (cont.)

The first is reminiscence, and it is at home in writing. The second is an argument - somebody has just said the river was always filthy. Position tells them apart in practice: `ものだ`／`ものです` carries a copula and can be followed by more sentence; reason `もの` is bare, sits at the very end, and is nearly always `もん`.

---

# ～ばかりに

**Meaning** · just because ～; simply on account of ～ - one small thing went wrong, and the price was out of all proportion

`ばかりに` is regret with a grammar attached. It names a cause that looks trivial or avoidable in hindsight, then reports a consequence far larger than the cause deserved. The English that fits it is not "because" but "**just** because" or "if only I hadn't", and the speaker is normally the one paying.

---

| Attaches to | Form | Example |

| Verb (negative) | ない-form + ばかりに | <ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめなかった → <ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめなかったばかりに |
| Verb (non-past) | plain form + ばかりに | <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない → <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないばかりに |
| い-adjective | plain form + ばかりに | <ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>が<ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>が<ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>いばかりに |
| な-adjective | ～**な** + ばかりに | <ruby>正直<rt>しょうじき</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>正直<rt>しょうじき</rt></ruby>なばかりに |
| Noun | Noun + **である** + ばかりに | <ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby>であるばかりに |

---

## ～ばかりに · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the clause after `ばかりに` has to be a **bad** outcome, and one that has already happened. `てしまった`, `ことになった` and plain negatives cluster there for that reason. A good result, or a plan for the future, cannot follow it.

The `～たいばかりに` row is a separate sense worth learning on its own: there the cause is the *wanting*, and what follows is usually something excessive or unwise that the speaker did in pursuit of it.

<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>たなかった**ばかりに**、ずぶぬれになった。<br>*Just because I didn't take an umbrella, I got soaked through.*

<ruby>一言<rt>ひとこと</rt></ruby><ruby>嘘<rt>うそ</rt></ruby>をついた**ばかりに**、<ruby>信用<rt>しんよう</rt></ruby>をなくしてしまった。<br>*One lie, and I lost their trust for good.*

<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>選<rt>えら</rt></ruby>んだ**ばかりに**、<ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>修理<rt>しゅうり</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>すことになった。<br>*Just because I went for the cheaper one, I've had to send it in for repairs again and again.*

<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>が<ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>い**ばかりに**、<ruby>断<rt>ことわ</rt></ruby>れずに<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>けてしまった。<br>*Simply because I'm a soft touch, I couldn't say no and ended up taking on the lot.*

---

## ～ばかりに · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>保証人<rt>ほしょうにん</rt></ruby>になった**ばかりに**、<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きな<ruby>借金<rt>しゃっきん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>背負<rt>せお</rt></ruby>うことになった。<br>*He stood as guarantor, and for that alone he was saddled with an enormous debt.*

<ruby>一位<rt>いちい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>りたい**ばかりに**、<ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>壊<rt>こわ</rt></ruby>すまで<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>り<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けてしまった。<br>*Out of sheer determination to come first, she ran herself into the ground.*

## ～ばかりに · Examples (cont.)

---

## ～ばかりに · Nuance & register

`ばかりに` is a shade more literary than the rest of this family. It is at home in narrative - a novel, a personal essay, the part of a conversation where somebody is telling a story with feeling - and it sounds heavy in a passing remark. If a friend asks why you are wet, you say <ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby><ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れたから; you would only reach for `ばかりに` if you were recounting the disaster afterwards.

---

## ～ばかりに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The emotion is **regret**, not blame. That is what separates it from `せいで`. `せいで` points outward and can be aimed at anyone; `ばかりに` looks back at a hinge point and wishes it had gone the other way, which is why the cause is so often something the speaker themselves did, was, or failed to do. When it *is* aimed at someone else, it still keeps the "if only" shape: <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>一言<rt>ひとこと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>真<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>けたばかりに - the speaker is still the one who acted.

Two smaller points. The cause is presented as **small** - a single word, one decision, one missing precaution - and the disproportion is the point of the sentence; a cause that is already enormous does not need `ばかりに`. And because `ばかりに` looks back on a settled outcome, it does not combine with plans, suggestions or anything in the future.

---

## ～ばかりに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Bare `ため`, `せいで` and `から` all remain the ordinary choices for everyday causes; `ばかりに` is what you use when the sentence is really about the regret.

---

## ～ばかりに · ～ばかりに vs ～せいで

> **Rule of thumb** - both give a bad outcome. `せいで` says *whose fault* it was; `ばかりに` says *how small* the cause was and how much it cost.

<ruby>係<rt>かかり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりにくかった**せいで**、<ruby>列<rt>れつ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>並<rt>なら</rt></ruby>び<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>すはめになった。<br>*Because the attendant's explanation was so unclear, I had to go and queue all over again.*

<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>最後<rt>さいご</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>かなかった**ばかりに**、<ruby>列<rt>れつ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>並<rt>なら</rt></ruby>び<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>すはめになった。<br>*Just because I didn't listen to the end of the explanation, I had to go and queue all over again.*

---

## ～ばかりに · ～ばかりに vs ～せいで (cont.)

Same misfortune, different posture. The first sentence blames the staff; the second is the speaker kicking themselves. Note also that `せいで` attaches to a noun with `の` - <code><ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>のせいで</code> - while `ばかりに` needs a clause, or `である` if you insist on a noun.

---

## ～ばかりに · ～ばかりに vs ～だけに

> **Rule of thumb** - `だけ` and `ばかり` may both mean "only", but these two compounds do not overlap at all. `ばかりに` = "just because, unfortunately". `だけに` = "precisely because, as you would expect".

<ruby>期待<rt>きたい</rt></ruby>していた**だけに**、<ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けたときのショックが<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きかった。<br>*Precisely because I'd had high hopes, the defeat hit me hard.*

<ruby>期待<rt>きたい</rt></ruby>しすぎた**ばかりに**、<ruby>相手<rt>あいて</rt></ruby>にきつい<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>い<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>をしてしまった。<br>*Just because I expected too much, I ended up speaking harshly to her.*

---

## ～ばかりに · ～ばかりに vs ～だけに (cont.)

`だけに` says the result follows *naturally and fittingly* from the cause, and it works with good outcomes as happily as bad ones: <ruby>経験<rt>けいけん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>いだけに、<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりやすい。 `ばかりに` can never do that. If "no wonder" fits the English, you want `だけに`.

---

## ～ばかりに · ～ばかりに vs ～たばかり and ～ばかり

> **Rule of thumb** - one `に` changes everything. `～たばかり` = "only just did it"; `～ばかり` = "nothing but"; `～たばかりに` = "just because I did it, and I regret it".

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>してきた**ばかり**で、この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>のことはまだよく<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>りません。<br>*I've only just moved here, so I don't know the area yet.*

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>してきた**ばかりに**、<ruby>町内会<rt>ちょうないかい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>任<rt>まか</rt></ruby>された。<br>*Just because I was the new arrival, I got landed with every job in the neighbourhood association.*

Two sentences identical but for one syllable. The first is about **time** - the move was recent. The second is about **cause and regret** - being the newcomer is why the work landed on him. When you meet `ばかり` after a た-form, check for the `に` before you decide which one you are reading.

## ～ばかりに · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>宝<rt>たから</rt></ruby>くじを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った**ばかりに**、<ruby>百万円<rt>ひゃくまんえん</rt></ruby><ruby>当<rt>あ</rt></ruby>たりました。 → ✅ <ruby>宝<rt>たから</rt></ruby>くじを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った**おかげで**、<ruby>百万円<rt>ひゃくまんえん</rt></ruby><ruby>当<rt>あ</rt></ruby>たりました。<br>*Winning is a good outcome, and `ばかりに` has regret built into it. It cannot be used for luck that went your way.*

- ❌ <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>のばかりに、<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れました。 → ✅ <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>にあった**ばかりに**… ／ <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>の**せいで**<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れました。<br>*`ばかりに` does not take a bare noun with `の`. Give it a clause, or use `せいで`.*

- ❌ <ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた**ばかりに**、お<ruby>腹<rt>なか</rt></ruby>がいっぱいです。 → ✅ <ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた**ばかりで**、お<ruby>腹<rt>なか</rt></ruby>がいっぱいです。<br>*This is the "only just did it" `ばかり`. Adding `に` turns lunch into a regrettable mistake.*

---

## ～ばかりに · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いでいる**ばかりに**、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ます。 → ✅ <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いでいる**ので**、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*`ばかりに` reports a consequence that has already landed. A plan for tomorrow cannot follow it.*

- ❌ <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た**ばかりに**、<ruby>飛行機<rt>ひこうき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>ばなかった。 → ✅ <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>の**ため**、<ruby>飛行機<rt>ひこうき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>ばなかった。<br>*A typhoon is not a small avoidable slip that anybody could have chosen differently, so there is nothing for the "if only" to attach to. Use the neutral `ため`, or `せいで` if you want to grumble.*

# ～ことから

**Meaning** · from the fact that ～ - the evidence you reasoned from, not the cause of what happened

`ことから` introduces **grounds**, not a cause. What comes before it is something observed or established; what comes after is the conclusion somebody drew from it. The two clauses are linked by an act of reasoning, which is why the second half so often ends in <ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えられる, <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かる, <ruby>判断<rt>はんだん</rt></ruby>した or <ruby>呼<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ばれている rather than in a plain event.

---

## ～ことから · Formation

`こと` nominalises the clause in front of it, and `から` here is the `から` of source - "out of", "starting from".

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain) | plain form + ことから | <ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ている → <ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ていることから |
| Verb (past) | た-form + ことから | <ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>いた → <ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>いたことから |
| い-adjective | plain form + ことから | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いことから |
| な-adjective | ～**な**／**である** + ことから | <ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby>なことから |
| Noun | Noun + **である** + ことから | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>であることから |
| Referring back | このことから、～ | このことから、～と<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>える |

---

## ～ことから · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - what follows `ことから` must be a **conclusion**: an inference, a judgement, a name, or a statement of what is generally said. It cannot be a decision, a request, an invitation or a plain report of an event.

---

## ～ことから · Examples

<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>いていた**ことから**、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ったと<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かった。<br>*The tidy desk told me he had already gone home.*

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いていた**ことから**、<ruby>犯人<rt>はんにん</rt></ruby>はここから<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ったと<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えられる。<br>*From the fact that the window was open, it is thought the intruder came in this way.*

<ruby>葉<rt>は</rt></ruby>の<ruby>形<rt>かたち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>のひらに<ruby>似<rt>に</rt></ruby>ている**ことから**、この<ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>はそう<ruby>名付<rt>なづ</rt></ruby>けられた。<br>*The tree got its name from the fact that its leaves look like the palm of a hand.*

<ruby>気温<rt>きおん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>例年<rt>れいねん</rt></ruby>より<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い**ことから**、<ruby>開花<rt>かいか</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>まる<ruby>見込<rt>みこ</rt></ruby>みです。<br>*Given that temperatures are above the seasonal average, the blossom is expected early.*

---

## ～ことから · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby>である**ことから**、この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>初心者<rt>しょしんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>いていると<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>える。<br>*Its careful explanations make this a book one can call well suited to beginners.*

<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>いが<ruby>三度<rt>さんど</rt></ruby><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>いた**ことから**、<ruby>手順<rt>てじゅん</rt></ruby>そのものに<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>があると<ruby>判断<rt>はんだん</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*Since the same error occurred three times running, we concluded that the procedure itself was at fault.*

**このことから**、<ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>原因<rt>げんいん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>整備<rt>せいび</rt></ruby><ruby>不足<rt>ぶそく</rt></ruby>であったと<ruby>結論<rt>けつろん</rt></ruby>づけられた。<br>*From this it was concluded that the accident had been caused by inadequate maintenance.*

---

## ～ことから · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>回答<rt>かいとう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>くが<ruby>三十代<rt>さんじゅうだい</rt></ruby><ruby>以下<rt>いか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>集中<rt>しゅうちゅう</rt></ruby>している**ことから**、この<ruby>調査<rt>ちょうさ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>をそのまま<ruby>全体<rt>ぜんたい</rt></ruby>にあてはめることはできないと<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えられます。<br>*Because the responses are concentrated among people in their thirties and below, the findings cannot simply be generalised to the population as a whole.*

---

## ～ことから · Nuance & register

`ことから` is written Japanese. It belongs to reports, analyses, news copy, museum captions, academic prose and the more careful kind of explanation - the registers in which somebody shows their working. In casual speech it sounds like a person reading aloud from a document; the spoken equivalent is simply `から` plus a verb of thinking: <ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>いてたから、もう<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ったんだと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>った。

Its second home is **naming**. A very large number of Japanese explanations of how something got its name are built on this pattern - the shape of a mountain, the colour of a fish, a line in an old poem - and they nearly always close with <ruby>呼<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ばれるようになった or <ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>がついた. If you read guidebooks or <ruby>由来<rt>ゆらい</rt></ruby> sections, you will meet it constantly.

---

## ～ことから · Nuance & register (cont.)

The thing to keep straight is that `ことから` **does not claim causation**. In <ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>がついていることから、<ruby>在宅<rt>ざいたく</rt></ruby>だと<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かる, the light did not cause anybody to be at home; it is merely how the speaker knows. Feeding it a cause-and-effect pair produces a sentence that sounds oddly indirect - as though you had inferred your own headache from evidence.

There is no politeness dimension to worry about: the pattern itself does not change shape, and the register lives in the vocabulary and the sentence ending around it.

---

## ～ことから · ～ことから vs ～から・～ので

> **Rule of thumb** - `から` and `ので` join a cause to its result. `ことから` joins evidence to a conclusion. Ask whether the first clause *made* the second happen, or merely *revealed* it.

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>の<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>がついていた**ので**、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>から<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>をかけた。<br>*The light was on in the room, so I called in at the window.*

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>の<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>がついている**ことから**、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>戻<rt>もど</rt></ruby>っていると<ruby>判断<rt>はんだん</rt></ruby>した。<br>*From the fact that the light was on, I judged that he was already back.*

In the first, the light is why the speaker acted. In the second, the light is the speaker's evidence for a belief - and the belief was true before anybody looked. The `ので` version can be followed by anything at all; the `ことから` version can only be followed by an inference.

---

## ～ことから · ～ことから vs ～ことだから

> **Rule of thumb** - one syllable apart, and neither is a variant of the other. `ことから` = "from the fact that…". `Noun + のことだから` = "knowing what X is like, naturally…".

<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>いてある**ことから**、<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いていると<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かる。<br>*The bag on the desk tells me she has already arrived.*

<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>の**ことだから**、<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>には<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いているだろう。<br>*Knowing her, she'll have got there ten minutes early.*

`ことだから` takes a **person** (or occasionally an organisation) with `の` in front of it, and its conclusion rests on that person's known character rather than on anything observed today. `ことから` takes a whole clause and rests on the observation itself. If you can insert "typical of them" into the English, it is `ことだから`.

---

## ～ことから · ～ことから vs ～ものだから

> **Rule of thumb** - same length, opposite worlds. `ことから` is written reasoning from evidence; `ものだから` is spoken excuse-making. Neither can be swapped for the other in any sentence.

<ruby>地面<rt>じめん</rt></ruby>がぬれている**ことから**、<ruby>夜中<rt>よなか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ったことが<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かる。<br>*The wet ground shows that it rained during the night.*

<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ってきた**ものだから**、<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>返<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>してきました。<br>*It suddenly started raining, you see, so I turned back halfway.*

The first sentence is cool and impersonal, and its conclusion is a fact about the world. The second is warm and defensive, and its conclusion is about what the speaker did and would rather not be criticised for. Learners who have met both patterns in the same week tend to blur them; the register test settles it instantly.

---

## ～ことから · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>頭<rt>あたま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>い**ことから**、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みます。 → ✅ <ruby>頭<rt>あたま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>い**ので**、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*A personal reason followed by a decision. `ことから` needs observed evidence and an inference, not a headache and a plan.*

- ❌ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っている**ことから**、<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>って<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってください。 → ✅ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っている**ので**、<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>って<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*Requests cannot follow `ことから` at all.*

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>のことから、<ruby>割引<rt>わりびき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>けられる。 → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**である**ことから、<ruby>割引<rt>わりびき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>けられる。<br>*A noun links with `である`, not `の`. (With `の` you have accidentally written the opening of `のことだから`.)*

---

## ～ことから · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだことから → ✅ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**な**ことから ／ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**である**ことから<br>*Plain `だ` cannot stand in front of `こと`; use the attributive `な`, or `である` in formal writing.*

- ❌ <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんの**ことから**、きっと<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>どおりに<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ますよ。 → ✅ <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんの**ことだから**、きっと<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>どおりに<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ますよ。<br>*Predicting from somebody's character is the `ことだから` pattern. `ことから` would need an observation, not a person.*

{reveal}

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>割<rt>わ</rt></ruby>れていた**ので**、<ruby>警察<rt>けいさつ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>しました。 (b) <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>割<rt>わ</rt></ruby>れていた**ことから**、<ruby>泥棒<rt>どろぼう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ったと<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えられます。

# その結果

**Meaning** · as a result - what came out of the process just described

<code>その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> is a connector between sentences. The sentence before it describes something that ran its course - an effort, an investigation, a negotiation, a habit kept up for years - and <code>その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> announces what that produced. It is neutral about whether the outcome was welcome; it reports outcomes, it does not evaluate them.

---

## その結果 · Formation

<code>その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> is a fixed phrase, not a suffix, so nothing conjugates in front of it. What the table below lists is the **position** it can occupy.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|

| A clause, mid-sentence | ～し／～て、その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>、～ | <ruby>値上<rt>ねあ</rt></ruby>げをして、その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>、<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>減<rt>へ</rt></ruby>った |

---

## その結果 · Nuance & register

This is a **written and formal-spoken** connector: reports, minutes, news, a presentation, the explanatory half of an essay. In relaxed conversation it sounds like somebody quoting a document, and the natural spoken equivalents are `それで` and `で`. That said, it is not stiff in the way keigo is stiff - a colleague summarising what a meeting decided will use it happily.

Two things govern whether it fits. First, the sentence in front has to describe a **process**, not a single flat fact. Years of effort, repeated attempts, negotiations, a policy left running: something with duration or intention behind it. <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>った。その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>、… is odd for exactly this reason - rain is an event, not a process anybody conducted.

---

## その結果 · Nuance & register (cont.)

Second, it is **evaluatively neutral**. Unlike `おかげで` and `せいで`, it takes no view: the outcome that follows can be a triumph, a disaster or a set of numbers. That neutrality is what makes it the natural connector for a report on something that went badly, where naming a culprit would be out of place.

One habit worth copying from native writing: <code>その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> very often introduces a clause ending in `～ことになった`, `～ようになった` or <code>～という<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>になった</code>. The pattern likes outcomes stated as settled states of affairs rather than as momentary actions.

---

## その結果 · その結果 vs そのため

> **Rule of thumb** - `そのため` treats the previous sentence as a **cause** and can be followed by anything, including a plan or an instruction. <code>その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> treats it as a **process** and can only be followed by its outcome.

<ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>づいています。**そのため**、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>便<rt>びん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>欠航<rt>けっこう</rt></ruby>となります。<br>*A typhoon is approaching. Accordingly, tomorrow's flights are cancelled.*

<ruby>欠航<rt>けっこう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>きました。**その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>空港<rt>くうこう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>一万人<rt>いちまんにん</rt></ruby><ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>足止<rt>あしど</rt></ruby>めされました。<br>*Flights were cancelled for three days, and as a result nearly ten thousand passengers were stranded at the airport.*

---

## その結果 · その結果 vs そのため (cont.)

Swap them and you feel the difference immediately. ❌An approaching typhoon is not a process whose *result* is a cancellation - the cancellation is a decision taken because of it. Three days of cancellations, on the other hand, is a situation that ran on and produced something.

`そのため` is also the one you can put in front of a measure to be taken: そのため、<ruby>窓口<rt>まどぐち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>やすことにいたしました。

---

## その結果 · その結果 vs それで・だから

> **Rule of thumb** - `それで` and `だから` are conversation; <code>その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> is report. `だから` also carries the speaker's own reasoning in a way the other two do not.

<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れちゃって。**それで**、<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>戻<rt>もど</rt></ruby>ったんだ。<br>*I forgot my wallet - so I went back home for it.*

<ruby>調査<rt>ちょうさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けた。**その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>傾向<rt>けいこう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>各地<rt>かくち</rt></ruby>で<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>された。<br>*The survey ran for three years, and as a result the same trend was confirmed across the country.*

---

## その結果 · その結果 vs それで・だから (cont.)

Reading the first sentence with <code>その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> in it is comic: it promotes forgetting your wallet to the status of a research programme. Conversely, `だから` in a report reads as though the writer were arguing with the reader rather than presenting findings.

---

## その結果 · その結果 vs 結局

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> says "and this is what it produced". <code><ruby>結局<rt>けっきょく</rt></ruby></code> says "and in the end, after all that". The second carries a shrug: the effort was long, and often it did not get you where you hoped.

いろいろ<ruby>試<rt>ため</rt></ruby>してみたが、**<ruby>結局<rt>けっきょく</rt></ruby>**<ruby>元<rt>もと</rt></ruby>のやり<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>に<ruby>戻<rt>もど</rt></ruby>した。<br>*We tried all sorts of things, but in the end we went back to the old way of doing it.*

<ruby>手順<rt>てじゅん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見直<rt>みなお</rt></ruby>した。**その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>作業<rt>さぎょう</rt></ruby><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>三割<rt>さんわり</rt></ruby><ruby>短<rt>みじか</rt></ruby>くなった。<br>*We reviewed the procedure, and as a result the job now takes thirty per cent less time.*

---

## その結果 · その結果 vs 結局 (cont.)

<code><ruby>結局<rt>けっきょく</rt></ruby></code> is also an adverb rather than a connector, so it sits inside its clause and needs no comma after it, and it is perfectly at home in casual speech. If your English is "in the end" or "it turned out that after all", it is <code><ruby>結局<rt>けっきょく</rt></ruby></code>; if it is "as a result", it is <code>その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code>.

- ❌ その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まってください。 → ✅ **そのため**、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まってください。<br>*An instruction is not an outcome. `そのため` accepts one; this phrase does not.*

# ～た結果・～の結果

**Meaning** · as a result of ～ - the outcome of something that was deliberately worked through

<code>～た<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> packs a process and its outcome into a single sentence. The clause in front names something that was carried out - a discussion, an investigation, repeated attempts - and what follows is what came of it. With a noun, <code>～の<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> does the same job in fewer syllables. Both are the language of reports and decisions.

---

## ～た結果・～の結果 · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (past) | た-form + <ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby> | <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った → <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby> |
| Noun of activity | Noun + の + <ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby> | <ruby>検査<rt>けんさ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>検査<rt>けんさ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby> |
| Very formal writing | ます-form (past) + <ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby> | <ruby>検討<rt>けんとう</rt></ruby>いたしました<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby> |
| Sentence-final | ～のは～した<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>だ | <ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>ったのは<ruby>努力<rt>どりょく</rt></ruby>した<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>です |
| Ordinary noun (not this pattern) | <ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby> + を／が／は | <ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>るまで<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ |

---

## ～た結果・～の結果 · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the verb must be **past tense** and must name something somebody *did*: <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べる, <ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>する, <ruby>議論<rt>ぎろん</rt></ruby>する, <ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける, <ruby>繰<rt>く</rt></ruby>り<ruby>返<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>す. A momentary accident that simply happened to you does not take this pattern.

The nouns that work with <code>の<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> are the matching ones - <ruby>調査<rt>ちょうさ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>検査<rt>けんさ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>い, <ruby>投票<rt>とうひょう</rt></ruby>, <ruby>努力<rt>どりょく</rt></ruby>, <ruby>選考<rt>せんこう</rt></ruby> - all of them names of activities rather than of things.

---

## ～た結果・～の結果 · Nuance & register

This belongs to written and formal-spoken Japanese: minutes, reports, medical results, business email, the news. It is not stiff enough to be comic in conversation - a colleague may well say <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>こうなりました - but among friends the ordinary choice is `から`, `ので` or simply `て`.

Its most conspicuous habitat is the **polite refusal**. The frame <ruby>検討<rt>けんとう</rt></ruby>いたしました<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>、～<ruby>見送<rt>みおく</rt></ruby>らせていただきます is close to a fixed formula in business Japanese, and the `結果` is doing real work in it: by presenting the answer as the outcome of a process, the writer makes the refusal sound considered rather than personal. Note the shape there - a ます-form in front of `結果`, which is otherwise unusual and belongs to this very formal register only.

---

## ～た結果・～の結果 · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two things to keep in view. `結果` is evaluatively neutral, so what follows can be good, bad or merely numerical; that is why it is safe in writing where blame would be awkward. And the pattern insists on a **process**: <ruby>転<rt>ころ</rt></ruby>んだ<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>、けがをした is odd, because falling over is not something you conduct. Where the cause is an accident or a state of affairs, the neutral `ため` or the blaming `せいで` fit better.

---

## ～た結果・～の結果 · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, <code><ruby>検査<rt>けんさ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>、～</code> can be read two ways: as this connective pattern, or as the plain noun phrase "the results of the test" used adverbially. Nothing turns on the distinction - both readings deliver the same meaning - but it explains why `結果` also turns up with `を`, `が` and `は` in sentences that have nothing to do with this pattern.

---

## ～た結果・～の結果 · ～た結果 vs その結果

> **Rule of thumb** - same idea, different architecture. <code>～た<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> tucks the process into a subordinate clause; <code>その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> gives it a sentence of its own and then comments on it.

<ruby>条件<rt>じょうけん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見直<rt>みなお</rt></ruby>した**<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>契約<rt>けいやく</rt></ruby>がまとまりました。<br>*Revising the terms brought the contract together.*

<ruby>条件<rt>じょうけん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>見直<rt>みなお</rt></ruby>しました。**その<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>契約<rt>けいやく</rt></ruby>がまとまりました。<br>*We revised the terms several times over. As a result, the contract came together.*

Use the subordinate version when the process is background and the outcome is the news. Use the two-sentence version when the process deserves description in its own right - which is why it is the one that appears after a long account of what was tried.

---

## ～た結果・～の結果 · ～た結果 vs ～たあとで

> **Rule of thumb** - `たあとで` is pure sequence: this happened, then that. <code>た<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> claims the second thing **came out of** the first.

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わった**あとで**、<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>配<rt>くば</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*I handed out the documents after the meeting finished.*

<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>く<ruby>議論<rt>ぎろん</rt></ruby>した**<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>り<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>すことになりました。<br>*A long discussion ended with our having to redo the documents.*

Handing out papers is not produced by the meeting ending; it merely follows it. If you can insert "and then" and lose nothing, you want `あとで`. If the second clause answers "what came of it?", you want `結果`.

---

## ～た結果・～の結果 · ～た結果 vs ～たら

> **Rule of thumb** - both can report what you found out by doing something. `たら` is the spoken discovery - "I did X, and lo". <code>た<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby></code> is its written counterpart and frames the finding as the product of a procedure.

<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べて**みたら**、<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じことが<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きていた。<br>*I looked into it, and it turns out the same thing happened last year too.*

<ruby>過去<rt>かこ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>記録<rt>きろく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べた**<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>事例<rt>じれい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>された。<br>*An examination of past records confirmed a comparable case last year as well.*

---

## ～た結果・～の結果 · ～た結果 vs ～たら (cont.)

`たら` carries surprise and belongs to speech; `結果` carries none and belongs to the report that gets written afterwards. Notice how the whole sentence changes register with it - <ruby>事例<rt>じれい</rt></ruby> and <ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>された arrive in its company.

---

## ～た結果・～の結果 · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>転<rt>ころ</rt></ruby>んだ**<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>をけがしました。 → ✅ <ruby>転<rt>ころ</rt></ruby>んで<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>をけがしました。<br>*Falling over is an accident, not a procedure. `結果` needs something that was carried out on purpose or sustained over time.*

- ❌ <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う**<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めましょう。 → ✅ <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った**<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>**、こうなりました。<br>*The verb has to be in the past. An outcome cannot precede the process that produced it.*

- ❌ <ruby>検討<rt>けんとう</rt></ruby>した<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>と<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>まりました。 → ✅ <ruby>検討<rt>けんとう</rt></ruby>した<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>、<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>と<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>まりました。<br>*No particle after `結果` in this pattern - a comma does the joining.*

## ～た結果・～の結果 · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>努力<rt>どりょく</rt></ruby>した<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>の**おかげで**<ruby>合格<rt>ごうかく</rt></ruby>しました。 → ✅ <ruby>努力<rt>どりょく</rt></ruby>した**<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>**<ruby>合格<rt>ごうかく</rt></ruby>しました。 ／ ✅ <ruby>努力<rt>どりょく</rt></ruby>の**おかげで**<ruby>合格<rt>ごうかく</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*Stacking the two is redundant, and it mixes a neutral report with a word of thanks. # なぜなら・なぜかというと

**Meaning** · because; the reason is - you make a claim, then turn round and justify it

`から` and `ので` bury the reason inside the sentence, ahead of the point. `なぜなら` and `なぜかというと` do the opposite: the claim goes first, in a sentence of its own, and the reason follows in a second sentence flagged as a reason. That order is the whole value of the pattern - it is how you argue rather than merely explain.

---

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · Formation

These are sentence-opening phrases, not suffixes, so nothing conjugates in front of them. What matters is what comes **after**: the explanation is normally closed off with からだ／からです, and leaving that off makes the sentence sound truncated.

---

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A stated claim (written) | ～。なぜなら、～からだ／からです。 | <ruby>反対<rt>はんたい</rt></ruby>です。なぜなら、<ruby>危<rt>あぶ</rt></ruby>ないからです。 |
| Emphatic written variant | ～。なぜならば、～からである。 | なぜならば、<ruby>記録<rt>きろく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>らないからである |
| Spoken and explanatory | ～。なぜかというと、～からです。 | なぜかというと、<ruby>順番<rt>じゅんばん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>逆<rt>ぎゃく</rt></ruby>だからです |
| Same frame, other shapes | なぜかといえば／どうしてかというと | どうしてかというと、<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いからです |
| Closing with a noun-reason | なぜなら、～ためだ／～のだ | なぜなら、<ruby>人手<rt>ひとで</rt></ruby>が<ruby>足<rt>た</rt></ruby>りないためだ |
| Casual equivalent | だって、～から／～んだもん | だって、<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いんだもん |

---

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the claim comes **first**. `なぜなら` can never open the pair; if you find yourself writing it at the head of a paragraph with nothing before it, what you actually want is `から` or `ので`.

---

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · Examples

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ります。**なぜなら**、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>があるからです。<br>*I'm going home early today. The reason is that I have an exam tomorrow.*

この<ruby>方法<rt>ほうほう</rt></ruby>はおすすめしません。**なぜなら**、<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がかかりすぎるからです。<br>*I wouldn't recommend this method, because it takes far too long.*

<ruby>締<rt>し</rt></ruby>め<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>りは<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>めに<ruby>設定<rt>せってい</rt></ruby>しています。**なぜかというと**、<ruby>必<rt>かなら</rt></ruby>ず<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>かが<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きるからです。<br>*I set the deadlines early. The reason being, something always goes wrong.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>紙<rt>かみ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けている。**なぜなら**、<ruby>前後<rt>ぜんご</rt></ruby>の<ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>目<rt>め</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>るからだ。<br>*I still use a paper dictionary, because the words on either side catch my eye.*

---

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>記録<rt>きろく</rt></ruby>はすべて<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>しておくべきである。**なぜならば**、<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>から<ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめる<ruby>方法<rt>ほうほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>他<rt>ほか</rt></ruby>にないからである。<br>*All records should be kept, for the reason that there is no other way of checking them later.*

<ruby>値上<rt>ねあ</rt></ruby>げには<ruby>反対<rt>はんたい</rt></ruby>です。**なぜなら**、いちばん<ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>るのは<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>っている<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>たちだからです。<br>*I'm against the price rise, because the people it will hurt most are the ones who use it every day.*

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby>には<ruby>最初<rt>さいしょ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>一<rt>いっ</rt></ruby>か<ruby>月<rt>げつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>失敗<rt>しっぱい</rt></ruby>しても<ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>な<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>だけを<ruby>任<rt>まか</rt></ruby>せています。**なぜかというと**、その<ruby>時期<rt>じき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>叱<rt>しか</rt></ruby>られた<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>ほど、あとで<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>をしなくなるからです。<br>*For their first month I give new staff only work where a mistake does no harm - because the ones who get told off in that first month are exactly the ones who stop asking questions later.*

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · Nuance & register

`なぜなら` is written Japanese, and specifically **argumentative** written Japanese: opinion columns, essays, a slide deck, the paragraph in a report where you defend a recommendation. It marks the sentence after it as *support for a claim*, which is why it sounds slightly grand in ordinary chat - dropping it into a conversation about lunch is a little like saying "for the following reason" out loud. `なぜならば` is the same word with an extra formal weight, at home with `である` endings.

`なぜかというと` is the spoken member of the family. Literally "if you ask why", it builds the imagined question into the phrase, and that makes it friendly and explanatory rather than argumentative: teachers, guides and anyone walking a listener through their thinking use it constantly. `どうしてかというと` is the same thing a step more casual. All of them still expect からです at the end.

---

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · Nuance & register (cont.)

Down at the casual end sits `だって`, which is a different animal - it is defensive rather than expository, it belongs at the head of a reply, and it pairs with the sentence-final `もん`. Between friends, <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない。だって<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>いんだもん is what actually gets said.

One usage note that catches learners out: when somebody has **asked** you どうして?, you do not answer with `なぜなら`. The question has already flagged that a reason is coming, so the signpost is redundant, and the natural answer is a bare <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まったからです。 `なぜなら` is for reasons **you** decide to supply, unprompted.

---

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, do not sprinkle it. An essay in which every paragraph turns on `なぜなら` reads as mechanical to a Japanese reader, exactly as a run of "because of this" does in English. Use it where the reason genuinely needs marking as a reason.

---

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · なぜなら vs ～から・～ので

> **Rule of thumb** - same content, opposite order. `から` and `ので` put the reason first and inside the sentence; `なぜなら` puts the claim first and gives the reason a sentence of its own.

<ruby>人手<rt>ひとで</rt></ruby>が<ruby>足<rt>た</rt></ruby>りない**ので**、<ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>の<ruby>営業<rt>えいぎょう</rt></ruby>をやめました。<br>*We stopped opening on Saturdays because we're short-staffed.*

<ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>の<ruby>営業<rt>えいぎょう</rt></ruby>をやめました。**なぜなら**、<ruby>人手<rt>ひとで</rt></ruby>が<ruby>足<rt>た</rt></ruby>りないからです。<br>*We've stopped opening on Saturdays. The reason is that we're short-staffed.*

---

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · なぜなら vs ～から・～ので (cont.)

Choose by asking what the sentence is *for*. If you are simply explaining a situation, the `ので` version is shorter and more natural. If you are defending a decision that the listener may not like, putting it first and then supporting it - claim, evidence - is what `なぜなら` was made for. Note that the reason clause still ends in からです even though `なぜなら` already means "because"; the two are not redundant in Japanese, they bracket the reason at both ends.

---

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · なぜなら vs なぜかというと

> **Rule of thumb** - identical function, different register. `なぜなら` argues on paper; `なぜかというと` explains out loud.

この<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>見直<rt>みなお</rt></ruby>すべきだ。**なぜなら**、<ruby>費用<rt>ひよう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>見積<rt>みつ</rt></ruby>もりが<ruby>甘<rt>あま</rt></ruby>すぎるからだ。<br>*This proposal needs revisiting, because the cost estimates are far too optimistic.*

ここだけ<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>しますね。**なぜかというと**、あとの<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby>ここにつながるからです。<br>*Let me explain this bit first - the reason being that everything after it hangs on it.*

The first is a written argument and could not be said aloud without sounding as though you were reading it. The second builds in the listener's imagined "why?" and is warm; in a written report it would look chatty. `どうしてかというと` shifts one notch further towards conversation again.

---

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · なぜなら vs というのは・だって

> **Rule of thumb** - `なぜなら` supports a claim. `というのは` fills in background the listener is missing. `だって` defends the speaker against criticism.

<ruby>今週<rt>こんしゅう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>せません。**というのは**、<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入院<rt>にゅういん</rt></ruby>してしまったんです。<br>*I can't come by this week - the thing is, my mother's been taken into hospital.*

「どうして<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わなかったの?」「**だって**、<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>させたくなかったんだもん。」<br>*"Why didn't you say something earlier?" "Because I didn't want to worry you."*

`というのは` is doing something closer to "the thing is" - it supplies circumstances rather than proving a point, and it does not require からです at the end. `だって` answers an accusation and belongs to casual speech; used to a superior it sounds like a child talking back. Between them and `なぜなら` you have most of the register range covered.

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>通勤<rt>つうきん</rt></ruby>することにしました。____、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>より<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>くことが<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かったからです。

{reveal}

{reveal}

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · Practice 2

**2.** Choose, in a conversation with a close friend: <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないよ。（なぜならば・だって）、<ruby>会費<rt>かいひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>すぎるんだもん。

{reveal}
<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないよ。**だって**、<ruby>会費<rt>かいひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>すぎるんだもん。

{reveal}
The plain endings and the sentence-final `もん` set the register, and `だって` is the member of this family that belongs there. `なぜならば` with `んだもん` after it is a collision of the most formal and the most casual options available.

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>って<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った<ruby>方<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>がいいですよ。なぜなら、<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>から<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>です。

{reveal}
The reason clause has to close with からだ／からです, and after a noun that means `だから`. Without it the second sentence reads as an unfinished thought - the listener is still waiting for the end of it.

## なぜなら・なぜかというと · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見送<rt>みおく</rt></ruby>ります。**なぜなら**、<ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>にお<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>がかかるからです。 (b) <ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見送<rt>みおく</rt></ruby>ります。**というのは**、<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>をすることになったんです。

{reveal}
(a) argues. The decision is stated, then defended with a reason marked as a reason, and the からです closes the argument off. It is what you would write in a message that expects to be questioned.

{reveal}
(b) confides. `というのは` supplies background the listener did not have - "the thing is, we're having work done on the house" - and it needs no からです. It is softer and more conversational, and it invites sympathy rather than agreement.
