# ～たとたん

**Meaning** · the instant X happened, Y happened - and Y was not expected

`たとたん` welds two past events together with no gap at all. What separates it from every other "as soon as" pattern is the second half: the thing that happens is a **surprise**, something the speaker noticed rather than arranged. If you planned it, `たとたん` is the wrong word.

---

## ～たとたん · Formation

`とたん` is really the noun <code><ruby>途端<rt>とたん</rt></ruby></code>, "the very edge of the moment", so it takes a plain **past** verb in front of it, exactly the way a noun takes a modifying clause.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (た-form) | た-form + とたん | <ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたとたん |
| Verb (た-form) | た-form + とたんに | <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ける → <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けたとたんに |
| As a bare noun | その + <ruby>途端<rt>とたん</rt></ruby> | その<ruby>途端<rt>とたん</rt></ruby>、<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>消<rt>き</rt></ruby>えた |
| ❌ Dictionary form | ❌<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>くとたん | ✅<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたとたん |
| ❌ Non-past main clause | ❌…とたん、<ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>く | ✅…とたん、<ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>いた |
| ❌ Request or invitation | ❌…とたん、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>してください | ✅…たら、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>してください |

---

## ～たとたん · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - three things must all be true: the verb in front is **た**, the main clause is **past**, and nobody **planned** the result. Break any one of them and you want a different pattern.

---

## ～たとたん · Examples

ベッドに<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っ**たとたん**、<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>ってしまった。<br>*The moment I got into bed, I was asleep.*

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け**たとたん**、<ruby>冷<rt>つめ</rt></ruby>たい<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってきた。<br>*The instant I opened the window, cold air poured in.*

<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がっ**たとたんに**、めまいがした。<br>*I stood up and the room span, just like that.*

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>**たとたん**、<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*The second he saw the teacher's face, my little brother burst into tears.*

---

## ～たとたん · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**たとたん**、<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*No sooner had the match finished than it started to rain.*

ドアを<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>め**たとたんに**、<ruby>鍵<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>いてきたことに<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>づいた。<br>*The instant the door shut behind me, I realised I'd left the key inside.*

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってき**たとたん**、<ruby>理由<rt>りゆう</rt></ruby>も<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わずに<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きな<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*The manager walked into the room and immediately started shouting, without saying why.*

<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby><ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いた<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>辞<rt>や</rt></ruby>め**たとたん**、<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになりすぎて、かえって<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>かなかった。<br>*The day I left the company I'd worked at for ten years, my life went so quiet it actually unsettled me.*

---

## ～たとたん · Nuance & register

`たとたん` is neutral in politeness - the pattern sits in the middle of the sentence, so the ending decides the register: <code><ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した</code> or <code><ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しました</code>, either is fine. It is slightly more common in narration and in written accounts of events than in moment-to-moment chat, because it is a storyteller's device: it sets up a punchline.

The `に` is optional and carries almost no meaning. `とたんに` is a touch more deliberate, a beat of extra weight before the surprise; `とたん` alone is quicker. Most speakers use both without thinking about it.

---

## ～たとたん · Nuance & register (cont.)

The word is usually written in kana as `とたん`, though <code><ruby>途端<rt>とたん</rt></ruby></code> in kanji turns up in novels and newspapers. As a standalone noun it survives in その<code><ruby>途端<rt>とたん</rt></ruby></code> - "at that very instant" - which is worth knowing because it lets you split the two events into separate sentences.

Three neighbours are worth naming, because JLPT questions live on the difference:

---

## ～たとたん · Nuance & register (cont.)

- <code>～<ruby>次第<rt>しだい</rt></ruby></code> points **forwards**, not back. It is formal, it takes the<br>*verb stem rather than た, and the second clause is the speaker's own planned action: <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わり<ruby>次第<rt>しだい</rt></ruby>、ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>します。 Everything `たとたん` forbids, <code><ruby>次第<rt>しだい</rt></ruby></code> requires.*
- <code>～や<ruby>否<rt>いな</rt></ruby>や</code> means the same as `たとたん` but is literary. You will meet<br>*it in print and essentially never in conversation.*
- <code>～かと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>うと</code> also carries surprise, but it specialises in things that<br>*flip: <ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>いていたかと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>うと、もう<ruby>笑<rt>わら</rt></ruby>っている。*

---

## ～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～たら

> **Rule of thumb** - `たら` lets you put a request, an instruction, an invitation or a plan in the main clause. `たとたん` allows none of those. If the second half is something you want to *happen*, it has to be `たら`.

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたら、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Call me when you get to the station.*

Swap in `とたん` and the sentence collapses, because you cannot ask somebody to be surprised on cue. With a past clause and an unplanned result, the same setup is fine:

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>い**たとたん**、<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>がないことに<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>づいた。<br>*The moment I reached the station, I realised my wallet was gone.*

---

## ～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～たら (cont.)

`たら` is also happy with a gap in time - <ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たら<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>きます covers years. `たとたん` covers a fraction of a second.

---

## ～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～てすぐ(に)

> **Rule of thumb** - `てすぐ` means "very soon after", and lets you do the second thing on purpose. `たとたん` means "in the same instant", and the second thing happens *to* you.

<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってすぐ、シャワーを<ruby>浴<rt>あ</rt></ruby>びた。<br>*I had a shower as soon as I got home.*

That is a decision, so `たとたん` would be odd. Compare:

<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>っ**たとたん**、<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>びついてきた。<br>*The second I got home, the cat launched itself at my legs.*

Nobody arranged that, which is exactly why `たとたん` fits. The same test explains why a diary entry uses `てすぐ` for a routine and `たとたん` for the day something went wrong.

---

## ～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～と

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` states a rule that holds every time. `たとたん` reports one particular moment that has already gone past.

このボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>すと、お<ruby>湯<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*Press this button and hot water comes out.*

That is how the machine works, always - `たとたん` cannot express it, because it is anchored to a single past event. But a one-off goes the other way:

ボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>し**たとたん**、<ruby>機械<rt>きかい</rt></ruby>から<ruby>変<rt>へん</rt></ruby>な<ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>がした。<br>*The instant I pressed the button, the machine made a strange noise.*

---

## ～たとたん · ～たとたん vs ～と (cont.)

`と` can also carry discovery in the past - ドアを<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けると、<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>もいなかった - and there it comes close to `たとたん`. The difference is speed and shock: `と` narrates smoothly, `たとたん` snaps.

---

## ～たとたん · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>るとたん、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>鳴<rt>な</rt></ruby>った。 → ✅ <ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>っ**たとたん**、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>鳴<rt>な</rt></ruby>った。<br>*The verb in front of `とたん` is always た-form, even when the sentence is about the past. Dictionary form is simply ungrammatical here.*

- ❌ ベルが<ruby>鳴<rt>な</rt></ruby>ったとたん、<ruby>教室<rt>きょうしつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ましょう。 → ✅ ベルが<ruby>鳴<rt>な</rt></ruby>ったら、<ruby>教室<rt>きょうしつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ましょう。<br>*An invitation is a plan, and `とたん` cannot host a plan. `たら` can.*

- ❌ <ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きたとたん<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む。 → ✅ <ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きたらすぐ<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む。<br>*A daily habit is neither a single moment nor a surprise. `とたん` reports one event, once.*

---

## ～たとたん · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たとたん、<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>すでしょう。 → ✅ <ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たとたん、<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*The main clause has to be past. There is no way to predict a `とたん`; by the time you use it, it has happened.*

---

## ～たとたん · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>っ____、ドアが<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>まった。

{reveal}
<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>っ**たとたん**、ドアが<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>まった。

{reveal}
The verb takes the た-form before `とたん`, and the main clause is past. The doors closing is outside the speaker's control, which is what licenses the pattern.

---

## ～たとたん · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>い（たとたん・たら）、<ruby>中身<rt>なかみ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>してください。

{reveal}
<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>い**たら**、<ruby>中身<rt>なかみ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>してください。

{reveal}
The main clause is a request, and `とたん` refuses requests, commands and invitations. Anything you are asking the listener to do belongs with `たら`.

---

## ～たとたん · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The moment I sat down on the bench, it started to rain."

{reveal}
ベンチに<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>っ**たとたん**、<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。

{reveal}
Both halves are past, the rain is unplanned, and the two events share a single instant - the ideal conditions for `たとたん`. Adding the optional `に` after `とたん` changes nothing.

---

## ～たとたん · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>になるとたん、<ruby>桜<rt>さくら</rt></ruby>が<ruby>咲<rt>さ</rt></ruby>きます。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>になると、<ruby>桜<rt>さくら</rt></ruby>が<ruby>咲<rt>さ</rt></ruby>きます。

{reveal}
Two problems at once: `とたん` needs a た-form, and more importantly this sentence states what happens every year. A general rule takes `と`, never `とたん`.

---

## ～たとたん · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>**たとたん**、みんなが<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた。 (b) <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>てから、みんなが<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた。

{reveal}
(a) puts the two events in the same instant and frames the outburst of talk as something the speaker witnessed and did not expect - the room had clearly been holding its tongue. There is an edge of "the second he was gone".

{reveal}
(b) simply orders them: first he left, then people talked. It is neutral about how long the gap was and implies nothing about surprise, so it works equally well in a dry report of a meeting.

# ～上で

**Meaning** · after doing X - and on the strength of what X told you - do Y

<code><ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で</code> (read うえで) is not a plain "after". It says the first action was done **in order to find something out**, and the second action is the decision you reached with that information in hand. It is a formal word: paperwork, meetings, notices.

---

## ～上で · Formation

The same word covers two jobs, and the verb form in front tells you which. **た-form means "after"**; **dictionary form means "in the course of"**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (た-form) - after | た-form + <ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で | <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べる → <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べた<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で |
| Action noun - after | Noun + の + <ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で | <ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で |
| Verb (dictionary) - in the course of | dictionary + <ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で | <ruby>暮<rt>く</rt></ruby>らす → <ruby>暮<rt>く</rt></ruby>らす<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で |
| Noun - in the course of | Noun + の + <ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で | <ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby> → <ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で |
| Before a noun | ～<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>での + Noun | <ruby>検討<rt>けんとう</rt></ruby>した<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>での<ruby>判断<rt>はんだん</rt></ruby> |
| Formal notice style | ～の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>、… | ご<ruby>記入<rt>きにゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>、お<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しください |
| ❌ て-form | ❌<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べて<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で | ✅<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べた<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で |

---

## ～上で · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - for the "after" sense, ask whether the first action **produces information** the second action needs. <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>める works because looking is what tells you how to decide. <ruby>歯<rt>は</rt></ruby>をみがいた<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>る does not, because brushing your teeth informs nothing.

---

## ～上で · Examples

よく<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え**た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**、お<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>します。<br>*I'll give you my answer once I've thought it through.*

<ruby>両親<rt>りょうしん</rt></ruby>と<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>し**た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めます。<br>*I'll decide after talking it over with my parents.*

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>**た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>りるかどうか<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めたいです。<br>*I'd like to see the flat before deciding whether to rent it.*

<ruby>外国<rt>がいこく</rt></ruby>で<ruby>暮<rt>く</rt></ruby>ら**す<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby><ruby>苦労<rt>くろう</rt></ruby>するのは<ruby>言葉<rt>ことば</rt></ruby>です。<br>*The hardest part of living abroad is the language.*

---

## ～上で · Examples (cont.)

この<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**る<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>体力<rt>たいりょく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>欠<rt>か</rt></ruby>かせません。<br>*Stamina is essential if you're going to keep doing this job.*

<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>をお<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みになっ**た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**、ご<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>をお<ruby>寄<rt>よ</rt></ruby>せください。<br>*Please read the materials and then send us your questions.*

<ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>費用<rt>ひよう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>正確<rt>せいかく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>計算<rt>けいさん</rt></ruby>し**た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**、あらためてお<ruby>見積<rt>みつ</rt></ruby>もりをお<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りいたします。<br>*Once we have costed the work precisely, we'll send you a revised quotation.*

<ruby>皆<rt>みな</rt></ruby>さんのご<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>をうかがっ**た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>最終<rt>さいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めたいと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。<br>*I'd like to hear everyone's views first, and then settle the final proposal at next week's meeting.*

---

## ～上で · Nuance & register

This is a **formal** pattern. It belongs to written Japanese, business speech and public notices, and it sounds stiff in a chat with a friend - where you would simply say <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てから<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>める</code>. Using it well is one of the fastest ways to make a work email sound like it was written by an adult.

In notices and forms the `で` is often dropped, leaving ～の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>、 with a comma: ご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>送信<rt>そうしん</rt></ruby>してください。 That clipped form is almost a fixed register marker; it appears on websites, application forms and station signs, and never in speech.

Two particles slot in usefully. <code><ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>では</code> narrows the frame to one domain - <code><ruby>法律<rt>ほうりつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>では<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>ありません</code>, "legally speaking there's no problem". <code><ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>でも</code> adds "for that purpose too".

---

## ～上で · Nuance & register (cont.)

> **Note** - do not confuse this with <code>～<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>に</code>, which is a completely different pattern meaning "and what's more": この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>においしい。 Same kanji, `に` instead of `で`, and it stacks reasons rather than ordering events. There is also <code>～<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>は</code>, "now that (you have done X)", which is a step above N3.

---

## ～上で · ～た上で vs ～てから

> **Rule of thumb** - `てから` only says *later*. <code><ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で</code> says *later, and because of what the first step revealed*. Every <code><ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で</code> sentence could be `てから`; the reverse is not true.

<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ってから、ケーキを<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>り<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた。<br>*I washed my hands and then started making the cake.*

Washing your hands tells you nothing, so <code><ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>った<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で</code> would be strange. Now a case where the first step really does feed the second:

アンケートの<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>を<ruby>分析<rt>ぶんせき</rt></ruby>し**た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>企画<rt>きかく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>てます。<br>*We'll draw up the new plan after analysing the survey results.*

---

## ～上で · ～た上で vs ～てから (cont.)

Register separates them too. `てから` is neutral and used at every level of politeness; <code><ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で</code> raises the whole sentence a notch.

---

## ～上で · ～た上で vs ～た後で

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で</code> measures **time**: X finished, then Y. <code><ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で</code> measures **dependence**: Y is shaped by X.

<ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*After the meal we went for a walk.*

Nothing about the meal determined the walk, so this is <code><ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で</code> and only <code><ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で</code>. Compare:

<ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>い**た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>手術<rt>しゅじゅつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>けるかどうか<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えます。<br>*I'll think about whether to have the operation after hearing what the doctor says.*

---

## ～上で · ～た上で vs ～た後で (cont.)

Here <code><ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で</code> would be grammatical but flat; it would lose the point, which is that the doctor's explanation is the basis of the decision. A second, harder difference: <code><ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で</code> accepts events nobody controls - <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>過<rt>す</rt></ruby>ぎた<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で - while <code><ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で</code> needs a **deliberate** first action, so ❌<ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>過<rt>す</rt></ruby>ぎた<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で is wrong.

---

## ～上で · ～る上で vs ～た上で

> **Rule of thumb** - dictionary form + <code><ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で</code> means "in doing X / when it comes to X" and points at a whole activity. た-form + <code><ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で</code> means "after X" and points at one completed step.

<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>を<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>す**る<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>はとても<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>です。<br>*When it comes to studying Japanese, knowing how to use a dictionary matters a great deal.*

<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>を<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>し**た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>するかどうか<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めます。<br>*I'll study some Japanese first, and then decide whether to go abroad.*

The first sentence never happened and never will "happen" - it describes the activity in general. The second describes a step you take and then finish. Getting the two mixed up is the single most common error with this pattern, and the fix is always the verb form.

---

## ～上で · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>歯<rt>は</rt></ruby>をみがいた<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ます。 → ✅ <ruby>歯<rt>は</rt></ruby>をみがいてから<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*A routine sequence with no decision in it takes `てから`. Brushing your teeth does not give you information to act on.*

- ❌ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>った<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になった。 → ✅ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ったので、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になった。<br>*The first clause must be something somebody chose to do. Weather is not a deliberate act, and here the relation is cause, not preparation.*

- ❌ <ruby>見積<rt>みつ</rt></ruby>もりを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>て<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>します。 → ✅ <ruby>見積<rt>みつ</rt></ruby>もりを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>します。<br>*<code><ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby></code> is a noun, so the clause in front of it ends in a plain form - た or dictionary - never て.*

---

## ～上で · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いた<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>知識<rt>ちしき</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>く<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>知識<rt>ちしき</rt></ruby><br>*"Knowledge you need in order to work at a company" is the *in the course of* sense, which takes the dictionary form.*

---

## ～上で · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>条件<rt>じょうけん</rt></ruby>をよく<ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめ____、<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでください。

{reveal}
<ruby>条件<rt>じょうけん</rt></ruby>をよく<ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめ**た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでください。

{reveal}
Checking the conditions is exactly the kind of deliberate, informing step this pattern wants, and the application is the decision that follows from it. Note the た-form: the checking has to be finished.

---

## ～上で · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>（する・した）<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>をつけることは<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>ですか。

{reveal}
<ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>**する**<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>をつけることは<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>ですか。

{reveal}
The question is about driving safely as an ongoing activity, not about something finished, so it needs the dictionary form and the "in the course of" reading.

---

## ～上で · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "We would like to make a decision after hearing the customer's opinion."

{reveal}
お<ruby>客様<rt>きゃくさま</rt></ruby>のご<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>をうかがっ**た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めたいと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
A business setting, a deliberate first step, and a decision that depends on it - all three signals point at <code><ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で</code> rather than a plain `てから`.

---

## ～上で · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きた<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まりました。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きたため、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まりました。

{reveal}
An earthquake is not an action anyone performed to gather information, and the trains stopping is a consequence rather than a considered decision. This sentence needs a cause marker such as `ため` or `ので`.

---

## ～上で · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>全員<rt>ぜんいん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>い**た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めます。 (b) <ruby>全員<rt>ぜんいん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてから<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めます。

{reveal}
(a) commits the speaker to letting those opinions shape the decision. It is the sentence a manager uses to promise that the consultation is real, and its formal tone suits a meeting or an email.

{reveal}
(b) only promises an order of events: opinions first, decision second. It is perfectly polite but leaves open the possibility that the decision was made already and the listening is a formality.

# ～て初めて

**Meaning** · not until X did Y become clear - it took X for the penny to drop

<code>て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて</code> is the grammar of hindsight. You did something, or something happened to you, and only then did you understand, notice or become able to do the thing in the second clause. The English that fits it best is almost always "only after…" or "it wasn't until…".

---

## ～て初めて · Formation

The pattern is simply the て-form followed by the adverb <code><ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて</code>. Word order is doing all the work here, so guard it: the て-form comes **first**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (て-form) | て-form + <ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて | <ruby>失<rt>うしな</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>失<rt>うしな</rt></ruby>って<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて |
| Verb + てみる | てみて + <ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて | <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>ってみて<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて |
| Noun (a new status) | Noun + になって + <ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて | <ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby> → <ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>になって<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて |
| Often rounded off with のだ | ～て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて…のだ | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んで<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めてわかったのだ |
| ❌ た-form | ❌<ruby>失<rt>うしな</rt></ruby>った<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて | ✅<ruby>失<rt>うしな</rt></ruby>って<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて |
| ❌ Adverb before the verb | ❌<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて<ruby>失<rt>うしな</rt></ruby>って | ✅<ruby>失<rt>うしな</rt></ruby>って<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて |

---

## ～て初めて · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the main clause has to be a **discovery**: わかる、<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>づく、 <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る、できるようになる、<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えてくる. If the second half is something you decided to do, you want `てから`, not <code>て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて</code>.

---

## ～て初めて · Examples

<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>ってみ**て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて**、この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>のよさが<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かった。<br>*It wasn't until I used it that I saw how good this dictionary is.*

<ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>になっ**て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて**、<ruby>健康<rt>けんこう</rt></ruby>のありがたさが<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かった。<br>*Only when I got ill did I appreciate being healthy.*

<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で<ruby>暮<rt>く</rt></ruby>らし**て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて**、<ruby>家事<rt>かじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>さに<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>づいた。<br>*It took living on my own for me to realise how much work housework is.*

<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>い**て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて**、この<ruby>機械<rt>きかい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>が<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりました。<br>*I only understood how to use this machine after hearing the explanation.*

---

## ～て初めて · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>失<rt>うしな</rt></ruby>っ**て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて**、その<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>さに<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>づくものだ。<br>*People never see the value of something until they've lost it.*

<ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>**て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて**、<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>がどれだけ<ruby>遠<rt>とお</rt></ruby>くまで<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いてきたのか<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>った。<br>*Not until I looked at the map did I realise how far I'd walked.*

<ruby>外国<rt>がいこく</rt></ruby>で<ruby>生活<rt>せいかつ</rt></ruby>してみ**て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて**、<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>のことを<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らなかったのだと<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>づかされた。<br>*Living abroad was what made me realise I knew nothing about my own country.*

<ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>になっ**て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて**、<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>がどうしてあんなに<ruby>厳<rt>きび</rt></ruby>しかったのか<ruby>理解<rt>りかい</rt></ruby>できるようになった。<br>*It was only once I became a parent that I could understand why my father was so strict.*

---

## ～て初めて · Nuance & register

The pattern is register-neutral in form - the ending decides politeness - but it is **tonally** marked. It draws a lesson, so it turns up in essays, in speeches, in the reflective part of a conversation, and in the kind of sentence that gets quoted. Using it for something trivial is faintly comic: <ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けて<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて、<ruby>牛乳<rt>ぎゅうにゅう</rt></ruby>がないことに<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>づいた is grammatical but sounds as though you are narrating your own life as a novel.

---

## ～て初めて · Nuance & register (cont.)

Three companions show up constantly. `てみて` in front, because trying something is the classic route to a discovery. `のだ`/`んです` at the end, because the sentence is explaining a state of affairs rather than reporting an event. And <code><ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>の</code> or <code><ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>に</code> inside the main clause - <ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>意味<rt>いみ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かった - because the whole point is that a shallower version of the understanding existed before.

Written Japanese sometimes reverses the halves for effect: <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>づくのだ、<ruby>失<rt>うしな</rt></ruby>って<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて。 That is a stylistic inversion, not a second pattern, and you will not need to produce it.

---

## ～て初めて · ～て初めて vs ～てから

> **Rule of thumb** - `てから` tells you the **order**. <code>て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて</code> adds a claim about everything before that point: until then, it had not happened and *could* not have.

<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>てから、<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>み<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めました。<br>*I started living alone after I finished university.*

That is a timeline. Now the same shape with the "not until" claim added:

<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>**て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて**、<ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>する<ruby>気持<rt>きも</rt></ruby>ちが<ruby>生<rt>う</rt></ruby>まれた。<br>*It was leaving university that first gave me any sense of gratitude towards my parents.*

---

## ～て初めて · ～て初めて vs ～てから (cont.)

The practical test: can you add "and not before"? If yes, <code>て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて</code>. If the sentence is just two things in order - and especially if the second one is planned - it is `てから`.

---

## ～て初めて · ～て初めて vs 初めて (the adverb)

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて</code> standing in front of a verb means "for the first time" and describes the verb. <code>て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて</code> sits **between** two clauses and says the first one unlocked the second.

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>**<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて**<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I went to Kyoto for the first time last year.*

<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>っ**て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて**、<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>庭<rt>にわ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>美<rt>うつく</rt></ruby>しさが<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりました。<br>*It was going to Kyoto that showed me how beautiful Japanese gardens are.*

---

## ～て初めて · ～て初めて vs 初めて (the adverb) (cont.)

Both sentences contain the same three words; only the position of <code><ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて</code> differs, and it changes what is being claimed. In the first, the trip was a first. In the second, the trip is the cause and the understanding is the first.

---

## ～て初めて · ～て初めて vs ～ないと分からない

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて</code> reports something that **actually happened**. `ないと`／`なければ` makes a hypothetical claim about what would be true in general.

やってみないと、<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>いているかどうか<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりませんよ。<br>*You won't know if it suits you unless you give it a go.*

That is advice, aimed at the future, and nothing has been experienced yet. Compare the report of an experience already had:

やってみ**て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて**、<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>いていないと<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりました。<br>*Only after trying it did I work out that it wasn't for me.*

---

## ～て初めて · ～て初めて vs ～ないと分からない (cont.)

Because of that difference, <code>て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて</code> almost never appears with a future-tense main clause, while <code>ないと<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からない</code> almost always does.

---

## ～て初めて · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んだ<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて、<ruby>作者<rt>さくしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>気持<rt>きも</rt></ruby>ちが<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かった。 → ✅ <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んで<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて、<ruby>作者<rt>さくしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>気持<rt>きも</rt></ruby>ちが<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かった。<br>*The verb takes the て-form, not た. The past meaning is already carried by the main clause.*

- ❌ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>て、<ruby>文化<rt>ぶんか</rt></ruby>の<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>いが<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かった。 → ✅ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>て<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて、<ruby>文化<rt>ぶんか</rt></ruby>の<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>いが<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かった。<br>*Put <code><ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて</code> before the verb and you have said "I came to Japan for the first time"; the "not until" reading is gone.*

- ❌ <ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>して<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて、<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>こうと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。 → ✅ <ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>してから、<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>こうと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。<br>*The main clause is a plan, and this pattern needs a realisation. A plan takes `てから`.*

---

## ～て初めて · Common mistakes (cont.)

# ～てからでないと

**Meaning** · unless X is done first, Y is impossible - X is a precondition, not just an earlier step

`てからでないと` sets a gate. It names something that has to be finished before the rest becomes possible, and then says what cannot happen until it is. The second half of the sentence is **always negative** in meaning; that is the single fact that makes this pattern behave differently from every other "after".

---

## ～てからでないと · Formation

Take the て-form, add `からでないと`, and finish the sentence with something that cannot happen. Several endings are interchangeable and differ only in register.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (て-form) | て-form + からでないと | <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べる → <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べてからでないと |
| Verb (て-form), formal | て-form + からでなければ | <ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>してからでなければ |
| Verb (て-form), stiff/written | て-form + からでなくては | <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>いてからでなくては |
| Verb (て-form), casual | て-form + からじゃないと | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてからじゃないと |
| Main clause | must be negative or "impossible" | …<ruby>答<rt>こた</rt></ruby>えられません／<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>です／だめです |
| ❌ Dictionary form | ❌<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べるからでないと | ✅<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べてからでないと |
| ❌ Positive main clause | ❌…てからでないと、できます | ✅…てからでないと、できません |

---

## ～てからでないと · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - write the ending first. If you cannot finish the sentence with できない、<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>だ、いけない、わからない or another negative, you do not want this pattern; you want plain `てから`.

---

## ～てからでないと · Examples

<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>っ**てからでないと**、<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてはいけません。<br>*You mustn't eat until you've washed your hands.*

<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>し**てからでないと**、<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>れませんよ。<br>*You can't get in unless you book first.*

<ruby>説明書<rt>せつめいしょ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ん**でからでないと**、この<ruby>機械<rt>きかい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>えない。<br>*You can't use this machine without reading the manual first.*

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>い**てからでないと**、お<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>できません。<br>*I can't give you an answer until I've asked the manager.*

---

## ～てからでないと · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ**てからじゃないと**、<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>できないな。<br>*I can't order it till I've checked the price.*

<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby><ruby>運動<rt>うんどう</rt></ruby>をし**てからでないと**、プールに<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってはいけないことになっている。<br>*The rule is that you may not enter the pool without doing warm-up exercises first.*

<ruby>契約<rt>けいやく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>内容<rt>ないよう</rt></ruby>をすべて<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>し**てからでなければ**、サインすることはできません。<br>*I cannot sign until I have checked every part of the contract.*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもが<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>**てからでないと**、<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>なんてまったく<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>れないんです。<br>*Honestly, I get no time to myself at all until the children are asleep.*

---

## ～てからでないと · Nuance & register

The pattern is long, and that length is part of its job: it is a **polite way of saying no**. A shop assistant, a receptionist or a colleague who cannot commit reaches for it constantly, because it refuses while pointing at the step that would unblock things - <code><ruby>上司<rt>じょうし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>してからでないと、お<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>できかねます</code>. Learners who only know `できません` sound much blunter than they intend.

The four endings form a rough ladder. `からじゃないと` is casual speech and very common among friends; `てからでないと` is the neutral all-purpose form; `からでなければ` and `からでなくては` lean formal and written, the sort of thing you meet in rules, contracts and announcements. Nothing else changes.

---

## ～てからでないと · Nuance & register (cont.)

> **Note** - do not confuse this with `～てからでは`, which looks almost identical and means the opposite kind of thing: "once X has happened, it's too late." <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きてからでは<ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>い。 There, X is something you want to avoid; in `てからでないと`, X is something you want done.

Because the main clause is negative, the pattern often ends up describing rules, and it is very common in the passive-ish `～ことになっている` frame that states how things are done at a company, a school or a pool.

---

## ～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～てから

> **Rule of thumb** - `てから` puts two events in order. `てからでないと` claims the second one is **blocked** until the first is finished.

シャワーを<ruby>浴<rt>あ</rt></ruby>びてから、<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけます。<br>*I'll have a shower and then go out.*

That is a plan, and nothing is impossible about going out first. Now a genuine gate:

チケットを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>っ**てからでないと**、<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>れません。<br>*You can't go inside until you've bought a ticket.*

---

## ～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～てから (cont.)

The two are not interchangeable in either direction. Say ❌チケットを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ってからでないと、<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>れます and the sentence falls apart, because the pattern demands a negative. Say <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ってから<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ります and you have merely described your afternoon.

---

## ～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～ないと

> **Rule of thumb** - `ないと` negates the **action**: if you don't do X, no Y. `てからでないと` negates the **timing**: X has to be *finished* before Y is even possible.

めがねをかけないと、<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めません。<br>*I can't read the writing without my glasses.*

Nothing here is about completion - the glasses are a condition that holds throughout. Compare a case where finishing is the whole point:

<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>**てからでないと**、<ruby>次<rt>つぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>てられない。<br>*I can't make any further plans until the exam results are out.*

---

## ～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～ないと (cont.)

In practice, when either would work, `てからでないと` sounds more considered and more formal, and it stresses order; `ないと` is shorter and states a bare condition. A useful test: if you can insert "first" into the English, the longer pattern is available.

---

## ～てからでないと · ～てからでないと vs ～た上で

> **Rule of thumb** - both are formal and both put a considered step first, but <code>た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で</code> leads to a **decision that will happen**, while `てからでないと` leads to a **refusal**.

<ruby>見積<rt>みつ</rt></ruby>もりを<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>した<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で、ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>いたします。<br>*I'll be in touch once I've gone over the quotation.*

<ruby>見積<rt>みつ</rt></ruby>もりを<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>し**てからでないと**、ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>いたしかねます。<br>*I'm afraid I can't get back to you until I've gone over the quotation.*

Same first step, opposite framing. The first sentence promises; the second declines for now. In a business email that difference is very visible, so choose by what you want the reader to take away.

---

## ～てからでないと · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>してからでないと、<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>れます。 → ✅ <ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>してからでないと、<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>れません。<br>*The main clause must be negative. With a positive ending, the sentence contradicts itself.*

- ❌ <ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>するからでないと、<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>れません。 → ✅ <ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>してからでないと、<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>れません。<br>*It is built on the て-form. The dictionary form cannot take `からでないと`.*

- ❌ <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>になってからでないと<ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>いですよ。 → ✅ <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>になってからでは<ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>いですよ。<br>*"Once you're ill it's too late" is the `てからでは` pattern. `でないと` would make falling ill a precondition for something, which is not the intent.*

---

## ～てからでないと · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>になってからでないと、<ruby>桜<rt>さくら</rt></ruby>が<ruby>咲<rt>さ</rt></ruby>きます。 → ✅ <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>にならないと、<ruby>桜<rt>さくら</rt></ruby>は<ruby>咲<rt>さ</rt></ruby>きません。<br>*Two faults: the ending has to be negative, and a natural process that nobody performs sits better with plain `ないと` than with the deliberate, step-completing `てからでないと`.*

# ～ついでに

**Meaning** · while you're doing X anyway, do Y as well - Y gets a free ride

`ついでに` is the grammar of efficiency. X is the real reason you are going, doing or moving; Y is a smaller job you slip in because the opportunity is already there. If Y is the reason and X is the excuse, you have the two the wrong way round.

---

## ～ついでに · Formation

`ついで` is a noun - "an occasion that happens to arise" - so a clause modifies it in the plain form, and a noun attaches with `の`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (dictionary) - not yet done | dictionary + ついでに | <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かける → <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけるついでに |
| Verb (た-form) - already under way | た-form + ついでに | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったついでに |
| Action noun | Noun + の + ついでに | <ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>のついでに |
| On its own, at the head of a sentence | ついでに、… | ついでに、これもお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>い |
| As a plain noun | ついでが+ある／ついでで | ついでがあったら<ruby>寄<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ります |
| ❌ の after a verb | ❌<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くのついでに | ✅<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くついでに |
| ❌ Y bigger than X | ❌<ruby>切手<rt>きって</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>うついでに<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>した | ✅<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>すついでに<ruby>切手<rt>きって</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った |

---

## ～ついでに · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `ついでに` needs a **main errand** and a **minor extra**, in that order, and they must share one outing, one trip or one stretch of activity. The dictionary form means the errand is still ahead of you; the た-form means you are already out.

---

## ～ついでに · Examples

<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>の**ついでに**、<ruby>郵便局<rt>ゆうびんきょく</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>寄<rt>よ</rt></ruby>った。<br>*While I was out shopping I stopped by the post office too.*

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>をふく**ついでに**、カーテンも<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ってしまおう。<br>*Since I'm cleaning the windows anyway, I may as well wash the curtains.*

---

## ～ついでに · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た**ついでに**、<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>になっていた<ruby>美術館<rt>びじゅつかん</rt></ruby>をのぞいてきました。<br>*Since the meeting brought me all the way to Tokyo, I looked in on a museum I'd been curious about.*

## ～ついでに · Nuance & register

`ついでに` is register-neutral and enormously common in everyday speech, but it carries a social charge that learners should feel. Attached to a **request**, it shrinks the favour: コンビニに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くついでに means "this costs you nothing extra", and that is why it makes an imposition easy to accept. Attached to your **own** offer, it does the same work in reverse - 「ついでですから、<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>にしないでください」 is the standard way to wave off thanks for a lift or a delivery.

---

## ～ついでに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The flip side is that calling something a `ついで` **demotes** it. Say <ruby>結婚式<rt>けっこんしき</rt></ruby>のついでに and you have announced that the wedding was a convenient excuse. Around anything the other person takes seriously - a funeral, a hospital visit, a formal invitation - reach for a neutral phrase such as <code><ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りに</code> or <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った<ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>に</code> instead. Japanese speakers also avoid `ついでに` when talking about something they did *for* a superior, since it implies the superior's business was the side job.

The choice between dictionary and た-form is aspect, not politeness. The dictionary form plans: <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くついでに<ruby>寄<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ろう. The た-form reports from inside the trip: <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったついでに<ruby>寄<rt>よ</rt></ruby>った. Both are common, and native speakers slide between them without ceremony.

---

## ～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～ながら

> **Rule of thumb** - `ながら` means the two actions happen **at the same time**, both by the same person. `ついでに` means one **occasion** covers two separate jobs; they can be minutes apart.

<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きながら<ruby>皿<rt>さら</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*I'm doing the washing-up while listening to music.*

You genuinely do both at once, so this cannot be `ついでに`. Now something you cannot do simultaneously:

ゴミを<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**ついでに**、ポストに<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れてきた。<br>*I put the letter in the postbox while I was out taking the rubbish down.*

---

## ～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～ながら (cont.)

Note also which clause is the main one. With `ながら` the main action is the **second** clause; with `ついでに` the main errand is the **first**. Swap them and you change what the sentence is about.

---

## ～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～途中で

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で</code> is about **position on a route**: somewhere between start and finish. `ついでに` is about **taking advantage of an occasion**, and the extra job does not have to lie on the way.

<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>本屋<rt>ほんや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>寄<rt>よ</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*I dropped into a bookshop on the way home.*

<ruby>実家<rt>じっか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る**ついでに**、<ruby>祖母<rt>そぼ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*Since I was going back to my parents' anyway, I looked in on my grandmother too.*

---

## ～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～途中で (cont.)

The grandmother's house may be a detour of an hour; `ついでに` does not care, as long as the one trip made it worth doing. <code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で</code> would be false if she lived in the opposite direction. Conversely, <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ってきた is fine and `ついでに` is impossible there, because rain is not an errand you slip in.

---

## ～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～際に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に</code> is a formal "on the occasion of" and says nothing about efficiency. `ついでに` always implies the second job was tacked on to save a trip - which is why it is out of place in a notice or a polite request to a customer.

ご<ruby>来店<rt>らいてん</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に**、こちらのカードをお<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>せください。<br>*Please show this card when you visit the shop.*

Replacing that with ご<ruby>来店<rt>らいてん</rt></ruby>のついでに would tell customers their visit is a side errand - polite in form, insulting in content. Among friends the same information is perfectly friendly:

---

## ～ついでに · ～ついでに vs ～際に (cont.)

<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**ついでに**、このカード<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>せてきてよ。<br>*Show them this card while you're at the shop.*

So the split is two-way: register (formal versus everyday) and meaning (occasion versus opportunity taken).

---

## ～ついでに · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>するのついでに、パンを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った。 → ✅ <ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>するついでに、パンを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った。<br>*A verb modifies `ついで` directly, with no `の`. The `の` appears only after a noun: <ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>のついでに.*

- ❌ <ruby>切手<rt>きって</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>うついでに、<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しの<ruby>手続<rt>てつづ</rt></ruby>きをした。 → ✅ <ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しの<ruby>手続<rt>てつづ</rt></ruby>きのついでに、<ruby>切手<rt>きって</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った。<br>*The bigger errand has to come first. As written, the sentence claims a house move was a minor extra attached to buying stamps.*

- ❌ お<ruby>見舞<rt>みま</rt></ruby>いのついでに、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>をしてきました。 → ✅ お<ruby>見舞<rt>みま</rt></ruby>いの<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りに、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>をしてきました。<br>*Grammatically fine, socially cold: it says visiting the patient was the convenient excuse. Use <code><ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りに</code> when the first event matters to someone.*

---

## ～ついでに · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>するついでに<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いています。 → ✅ <ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しながら<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*Two activities that overlap continuously need `ながら`. `ついでに` wants two distinct jobs sharing one occasion.*

{reveal}

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>結婚式<rt>けっこんしき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったついでに、<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>してもらいました。

{reveal}

## ～ついでに · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**ついでに**、<ruby>叔父<rt>おじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>います。 (b) <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った<ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>叔父<rt>おじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>います。

# ～際に

**Meaning** · at the time of; on the occasion of - the formal word for "when"

<code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に</code> (read さいに) does the job of <code><ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>に</code>, but it belongs to a different world: notices, manuals, contracts, announcements and careful business speech. It also asks for a real **occasion** - something that takes place - rather than any old state of affairs.

---

## ～際に · Formation

<code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby></code> is a noun, so the clause in front of it takes a plain form, and a noun attaches with `の`.

---

## ～際に · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (dictionary) | dictionary + <ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に | <ruby>利用<rt>りよう</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>利用<rt>りよう</rt></ruby>する<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に |
| Verb (た-form) | た-form + <ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に | <ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んだ<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に |
| Event noun | Noun + の + <ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に | <ruby>入学<rt>にゅうがく</rt></ruby> → <ruby>入学<rt>にゅうがく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に |
| With は - "in the event of" | ～<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>は | お<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りの<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>は |
| Before a noun | ～<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>の + Noun | <ruby>使用<rt>しよう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby> |

---

## ～際に · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - two tests. Is the sentence formal enough that <code><ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby></code> would sound too casual? And is the clause an **event** rather than a state? Both must be yes. Adjectives never pass the second test.

---

## ～際に · Examples

ご<ruby>利用<rt>りよう</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>は**、こちらのボタンをお<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>しください。<br>*Please press this button when using the machine.*

お<ruby>降<rt>お</rt></ruby>りの**<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>は**、<ruby>足元<rt>あしもと</rt></ruby>にご<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>ください。<br>*Please watch your step as you get off.*

<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>みの**<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>身分証明書<rt>みぶんしょうめいしょ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>です。<br>*You will need identification when you apply.*

<ruby>面接<rt>めんせつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>けた**<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>給料<rt>きゅうりょう</rt></ruby>のことも<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いておきました。<br>*I asked about the salary as well when I had the interview.*

---

## ～際に · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>海外<rt>かいがい</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>する**<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>には**、<ruby>保険<rt>ほけん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っておいたほうがいい。<br>*When you travel abroad on business, you'd do well to take out insurance.*

<ruby>非常<rt>ひじょう</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>は**、エレベーターを<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>わずに<ruby>階段<rt>かいだん</rt></ruby>をご<ruby>利用<rt>りよう</rt></ruby>ください。<br>*In an emergency, please use the stairs rather than the lift.*

<ruby>契約<rt>けいやく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>更新<rt>こうしん</rt></ruby>する**<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>には**、<ruby>三<rt>さん</rt></ruby>か<ruby>月<rt>げつ</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>までにご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>いただく<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がございます。<br>*If you wish to renew the contract, you must notify us at least three months in advance.*

<ruby>退職<rt>たいしょく</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に**お<ruby>世話<rt>せわ</rt></ruby>になった<ruby>方々<rt>かたがた</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>ずつ<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I wrote individually to everyone who helped me when I left the company.*

---

## ～際に · Nuance & register

This is a **formal** word and it never stops being one. Put it in a conversation about breakfast and it sounds like a train announcement. The natural homes for it are: station and shop announcements, instruction manuals, application forms, company rules, and speech where you are addressing a customer or a superior.

It pairs constantly with honorific prefixes - ご<ruby>利用<rt>りよう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>、 お<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りの<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>、ご<ruby>来館<rt>らいかん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby> - because the same register that wants <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby></code> also wants お／ご. If you are writing a notice for customers, that combination is the default.

---

## ～際に · Nuance & register (cont.)

The particle after it shifts the feel slightly. <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に</code> points at a moment. <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>は</code> sets up a condition and is the form you see in warnings - "should this happen, do that". <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>には</code> is both at once and is very common in careful writing. In printed notices the particle often disappears altogether, leaving a comma: ご<ruby>記入<rt>きにゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>、 <ruby>黒<rt>くろ</rt></ruby>のペンをお<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>いください。

> **Note** - a close relative, <code>～に<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>して</code>, means "on the occasion of" in an even weightier way and belongs to N2: <ruby>開会<rt>かいかい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>して<ruby>一言<rt>ひとこと</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>挨拶<rt>あいさつ</rt></ruby><ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げます。 It marks ceremonies and turning points, not everyday procedures.

---

## ～際に · ～際に vs ～時に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby></code> takes anything: verbs, adjectives, nouns, states, trivia. <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby></code> takes only **events**, and only in formal contexts. Everywhere <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby></code> works, <code><ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby></code> also works; the reverse fails constantly.

<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さい<ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>、よく<ruby>祖父<rt>そふ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>泊<rt>と</rt></ruby>まった。<br>*When I was small I often stayed at my grandfather's house.*

An adjective and a childhood state - <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby></code> is impossible here on two counts. Compare a procedure described to a customer:

---

## ～際に · ～際に vs ～時に (cont.)

<ruby>商品<rt>しょうひん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>返品<rt>へんぴん</rt></ruby>される**<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>は**、レシートをお<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちください。<br>*When returning an item, please bring your receipt.*

There <code><ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby></code> would be grammatical but would flatten the register; the notice would read as if a friend had scribbled it. The tense rule you learned for <code><ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby></code> carries over unchanged: dictionary form for "before/as you do it", た-form for "after you have done it".

---

## ～際に · ～際に vs ～場合は

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby></code> assumes the event **will** happen and tells you what to do at that moment. <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code> says "if it should turn out that…" and branches between possibilities.

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>体育館<rt>たいいくかん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>おこな</rt></ruby>います。<br>*If it rains, it will be held in the gym.*

Rain may or may not come, and the sentence exists to cover the branch - that is <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code>, and <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby></code> would be wrong because rain is not an occasion anyone attends. Now something that is definitely going to happen:

---

## ～際に · ～際に vs ～場合は (cont.)

ご<ruby>入場<rt>にゅうじょう</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に**、チケットを<ruby>拝見<rt>はいけん</rt></ruby>いたします。<br>*We will check your ticket as you enter.*

Everyone entering will enter, so there is no branch to state. A quick diagnostic: if the English wants "if", think <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code>; if it wants "when", think <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby></code>. Both are formal, so register does not separate them.

---

## ～際に · ～際に vs ～た上で

> **Rule of thumb** - both live in the same formal register, but <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に</code> locates something **at the time of** an event, while <code>た<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で</code> puts it **after** the event and makes it depend on the result.

お<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>みの**<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に**、ご<ruby>希望<rt>きぼう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>をお<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きください。<br>*Please write your preferred date when you apply.*

お<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>内容<rt>ないよう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>した<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>で、ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>いたします。<br>*We will contact you once we have checked the details of your application.*

---

## ～際に · ～際に vs ～た上で (cont.)

In the first, the writing happens during the application. In the second, the contact happens afterwards *because of* what the check revealed. Swapping them produces sentences that are still grammatical but say something the writer did not mean, which is why forms and letters keep the two carefully apart.

---

## ～際に · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に、アルバイトをたくさんしました。 → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>に、アルバイトをたくさんしました。<br>*"Being a student" is a state that lasted for years, not an occasion. <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby></code> needs a bounded event.*

# ～たびに

**Meaning** · every time X happens, Y happens - without exception, as far as the speaker has seen

`たびに` counts **occasions**, not intervals. Each time the first event comes round, the second one comes with it. It is the pattern you reach for when a pairing has become reliable enough to comment on: every time I see him, every time it rains, every time that song comes on.

---

## ～たびに · Formation

`たび` is the noun <code><ruby>度<rt>たび</rt></ruby></code>, "an occurrence", so a clause modifies it in the plain form and a noun attaches with `の`. The verb is in the **dictionary form** no matter when the events happened.

---

## ～たびに · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (dictionary) | dictionary + たびに | <ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>うたびに |
| Action noun | Noun + の + たびに | <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>のたびに |
| Written style | ～<ruby>度<rt>たび</rt></ruby>に | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る<ruby>度<rt>たび</rt></ruby>に |
| As a connector | そのたびに | …。そのたびに<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>している |
| ❌ た-form | ❌<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ったたびに | ✅<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>うたびに |
| ❌ Noun without の | ❌<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>たびに | ✅<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>のたびに |
| ❌ A measured interval | ❌<ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>たびに | ✅<ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>ごとに |

---

## ～たびに · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - if you can put "every time" in front of an English *clause*, you want `たびに`. If what follows "every" is a **number plus a unit** - every three days, every two rows - you want `ごとに` instead.

---

## ～たびに · Examples

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う**たびに**、<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>をもらう。<br>*Every time I see him I come away with more energy.*

この<ruby>歌<rt>うた</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>く**たびに**、<ruby>高校<rt>こうこう</rt></ruby><ruby>時代<rt>じだい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す。<br>*Every time I hear this song I think of my high-school days.*

<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>の**たびに**、<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じかばんを<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*I use the same bag every time I travel.*

<ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る**たびに**、この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>停電<rt>ていでん</rt></ruby>する。<br>*Every time a typhoon comes through, this area loses power.*

---

## ～たびに · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>甥<rt>おい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う**たびに**<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>びていて、おどろかされる。<br>*My nephew is taller every time I see him - it's startling.*

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しの**たびに**、<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby><ruby>箱<rt>ばこ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>捨<rt>す</rt></ruby>てることになる。<br>*Every move ends with me throwing out several boxes of books.*

<ruby>祖母<rt>そぼ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>訪<rt>たず</rt></ruby>ねる**たびに**、<ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>のものを<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>たせようとする。<br>*Every time I visit, my grandmother tries to send me home with the entire contents of her fridge.*

<ruby>担当者<rt>たんとうしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わる**たびに**<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>をやり<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>さなければならず、<ruby>正直<rt>しょうじき</rt></ruby>うんざりしています。<br>*Having to start the whole explanation again every time they change our contact person is, frankly, wearing me down.*

---

## ～たびに · Nuance & register

`たびに` is register-neutral: it appears in casual speech, in polite speech and in writing without changing shape, and the kanji spelling <code><ruby>度<rt>たび</rt></ruby></code> is simply a written-style choice. What it does carry is a mild **emotional charge**. The speaker has noticed the pattern and usually has a feeling about it - affection, irritation, nostalgia, surprise. Sentences with `たびに` very often end in <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code>, `うれしくなる`, <code><ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>る</code> or a passive of surprise.

That is also why it loves **change over time**. With a comparative or a verb of growth, `たびに` produces the "more and more each time" reading: <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>むたびに<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>発見<rt>はっけん</rt></ruby>がある。 Nothing extra is added to the grammar; the repetition does the work.

---

## ～たびに · Nuance & register (cont.)

`そのたびに` is worth learning as a fixed connector for narrating: you describe a recurring situation in one sentence, then open the next with そのたびに - "and each time that happened…". It saves you repeating the whole clause.

One limit: because the pattern generalises, it does not fit a **single** future occasion. <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>うたびに makes no sense, because you are meeting once.

---

## ～たびに · ～たびに vs ～ごとに

> **Rule of thumb** - `たびに` counts **events**; `ごとに` counts **units**. If a number and a counter come before it, use `ごとに`; if a clause comes before it, use `たびに`.

バスは<ruby>十五分<rt>じゅうごふん</rt></ruby>ごとに<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ています。<br>*The buses run every fifteen minutes.*

You cannot say ❌<ruby>十五分<rt>じゅうごふん</rt></ruby>たびに, because fifteen minutes is a measured interval rather than an occasion. The other direction fails just as hard:

<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>は<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>する**たびに**、<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>をする。<br>*Every time I ring, my mother tells me the same story.*

---

## ～たびに · ～たびに vs ～ごとに (cont.)

Here the phone calls happen when they happen - there is no fixed interval - and `ごとに` would be wrong. There is a small overlap with nouns: <ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>のたびに and <ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>ごとに are both possible, but the first means "each time there is a match" and the second is closer to "match by match", tracking a change across a series.

---

## ～たびに · ～たびに vs ～と

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` states an **automatic consequence**: this causes that, every time, as a matter of how the world works. `たびに` merely reports that the two keep coming together, and it can carry the speaker's reaction.

<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>になると、この<ruby>川<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えます。<br>*When spring comes, the water in this river rises.*

That is nature, not commentary. Compare an observation with a person in it:

<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>酒<rt>さけ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む**たびに**、<ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>いころの<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める。<br>*Every time my father drinks, he starts on stories about his youth.*

---

## ～たびに · ～たびに vs ～と (cont.)

`と` would also be grammatical there, but it would present the storytelling as a mechanical reflex. `たびに` keeps the speaker in the sentence - you can hear the raised eyebrow. Conversely, `と` accepts states and conditions (<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いと<ruby>売<rt>う</rt></ruby>れる) where `たびに` needs a repeatable event.

---

## ～たびに · ～たびに vs ～時に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>に</code> picks out **one** occasion, or is neutral about how many. `たびに` insists on **all** of them.

<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った<ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>に、この<ruby>茶碗<rt>ちゃわん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*I bought this teacup when I went to Kyoto.*

One trip, one purchase. Now the generalising version:

<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**たびに**、<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>器<rt>うつわ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ってしまう。<br>*Every time I go to Kyoto I end up buying pottery at the same shop.*

---

## ～たびに · ～たびに vs ～時に (cont.)

The tense rule also differs. <code><ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby></code> chooses between dictionary and た-form to show whether the main clause happens before or after it; `たびに` has no such choice, because it is always the dictionary form.

---

## ～たびに · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったたびに、<ruby>温泉<rt>おんせん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ります。 → ✅ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くたびに、<ruby>温泉<rt>おんせん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*`たびに` always follows the dictionary form. The past reading, if any, comes from the main clause.*

- ❌ <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>たびに、<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>り<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す。 → ✅ <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>のたびに、<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>り<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す。<br>*`たび` is a noun, so another noun has to be linked with `の`.*

- ❌ <ruby>二年<rt>にねん</rt></ruby>たびに、<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>替<rt>か</rt></ruby>えている。 → ✅ <ruby>二年<rt>にねん</rt></ruby>ごとに、<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>替<rt>か</rt></ruby>えている。<br>*A number plus a unit of time is an interval, and intervals take `ごとに` or `おきに`, never `たびに`.*

# ～ごとに

**Meaning** · each; every; at intervals of - one unit at a time, with none skipped

`ごとに` divides something into equal units and says that whatever follows applies to **every one of them**. The units can be measured - three days, five metres - or they can be categories: each country, each department, each class. Either way, nothing is left out and nothing is skipped over.

---

## ～ごとに · Formation

`ごと` is a suffix. It attaches straight to a quantity or to a noun, with no particle in between.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Number + counter (time) | Quantity + ごとに | <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby> → <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>ごとに |
| Number + counter (distance, things) | Quantity + ごとに | <ruby>五<rt>ご</rt></ruby>メートル → <ruby>五<rt>ご</rt></ruby>メートルごとに |
| Noun as a category | Noun + ごとに | <ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby> → <ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>ごとに |
| Verb (dictionary), literary | dictionary + ごとに | <ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>るごとに |
| Before a noun | ～ごとの + Noun | <ruby>部署<rt>ぶしょ</rt></ruby>ごとの<ruby>予算<rt>よさん</rt></ruby> |
| ❌ の between noun and ごと | ❌<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>のごとに | ✅<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>ごとに |
| ❌ な before a noun | ❌<ruby>一年<rt>いちねん</rt></ruby>ごとな<ruby>検査<rt>けんさ</rt></ruby> | ✅<ruby>一年<rt>いちねん</rt></ruby>ごとの<ruby>検査<rt>けんさ</rt></ruby> |
| ❌ Doubling with <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt></ruby>～ | ❌<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby>ごとに | ✅<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby> / <ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>ごとに |

---

## ～ごとに · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `ごとに` states the **length of the cycle**, counting the unit itself. <ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>ごとに is therefore *every day*, and <ruby>二日<rt>ふつか</rt></ruby>ごとに is *every second day*. Do not translate `ごと` as "other".

---

## ～ごとに · Examples

<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>は<ruby>二日<rt>ふつか</rt></ruby>**ごとに**やってください。<br>*Please water it every two days.*

<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>**ごとに**<ruby>習慣<rt>しゅうかん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>います。<br>*Customs differ from country to country.*

クラス**ごとに**<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*We'll take the photographs class by class.*

この<ruby>検査<rt>けんさ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>半年<rt>はんとし</rt></ruby>**ごとに**<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>けることになっている。<br>*You're supposed to have this check-up every six months.*

---

## ～ごとに · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>の<ruby>両側<rt>りょうがわ</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>五<rt>ご</rt></ruby>メートル**ごとに**<ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>が<ruby>植<rt>う</rt></ruby>えてある。<br>*Trees have been planted every five metres along both sides of the road.*

<ruby>部署<rt>ぶしょ</rt></ruby>**ごとに**<ruby>予算<rt>よさん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>められているので、<ruby>勝手<rt>かって</rt></ruby>には<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>えません。<br>*The budget is set department by department, so I can't just spend it as I like.*

<ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る**ごとに**、<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>過<rt>す</rt></ruby>ぎるのが<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>感<rt>かん</rt></ruby>じられるようになった。<br>*With each passing year, time seems to move faster.*

この<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>ではプロジェクト**ごとに**チームを<ruby>組<rt>く</rt></ruby>み<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>すので、<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby>ぶれで<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>くことは<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>ない。<br>*This company rebuilds its teams for each project, so you rarely work with the same faces twice.*

---

## ～ごとに · Nuance & register

`ごとに` is neutral and slightly businesslike. It is the word of timetables, manuals, survey results and internal rules - anything that has to state a rate or a breakdown precisely - and it sounds perfectly normal in speech too. It is not formal in the way <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に</code> is; it is simply exact.

The two jobs it does feel quite different in use. With a **number** it gives a rate, and English usually says "every X". With a **category noun** it gives a breakdown, and English switches to "by" or "each": <ruby>地域<rt>ちいき</rt></ruby>ごとに → "region by region"; <ruby>家庭<rt>かてい</rt></ruby>ごとに → "in each household". That second use is extremely common in reports, almost always paired with <code><ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う</code>, <code><ruby>異<rt>こと</rt></ruby>なる</code> or <code><ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>める</code>.

---

## ～ごとに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The verb form - <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>るごとに、<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>うごとに - is real but literary, and in ordinary speech `たびに` has taken the job over. You can safely read it and avoid writing it.

> **Note** - a different `ごと` exists, with no `に`, meaning "together with the whole of it": りんごを<ruby>皮<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>ごと<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる, "eat the apple skin and all"; <ruby>丸<rt>まる</rt></ruby>ごと, "the whole thing". It attaches to a noun exactly like ours, so read the sentence, not the suffix - if there is no `に` and no counting, it is this other word.

---

## ～ごとに · ～ごとに vs ～おきに

> **Rule of thumb** - `ごとに` measures the **cycle**; `おきに` measures the **gap left empty** between one occurrence and the next. With days and countable things the two therefore differ by one.

<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>**ごとに**<ruby>記録<rt>きろく</rt></ruby>をつけています。<br>*I keep a record every day.*

<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>おきに<ruby>記録<rt>きろく</rt></ruby>をつけています。<br>*I keep a record every other day.*

Same number, different arithmetic: the first is Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday; the second is Monday, Wednesday, Friday. Rewritten with `ごとに`, the second sentence becomes <ruby>二日<rt>ふつか</rt></ruby>ごとに.

---

## ～ごとに · ～ごとに vs ～おきに (cont.)

With units of duration or length the two happen to coincide, because there is no "container" to leave empty: <ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>ごとに and <ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>おきに both describe something happening every ten minutes. That coincidence is exactly why learners generalise from minutes to days and get it wrong. When the unit is a **day, a week, a seat or an item**, stop and check which one you mean.

`ごとに` also has a whole second life that `おきに` does not: the category use. <ruby>学年<rt>がくねん</rt></ruby>ごとに<ruby>時間割<rt>じかんわり</rt></ruby>が<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う is fine, and ❌<ruby>学年<rt>がくねん</rt></ruby>おきに would mean skipping alternate year groups.

---

## ～ごとに · ～ごとに vs ～たびに

> **Rule of thumb** - `ごとに` follows a **unit**; `たびに` follows a **clause** describing an event. "Every three days" is `ごとに`; "every time I go" is `たびに`.

<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>の**たびに**<ruby>胃<rt>い</rt></ruby>が<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>くなる。<br>*My stomach hurts every time there's an exam.*

<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>**ごとに**<ruby>成績<rt>せいせき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>記録<rt>きろく</rt></ruby>しています。<br>*We record the results for each exam.*

Both are grammatical, and the pair shows the difference cleanly. The first counts occasions and reports the speaker's reaction to all of them. The second treats the exams as a series of units and does something *per unit* - a breakdown, not a reaction. That is why `ごとに` is at home in a spreadsheet and `たびに` is at home in a diary.

---

## ～ごとに · ～ごとに vs ～ずつ

> **Rule of thumb** - `ずつ` distributes an **amount**: how much each one gets. `ごとに` marks the **unit itself**: for each of these, such-and-such is true.

<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>に<ruby>二<rt>ふた</rt></ruby>つずつ<ruby>配<rt>くば</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*Please hand out two to each person.*

The number that matters there is the two. Now a sentence where the number that matters is the unit:

<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>**ごとに**グループを<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*Please form groups of three.*

A useful diagnostic: `ずつ` answers "how many each?", `ごとに` answers "per what?". They can appear together - <ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>ごとに<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つずつ<ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>す - and when they do, each is doing its own half of the job.

---

## ～ごとに · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>毎週<rt>まいしゅう</rt></ruby>ごとに<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>があります。 → ✅ <ruby>毎週<rt>まいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*<code><ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt></ruby>～</code> already means "every", so adding `ごとに` says it twice. Use one or the other.*

- ❌ <ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>ごとに<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。（intending every other day） → ✅ <ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>おきに<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*`ごとに` counts the cycle, so <ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>ごとに is *every* day. ---

- ❌ プリントを<ruby>一<rt>いち</rt></ruby>まいごとに<ruby>配<rt>くば</rt></ruby>ってください。 → ✅ プリントを<ruby>一<rt>いち</rt></ruby>まいずつ<ruby>配<rt>くば</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*The sentence is about how many each person receives, which is `ずつ`. `ごとに` would make each sheet a unit that something is done per.*

# ～から～にかけて

**Meaning** · from roughly A through to roughly B - one continuous stretch, with soft edges

`から～にかけて` draws a band across time or across a map. Something goes on right the way through it, and the two ends are deliberately **approximate**: not "from exactly here to exactly there", but "somewhere around here to somewhere around there". Weather forecasts and history books run on it.

---

## ～から～にかけて · Formation

Both halves take nouns. The pattern is a pair, and neither half works alone.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Time expressions | Noun + から + Noun + にかけて | <ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby> → <ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby>から<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>にかけて |
| Places | Noun + から + Noun + にかけて | <ruby>関東<rt>かんとう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>関東<rt>かんとう</rt></ruby>から<ruby>東北<rt>とうほく</rt></ruby>にかけて |
| Dates, months, seasons | Noun + から + Noun + にかけて | <ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>四月<rt>しがつ</rt></ruby>にかけて |
| Eras | Noun + から + Noun + にかけて | <ruby>昭和<rt>しょうわ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>平成<rt>へいせい</rt></ruby>にかけて |
| Before a noun | ～から～にかけての + Noun | <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>から<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>にかけての<ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby> |

---

## ～から～にかけて · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the thing you describe must **spread across** the whole band, not fill it exactly. If you could put a stopwatch on it, use `から～まで`.

---

## ～から～にかけて · Examples

<ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>**にかけて**、<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>くなるでしょう。<br>*The rain will get heavier from evening into the night.*

<ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>四月<rt>しがつ</rt></ruby>**にかけて**、<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しの<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>りにくくなる。<br>*Removal firms get hard to book anywhere from March through April.*

<ruby>関東<rt>かんとう</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>東北<rt>とうほく</rt></ruby>**にかけて**、<ruby>大雪<rt>おおゆき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*Heavy snow is falling across the Kanto and Tohoku regions.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>**にかけて**、かぜが<ruby>流行<rt>はや</rt></ruby>った。<br>*Colds went round from last winter into this spring.*

---

## ～から～にかけて · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>北口<rt>きたぐち</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>**にかけて**、<ruby>桜<rt>さくら</rt></ruby>の<ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>が<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>いている。<br>*Cherry trees run all the way from the station's north exit to the park.*

<ruby>昭和<rt>しょうわ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わり**から**<ruby>平成<rt>へいせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>め**にかけて**、この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人口<rt>じんこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きく<ruby>減<rt>へ</rt></ruby>った。<br>*This town's population fell sharply from the end of the Showa era into the early Heisei years.*

<ruby>今週<rt>こんしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>水曜日<rt>すいようび</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>**にかけて**、<ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>のため<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まる<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*There will be periods without water from Wednesday through Friday this week owing to building work.*

<ruby>明<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け<ruby>方<rt>がた</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>昼前<rt>ひるまえ</rt></ruby>**にかけて**の<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>く、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby>か<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まった。<br>*The wind between dawn and late morning was strong enough to stop the trains several times.*

---

## ～から～にかけて · Nuance & register

The register is neutral to slightly formal. You will hear it every evening on the weather forecast, read it in newspapers and see it in official notices, and it is perfectly natural in careful conversation. It is not a stiff pattern; it is a **precise-sounding** one, which is why announcements like it.

What it will not tolerate is a sharp edge. `から～まで` says "these are the boundaries"; `から～にかけて` says "roughly this stretch". That is why clock times are wrong with it and vaguer time words - <ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby>、<ruby>夜中<rt>よなか</rt></ruby>、 <ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby> - are right. For the same reason the main clause usually describes something **spread out or repeated**: rain falling, prices rising, trees continuing, colds going round. A single completed action inside the band does not fit.

---

## ～から～にかけて · Nuance & register (cont.)

The `の` form is worth practising, because it is how the pattern gets in front of a noun: <ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>秋<rt>あき</rt></ruby>にかけての<ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>、 <ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby>から<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>にかけての<ruby>混雑<rt>こんざつ</rt></ruby>. Note that no `な` is possible.

> **Note** - the same `にかけて` turns up in a completely unrelated sense, "when it comes to", where it normally carries `は` and praises somebody's ability: <ruby>計算<rt>けいさん</rt></ruby>にかけては<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けない。 The giveaway is the missing `から`: no `から`, no span.

---

## ～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～から～まで

> **Rule of thumb** - `まで` gives a **boundary**; `にかけて` gives a **band**. `まで` can be measured and can end an action; `にかけて` is vague at both ends and describes what is going on across the middle.

<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*The bank is open from nine until three.*

Those are exact, and the sentence would be nonsense with `にかけて`. Now something whose edges nobody can pin down:

<ruby>夜中<rt>よなか</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>**にかけて**、<ruby>気温<rt>きおん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がり<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けた。<br>*The temperature kept falling from the small hours through to morning.*

---

## ～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～から～まで (cont.)

There is a second, quieter difference. `まで` takes an endpoint that the action reaches and stops at - <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く. `にかけて` never means "as far as and then stop"; it means "and everywhere in between", which is why <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>から<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>にかけて describes a region, not a journey.

---

## ～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～の間に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code> puts a **single event** somewhere inside a period. `にかけて` says the event **occupies** the period.

<ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>みの<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>運転免許<rt>うんてんめんきょ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*I got my driving licence during the summer holidays.*

One licence, obtained at one point inside the holiday. `にかけて` would be wrong, because you cannot spread a single acquisition across the whole span. The pattern wants something continuous or repeated:

<ruby>七月<rt>しちがつ</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>九月<rt>くがつ</rt></ruby>**にかけて**、<ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つも<ruby>上陸<rt>じょうりく</rt></ruby>した。<br>*Three separate typhoons made landfall between July and September.*

---

## ～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～の間に (cont.)

Three events distributed across the band is exactly right; one event at a point inside it is <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code>.

---

## ～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～にわたって

> **Rule of thumb** - `にわたって` emphasises **how much** was covered - the scale of the span. `にかけて` emphasises **where** the span runs, from about here to about there.

<ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>にわたって<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*The work went on for three whole years.*

That names a duration, not two endpoints, and it sounds impressed by the length. Compare:

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>秋<rt>あき</rt></ruby>**から**<ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>**にかけて**、<ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*The work went on from last autumn through to this spring.*

---

## ～から～にかけて · ～から～にかけて vs ～にわたって (cont.)

`にわたって` is also the more formal of the two and is common in written reports. When a sentence gives you both a start and an end, `にかけて` is usually the natural choice; when it gives you a total, reach for `にわたって`.

---

## ～から～にかけて · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>にかけて<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。 → ✅ <ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*Clock times are exact boundaries, and this pattern needs vague ones. Working hours take `まで`.*

- ❌ <ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>にかけて<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>りました。 → ✅ <ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby>から<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>にかけて<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*The `から` half is not optional in this sense. Without it, `にかけて` is read as the unrelated "when it comes to" pattern, or simply fails.*

- ❌ <ruby>七月<rt>しちがつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>八月<rt>はちがつ</rt></ruby>にかけて<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>しました。 → ✅ <ruby>七月<rt>しちがつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>八月<rt>はちがつ</rt></ruby>にかけて<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>回<rt>まわ</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*A single trip taken at some point in the band needs <code>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code>. If the travelling itself filled the two months, a verb like <code><ruby>回<rt>まわ</rt></ruby>る</code> makes that continuous sense explicit.*

---

## ～から～にかけて · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>から<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>にかけてな<ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりやすい。 → ✅ <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>から<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>にかけての<ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりやすい。<br>*Before a noun the linker is `の`. There is no な form.*

# ～にかけて

**Meaning** · when it comes to X - narrow the field to this one thing, and the claim that follows is absolute

Used on its own, `にかけて` - nearly always with `は` - picks out a single area of skill or quality and then makes a strong claim inside it: nobody better, no competition, complete confidence. The same string also closes the paired span pattern `から～にかけて`, and telling the two apart is half of what this page is for.

---

## ～にかけて · Formation

`にかけて` follows a **noun**. A verb has to be turned into a noun first, normally with `こと`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (a field or skill) | Noun + にかけては | <ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby> → <ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>にかけては |
| Verb, nominalised | dictionary + こと + にかけては | <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ることにかけては |
| Fixed superlative frame | ～にかけては…の<ruby>右<rt>みぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>る<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>はいない | <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>の<ruby>右<rt>みぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>る<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>はいない |
| Swearing on something | Noun + にかけて + <ruby>誓<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>う／<ruby>保証<rt>ほしょう</rt></ruby>する | <ruby>名誉<rt>めいよ</rt></ruby>にかけて<ruby>誓<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>う |
| Span (needs から) | Noun + から + Noun + にかけて | <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>にかけて |
| ❌ Verb without こと | ❌<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐにかけては | ✅<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐことにかけては |
| ❌ Span without から | ❌<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>にかけて<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>った | ✅<ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby>から<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>にかけて<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>った |

---

## ～にかけて · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - look for `から`. If there is a `から` earlier in the sentence, `にかけて` is closing a span. If there is a `は` attached to it and no `から`, it means "when it comes to".

---

## ～にかけて · Examples

<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ること**にかけては**、クラスで<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*When it comes to running, he's the best in the class.*

<ruby>記憶力<rt>きおくりょく</rt></ruby>**にかけては**、<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けない。<br>*When it comes to memory, I'm second to none.*

あの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>さ**にかけては**<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>で<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*For sheer cheapness, that shop beats anything in town.*

<ruby>魚<rt>さかな</rt></ruby>をさばくこと**にかけては**、<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>の<ruby>右<rt>みぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>る<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>はいない。<br>*Nobody can touch my father when it comes to filleting fish.*

---

## ～にかけて · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>技術<rt>ぎじゅつ</rt></ruby>**にかけては**<ruby>自信<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>がありますが、<ruby>営業<rt>えいぎょう</rt></ruby>はまったく<ruby>苦手<rt>にがて</rt></ruby>です。<br>*I'm confident about the technical side, but I'm hopeless at sales.*

<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>数字<rt>すうじ</rt></ruby>**にかけては**<ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>く、<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>即座<rt>そくざ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>答<rt>こた</rt></ruby>えられる。<br>*She is genuinely formidable with numbers and can answer on the spot even in meetings.*

この<ruby>品質<rt>ひんしつ</rt></ruby>については、<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>名<rt>な</rt></ruby>**にかけて**<ruby>保証<rt>ほしょう</rt></ruby>いたします。<br>*We guarantee this quality on the company's name.*

<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>すぎから<ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby>**にかけて**、<ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>い<ruby>列<rt>れつ</rt></ruby>ができていた。<br>*From early afternoon into the evening there was a long queue outside the venue.*

---

## ～にかけて · Nuance & register

The "when it comes to" use is emphatic by nature. It exists to set up a **superlative**, so the main clause almost always contains something like <ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby>、<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けない、<ruby>右<rt>みぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>る<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>はいない or <ruby>自信<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>がある. A flat, neutral statement after `にかけては` sounds unfinished.

It is normally **praise**, and praising yourself with it is fine in Japanese in a way that a bare boast would not be, because the pattern explicitly limits the claim to one narrow area - the implication is "only here". That is why <ruby>技術<rt>ぎじゅつ</rt></ruby>にかけては<ruby>自信<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>があります works well in a job interview. Speakers do turn it ironic - <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れることにかけては<ruby>天才<rt>てんさい</rt></ruby>だ, "a genius at forgetting things" - and the joke works precisely because the frame is built for compliments.

---

## ～にかけて · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral, leaning a little literary; you will meet it in profiles, introductions and reviews more than in idle chat. The `に` is fixed and the `は` is close to obligatory in this sense - `にかけて` without `は` is usually either the span pattern or the "swearing on" one.

> **Note** - the oath use is a small, fixed set: <ruby>命<rt>いのち</rt></ruby>にかけて、<ruby>名誉<rt>めいよ</rt></ruby>にかけて、<ruby>社<rt>しゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>信用<rt>しんよう</rt></ruby>にかけて. It is dramatic and formal, at home in speeches, courtroom scenes and corporate apologies, and it always attaches to a verb of promising or guaranteeing.

---

## ～にかけて · ～にかけては vs ～について・～に関して

> **Rule of thumb** - `について` introduces a **subject matter** and is neutral about what comes next. `にかけては` introduces an **arena of ability** and commits you to a claim of superiority inside it.

<ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>について、<ruby>専門家<rt>せんもんか</rt></ruby>が<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*An expert gave an explanation about earthquakes.*

Nothing there is being ranked, and `にかけては` would be nonsense. Compare a sentence built to rank:

<ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>研究<rt>けんきゅう</rt></ruby>**にかけては**、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>国内<rt>こくない</rt></ruby>で<ruby>屈指<rt>くっし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>存在<rt>そんざい</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*When it comes to earthquake research, he is one of the leading figures in the country.*

---

## ～にかけて · ～にかけては vs ～について・～に関して (cont.)

A quick test: if you could continue with <code><ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>します</code> or <code><ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>します</code>, use `について`. If you want to continue with "he's the best", use `にかけては`.

---

## ～にかけて · ～にかけては vs ～は・～では

> **Rule of thumb** - plain `は` or `では` also narrows the frame, but softly. `にかけては` narrows it hard and signals that a strong claim is coming.

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>得意<rt>とくい</rt></ruby>です。<br>*I'm good at kanji.*

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>**にかけては**、クラスの<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けません。<br>*When it comes to kanji, I won't lose to anyone in the class.*

---

## ～にかけて · ～にかけては vs ～は・～では (cont.)

Both restrict the topic; only the second promises a superlative. The consequence is practical: `にかけては` cannot be followed by a modest or a negative evaluation. ❌<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>にかけては<ruby>苦手<rt>にがて</rt></ruby>です is wrong, and the fix is simply <ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>苦手<rt>にがて</rt></ruby>です. If what you want to say is "in this area I'm weak", the plain topic particle is the whole answer.

---

## ～にかけて · ～にかけて（span）vs ～にかけては（ability）

> **Rule of thumb** - the span sense needs a **partner** `から` and two nouns of time or place. The ability sense needs **one** noun and `は`. They never overlap in a real sentence.

<ruby>水曜日<rt>すいようび</rt></ruby>から<ruby>木曜日<rt>もくようび</rt></ruby>**にかけて**、<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>くそうだ。<br>*They say the rain will continue from Wednesday into Thursday.*

<ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>予想<rt>よそう</rt></ruby>**にかけては**、<ruby>祖父<rt>そふ</rt></ruby>はテレビの<ruby>予報<rt>よほう</rt></ruby>より<ruby>当<rt>あ</rt></ruby>たる。<br>*When it comes to predicting the weather, my grandfather beats the television.*

Two nouns and a `から` in the first; one noun and a `は` in the second. If you ever meet a bare `にかけて` with neither marker, it is almost certainly the oath use, and the verb at the end of the sentence will tell you so.

---

## ～にかけて · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐにかけては、<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby>だ。 → ✅ <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐことにかけては、<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*`にかけて` follows a noun. A verb needs `こと` to become one.*

- ❌ <ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>にかけては<ruby>下手<rt>へた</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ <ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>は<ruby>下手<rt>へた</rt></ruby>です。<br>*The pattern sets up a boast. Following it with an admission of weakness contradicts the frame it just built.*

- ❌ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>歴史<rt>れきし</rt></ruby>にかけて<ruby>研究<rt>けんきゅう</rt></ruby>しています。 → ✅ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>歴史<rt>れきし</rt></ruby>について<ruby>研究<rt>けんきゅう</rt></ruby>しています。<br>*"Researching about X" is subject matter, not ranked ability, so it needs `について`.*

- ❌ <ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>にかけて<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しました。 → ✅ <ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby>から<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>にかけて<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*The span sense is a two-part pattern. Without `から` there is no span, and the sentence reads as a broken version of the ability use.*

# ～ぶりに

**Meaning** · for the first time in - that much time has passed since it last happened, and now it has happened again

`ぶりに` measures a **gap**. You attach it to a length of time, and the sentence says two things at once: the last time was that long ago, and it has just happened again. Both halves matter - if it has not happened again, `ぶりに` is the wrong word.

---

## ～ぶりに · Formation

`ぶり` is a suffix. It attaches directly to a quantity of time, with no particle in between, and takes `に` when it modifies a verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Length of time + verb | Quantity + ぶりに | <ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>ぶりに<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う |
| As a predicate | Quantity + ぶりだ／です | <ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby>ぶりです |
| Before a noun | Quantity + ぶりの + Noun | <ruby>五年<rt>ごねん</rt></ruby>ぶりの<ruby>優勝<rt>ゆうしょう</rt></ruby> |
| Vague length | <ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>し + ぶりに | <ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>しぶりに<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>した |
| As a greeting | お<ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>しぶりです | - |
| ❌ な before a noun | ❌<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>ぶりな<ruby>再会<rt>さいかい</rt></ruby> | ✅<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>ぶりの<ruby>再会<rt>さいかい</rt></ruby> |
| ❌ Event noun | ❌<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>ぶりに | ✅<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby><ruby>以来<rt>いらい</rt></ruby> |

---

## ～ぶりに · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - what goes in front of `ぶり` is a **stretch of time**, never an event. <ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>ぶり and <ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>しぶり are fine; ❌<ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>ぶり is not. For an event, use <code>～<ruby>以来<rt>いらい</rt></ruby></code>.

---

## ～ぶりに · Examples

<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>**ぶりに**<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I went home for the first time in three years.*

<ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>し**ぶりに**<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I went to the cinema for the first time in ages.*

<ruby>一週間<rt>いっしゅうかん</rt></ruby>**ぶりに**<ruby>晴<rt>は</rt></ruby>れましたね。<br>*It's the first clear day in a week, isn't it?*

<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby>**ぶりに**<ruby>中学<rt>ちゅうがく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちから<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た。<br>*A friend from junior high got in touch after ten years.*

---

## ～ぶりに · Examples (cont.)

うちのチームは<ruby>五年<rt>ごねん</rt></ruby>**ぶりの**<ruby>優勝<rt>ゆうしょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>果<rt>は</rt></ruby>たした。<br>*Our team took the title for the first time in five years.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三十五度<rt>さんじゅうごど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>超<rt>こ</rt></ruby>えて、<ruby>八年<rt>はちねん</rt></ruby>**ぶりの**<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>さだそうだ。<br>*It's over thirty-five degrees today - apparently the hottest in eight years.*

<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しくて、<ruby>半年<rt>はんとし</rt></ruby>**ぶりに**<ruby>美容院<rt>びよういん</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったら、<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わっていた。<br>*Work kept me away, and when I finally got to the hairdresser after six months my usual stylist had gone.*

<ruby>退院<rt>たいいん</rt></ruby>して、<ruby>二<rt>に</rt></ruby>か<ruby>月<rt>げつ</rt></ruby>**ぶりに**<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>のベッドで<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>ることができた。<br>*I came out of hospital and slept in my own bed for the first time in two months.*

---

## ～ぶりに · Nuance & register

`ぶりに` is register-neutral and extremely common in ordinary speech. It carries a small lift of feeling - pleasure, relief, or simply the sense that a long stretch has been broken. Sports reporting loves it (<ruby>三十年<rt>さんじゅうねん</rt></ruby>ぶりの<ruby>優勝<rt>ゆうしょう</rt></ruby>), weather reports love it (<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby>ぶりの<ruby>大雪<rt>おおゆき</rt></ruby>), and so does anyone greeting an old friend.

The greeting forms are worth learning as fixed items. <ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>しぶり! is casual, お<ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>しぶりです is the everyday polite version, and in business writing the formal equivalent is ご<ruby>無沙汰<rt>ぶさた</rt></ruby>しております, which does not use `ぶり` at all. Note the shape: the greeting has **no** `に`, because it is a predicate - お<ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>しぶりです, not ❌お<ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>しぶりにです. The `に` appears only when `ぶり` is modifying a verb.

---

## ～ぶりに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Speakers happily stretch the pattern for comic effect over absurdly short intervals - <ruby>五分<rt>ごふん</rt></ruby>ぶりだね, "long time no see (five minutes)". That is a joke, and it works because the pattern normally implies a genuinely long gap.

> **Note** - a different `ぶり` means "the manner of doing something": <ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>ぶり, <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しぶり, <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みっぷり. It attaches to a verb stem or an activity noun rather than to a length of time, and it never takes the "for the first time in" reading. <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>ぶりには<ruby>感心<rt>かんしん</rt></ruby>した is about *how* he works, not about how long it had been.

---

## ～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs ～後に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>に</code> counts **forward** from a moment already in the story. `ぶりに` counts **backward** to the last time the same thing happened.

<ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>った。<br>*Three years after graduating, he started a company.*

The three years run from graduation to the founding - two different events. Now the same number doing the other job:

<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>**ぶりに**<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わせた。<br>*We met up at the same place for the first time in three years.*

---

## ～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs ～後に (cont.)

Here the three years run from the *previous* meeting to this one - the same event twice. That is the whole distinction, and it is why <ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った and <ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>ぶりに<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った describe quite different situations. Note the reading too: in this counting sense, <code><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby></code> is read ご, not あと.

---

## ～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs 久しぶりに

> **Rule of thumb** - same pattern, different precision. <code><ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>しぶりに</code> is the version for when you do not want to name a number, and it is far more frequent in conversation.

<ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>し**ぶりに**<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>の<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いて、ほっとした。<br>*Hearing my mother's voice after so long was a relief.*

<ruby>四年<rt>よねん</rt></ruby>**ぶりに**<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>い、<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っていたより<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>そうで<ruby>安心<rt>あんしん</rt></ruby>した。<br>*I saw my mother for the first time in four years, and she looked better than I'd expected.*

---

## ～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs 久しぶりに (cont.)

Naming the number makes the sentence a small piece of news, which is why headlines and announcements prefer it. Leaving it vague makes the sentence about the feeling. The one place they are not interchangeable is the greeting: only <ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>しぶり works there, since ❌<ruby>四年<rt>よねん</rt></ruby>ぶりです! is a statement of fact rather than a hello.

---

## ～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs ～以来

> **Rule of thumb** - `ぶり` takes a **length of time**; <code><ruby>以来<rt>いらい</rt></ruby></code> takes an **event or a point in time**, and it does not require the thing to have happened again.

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby><ruby>以来<rt>いらい</rt></ruby>、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>とは<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>っていない。<br>*I haven't seen him since last summer.*

Notice the negative: nothing has happened again, and `ぶり` could not be used at all. Compare a sentence whose whole point is that the streak has just ended:

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby><ruby>以来<rt>いらい</rt></ruby>、<ruby>半年<rt>はんとし</rt></ruby>**ぶりに**<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>と<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I saw him for the first time in six months, the first time since last summer.*

---

## ～ぶりに · ～ぶりに vs ～以来 (cont.)

The two happily sit in one sentence, each doing its own job: <code><ruby>以来<rt>いらい</rt></ruby></code> names the starting event, `ぶりに` gives the length of the gap. This also explains the commonest error with `ぶり` - attaching it to an event, as in ❌<ruby>入学<rt>にゅうがく</rt></ruby>ぶりに. Swap in <code><ruby>入学<rt>にゅうがく</rt></ruby><ruby>以来<rt>いらい</rt></ruby></code> and the sentence works.

---

## ～ぶりに · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>ぶりに<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いました。 → ✅ <ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby><ruby>以来<rt>いらい</rt></ruby>、<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*`ぶり` needs a length of time in front of it. Graduation is an event, which takes <code><ruby>以来<rt>いらい</rt></ruby></code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby>ぶりな<ruby>再会<rt>さいかい</rt></ruby>でした。 → ✅ <ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby>ぶりの<ruby>再会<rt>さいかい</rt></ruby>でした。<br>*Before a noun the linker is `の`. `ぶり` behaves like a noun, not a な-adjective.*

- ❌ お<ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>しぶりにですね。 → ✅ お<ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>しぶりですね。<br>*The `に` belongs only when `ぶり` modifies a verb. As a greeting it is a predicate and stands alone.*

---

## ～ぶりに · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>二年<rt>にねん</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>に<ruby>温泉<rt>おんせん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った。（intending "went to a hot spring for the first time in two years"） → ✅ <ruby>二年<rt>にねん</rt></ruby>ぶりに<ruby>温泉<rt>おんせん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った。<br>*<code><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>に</code> would mean two years after some other event. To measure back to the previous visit, you need `ぶりに`.*

## ～ぶりに · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>五年<rt>ごねん</rt></ruby>**ぶりに**ピアノを<ruby>弾<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>いた。 (b) <ruby>五年<rt>ごねん</rt></ruby><ruby>間<rt>かん</rt></ruby>、ピアノを<ruby>弾<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>いていなかった。

{reveal}
(a) puts the emphasis on the moment the gap ended: the playing has just happened, and the five years are there to tell you how remarkable that is. There is usually a flicker of feeling attached.

{reveal}
(b) describes the gap itself and says nothing about whether it has ended. It is the sentence you use to explain why your fingers are stiff, not to announce that you have started again.
