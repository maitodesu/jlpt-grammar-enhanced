# N3 - While it's happening

- ～うちに
- ～ないうちに
- 間 / 間に - while, during
- 間 / 間に - between
- ～中
- ～最中に
- ～途中に / ～途中で

---

# ～うちに

**Meaning** · while a state still holds - and with the sense that it will not hold much longer

`～うちに` opens a window and tells you to act inside it. The window is a state that is currently true - it's hot, you're young, the baby is asleep - and the pattern quietly carries the reason for the hurry: **that state is going to end.** A second, softer use drops the urgency and reports a change that crept up on you while something was going on: <code><ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いているうちに…</code>, "as I listened…".

---

## ～うちに · Formation

`うちに` is the noun `うち` ("interior, span") plus `に`, so everything in front of it is in the shape that modifies a noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ている) | ている + うちに | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ → <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っているうちに |
| Verb (stative, dictionary) | Plain non-past + うちに | いる → <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>にいるうちに |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + うちに | <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れる → <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れないうちに |
| い-adjective | Plain form + うちに | <ruby>熱<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>熱<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いうちに |
| な-adjective | な + うちに | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>なうちに |
| Noun | の + うちに | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>のうちに |

---

## ～うちに · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the subject inside the `うちに` clause takes `が`, never `は`: <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもが<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ているうちに. This is the same embedding rule you met with たら and とき clauses at N4.

> **Note** - a plain dictionary-form verb before `うちに` is limited to verbs that describe a *lasting situation* - いる, <ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>む, <ruby>勤<rt>つと</rt></ruby>める. For an ordinary action verb you need ている: <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいるうちに, not <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>むうちに.

---

## ～うちに · Examples

スープが**<ruby>熱<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いうちに**どうぞ。<br>*Have the soup while it's hot.*

**<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るいうちに**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ろう。<br>*Let's head back while it's still light.*

**<ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>いうちに**、いろいろな<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ておきたい。<br>*I want to see plenty of countries while I'm young.*

**<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>のうちに**<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>の<ruby>免許<rt>めんきょ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>っておいた。<br>*I made sure to get my driving licence while I was still a student.*

---

## ～うちに · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>両親<rt>りょうしん</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>なうちに**、<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>温泉<rt>おんせん</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きたい。<br>*I'd like to go to a hot spring with my parents once, while they're still in good health.*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもが<ruby>昼寝<rt>ひるね</rt></ruby>を**しているうちに**、たまった<ruby>洗濯物<rt>せんたくもの</rt></ruby>をたたんだ。<br>*While the kids were napping I folded the laundry that had piled up.*

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いているうちに**、だんだん<ruby>意味<rt>いみ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かってきた。<br>*As I listened to the teacher it gradually began to make sense.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>まったことは、**<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えているうちに**メモしておかないと、<ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby>には<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れてしまいますよ。<br>*If you don't jot down what was decided in the meeting while you still remember it, by evening you'll have lost the lot.*

---

## ～うちに · Nuance & register

`うちに` is register-neutral. It takes ます or plain endings without changing character, and it turns up in signage, in advice from your grandmother and in company email alike.

What is *not* neutral is the shape of the main clause. Because the pattern exists to say "act before the window shuts", it pulls strongly toward volitional and advisory endings - よう, たい, ておく, ておこう, てください, たほうがいい, ないと. A flat past statement with no connection to the window closing sounds off, and that is usually the signal that you wanted <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code> instead.

---

## ～うちに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The `～ているうちに` use is the exception, and it is worth learning as its own thing. There the main clause is a **change that happened to you**, typically unnoticed and typically expressed with てくる, てしまう or a なる verb: <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いているうちに<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>迷<rt>まよ</rt></ruby>ってしまった. Nobody is being advised to hurry; the sentence is reporting drift.

Two fixed expressions to recognise. `そのうちに` (often just そのうち) is an adverb meaning "before long, one of these days" - そのうち<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>びに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます is a polite, pleasantly vague "I'll drop by sometime." And <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないうちに</code> means "without my noticing", not "while I didn't know" in any literal sense.

---

## ～うちに · Nuance & register (cont.)

There is a mild literary flavour to <code><ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>いうちに</code> and <code><ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>なうちに</code>, which show up in proverbs and in the kind of advice older speakers give. Nothing about them is stiff, but they carry a whiff of life-lesson, which is exactly why they are used.

---

## ～うちに · ～うちに vs ～間に

> **Rule of thumb** - both put an event inside a window, but <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code> only says *when*, while `うちに` adds *before it's too late*. If the window closing does not matter, use <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code>.

<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>が<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>っている**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>宅配便<rt>たくはいびん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>いた。<br>*A delivery arrived while my mother was out shopping.*

Nothing here is racing the clock; the shopping trip is just a stretch of time that happened to contain the delivery. Swap in `うちに` and the sentence starts to imply the courier deliberately used her absence, which is not what happened.

<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>が<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>っている**うちに**、ケーキを<ruby>隠<rt>かく</rt></ruby>しておこう。<br>*Let's hide the cake while Mum's out shopping.*

---

## ～うちに · ～うちに vs ～間に (cont.)

Now it fits: she is coming back, and that is the whole point.

The mirror-image restriction is also useful. `うちに` resists windows defined by the clock or the calendar, because a clock window has no "state" to run out: ❌<code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>のうちに</code> → ✅<code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code>.

---

## ～うちに · ～うちに vs ～前に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> orders two events; `うちに` judges an opportunity. <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> is neutral, and it looks forward at the event; `うちに` looks around at the situation you are still standing in.

<ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>建<rt>た</rt></ruby>てるつもりだ。<br>*I intend to build a house before I get old.*

**<ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>いうちに**、<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>建<rt>た</rt></ruby>てるつもりだ。<br>*I intend to build a house while I'm still young.*

---

## ～うちに · ～うちに vs ～前に (cont.)

The first is a plain schedule: house, then old age. The second says youth is what makes it possible, and that the possibility is draining away. Note also that <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> takes a dictionary-form verb naming the *event*, while `うちに` takes the *state* that precedes it - which is why the two versions above look nothing alike.

---

## ～うちに · ～のうちに vs ～中に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> is a deadline; <code><ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>のうちに</code> is an opportunity. Same span, different argument.

この<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Please submit these documents by the end of today.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいから、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>**のうちに**<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しておこう。<br>*Tomorrow's going to be busy, so let's get it submitted while we still have today.*

---

## ～うちに · ～のうちに vs ～中に (cont.)

<code><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> states a limit somebody has imposed. `のうちに` gives a reason, and the reason is always some version of "afterwards will be worse". <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> also attaches only to nouns naming a measured span; you cannot say ❌<code><ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> for <code><ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>なうちに</code>.

---

## ～うちに · Common mistakes

- ❌ **<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>うちに**<ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>したい。 → ✅ **<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>のうちに**<ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>したい。<br>*`うち` is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs `の`. A な-adjective likewise needs な: ❌<code><ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>のうちに</code> → ✅<code><ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>なうちに</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>十時<rt>じゅうじ</rt></ruby>**のうちに**<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>します。 → ✅ <ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>十時<rt>じゅうじ</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>します。<br>*A window fixed by the clock has no state to lose, so `うちに` has nothing to work with. Use <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>むうちに**<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>くなった。 → ✅ <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいるうちに**<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>くなった。<br>*An action verb has to be in ている. The dictionary form is reserved for verbs that already describe a standing situation, like いる or <ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>む.*

---

## ～うちに · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ているうちに<ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>があった。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**が**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ているうちに<ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>があった。<br>*The subject of a subordinate clause takes `が`. `は` cannot be trapped inside a clause that only modifies `うち`.*

---

## ～うちに · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>ちゃんが<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ている____、<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いで<ruby>晩<rt>ばん</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ってしまおう。

{reveal}
<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>ちゃんが<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ている**うちに**、<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いで<ruby>晩<rt>ばん</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ってしまおう。

{reveal}
The baby is going to wake up, and that is exactly why the cooking has to happen now - the window is closing, which is `うちに`'s job. <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code> would also be grammatical but would drop the urgency that いそいで and てしまおう are already signalling.

---

## ～うちに · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一時<rt>いちじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>までです。その（うちに・<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に）<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>トイレに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってもいいです。

{reveal}
その**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>トイレに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってもいいです。

{reveal}
The window is defined by two clock times, not by a state that is about to expire, so `うちに` has no opportunity to point at. This is the plain "at some point inside the span" meaning, which belongs to <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code>.

---

## ～うちに · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "While I was living in Osaka, I got used to the local accent."

{reveal}
<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいる**うちに**、<ruby>関西弁<rt>かんさいべん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れた。

{reveal}
This is the drift use: nobody decided to get used to the accent, it simply happened over the course of the stay. The main clause is a change of state, which is the signature of `～ているうちに`. <ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>む also allows the dictionary form here, since it names a lasting situation.

---

## ～うちに · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>いのうちに<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>下<rt>お</rt></ruby>りたほうがいい。

{reveal}
✅ **<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るいうちに**<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>下<rt>お</rt></ruby>りたほうがいい。

{reveal}
Two problems. An い-adjective attaches directly, with no `の` - <code><ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>いうちに</code>, not ❌<code><ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>いのうちに</code>. And the meaning was inverted: <code><ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>いうちに</code> would mean "while it is still dark", i.e. set off before dawn. The state you want to use up is the daylight.

---

## ～うちに · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>にいる**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>富士山<rt>ふじさん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>りました。 (b) <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>にいる**うちに**、<ruby>富士山<rt>ふじさん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>りました。

{reveal}
(a) is a neutral report: the climb took place somewhere inside the period of the stay. It answers "when did you climb it?" and says nothing about why then.

{reveal}
(b) adds a motive. It frames the stay as a limited chance that was running out, so the climb reads as something done deliberately before the opportunity disappeared - closer to "I made sure to climb Fuji while I was still in Japan." With a plain past main clause (b) also sounds slightly more natural if you keep a marker of intent, such as <ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>っておきました.

# ～ないうちに

**Meaning** · before something happens - said from inside the time when it still hasn't

English says "before it rains." Japanese takes the opposite angle and says "while it is not-yet raining": <code><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>らないうちに</code>. There is no natural English shape for that, which is why the pattern feels strange for a long time and then suddenly feels obvious. The thing in the ない clause is nearly always something you would rather avoid, and the main clause is what you do to get ahead of it.

---

## ～ないうちに · Formation

Take the plain negative and put `うちに` straight after it. The negative does the work; nothing else changes.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ない-form + うちに | <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>らないうちに |
| Verb (る-verb) | ない-form + うちに | <ruby>冷<rt>さ</rt></ruby>める → <ruby>冷<rt>さ</rt></ruby>めないうちに |
| Verb (する) | しないうちに | <ruby>変化<rt>へんか</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>変化<rt>へんか</rt></ruby>しないうちに |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないうちに | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないうちに |
| い-adjective (via なる) | ～く + ならないうちに | <ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>くならないうちに |
| な-adjective / Noun (via なる) | ～に + ならないうちに | <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby> → <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>にならないうちに |

---

## ～ないうちに · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - う-verb / る-verb are the labels used throughout this book; you will also see them as <ruby>五段<rt>ごだん</rt></ruby>/Group 1 and <ruby>一段<rt>いちだん</rt></ruby>/Group 2. The irregular one to watch is <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る</code>, whose negative stem is **こ**: <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないうちに, never ❌くないうちに.

> **Note** - adjectives almost never attach directly. ❌<code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くないうちに</code> is not how this is said; route it through ならない - <code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くならないうちに</code>. The negative has to name an **event that has not started**, and adjectives name states, so なる supplies the missing event.

---

## ～ないうちに · Examples

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>らないうちに**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ろう。<br>*Let's go home before it rains.*

**<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れないうちに**メモしておきます。<br>*I'll write it down before I forget.*

**<ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>くならないうちに**<ruby>洗濯物<rt>せんたくもの</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>り<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んだ。<br>*I brought the washing in before it got dark.*

コーヒーが**<ruby>冷<rt>さ</rt></ruby>めないうちに**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでしまってください。<br>*Do drink your coffee before it goes cold.*

---

## ～ないうちに · Examples (cont.)

**<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないうちに**<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>がなくなっていた。<br>*My wallet had vanished without my noticing.*

<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えないうちに**<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>っておこう。<br>*Let's grab seats before the place fills up.*

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>戻<rt>もど</rt></ruby>ってこないうちに**、<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>だけでも<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けておいたほうがいいよ。<br>*You'd better at least clear your desk before the manager gets back.*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもは、こちらが**<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>づかないうちに**<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>びていて、<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>がもう<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>らない。<br>*Children grow before you notice, and the clothes we bought last year already don't fit.*

---

## ～ないうちに · Nuance & register

Register-neutral, and common in both speech and writing. What is not free is the shape of the main clause: it is almost always something the speaker **chooses to do in response** - よう, ておく, てしまう, たほうがいい, てください, なさい, ないと. That is the whole reason the pattern exists.

The ない clause is normally a change for the worse: rain, cold food, darkness, crowds, your boss returning. This is a strong tendency rather than a hard rule, but if the event is welcome the sentence starts to sound odd, and you probably wanted <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code>.

Three expressions have hardened into set phrases, and they behave like adverbs rather than like clauses:

---

## ～ないうちに · Nuance & register (cont.)

- <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないうちに</code> - without one's knowledge
- <code><ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>づかないうちに</code> - without noticing
- <code><ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からないうちに</code> - before one has worked out what is going on

In these the main clause is not an action you took but a change you discovered afterwards, usually with ていた or てしまう: <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないうちに<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>過<rt>す</rt></ruby>ぎていた.

In casual speech the whole thing survives intact - this is not a pattern that gets contracted. What does happen is that the main clause loses its particles: 「<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby><ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>らないうちに<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ろ」.

---

## ～ないうちに · ～ないうちに vs ～前に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> puts two events in order and stops there. `ないうちに` says the later event is a **problem you are outrunning**.

<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かける**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めた。<br>*I shut the windows before going out.*

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>って**こないうちに**<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めた。<br>*I shut the windows before the rain could get in.*

---

## ～ないうちに · ～ないうちに vs ～前に (cont.)

The first is a checklist item. The second has a threat in it. Notice too that <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> takes a **dictionary form** and `ないうちに` takes a **negative**, even though the English translation is "before" both times - this is the single most reliable source of errors with the pattern.

One more practical difference: <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> is happy with a scheduled, neutral event (<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まる<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に), while `ないうちに` wants something that might land on you at any moment. You would not normally say ❌<code><ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まらないうちに</code> for a meeting that starts at ten sharp - unless you mean you are racing it.

---

## ～ないうちに · ～ないうちに vs the affirmative ～うちに

> **Rule of thumb** - the same situation can be framed from either side. Affirmative `うちに` names the **good state you still have**; `ないうちに` names the **bad state that hasn't arrived**.

**<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るいうちに**<ruby>洗濯物<rt>せんたくもの</rt></ruby>を<ruby>干<rt>ほ</rt></ruby>してしまおう。<br>*Let's get the washing hung out while it's still light.*

**<ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>くならないうちに**<ruby>洗濯物<rt>せんたくもの</rt></ruby>を<ruby>干<rt>ほ</rt></ruby>してしまおう。<br>*Let's get the washing hung out before it gets dark.*

---

## ～ないうちに · ～ないうちに vs the affirmative ～うちに (cont.)

These are interchangeable here, and either is natural. The choice matters when only one side of the pair has a name. There is a comfortable word for the good state in <ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るい, so both work; but for "before the shop closes" there is no adjective for "still open in the sense that matters", so you reach for <code><ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>まらないうちに</code>. Conversely, <ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>いうちに has no useful negative twin - ❌<ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>らないうちに is understandable but distinctly odd.

---

## ～ないうちに · ～ないうちに vs ～ない間に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>ない<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code> is a **stretch of non-happening** with a start and an end. `ないうちに` is the run-up **before the thing starts**.

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ていない**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>魚<rt>さかな</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてしまった。<br>*The cat ate the fish while I wasn't looking.*

<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>き**ないうちに**、<ruby>魚<rt>さかな</rt></ruby>をしまっておこう。<br>*Let's put the fish away before the cat wakes up.*

---

## ～ないうちに · ～ないうちに vs ～ない間に (cont.)

In the first, "not looking" is a period that has already begun and already ended - the cat operated inside it. In the second, "waking up" has not happened at all yet, and the sentence is about getting in first. A quick test: if you can put ずっと in front of the negative clause, it is <code>ない<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code>.

---

## ～ないうちに · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るないうちに**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ります。 → ✅ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>らないうちに**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*`うちに` attaches to the finished ない-form, not to the dictionary form plus ない. Build <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>らない first, then add うちに.*

- ❌ **<ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>いうちに**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りましょう。（meaning "before it gets dark"） → ✅ **<ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>くならないうちに**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りましょう。<br>*<code><ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>いうちに</code> is perfectly grammatical, but it means "while it is still dark" - the pre-dawn start, not the evening deadline. The two are opposites.*

- ❌ **<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れないうちで**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いておきます。 → ✅ **<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れないうちに**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いておきます。<br>*The particle is fixed at `に`. `うちで` is not a variant of this pattern.*

---

## ～ないうちに · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないうちに<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けよう。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**が**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないうちに<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けよう。<br>*The ない clause is embedded, so its subject takes `が`. And note the stem: <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る</code> goes to <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない, not ❌くない.*

Using the affirmative <ruby>冷<rt>さ</rt></ruby>めるうちに would be ungrammatical here - there is no state of "being in the middle of cooling" to sit inside.

{reveal}

## ～ないうちに · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>留守<rt>るす</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**、だれかが<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ったようだ。 (b) <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>って**こないうちに**、<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けておこう。

{reveal}

# 間 / 間に - while, during

**Meaning** · a window of time (<code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code>) - and `に` decides whether the main clause **fills** it or **sits inside** it

<code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> is a noun meaning "the space between". Used with time it marks off a stretch: while the meeting is on, while she was out, all through the summer. The one thing you have to get right is the little `に`, and it is not decoration:

- **<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>** - the main clause is true for the **whole** window, start to end.
- **<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に** - the main clause happens **at some point** inside the window.

---

## 間 / 間に - while, during · Formation

<code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> is a noun, so whatever comes before it is in noun-modifying shape. The `に` is then added or not according to the rule above.

---

| Verb (stative, dictionary) | Plain non-past + <ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>（に） | いる → <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>にいる<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + <ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>（に） | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ていない<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に |

## 間 / 間に - while, during · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the subject inside the <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> clause takes `が`, or `の` in more compact writing: <ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>が<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ている<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby> ≒ <ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>の<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ている<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>. `は` cannot appear there.

> **Note** - do not confuse this <ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby> with the counter <ruby>間<rt>かん</rt></ruby>: <ruby>一週間<rt>いっしゅうかん</rt></ruby>, <ruby>二時間<rt>にじかん</rt></ruby>, <ruby>三日間<rt>みっかかん</rt></ruby>. Those are lengths of time. The あいだ of this page is a *window* you locate something in. You can stack them: <ruby>三日間<rt>みっかかん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>, though <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby> is tidier.

## 間 / 間に - while, during · Examples

<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>が<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ている**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けた。<br>*I tidied the room while my mother was asleep.*

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>にいる**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きたい。<br>*I want to get to Hokkaido once while I'm in Japan.*

## 間 / 間に - while, during · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>信号<rt>しんごう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っている**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>はずっとスマホを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ていた。<br>*My brother stared at his phone the entire time we were waiting at the lights.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>外<rt>はず</rt></ruby>している**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>三回<rt>さんかい</rt></ruby>も<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>をくださったそうだ。<br>*Apparently the section chief rang three times while I was away from my desk.*

<ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>をしている**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**、この<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>れませんので、<ruby>回<rt>まわ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>をお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします。<br>*For as long as the roadworks are going on this street is closed to traffic, so please take a detour.*

## 間 / 間に - while, during · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> is at home in a train announcement, a contract and a text message alike, and it does not change shape for politeness.

The useful stylistic observations are elsewhere. First, in relaxed speech ている contracts as usual, so you hear <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってる<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に rather than <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っている<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に. Second, writing sometimes prefers `の` over `が` for the embedded subject (<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>の<ruby>留守<rt>るす</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に), which reads as slightly compact and formal.

---

## 間 / 間に - while, during · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two adverbial fossils are worth recognising. <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code> can appear with no clause at all as そのあいだに, "meanwhile, in the meantime". And <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>じゅう</code> - <ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>じゅう - is an emphatic version of the no-`に` reading: "the entire time, without a break". If you find yourself wanting to add ずっと, <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>じゅう</code> says it for you.

Finally, a piece of pronunciation. Written `間`, this word is あいだ here, かん in counters, ま in <ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う and <ruby>居間<rt>いま</rt></ruby>, and けん in some measurements. Kanji alone will not tell you which; the grammar around it will.

---

## 間 / 間に - while, during · 間 vs 間に

> **Rule of thumb** - ask what the main clause does with the window. If it **occupies** the whole window, drop the `に`. If it **lands somewhere inside** it, keep the `に`.

<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>ちゃんが<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ている**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいました。<br>*I read a book the whole time the baby was asleep.*

<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>ちゃんが<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ている**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>一冊<rt>いっさつ</rt></ruby><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでしまいました。<br>*I got through a whole book while the baby was asleep.*

Same nap, two different reports. In the first, reading and sleeping are the same length; in the second, the book was finished at some moment before the baby woke.

---

## 間 / 間に - while, during · 間 vs 間に (cont.)

That is why the two versions attract different main verbs. Without `に` you find **durative** predicates - ている, ずっと, adjectives, negatives of ongoing action:

<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>はずっとよかった。<br>*The weather was good for the whole trip.*

With `に` you find **bounded** ones - a completed action, a sudden event, てしまう, a counted quantity:

<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>をなくしてしまった。<br>*I lost my wallet at some point during the trip.*

---

## 間 / 間に - while, during · 間 vs 間に (cont.)

Swap those and both sentences break. ❌<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby><ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>をなくした would claim the losing went on for the length of the holiday.

> **Note** - one honest complication. When the main clause is durative but *shorter* than the window, `に` comes back: <ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>た. The test is not "is the verb punctual" but "does the main clause stretch across the whole window". Punctual verbs simply always fail that test.

---

## 間 / 間に - while, during · 間に vs うちに

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code> tells you **when**; `うちに` tells you **when, and hurry**. Only `うちに` implies the window is about to shut.

<ruby>冬休<rt>ふゆやす</rt></ruby>みの**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>歯<rt>は</rt></ruby>を<ruby>治<rt>なお</rt></ruby>した。<br>*I got my teeth seen to over the winter break.*

<ruby>冬休<rt>ふゆやす</rt></ruby>みの**うちに**<ruby>歯<rt>は</rt></ruby>を<ruby>治<rt>なお</rt></ruby>しておこう。<br>*Let's get my teeth seen to while we've still got the winter break.*

The second says the holiday is a chance that will not come again soon. `うちに` pairs naturally with ておく, よう and たほうがいい for that reason, while <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code> is content with a plain past.

---

## 間 / 間に - while, during · 間に vs うちに (cont.)

The division of labour is clean in one direction: a window defined by clock or calendar takes <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code> and refuses `うちに` - <ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>十時<rt>じゅうじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に, not ❌<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>十時<rt>じゅうじ</rt></ruby>のうちに. A window defined by a state that will end accepts both, with `うちに` adding the pressure.

---

## 間 / 間に - while, during · 間 vs ながら

> **Rule of thumb** - `ながら` requires **one person** doing both things at once. <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> does not care who the subjects are, and is the only option when they differ.

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>が<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>をしている**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>皿<rt>さら</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>っていた。<br>*I washed dishes the whole time my sister was cooking.*

Two people, so `ながら` is impossible: ❌<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>が<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>をしながら<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は… is simply ungrammatical. Where the subject *is* shared, both work, and the difference becomes one of framing: `ながら` welds the two actions into one activity, while <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> treats one as a container for the other.

---

## 間 / 間に - while, during · 間 vs ながら (cont.)

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>っている**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**、ずっと<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いていた。<br>*I listened to music the whole time I was on the train.*

Here <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> is better than <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>りながら, because riding a train is a *situation* you are in rather than an *action* you perform - and `ながら` wants two actions.

---

## 間 / 間に - while, during · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ている**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**、ずっといい<ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ていた。 → ✅ <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ている**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**、ずっといい<ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ていた。<br>*ずっと…ていた fills the whole window, so the `に` has to go. `に` would ask you to locate the dreaming at one moment inside the sleep.*

- ❌ <ruby>留守<rt>るす</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>泥棒<rt>どろぼう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>った。 → ✅ <ruby>留守<rt>るす</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>泥棒<rt>どろぼう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>った。<br>*A break-in is a single moment, not a state lasting all day. A bounded event inside the window needs `に`.*

- ❌ <ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>み**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**アルバイトをしました。 → ✅ <ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>み**の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**アルバイトをしました。<br>*<code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs `の`. Compare the counter <ruby>一週間<rt>いっしゅうかん</rt></ruby>, which is a single word and takes nothing.*

---

## 間 / 間に - while, during · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>している<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちに<ruby>預<rt>あず</rt></ruby>けた。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**が**<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>している<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちに<ruby>預<rt>あず</rt></ruby>けた。<br>*The subject of the embedded clause takes `が`. A topic `は` cannot live inside a clause that is only modifying <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code>.*

# 間 / 間に - between

**Meaning** · the gap between two things - physically, or between two parties, or across a group

Same word (<code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code>), same core idea of "the space in the middle", but here the two edges are **things** rather than the start and end of a stretch of time. Between the station and the bank. Between the two of them. Popular among students. The grammar to get right is which particle follows: `に` for something *located* in the gap, `で` for something *happening* across a group, `の` when you are modifying a noun.

---

## 間 / 間に - between · Formation

<code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> is a noun. Two nouns joined by `と` form the two edges, and `の` links the pair to <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code>.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Two nouns (space) | AとBの<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby> | <ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>・いす → <ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>といすの<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby> |
| Location of something | …の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に + ある/いる | <ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby> → <ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>と<ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>がある |
| Across a group | Noun (plural sense) + の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で |
| Between two parties | AとBの<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で / に | <ruby>両国<rt>りょうこく</rt></ruby> → <ruby>両国<rt>りょうこく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で |
| Two endpoints | AからBまでの<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby> | <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>・<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby> → <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>から<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>までの<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby> |
| Modifying a noun | …の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>の + Noun | <ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby> → <ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby> |

---

## 間 / 間に - between · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - with two edges of the same kind you may repeat the noun instead of listing two: <ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>と<ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>と<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>. This is idiomatic and reads as "between one X and the next".

---

## 間 / 間に - between · Examples

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>と<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>花屋<rt>はなや</rt></ruby>がある。<br>*There's a florist between the station and the bank.*

ソファとかべの**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**から<ruby>鍵<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>てきた。<br>*The keys turned up from between the sofa and the wall.*

その<ruby>歌<rt>うた</rt></ruby>は<ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>たちの**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で**はやっているらしい。<br>*That song is apparently a hit among young people.*

<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>までの**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>をください。<br>*Please call me between nine and five.*

---

## 間 / 間に - between · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>と<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**を<ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>往復<rt>おうふく</rt></ruby>した。<br>*I shuttled back and forth between Tokyo and Osaka more times than I can count.*

<ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>があったのか、だれも<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない。<br>*Nobody knows what went on between those two.*

<ruby>両国<rt>りょうこく</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>貿易<rt>ぼうえき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いが<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まった。<br>*Fresh trade talks have opened between the two countries.*

<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>と<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>細<rt>ほそ</rt></ruby>い<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>があって、そこを<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>ると<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>五分<rt>ごふん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*There's a narrow lane between the two buildings, and if you cut through it you reach the station in five minutes.*

---

## 間 / 間に - between · Nuance & register

The physical sense is entirely everyday. The other two lean slightly formal.

<code>…の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で</code> meaning "among" belongs to news writing, market reports and anything describing a trend: <ruby>中高生<rt>ちゅうこうせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>人気<rt>にんき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>まっている. In casual speech people usually say <code>…の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で</code> or just name the group with `は`. It is not stiff, but it does sound like something you read.

<code><ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> for a relationship is idiomatic and slightly delicate. <ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもが<ruby>生<rt>う</rt></ruby>まれた is a neutral way of saying a couple had a child; <ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>かあった gestures at trouble without naming it, and that discretion is exactly why the phrase gets used. The related noun <code><ruby>間柄<rt>あいだがら</rt></ruby></code> ("the relationship between parties") is a step more formal again and belongs to writing.

---

## 間 / 間に - between · Nuance & register (cont.)

A note on the spatial sense in speech: when the two edges are obvious, Japanese often drops one and says <code>…の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> with a plural or a repeated noun rather than spelling out A と B. 「<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>にはさんでおいた」 - "I slipped it in between the books" - needs no list.

Finally, this <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> is a full noun and can take any particle a noun can: を (<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>る), から, まで, は. The time-window use on the previous page is much more restricted.

---

## 間 / 間に - between · 間 (between) vs 間 (while)

> **Rule of thumb** - look at what precedes it. **Two nouns joined by と**, or a から…まで pair, gives you *between*. **A clause, or a single noun naming a period**, gives you *while*.

<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>と<ruby>秋<rt>あき</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きたい。<br>*I want to get to the sea once more between summer and autumn.*

<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きたい。<br>*I want to get to the sea once more during the summer.*

One `と` is the entire difference. The first locates the trip in the gap *between* two seasons; the second locates it *inside* one of them.

---

## 間 / 間に - between · 間 (between) vs 間 (while) (cont.)

The ambiguity is real but rarely a problem, because the two readings need different material in front of them. Where it does bite is with から…まで: <code><ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>までの<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code> is simultaneously "between nine and five" and "during nine-to-five", and Japanese does not distinguish them - the window *is* the gap.

---

## 間 / 間に - between · 間に vs 間で

> **Rule of thumb** - `に` puts something **in** the gap; `で` makes the gap the **arena** in which something goes on. Existence and placement take `に`; activity, exchange and popularity take `で`.

<ruby>棚<rt>たな</rt></ruby>と<ruby>壁<rt>かべ</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**ほこりがたまっていた。<br>*Dust had built up between the shelf and the wall.*

<ruby>社員<rt>しゃいん</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で**その<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>まっている。<br>*Word of it has already got around among the staff.*

Both particles are possible with two named parties, and then they say different things:

<ruby>両親<rt>りょうしん</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>がある。<br>*There is a problem between my parents. (it exists, sitting there)*

---

## 間 / 間に - between · 間に vs 間で (cont.)

<ruby>両親<rt>りょうしん</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いが<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>いている。<br>*Discussions are going on between my parents. (an activity they are conducting)*

Verbs of existence and appearance - ある, いる, できる, <ruby>生<rt>う</rt></ruby>まれる - take `に`. Verbs of doing, spreading and agreeing take `で`. If in doubt, ask whether the thing *sits* there or *happens* there.

---

## 間 / 間に - between · 間 vs 中

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> is the gap **between** things; <code><ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby></code> is the inside of a **container or a set**. Comparison and superlatives always take <code><ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby></code>.

クラスの**<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby><ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>い。<br>*She's the fastest in the class.*

クラスの**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で**そのうわさが<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>がった。<br>*The rumour spread through the class.*

---

## 間 / 間に - between · 間 vs 中 (cont.)

<code><ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で</code> looks into a group and picks a member out; <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で</code> looks at the space between the members and describes what travels across it. That is why ❌<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby><ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い is wrong: choosing one of three is a <code><ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby></code> job.

> **Note** - the `中` in this contrast is **なか**, the ordinary noun. It is a different word from the suffix `中` read ちゅう/じゅう, which means "during" or "throughout" and attaches with no `の`. <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で = "within the meeting's content"; <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby> = "the meeting is in progress".

---

## 間 / 間に - between · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>とベッドの**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>本棚<rt>ほんだな</rt></ruby>があります。 → ✅ <ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>とベッドの**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>本棚<rt>ほんだな</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*ある and いる state that something is *located* somewhere, and location in a gap takes `に`. `で` would need an action to host.*

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>人気<rt>にんき</rt></ruby>があります。 → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>人気<rt>にんき</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*Popularity, rumour, trends and discussions circulate **across** a group, and that is the `で` use. This one is worth memorising as a set: <code>…の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>人気<rt>にんき</rt></ruby>がある</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>四人<rt>よにん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby><ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>いのは<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ <ruby>四人<rt>よにん</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>**で<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby><ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>いのは<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>です。<br>*Picking the extreme member of a set is a <code><ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby></code> job. <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> would be the space between them, where nobody stands.*

---

## 間 / 間に - between · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>と<ruby>郵便局<rt>ゆうびんきょく</rt></ruby>**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**に → ✅ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>と<ruby>郵便局<rt>ゆうびんきょく</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**に<br>*The `の` linking the pair to <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> is not optional. Both edges go in front, joined by `と`, then `の`.*

# ～中

**Meaning** · a suffix meaning "in the middle of" (read **ちゅう**) or "all through, all over" (read **じゅう**)

This is not the noun <ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>. It is a suffix that glues straight onto the back of another noun with no `の`, and it comes in two flavours that share one kanji:

- **ちゅう** - something is **in progress**, or a period is a **deadline** you must<br>*act inside. <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>, <ruby>午前<rt>ごぜん</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>に.*
- **じゅう** - a span or a space is covered **from end to end**.<br>*<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>, <ruby>世界<rt>せかい</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>.*

---

## ～中 · Formation

Nothing conjugates; you are building a compound noun. What varies is the reading and whether `に` follows.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Activity noun → in progress | Noun + <ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby> | <ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby> |
| Period → deadline | Noun + <ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>に / <ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に | <ruby>午前<rt>ごぜん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>午前<rt>ごぜん</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>に |
| Period → whole extent | Noun + <ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby> | <ruby>一年<rt>いちねん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>一年<rt>いちねん</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby> |
| Place or body → whole extent | Noun + <ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby> | <ruby>世界<rt>せかい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>世界<rt>せかい</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby> |
| As a predicate | Noun + <ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>だ／です | <ruby>使用<rt>しよう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>使用<rt>しよう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>です |

---

## ～中 · Examples

<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>**です。<br>*My father is on the phone at the moment.*

---

## ～中 · Examples (cont.)

そのニュースはあっという<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>世界<rt>せかい</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>がった。<br>*The news spread around the world in no time.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>**が<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>くて、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>もする<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>が<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きなかった。<br>*I ached all over yesterday and couldn't summon the will to do anything.*

<ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>**のため、こちらの<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>り<ruby>口<rt>ぐち</rt></ruby>はお<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>いいただけません。<br>*This entrance is out of use owing to construction work.*

<ruby>午前<rt>ごぜん</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>**は<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っておりますので、<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>にもう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>お<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>いただけますか。<br>*I'm in meetings all morning, so could I ask you to call again in the afternoon?*

---

## ～中 · Nuance & register

The suffix itself is neutral, but the words it builds cluster in particular places. <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> compounds are the language of **signs and status messages**: <ruby>営業<rt>えいぎょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby> on a shop door, <ruby>使用<rt>しよう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby> on a toilet lock, <ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby> in a restaurant window, <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby> on a colleague's chat status. Learning to read them is worth more than learning to produce them.

<code><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby></code> compounds lean the other way: they are emphatic and slightly emotional, and they turn up in complaints and enthusiasm alike - <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>ほこりだらけだ, <ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>探<rt>さが</rt></ruby>した. The nuance is "and I mean *everywhere*", so a flat, factual sentence often reads better with <ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby> or ずっと instead.

---

## ～中 · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two things about the particle. When <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code>/<code><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby></code> names the window an action must land in, `に` is required: <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ります. When it merely describes the background, `に` is absent, and `は` often appears instead: <ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>は… - this is the same window-filling logic as <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> without `に`.

One reading to flag honestly: for <ruby>今週<rt>こんしゅう</rt></ruby> and <ruby>今月<rt>こんげつ</rt></ruby> both readings circulate, and dictionaries generally give ちゅう - <ruby>今月<rt>こんげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>に. For <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby> the reading is solidly じゅう. If you hear a native speaker say it the other way round, they are not wrong; this is a genuinely unsettled corner.

---

## ～中 · 中 vs 中

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> looks at a **point inside** a period: something is going on, or must be finished, within it. <code><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby></code> sweeps the **whole extent**: nothing is left out.

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>見積<rt>みつ</rt></ruby>もりをお<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りします。<br>*I'll send the quote before the day is out.*

<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ていた。<br>*On Sunday I was asleep the whole day.*

Note that <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby> takes じゅう even though it is a deadline - the reading follows the *word*, not the meaning. The pattern is only a tendency.

---

## ～中 · 中 vs 中 (cont.)

Where a noun genuinely takes both, the meanings separate cleanly: <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby> is "the exam is under way", while <ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby> is "all summer long". You will not find a word that means both at once.

---

## ～中 · ～中に vs ～のうちに

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> is a **limit**, usually one somebody else set. `のうちに` is an **opportunity** you are choosing to use.

レポートは<ruby>今月<rt>こんげつ</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>提出<rt>ていしゅつ</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Please hand the report in by the end of this month.*

<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>いから、<ruby>今月<rt>こんげつ</rt></ruby>**のうちに**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてしまおう。<br>*I've a lot of business trips next month, so let's get it written while we still have this month.*

---

## ～中 · ～中に vs ～のうちに (cont.)

The first is an instruction; the second is a plan with a reason attached. There is also a hard structural difference: <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> takes only nouns naming a measured span, while `うちに` takes clauses, adjectives and states - <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>なうちに has no <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> equivalent at all.

---

## ～中 · ～中 vs ～の最中に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> just says the activity is on. <code><ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> says you are in the **thick** of it, and something has cut across it.

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>**ですので、<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>でかけ<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>します。<br>*I'm in a meeting, so I'll call back later.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>停電<rt>ていでん</rt></ruby>して、<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby>やり<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>しになった。<br>*The power went out in the middle of the meeting and we had to start over.*

---

## ～中 · ～中 vs ～の最中に (cont.)

<code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> is a neutral status label and works fine as a whole predicate. <code><ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> is dramatic and expects an interruption to follow. Note the different attachment too: <code><ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> with no `の`, but <code><ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby></code> with one.

---

## ～中 · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>**の<ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。 → ✅ <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*This `中` is a suffix and takes no `の`. Writing <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で changes the word to なか and means "within the content of the meeting".*

- ❌ <ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>**<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>きました。 → ✅ <ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>**<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*"All day" is the whole-extent sense, which is じゅう. The kanji gives you no clue, so the reading has to be learned with the word.*

- ❌ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらせます。 → ✅ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらせます。<br>*A window that an action must land inside takes `に`, exactly as with <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code>. `で` would be marking a place or a means.*

- ❌ <ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>**しています**。 → ✅ <ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>です**。<br>*<code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> makes a **noun**, so it ends in だ／です, not in a verb. Say either <ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>です or <ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しています, never both.*

# ～最中に

**Meaning** · right in the thick of it - the peak of an activity, cut across by something else

<code><ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby></code> is <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> with the kanji for "most" bolted on the front, and that is exactly what it adds: not merely *during*, but *at the busiest, most involved point of*. Nine times out of ten the main clause is an interruption - the phone rings, the power fails, somebody walks in. If nothing cuts across, you probably wanted <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> or <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code>.

---

## ～最中に · Formation

<code><ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby></code> is a noun, so what precedes it is in noun-modifying shape. Unlike the suffix <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code>, it **requires** the `の` after a noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Activity noun | Noun + の + <ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に | <ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に |
| Verb | ている + <ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に | <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す → <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>している<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に |
| As a predicate | …の<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>だ／です | <ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby> → <ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>の<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>です |
| Emphatic | <ruby>真<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby> | <ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>真<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>だ |

---

## ～最中に · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the noun in front has to name an **activity with a middle**: <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>, <ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>, <ruby>発表<rt>はっぴょう</rt></ruby>, <ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>し. States and life stages are refused: ❌<ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>, ❌<ruby>留守<rt>るす</rt></ruby>の<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>.

> **Note** - the same two kanji, `最中`, also spell **もなか**, a wafer-and-bean-paste sweet. Context separates them without difficulty, but the word does exist and you will meet it in a confectionery shop.

## ～最中に · Nuance & register

Register-neutral in itself, and usable in both ます and plain speech. What marks it is **tone**: <code><ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> is vivid. It dramatises the timing, and the speaker is nearly always annoyed, embarrassed or startled. A neutral report of when something happened does not want it - that is <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code>'s job.

Because of that, the pattern is at home in complaints and anecdotes and comparatively rare in formal writing, where <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> or <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に</code> would be used instead. A company notice says <ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>; a colleague telling you about their week says <ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に.

---

## ～最中に · Nuance & register (cont.)

<code><ruby>真<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby></code> intensifies it further and is common as a whole predicate: <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>の<ruby>真<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>です. Note that the intensified form usually appears *without* `に`, describing the current state rather than locating an event.

One structural habit worth internalising: the clause before <code><ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby></code> is almost always ている or a noun + `の`. A bare dictionary form is wrong - ❌<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に - because the pattern needs the activity to be visibly under way, and only ている says that.

---

## ～最中に · 最中に vs 間に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code> puts an event anywhere in the window, including the quiet edges. <code><ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> puts it at the **peak**, and implies it should not have happened there.

<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ている**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>二回<rt>にかい</rt></ruby>メールが<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た。<br>*Two emails came in while I was watching the film.*

<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>の<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby>いいところの**<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がった。<br>*The person in front stood up right at the best bit of the film.*

---

## ～最中に · 最中に vs 間に (cont.)

The first is a neutral count of when the mail arrived. The second is a grievance. Note also that <code><ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> resists a durative main clause: ❌<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>の<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>にずっと<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ていた does not work, because a peak is a point and cannot host something lasting. Use <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code>, with no `に`, for that.

---

## ～最中に · 最中に vs ～中に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> is a **label**: this activity is on. <code><ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby></code> is a **judgement**: this activity was at full tilt, and look what happened.

<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>**は<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>られません。<br>*I can't take calls while driving.*

<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>している**<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に**タイヤがパンクした。<br>*I had a blowout in the middle of driving.*

---

## ～最中に · 最中に vs ～中に (cont.)

The forms differ too, and the difference is easy to get wrong: <code><ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> glues on with no `の`, whereas <code><ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby></code> demands one. <code><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code> also happily takes `は` for a rule that holds throughout; <code><ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby></code> almost never does, because a peak is not a period you lay rules over.

---

## ～最中に · 最中に vs ～ているところに

> **Rule of thumb** - both host interruptions, but <code><ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby></code> emphasises **how deep into it** you were, while `ところ` emphasises **which stage** you were at - about to, just doing, just finished.

ケーキを<ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>いている**<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た。<br>*Guests turned up while I was right in the middle of baking a cake.*

ケーキを<ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>こうとしている**ところに**<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た。<br>*Guests turned up just as I was about to bake a cake.*

---

## ～最中に · 最中に vs ～ているところに (cont.)

`ところ` can slide along the timeline - <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>くところ, <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>いているところ, <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>いたところ - and each position means something different. <code><ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby></code> has only one position: the middle. That is why it cannot be used for "just about to" or "just after".

# ～途中に / ～途中で

**Meaning** · partway along - on the way from A to B, or partway through something that has a beginning and an end

<code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby></code> is "the middle stretch of a route". Japanese uses the same word for a physical journey (<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>) and for any process that runs from start to finish (<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>), so "on the way" and "partway through" are one word here. The choice between `で` and `に` is not free: `で` for something that **happens**, `に` for something that **is there**.

---

## ～途中に / ～途中で · Formation

<code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby></code> is a noun. A verb in front is in plain non-past form - even when the whole sentence is in the past.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb of motion | Plain non-past + <ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で／に | <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で |
| Other verb | ている + <ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で | <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す → <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>している<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で |
| Noun | の + <ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で／に | <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で |
| Partial extent | <ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>まで／から | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んだ |
| As a predicate | …の<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>だ／です | <ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby> → まだ<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>の<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>です |

---

## ～途中に / ～途中で · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the plain non-past in front is fixed: <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>られた, never ❌<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>った<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で. The verb names the journey, and the journey has not finished - that is the whole point of being partway along it.

> **Note** - the bare non-past belongs to verbs of **going**: <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く, <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>かう, <ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>戻<rt>もど</rt></ruby>る. For other activities use ている (<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいる<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で) or the noun form (<ruby>読書<rt>どくしょ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で).

<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る**<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>茶屋<rt>ちゃや</rt></ruby>があって、そこで<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んでから<ruby>頂上<rt>ちょうじょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>目指<rt>めざ</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*There's a little teahouse partway up the mountain; we rested there before pressing on for the summit.*

---

## ～途中に / ～途中で · Nuance & register

Register-neutral and extremely common in everyday speech. There is no polite or plain variant to learn; only the particle choice matters.

The journey sense is so ordinary that it has produced fixed compounds: <code><ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby></code> for the way home (more idiomatic than <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby> when you mean the route rather than the timing), and <code><ruby>通勤<rt>つうきん</rt></ruby><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>帰宅<rt>きたく</rt></ruby><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby></code> in news writing and insurance forms, where the noun attaches with no `の`.

<code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>まで</code> and <code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>から</code> are worth learning as separate items. 「<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう」 - "let's walk part of the way together" - is a normal, friendly thing to say, and 「<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>から<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たので、よく<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりませんでした」 explains that you joined a film or a meeting late.

---

## ～途中に / ～途中で · Nuance & register (cont.)

Used as a predicate, <code>まだ<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>です</code> is a mild, useful hedge: "I'm not finished yet." It sounds considerably softer than まだ<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっていません, because it presents the work as in motion rather than as incomplete.

---

## ～途中に / ～途中で · 途中で vs 途中に

> **Rule of thumb** - `で` for an **event** that occurs partway along; `に` for a **thing** that sits partway along. If the verb is ある or いる, it is `に`.

<ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>同僚<rt>どうりょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I bumped into a former colleague partway through my walk.*

<ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>コースの**<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>神社<rt>じんじゃ</rt></ruby>がある。<br>*There's a small shrine partway along the walking route.*

---

## ～途中に / ～途中で · 途中で vs 途中に (cont.)

The distinction mirrors the ordinary `で`/`に` split you already know: `で` marks where an action takes place, `に` marks where something exists. It follows that the great majority of <code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby></code> sentences take `で`, because most of them report events. `に` shows up mainly with ある, いる, <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>っている and the like.

> **Note** - with a noun naming a process rather than a route, `で` is effectively the only option: <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で, <ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で. A meeting is not a road, so nothing can be standing beside it.

---

## ～途中に / ～途中で · 途中で vs 最中に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で</code> measures **progress**: you had got partway and had not finished. <code><ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> measures **intensity**: you were at the busiest point.

<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>してもいいですか。<br>*May I ask a question partway through your explanation?*

<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>されて、<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まってしまった。<br>*I was asked a question right in the middle of my explanation and lost my thread.*

---

## ～途中に / ～途中で · 途中で vs 最中に (cont.)

The first is a polite request; the second is a complaint. Only <code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby></code> works with a journey - <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で, never ❌<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く<ruby>最中<rt>さいちゅう</rt></ruby>に - and only <code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby></code> gives you まで and から, because only <code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby></code> thinks of the activity as a line you can stop on.

---

## ～途中に / ～途中で · 途中で vs 間に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code> needs a **window with two ends** and places an event inside it. <code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で</code> needs a **process with a direction** and says how far along you were.

<ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>三回<rt>さんかい</rt></ruby><ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>した。<br>*I asked three questions during the lesson.*

<ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>教室<rt>きょうしつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>て<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った。<br>*He walked out of the classroom partway through the lesson.*

---

## ～途中に / ～途中で · 途中で vs 間に (cont.)

Both are grammatical here, and the difference is one of emphasis: <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code> is neutral about position within the window, while <code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で</code> insists that the lesson was left unfinished. That is why <code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で</code> pairs so naturally with verbs of stopping, leaving and giving up - やめる, <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>降<rt>お</rt></ruby>りる, あきらめる.

---

## ～途中に / ～途中で · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>った<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>をした。 → ✅ <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>をした。<br>*The verb stays in plain non-past however far in the past the sentence is. A past-tense verb would say the journey was already over, which contradicts <code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby></code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>**で**コンビニがあります。 → ✅ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>**に**コンビニがあります。<br>*あります states existence, and existence takes `に`. Use `で` only when something happens there.*

- ❌ **<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>けてもいいですか。 → ✅ **<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>けてもいいですか。<br>*An ordinary noun needs `の`. The bare-attachment compounds (<ruby>通勤<rt>つうきん</rt></ruby><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby> and similar) are fixed vocabulary items, not a general rule.*

---

## ～途中に / ～途中で · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てしまった。 → ✅ <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいる<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てしまった。<br>*Only verbs of going take the bare non-past. Anything else needs ている, or recast it as a noun: <ruby>読書<rt>どくしょ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で.*

## ～途中に / ～途中で · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いた<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>としました。

## ～途中に / ～途中で · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>本屋<rt>ほんや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>寄<rt>よ</rt></ruby>った。 (b) <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>本屋<rt>ほんや</rt></ruby>がある。

{reveal}
(a) reports an action performed partway along the journey: you interrupted the walk to drop into the shop. `で` is the particle of "where an action happens", and <ruby>寄<rt>よ</rt></ruby>った is plainly an action.

{reveal}
(b) reports a fact about the route itself: a bookshop stands somewhere along it, regardless of whether anyone goes in. `に` is the particle of location, and ある is the verb that demands it. The two sentences describe the same street and answer completely different questions.
