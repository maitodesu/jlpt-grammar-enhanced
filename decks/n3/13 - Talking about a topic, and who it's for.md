# ～について

**Meaning** · about; concerning - names the subject matter that the talking, writing, thinking or investigating is *about*

`について` marks the **topic of an activity**. Something is being said, asked, written, researched or thought - and `について` says what it is about. It does not say who it is aimed at, and it does not say whose point of view we are taking. Those are jobs for other members of this same `に` family, and keeping them apart is most of the work at N3.

---

## ～について · Formation

`について` is a compound: the particle `に` plus the て-form of `つく` ("attach to"). It behaves as a unit and attaches to a bare noun - never to a verb or an adjective.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun, before a verb | Noun + について + verb | <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>について<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す |
| Noun, modifying another noun | Noun + についての + Noun | <ruby>環境<rt>かんきょう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>環境<rt>かんきょう</rt></ruby>についての<ruby>記事<rt>きじ</rt></ruby> |
| Noun, as topic | Noun + については | <ruby>費用<rt>ひよう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>費用<rt>ひよう</rt></ruby>については<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で |
| Noun, as addition | Noun + についても | <ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>についても<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>える |
| Noun, formal / business | Noun + につきまして（は） | <ruby>日程<rt>にってい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>日程<rt>にってい</rt></ruby>につきましては |
| Question word | <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby> / どこ など + について | <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby> → <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>について<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きますか |

---

## ～について · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the `の` in `についての` is not optional. `について` is adverbial: it can modify a verb on its own, but to hang it on a noun you have to convert it with `の`, exactly as you would with <code><ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>での<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby></code>.

---

## ～について · Examples

<ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>**について**<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べています。<br>*I'm looking into earthquakes.*

その<ruby>件<rt>けん</rt></ruby>**について**<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*I have a question about that matter.*

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>進路<rt>しんろ</rt></ruby>**について**<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*I talked to my teacher about which path to take after school.*

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>祭<rt>まつ</rt></ruby>り**についての**<ruby>番組<rt>ばんぐみ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>録画<rt>ろくが</rt></ruby>しておいた。<br>*I recorded a programme about Japanese festivals.*

---

## ～について · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>費用<rt>ひよう</rt></ruby>**については**、あとでまとめてメールで<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らせます。<br>*As for the cost of the trip, I'll send you the whole thing by email later.*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>規則<rt>きそく</rt></ruby>**について**、<ruby>社員<rt>しゃいん</rt></ruby>からいくつも<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ている。<br>*Staff have raised a number of points about the new rules.*

その<ruby>画家<rt>がか</rt></ruby>**について**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないまま<ruby>展覧会<rt>てんらんかい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>っても、たぶん<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しめないだろう。<br>*If I go to the exhibition knowing nothing about the painter, I probably won't enjoy it.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あす</rt></ruby>の<ruby>説明会<rt>せつめいかい</rt></ruby>では、<ruby>来年度<rt>らいねんど</rt></ruby>の<ruby>予算<rt>よさん</rt></ruby>**につきまして**ご<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>いたします。<br>*At tomorrow's briefing I will go over next year's budget.*

---

## ～について · Nuance & register

`について` sits in the middle of the register scale, and that is why it is the one to learn first. It is at home in a polite conversation, an email, a report, a university seminar and a news broadcast alike. Nothing about it is stiff; nothing about it is casual either.

Casual speech mostly does without it. Friends say <code><ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>のこと</code>, <code>あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>のこと</code>, or just `～って`:

- あの<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>のこと、どう<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>った? - "What did you think about that film?"
- <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>って、いつだっけ。 - "The exam - when was it again?"

---

## ～について · Nuance & register (cont.)

Using について there is not wrong, but it lifts the sentence a register: it sounds like you are opening an agenda item rather than chatting. Conversely, an email to a client that says <code><ruby>納期<rt>のうき</rt></ruby>のことですが</code> where it could have said <code><ruby>納期<rt>のうき</rt></ruby>につきましては</code> reads as slightly under-dressed.

The `につきまして` form is worth recognising even if you never produce it. It is the same word with a humble-polite `ます` stem inserted, and it belongs to shop announcements, customer service and business correspondence: <code><ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>営業<rt>えいぎょう</rt></ruby><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>につきましては…</code>. In ordinary polite speech it would sound over-formal, almost parodic.

---

## ～について · Nuance & register (cont.)

One structural habit to build: `について` likes to be followed by a verb of **speaking, writing or finding out** - <code><ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す</code>, <code><ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>く</code>, <code><ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>する</code>, <code><ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べる</code>, <code><ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>える</code>, <code><ruby>議論<rt>ぎろん</rt></ruby>する</code>, <code><ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>する</code>, <code><ruby>発表<rt>はっぴょう</rt></ruby>する</code>. If the predicate is an emotion or an evaluation instead, `について` is usually the wrong tool.

---

## ～について · ～について vs ～に関して

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register and different width. `について` points at a **specific** topic and is fine in speech; <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して</code> is more formal, mostly written, and takes in the **surrounding area** of a topic as well.

You can swap them in most sentences without producing anything ungrammatical. What changes is how the sentence sounds, and how tightly the topic is drawn.

この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>**について**<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いた。<br>*I asked the teacher about how this kanji is read.*

---

## ～について · ～について vs ～に関して (cont.)

<ruby>外国人<rt>がいこくじん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>け<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れに**<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して**、<ruby>市<rt>し</rt></ruby>は<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>方針<rt>ほうしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>示<rt>しめ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*The city has set out a new policy on accepting foreign residents.*

The first is one narrow question, asked out loud - `について` fits. The second covers a whole policy area, in writing, in a public register - <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して</code> fits. Reverse them and neither is wrong, but the first would sound like a committee paper and the second like small talk.

---

## ～について · ～について vs ～に対して

> **Rule of thumb** - `について` is the **topic**; <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code> is the **target**. "Talk about the customer" is `について`; "be polite to the customer" is <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code>.

This is the pair that costs learners the most marks, because English "about" and "toward" both come out as "regarding" in a dictionary.

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby>**について**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っていましたか。<br>*Did the manager say anything about the new hire?*

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**<ruby>厳<rt>きび</rt></ruby>しすぎると<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う。<br>*I think the manager is too hard on the new hire.*

---

## ～について · ～について vs ～に対して (cont.)

In the first, the new hire is the subject matter of some remarks. In the second, the new hire is the person the manager's behaviour is aimed at. A useful test: if you can replace the phrase with "on the subject of", it is `について`. If you can replace it with "at / to / against", it is <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code>.

---

## ～について · ～について vs ～のこと

> **Rule of thumb** - `のこと` is the everyday equivalent and, unlike `について`, it can be the **object of a feeling**. Verbs and adjectives of emotion take `のこと` (or a plain particle), never `について`.

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>のこと**を**<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>しています。<br>*I'm worried about my little brother.*

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>**について**<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>な<ruby>点<rt>てん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えました。<br>*I told the teacher the points about my brother that concern me.*

`について` needs the sentence to be *presenting information on a topic*. In the first sentence there is no presenting going on - there is only the feeling, and the brother is its object. The second sentence adds an act of communication, and `について` becomes available again.

---

## ～について · ～について vs ～のこと (cont.)

The same trap catches <code><ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き</code>, <code><ruby>嫌<rt>きら</rt></ruby>い</code>, <code><ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>る</code> and <code><ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えている</code>. ❌<code>この<ruby>歌手<rt>かしゅ</rt></ruby>について<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです</code> is simply not Japanese; you want <code>この<ruby>歌手<rt>かしゅ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです</code>.

---

## ～について · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>について<ruby>記事<rt>きじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んだ。（intending "an article about the accident"） → ✅ <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>**についての**<ruby>記事<rt>きじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んだ。<br>*Without `の`, `について` modifies the verb <code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んだ</code>, which gives "I read an article, about the accident" - a different and odd sentence. To attach it to a noun you need `についての`.*

- ❌ アニメについて<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。 → ✅ アニメ**が**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*`について` introduces the subject matter of an act of communication or thought. A feeling takes its object with `が` or `を`, not with `について`.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>についてこの<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**にとって**この<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>です。<br>*"For me, this job is important" is a standpoint, not a topic. Standpoints take `にとって`.*

---

## ～について · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>について<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>な<ruby>態度<rt>たいど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>った。 → ✅ <ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>な<ruby>態度<rt>たいど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>った。<br>*An attitude is directed *at* somebody. That is <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code>; `について` would mean the rudeness was somehow on the topic of the customer.*

---

## ～について · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>では、<ruby>工場<rt>こうじょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>移転<rt>いてん</rt></ruby>____<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
<ruby>工場<rt>こうじょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>移転<rt>いてん</rt></ruby>**について**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
The factory move is the subject matter of the discussion, and <code><ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う</code> is exactly the kind of communication verb `について` was built for. <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して</code> would also be grammatical, just a notch more formal than a normal meeting warrants.

## ～について · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>店長<rt>てんちょう</rt></ruby>はアルバイトの<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>（について・に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して）とても<ruby>親切<rt>しんせつ</rt></ruby>だ。

{reveal}
<ruby>店長<rt>てんちょう</rt></ruby>はアルバイトの<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**とても<ruby>親切<rt>しんせつ</rt></ruby>だ。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>親切<rt>しんせつ</rt></ruby>だ</code> describes how the manager behaves **toward** somebody, so the students are the target of an attitude, not a topic of conversation. Say `について` and the sentence would try to mean that he is kind on the subject of part-timers, which is nonsense.

{reveal}

## ～について · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>についてのレポートを<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください。それから、<ruby>締<rt>し</rt></ruby>め<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>りについて<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>け<ruby>付<rt>つ</rt></ruby>けません。

{reveal}
✅ …<ruby>締<rt>し</rt></ruby>め<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>り**についての**<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>け<ruby>付<rt>つ</rt></ruby>けません。

{reveal}
The first `について` is correct because it already carries `の` before the noun `レポート`. The second one sits directly in front of the noun <code><ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby></code> and needs the same `の`. This is the single most common slip with this pattern.

## ～について · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん**について**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しました。 (b) <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん**と**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しました。

{reveal}
(a) makes <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん the **subject matter**: somebody discussed him, and he may well not have been in the room. Depending on the context it can sound slightly ominous, in the same way English "we talked about you" does.

{reveal}
(b) makes him the **conversation partner** - you and he talked, about anything at all. `と` marks the person you do something jointly with; `について` marks what the doing is on the topic of. The two can even be stacked: <code><ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんと<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんについて<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しました</code> - "I talked with Yamada about Tanaka."

# ～に関する / ～に関して

**Meaning** · concerning; relating to; in connection with - the formal, largely written cousin of について（かんする・かんして）

<code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する</code> and <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して</code> mark the subject matter of a statement, a document, a rule or a piece of research. They mean what `について` means, but they belong to a different register - reports, notices, contracts, academic writing, the news - and they draw the topic **wider**: not just the thing named, but everything connected to it.

The two forms are one word doing two jobs. <code><ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する</code> is the dictionary form of a verb, so it modifies **nouns**. <code><ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して</code> is its て-form, so it modifies **verbs**.

---

## ～に関する / ～に関して · Formation

`に` + <code><ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する</code> ("to be connected to"). The noun in front takes `に`, never `を`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun, modifying a noun | Noun + に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する + Noun | <ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する<ruby>規則<rt>きそく</rt></ruby> |
| Noun, modifying a verb | Noun + に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して + verb | <ruby>費用<rt>ひよう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>費用<rt>ひよう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す |
| Noun, as topic | Noun + に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>しては | その<ruby>点<rt>てん</rt></ruby> → その<ruby>点<rt>てん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>しては |
| Noun, as addition | Noun + に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>しても | <ruby>人数<rt>にんずう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>人数<rt>にんずう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>しても |
| Noun, business / keigo | Noun + に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>しまして（は） | <ruby>納期<rt>のうき</rt></ruby> → <ruby>納期<rt>のうき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>しましては |
| Noun, modifying a noun (rarer) | Noun + に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>しての + Noun | <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>しての<ruby>報告<rt>ほうこく</rt></ruby> |

---

## ～に関する / ～に関して · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する</code> before a noun, <code><ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して</code> before a verb. <code><ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>するの</code> does not exist: the `の` you are reaching for is already built into the verb form.

---

## ～に関する / ～に関して · Examples

これは<ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する**<ruby>規則<rt>きそく</rt></ruby>です。<br>*These are the safety rules.*

その<ruby>点<rt>てん</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して**は、<ruby>後<rt>のち</rt></ruby>ほどお<ruby>答<rt>こた</rt></ruby>えします。<br>*I'll answer that point later.*

<ruby>健康<rt>けんこう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する**<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>何冊<rt>なんさつ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ってしまった。<br>*I've ended up buying any number of books on health.*

<ruby>料金<rt>りょうきん</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して**ご<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>のある<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>までお<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しください。<br>*If you have questions concerning the fees, please come to the reception desk.*

---

## ～に関する / ～に関して · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>気候<rt>きこう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>変化<rt>へんか</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する**<ruby>研究<rt>けんきゅう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>世界中<rt>せかいじゅう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>められている。<br>*Research relating to climate change is being carried forward all over the world.*

## ～に関する / ～に関して · Nuance & register

This is a **written** pattern first and a formal-spoken pattern second. Its natural homes are notices, headings, minutes, contracts, textbooks, news copy and the polished parts of a presentation. If you say it to a friend over coffee you will sound like you are reading from a slide.

A useful gauge: the more institutional the speaker, the more likely <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して</code> is. A company says <code><ruby>停電<rt>ていでん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>してお<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らせします</code>; a housemate says <code><ruby>停電<rt>ていでん</rt></ruby>のことなんだけど</code>. Both are correct in their own place, and swapping them is the mistake.

<code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>しまして</code> raises it one more step, into customer-facing business Japanese. It is extremely common in announcements and service email, and you should expect to hear it far more often than you produce it.

---

## ～に関する / ～に関して · Nuance & register (cont.)

There is also a **width** difference that survives the register difference. `について` picks out a topic; <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して</code> draws a circle around it. <code><ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する<ruby>情報<rt>じょうほう</rt></ruby></code> covers the typhoon, the train stoppages, the school closures and the shop opening hours - everything connected to it. <code><ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>についての<ruby>情報<rt>じょうほう</rt></ruby></code> is more likely to be information about the typhoon itself. This is a tendency rather than a hard line, but it is why headings and document titles overwhelmingly prefer <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する</code>.

Finally, do not confuse the phrase with the noun <code><ruby>関係<rt>かんけい</rt></ruby></code>. <code><ruby>関係<rt>かんけい</rt></ruby>がある</code> claims a real-world connection between two things; <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する</code> merely announces a topic. <code>この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby></code> is "material on this problem"; <code>この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>と<ruby>関係<rt>かんけい</rt></ruby>がある<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby></code> is "material that turns out to be connected to this problem."

---

## ～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関して vs ～について

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different clothes. `について` is the everyday form and works in speech; <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して</code> is formal, mostly written, and covers a broader area around the topic.

Nearly every sentence accepts both. Choose by asking who is speaking and where.

<ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>みの<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>**について**<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>で<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>した。<br>*The family talked about plans for the summer holiday.*

<ruby>市<rt>し</rt></ruby>の<ruby>予算<rt>よさん</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して**、<ruby>議会<rt>ぎかい</rt></ruby>で<ruby>激<rt>はげ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>議論<rt>ぎろん</rt></ruby>があった。<br>*There was a fierce debate in the assembly concerning the city budget.*

---

## ～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関して vs ～について (cont.)

Put <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して</code> in the first sentence and the family sounds like a board of directors. Put `について` in the second and nothing breaks - it is simply one notch less formal than assembly minutes would be.

---

## ～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関する + Noun vs ～についての + Noun

> **Rule of thumb** - both attach a topic to a noun, but the shapes differ: <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する</code> takes **no** `の`, `について` takes an obligatory one. Titles, headings and formal documents prefer <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する</code>.

<ruby>留学生<rt>りゅうがくせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>まいに**<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する**アンケート<br>*A survey concerning housing for international students*

---

## ～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関する + Noun vs ～についての + Noun (cont.)

The second is perfectly good Japanese and is what you would say out loud. The first is what would be printed at the top of the form. Note the ❌<code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>するの</code> error is impossible to make with `について`, and the ❌<code>について<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby></code> error (dropping `の`) is impossible to make with <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>する</code> - the two patterns fail in opposite directions.

---

## ～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関して vs ～において

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して</code> gives the **topic**; `において` gives the **setting** - the place, the era, the field or the situation in which something holds.

Both are formal, both translate as "in" or "regarding" at some point in a dictionary, and both appear in the same kind of document, so they blur together.

<ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby><ruby>対策<rt>たいさく</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して**、<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>された。<br>*A new proposal has been put forward concerning safety measures.*

<ruby>建設<rt>けんせつ</rt></ruby><ruby>現場<rt>げんば</rt></ruby>**において**、<ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby><ruby>対策<rt>たいさく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>よりも<ruby>優先<rt>ゆうせん</rt></ruby>される。<br>*On construction sites, safety measures take priority over everything.*

---

## ～に関する / ～に関して · ～に関して vs ～において (cont.)

In the first, safety measures are what the proposal is *about*. In the second, the building site is *where* the rule applies. If the noun could be preceded by "on the subject of", it is <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して</code>; if it could be preceded by "in" or "at", it is `において`.

# ～において / ～における

**Meaning** · in; at; on - the formal written stand-in for で, naming the place, time, situation or field in which something holds（において・における）

`において` sets the **stage**. It tells you where something happens, when it happens, or within what field or respect a statement is true. Everything it does, the particle `で` also does - but `で` is the everyday word and `において` is the one that appears in announcements, papers, contracts and ceremonies.

`における` is the same word in noun-modifying dress: <code><ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>において<ruby>行<rt>おこな</rt></ruby>う</code> ("carry out at the venue") but <code><ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>における<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby><ruby>事項<rt>じこう</rt></ruby></code> ("points to note at the venue").

---

## ～において / ～における · Formation

Attaches to a bare noun. The `に` is part of the pattern and is not replaceable.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (place), modifying a verb | Noun + において | <ruby>本社<rt>ほんしゃ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>本社<rt>ほんしゃ</rt></ruby>において<ruby>行<rt>おこな</rt></ruby>う |
| Noun (time or era) | Noun + において | <ruby>明治<rt>めいじ</rt></ruby><ruby>時代<rt>じだい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>明治<rt>めいじ</rt></ruby><ruby>時代<rt>じだい</rt></ruby>において |
| Noun (field or respect) | Noun + において | <ruby>技術<rt>ぎじゅつ</rt></ruby><ruby>面<rt>めん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>技術<rt>ぎじゅつ</rt></ruby><ruby>面<rt>めん</rt></ruby>において |
| Noun, modifying a noun | Noun + における + Noun | <ruby>職場<rt>しょくば</rt></ruby> → <ruby>職場<rt>しょくば</rt></ruby>における<ruby>関係<rt>かんけい</rt></ruby> |

## ～において / ～における · Nuance & register

This pattern is **formal and written**, with essentially no casual life at all. Notices, minutes, academic papers, legal text, ceremonial speech and the polished register of the news are its habitat. Saying ❌<code>コンビニにおいてパンを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った</code> is not a grammar error - it is a tonal disaster, roughly like writing "I did procure bread at the convenience store."

Two practical consequences.

First, **`において` covers only some of what `で` covers**. `で` marks place, but also means ("with a knife"), cause ("because of a cold"), material, and limit ("within a week"). `において` inherits only the **place, time, situation and field** senses. There is no ❌<code>ナイフにおいて<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>る</code>.

---

## ～において / ～における · Nuance & register (cont.)

Second, the "field or respect" sense is where `において` earns its keep, because `で` there can be ambiguous. <code>この<ruby>点<rt>てん</rt></ruby>において</code> - "in this respect" - is crisper in formal prose than <code>この<ruby>点<rt>てん</rt></ruby>で</code>, which could momentarily be read as a location.

`において` also carries a faint ceremonial weight when used of an event. A wedding invitation says <code>ホテルの<ruby>大<rt>だい</rt></ruby>ホールにおいて</code>; a text to a friend says `ホテルで`. The formality is doing real work in the first case - it signals that this is an occasion.

---

## ～において / ～における · Nuance & register (cont.)

One last thing worth noticing: `における` is a fixture of **titles**. <code><ruby>現代<rt>げんだい</rt></ruby><ruby>社会<rt>しゃかい</rt></ruby>における<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>役割<rt>やくわり</rt></ruby></code> is exactly the shape of an essay title, a chapter heading, a conference paper. If you are writing academic Japanese, this is a phrase you will use constantly.

---

## ～において / ～における · ～において vs ～で

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register, narrower coverage. `において` can replace the **place / time / situation / field** uses of `で` and nothing else, and only in formal writing or formal speech.

<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>行<rt>おこな</rt></ruby>われた。<br>*The match was held in Osaka.*

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まっている。<br>*The trains are stopped because of an accident.*

❌<code><ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>において<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まっている</code> is ungrammatical, because this `で` marks a **cause**, and `において` has no cause sense. The same goes for <code>バスで<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く</code> (means) and <code><ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>で<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>る</code> (material) - neither converts.

---

## ～において / ～における · ～における + Noun vs ～の + Noun

> **Rule of thumb** - `の` says the two nouns are connected somehow; `における` says specifically **"within / inside / in the context of"**. When the relationship needs pinning down, `における` does it.

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>**の**<ruby>教育<rt>きょういく</rt></ruby><br>*Japanese education*

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>**における**<ruby>外国語<rt>がいこくご</rt></ruby><ruby>教育<rt>きょういく</rt></ruby><br>*foreign-language education in Japan*

The first could mean education that is Japanese in style, education owned by Japan, education about Japan - `の` does not say. The second is unambiguous: it is education happening **inside** Japan, seen as a setting. This precision is why academic titles reach for `における` rather than `の`, and why replacing it with `の` in an essay title makes the title vaguer even though it stays grammatical.

---

## ～において / ～における · ～における + Noun vs ～の + Noun (cont.)

Between the two sits `での`, which is the neutral-register version of the same idea: <code><ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>での<ruby>生活<rt>せいかつ</rt></ruby></code>, "life in Japan". Use `での` when you are speaking, `における` when you are writing formally.

---

## ～において / ～における · ～において vs ～について

> **Rule of thumb** - `において` is the **setting**; `について` is the **subject matter**. "Discussed at the meeting" is `において`; "discussed about the meeting" is `について`.

Both are formal-ish, both hang off `に`, and English "regarding" is offered as a translation of both, which is how learners end up swapping them.

その<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>先日<rt>せんじつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>**において**<ruby>議論<rt>ぎろん</rt></ruby>された。<br>*That issue was debated at the meeting the other day.*

<ruby>先日<rt>せんじつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>**について**<ruby>報告書<rt>ほうこくしょ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いた。<br>*I wrote a report about the meeting the other day.*

---

## ～において / ～における · ～において vs ～について (cont.)

In the first, the meeting is the setting the debating happened in. In the second, the meeting is what the report is on. Substituting one for the other produces a sentence that is grammatical and simply says something else - which is the most dangerous kind of mistake.

---

## ～において / ～における · Common mistakes

- ❌ はさみにおいて<ruby>紙<rt>かみ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>った。 → ✅ はさみ**で**<ruby>紙<rt>かみ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>った。<br>*`において` inherits only the place, time, situation and field senses of `で`. It never marks an instrument.*

- ❌ <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>において<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みになった。 → ✅ <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>**で**<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みになった。<br>*This `で` is a cause, and there is no cause sense in `において`. If you want the formal register, use <code><ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>のため</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>職場<rt>しょくば</rt></ruby>において<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えている。（intending "problems in the workplace") → ✅ <ruby>職場<rt>しょくば</rt></ruby>**における**<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えている。<br>*Before a noun you need `における`. As written, the sentence says the increasing is happening at the workplace, which is not quite the same claim.*

---

## ～において / ～における · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>駅前<rt>えきまえ</rt></ruby>のカフェにおいて<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちに<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った。 → ✅ <ruby>駅前<rt>えきまえ</rt></ruby>のカフェ**で**<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちに<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った。<br>*Grammatically fine, tonally absurd. A private, everyday event does not take announcement vocabulary.*

# ～に対して

**Meaning** · toward; at; in response to - and, from a clause, "whereas"（にたいして）

<code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code> points. It names the **person or thing an action, an attitude or a reaction is aimed at**: who you are polite to, what you are objecting to, what a reply is a reply to. Say "about" in your head and you will reach for it wrongly - this is not the topic of the sentence, it is the target.

A second use falls out of the same image. If you set one thing opposite another, you get contrast: <code>Aのに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して、B</code> - "A, whereas B". And a third, narrower use gives ratios: "per one participant, two bottles."

---

## ～に対して · Formation

<code><ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>する</code> is a verb ("face, confront"), so it inflects: the て-form modifies verbs and adjectives, the dictionary form modifies nouns.

---

## ～に対して · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (target) | Noun + に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して | <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども → <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>してやさしい |
| Noun, modifying a noun | Noun + に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>する + Noun | <ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby> → <ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>する<ruby>態度<rt>たいど</rt></ruby> |
| Verb / い-adjective (contrast) | Plain form + のに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いのに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して |
| な-adjective (contrast) | ～な + のに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなのに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して |

## ～に対して · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the contrastive use needs `の` before it. A clause cannot attach directly: ❌<code><ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code>. The `の` turns the clause into something noun-like, which is what <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code> requires.

## ～に対して · Examples

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**やさしい<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>です。<br>*He's a person who is kind to children.*

その<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>答<rt>こた</rt></ruby>えなかった。<br>*He gave no answer to that question.*

<ruby>目上<rt>めうえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**そんな<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>い<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>をしてはいけない。<br>*You mustn't speak that way to someone senior to you.*

<ruby>市<rt>し</rt></ruby>の<ruby>決定<rt>けってい</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**、<ruby>住民<rt>じゅうみん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>く<ruby>反発<rt>はんぱつ</rt></ruby>している。<br>*Residents are pushing back hard against the city's decision.*

---

## ～に対して · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>参加者<rt>さんかしゃ</rt></ruby><ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**、<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>を<ruby>二本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>ずつ<ruby>用意<rt>ようい</rt></ruby>した。<br>*We laid on two drinks per participant.*

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>が<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>で<ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>かすのが<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな**のに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**、<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一日中<rt>いちにちじゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいる。<br>*Where my older sister loves being outside and moving about, my younger sister reads all day.*

お<ruby>客様<rt>きゃくさま</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>する**<ruby>言葉遣<rt>ことばづか</rt></ruby>いについては、<ruby>入社<rt>にゅうしゃ</rt></ruby>したばかりのころに<ruby>厳<rt>きび</rt></ruby>しく<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>された。<br>*I got told off sharply about how I spoke to customers when I had only just joined.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>売上<rt>うりあげ</rt></ruby>がまったく<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>びなかった**のに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**、<ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>からずっと<ruby>好調<rt>こうちょう</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*Whereas last year's sales didn't grow at all, this year has been strong right through from spring.*

---

## ～に対して · Nuance & register

The target use leans **formal**, but not so far that it is out of place in ordinary polite conversation. <code><ruby>部下<rt>ぶか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して<ruby>厳<rt>きび</rt></ruby>しい</code> is something a colleague might say out loud. What marks it as slightly weighty is that plain `に` would usually do the job: <code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもにやさしい</code> and <code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>してやさしい</code> mean the same thing, and the second is more deliberate - it puts the relationship itself under a spotlight. In relaxed speech you would normally drop it.

Where <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code> is not optional is when the noun is the target of a **reaction**: a protest, a reply, a complaint, a demand, an evaluation. <code><ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>た</code> - the questions came *back at* the explanation. Bare `に` there is thinner and sometimes ambiguous.

---

## ～に対して · Nuance & register (cont.)

The contrastive use is squarely **written**. It belongs in comparisons, reports and essays, and it has a cool, analytical tone: the writer is laying two facts side by side, not complaining. That distinguishes it from `のに`, which contrasts *and* carries dissatisfaction.

<code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>し</code>, without `て`, is the clipped written form. It is standard in newspapers and official documents - <code><ruby>組合<rt>くみあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>し<ruby>回答<rt>かいとう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>求<rt>もと</rt></ruby>めた</code> - and sounds stiff if spoken aloud outside a formal announcement.

> **Note** - the ratio use is common in explanations of quantity and in statistics: <code><ruby>人口<rt>じんこう</rt></ruby><ruby>千人<rt>せんにん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して<ruby>医師<rt>いし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby></code>. It is the one sense where the noun in front is a **number**, so it is easy to spot and easy to keep separate from the other two.

---

## ～に対して · ～に対して vs ～について

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code> is the **target** of an action or attitude; `について` is the **subject matter** of a statement. Aim vs topic.

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>制度<rt>せいど</rt></ruby>**について**<ruby>社員<rt>しゃいん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>した。<br>*I explained the new system to the staff.*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>制度<rt>せいど</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**<ruby>社員<rt>しゃいん</rt></ruby>から<ruby>不満<rt>ふまん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>た。<br>*Complaints came from the staff against the new system.*

---

## ～に対して · ～に対して vs ～について (cont.)

In the first the system is what the explanation is *about*. In the second it is what the complaints are aimed *at*. Note that the two sentences have different verbs, and that is the reliable clue: verbs of **saying, writing and investigating** take `について`, while verbs of **reacting** - <code><ruby>反対<rt>はんたい</rt></ruby>する</code>, <code><ruby>抗議<rt>こうぎ</rt></ruby>する</code>, <code><ruby>答<rt>こた</rt></ruby>える</code>, <code><ruby>文句<rt>もんく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う</code> - take <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code>.

---

## ～に対して · ～に対して vs ～にとって

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code> looks **outward from the subject toward** the noun; `にとって` looks **outward from the noun** at the world. Target vs standpoint.

<ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**あんな<ruby>口<rt>くち</rt></ruby>をきくべきではない。<br>*You shouldn't talk to your parents like that.*

<ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>**にとって**、<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの<ruby>健康<rt>けんこう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*For parents, their children's health is the biggest worry.*

---

## ～に対して · ～に対して vs ～にとって (cont.)

In the first, the parents are on the receiving end of the speaking. In the second, nothing is aimed at them at all - we have simply climbed inside their heads to judge what matters. A quick test: `にとって` is nearly always followed by an **evaluation** (<code><ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>だ</code>, <code><ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しい</code>, `ありがたい`), whereas <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code> is followed by an **action or a stance**.

---

## ～に対して · ～のに対して vs ～のに

> **Rule of thumb** - both put two clauses against each other, but <code>のに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code> is a **neutral comparison** and `のに` is a **complaint**. If you are annoyed, you want `のに`.

<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>晴<rt>は</rt></ruby>れの<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い**のに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**、<ruby>日本海側<rt>にほんかいがわ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>く。<br>*Winter in Tokyo has many clear days, whereas on the Japan Sea side the snow goes on and on.*

<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>した**のに**、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかった。<br>*He promised, and yet he didn't come.*

---

## ～に対して · ～のに対して vs ～のに (cont.)

The first sentence has no feeling in it: two regions, two climates, stated side by side. The second is a grievance. There is also a structural difference - <code>のに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code> compares two things **of the same kind** along the same axis (winters with winters, sales with sales), while `のに` simply says the second clause is unexpected given the first.

For the same neutral comparison you will also meet <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>（で）</code>, which does much the same work and joins sentences rather than clauses.

---

## ～に対して · Common mistakes

- ❌ この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いましょう。 → ✅ この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>**について**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いましょう。<br>*<code><ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う</code> takes a topic, not a target. Use <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code> only when something is being aimed at the noun.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して、この<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>宝物<rt>たからもの</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**にとって**、この<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>宝物<rt>たからもの</rt></ruby>です。<br>*"For me" meaning "from my point of view" is `にとって`. <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code> would mean the photograph is doing something to you.*

- ❌ <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して、<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さい。 → ✅ <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い**のに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**、<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さい。<br>*The contrastive use attaches to a **nominalised** clause. The `の` is compulsory, and with a な-adjective it becomes <code>な+のに<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code>.*

## ～に対して · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>上司<rt>じょうし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して<ruby>態度<rt>たいど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>い。（intending "his attitude toward his boss is bad"） → ✅ <ruby>上司<rt>じょうし</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>する**<ruby>態度<rt>たいど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>い。<br>*Before a noun the verb takes its dictionary form. # ～にとって

**Meaning** · for; from the standpoint of - the sentence is judged from inside the position of the person or group named

`にとって` hands the microphone to somebody else. It says: seen from where **they** stand, here is how things look. What follows is almost always an **evaluation** - important, hard, welcome, painful, worthless - because that is the only kind of statement a standpoint can change.

It cannot mean "about", and it cannot mark the target of an action. Those two facts do most of the work of keeping it apart from its neighbours.

---

## ～にとって · Formation

Attaches to a bare noun naming somebody capable of having a stake in the matter: a person, a group, a company, a country, sometimes a living thing.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (person or group) | Noun + にとって | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby> → <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>にとって<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>だ |
| Noun, modifying a noun | Noun + にとっての + Noun | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>にとっての<ruby>理想<rt>りそう</rt></ruby> |
| Noun, as topic | Noun + にとっては | <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>にとっては<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>だ |
| Noun, as addition | Noun + にとっても | <ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>にとっても<ruby>損<rt>そん</rt></ruby>だ |
| Noun, business keigo | Noun + にとりまして | <ruby>弊社<rt>へいしゃ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>弊社<rt>へいしゃ</rt></ruby>にとりましては |

---

## ～にとって · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - after `にとって` you need an **evaluation**, not an action. <code><ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>だ・<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>だ・<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しい・ありがたい・<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>だ・<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れられない</code> all work. A plain report of what somebody did does not: ❌<code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>にとって<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っています</code>.

---

## ～にとって · Examples

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**にとって**、<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby><ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>です。<br>*For me, family comes first.*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**にとって**この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しすぎる。<br>*This book is too hard for a child.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**にとっては**<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いが、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>う<ruby>価値<rt>かち</rt></ruby>はあると<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う。<br>*It's a bit pricey as far as I'm concerned, but I think it's worth buying.*

<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>**にとって**、<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>育<rt>そだ</rt></ruby>てることは<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby><ruby>確実<rt>かくじつ</rt></ruby>な<ruby>投資<rt>とうし</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*For a company, developing its people is the surest investment there is.*

---

## ～にとって · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>北国<rt>きたぐに</rt></ruby>で<ruby>育<rt>そだ</rt></ruby>った<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>**にとって**、<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>はほとんど<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くないらしい。<br>*Apparently, for people raised in the far north, a Tokyo winter is barely cold at all.*

あのときの<ruby>一言<rt>ひとこと</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>**にとって**<ruby>一生<rt>いっしょう</rt></ruby><ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れられないものになった。<br>*That one remark became something she would never forget as long as she lived.*

<ruby>留学生<rt>りゅうがくせい</rt></ruby>**にとっての**<ruby>最初<rt>さいしょ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>壁<rt>かべ</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>言葉<rt>ことば</rt></ruby>よりも<ruby>生活<rt>せいかつ</rt></ruby><ruby>習慣<rt>しゅうかん</rt></ruby>かもしれない。<br>*The first wall for an exchange student may be daily customs rather than language.*

<ruby>地方<rt>ちほう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>**にとって**、<ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ていくことは<ruby>放<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>っておけない<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*For a small rural town, young people leaving is a problem that cannot be left alone.*

---

## ～にとって · Nuance & register

`にとって` is close to register-neutral. It is at home in an essay, a news article, a job interview and a conversation with a friend. Nothing about it is stiff, and unlike <code>に<ruby>関<rt>かん</rt></ruby>して</code> or `において` it has not been pushed out of speech by a plainer alternative.

What it does carry is a mild rhetorical weight. Because it explicitly frames a judgement as belonging to somebody, it invites the reader to notice that other people might see it differently. <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>にとっては<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い</code> is politer than <code><ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い</code> for exactly that reason: it presents the verdict as personal rather than absolute. Sales staff and diplomats lean on this.

---

## ～にとって · Nuance & register (cont.)

In casual speech, `には` covers most of the same ground with fewer syllables - <code><ruby>僕<rt>ぼく</rt></ruby>にはちょっと<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>かな</code> - and younger speakers also reach for <code><ruby>個人的<rt>こじんてき</rt></ruby>には</code>, "personally speaking". `にとって` is not unnatural in conversation, but a whole conversation full of it would sound like an essay read aloud.

`にとりまして` is the business-keigo form, common in apologies and in speeches: <code><ruby>弊社<rt>へいしゃ</rt></ruby>にとりまして<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>ありがたいお<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>です</code>. Recognise it; you will rarely need to produce it.

---

## ～にとって · Nuance & register (cont.)

One restriction worth stating plainly: the noun has to be something that can **have interests**. People, groups, companies, countries, and living things all qualify - <code><ruby>植物<rt>しょくぶつ</rt></ruby>にとって<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>は<ruby>欠<rt>か</rt></ruby>かせない</code> is perfectly normal. A pure abstraction or an inanimate object usually does not: ❌<code>この<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>にとって</code> is odd, because a desk has no point of view to speak from.

---

## ～にとって · ～にとって vs ～には

> **Rule of thumb** - they overlap heavily, and `には` is the shorter, more spoken option. `にとって` is unambiguous; `には` is a stack of two particles and can also mean "to" or "in", so context has to sort it out.

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**には**この<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>がよく<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からなかった。<br>*I didn't really follow this explanation.*

<ruby>初心者<rt>しょしんしゃ</rt></ruby>**にとって**、この<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>不親切<rt>ふしんせつ</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*For a beginner, this explanation is unhelpful.*

---

## ～にとって · ～にとって vs ～には (cont.)

Both frame a judgement from somebody's position. The difference shows up when the sentence could be read another way. <code><ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんには<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>した</code> means "I told Yamada" - `に` marking the recipient, `は` marking contrast - and has nothing to do with standpoints. <code><ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんにとって</code> can only ever mean "from Yamada's point of view". In writing, where you cannot lean on intonation, that certainty is worth the extra syllables.

---

## ～にとって · ～にとって vs ～に対して

> **Rule of thumb** - `にとって` is the **viewer**; <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code> is the **target**. Nothing is being aimed at the noun in a `にとって` sentence.

<ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby>**にとって**、<ruby>最初<rt>さいしょ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>一年<rt>いちねん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えることばかりだ。<br>*For a new employee, the first year is nothing but things to learn.*

<ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**、もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>したほうがいい。<br>*You'd do better to explain things to a new employee a bit more carefully.*

---

## ～にとって · ～にとって vs ～に対して (cont.)

The first sentence describes the world as the newcomer experiences it. The second describes what somebody else should do **to** them. A quick test: if you can add "…and they may be the only one who sees it that way", it is `にとって`. If you can add "…and they are on the receiving end", it is <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code>.

---

## ～にとって · ～にとって vs ～として

> **Rule of thumb** - `にとって` = "**for** X, from X's side of the fence". `として` = "**as** X, in the role of X". `にとって` looks out from the noun; `として` puts the subject *into* the noun.

Both come out as English "as" or "for" often enough to blur, and they can even sit in the same slot with different meanings.

<ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>**にとって**、<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの<ruby>進路<rt>しんろ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>口<rt>くち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しにくい<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*For a parent, a child's career choice is a hard thing to weigh in on.*

<ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>**として**、<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>は<ruby>本音<rt>ほんね</rt></ruby>を<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えておきたい。<br>*As a parent, I'd like to say what I really think at least once.*

---

## ～にとって · ～にとって vs ～として (cont.)

In the first, parents are the group whose viewpoint we are borrowing; the speaker need not be one. In the second, the speaker **is** a parent and is acting in that capacity. Notice also what follows each: `にとって` is followed by an evaluation, `として` by an action.

---

## ～にとって · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>にとって<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby>コーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby>コーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*`にとって` frames a judgement, so it needs an evaluative predicate. A plain report of what you do takes an ordinary topic `は`.*

- ❌ この<ruby>計画<rt>けいかく</rt></ruby>にとって<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>かせてください。 → ✅ この<ruby>計画<rt>けいかく</rt></ruby>**について**<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>かせてください。<br>*`にとって` cannot mean "about". A plan is not a viewpoint-holder; it is a topic.*

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>にとって<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>なことを<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってしまった。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>なことを<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってしまった。<br>*Rudeness is aimed **at** someone. That is <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code>; `にとって` would put the teacher in the judge's seat instead of the victim's.*

---

## ～にとって · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>にとって<ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>しています。 → ✅ <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>しています。<br>*Gratitude has a recipient, not a standpoint. Plain `に` is what <code><ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>する</code> takes.*

✅ <ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>には、<ruby>社員<rt>しゃいん</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>制度<rt>せいど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>する<ruby>義務<rt>ぎむ</rt></ruby>がある。

{reveal}
The second `にとって` is wrong: the employees are who the explanation is delivered **to**, so they need <code>に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して</code>. The first is better replaced by plain `には`, since <code><ruby>義務<rt>ぎむ</rt></ruby>がある</code> states a fact about the company rather than judging something from its viewpoint.

## ～にとって · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん**にとって**この<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>だ。 (b) この<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>だ。

{reveal}
(b) states the job's difficulty as a plain fact about the job - anyone would find it easy.

{reveal}
(a) attaches the verdict to one person. It says the job is easy **as Tanaka experiences it**, and it quietly implies that this is not everybody's experience: someone else might struggle. That built-in "…though not necessarily for others" is the whole reason the pattern exists, and it is why `にとって` is useful for softening an opinion you do not want to present as objective truth.

# ～として

**Meaning** · as; in the capacity of; in the role of - the hat somebody or something is wearing while it does what it does

`として` assigns a **role**. A person acts *as* a representative, a building is used *as* a gallery, a sum of money is handed over *as* a gift. The thing itself does not change; what `として` supplies is the capacity in which it is being treated.

Two extensions follow naturally. `としては` sets a **standard of judgement** - "measured as an X" - and, with a person, "speaking for myself". And a handful of fixed adverbs like <code><ruby>原則<rt>げんそく</rt></ruby>として</code> and <code><ruby>参考<rt>さんこう</rt></ruby>として</code> are simply this pattern frozen solid.

---

## ～として · Formation

Attaches to a bare noun. No `な`, no `の` before it.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (role), modifying a verb | Noun + として | <ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>として<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>く |
| Noun, modifying a noun | Noun + としての + Noun | <ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby> → <ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>としての<ruby>責任<rt>せきにん</rt></ruby> |
| Noun, as standard or standpoint | Noun + としては | <ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby>としてはよくやっている |
| Noun, as addition | Noun + としても | <ruby>趣味<rt>しゅみ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>趣味<rt>しゅみ</rt></ruby>としても<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しめる |
| Noun, ruling one out | Noun + としてではなく | <ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby> → <ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>としてではなく |
| Fixed adverbs | <ruby>原則<rt>げんそく</rt></ruby>／<ruby>例外<rt>れいがい</rt></ruby>／<ruby>参考<rt>さんこう</rt></ruby> + として | <ruby>原則<rt>げんそく</rt></ruby> → <ruby>原則<rt>げんそく</rt></ruby>として<ruby>禁止<rt>きんし</rt></ruby>です |

---

## ～として · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - beyond N3 you will meet <code><ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>として…ない</code>, "not a single person", where `として` combines with a counter and a negative. It is the same word doing an emphatic job, and it is worth recognising even though you do not need to produce it yet.

---

## ～として · Examples

<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>**として**<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*My older brother works as a doctor.*

<ruby>趣味<rt>しゅみ</rt></ruby>**として**<ruby>絵<rt>え</rt></ruby>を<ruby>描<rt>か</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*I paint as a hobby.*

<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>代表<rt>だいひょう</rt></ruby>**として**<ruby>式<rt>しき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出席<rt>しゅっせき</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*I attended the ceremony as the company's representative.*

この<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、<ruby>美術館<rt>びじゅつかん</rt></ruby>**として**<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>われている。<br>*This building is now used as an art museum.*

---

## ～として · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>参考<rt>さんこう</rt></ruby>**として**、<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>もお<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りしておきます。<br>*I'll send last year's materials along as well, for reference.*

<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ったばかりの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>**としては**、よくやっているほうだと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う。<br>*For someone who's only just joined, I'd say he's doing well.*

<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だち**としては**<ruby>最高<rt>さいこう</rt></ruby>だが、<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>のパートナー**としては**<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*She's wonderful as a friend, but as a working partner I have my doubts.*

<ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>語学<rt>ごがく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>**としてだけでなく**、<ruby>人生<rt>じんせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>経験<rt>けいけん</rt></ruby>**としても**<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きな<ruby>意味<rt>いみ</rt></ruby>がある。<br>*Studying abroad matters not only as language study but as a life experience too.*

---

## ～として · Nuance & register

`として` is **register-neutral**. It appears in casual conversation, in job interviews, in newspapers and in contracts without changing shape or feeling. Nothing about it is stiff.

What is register-marked is the company it keeps. The fixed adverbs - <code><ruby>原則<rt>げんそく</rt></ruby>として</code> ("as a rule"), <code><ruby>例外<rt>れいがい</rt></ruby>として</code> ("by way of exception"), <code><ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>として</code> ("as a result") - belong to written and formal speech. A friend says `だいたいダメだよ`; a notice says <code><ruby>原則<rt>げんそく</rt></ruby>としてお<ruby>断<rt>ことわ</rt></ruby>りしております</code>.

`としては` deserves its own line, because it does two different things and speakers rely on both.

---

## ～として · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **As a category standard**: <code><ruby>中古<rt>ちゅうこ</rt></ruby>としては<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い</code> -<br>*"expensive for a second-hand one." The judgement is made against what is normal for the category.*
- **As a personal position**: <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>としては<ruby>反対<rt>はんたい</rt></ruby>です</code> -<br>*"my own position is that I'm against it." This is a politeness device. It marks the opinion as yours alone and leaves room for the group to decide otherwise, which is why it is everywhere in meetings.*

The second of those is worth practising. Where English says "personally, I…", Japanese very often says <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>としては</code>, and a learner who reaches for <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は</code> instead sounds more insistent than they meant to.

---

## ～として · ～として vs ～にとって

> **Rule of thumb** - `として` puts the subject **into** the role; `にとって` looks out **from** the noun's viewpoint. "As a teacher, I…" is `として`; "for a teacher, this is…" is `にとって`.

<ruby>教師<rt>きょうし</rt></ruby>**として**、<ruby>生徒<rt>せいと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>はきちんと<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きたい。<br>*As a teacher, I want to listen properly to what my students say.*

<ruby>教師<rt>きょうし</rt></ruby>**にとって**、<ruby>生徒<rt>せいと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>信頼<rt>しんらい</rt></ruby>されることほどうれしいことはない。<br>*For a teacher, nothing is more gratifying than being trusted by the students.*

In the first sentence the speaker **is** a teacher and is acting in that capacity. In the second the speaker is standing outside, describing how the world looks to teachers - and need not be one. The predicate gives it away too: `として` is followed by an action or an intention, `にとって` by an evaluation.

---

## ～として · ～として vs ～で

> **Rule of thumb** - `で` can also mark a capacity, but only loosely and only in casual speech. `として` states the role explicitly and works at any register.

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>はアルバイト**で**<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っていました。<br>*I was there on a part-time basis last year.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>はアルバイト<ruby>職員<rt>しょくいん</rt></ruby>**として**<ruby>採用<rt>さいよう</rt></ruby>されました。<br>*Last year I was taken on as a part-time member of staff.*

---

## ～として · ～として vs ～で (cont.)

Both are natural; the second is what you would write on a form. The gap widens when the role is formal or contested - ❌<code><ruby>代表<rt>だいひょう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>述<rt>の</rt></ruby>べた</code> is at best vague about whether you were *the* representative, while <code><ruby>代表<rt>だいひょう</rt></ruby>として<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>述<rt>の</rt></ruby>べた</code> states it plainly. When the role is what matters, use `として`.

---

## ～として · ～としては vs ～にしては

> **Rule of thumb** - `としては` measures something **within** its category; `にしては` says the result is **surprising for** that category. Add "considering" and a raised eyebrow, and you have `にしては`.

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**としては**よく<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えているほうだ。<br>*As children go, he thinks things through well.*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**にしては**よく<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えている。<br>*He thinks things through well, for a child.*

---

## ～として · ～としては vs ～にしては (cont.)

The first ranks him among children - a neutral placement inside the category. The second is mildly astonished: given that he is a child, this is more than expected. Where they part company completely is the personal-position use. <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>としては<ruby>賛成<rt>さんせい</rt></ruby>です</code> means "my position is that I'm in favour"; ❌<code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>にしては<ruby>賛成<rt>さんせい</rt></ruby>です</code> would have to mean "surprisingly for me, I'm in favour", which is rarely what anyone intends.

---

## ～として · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>通訳<rt>つうやく</rt></ruby>になって<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ました。（intending "attended as an interpreter"） → ✅ <ruby>通訳<rt>つうやく</rt></ruby>**として**<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*`になる` means *become*: the wrong version says you turned into an interpreter and then went. `として` names the capacity you attended in.*

- ❌ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>倉庫<rt>そうこ</rt></ruby>にして<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>っています。 → ✅ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>倉庫<rt>そうこ</rt></ruby>**として**<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*`にする` is "turn it into"; <code>として<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>う</code> is "use it as". Only the second describes an ongoing role.*

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>としての<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**として**<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*`としての` exists only in front of a **noun** (<code><ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>としての<ruby>経験<rt>けいけん</rt></ruby></code>). Before a verb, drop the `の`.*

---

## ～として · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>として、この<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>はとても<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**にとって**、この<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>はとても<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>です。<br>*This is a judgement made from your side, not a role you are performing. <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>としては</code> would be possible only if you were stating a position in a discussion.*

{reveal}

# ～向き - facing

**Meaning** · facing such-and-such a way - the direction a thing is turned toward（むき）

<code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> is the noun form of <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>く</code>, "to face". Stuck onto a direction word it gives you the orientation of something that is standing still: a south-facing room, an arrow pointing down, an envelope placed face up.

There is no movement in it. A south-facing window is not going anywhere; it is simply turned that way. That single fact separates this pattern from <code>に<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>かって</code>, and it is where learners slip.

---

## ～向き - facing · Overview

The same word has a second, unrelated-looking life meaning "suited to", as in <code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きの<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby></code>. That sense is treated separately; this page is only about direction.

## ～向き - facing · Nuance & register

This is an ordinary, register-neutral noun. Nothing about it is formal or casual; it turns up in estate agents' listings, in assembly instructions, in weather reports and in conversation, all in the same shape.

Two of its habitats are worth knowing about, because they are where you will actually meet it.

**Property.** <code><ruby>南<rt>みなみ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> is a selling point in Japan, and listings advertise it the way an English listing advertises a garden. The reason is practical - sun for the laundry and warmth in winter - and the vocabulary is fixed: <code><ruby>南<rt>みなみ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きバルコニー</code>, <code><ruby>東<rt>ひがし</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きの<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby></code>.

---

## ～向き - facing · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Attitude.** <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> and <code><ruby>後<rt>うし</rt></ruby>ろ<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> have drifted from literal facing to mean "positive / constructive" and "negative / defeatist". These are extremely common in work Japanese: <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きに<ruby>検討<rt>けんとう</rt></ruby>します</code> is what a company says when it wants to sound willing.

> **Note** - <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きに<ruby>検討<rt>けんとう</rt></ruby>します</code> is a famous soft refusal. Literally "we will consider it positively", it very often means the matter will quietly go nowhere. Take it as encouragement, not as a promise.

---

## ～向き - facing · Nuance & register (cont.)

The bare noun <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> also appears in a fixed phrase you will hear in shops and offices: <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きを<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>える</code>, "turn it round". And <code><ruby>風<rt>かざ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> has its own figurative use - <code><ruby>風<rt>かざ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きが<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わった</code>, "the wind has changed", said of a situation turning in someone's favour.

---

## ～向き - facing · ～向き vs ～に向かって

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> is a **static orientation**; <code>に<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>かって</code> is **movement or action toward** something. If anything is travelling, you need <code>に<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>かって</code>.

その<ruby>像<rt>ぞう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>のほうを<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>いて<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>っている。<br>*The statue stands facing the sea.*

<ruby>船<rt>ふね</rt></ruby>は<ruby>島<rt>しま</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>かって**<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>んでいる。<br>*The ship is heading toward the island.*

---

## ～向き - facing · ～向き vs ～に向かって (cont.)

The statue is not going to the sea; it is merely turned that way, and <code><ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> would describe it. The ship is under way, so <code>に<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>かって</code> is the only option: ❌<code><ruby>島<rt>しま</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きに<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む</code> does not work. Note also the grammatical shape - <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> attaches directly to the noun with no particle, while <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>かって</code> demands `に`.

---

## ～向き - facing · 向き vs 方向

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> is **which way a thing is turned**; <code><ruby>方向<rt>ほうこう</rt></ruby></code> is **a direction out in the world**, the way you go. A chair has a <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code>; a road has a <code><ruby>方向<rt>ほうこう</rt></ruby></code>.

テーブルの**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き**を<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>右<rt>みぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>回<rt>まわ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*I turned the table a little to the right.*

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>はこの**<ruby>方向<rt>ほうこう</rt></ruby>**でいいですか。<br>*Is the station this way?*

---

## ～向き - facing · 向き vs 方向 (cont.)

Swapping them produces sentences that sound off rather than wrong. ❌<code>テーブルの<ruby>方向<rt>ほうこう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>える</code> suggests moving the table somewhere else, not rotating it; ❌<code><ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>はこの<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きですか</code> sounds as though the station is facing you. The pair <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きを<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>える</code> (rotate) versus <code><ruby>方向<rt>ほうこう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>える</code> (change course) is the cleanest way to hold them apart.

---

## ～向き - facing · ～向き vs ～向け

> **Rule of thumb** - only <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> does direction. <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> means "made for, aimed at an audience" and never means "facing".

<ruby>北<rt>きた</rt></ruby>**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き**の<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>からは<ruby>一日中<rt>いちにちじゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>らない。<br>*A north-facing window gets no sun all day.*

これは<ruby>海外<rt>かいがい</rt></ruby>**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け**に<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>られたモデルです。<br>*This is a model built for overseas markets.*

---

## ～向き - facing · ～向き vs ～向け (cont.)

❌<code><ruby>北<rt>きた</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>けの<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby></code> is not Japanese. The two suffixes do overlap in the *other* sense of <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> - "suitable for" - where <code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> and <code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> are both possible and differ subtly. In the direction sense there is no overlap at all, so here at least the choice is easy.

# ～向き - suited to

**Meaning** · suitable for; a good fit for - it happens to suit that person, group or purpose, whether or not anyone planned it that way（むき）

This is the same <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> that gives you <code><ruby>南<rt>みなみ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きの<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby></code>, but with the direction sense worn away. Here it says: **this thing and that audience match**. A book that suits beginners, a dish that suits summer, a job that suits people who like talking.

The key word is *happens to*. <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> is the speaker's assessment of a fit. It makes no claim that anybody designed the thing for that audience - and that is exactly what separates it from <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code>, which does.

## ～向き - suited to · Nuance & register

Register-neutral and fully at home in speech. You can say <code>これ、<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きだね</code> to a friend in a shop without sounding formal, and the same phrase can appear in a product review.

What the word carries is a **judgement**, and judgements belong to somebody. When you say <code><ruby>初心者<rt>しょしんしゃ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きだ</code> you are giving your reading of how well the thing fits - you are not reporting a fact about the publisher's intentions. This is why <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> works so naturally in reviews, recommendations and warnings, and why it so often appears with hedges: <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きかもしれない</code>, <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きだと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code>, <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きとは<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>えない</code>.

---

## ～向き - suited to · Nuance & register (cont.)

The negative <code><ruby>不向<rt>ふむ</rt></ruby>き</code> is a single word, not <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> plus a prefix you can apply freely, and it is used above all of **people and jobs**: <code><ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>接客<rt>せっきゃく</rt></ruby>には<ruby>不向<rt>ふむ</rt></ruby>きだ</code>. It is blunter than <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きではない</code> and can sting, so in careful speech people soften it: <code>あまり<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>いていないかもしれませんね</code>.

<code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き<ruby>不向<rt>ふむ</rt></ruby>き</code> as a pair is a small fixed noun meaning "aptitude" - literally the suits and the does-not-suits. <code><ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>には<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き<ruby>不向<rt>ふむ</rt></ruby>きがある</code> is a stock consoling phrase: "people are cut out for different things."

---

## ～向き - suited to · ～向き vs ～向け

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> = it **happens to suit** them. <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> = it was **made for** them. Fit versus intention.

This is the pair the whole page exists for, and the difference is real even though many sentences accept both.

この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きいので<ruby>高齢者<rt>こうれいしゃ</rt></ruby>**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き**だ。<br>*This dictionary has large print, so it works well for older readers.*

---

## ～向き - suited to · ～向き vs ～向け (cont.)

The first is a reader's verdict: whatever the publisher had in mind, the big print suits them. The second is a statement about the design brief. Consequently <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> is what appears in business and marketing language - <code><ruby>海外<rt>かいがい</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code>, <code><ruby>法人<rt>ほうじん</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> - while <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> is what appears in reviews and advice.

One useful consequence: <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> takes `に` before a verb naturally (<code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>けに<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>る</code>, <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>けに<ruby>開<rt>ひら</rt></ruby>く</code>) because intention modifies an action. <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> mostly stays a predicate or a noun modifier, which is why ❌<code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きに<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>る</code> sounds wrong to a native ear.

---

## ～向き - suited to · ～向き vs ～用

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>用<rt>よう</rt></ruby></code> states a **designated use**: this item is *for* that and not for anything else. <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> only rates a fit.

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**<ruby>用<rt>よう</rt></ruby>**のいすはあちらにございます。<br>*The children's chairs are over there.*

この<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>は<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き**だと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う。<br>*I think this film is one for children.*

---

## ～向き - suited to · ～向き vs ～用 (cont.)

<code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども<ruby>用<rt>よう</rt></ruby></code> is a category label - the chair is a child's chair, and an adult using it is misusing it. <code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> is a recommendation; an adult can watch the film perfectly well, it just fits children better. Labels on shelves and menus overwhelmingly use <code><ruby>用<rt>よう</rt></ruby></code>: <code><ruby>業務用<rt>ぎょうむよう</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>洗濯用<rt>せんたくよう</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>家庭用<rt>かていよう</rt></ruby></code> - it is the suffix of designated purpose.

---

## ～向き - suited to · ～向きだ vs ～に向いている

> **Rule of thumb** - the two say the same thing from opposite ends. <code>A は B <ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きだ</code>: the **thing** is described, B is the audience. <code>B は A に<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>いている</code>: the **person** is described, A is the activity.

この<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>細<rt>こま</rt></ruby>かい<ruby>作業<rt>さぎょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き**だ。<br>*This job suits people who like detailed work.*

<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>細<rt>こま</rt></ruby>かい<ruby>作業<rt>さぎょう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>いている**。<br>*My younger sister is well suited to detailed work.*

---

## ～向き - suited to · ～向きだ vs ～に向いている (cont.)

Getting these crossed is the commonest error with the pattern. Remember that <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>いている</code> is a verb and needs `に`, while <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> is a suffix and needs nothing at all. Also note that with a named individual, <code>に<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>いている</code> is far more natural: <code><ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きだ</code> would be odd, because <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> prefers a **type** of person rather than a particular one.

---

## ～向き - suited to · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きに<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>られた<ruby>番組<rt>ばんぐみ</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け**に<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>られた<ruby>番組<rt>ばんぐみ</rt></ruby>です。<br>*Being *made for* an audience is intention, and intention is <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code>. <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> would only rate the result.*

- ❌ <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はこの<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きです。 → ✅ <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はこの<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>いています**。<br>*The two ends are reversed. <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> attaches to the **audience**, so the correct form with this word order would be <code>この<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>のような<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きです</code>.*

## ～向き - suited to · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ このコースは<ruby>体力<rt>たいりょく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>自信<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>がない<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きです。 → ✅ …<ruby>自信<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>がない<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き**です。<br>*No particle stands between the audience and <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code>; they form a compound noun. # ～向け

**Meaning** · intended for; aimed at - made, written, priced or organised with that audience or market in mind（むけ）

<code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> comes from <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>ける</code>, "to point something at". Somebody has aimed this thing at somebody: a programme at children, a car at the export market, a seminar at second-year students. The word carries **intention**, and that is its whole meaning.

It says nothing about whether the aim succeeded. A textbook can be <code><ruby>初心者<rt>しょしんしゃ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> and still be far too hard for a beginner - the publisher aimed, and missed. Judging the fit is the job of <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code>.

## ～向け · Nuance & register

The word itself is register-neutral, but its **habitat** is not. You will meet it in product descriptions, publishers' catalogues, course listings, TV schedules, press releases and company presentations, far more often than in casual chat. That is not because it sounds formal - it is because talking about target audiences is mostly a business activity.

In conversation it turns up whenever the topic is products or content: <code>これ、<ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>けだよね</code> is perfectly ordinary spoken Japanese. What you would not do is use it about your own private arrangements - see the contrast with `のために` below.

---

## ～向け · Nuance & register (cont.)

A few collocations are worth having ready-made, because they are how the pattern actually appears in the wild: <code><ruby>海外<rt>かいがい</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code>, <code><ruby>国内<rt>こくない</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code>, <code><ruby>輸出<rt>ゆしゅつ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code>, <code><ruby>法人<rt>ほうじん</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> ("for businesses"), <code><ruby>個人<rt>こじん</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> ("for individuals"), <code><ruby>初心者<rt>しょしんしゃ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code>, <code><ruby>女性<rt>じょせい</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code>. The last of these has become slightly loaded in recent years - marketing categories drawn along gender lines attract comment - so it is worth noticing that it describes a **marketing decision** and not a claim about who may buy the thing.

> **Note** - <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> wants a **group or market**, not a named individual. <code><ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>けの<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby></code> is odd unless you mean managers as a category. For one person, use <code><ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>せる<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby></code> or <code><ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>のための<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby></code>.

---

## ～向け · ～向け vs ～向き

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> = somebody **aimed** it there. <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> = it **turns out to suit** them. Intention versus fit.

この<ruby>問題集<rt>もんだいしゅう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>受験生<rt>じゅけんせい</rt></ruby>**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け**に<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>られた。<br>*This question book was produced for exam candidates.*

この<ruby>問題集<rt>もんだいしゅう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby>なので<ruby>独学<rt>どくがく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き**だ。<br>*The explanations are thorough, so this question book is good for people studying alone.*

---

## ～向け · ～向け vs ～向き (cont.)

The first reports a decision made by the publisher. The second is the speaker's verdict, and it could easily be about an audience the publisher never considered. This is why a shop sign says <code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>けコーナー</code> - the shop has designated it - while a review says <code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>きだと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code>.

Only <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> combines comfortably with `に` plus a verb of making, writing, opening or selling, precisely because those verbs describe the act of aiming. And only <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> can be preceded by a compass point: <code><ruby>南<rt>みなみ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き</code> is a direction, and ❌<code><ruby>南<rt>みなみ</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> is nothing at all.

---

## ～向け · ～向け vs ～用

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> names the **audience** something was aimed at; <code><ruby>用<rt>よう</rt></ruby></code> names the **use** something is for. Who versus what-for.

<ruby>自宅<rt>じたく</rt></ruby>**<ruby>用<rt>よう</rt></ruby>**ですか、<ruby>贈<rt>おく</rt></ruby>り<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>**<ruby>用<rt>よう</rt></ruby>**ですか。<br>*Is it for yourself, or is it a gift?*

<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け**のサービスも<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めました。<br>*We've launched a service for businesses as well.*

---

## ～向け · ～向け vs ～用 (cont.)

The shop assistant's question is about the **purpose** the item will serve, so <code><ruby>用<rt>よう</rt></ruby></code>. The second sentence names the **customer segment**, so <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code>. They can even stack in the same phrase without redundancy: <code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>けの<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby><ruby>用<rt>よう</rt></ruby>アプリ</code> - an exam-taking app aimed at students.

Note that <code><ruby>用<rt>よう</rt></ruby></code> attaches to activities and occasions as readily as to people (<code><ruby>旅行用<rt>りょこうよう</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>非常用<rt>ひじょうよう</rt></ruby></code>), whereas <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け</code> is essentially restricted to audiences and markets.

## ～向け · ～向けに vs ～のために

> **Rule of thumb** - `のために` is **for someone's sake**, personal and warm. <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>けに</code> is **targeted at a market**, impersonal and commercial.

<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>**のために**<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I bought a large-print book for my mother.*

<ruby>目<rt>め</rt></ruby>が<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>い<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>けに**<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>されている。<br>*Large-print books are published for people with poor eyesight.*

## ～向け · ～向けに vs ～のために (cont.)

Swapping them is a tonal mistake rather than a grammatical one, and it is a memorable one. ❌<code><ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>けに<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った</code> makes your mother sound like a demographic segment. Conversely, a publisher's catalogue would not write <code><ruby>目<rt>め</rt></ruby>が<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>い<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>のために</code> unless it wanted to sound charitable rather than commercial - which, occasionally, it does.

## ～向け · Common mistakes

## ～向け · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>小学生<rt>しょうがくせい</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>けに<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>られているが、<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>短<rt>みじか</rt></ruby>くて<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりやすいので、<ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>けにもいい。

{reveal}
✅ …<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>短<rt>みじか</rt></ruby>くて<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりやすいので、<ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby>**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>き**でもある。

## ～向け · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>け**に<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>かれた<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby> (b) <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**のために**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>かれた<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>

{reveal}
(a) is a statement about the **target readership**: the writer pitched the level, vocabulary and length at children. It is neutral and professional, the kind of thing said about published material, and it implies nothing about the writer's feelings.

{reveal}
(b) is about **benefit**. Somebody wrote it so that children would be helped, and the phrase carries warmth - it would suit a hospital leaflet or a note written by a teacher for their own class. In practice (b) also allows the reader and the beneficiary to differ: an explanation written *for the sake of* children might in fact be addressed to their parents, which (a) rules out.
