# によって / による

**Meaning** · because of; by means of; by (the one who did it); varying with

One shape, four jobs. `によって` names the **cause** of an outcome, the **method** that produced it, the **agent** of a passive verb, or the thing a result **varies with**. Which of the four you are reading is decided by the verb at the end of the sentence, not by `によって` itself.

---

## によって / による · Formation

`によって` attaches to a noun and leaves the noun untouched. The variants in the last three rows are the same word in different clothes.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun - cause | Noun + によって | <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>によって<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になった |
| Noun - method | Noun + によって | <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いによって<ruby>解決<rt>かいけつ</rt></ruby>する |
| Noun - agent of a passive | Noun + によって + passive verb | ゴッホ → ゴッホによって<ruby>描<rt>えが</rt></ruby>かれた |
| Noun - what a result varies with | Noun + によって + <ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う・<ruby>異<rt>こと</rt></ruby>なる | <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> → <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>によって<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う |
| Before a noun | Noun + による + Noun | <ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>による<ruby>被害<rt>ひがい</rt></ruby> |
| Stiff written style, notices | Noun + により | <ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>により<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>れません |
| "in some cases, depending on" | Noun + によっては | <ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>によっては<ruby>延期<rt>えんき</rt></ruby>します |

---

## によって / による · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - `による` is what `によって` turns into when it has to modify a noun: <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>によって<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れる (before a verb) but <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>による<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れ (before a noun). There is also `によるもの` as a predicate: <ruby>今回<rt>こんかい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>失敗<rt>しっぱい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby><ruby>不足<rt>ぶそく</rt></ruby>によるものです。

---

## によって / による · Examples

<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>**によって**<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まりました。<br>*The trains stopped because of the snow.*

<ruby>味<rt>あじ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>**によって**<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>います。<br>*The taste varies from shop to shop.*

この<ruby>橋<rt>はし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>百年<rt>ひゃくねん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>にフランスの<ruby>技師<rt>ぎし</rt></ruby>**によって**<ruby>造<rt>つく</rt></ruby>られました。<br>*This bridge was built a hundred years ago by a French engineer.*

<ruby>集合<rt>しゅうごう</rt></ruby><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>い**によって**<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めましょう。<br>*Let's settle the meeting time by talking it over.*

---

## によって / による · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>大雨<rt>おおあめ</rt></ruby>**による**<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れで、<ruby>到着<rt>とうちゃく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>くなります。<br>*Because of a delay caused by the heavy rain, we will arrive about thirty minutes late.*

<ruby>天候<rt>てんこう</rt></ruby>**によっては**、<ruby>頂上<rt>ちょうじょう</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かずに<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>返<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>すこともあります。<br>*Depending on the weather, we sometimes turn back before reaching the summit.*

<ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>**により**、<ruby>東口<rt>ひがしぐち</rt></ruby>の<ruby>階段<rt>かいだん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今月<rt>こんげつ</rt></ruby>いっぱいご<ruby>利用<rt>りよう</rt></ruby>いただけません。<br>*Owing to construction work, the east-exit stairs are unavailable for the rest of this month.*

<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>出来事<rt>できごと</rt></ruby>でも、<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>く<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>立場<rt>たちば</rt></ruby>**によって**<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>け<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>り<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>がまるで<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わってくる。<br>*Even one and the same event is taken quite differently depending on where the listener stands.*

---

## によって / による · Nuance & register

`によって` is a **written** connector. Reports, notices, textbooks and news copy are full of it; ordinary conversation reaches for `で`, `から`, `ので` or `～のため` instead. Saying 「バスが<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかったことによって<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れました」 to a friend sounds like you are reading out a formal apology.

`により` is the stiffest of the family. It belongs to station announcements, municipal notices and contracts, where it is often followed by a bare noun predicate: 「<ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby><ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>のため<ruby>停車<rt>ていしゃ</rt></ruby>しております」-type language. In polite speech it can stretch to `によりまして`.

---

## によって / による · Nuance & register (cont.)

`によっては` is not simply the topic-marked version of `によって`. It narrows the sentence to *some* values of the noun: 「<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>によって<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う」 says everyone is different, while 「<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>によっては<ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>るかもしれない」 says that among the range of people, some would be angry. It very often ends in `かもしれない`, `こともある` or `場合がある`.

One member of the family is thoroughly colloquial: `による` used as an ordinary predicate, meaning "it depends". 「<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くかどうかは<ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>による。」 - "Whether we go depends on the weather." Shortened further to 「<ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>と<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby> によるね」 or a flat 「<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>によるよ」, it is everyday spoken Japanese, and it is worth learning separately from the formal uses above.

---

## によって / による · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, the passive-agent `によって` carries a whiff of the encyclopaedia. It is the natural choice for creating, discovering, building and founding - <ruby>発見<rt>はっけん</rt></ruby>される, <ruby>建<rt>た</rt></ruby>てられる, <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>かれる - and for institutional actors. For ordinary "someone did something to me" passives, see the contrast below.

---

## によって / による · によって vs で

> **Rule of thumb** - `で` is the everyday particle for cause and means. `によって` is its formal counterpart, and it prefers **methods and procedures that produce a result** over plain physical tools.

For an instrument you hold in your hand, `で` is not merely more casual - `によって` is wrong:

はし**で**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてもいいですか。<br>*May I eat with chopsticks?*

But once the "means" is a procedure, a system or a decision-making method, `によって` becomes available and sounds appropriately weighty:

<ruby>会長<rt>かいちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>投票<rt>とうひょう</rt></ruby>**によって**<ruby>選<rt>えら</rt></ruby>ばれます。<br>*The chairperson is chosen by a vote.*

---

## によって / による · によって vs で (cont.)

There is also a hard grammatical line: `で` cannot mark the **agent** of a passive. ❌<code>ピカソで<ruby>描<rt>えが</rt></ruby>かれた<ruby>絵<rt>え</rt></ruby></code> is not a stylistic slip, it is broken.

---

## によって / による · によって vs に (agent of a passive)

> **Rule of thumb** - `に` for someone doing something *to* you; `によって` for the person or body that **produced** the thing. Inside a clause modifying a noun, `によって` is usually the only option.

`に` keeps the subject in the picture as an affected party:

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**に**ほめられて、<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>うれしそうだった。<br>*My little brother was praised by the teacher and looked pleased all day.*

`によって` steps back and reports a fact of record:

この<ruby>寺<rt>てら</rt></ruby>は<ruby>江戸<rt>えど</rt></ruby><ruby>時代<rt>じだい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>職人<rt>しょくにん</rt></ruby>**によって**<ruby>建<rt>た</rt></ruby>てられた。<br>*This temple was built by craftsmen of the Edo period.*

---

## によって / による · によって vs に (agent of a passive) (cont.)

Swap in `に` before a noun-modifying passive and the sentence goes strange: ❌<code><ruby>職人<rt>しょくにん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>建<rt>た</rt></ruby>てられた<ruby>寺<rt>てら</rt></ruby></code> reads as though the temple suffered something at the craftsmen's hands.

---

## によって / による · によって（違う） vs によっては

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>Aによって<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う</code> says the whole set varies with A, so there is no single answer. `Aによっては` picks out *some* values of A where something special happens.

<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いている<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>**によって**<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>います。<br>*Opening hours differ from shop to shop.*

<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>**によっては**、<ruby>夜中<rt>よなか</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いているところもあります。<br>*Some shops stay open until the middle of the night.*

The second sentence makes no claim about shops in general - it only says that somewhere in the range there are late-opening ones. That is why it pairs so naturally with `～もある`.

---

## によって / による · Common mistakes

- ❌ ボールペン**によって**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください。 → ✅ ボールペン**で**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください。<br>*A physical tool takes `で`. `によって` wants a method or a procedure, not an object you hold.*

- ❌ <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>**によっては**<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>が<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>います。 → ✅ <ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>**によって**<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>が<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>います。<br>*"Everyone thinks differently" is a claim about the whole set, so no `は`. With `は` you are saying "there are some people who…", which needs a different ending.*

- ❌ <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>**によって**<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れのため、<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>められません。 → ✅ <ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>**による**<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れのため、<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>められません。<br>*Directly in front of a noun, `によって` has to become `による`.*

---

## によって / による · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ この<ruby>寺<rt>てら</rt></ruby>は<ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>な<ruby>大工<rt>だいく</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>建<rt>た</rt></ruby>てられた<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ この<ruby>寺<rt>てら</rt></ruby>は<ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>な<ruby>大工<rt>だいく</rt></ruby>**によって**<ruby>建<rt>た</rt></ruby>てられた<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>です。<br>*A passive that modifies a noun takes its agent with `によって`.*

---

## によって / による · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>大雪<rt>おおゆき</rt></ruby>____<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になりました。

{reveal}
<ruby>大雪<rt>おおゆき</rt></ruby>**によって**（or **により**）<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になりました。

{reveal}
The snow is the cause of the cancellation, and 「<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になる」 is a reported outcome rather than a chatty remark, so the formal cause marker fits. In conversation you would more likely say 「<ruby>大雪<rt>おおゆき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になった」.

---

## によって / による · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: この<ruby>花瓶<rt>かびん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>な<ruby>作家<rt>さっか</rt></ruby>（に・によって）<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>られたものです。

{reveal}
この<ruby>花瓶<rt>かびん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>な<ruby>作家<rt>さっか</rt></ruby>**によって**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>られたものです。

{reveal}
The artist is the *producer* of the vase, not someone doing something to an affected subject, and the passive is modifying `もの`. Both facts point to `によって`.

---

## によって / による · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Ticket prices vary depending on the season."

{reveal}
チケットの<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>季節<rt>きせつ</rt></ruby>**によって**<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う</code> or <code><ruby>異<rt>こと</rt></ruby>なる</code> after `によって` is the fixed frame for "varies with". Note that `によっては` would be wrong here: you are describing the whole range of seasons, not singling out a few of them.

---

## によって / による · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>によって<ruby>被害<rt>ひがい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べる<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>たちが<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>った。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>**による**<ruby>被害<rt>ひがい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べる<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>たちが<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>った。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>被害<rt>ひがい</rt></ruby></code> is a noun, and `によって` cannot sit directly in front of one. Left as it was, the sentence tries to attach 「<ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>によって」 to <code><ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べる</code>, which would mean the earthquake did the investigating.

---

## によって / による · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>の<ruby>宿<rt>やど</rt></ruby>は<ruby>時期<rt>じき</rt></ruby>**によって**<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>います。 (b) この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>の<ruby>宿<rt>やど</rt></ruby>は<ruby>時期<rt>じき</rt></ruby>**によっては**<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>倍<rt>ばい</rt></ruby>になります。

{reveal}
(a) is a general statement about the whole year: whatever season you name, the price attached to it is a different one. It is the answer to "is the price always the same?"

{reveal}
(b) reaches into that range and pulls out the extreme cases - there exist periods, not all of them, when the price doubles. The `は` is doing its usual contrastive work, and that is why the sentence needs a striking predicate like <code><ruby>倍<rt>ばい</rt></ruby>になる</code> rather than a flat <code><ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う</code>.

# にかわって / にかわり

**Meaning** · in place of; standing in for; taking over from

Read `にかわって`, and written ～に<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わって when kanji is used. Two situations, one pattern: a **person** doing something that somebody else was supposed to do, and a **thing** taking over the role another thing used to fill.

---

## にかわって / にかわり · Formation

The pattern attaches only to nouns. `にかわり` is the written form of exactly the same thing.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (person) - filling in | Noun + にかわって | <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby> → <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>にかわって<ruby>出席<rt>しゅっせき</rt></ruby>する |
| Noun (thing) - superseding | Noun + にかわって | ろうそく → ろうそくにかわって<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>われる |
| Written notices, reports | Noun + にかわり | <ruby>担当者<rt>たんとうしゃ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>担当者<rt>たんとうしゃ</rt></ruby>にかわりご<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>します |
| Formal spoken, ceremonies | Noun + にかわりまして | <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>にかわりましてお<ruby>礼<rt>れい</rt></ruby><ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げます |
| Before a noun | Noun + にかわる + Noun | <ruby>石油<rt>せきゆ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>石油<rt>せきゆ</rt></ruby>にかわるエネルギー |

---

## にかわって / にかわり · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the kanji is `代` (substitute), not `変` (change). <code><ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わって</code> is a different verb altogether and produces a different sentence; an IME will happily offer you the wrong one. If you are unsure, kana is safe.

---

## にかわって / にかわり · Examples

<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>**にかわって**<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*I'll attend the meeting in my father's place.*

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん**にかわって**<ruby>山口<rt>やまぐち</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>司会<rt>しかい</rt></ruby>をします。<br>*Yamaguchi will chair the session in place of Tanaka.*

けがをした<ruby>選手<rt>せんしゅ</rt></ruby>**にかわって**、<ruby>一年生<rt>いちねんせい</rt></ruby>がコートに<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>った。<br>*A first-year went onto the court in place of the injured player.*

<ruby>留守<rt>るす</rt></ruby>の<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>**にかわり**、<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>け<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>っておきました。<br>*I took delivery of the parcel on behalf of my mother, who was out.*

---

## にかわって / にかわり · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>紙<rt>かみ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>**にかわって**、スマホのアプリを<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>う<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えている。<br>*Students are increasingly using phone apps in place of paper dictionaries.*

この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>では、<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>からの<ruby>市場<rt>いちば</rt></ruby>**にかわって**<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きなスーパーが<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>になった。<br>*In this town a big supermarket has taken over from the old market as the centre of shopping.*

<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>**にかわりまして**、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby><ruby>中村<rt>なかむら</rt></ruby>がご<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>いたします。<br>*Today I, Nakamura, will give the presentation in the department manager's place.*

<ruby>石油<rt>せきゆ</rt></ruby>**にかわる**エネルギーを<ruby>探<rt>さが</rt></ruby>す<ruby>研究<rt>けんきゅう</rt></ruby>が、<ruby>世界中<rt>せかいじゅう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>められている。<br>*Research into an energy source to replace oil is under way all over the world.*

---

## にかわって / にかわり · Nuance & register

This is **formal, mostly written** Japanese. Between friends it sounds like a press release: to ask someone to watch your bag while you step out, you want <code><ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに</code>, not `にかわって`.

`にかわり` narrows the register further - notices, minutes, business email. And `にかわりまして` is the ceremonial form you hear at weddings, funerals, opening speeches and company presentations, where somebody speaks for an absent superior or for a family. 「<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>にかわりまして、ひとことごあいさつ<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げます」 is about as fixed as a phrase gets.

---

## にかわって / にかわり · Nuance & register (cont.)

The "thing supersedes thing" use belongs to news and essay writing, and it almost always ends in a verb of change: <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える, <ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>がる, <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>われるようになる, <ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>になる. A static predicate after it sounds unfinished.

Note also the order. In `Aにかわって B`, **A is the one being replaced** and B is the newcomer. Learners reverse this constantly, and because both halves are plausible nouns the mistake produces a perfectly grammatical sentence that means the opposite of what you intended.

---

## にかわって / にかわり · にかわって vs 代わりに

> **Rule of thumb** - same substitution meaning, but `にかわって` is formal and attaches only to nouns, while <code><ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに</code> is everyday, attaches to verbs and adjectives too, and has a second sense - "in exchange for" - that `にかわって` does not have at all.

ちょっと<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>外<rt>はず</rt></ruby>すので、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに**<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>てもらえますか。<br>*I'm stepping out for a moment - could you take any calls for me?*

An office favour asked with 「<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>にかわって<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>てください」 is not wrong, but it lands with the weight of an official handover.

The exchange sense is where the two genuinely part company:

---

## にかわって / にかわり · にかわって vs 代わりに (cont.)

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しを<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>う**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに**、ごはんをおごってね。<br>*I'll help you move - you're buying dinner in return.*

Nothing here is standing in for anything, so `にかわって` is simply unavailable.

---

## にかわって / にかわり · にかわって vs として

> **Rule of thumb** - `にかわって` says the person who should be doing this is absent. `として` says you hold the role in your own right.

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>**にかわって**<ruby>報告<rt>ほうこく</rt></ruby>いたします。<br>*I'll deliver the report in the section chief's place.*

チームの<ruby>代表<rt>だいひょう</rt></ruby>**として**<ruby>報告<rt>ほうこく</rt></ruby>いたします。<br>*I'll deliver the report as the team's representative.*

---

## にかわって / にかわり · にかわって vs として (cont.)

The first implies an empty chair; the second implies a title. Combining them is possible and common in speeches - 「<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>代表<rt>だいひょう</rt></ruby>し、<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>にかわりましてごあいさつ<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げます」 - precisely because they say different things.

---

## にかわって / にかわり · AにかわってB vs AがBに変わる

> **Rule of thumb** - the two patterns put opposite things in front of `に`. In `にかわって`, the noun before `に` is what gets **replaced**. In <code>～に<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わる</code>, the noun before `に` is what something **turns into**.

<ruby>現金<rt>げんきん</rt></ruby>**にかわって**カードで<ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>う<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>くなった。<br>*More and more people pay by card rather than cash.*

<ruby>信号<rt>しんごう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>青<rt>あお</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わった**ので、<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*The light turned green, so the cars started moving.*

---

## にかわって / にかわり · AにかわってB vs AがBに変わる (cont.)

In the first, cash is on the way out. In the second, green is what has arrived. Reading the second pattern into the first is the single most productive source of back-to-front sentences with `にかわって`.

---

## にかわって / にかわり · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>石炭<rt>せきたん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>石油<rt>せきゆ</rt></ruby>**にかわって**<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>われるようになった。（meaning: oil replaced coal） → ✅ <ruby>石炭<rt>せきたん</rt></ruby>**にかわって**<ruby>石油<rt>せきゆ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>われるようになった。<br>*The noun before `に` is the one being pushed out. As written, the first sentence says coal replaced oil.*

- ❌ <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**にかわって**、ネットで<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>した。 → ✅ <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに**、ネットで<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>した。<br>*`にかわって` takes a noun and nothing else. - ❌ <ruby>皿<rt>さら</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ってあげる**にかわって**、アイスを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>って。 → ✅ <ruby>皿<rt>さら</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ってあげる**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに**、アイスを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>って。<br>*There is no "in exchange for" sense in `にかわって`, even with a noun in front of it.*

# 代わりに

**Meaning** · instead of; in place of - and, in its other life, in return for

Read <code><ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに</code>. Two senses live in this one word and learners routinely melt them together. **Substitution**: A was going to fill the slot, but B fills it instead. **Compensation**: A and B are both true, and the second balances out the first. Which one you get depends almost entirely on what the word attaches to.

---

## 代わりに · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + の + <ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに | バター → バターの<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりにオリーブオイル |
| Verb (plain, non-past) | Verb + <ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに |
| い-adjective | い-adj + <ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに |
| な-adjective | な-adj + な + <ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かな<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに |
| Standalone adverb | …。<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに… | バスが<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない。<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いた |
| Sentence connector | …。その<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わり(に)、… | <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む。その<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わり<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>やる |

---

## 代わりに · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the attachment decides the sense. An **adjective** in front can only give the compensation reading, because an adjective is not something that can fill a slot for something else. A **noun** or a **verb** in front leaves both readings open, and context settles it.

## 代わりに · Examples

さとうの**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに**はちみつを<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れました。<br>*I put honey in instead of sugar.*

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに**<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>連<rt>つ</rt></ruby>れて<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I took the dog for a walk instead of my sister.*

バスが<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかったので、**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに**<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いて<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*The bus never came, so I walked home instead.*

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>家賃<rt>やちん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに**、<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>からかなり<ruby>遠<rt>とお</rt></ruby>い。<br>*This flat is cheap, but the trade-off is that it's a long way from the station.*

---

## 代わりに · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しを<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>う**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに**、<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>ごはんをごちそうしてね。<br>*I'll help you move, but you're treating me to dinner in return.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>らせてください。その**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わり**、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby><ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*Please let me leave early today - in return, I'll come in early tomorrow.*

<ruby>海外<rt>かいがい</rt></ruby><ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに**、<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>くの<ruby>温泉<rt>おんせん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>三日間<rt>みっかかん</rt></ruby>ゆっくりすることにした。<br>*Instead of going abroad, we decided to take three quiet days at a nearby hot spring.*

---

## 代わりに · Nuance & register

<code><ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに</code> is register-neutral. It works in conversation, in email and in writing, and neither sense sounds childish or stiff. In casual writing it is often left in kana as かわりに; in print, kanji. Watch the kanji itself - `代` is "substitute", and `変` ("change") is a different word that an IME will offer you.

The standalone connector その<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わり is extremely common in speech and usually loses its `に`: 「わかった。その<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わり、<ruby>次<rt>つぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>ね。」 It is the fastest way to propose a deal in Japanese, and it can be said with a smile or with an edge depending on tone alone.

---

## 代わりに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The compensation sense is neutral about which side is the good one. 「<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに<ruby>遠<rt>とお</rt></ruby>い」 and 「<ruby>遠<rt>とお</rt></ruby>い<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い」 are both fine; the word only asserts that the two facts come as a package. What it will not do is join two facts that have nothing to do with each other - there must be a felt balance between them.

When the exchange is a favour you are asking for, <code><ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに</code> makes the bargain explicit, which is friendly among equals and blunt with a superior. Softened forms - 「<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>いますので、その<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりと<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っては<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>ですが…」 - exist precisely because the bare version is so direct.

---

## 代わりに · Nuance & register (cont.)

For business and ceremonial situations, the formal counterpart of the substitution sense is `にかわって` / `にかわりまして`, not this word.

---

## 代わりに · Substitution 代わりに vs compensation 代わりに

> **Rule of thumb** - if the two things **compete for one slot** (only one of them actually happens), it is substitution. If **both are true** and the second offsets the first, it is compensation. An adjective in front forces the second reading.

Substitution - the honey is in the cake, the sugar is not:

<ruby>牛乳<rt>ぎゅうにゅう</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに**<ruby>豆乳<rt>とうにゅう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>っても<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>れます。<br>*You can make it with soy milk in place of cow's milk.*

Compensation - both halves happened:

<ruby>給料<rt>きゅうりょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>ない**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに**、<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みがきちんと<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>れる。<br>*The pay is low, but on the other side of it you can actually take your holidays.*

---

## 代わりに · Substitution 代わりに vs compensation 代わりに (cont.)

With a verb, context decides. 「<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>する<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>が<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ていた」 is substitution (one of us drove). 「<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>する<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんをおごってね」 is compensation (I drive *and* you pay). The tell is whether the second half is a **consequence for the other person** - if it is, you are trading, not substituting.

---

## 代わりに · 代わりに vs にかわって

> **Rule of thumb** - the same substitution meaning at a different altitude. `にかわって` is formal, attaches only to nouns, and has no compensation sense.

<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだ<ruby>同僚<rt>どうりょう</rt></ruby>**にかわって**、<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>つことになった。<br>*I ended up on the front desk in place of a colleague who called in sick.*

Said to a friend that evening, the same fact comes out as 「<ruby>同僚<rt>どうりょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>やらされた」. Neither is more correct; they belong to different rooms.

---

## 代わりに · 代わりに vs ～ないで・～ずに

> **Rule of thumb** - English "instead of doing X" is `～ないで` when X is simply **not done**. <code><ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに</code> is only right when X and Y are genuine alternatives for one job, or when they trade off against each other.

<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べないで**<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I went to school without eating breakfast.*

❌<code><ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました</code> is odd because going to school is not an alternative way of having breakfast. Give the two things the same job and the pattern comes back:

---

## 代わりに · 代わりに vs ～ないで・～ずに (cont.)

<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんの**<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに**バナナを<ruby>一本<rt>いっぽん</rt></ruby><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました。<br>*I had a banana in place of breakfast.*

---

# と共に

**Meaning** · together with; at the same moment as; as one thing goes, so goes the other

Read <code>と<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に</code>, and just as often written in kana as とともに. It starts as a formal replacement for <code>と<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に</code>, but it stretches much further than that: it can join two events at a point in time, track two things that move in step, and pin two properties onto one subject at once.

---

<ruby>工場<rt>こうじょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>郊外<rt>こうがい</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>移<rt>うつ</rt></ruby>ると**<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>駅前<rt>えきまえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>商店街<rt>しょうてんがい</rt></ruby>も<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しずつ<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになっていった。<br>*As the factory moved out to the suburbs, the shopping street by the station gradually grew quieter too.*

---

## と共に · Nuance & register

<code>と<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に</code> belongs to **written and formal spoken** Japanese: essays, reports, news copy, and speeches. In everyday conversation the accompaniment sense sounds comically grand - 「<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちとともにラーメンを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた」 is the sort of thing said as a joke. Use <code>と<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に</code> there.

The word <code><ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に</code> also lives on its own as an adverb meaning "together", and it turns up in set encouragements: 「<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に<ruby>頑張<rt>がんば</rt></ruby>りましょう」, 「<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に<ruby>歩<rt>あゆ</rt></ruby>む」. This is speech-making register, warm and slightly old-fashioned, and it is one of the few places the pattern feels emotional rather than neutral.

---

## と共に · Nuance & register (cont.)

The <code>であると<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に</code> use - "A and also B" - is firmly essay language. It sets two roles or two qualities side by side and is usually followed by `～でもある` or `～でもあった`. In speech you would say 「<ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>でもあり、<ruby>作家<rt>さっか</rt></ruby>でもある」 instead.

One point of care with the "in step" sense: both halves have to be things that can **move**. Growing older, prices rising, technology advancing, a season turning - these work. A fixed fact does not: 「<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いとともに」 is not Japanese.

---

## と共に · と共に vs と一緒に

> **Rule of thumb** - for plain accompaniment they mean the same thing; <code>と<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に</code> is spoken and neutral, <code>と<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に</code> is written and ceremonious. Only <code>と<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に</code> can do simultaneity and change-in-step.

<ruby>昼休<rt>ひるやす</rt></ruby>みに<ruby>同僚<rt>どうりょう</rt></ruby>と**<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>弁当<rt>べんとう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた。<br>*I ate my packed lunch with a colleague at lunchtime.*

<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>成長<rt>せいちょう</rt></ruby>と**<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>社員<rt>しゃいん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>数<rt>かず</rt></ruby>も<ruby>三倍<rt>さんばい</rt></ruby>になった。<br>*As the company grew, the number of employees tripled along with it.*

Substituting <code>と<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に</code> into the second sentence fails: growth is not a companion you do things alongside.

---

## と共に · と共に vs につれて・にしたがって

> **Rule of thumb** - `につれて` needs **gradual, continuous change on both sides**. <code>と<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に</code> also accepts a single moment or a one-off event. `にしたがって` overlaps heavily with `につれて`, but it has a second, unrelated everyday sense: "in accordance with (instructions)".

Gradual on both sides - all three are possible, and `につれて` is the most idiomatic:

<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む**につれて**、<ruby>応援<rt>おうえん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなっていった。<br>*As the match went on, the cheering grew louder and louder.*

A single moment - only <code>と<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に</code> works:

---

## と共に · と共に vs につれて・にしたがって (cont.)

<ruby>開店<rt>かいてん</rt></ruby>と**<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>次々<rt>つぎつぎ</rt></ruby>と<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってきた。<br>*The moment the shop opened, customers began pouring in.*

And the other `にしたがって`, which has nothing to do with gradual change at all:

<ruby>係員<rt>かかりいん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>指示<rt>しじ</rt></ruby>**にしたがって**、<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>てください。<br>*Please go outside in accordance with the staff's instructions.*

Be honest with yourself about the first group: in the gradual-change sense, `につれて` and `にしたがって` are very close, and most sentences accept either. `にしたがって` is a shade more formal and is the one you meet in written reports.

---

## と共に · と共に vs と同時に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>と<ruby>同時<rt>どうじ</rt></ruby>に</code> pins two things to the **same instant**. <code>と<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に</code> can do that too, but it also stretches over a period, which <code>と<ruby>同時<rt>どうじ</rt></ruby>に</code> does not.

ベルが<ruby>鳴<rt>な</rt></ruby>る**と<ruby>同時<rt>どうじ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>生徒<rt>せいと</rt></ruby>たちが<ruby>廊下<rt>ろうか</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>び<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*The instant the bell rang, the pupils shot out into the corridor.*

<ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>経<rt>た</rt></ruby>つ**と<ruby>共<rt>とも</rt></ruby>に**、あのときの<ruby>悔<rt>くや</rt></ruby>しさもうすれてきた。<br>*As time has passed, the bitterness of that day has faded.*

Put <code>と<ruby>同時<rt>どうじ</rt></ruby>に</code> in the second sentence and it claims that time passing and the feeling fading happened at one identifiable moment, which is not what anyone means.

---

# を通じて / を通して

**Meaning** · by way of - through a go-between, a channel or an experience; or right through a stretch of time

Read <code>を<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて</code> and <code>を<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して</code>. Same kanji, two readings, and - this is the honest part - very nearly the same meaning. Both put something in the middle of you and your goal: a person who passes the message on, a medium that carries it, an experience you came out the other side of. Both also mark a period of time that a state holds right across.

---

## を通じて / を通して · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|

## を通じて / を通して · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the last row is the one place the two really do split. <code>を<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して</code> keeps the literal sense of the verb <code><ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>す</code>, "to pass something through", so light, sound and air can travel through things with it. <code>を<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて</code> has lost that literal meaning entirely.

## を通じて / を通して · Examples

---

## を通じて / を通して · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>市<rt>し</rt></ruby>の<ruby>窓口<rt>まどぐち</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて**<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>めば、<ruby>費用<rt>ひよう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>半分<rt>はんぶん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>済<rt>す</rt></ruby>みます。<br>*If you apply via the city office counter, it costs only half as much.*

<ruby>通訳<rt>つうやく</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>すと、どうしても<ruby>細<rt>こま</rt></ruby>かい<ruby>気持<rt>きも</rt></ruby>ちまでは<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>わりにくい。<br>*When you speak through an interpreter, the finer feelings inevitably fail to carry.*

あの<ruby>事件<rt>じけん</rt></ruby>のことは、<ruby>新聞<rt>しんぶん</rt></ruby>やテレビの<ruby>報道<rt>ほうどう</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて**<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>った<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>がほとんどだろう。<br>*Most people will have learned of that incident through newspaper and television coverage.*

---

## を通じて / を通して · Nuance & register

Both forms are a step above casual speech. When the "channel" is an ordinary everyday tool, Japanese uses plain `で`: 「メールで<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ります」, not 「メールを<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ります」. Reach for these patterns when the point is that something had to **pass through** an intermediary, or when you are writing.

The difference between the two is real but slight, and it is a matter of tendency rather than rule. Where speakers do feel a difference:

---

## を通じて / を通して · Nuance & register (cont.)

- <code>を<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して</code> leans towards the subject **actively using** the medium,<br>*and towards experience: <code><ruby>経験<rt>けいけん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して</code>, <code><ruby>活動<rt>かつどう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して</code>, <code><ruby>三年間<rt>さんねんかん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して</code>. It is a little more at home in speech.*
- <code>を<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて</code> leans towards **routing and connection**, and towards<br>*written and journalistic style: <code>マスコミを<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて</code>, <code><ruby>代理店<rt>だいりてん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて</code>, <code><ruby>一年<rt>いちねん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて</code>.*

Say plainly what is true: in the great majority of sentences either is acceptable, native speakers disagree about which sounds better, and no examiner can build a fair question on the choice alone. The one place you can be confident is the literal physical sense - light through a window, a voice through a wall - which belongs to <code>を<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して</code> only.

---

## を通じて / を通して · Nuance & register (cont.)

Before a noun, both need `の`: <code><ruby>友人<rt>ゆうじん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じての<ruby>紹介<rt>しょうかい</rt></ruby></code>. This form is stiff and mostly appears in notices and forms.

---

## を通じて / を通して · を通じて vs を通して

> **Rule of thumb** - in the go-between, channel and time-span senses, treat them as interchangeable. Prefer <code>を<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して</code> when the subject deliberately works *through* something, and when the passage is physical.

<ruby>部活<rt>ぶかつ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して**、<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>頼<rt>たよ</rt></ruby>ることを<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えた。<br>*Through club activities I learned how to rely on other people.*

<ruby>大使館<rt>たいしかん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて**<ruby>正式<rt>せいしき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れがあった。<br>*A formal approach was made through the embassy.*

---

## を通じて / を通して · を通じて vs を通して (cont.)

Each sentence would survive the swap. The first has a person putting themselves through an experience, which suits <code><ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して</code>; the second is a routed, official message, which suits <code><ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて</code>. Those are leanings, not laws.

The genuine split:

すりガラスを**<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して**、<ruby>人影<rt>ひとかげ</rt></ruby>がぼんやりと<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えた。<br>*A figure showed dimly through the frosted glass.*

❌<code>すりガラスを<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて</code> does not work - nothing is being routed, something is physically passing through.

---

## を通じて / を通して · を通して vs で

> **Rule of thumb** - `で` names the tool you used. These patterns name a **path something took**, and they imply that the path mattered.

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>件<rt>けん</rt></ruby>はメール**で**<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らせておきました。<br>*I let them know about yesterday's business by email.*

<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えは、<ruby>秘書<rt>ひしょ</rt></ruby>**を<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して**<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えられた。<br>*The president's thinking was conveyed to the manager through his secretary.*

---

## を通じて / を通して · を通して vs で (cont.)

Replace `で` in the first sentence and it starts to sound as though the email were a diplomatic channel. Replace <code>を<ruby>通<rt>とお</rt></ruby>して</code> in the second with `で` and the secretary becomes an instrument rather than a person passing a message on.

---

## を通じて / を通して · を通じて (a period) vs にわたって

> **Rule of thumb** - with a stretch of time, <code>を<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて</code> says a state held at **every point inside** it. `にわたって` says an **event spanned** it from one end to the other.

この<ruby>地方<rt>ちほう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて**<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>ない。<br>*This region gets little snow right through the winter.*

<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いは<ruby>三日間<rt>みっかかん</rt></ruby>**にわたって**<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けられた。<br>*The talks went on over three days.*

---

## を通じて / を通して · を通じて (a period) vs にわたって (cont.)

The first describes a condition you could check on any given day; the second measures how far a single continuous event reached. A one-off event with <code>を<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて</code> - ❌「<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>を<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて<ruby>開<rt>ひら</rt></ruby>かれた」 - sounds wrong for exactly that reason.

---

## を通じて / を通して · Common mistakes

## を通じて / を通して · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>二日<rt>ふつか</rt></ruby>を<ruby>通<rt>つう</rt></ruby>じて<ruby>行<rt>おこな</rt></ruby>われた。 → ✅ <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>二日間<rt>ふつかかん</rt></ruby>**にわたって**<ruby>行<rt>おこな</rt></ruby>われた。<br>*A bounded event spanning a period wants `にわたって`. # を中心に

**Meaning** · with X at the centre of it; centring on X; mainly X

Read <code>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に</code>. The noun before it is the **core**, and everything else in the sentence is arranged around that core - physically, as a circle on a map; or in terms of weight, as the part of an activity that gets most of the attention.

---

## を中心に · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に | <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby> → <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>がる |
| Noun (stiffer, written) | Noun + を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>として | <ruby>若者<rt>わかもの</rt></ruby> → <ruby>若者<rt>わかもの</rt></ruby>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>として<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>まる |
| Noun (deliberate arrangement) | Noun + を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>にして | <ruby>文法<rt>ぶんぽう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>文法<rt>ぶんぽう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>にして<ruby>復習<rt>ふくしゅう</rt></ruby>する |
| Before a noun | Noun + を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>とする／とした + Noun | <ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby> → <ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>とする<ruby>研究<rt>けんきゅう</rt></ruby>チーム |

## を中心に · Nuance & register

The pattern is at home in **written and semi-formal** Japanese - reports, weather bulletins, class plans, meeting agendas - but unlike many N3 noun patterns it is not stiff. 「<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>文法<rt>ぶんぽう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>にやります」 is a perfectly ordinary thing for a teacher to say out loud.

The three variants shade upward in formality: <code>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に</code> is the plain one, <code>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>にして</code> puts a little more weight on the arranging being deliberate, and <code>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>として</code> is the essay-and-report form. Nothing turns on the choice; use the first unless you are writing.

---

## を中心に · Nuance & register (cont.)

<code><ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby></code> is still an ordinary noun meaning "centre", so the literal reading never quite goes away. That is a feature: 「<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>る」 genuinely pictures rain spreading outward from a point on the map, and weather forecasts use it exactly that way. When the noun is a group of people rather than a place, the picture becomes one of concentration - the thing is happening most intensely there and thinning out at the edges.

One consequence worth noticing: the pattern implies that **other things are also included**, just less prominently. If a course covers grammar and nothing else, 「<ruby>文法<rt>ぶんぽう</rt></ruby>**だけ**」 is the honest wording; <code>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に</code> promises a core plus a periphery.

---

## を中心に · を中心に vs 主に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>主<rt>おも</rt></ruby>に</code> is a plain adverb meaning "mostly". <code>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に</code> takes the thing as its object and adds the picture of a core with other material arranged around it.

この<ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>では**<ruby>主<rt>おも</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>をします。<br>*In this class we mostly do speaking practice.*

この<ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>では<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>き<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>りもやります。<br>*This class centres on speaking practice, with listening as well.*

The second sentence needs the rest of the field to exist; the first does not care. That is why <code>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に</code> so often has a `も` clause after it.

---

## を中心に · を中心に vs をはじめ

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に</code> ranks by **weight**: X is the biggest part. `をはじめ` ranks by **order of mention**: X is the first and most representative member of a group, and the group is then named.

<ruby>関東<rt>かんとう</rt></ruby><ruby>地方<rt>ちほう</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>い<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>吹<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>いている。<br>*Strong winds have been blowing since morning, centred on the Kanto region.*

<ruby>関東<rt>かんとう</rt></ruby><ruby>地方<rt>ちほう</rt></ruby>を**はじめ**、<ruby>全国<rt>ぜんこく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>くの<ruby>地域<rt>ちいき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>まっている。<br>*Winds are strengthening in many regions across the country, Kanto among the first of them.*

---

## を中心に · を中心に vs をはじめ (cont.)

The first sentence maps intensity; the second opens a list. Swapping them produces sentences that are grammatical and slightly beside the point.

---

## を中心に · を中心に vs をめぐって

> **Rule of thumb** - `をめぐって` means the thing is the **point at issue**, and it usually brings disagreement with it. <code>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に</code> is neutral about whether anyone is arguing.

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>規則<rt>きそく</rt></ruby>を**めぐって**、<ruby>社内<rt>しゃない</rt></ruby>で<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かれている。<br>*Opinion inside the company is split over the new rules.*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>規則<rt>きそく</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>があった。<br>*There was about an hour of explanation, focused on the new rules.*

`をめぐって` implies parties pulling in different directions; if all you mean is that a topic took up most of the time, it is the wrong word.

---

## を中心に · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>文法<rt>ぶんぽう</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>します。 → ✅ <ruby>文法<rt>ぶんぽう</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>します。<br>*The particle is `を`. The core is treated as something you *take* as the centre, not somewhere you put yourself.*

- ❌ この<ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大学生<rt>だいがくせい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まりです。 → ✅ この<ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大学生<rt>だいがくせい</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>とした**<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まりです。<br>*<code>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に</code> needs a verb to modify. In front of a noun the pattern must become <code><ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>とした</code> or <code><ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>とする</code>.*

- ❌ この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>両国<rt>りょうこく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>激<rt>はげ</rt></ruby>しく<ruby>対立<rt>たいりつ</rt></ruby>している。 → ✅ この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>を**めぐって**、<ruby>両国<rt>りょうこく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>激<rt>はげ</rt></ruby>しく<ruby>対立<rt>たいりつ</rt></ruby>している。<br>*A dispute is fought *over* something, not *around* it.*

---

## を中心に · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べています。<ruby>肉<rt>にく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一切<rt>いっさい</rt></ruby><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べません。 → ✅ <ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>**だけ**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べています。<br>*<code>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に</code> promises that other things are in the picture too. If there is no periphery, the pattern contradicts the next sentence.*

# をはじめ / をはじめとする

**Meaning** · starting with X - X being the leading member of a group, with the rest following

Written ～を<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>め in kanji, though kana is now more usual. The pattern names one item as the **most prominent or most representative** of a set, and then goes on to talk about the set as a whole. It is a listing device, not a substitute for "for example": what follows it has to be the group X belongs to.

---

## をはじめ / をはじめとする · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + をはじめ、… | <ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby> → <ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>をはじめ、<ruby>各地<rt>かくち</rt></ruby>から<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まる |
| Noun (formal, written) | Noun + をはじめとして、… | <ruby>校長<rt>こうちょう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>校長<rt>こうちょう</rt></ruby>をはじめとして、<ruby>先生方<rt>せんせいがた</rt></ruby>が<ruby>並<rt>なら</rt></ruby>んだ |
| Before a noun | Noun + をはじめとする + Noun | <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>をはじめとする<ruby>大都市<rt>だいとし</rt></ruby> |
| Before a noun (alternative) | Noun + をはじめとした + Noun | パンダ → パンダをはじめとした<ruby>動物<rt>どうぶつ</rt></ruby>たち |

---

## をはじめ / をはじめとする · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the clause after the pattern must state the wider group, usually with a quantity word: <ruby>全員<rt>ぜんいん</rt></ruby>, <ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>くの～, さまざまな～, ～など. A sentence that names the leading item and then never mentions the set feels cut off halfway.

---

## をはじめ / をはじめとする · Examples

<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>**をはじめ**、<ruby>社員<rt>しゃいん</rt></ruby><ruby>全員<rt>ぜんいん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>式<rt>しき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*The whole staff attended the ceremony, the president included.*

パンダ**をはじめ**、この<ruby>動物園<rt>どうぶつえん</rt></ruby>には<ruby>珍<rt>めずら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>動物<rt>どうぶつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い。<br>*Starting with the pandas, this zoo has a lot of unusual animals.*

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>では、<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>**をはじめとする**<ruby>大都市<rt>だいとし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>人口<rt>じんこう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まっている。<br>*In Japan the population is concentrated in the big cities, Tokyo foremost among them.*

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>では、<ruby>米<rt>こめ</rt></ruby>や<ruby>味噌<rt>みそ</rt></ruby>**をはじめとする**<ruby>地元<rt>じもと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>品<rt>しな</rt></ruby>を<ruby>売<rt>う</rt></ruby>っている。<br>*This shop sells local produce - rice and miso and much else besides.*

---

## をはじめ / をはじめとする · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby><ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**をはじめとして**、お<ruby>世話<rt>せわ</rt></ruby>になった<ruby>方々<rt>かたがた</rt></ruby>に<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いた。<br>*I wrote to everyone who had looked after me, beginning with Tanaka-sensei.*

<ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>みには、<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>の<ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>**をはじめ**、<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもが<ruby>巻<rt>ま</rt></ruby>き<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>まれる<ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える。<br>*Over the summer holidays, accidents involving children - drownings first of all - increase.*

<ruby>今回<rt>こんかい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>大会<rt>たいかい</rt></ruby>には、アジア<ruby>各国<rt>かっこく</rt></ruby>**をはじめとする**<ruby>三十<rt>さんじゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>以上<rt>いじょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>と<ruby>地域<rt>ちいき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>参加<rt>さんか</rt></ruby>する。<br>*More than thirty countries and territories will take part in this tournament, the nations of Asia among the first of them.*

---

## をはじめ / をはじめとする · Examples (cont.)

この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>江戸<rt>えど</rt></ruby><ruby>時代<rt>じだい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>旅館<rt>りょかん</rt></ruby>**をはじめ**、<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>のままの<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>が<ruby>数多<rt>かずおお</rt></ruby>く<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>っている<ruby>珍<rt>めずら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>場所<rt>ばしょ</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*This is an unusual town where a great many buildings survive unchanged from the past, an Edo-period inn above all.*

---

## をはじめ / をはじめとする · Nuance & register

This is **formal, largely written** Japanese: guidebooks, news reports, company announcements, and the opening of speeches. It is not something you say about your evening. 「<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>はビールをはじめ、いろいろ<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだ」 sounds like a press release about a night out; 「ビールとか、いろいろ<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだ」 is what a person says.

It is especially common in **thanks and greetings**, where naming the highest- ranking person first and then sweeping in everyone else is a fixed courtesy: 「<ruby>会長<rt>かいちょう</rt></ruby>をはじめ、<ruby>皆様<rt>みなさま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>よりお<ruby>礼<rt>れい</rt></ruby><ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げます」. Getting the order right matters - the named person should genuinely be the most senior or the most representative, and putting a junior name there reads as a slight.

---

## をはじめ / をはじめとする · Nuance & register (cont.)

On the kanji: modern writing usually leaves it in kana. If you do write it, it is `始` (as in <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code>, "to begin"), never `初`. And keep it apart from <code><ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて</code>, "for the first time" - a different word that sounds almost the same and produces nonsense if it slips in here.

Of the four forms, `をはじめ` is the everyday one, `をはじめとして` the most formal, and `をはじめとする` / `をはじめとした` are what you use in front of a noun. There is no meaning difference between the last two.

---

## をはじめ / をはじめとする · をはじめ vs など

> **Rule of thumb** - `など` only says the list is partial. `をはじめ` additionally **ranks**: the named item leads the group, and the group has to be stated afterwards.

<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>には、ペンやノート**など**が<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>いてあった。<br>*There were pens, notebooks and so on lying on the desk.*

この<ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>には、<ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>い<ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>**をはじめ**、<ruby>貴重<rt>きちょう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>がそろっている。<br>*This library holds a full range of valuable materials, old maps above all.*

Nothing about the pens is special; the old maps are the library's pride. The two often appear together - 「Aをはじめ、B や C など」 - with `をはじめ` picking the headline item and `など` closing the list off.

---

## をはじめ / をはじめとする · をはじめ vs を中心に

> **Rule of thumb** - `をはじめ` opens a **list**; <code>を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に</code> describes a **distribution**, with the named thing carrying the most weight and everything else thinning out around it.

<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>のうちに<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>もった。<br>*Snow settled overnight, most heavily up the mountain.*

<ruby>北<rt>きた</rt></ruby>の<ruby>地方<rt>ちほう</rt></ruby>**をはじめ**、<ruby>各地<rt>かくち</rt></ruby>で<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>もった。<br>*Snow settled in various places, in the northern regions first of all.*

The first is a map with a hot spot. The second is a roll call. `をはじめ` needs the group to be countable in some sense; a gradient of intensity is <code><ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby></code>'s job.

---

## をはじめ / をはじめとする · をはじめ vs 例えば

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>例<rt>たと</rt></ruby>えば</code> is an adverb that opens a clause and introduces an illustration. `をはじめ` is a postposition that sits on a noun inside the sentence, and it cannot stand on its own.

<ruby>果物<rt>くだもの</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。**<ruby>例<rt>たと</rt></ruby>えば**、りんごやぶどうをよく<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います。<br>*I like fruit - apples and grapes, for instance, are things I buy often.*

りんご**をはじめ**、<ruby>秋<rt>あき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>果物<rt>くだもの</rt></ruby>はどれも<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くなってきた。<br>*Autumn fruit has all come down in price, apples first among them.*

---

## をはじめ / をはじめとする · をはじめ vs 例えば (cont.)

<code><ruby>例<rt>たと</rt></ruby>えば</code> picks a random illustration and makes no claim about importance. `をはじめ` claims the item is the leading one, and it needs the group in the same sentence. ❌「をはじめ、りんごが<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです」 is not a sentence.

---

## をはじめ / をはじめとする · Common mistakes

- ❌ ケーキ**をはじめて**、<ruby>甘<rt>あま</rt></ruby>いものが<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。 → ✅ ケーキ**をはじめ**、<ruby>甘<rt>あま</rt></ruby>いものが<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*<code><ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて</code> ("for the first time") is a different word. The pattern ends in はじめ, or in はじめとして.*

- ❌ <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>をはじめ<ruby>都市<rt>とし</rt></ruby>で<ruby>家賃<rt>やちん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がっている。 → ✅ <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>**をはじめとする**<ruby>都市<rt>とし</rt></ruby>で<ruby>家賃<rt>やちん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がっている。<br>*In front of a noun the pattern needs `とする` or `とした`. Without it the reader stumbles over which word <code><ruby>都市<rt>とし</rt></ruby></code> belongs to.*

- ❌ カレー**をはじめ**、パンも<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。 → ✅ カレー**をはじめ**、<ruby>辛<rt>から</rt></ruby>い<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*The named item has to be a member of the group that follows. Curry does not lead a list whose next entry is bread.*

---

## をはじめ / をはじめとする · Common mistakes (cont.)

**2.** Choose: この<ruby>地方<rt>ちほう</rt></ruby>は、りんご（をはじめ・を<ruby>中心<rt>ちゅうしん</rt></ruby>に）<ruby>果物<rt>くだもの</rt></ruby>の<ruby>産地<rt>さんち</rt></ruby>として<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>られている。

# をこめて

**Meaning** · with X poured into it - putting a feeling, a wish or physical force into what you make or do

Written ～を<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>めて when kanji is used. The verb <code><ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>める</code> means to pack something into a container, and that is the picture: you are packing a feeling into an object or an action. The English gloss "filled with" is a trap - this pattern is never about ordinary physical fullness.

---

## をこめて · Formation

The noun in front comes from a small, closed set: feelings, wishes, and force.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (feeling) | Noun + をこめて + verb | <ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>をこめて<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>る |
| Noun (wish, prayer) | Noun + をこめて + verb | <ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いをこめて<ruby>贈<rt>おく</rt></ruby>る |
| Noun (physical force) | Noun + をこめて + verb | <ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby> → <ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>をこめて<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>す |
| Longer feeling phrase | Noun + の<ruby>気持<rt>きも</rt></ruby>ち + をこめて | <ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>気持<rt>きも</rt></ruby>ちをこめて |
| Before a noun | Noun + をこめた + Noun | <ruby>愛情<rt>あいじょう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>愛情<rt>あいじょう</rt></ruby>をこめた<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby> |
| Passive | Noun + がこめられている | <ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いがこめられている |

---

## をこめて · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the nouns that work here are few enough to learn as a list: <ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>愛<rt>あい</rt></ruby>, <ruby>愛情<rt>あいじょう</rt></ruby>, <ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い, <ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>い, <ruby>祈<rt>いの</rt></ruby>り, <ruby>気持<rt>きも</rt></ruby>ち, <ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>. If your noun is not an emotion, a wish or force, the pattern is almost certainly the wrong one.

---

## をこめて · Examples

<ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>**をこめて**ケーキを<ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I baked the cake with all my heart.*

<ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>**をこめて**<ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いドアを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>した。<br>*I pushed the heavy door with all my strength.*

<ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>気持<rt>きも</rt></ruby>ち**をこめて**、<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>を<ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>した。<br>*We handed the teacher flowers as a token of our thanks.*

<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>は<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>愛情<rt>あいじょう</rt></ruby>**をこめて**お<ruby>弁当<rt>べんとう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ってくれた。<br>*Every morning my mother made me a packed lunch with real love in it.*

---

## をこめて · Examples (cont.)

「<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>になってね」という<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>い**をこめて**、<ruby>折<rt>お</rt></ruby>り<ruby>鶴<rt>づる</rt></ruby>を<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>けた。<br>*We sent origami cranes, carrying the wish that she'd get well soon.*

この<ruby>曲<rt>きょく</rt></ruby>には、<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>った<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>い<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い**がこめられて**いる。<br>*There is a powerful feeling packed into this piece of music.*

<ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>する<ruby>先輩<rt>せんぱい</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>**をこめた**アルバムをみんなで<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>った。<br>*We all made an album, full of our thanks, for the graduating seniors.*

<ruby>優勝<rt>ゆうしょう</rt></ruby>してほしいという<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>い**をこめて**、<ruby>応援席<rt>おうえんせき</rt></ruby>から<ruby>最後<rt>さいご</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けた。<br>*Willing them to win, we kept up our shouting from the stands right to the end.*

---

## をこめて · Nuance & register

The pattern is **warm and slightly literary**. It belongs to gift-giving, cards, speeches, product labelling and song lyrics, and it always implies that the speaker took care. Said about your own work it can sound either sincere or a little self-congratulatory, which is why advertising loves it: 「<ruby>職人<rt>しょくにん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>をこめて<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つずつ<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りました」 is close to a cliché on Japanese packaging.

Two of the collocations behave differently from the rest. <code><ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>をこめて</code> is plain and physical with no emotional colour at all - gripping, pushing, swinging. And `～がこめられている`, the passive, is the form you use to talk about meaning already inside something: a name, a song, a tradition. 「この<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>には<ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>の<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いがこめられている。」

---

## をこめて · Nuance & register (cont.)

In casual speech people mostly do not use it. To say a friend's cooking was made with care you would more likely say 「<ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ってあるね」. Where it does turn up in conversation is in fixed farewells and dedications - 「<ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>をこめて」 written at the bottom of a card, or announced before a performance.

Note finally that the verb after it has to be an act of **making, giving or doing**. There is nothing to pack a feeling into if the predicate is a state: ❌「<ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>をこめてうれしいです」 has no container.

---

## をこめて · をこめて vs ～でいっぱい・～だらけ

> **Rule of thumb** - the English "filled with" covers three unrelated Japanese patterns. `をこめて` is a **feeling put into an act**. Plain physical fullness is `でいっぱい`. Unpleasant covering is `だらけ`.

バケツは<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>**でいっぱい**だった。<br>*The bucket was full of water.*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>**だらけ**になっていた。<br>*The child's clothes were covered in mud.*

<ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>**をこめて**<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いた。<br>*I wrote the letter with everything I had.*

---

## をこめて · をこめて vs ～でいっぱい・～だらけ (cont.)

❌<code><ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>をこめたバケツ</code> is not a stylistic problem, it is meaningless - water is not a feeling, and a bucket is not something you make. `でいっぱい` also handles abstract fullness where no act is involved: <code><ruby>不安<rt>ふあん</rt></ruby>でいっぱいだ</code>, "I'm full of worry."

---

## をこめて · をこめて vs をもって

> **Rule of thumb** - `をもって` is formal "with / by means of". It states the manner or instrument of an official act. `をこめて` says an emotion went **into** what you produced.

<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>として、<ruby>誠意<rt>せいい</rt></ruby>**をもって**<ruby>対応<rt>たいおう</rt></ruby>してまいります。<br>*As a company, we will respond to this in good faith.*

<ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>**をこめて**、この<ruby>歌<rt>うた</rt></ruby>を<ruby>皆様<rt>みなさま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>贈<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*With our thanks, we dedicate this song to you all.*

`をもって` is business and legal register - <code><ruby>書面<rt>しょめん</rt></ruby>をもって</code>, <code><ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby>をもって</code> - and it never carries warmth. Swap the two and the first sentence becomes oddly emotional while the second turns bureaucratic.

---

## をこめて · 力をこめて vs 力を入れる

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>をこめる</code> is **physical force at a moment**. <code>～に<ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れる</code> is **effort and resources put into an area over time**.

バットに<ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>**をこめて**、<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>り<ruby>振<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I put everything into the bat and swung as hard as I could.*

<ruby>市<rt>し</rt></ruby>は<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>から<ruby>子育<rt>こそだ</rt></ruby>て<ruby>支援<rt>しえん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>**を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れて**いる。<br>*Since last year the city has been putting real effort into childcare support.*

The second is a policy, not a muscle. Note the particle too: `をこめる` takes the force as its object, while <code><ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れる</code> marks the field with `に`.

---

## をこめて · Common mistakes

- ❌ コップに<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>**をこめて**ください。 → ✅ コップに<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>**を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れて**ください。<br>*Only feelings, wishes and force can be packed in. For putting an actual substance somewhere, the verb is <code><ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れる</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>**にこめて**<ruby>歌<rt>うた</rt></ruby>います。 → ✅ <ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>**をこめて**<ruby>歌<rt>うた</rt></ruby>います。<br>*The particle is `を`. The feeling is what gets packed, not the place it goes.*

- ❌ <ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>**をこめて**<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>をもらった。 → ✅ <ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>**をこめた**<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>をもらった。<br>*In front of a noun the pattern takes `た`. As written, the sentence says *you* poured the gratitude in while receiving the letter.*

---

## をこめて · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>**をこめて**この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。 → ✅ <ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>から この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*A feeling needs something to be packed into - an act of making, giving or doing. For "from the bottom of my heart" plus a state, the phrase is <code><ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>から</code>.*

---

Handing over flowers is an act you can pack a feeling into, and <code><ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>気持<rt>きも</rt></ruby>ち</code> is one of the pattern's standard nouns. The longer <code>の<ruby>気持<rt>きも</rt></ruby>ち</code> version is slightly warmer than a bare <code><ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>をこめて</code>.

## をこめて · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>**をこめて**ロープを<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>いた。 (b) <ruby>心<rt>こころ</rt></ruby>**をこめて**ロープを<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>いた。

{reveal}

{reveal}

The pattern always asks "what is this feeling being packed into?", and an ordinary tug of a rope has no room for it.
