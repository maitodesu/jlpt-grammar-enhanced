# たとえ～ても

**Meaning** · even if - the speaker grants a supposition and says the outcome would not change

`たとえ` is an adverb parked at the head of the clause. It warns the listener in advance: *what follows is a supposition, and I am about to throw it away.* The grammar is carried by the `ても` you learned at N4; `たとえ` supplies the emphasis and the sense of "however extreme this gets."

This is the calm end of the concession family. `のに` and `くせに` react to a gap between expectation and reality that has already opened up, and they mind about it. `たとえ～ても` looks forward at a gap that has not opened yet, and announces that the speaker will not mind when it does.

---

## たとえ～ても · Formation

`たとえ` never changes shape and never attaches to anything. Everything conjugates on the `ても` side, exactly as at N4.

---

## たとえ～ても · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | たとえ + て-form + も | <ruby>断<rt>ことわ</rt></ruby>られる → たとえ<ruby>断<rt>ことわ</rt></ruby>られても |
| Verb (negative) | たとえ + なくて + も | <ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わない → たとえ<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わなくても |
| い-adjective | たとえ + くて + も | <ruby>厳<rt>きび</rt></ruby>しい → たとえ<ruby>厳<rt>きび</rt></ruby>しくても |
| い-adjective (negative) | たとえ + くなくて + も | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くない → たとえ<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くなくても |
| な-adjective | たとえ + で + も | <ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby> → たとえ<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>でも |
| Noun | たとえ + で + も | <ruby>冗談<rt>じょうだん</rt></ruby> → たとえ<ruby>冗談<rt>じょうだん</rt></ruby>でも |
| Any plain clause (formal) | たとえ + plain form + としても | <ruby>失敗<rt>しっぱい</rt></ruby>する → たとえ<ruby>失敗<rt>しっぱい</rt></ruby>するとしても |
| Verb (literary) | たとえ + volitional + と（も） | <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>われる → たとえ<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>われようと |

---

## たとえ～ても · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `たとえ` is decoration you can delete; `ても` is not. Take `たとえ` out of たとえ<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くても<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く and you still have a sentence. Take `ても` out and you have nothing.

> **Note** - write it in kana. The kanji spelling <ruby>例<rt>たと</rt></ruby>え collides with <code><ruby>例<rt>たと</rt></ruby>えば</code> ("for example") and with <code><ruby>例<rt>たと</rt></ruby>える</code> ("to liken"), which are different words entirely. The older form たとい still turns up in legal and literary writing.

---

## たとえ～ても · Examples

たとえ**<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くても**、これは<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>わない。<br>*Even if it were cheap, I wouldn't buy this.*

たとえ**<ruby>冗談<rt>じょうだん</rt></ruby>でも**、そんなことは<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わないで。<br>*Don't say things like that, even as a joke.*

たとえ**<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>になっても**、ぼくはこの<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>賛成<rt>さんせい</rt></ruby>します。<br>*Even if I end up the only one, I'm backing this proposal.*

たとえ**<ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>反対<rt>はんたい</rt></ruby>されても**、<ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby><ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>するつもりだ。<br>*Even if my parents are against it, I intend to go abroad to study next year.*

---

## たとえ～ても · Examples (cont.)

たとえ**<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しくても**、<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>だけは<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>します。<br>*However busy things get, I'll at least send a reply before the day is out.*

たとえ**<ruby>失敗<rt>しっぱい</rt></ruby>したとしても**、<ruby>挑戦<rt>ちょうせん</rt></ruby>したことは<ruby>無駄<rt>むだ</rt></ruby>にならないよ。<br>*Even supposing it doesn't work out, having tried won't have been for nothing.*

たとえ**<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>指示<rt>しじ</rt></ruby>でも**、<ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>後回<rt>あとまわ</rt></ruby>しにするわけにはいきません。<br>*Even if the order comes from the president, we can't put safety second.*

たとえ**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>われようと**、<ruby>祖父<rt>そふ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めないと<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めている。<br>*Whatever anyone says to me, I've made up my mind not to close my grandfather's shop.*

---

## たとえ～ても · Nuance & register

`たとえ` is not casual, but it is not stiff either. It is **emphatic**, and emphasis has its own register: you hear it in speeches, in resolutions, in job interviews, and in the moments when someone is stating what they will not compromise on. In an ordinary exchange about the weather, people just use bare `ても` and leave `たとえ` out.

Because `たとえ` announces a supposition, it pulls the main clause toward **will and judgement** rather than plain report. The natural endings are `つもりだ`, `～たい`, `～なければならない`, `～わけにはいかない`, a negative, or a command. A flat factual ending sounds like the sentence lost its point halfway through.

Three shapes, roughly by formality:

---

## たとえ～ても · Nuance & register (cont.)

- たとえ～ても - the everyday one.
- たとえ～としても - a step more formal and more clearly hypothetical; common in<br>*writing and in careful speech.*
- たとえ～（よ）うと（も） - literary and declamatory. Fine in a written pledge or a<br>*dramatic line, overblown in conversation.*

`たとえ` also survives in fixed emotional phrasing: たとえ<ruby>一<rt>いち</rt></ruby>パーセントでも<ruby>可能性<rt>かのうせい</rt></ruby>があるなら - "if there's even a one percent chance." Notice the pattern: `たとえ` plus a deliberately tiny quantity plus `でも` is how Japanese says "even so much as."

---

## たとえ～ても · たとえ～ても vs bare ～ても

> **Rule of thumb** - bare `ても` covers both "even if" (a supposition) and "even when" (a habit). `たとえ` selects the supposition reading and turns the volume up.

<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>が**あっても**、<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ている。<br>*My brother turns up to practice even when he has a temperature.*

That is a report of what actually keeps happening. Adding `たとえ` there would be odd, because there is nothing to suppose - it is already true. Now the other reading:

たとえ<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>が**あっても**、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>まない。<br>*Even if I'm running a temperature, I'm not missing practice tomorrow.*

Nothing has happened; the speaker is pre-committing. That is `たとえ`'s home ground.

---

## たとえ～ても · たとえ～ても vs いくら／どんなに～ても

> **Rule of thumb** - `たとえ` concedes **whether**; `いくら` and `どんなに` concede **how much**. If there is no scale of degree or repetition, `いくら` has nothing to measure.

たとえ<ruby>一円<rt>いちえん</rt></ruby>**でも**、<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>のお<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>に<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>をつけてはいけない。<br>*Even one yen of someone else's money is not yours to touch.*

いくら<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>**しても**、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>納得<rt>なっとく</rt></ruby>してくれなかった。<br>*No matter how much I explained, he wouldn't accept it.*

---

## たとえ～ても · たとえ～ても vs いくら／どんなに～ても (cont.)

The first is a single yes-or-no supposition, so `たとえ`. The second is about repeated effort piling up with no result, so `いくら`. Where a degree really is at stake both can appear together: たとえどんなに<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がかかっても。

---

## たとえ～ても · たとえ～ても vs ～のに

> **Rule of thumb** - `たとえ～ても` is about a gap that has **not happened yet** and the speaker does not mind. `のに` is about a gap that **has** happened and the speaker does mind. They never swap.

たとえ<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遠<rt>とお</rt></ruby>**くても**、<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>通<rt>かよ</rt></ruby>います。<br>*Even if the office turns out to be far, I'll cycle in.*

<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遠<rt>とお</rt></ruby>い**のに**、<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>通<rt>かよ</rt></ruby>っている。<br>*The office is a long way off and yet he cycles in every day.*

The first speaker is deciding something in advance. The second is looking at a fact and reacting to it - with surprise here, but `のに` can just as easily carry a complaint. That is the axis this whole group of patterns is sorted on: how much the speaker minds.

---

## たとえ～ても · Common mistakes

- ❌ たとえ<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>だても → ✅ たとえ<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>でも<br>*Nouns and な-adjectives take `で` + `も`. `だ` never survives in front of `ても`.*

- ❌ たとえ<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ったても → ✅ たとえ<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っても<br>*No tense goes in front of `ても`. The final verb decides whether the sentence is about the future or the past.*

- ❌ たとえば<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くても<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います → ✅ たとえ<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くても<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います<br>*`たとえば` is "for example" - a different word that happens to start the same way. There is no `ば` in this pattern.*

---

## たとえ～ても · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ たとえ<ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>っても、<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>はまだ<ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>っている → ✅ <ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>った**のに**、<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>はまだ<ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>っている<br>*If the apology actually happened, it is not a supposition any more. Reporting a real, disappointing outcome is `のに`'s job.*

---

## たとえ～ても · Practice 1

**1.** たとえ<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども____、この<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>守<rt>まも</rt></ruby>らせます。

{reveal}
たとえ<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**でも**、この<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>守<rt>まも</rt></ruby>らせます。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども</code> is a noun, so the て-form it needs is `で`, giving `でも`. The sentence says the speaker will hold even a child to the promise - a small, extreme case granted and then dismissed, which is what `たとえ` is for.

---

## たとえ～ても · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た（たとえ・のに）、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>どおり<ruby>行<rt>おこな</rt></ruby>われた。

{reveal}
<ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た**のに**、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>どおり<ruby>行<rt>おこな</rt></ruby>われた。

{reveal}
The typhoon actually arrived, so this is a real event, not a supposition - and `たとえ` cannot attach to a past plain form anyway. The forward-looking version would be たとえ<ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ても、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>はやります。

---

## たとえ～ても · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Even if nobody agrees with me, I'm going to say what I think."

{reveal}
たとえ**だれも<ruby>賛成<rt>さんせい</rt></ruby>してくれなくても**、<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っていることは<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
The negative て-form is `なくて`, so the concessive is `なくても`. Note how naturally the main clause states an intention: `たとえ` sets up a supposition precisely so the speaker can declare that it changes nothing.

---

## たとえ～ても · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: たとえ<ruby>給料<rt>きゅうりょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いだても、この<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けたい。

{reveal}
✅ たとえ<ruby>給料<rt>きゅうりょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>**くても**、この<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けたい。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い</code> is an い-adjective, so its て-form is <code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くて</code> and the concessive is <code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くても</code>. The `だ` is a noun-sentence ending and has no business here; even with a noun the form would be `でも`, never `だても`.

---

## たとえ～ても · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) たとえ<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>でやることになっても、この<ruby>企画<rt>きかく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>めます。 (b) <ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>でやることになったのに、この<ruby>企画<rt>きかく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>めています。

{reveal}
(a) is a pledge made before the fact. The speaker imagines the worst version of the situation - being left to do it alone - grants it, and says the plan goes ahead regardless. There is no complaint in it; if anything it sounds determined.

{reveal}
(b) reports what did happen. The speaker was in fact left alone with the project and is carrying on, and `のに` colours that with feeling: admiration if said about someone else, and something close to a grumble if said about oneself.

# いくら～ても / いくら～でも

**Meaning** · no matter how much - turn the amount up as far as you like, the result still does not follow

`いくら` is the question word for a price: "how much?" Put it in front of a `ても` clause and it stops asking and starts conceding. Whatever quantity of effort, time, money, patience or intensity you want to imagine, the sentence says the outcome is unaffected.

On this batch's axis - how much the speaker minds the gap between expectation and outcome - `いくら～ても` sits in the middle. `たとえ～ても` grants one supposition and shrugs. `いくら～ても` grants an unlimited amount of effort and usually reports that it was wasted, so frustration leaks in even when no complaint is stated.

---

## いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Formation

`いくら` is an adverb. It stands at the head of the clause and the `ても` does the conjugating, exactly as at N4. `でも` is not a separate word to learn: it is simply what `ても` looks like after a noun or a な-adjective.

---

## いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | いくら + て-form + も | <ruby>呼<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ぶ → いくら<ruby>呼<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでも |
| Verb (negative) | いくら + なくて + も | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べない → いくら<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べなくても |
| い-adjective | いくら + くて + も | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い → いくら<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くても |
| い-adjective (negative) | いくら + くなくて + も | <ruby>甘<rt>あま</rt></ruby>くない → いくら<ruby>甘<rt>あま</rt></ruby>くなくても |
| な-adjective | いくら + で + も | <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き → いくら<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きでも |
| Noun | いくら + で + も | プロ → いくらプロでも |
| Fixed emphatic phrase | いくらなんでも + judgement | いくらなんでも<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>すぎる |

---

## いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `でも` here is the noun's て-form plus `も`, nothing else. い-adjectives already have their own て-form, so they take `くても` and never `でも`: ✅<code>いくら<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くても</code>, ❌<code>いくら<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いでも</code>.

> **Note** - `いくら` counts. It needs something that can be piled up: repetitions, hours, yen, or a degree on a scale. That is why it fits <code>いくら<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っても</code> ("however long I wait") but not a one-shot yes-or-no supposition, where `たとえ` is the word you want.

---

## いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Examples

いくら**<ruby>呼<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでも**、<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>てこなかった。<br>*No matter how much I called, the cat wouldn't come out.*

いくら**<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くても**、<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>上着<rt>うわぎ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>着<rt>き</rt></ruby>ない。<br>*However cold it gets, my little brother won't put a coat on.*

いくら**<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きでも**、<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby>カレーだと<ruby>飽<rt>あ</rt></ruby>きるよ。<br>*However much you like it, curry every single day gets old.*

いくら**<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えても**、いい<ruby>題名<rt>だいめい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いつかなかった。<br>*However long I thought about it, no good title came to me.*

---

## いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Examples (cont.)

いくら**<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>しても**、<ruby>発音<rt>はつおん</rt></ruby>だけはなかなか<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>らない。<br>*No matter how much I practise, my pronunciation is the one thing that won't improve.*

いくら**<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いでも**、この<ruby>渋滞<rt>じゅうたい</rt></ruby>では<ruby>終電<rt>しゅうでん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いそうにない。<br>*However much we hurry, in this traffic we're not going to make the last train.*

いくら**<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>頼<rt>たの</rt></ruby>まれても**、<ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>の<ruby>出勤<rt>しゅっきん</rt></ruby>はお<ruby>断<rt>ことわ</rt></ruby>りするつもりです。<br>*However hard the manager presses me, I intend to say no to working Saturday.*

**いくらなんでも**、<ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>で<ruby>百<rt>ひゃく</rt></ruby>ページの<ruby>翻訳<rt>ほんやく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>ですよ。<br>*A hundred pages of translation in three days is asking too much, surely.*

---

## いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Nuance & register

`いくら～ても` is thoroughly colloquial and completely neutral as to politeness. It appears in `ます` speech and in plain speech unchanged, and nothing about it is male, female, young or old.

What it *is* marked for is **futility**. The commonest ending by far is a negative or a disappointing result: <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかった, <ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>らない, <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からない, ダメだった. A learner who uses `いくら～ても` with a happy outcome will usually be misunderstood, because the pattern has been carrying resignation since the first word. When the ending is positive it is nearly always a statement of refusal or resolve - as in the Saturday example above - which is still the speaker pushing back against pressure.

Two set expressions worth memorising whole:

---

## いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **いくらなんでも** - "surely, that's beyond reason." Not "no matter what": it is a<br>*protest against something excessive, and it needs a judgement after it (`ひどい`, `無理だ`, `～すぎる`).*
- **いくら～からといって** - "just because ~". いくら<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いからといって、<ruby>三<rt>さん</rt></ruby>つも<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>うことはない。

In writing, `どんなに` and the stiffer `いかに` often replace `いくら`; a formal report is more likely to say いかに<ruby>努力<rt>どりょく</rt></ruby>しても than いくら<ruby>努力<rt>どりょく</rt></ruby>しても. In speech the proportion reverses.

---

## いくら～ても / いくら～でも · いくら～ても vs どんなに～ても

> **Rule of thumb** - `いくら` counts an **amount** (money, time, tries, effort); `どんなに` measures an **intensity** (how hard, how painful, how much someone cared). They overlap in most sentences; where a literal quantity is in view, `いくら` is the natural word, and where feeling is the point, `どんなに` is.

いくら**お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しても**、あの<ruby>絵<rt>え</rt></ruby>は<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>らないだろう。<br>*However much money you put up, that painting isn't going to be yours.*

どんなに**<ruby>悔<rt>くや</rt></ruby>しくても**、<ruby>判定<rt>はんてい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>文句<rt>もんく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってはいけない。<br>*However bitter you feel about it, you don't argue with the decision.*

---

## いくら～ても / いくら～でも · いくら～ても vs どんなに～ても (cont.)

Swap them and both sentences still parse; the second just loses some of its warmth. One thing only `どんなに` can do is head an exclamation: どんなに<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>したことか - "you have no idea how worried I was." `いくら` has no such use.

---

## いくら～ても / いくら～でも · いくら～ても vs たとえ～ても

> **Rule of thumb** - `たとえ` concedes **whether** something is so; `いくら` concedes **how much** of it there is. Ask whether the clause has a dial you can turn up.

たとえ**<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になっても**、<ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>はしておきます。<br>*Even if it gets cancelled, I'll have the venue set up.*

Cancellation has no degrees - it either happens or it does not - so `いくら` would be strange there. Compare a clause with a dial:

いくら**<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>しても**、<ruby>当日<rt>とうじつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>必<rt>かなら</rt></ruby>ず<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きる。<br>*However much you prepare, something always goes wrong on the day.*

---

## いくら～ても / いくら～でも · いくら～ても vs いくらでも / いくらか

> **Rule of thumb** - `いくらでも` on its own, with no `ても` clause around it, is not concessive at all. It means "as much as you like."

おかわりは**いくらでも**どうぞ。<br>*Help yourself to as many refills as you want.*

<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだら、**いくらか**<ruby>楽<rt>らく</rt></ruby>になりました。<br>*After the medicine I felt somewhat better.*

Three different words sharing a stem: `いくら～ても` concedes, `いくらでも` offers an unlimited amount, and `いくらか` means "some, a bit." Only the first has a verb in `ても` form after it.

---

## いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Common mistakes

- ❌ いくら<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いでも<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います → ✅ いくら<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>**くても**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います<br>*`でも` is the *noun* form. い-adjectives make their own て-form in `くて`.*

- ❌ いくら<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ったても → ✅ いくら<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っても<br>*Nothing is ever marked for tense in front of `ても`; the final verb carries the time.*

- ❌ いくら<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>しても、<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>になりました → ✅ たくさん<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>したので、<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>になりました<br>*`いくら～ても` promises that the effort did **not** pay off. With a result that matches the effort, the pattern contradicts itself.*

---

## いくら～ても / いくら～でも · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ いくらでも<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>しても、<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>わらなかった → ✅ いくら<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>しても、<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>わらなかった<br>*`でも` and `ても` are the same slot; you cannot have both. `いくらでも` belongs to a different construction entirely.*

---

# どんなに～ても

**Meaning** · no matter how - push the intensity as far as it will go, and the result still stands

`どんなに` asks "to what degree?" and then refuses to wait for an answer. Whatever level of cold, pain, effort, skill or worry you care to imagine, the `ても` clause grants it and the main clause carries on regardless.

Placed on this batch's axis - how much the speaker minds the gap between expectation and outcome - `どんなに～ても` is the emotional member of the "even if" branch. `たとえ～ても` grants a supposition coolly; `いくら～ても` counts up wasted effort; `どんなに～ても` reaches for the feeling behind the effort, which is why it is the one that shows up in pledges, song lyrics and speeches.

---

## どんなに～ても · Formation

`どんなに` is an adverb of degree standing at the head of the clause. All the conjugation happens on the `ても`, as at N4.

---

> **Rule of thumb** - `どんなに` modifies a **degree**, so it wants a verb or an adjective after it, not a bare noun. ❌<code>どんなに<ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>でも</code> → ✅<code>どんなに<ruby>経験<rt>けいけん</rt></ruby>のある<ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>でも</code>. When you really do want "even a doctor", `いくら` or plain `でも` handles it more comfortably.

どんなに**<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いでも**、ここから<ruby>空港<rt>くうこう</rt></ruby>までは<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>かかります。<br>*However much we rush, it's an hour from here to the airport.*

どんなに**<ruby>辛<rt>つら</rt></ruby>くても**、<ruby>最後<rt>さいご</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>り<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*However much it hurts, I'm running this to the finish.*

## どんなに～ても · Examples (cont.)

どんなに**<ruby>叱<rt>しか</rt></ruby>られても**、あの<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じいたずらを<ruby>繰<rt>く</rt></ruby>り<ruby>返<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>す。<br>*However often he's told off, that child repeats the same prank.*

どんなに**<ruby>高価<rt>こうか</rt></ruby>な<ruby>道具<rt>どうぐ</rt></ruby>でも**、<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>わなければただの<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*However expensive the tools, unused they're just so much luggage.*

どんなに**<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しくても**、<ruby>娘<rt>むすめ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>く<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>だけは<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>るようにしている。<br>*However busy I am, I try to keep aside time to listen to my daughter.*

どんなに**<ruby>条件<rt>じょうけん</rt></ruby>がよくても**、<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>と<ruby>離<rt>はな</rt></ruby>れて<ruby>暮<rt>く</rt></ruby>らす<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>選<rt>えら</rt></ruby>べません。<br>*However good the terms, I can't take a job that means living apart from my family.*

---

## どんなに～ても · Nuance & register

`どんなに` is neutral for politeness - it sits happily in `ます` speech and in plain speech - but it is not neutral for **temperature**. It reaches for feeling. A weather forecast says いくら, a wedding speech says どんなに.

That warmth gives it a use `いくら` does not have: the bare exclamation, with no concessive clause at all.

<ruby>合格<rt>ごうかく</rt></ruby>したと<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いて、**どんなに**<ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しかったことか。<br>*You can't imagine how glad I was to hear you'd passed.*

`どんなに～ことか` and `どんなに～だろう` are both this exclamatory use. They are distinctly written or heartfelt-spoken; nobody says them while ordering lunch.

---

## どんなに～ても · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register neighbours, roughly from stiff to slangy - treat these as tendencies rather than fixed rules:

- `いかに～ても` - formal writing, reports, editorials.
- `どんなに～ても` - the general-purpose word, speech and writing alike.
- `どれだけ～ても` - colloquial, and slightly more about quantity.
- `どんだけ～ても` - markedly casual, common in younger speech, out of place at work.

Finally, note the main clauses `どんなに` attracts. Roughly half are resigned (<code><ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>だ</code>, <code><ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わらない</code>) and half are defiant (`やめない`, <code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code>). What they have in common is that the speaker has already taken a position on the outcome.

---

## どんなに～ても · どんなに～ても vs いくら～ても

> **Rule of thumb** - `いくら` counts; `どんなに` feels. Where the clause is about a literal amount of money, time or repetitions, `いくら` is the plainer choice; where it is about how intensely something is experienced, `どんなに` is.

いくら**<ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>っても**、この<ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>い<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>らなかった。<br>*However much I paid, the old car never got fixed.*

どんなに**<ruby>悲<rt>かな</rt></ruby>しくても**、<ruby>人前<rt>ひとまえ</rt></ruby>では<ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>かない<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>だった。<br>*However sad she was, she was not one to cry in front of others.*

Most sentences accept either word. The two places they part company are the literal price tag, where `いくら` is idiomatic, and the exclamation どんなに～ことか, which `いくら` cannot form at all.

---

## どんなに～ても · どんなに～ても vs どんな～でも

> **Rule of thumb** - `どんなに` + degree + `ても` = "no matter **how** much." `どんな` + noun + `でも` = "**any** ~ at all." One syllable apart, completely different sentences.

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>は**どんな<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>でも**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>れます。<br>*My sister can cook anything you like.*

**どんなに<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>っても**、<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>の<ruby>味<rt>あじ</rt></ruby>にはならない。<br>*However many cooking lessons I take, it never tastes the way my mother's did.*

The first ranges over **kinds** of thing; the second ranges over **degrees** of effort. Remember that `どんな` needs a noun immediately after it, so if there is no noun there, the word you want is `どんなに`.

---

## どんなに～ても · どんなに～ても vs question word + ても

> **Rule of thumb** - `どんなに` concedes degree only. To concede **who**, **what**, **when** or **where**, put that question word in front of the `ても` clause instead.

**だれが<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ても**、この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>には<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れないでください。<br>*Whoever turns up, don't let them into this room.*

**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>われても**、<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めたことは<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>えません。<br>*Whatever I'm told, I'm not changing what I've decided.*

The frame is identical - question word, `ても`, unmoved main clause - and `どんなに` is simply the member of the set that handles "how much." They stack, too: だれが<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>おうと。

---

## どんなに～ても · Common mistakes

- ❌ どんなに<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>える → ✅ どんなに<ruby>優秀<rt>ゆうしゅう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>える<br>*`どんなに` needs something gradable to modify, and a bare noun gives it nothing to measure. Either supply an adjective, or drop to plain <code>どんな<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>でも</code>, which means "anyone at all."*

- ❌ どんなに<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>くても → ✅ どんなに<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>でも<br>*<code><ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby></code> is a な-adjective, so its て-form is <code><ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>で</code>. Only い-adjectives take `くても`.*

- ❌ どんなに<ruby>頑張<rt>がんば</rt></ruby>るても → ✅ どんなに<ruby>頑張<rt>がんば</rt></ruby>っても<br>*`も` attaches to the て-form, never to the dictionary form.*

---

## どんなに～ても · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ どんなに<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>しても、やっと<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かってくれた → ✅ <ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>して、やっと<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かってくれた<br>*The whole point of `どんなに～ても` is that the effort **fails**. If it finally worked, you need an ordinary て-form linking cause to result.*

# ～にしても

**Meaning** · even if; even granting that - the speaker accepts the other side's point and objects anyway

`にしても` picks up a reason, an excuse or a possibility that is already on the table, grants it, and then says it is not enough. Almost every `にしても` sentence has an argument going on inside it: *fine, suppose that is true - even so, this still does not follow.*

On this batch's axis of how much the speaker minds, `にしても` is the reasonable complainer. `のに` blurts out its dissatisfaction; `くせに` sneers; `にしても` concedes first and criticises second, which is why it is the concession you hear in meetings.

---

## ～にしても · Formation

`にしても` follows a **plain form** - a whole clause, with its own tense - and nouns and な-adjectives attach bare, with no `だ` and no `な`.

## ～にしても · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - no `だ` and no `な` before `にしても`: ✅<code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>にしても</code>, ❌<code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だにしても</code>. If you want the copula visible, the formal option is `である`: <code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>であるにしても</code>.

> **Note** - with a な-adjective the bare stem can be ambiguous, because <code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かにしても</code> also reads as "even if you make it quiet" (<code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かにする</code> + `ても`). In speech people dodge this by saying <code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだとしても</code> instead. In writing, `である` does the same job.

## ～にしても · Nuance & register

`にしても` is the vocabulary of **argument conducted politely**. It concedes in order to earn the right to object, which makes it a staple of meetings, negotiations, letters of complaint and mild parental scolding. Because the concession comes first, the objection that follows lands as reasonable rather than hostile - this is a genuinely useful piece of adult Japanese.

The main clause is where the speaker's feeling lives, and it is usually one of:

- a criticism - `～すぎる`, `ひどい`, `おかしい`;
- a minimum demand - `ぐらいは～てほしい`, `～べきだ`;
- a limit on a plan - <code>それでも～は<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>えない</code>.

A flat, neutral report after `にしても` sounds unfinished, because the listener is waiting for the objection the concession promised.

---

## ～にしても · Nuance & register (cont.)

Its family, roughly by register:

- `としても` - the most clearly hypothetical: "even supposing." Neutral.
- `にしても` - concedes something already raised, real or supposed. Neutral.
- `にしろ` - the same, a shade curt; common in spoken instructions.
- `にせよ` - formal and written; documents and speeches.

`それにしても` is worth learning as a single word. It re-opens a topic to comment on its degree: 「<ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>いね。」「うん、それにしても<ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>すぎるよ。」 It is not conceding anything specific - it just says "granting everything we have said, still."

---

## ～にしても · ～にしても vs ～ても

> **Rule of thumb** - `ても` attaches to a **te-form** and states a bare concession. `にしても` attaches to a **whole plain clause**, so it can carry past tense and nouns, and it concedes a *claim someone has actually made*.

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っ**ても**、<ruby>畑<rt>はたけ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>やりは<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>です。<br>*Even if it rains, the field still needs watering.*

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>った**にしても**、これだけしか<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>らなければ<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じことです。<br>*Granted it rained - but this little makes no difference.*

Notice what the second one does that the first cannot: it keeps the past tense (<code><ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>った</code>) and it answers somebody. `ても` cannot take tense at all, and it does not imply a conversation.

---

## ～にしても · ～にしても vs ～にしては

> **Rule of thumb** - one syllable, two different sentences. `にしても` = "even granting X, Y is still not acceptable." `にしては` = "measured against X, Y is surprising." `にしても` complains; `にしては` evaluates.

<ruby>初心者<rt>しょしんしゃ</rt></ruby>**にしては**、ずいぶん<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>に<ruby>削<rt>けず</rt></ruby>れていますね。<br>*For a beginner, that's remarkably well planed.*

<ruby>初心者<rt>しょしんしゃ</rt></ruby>**にしても**、<ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>な<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>は<ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>っておくべきだ。<br>*Beginner or not, he ought to have learned how to hold it safely.*

The first is a compliment: the standard "beginner" predicts little, and the result beat it. The second is a reproach: being a beginner is offered as an excuse, accepted, and ruled insufficient.

---

## ～にしても · ～にしても vs ～のに

> **Rule of thumb** - `のに` reports a **fact** that disappointed the speaker. `にしても` concedes a **premise** - which may be a fact or merely a supposition - and then argues from it.

<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>があった**のに**、<ruby>最後<rt>さいご</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>司会<rt>しかい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けた。<br>*She had a temperature and still chaired the whole thing.*

<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>があった**にしても**、<ruby>一言<rt>ひとこと</rt></ruby><ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってくれればよかったのに。<br>*Even with a temperature, she could have said something.*

The first admires a fact. The second takes the same fact as an excuse and pushes back on it. If you find yourself simply reporting something surprising with no argument attached, `のに` is the word.

---

## ～にしても · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だにしても → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>にしても<br>*Nouns and な-adjectives attach bare. `だ` never appears before `にしても`; the formal alternative is `である`.*

- ❌ <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいにしても、<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>いに<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てくれた → ✅ <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい**のに**、<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>いに<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てくれた<br>*`にしても` sets up an objection. When you are grateful rather than dissatisfied, the fact-reporting `のに` is what you want.*

# ～といっても

**Meaning** · although I say ~ - the word just used promises more than the reality delivers

`といっても` takes the label that has just been put on something - "I can cook", "it's near the station", "he's a company president" - and immediately cuts it down to size. The listener has drawn a conclusion from the label, and the speaker heads that conclusion off before it settles.

On this batch's axis, `といっても` is not really about minding at all. `のに` and `くせに` are unhappy about a gap between expectation and outcome. `といっても` is **correcting** one, usually the speaker's own, and it is more often modest or wry than annoyed.

---

## ～といっても · Formation

`といっても` follows a **plain form** or a bare noun. The `と` is the quotation particle, so what precedes it is treated as a quoted claim.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + といっても | できる → できるといっても |
| Verb (past) | plain past + といっても | <ruby>優勝<rt>ゆうしょう</rt></ruby>した → <ruby>優勝<rt>ゆうしょう</rt></ruby>したといっても |
| い-adjective | plain form + といっても | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いといっても |
| な-adjective | stem (+ だ) + といっても | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby> → <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>（だ）といっても |
| Noun | Noun (+ だ) + といっても | <ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>（だ）といっても |
| Start of a new sentence | といっても + correction | - → といっても、まだ<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>まっていません |

---

## ～といっても · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the clause after `といっても` must **shrink** what came before it, so it very often contains `だけ`, `ぐらい`, `ほんの`, `たった`, or `たいしたことはない`. If your second clause adds new information instead of scaling the first one down, `といっても` is the wrong join.

> **Note** - the same shape appears as a stand-alone connector at the head of a sentence. 「<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>します。といっても、<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ <ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>ですが。」 - "We're moving next week. Only within the same town, mind."

## ～といっても · Nuance & register

`といっても` is register-neutral and equally at home in speech and writing, but it is strongly associated with one social move: **modest self-presentation**. Japanese conversation makes a habit of naming an achievement and deflating it in the same breath, and `といっても` is the hinge that deflation turns on. Learners who say 「<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せます」 and stop can sound like they are boasting; 「<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せるといっても、まだ<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>な<ruby>会話<rt>かいわ</rt></ruby>だけですが」 lands much better.

Turned outward, it becomes a gentle correction of somebody else's overstatement, as in the "you say you're busy" example above. Said sharply it can needle, so keep the tone light - a `ね` or `けど` at the end helps.

Casual contractions are everywhere in speech:

---

## ～といっても · Nuance & register (cont.)

- `っていっても` - the everyday spoken shape after a vowel:<br>*「<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>っていっても、やるしかないよ。」*
- `つっても` - very casual, more common in men's relaxed speech.

Two neighbours to keep straight. `そうはいっても` ("even so") pushes back on what was said instead of shrinking it. `とはいえ` does much the same work as `といっても` but belongs to written and formal register - you will meet it in editorials, not in chat.

---

## ～といっても · ～といっても vs ～けど

> **Rule of thumb** - `けど` simply joins two clauses that lean opposite ways. `といっても` says something more specific: *the word I just used was too big.*

<ruby>給料<rt>きゅうりょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がった**けど**、<ruby>残業<rt>ざんぎょう</rt></ruby>も<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えた。<br>*My pay went up, but so did the overtime.*

<ruby>給料<rt>きゅうりょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がった**といっても**、<ruby>月<rt>つき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>二千円<rt>にせんえん</rt></ruby>ほどです。<br>*My pay went up - by about two thousand yen a month.*

The `けど` sentence adds a second, separate fact. The `といっても` sentence stays on the first fact and shows you it is smaller than it sounded. Swap them and the `けど` version loses nothing; the `といっても` version stops making sense, because "the overtime increased" does not measure the pay rise.

---

## ～といっても · ～といっても vs ～というより

> **Rule of thumb** - `といっても` **keeps** the label and shrinks it. `というより` **rejects** the label and offers a better one.

<ruby>趣味<rt>しゅみ</rt></ruby>**といっても**、<ruby>月<rt>つき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>触<rt>さわ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>程度<rt>ていど</rt></ruby>です。<br>*It's a hobby, if you can call something I touch once a month a hobby.*

あれは<ruby>趣味<rt>しゅみ</rt></ruby>**というより**、もう<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>ですね。<br>*That's not so much a hobby as a job by now.*

In the first the thing is still a hobby, just a thin one. In the second the word "hobby" is withdrawn and replaced.

---

## ～といっても · ～といっても vs そうはいっても

> **Rule of thumb** - `といっても` deflates the claim. `そうはいっても` accepts the claim and refuses the conclusion drawn from it.

<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い**といっても**、<ruby>中古<rt>ちゅうこ</rt></ruby>なら<ruby>当然<rt>とうぜん</rt></ruby>でしょう。<br>*Cheap, sure - but it's second-hand, so of course it is.*

<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいのはわかります。**そうはいっても**、<ruby>締<rt>し</rt></ruby>め<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>りは<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>かせません。<br>*I know you're busy. Even so, the deadline can't move.*

`そうはいっても` needs something to point back at, so it opens a sentence rather than joining one. In formal writing the equivalent is `とはいえ`.

## ～といっても · Common mistakes

# ところが

**Meaning** · however; but then - what actually happened defeated what everyone was expecting

`ところが` starts a new sentence and drops the floor out from under the previous one. The first sentence sets up an expectation; `ところが` announces that reality went the other way. Crucially, the thing that went the other way has **already happened** - `ところが` reports outcomes, never plans.

On this batch's axis of how much the speaker minds, `ところが` is close to neutral. It marks **surprise**, not dissatisfaction. The turn can be for the worse, as it usually is in a complaint, but it can equally be for the better - a cheap hotel that turned out to be lovely takes `ところが` just as happily.

---

## ところが · Formation

`ところが` is a conjunction, not a suffix, so there is nothing for it to attach to in the usual sense. What matters is the shape of the sentences on either side of it.

| Position | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Between two sentences | Sentence。ところが、Sentence。 | <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた。ところが、<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>まっていた。 |
| Head of a spoken turn | ところが、… | ところが、それが<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>ったんです |
| Inside one sentence (written) | Verb plain past + ところが | <ruby>頼<rt>たの</rt></ruby>んだ → <ruby>頼<rt>たの</rt></ruby>んだところが |
| What may follow it | a **realized fact** only | ✅<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>まっていた ❌<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めましょう |

---

## ところが · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - nothing forward-looking can follow `ところが`. No volitional, no request, no invitation, no command. ❌<code>ところが、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう</code> is simply not available; that sentence needs `しかし` or `でも`.

> **Note** - the written `～たところが` packs the same reversal into a single sentence: <ruby>問<rt>と</rt></ruby>い<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わせたところが、<ruby>担当者<rt>たんとうしゃ</rt></ruby>は <ruby>退職<rt>たいしょく</rt></ruby>していた。 It always follows a **past** verb, and it belongs to reports and essays rather than conversation.

---

## ところが · Examples

<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>って<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた。**ところが**、<ruby>一滴<rt>いってき</rt></ruby>も<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>らなかった。<br>*I took an umbrella out with me. Not a drop fell.*

<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しみにしていました。**ところが**、<ruby>当日<rt>とうじつ</rt></ruby><ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してしまいました。<br>*I'd been looking forward to it. Then on the day itself I came down with a fever.*

<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>な<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>だと<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いていた。**ところが**、<ruby>実際<rt>じっさい</rt></ruby>には<ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>かかった。<br>*I'd been told it was an easy job. In fact it took three days.*

## ところが · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>開店<rt>かいてん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>並<rt>なら</rt></ruby>んだ。**ところが**、<ruby>目当<rt>めあ</rt></ruby>てのパンはもう<ruby>売<rt>う</rt></ruby>り<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>れていた。<br>*I queued from before opening time. The bread I'd come for was already sold out.*

<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんなら<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っているだろうと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>って<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてみた。**ところが**、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>首<rt>くび</rt></ruby>をかしげるばかりだった。<br>*I asked Yamada, thinking he was bound to know. All he did was tilt his head at me.*

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>してみた**ところが**、その<ruby>場<rt>ば</rt></ruby>ですぐに<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めてくださった。<br>*When I took it to the section chief, he settled it on the spot.*

## ところが · Nuance & register

`ところが` is the storyteller's word. It carries no politeness of its own - it appears unchanged before `ます` sentences and plain ones - but it does presuppose a **narrative**: something was set up, and now it collapses. That is why it so often turns up with `のだ／んです` after it. The speaker is not just reporting the reversal, they are explaining it to you.

Because the reversal has to be real, `ところが` is overwhelmingly followed by past tense or by `ている`. A present-tense fact is possible when it is a discovered state (<code><ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>のより<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きいんです</code>), but a future or a decision is not.

Neighbours in the "but" family:

---

## ところが · Nuance & register (cont.)

- `しかし` - neutral, all-purpose, no surprise required; fine in writing and speech.
- `でも` - the casual everyday one, and the only one that comfortably opens a<br>*disagreement with the person you are talking to.*
- `ところが` - narrative reversal, expectation defeated, outcome already in.
- `それが` - the colloquial cousin of `ところが`, used to answer a question whose<br>*premise has failed: 「<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ましたか。」「それが、<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかったんです。」*

One more thing `ところが` does not do: it does not disagree with your listener. If someone has just stated an opinion and you want to push back, `でも` or `そうはいっても` is what you need. `ところが` argues with the world, not with people.

---

## ところが · ところが vs しかし・でも

> **Rule of thumb** - `しかし` and `でも` can be followed by anything, including a plan or a request. `ところが` can only be followed by a fact that has already turned out.

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。**でも**、<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>どおり<ruby>出発<rt>しゅっぱつ</rt></ruby>しましょう。<br>*It's started raining. Let's set off as planned anyway.*

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。**ところが**、<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていなかった。<br>*It started raining. Nobody had an umbrella.*

Replace `でも` with `ところが` in the first and the sentence breaks: a suggestion is not something that "turned out." The second works with either word, but `ところが` adds the click of an expectation failing, which `でも` leaves out.

---

## ところが · ところが vs ところで

> **Rule of thumb** - one syllable apart, and unrelated. `ところが` = "but then." `ところで` = "by the way", a change of topic.

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりました。**ところで**、この<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>ありますか。<br>*The meeting's over. By the way, are you free after this?*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わるはずだった。**ところが**、<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>いた。<br>*The meeting was supposed to finish at three. It ran until five.*

Note also the unrelated `～たところで` ("even if you were to…"), which is a concessive and belongs to a later level. Three similar strings, three separate words: check the final syllable and whether a full stop precedes.

---

## ところが · ところが vs ～のに

> **Rule of thumb** - `のに` lives **inside** a sentence and carries the speaker's dissatisfaction. `ところが` stands **between** sentences and carries surprise, which may be pleasant.

<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>った**のに**、<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>ひとつなかった。<br>*I waited a whole hour and didn't get so much as a message.*

<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>覚悟<rt>かくご</rt></ruby>で<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った。**ところが**、すぐに<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>れた。<br>*I went to the restaurant braced for an hour's wait. We were seated straight away.*

Try putting `のに` in the second one and it turns sour - `のに` would suggest the speaker resents being seated quickly. That is the difference in one line: `のに` minds, `ところが` merely did not see it coming.

## ところが · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>混<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいる。ところが、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう。 → ✅ <ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>が<ruby>混<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいる。**でも**、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう。<br>*A suggestion cannot follow `ところが`, which reports only what has already turned out.*

# ～どころか

**Meaning** · far from ~; never mind ~ - the word just offered is not merely wrong, it points the wrong way

`どころか` rejects a description and replaces it with one further along the scale, or on the other side of it altogether. Somebody expects an apology and gets laughter; somebody expects a ten-minute wait and waits an hour. `どころか` says: that is not the half of it.

On this batch's axis of how much the speaker minds, `どころか` is the emphatic end. `のに` reports a gap between expectation and outcome; `どころか` says the gap runs in the opposite direction from the one you assumed, and it usually says so with feeling.

---

## ～どころか · Formation

`どころか` attaches to a plain form or a bare noun. Nouns and な-adjectives take no `だ` and no `な`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + どころか | <ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>るどころか |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + どころか | <ruby>減<rt>へ</rt></ruby>らない → <ruby>減<rt>へ</rt></ruby>らないどころか |
| い-adjective | plain form + どころか | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いどころか |
| な-adjective | stem + どころか | <ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby>どころか |
| Noun | Noun + どころか | <ruby>休憩<rt>きゅうけい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>休憩<rt>きゅうけい</rt></ruby>どころか |
| "not even" frame | A どころか B + も／さえ + negative | <ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>どころかひらがなも<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めない |

---

## ～どころか · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - whatever follows `どころか` must **contradict or overshoot** what precedes it. A second clause that simply agrees with the first leaves the pattern with nothing to reject: ❌<code><ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がるどころか<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がった</code>, ✅<code><ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がるどころかかえって<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がった</code>.

> **Note** - in the "not even" frame the order looks backwards to English eyes. The **bigger** item goes before `どころか` and the **smaller** one after it, with a negative at the end: <ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>どころか、ひらがなも<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めない - "never mind kanji, he can't even read hiragana."

---

## ～どころか · Examples

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>る**どころか**、<ruby>笑<rt>わら</rt></ruby>っていた。<br>*Far from apologising, he was laughing.*

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>**どころか**、ひらがなもまだ<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えていません。<br>*Never mind kanji - I haven't even learned hiragana yet.*

<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む**どころか**、<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんも<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べていないんです。<br>*Rest? I haven't even had lunch.*

<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**どころか**、<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby><ruby>以上<rt>いじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たされました。<br>*Ten minutes? I was kept waiting over an hour.*

---

## ～どころか · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだのに、よくなる**どころか**<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がってしまった。<br>*I took the medicine, and instead of getting better my temperature went up.*

あの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby>**どころか**、<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>真<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ん<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>にありますよ。<br>*Inconvenient? That shop is right in front of the station.*

<ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>のせいで、<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>**どころか**、<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ることもできなかった。<br>*Because of the typhoon we couldn't go outside, let alone take our trip.*

<ruby>後輩<rt>こうはい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>したら、<ruby>反省<rt>はんせい</rt></ruby>する**どころか**、<ruby>口<rt>くち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>利<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてくれなくなった。<br>*I had a word with my junior colleague, and rather than take it on board he stopped speaking to me.*

---

## ～どころか · Nuance & register

`どころか` is neutral for politeness and appears in `です・ます` speech and in writing without changing, but it is far from neutral in **temperature**. It is a correction delivered with force, and it nearly always carries one of three feelings: indignation ("far from apologising…"), astonishment ("an hour, not ten minutes"), or self-deprecation ("never mind kanji…"). Said flatly it can sound like you are contradicting the listener, so in careful conversation people soften it with `よ`, `ね`, or an explanatory `んです`.

Both directions are available and it is worth noticing which one you are in:

- **Downward** - the reality falls short of the label:<br>*<ruby>上達<rt>じょうたつ</rt></ruby>するどころか、<ruby>下手<rt>へた</rt></ruby>になった<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>がする。*
- **Upward** - the label is too modest for the reality:<br>*<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>どころか、プロと<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>えられるほどだ。*

---

## ～どころか · Nuance & register (cont.)

The related `どころではない` looks similar and does something else: it says the situation rules the thing out entirely. 「<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>どころではない」 - "this is no time for travelling." Notice that it ends the sentence, while `どころか` is a join.

---

## ～どころか · ～どころか vs ～ばかりか・～だけでなく

> **Rule of thumb** - `ばかりか` and `だけでなく` **accept** the first item and add a second. `どころか` **rejects** the first item as an inadequate description.

<ruby>祖父<rt>そふ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>英語<rt>えいご</rt></ruby>**だけでなく**、ロシア<ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>も<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>します。<br>*My grandfather speaks not only English but Russian too.*

<ruby>祖父<rt>そふ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>英語<rt>えいご</rt></ruby>**どころか**、<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>もローマ<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>けません。<br>*Never mind English - my grandfather can't even write his own name in roman letters.*

In the first, the English is real and the Russian is extra. In the second, the English is thrown out as absurdly optimistic. That is why `どころか` in the "not even" frame almost always ends in a negative, and `だけでなく` almost never does.

---

## ～どころか · ～どころか vs ところが

> **Rule of thumb** - `どころか` joins two **descriptions inside one sentence** and denies the first. `ところが` joins two **sentences** and reports that events took an unexpected turn.

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った。**ところが**、<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>で<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>ずれができた。<br>*I bought new shoes. Within a day they'd given me blisters.*

その<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>楽<rt>らく</rt></ruby>になる**どころか**、<ruby>履<rt>は</rt></ruby>くたびに<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>くなる。<br>*Far from breaking in, those shoes hurt more every time I wear them.*

`ところが` needs a full stop before it and a finished event after it; `どころか` sits mid-sentence and needs two competing descriptions of the same thing.

---

## ～どころか · ～どころか vs ～どころではない

> **Rule of thumb** - `どころか` continues the sentence and offers the truth. `どころではない` ends it and says the thing is out of the question.

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しの<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る**どころではない**。<br>*With the move to get ready, watching a film is out of the question.*

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しの<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>**どころか**、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>けていない。<br>*What with the move, I haven't even managed the shopping, never mind a film.*

The first is a verdict on the situation. The second still describes what is and is not happening, and it needs its second half.

---

## ～どころか · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>どころか、ひらがなも<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めます → ✅ <ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>どころか、ひらがなも<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めません<br>*The "never mind A" frame is built on a negative. With an affirmative ending there is nothing being ruled out and the sentence collapses.*

- ❌ <ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がるどころか、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がった → ✅ <ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がるどころか、かえって<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がった<br>*What follows `どころか` has to run counter to what precedes it. Repeating the same direction, even weakly, gives the pattern nothing to reject.*

- ❌ <ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>った。どころか、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかった。 → ✅ <ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>った。**ところが**、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかった。<br>*`どころか` cannot start a sentence. A narrative reversal between two sentences is `ところが`.*

---

## ～どころか · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>残業<rt>ざんぎょう</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み<ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>どころか。 → ✅ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>残業<rt>ざんぎょう</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み<ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>**どころではない**。<br>*`どころか` is a join and needs a second half. To end the sentence with "that's out of the question", use `どころではない`.*

---

## ～どころか · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>貯金<rt>ちょきん</rt></ruby>____、<ruby>今月<rt>こんげつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>家賃<rt>やちん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>えない。

{reveal}
<ruby>貯金<rt>ちょきん</rt></ruby>**どころか**、<ruby>今月<rt>こんげつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>家賃<rt>やちん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>えない。

{reveal}
The bigger, more ambitious item - saving - goes first and is dismissed; the smaller one follows with `も` and a negative. That ordering is the fixed shape of the "never mind A" frame.

---

## ～どころか · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>はギター（どころか・だけでなく）ピアノも<ruby>弾<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>けます。

{reveal}
<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>はギター**だけでなく**ピアノも<ruby>弾<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>けます。

{reveal}
Both instruments are genuinely played, so the first item is being *accepted* and the second added - that is `だけでなく`. With `どころか` the sentence would have to deny the guitar, and it would need a negative ending.

---

## ～どころか · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Far from thanking me, she got angry."

{reveal}
<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>はお<ruby>礼<rt>れい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う**どころか**、<ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。

{reveal}
The plain form <code><ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う</code> takes `どころか` directly, and the second clause runs in the opposite direction to the first. This is the indignant use, and it is the commonest one in speech.

---

## ～どころか · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>いので、<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>びに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くどころか。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>いので、<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>びに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**どころではない**。

{reveal}
`どころか` is a connector: something has to follow it. When you want to end on "that is out of the question", the form is `どころではない` (or the more casual `どころじゃない`).

---

## ～どころか · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>くどころか、<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>より<ruby>散<rt>ち</rt></ruby>らかってしまった。 (b) <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けたのに、また<ruby>散<rt>ち</rt></ruby>らかってしまった。

{reveal}
(a) denies the expected direction outright: the tidying did not merely fail, it made things worse. `どころか` is doing the work of "far from", and the sentence carries a note of exasperation at how completely the expectation was inverted.

{reveal}
(b) reports two facts - the room was tidied, and it is messy again - and colours the mismatch with the speaker's dissatisfaction. Nothing is being denied; `のに` simply says the second half should not have followed the first.

# ～くせに

**Meaning** · even though; and yet - said about a person, with a curl of the lip

`くせに` marks the same expectation-versus-outcome gap that `のに` marks, and then adds something `のに` does not have: a **judgement on the person**. Whoever the sentence is about should have behaved otherwise, and the speaker is holding it against them.

Two restrictions come with that. The clause has to be about a **person**, and both halves of the sentence have to be about the **same** person. Those two facts are most of what there is to learn here, and they are what stops `くせに` from being a free substitute for `のに`.

---

## ～くせに · Formation

`くせに` follows a plain form. It began life as the noun `くせ` ("a habit, a trait"), which is why nouns link to it with `の` and な-adjectives with `な` - the same links a noun takes.

---

## ～くせに · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + くせに | <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている → <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っているくせに |
| Verb (past) | plain past + くせに | <ruby>断<rt>ことわ</rt></ruby>った → <ruby>断<rt>ことわ</rt></ruby>ったくせに |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + くせに | できない → できないくせに |
| い-adjective | plain form + くせに | <ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>いくせに |
| な-adjective | な + くせに | <ruby>下手<rt>へた</rt></ruby> → <ruby>下手<rt>へた</rt></ruby>なくせに |
| Noun | の + くせに | <ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby>のくせに |
| Sentence-final jab | …くせに。 | <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かっているくせに。 |
| Blunter colloquial variant | …くせして | <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないくせして |

---

## ～くせに · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - **one person, both clauses.** ✅ <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ってもらったくせに、お<ruby>礼<rt>れい</rt></ruby>も<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わない。 ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ったくせに、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はお<ruby>礼<rt>れい</rt></ruby>も<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わない。 The second sentence has two different subjects, so it must use `のに`. `のに` never cares who is doing what; `くせに` is passing judgement on one individual and cannot split its attention.

---

## ～くせに · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the subject also has to be a **person**. Textbook Japanese does not allow ❌<code>この<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>いくせに<ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>い</code>. In casual grumbling you will hear people stretch it to a shop, a phone or a piece of software they are cross with, which works precisely because it treats the object as somebody misbehaving. Keep it for people until you have a feel for that.

---

## ～くせに · Examples

<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている**くせに**、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてくれない。<br>*He knows perfectly well and won't tell me a thing.*

<ruby>下手<rt>へた</rt></ruby>な**くせに**、<ruby>文句<rt>もんく</rt></ruby>ばかり<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っている。<br>*He's no good at it himself, and all he does is complain.*

<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れてきた**くせに**、<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>遅刻<rt>ちこく</rt></ruby>には<ruby>厳<rt>きび</rt></ruby>しい。<br>*He turned up late himself, yet he's merciless about other people being late.*

<ruby>甘<rt>あま</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>は<ruby>嫌<rt>きら</rt></ruby>いだと<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っていた**くせに**、ケーキを<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つも<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた。<br>*He said he didn't like sweet things - then ate three slices of cake.*

---

## ～くせに · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>できない**くせに**、<ruby>助手席<rt>じょしゅせき</rt></ruby>でうるさく<ruby>指示<rt>しじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す。<br>*He can't drive himself, but he barks directions from the passenger seat.*

あんなに<ruby>反対<rt>はんたい</rt></ruby>していた**くせに**、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>では<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>よりも<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しんでいる。<br>*She was so against it, and now she's enjoying it more than any of us.*

<ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>寂<rt>さび</rt></ruby>しい**くせに**、<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>のほうが<ruby>楽<rt>らく</rt></ruby>だなんて<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っている。<br>*He's lonely really, and there he is saying he prefers being on his own.*

<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>では<ruby>一円<rt>いちえん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>していない**くせに**、<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>選<rt>えら</rt></ruby>び<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>にだけは<ruby>口<rt>くち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してくる。<br>*He hasn't put in a single yen, and yet he has plenty to say about which restaurant we pick.*

---

## ～くせに · Nuance & register

`くせに` is **never neutral**. Where `のに` ranges from mild surprise to real irritation, `くせに` starts at criticism and goes down from there: blame, scorn, resentment. Its politeness ceiling is low. You do not use it about a customer, a teacher, a manager or anyone else you owe deference to, and it does not belong in written Japanese outside dialogue.

Within that range it has two quite different lives:

- **Aimed at someone absent** - a complaint, and it can sound genuinely mean.
- **Aimed at a friend, face to face** - teasing, and it softens almost to affection.<br>*「<ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>いてたくせに。」 said with a grin between friends is nothing worse than "you *were* crying, though."*

---

## ～くせに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The sentence-final version, with the main clause simply left off, is common in casual speech: 「<ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きたいくせに。」 It works like a raised eyebrow - the listener knows what was left unsaid.

`くせして` is the same word, blunter and more colloquial. `くせに` is already strong; `くせして` is stronger, and rarer in mixed company.

A note about <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どものくせに and <ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby>のくせに. These fixed scoldings are still heard, but they land as high-handed and are drifting out of acceptable workplace speech. Recognise them; think twice before producing them.

---

## ～くせに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, `くせに` can be turned on yourself, and then it becomes self-mockery: <ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>げないくせに、<ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>いところまで<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってしまった。 The contempt is still there - the speaker has simply aimed it at their own behaviour.

---

## ～くせに · ～くせに vs ～のに

> **Rule of thumb** - `のに` reports a gap and expresses the speaker's dissatisfaction. `くせに` blames a person for the gap, and needs the **same person** in both clauses. Every `くせに` sentence could be `のに`; the reverse is not true.

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>誘<rt>さそ</rt></ruby>った**のに**、<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかった。<br>*I invited him again and again, and my brother never came once.*

Two subjects - I invited, he didn't come - so `くせに` is unavailable no matter how annoyed the speaker is. Now put both halves on one person:

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>から<ruby>誘<rt>さそ</rt></ruby>った**くせに**、<ruby>当日<rt>とうじつ</rt></ruby>になって<ruby>断<rt>ことわ</rt></ruby>ってきた。<br>*My brother was the one who suggested it, and then cancelled on the day.*

---

## ～くせに · ～くせに vs ～のに (cont.)

Even where both are possible, the feeling differs. <ruby>遠<rt>とお</rt></ruby>いのに<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てくれた is gratitude; ❌<ruby>遠<rt>とお</rt></ruby>いくせに<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てくれた is not a sentence anyone would say, because you cannot sneer and thank someone in the same breath.

---

## ～くせに · ～くせに vs ～にもかかわらず

> **Rule of thumb** - same gap, opposite register. `にもかかわらず` is formal, written and emotionally flat; `くせに` is spoken and hostile. `のに` sits between them.

`にもかかわらず` takes a plain form or a bare noun: <ruby>雨天<rt>うてん</rt></ruby>にもかかわらず、<ruby>大勢<rt>おおぜい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まった。

<ruby>悪天候<rt>あくてんこう</rt></ruby>**にもかかわらず**、<ruby>開会式<rt>かいかいしき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>どおり<ruby>行<rt>おこな</rt></ruby>われた。<br>*Despite the poor weather, the opening ceremony went ahead as scheduled.*

<ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>いと<ruby>文句<rt>もんく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っていた**くせに**、<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby><ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ていた。<br>*For all his moaning about the weather, he was the first one there.*

---

## ～くせに · ～くせに vs ～にもかかわらず (cont.)

Notice that the formal version happily takes a **non-human** subject - the weather - which `くせに` cannot. Reports, notices and speeches use `にもかかわらず` precisely because it registers the contradiction without any attitude attached.

---

## ～くせに · ～くせに vs ～ながら（も）

> **Rule of thumb** - `ながらも` concedes something about a person without attacking them, and often about the speaker themselves. It is literary where `くせに` is colloquial.

<ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>い<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>**ながらも**、<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で<ruby>暮<rt>く</rt></ruby>らせるのが<ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しかった。<br>*Cramped as the room was, I was glad to be living on my own.*

<ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>い<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいる**くせに**、<ruby>家具<rt>かぐ</rt></ruby>ばかり<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>っている。<br>*He lives in a cramped little room and still keeps buying furniture.*

`ながらも` accepts the drawback and moves on; `くせに` uses it as ammunition. `ながらも` also has no same-subject-must-be-human requirement of the kind `くせに` imposes, which is one reason it survives in formal writing.

---

## ～くせに · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>くせに → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**の**くせに<br>*`くせ` is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs `の`. な-adjectives need `な`: ✅<code><ruby>下手<rt>へた</rt></ruby>なくせに</code>, ❌<code><ruby>下手<rt>へた</rt></ruby>だくせに</code>.*

# ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも

**Meaning** · although; despite - a quality is granted, and something that does not fit it is asserted anyway

This is the **second** `ながら`. The one you met at N4 stacks two actions on top of each other ("while eating"); this one concedes. It grants a fact - the room is small, he knew the risk, she is only a child - and then states something that the fact should have ruled out.

The two are told apart by shape, not by guesswork. Concessive `ながら` attaches to い-adjectives, な-adjective stems, nouns and `ている` forms, none of which the "while" `ながら` can take. And `ながらも`, with the extra `も`, is always concessive.

---

## ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Overview

On this batch's axis of how much the speaker minds, concessive `ながら` minds least of all. It is dignified rather than annoyed, and it is often outright warm: <ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>いながらも<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>我<rt>わ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>家<rt>や</rt></ruby> is a compliment to a small flat.

---

## ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb of knowing／thinking | ます-stem + ながら | <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>りながら |
| Verb in ている | てい + ながら（も） | <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かっている → <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かっていながら |
| い-adjective | plain form + ながら（も） | <ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>いながらも |
| な-adjective | stem + ながら（も） | <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>ながら |
| Noun | Noun + ながら（も） | <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども → <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもながら |
| Noun + に (adverbial) | Noun + ながらに | <ruby>涙<rt>なみだ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>涙<rt>なみだ</rt></ruby>ながらに |
| Existence, fixed | いながらにして | <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>にいながらにして |

---

## ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - な-adjectives and nouns attach **bare**: ✅ <code><ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>ながら</code>, ❌<code><ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>なながら</code>, ❌<code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもだながら</code>. There is no `な` and no `だ` in front of this `ながら`.

> **Note** - `いながら` is just `～ている` plus `ながら` with the `て` swallowed in speech and writing alike: <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っていながら. It is the standard way to concede that somebody **was aware** and acted anyway, and it is noticeably sharper than a plain `のに` because awareness is built into it.

---

## ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Examples

<ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>い**ながらも**、<ruby>居心地<rt>いごこち</rt></ruby>のいい<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>です。<br>*It's a small room, but a comfortable one.*

**<ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>ながら**、<ruby>今回<rt>こんかい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>見送<rt>みおく</rt></ruby>らせていただきます。<br>*Regrettably, we'll be passing on it this time.*

<ruby>危険<rt>きけん</rt></ruby>だと<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>り**ながら**、<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>しようとしなかった。<br>*Everyone knew it was dangerous, and no one would say anything.*

<ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>いと<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かってい**ながら**、なかなかやめられません。<br>*I know full well it's bad for me, and still I can't quite stop.*

---

## ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>**ながら**、<ruby>常連<rt>じょうれん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>全国<rt>ぜんこく</rt></ruby>にいるそうだ。<br>*Small as it is, the shop apparently has regulars all over the country.*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**ながらに**、<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>が<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>をしていることは<ruby>感<rt>かん</rt></ruby>じていた。<br>*Young as I was, I could tell my mother was pushing herself too hard.*

<ruby>貧<rt>まず</rt></ruby>しい**ながらも**、<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby><ruby>全員<rt>ぜんいん</rt></ruby>がよく<ruby>笑<rt>わら</rt></ruby>う<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>だった。<br>*Poor though we were, ours was a house where everybody laughed a lot.*

<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に**いながらにして**、<ruby>世界中<rt>せかいじゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>けられる<ruby>時代<rt>じだい</rt></ruby>になりました。<br>*We've reached an age where you can take classes from anywhere in the world without leaving home.*

---

## ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Nuance & register

Concessive `ながら` is **written and formal**. In an essay, a notice, a speech or a carefully worded email it is entirely ordinary; in relaxed conversation it survives mainly as a handful of set phrases, and using it freely in chat sounds like you have swallowed a textbook.

The set phrases are worth learning as vocabulary, because they are extremely common:

---

## ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Nuance & register (cont.)

- <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>ながら - "regrettably", the standard soft refusal.
- <ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>ながら - "if I may be so bold", before a delicate question.
- <ruby>恥<rt>は</rt></ruby>ずかしながら - "I'm ashamed to say."
- <ruby>我<rt>われ</rt></ruby>ながら - "even if I say so myself."
- いつもながら - "as ever."
- <ruby>生<rt>う</rt></ruby>まれながら（に） - "from birth, innately."

---

## ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Nuance & register (cont.)

`ながらも` is the emphatic version. The `も` makes the concession unmistakable, which is useful precisely because bare `ながら` could be read as "while". It also adds a faint note of admiration, which is why it collocates so happily with things that are small, poor or difficult but turn out well.

`いながらにして` is a fixed expression meaning "without stirring from where you are", and it has drifted into technology writing: <ruby>自宅<rt>じたく</rt></ruby>にいながらにして <ruby>診察<rt>しんさつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>けられる.

---

## Concessive ～ながら vs simultaneous ～ながら

> **Rule of thumb** - look at what is in front of it. An adjective, a noun or a `ている` form can only be the **concessive** one. A plain action verb in ます-stem form is normally the **"while"** one, unless the verb is about knowing or thinking.

<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>き**ながら**、<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>をしています。<br>*I do the washing up with music on.*

<ruby>失敗<rt>しっぱい</rt></ruby>すると<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い**ながら**、<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>めなかった。<br>*I thought it would go wrong, and I didn't stop him.*

---

## Concessive ～ながら vs simultaneous ～ながら (cont.)

Both take a ます-stem, and the second is concessive only because <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code> is a verb of mental attitude - you cannot "do thinking while" in the way you can do listening while. When in doubt, add `も`: <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いながらも</code> can only mean "although."

The "while" `ながら` also demands two activities that last long enough to overlap; the concessive one has no such requirement, because nothing is overlapping.

---

## ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · ～ながらも vs ～のに

> **Rule of thumb** - same contradiction, different voice. `のに` is spoken and carries the speaker's dissatisfaction. `ながらも` is written and carries composure, and often respect.

<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて**ながらも**、<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いて<ruby>司会<rt>しかい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>務<rt>つと</rt></ruby>めていた。<br>*It was his first time, and yet he chaired it calmly.*

<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めてな**のに**、ずいぶん<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いていたね。<br>*For a first go you were awfully calm, weren't you.*

The `ながらも` version would suit a written report of the event; the `のに` version is what you say to the person afterwards. Note the small formal difference too: `のに` needs `な` after a noun, `ながらも` needs nothing.

---

## ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · ～ながらも vs ～つつも

> **Rule of thumb** - near twins, both literary. `つつも` attaches only to a ます-stem, so it cannot concede an adjective or a noun; `ながらも` can.

<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>だと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い**つつも**、もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>応募<rt>おうぼ</rt></ruby>してみた。<br>*Even while thinking it was hopeless, I applied one more time.*

<ruby>不安<rt>ふあん</rt></ruby>**ながらも**、<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めた<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>選<rt>えら</rt></ruby>んだ。<br>*Uneasy though I was, I chose the path I'd decided on myself.*

`つつも` is the more bookish of the two and is almost always about an inner conflict - the speaker knew better and did it anyway. `ながらも` covers a much wider range, including plain external facts like a room being small.

---

## ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>るながら → ✅ <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>りながら<br>*`ながら` takes the ます-stem, exactly as the "while" version does. The dictionary form never appears before it.*

- ❌ <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>なながら → ✅ <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>ながら<br>*な-adjectives attach by the bare stem. The same goes for nouns: ✅<code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもながら</code>, ❌<code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもだながら</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>ながらも、<ruby>今回<rt>こんかい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>です → ✅ <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>ながら、<ruby>今回<rt>こんかい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>です<br>*<code><ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>ながら</code> is a frozen adverb meaning "regrettably" and does not take the extra `も`. The `も` version belongs to live concessions such as <code><ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>いながらも</code>.*

---

## ～ながら / ～いながら / ～ながらも · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しながら、<ruby>成績<rt>せいせき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がらない → ✅ <ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>している**のに**、<ruby>成績<rt>せいせき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がらない<br>*With an ordinary action verb, `ながら` is read as "while", and "while studying, my grades don't rise" is not a sentence. Use `のに`, or make the awareness explicit with <code><ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>していながら</code>.*

{reveal}

# ～にしては

**Meaning** · for ~; considering it's ~ - measured against this standard, the result is not what you would predict

`にしては` names a yardstick and then reports that the thing being judged does not fit it. A first-year student who writes a firm hand; a March day that feels like May; a first attempt that came out well. The yardstick is real and specific, and the result overshoots or undershoots it.

On this batch's axis of how much the speaker minds, `にしては` sits at the mild end alongside `わりに`. It is an **evaluation**, not a complaint - most often a compliment, occasionally a gentle dig, and almost never anything sharper.

---

## ～にしては · Formation

`にしては` attaches to a noun or to a plain-form verb clause. Nothing intervenes: no `だ`, no `な`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + にしては | <ruby>一年生<rt>いちねんせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>一年生<rt>いちねんせい</rt></ruby>にしては |
| Noun of time／season | Noun + にしては | <ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby>にしては |
| Verb | plain form + にしては | <ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>うにしては |
| Verb (past) | plain past + にしては | <ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>った → <ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ったにしては |
| Verb (ている) | ている + にしては | <ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>している → <ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>しているにしては |
| Referring back | それにしては | - → それにしては<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だ |

---

## ～にしては · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the standard must be **specific enough to predict something**. ✅<code>この<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>にしては</code>, ❌<code><ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>にしては</code>. A bare "price" predicts nothing; *this* price predicts a great deal. When the standard is general rather than definite, the pattern you want is `わりに`.

> **Note** - い-adjectives and な-adjectives do not attach to `にしては` directly. ❌<code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いにしては</code> → ✅<code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>にしては</code>, or switch to <code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いわりに</code>, which takes adjectives happily.

---

## ～にしては · Examples

<ruby>一年生<rt>いちねんせい</rt></ruby>**にしては**、しっかりした<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きますね。<br>*For a first-year, she writes a very steady hand.*

<ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby>**にしては**<ruby>暖<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かい<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*It's been unusually warm for March.*

<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>った**にしては**、よくできていますよ。<br>*For a first attempt, that's turned out well.*

この<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>**にしては**、<ruby>生地<rt>きじ</rt></ruby>がしっかりしている。<br>*For this price, the fabric is remarkably solid.*

---

## ～にしては · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby><ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>った**にしては**、<ruby>傷<rt>きず</rt></ruby>が<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>ないですね。<br>*For a car that's been driven ten years, there are hardly any scratches.*

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たばかりだと<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っていた**にしては**、<ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby>がとても<ruby>自然<rt>しぜん</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*Considering he said he'd only just arrived in Japan, his honorific language is very natural.*

<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きな<ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>だった**にしては**、<ruby>被害<rt>ひがい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>なくて<ruby>済<rt>す</rt></ruby>んだ。<br>*For an earthquake that size, the damage was mercifully limited.*

<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めた**にしては**、<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>計画<rt>けいかく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>細<rt>こま</rt></ruby>かいところまでよくできている。<br>*For something decided at the last minute, the trip is planned down to the details.*

---

## ～にしては · Nuance & register

`にしては` is register-neutral. It appears unchanged in `ます` speech, in plain speech and in writing, and nothing about it is stiff or slangy. It pairs naturally with degree adverbs - `ずいぶん`, `なかなか`, `けっこう`, <code><ruby>意外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>に</code> - because the whole point of the sentence is that the result is off the predicted mark.

---

## ～にしては · Nuance & register (cont.)

What you do have to watch is the **social arithmetic**. `にしては` says out loud that your expectations for the standard were low. That is fine for a beginner, a first attempt or the weather, and it is not fine aimed upward at a person: 「<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>にしてはお<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>ですね」 tells your teacher you assumed teachers were bad at this. With superiors, praise them without the yardstick, or use `さすが` - 「さすが<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>ですね」 - which says the opposite: exactly what one would expect.

`それにしては` is the stand-alone version, pointing back at something already said: 「<ruby>徹夜<rt>てつや</rt></ruby>したんです。」「それにしては<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>ですね。」 Note that this is `それにしても` with one particle changed, and the meanings diverge completely - see the next section.

---

## ～にしては · ～にしては vs ～わりに

> **Rule of thumb** - `にしては` needs a **definite** standard; `わりに` accepts a **general** one and attaches to adjectives as well. Where both fit, they mean much the same thing.

このかばんは<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>の**わりに**じょうぶだ。<br>*This bag is sturdy for what it cost.*

<ruby>三千円<rt>さんぜんえん</rt></ruby>のかばん**にしては**じょうぶだ。<br>*For a three-thousand-yen bag, it's sturdy.*

---

## ～にしては · ～にしては vs ～わりに (cont.)

Both are natural. The difference is that `わりに` can leave "price" as an abstract scale, while `にしては` wants an actual figure or an actual category to measure against. That is also why `わりに` handles adjectives - <code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いわりに</code>, <code><ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>いわりに</code> - and `にしては` does not.

---

## ～にしては · ～にしては vs ～にしても

> **Rule of thumb** - `は` evaluates, `も` concedes. `にしては` says "measured against X, this is surprising." `にしても` says "even granting X, this still is not good enough."

<ruby>初日<rt>しょにち</rt></ruby>**にしては**、よく<ruby>売<rt>う</rt></ruby>れたほうだと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。<br>*For an opening day, I'd say it sold well.*

<ruby>初日<rt>しょにち</rt></ruby>**にしても**、この<ruby>数字<rt>すうじ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>なすぎませんか。<br>*Opening day or not, isn't this figure far too low?*

Same noun, same day, opposite verdicts. One particle decides whether you are praising the result or refusing to accept the excuse.

---

## ～にしては · ～にしては vs ～のに

> **Rule of thumb** - `のに` needs a **whole clause** and carries the speaker's dissatisfaction. `にしては` takes a bare noun as well, and carries mild surprise at how the measurement came out.

<ruby>一週間<rt>いっしゅうかん</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>していない**にしては**、<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>に<ruby>弾<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>けている。<br>*For someone who's only practised a week, she plays well.*

<ruby>一週間<rt>いっしゅうかん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>した**のに**、まだうまく<ruby>弾<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>けない。<br>*I practised for a whole week and I still can't play it properly.*

The first weighs the result against the effort and finds it impressive. The second weighs the same two things and feels cheated. If the sentence is a compliment, `にしては` is nearly always the safer choice; `のに` used for praise needs the rest of the sentence to make the warmth obvious.

---

## ～にしては · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だにしては → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>にしては<br>*Nouns attach bare. The `だ` here comes from thinking of `にしては` as following a complete sentence, which it does not have to.*

- ❌ プロ<ruby>野球<rt>やきゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>選手<rt>せんしゅ</rt></ruby>にしては<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>い → ✅ プロ<ruby>野球<rt>やきゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>選手<rt>せんしゅ</rt></ruby>**だけあって**<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>い<br>*A professional athlete being fast is exactly what the standard predicts, and `にしては` requires the result to defy the standard. When the result confirms it, the pattern is `だけあって`.*

- ❌ <ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>にしては<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → ✅ この<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>にしては<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い ／ <ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>の**わりに**<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い<br>*A bare "price" is not a definite standard. Either pin it down with `この`, or switch to `わりに`.*

---

## ～にしては · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いにしては、<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い → ✅ <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い**わりに**、<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い<br>*い-adjectives do not attach to `にしては`. Use `わりに`, or supply a noun: <code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>にしては</code>.*

# ～わりに

**Meaning** · for ~; considering ~ - the result is out of proportion to what the premise leads you to expect

The `わり` in `わりに` is the noun meaning "proportion, ratio", and that is exactly what the pattern measures. Set one fact against another - the price against the quality, the age against the speed, the hours put in against the marks that came back - and `わりに` says the two do not scale together.

On this batch's axis of how much the speaker minds, `わりに` is mild, like `にしては`. It notices a mismatch and comments on it. Whether the comment is admiring or slightly disappointed depends entirely on which way the mismatch runs.

---

## ～わりに · Formation

`わりに` behaves like the noun it came from: nouns link with `の`, な-adjectives with `な`. Adding `は` - `わりには` - is optional and very common.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + の + わりに（は） | <ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby> → <ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>のわりに |
| Verb | plain form + わりに（は） | よく<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → よく<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べるわりに |
| Verb (past) | plain past + わりに（は） | <ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>した → <ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>したわりに |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + わりに（は） | <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ていない → <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ていないわりに |
| い-adjective | plain form + わりに（は） | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いわりに |
| な-adjective | な + わりに（は） | <ruby>地味<rt>じみ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>地味<rt>じみ</rt></ruby>なわりに |
| Adverb (different word) | わりに／わりと + adjective | わりとおいしい |

---

## ～わりに · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the two halves must be **out of proportion**. If the result is what the premise predicts, `わりに` has nothing to say: ❌<code>たくさん<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>したわりに<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>になった</code>. Effort matching result is `だけあって`.

> **Note** - usually written in kana, though <ruby>割<rt>わり</rt></ruby>に is not wrong. Adding `は` sharpens the contrast a little: <code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いわりに</code> simply notes the mismatch, while <code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いわりには</code> leans on it. Both are entirely ordinary.

---

## ～わりに · Examples

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い**わりに**、<ruby>味<rt>あじ</rt></ruby>がいい。<br>*This place is good for what it charges.*

<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>の**わりに**<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>い。<br>*My father is quick on his feet for his age.*

<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>はよく<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる**わりに**、<ruby>全然<rt>ぜんぜん</rt></ruby><ruby>太<rt>ふと</rt></ruby>らないんです。<br>*My sister eats a lot and never puts on any weight.*

<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>かっている<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>い**わりには**、<ruby>点数<rt>てんすう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>びない。<br>*For all the hours he spends at his desk, his marks aren't going up.*

---

## ～わりに · Examples (cont.)

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>から<ruby>遠<rt>とお</rt></ruby>い**わりに**、<ruby>家賃<rt>やちん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>すぎませんか。<br>*Isn't the rent rather high for somewhere this far from the station?*

<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>に<ruby>半年<rt>はんとし</rt></ruby>かけた**わりには**、<ruby>当日<rt>とうじつ</rt></ruby>はあっという<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わってしまった。<br>*For six months of preparation, the day itself was over in no time.*

あの<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>は<ruby>評判<rt>ひょうばん</rt></ruby>がいい**わりに**、<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>がずいぶん<ruby>空<rt>す</rt></ruby>いていた。<br>*For a film with such good word of mouth, the cinema was pretty empty.*

<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きだと<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っている**わりに**、<ruby>道具<rt>どうぐ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つも<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていない。<br>*For someone who says he loves the mountains, he doesn't own a single piece of kit.*

---

## ～わりに · Nuance & register

`わりに` is colloquial-neutral: at home in conversation, in reviews, in casual writing, and unremarkable in `ます` speech. It carries no politeness of its own and no strong emotion. What it does carry is a faint air of **appraisal** - the speaker has weighed two things against each other and is reporting the ratio.

Which direction the appraisal runs is decided by the content, and both are common:

- **In the thing's favour** - <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いわりにおいしい, <ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>のわりに<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だ.
- **Against it** - <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いわりに<ruby>量<rt>りょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>ない,<br>*<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>をかけたわりに<ruby>中身<rt>なかみ</rt></ruby>がない.*

---

## ～わりに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Because the second use is so easy, `わりに` aimed at a person can sting a little more than `にしては` does: 「<ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>いわりに<ruby>体力<rt>たいりょく</rt></ruby>がないね」 is not a compliment. As with `にしては`, avoid pointing it at someone senior to you.

Then there is the **adverb** `わりに`, more often said as `わりと`, meaning "fairly, rather": 「<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>はわりと<ruby>暖<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かいね。」 It has no second half and compares nothing in particular - it is just a hedge, roughly the strength of `けっこう`. Same string, different word, and context separates them easily once you know both exist.

---

## ～わりに · ～わりに vs ～にしては

> **Rule of thumb** - `にしては` needs a **definite** yardstick and usually flatters. `わりに` takes a **general** one, attaches to adjectives, and is just as happy pointing out that something under-delivers.

<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby><ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>った**にしては**、よく<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せるほうだと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う。<br>*For three years of lessons, I'd say he speaks well.*

<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby><ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>った**わりに**、まだ<ruby>自己<rt>じこ</rt></ruby><ruby>紹介<rt>しょうかい</rt></ruby>しかできない。<br>*For three years of lessons, he can still only introduce himself.*

---

## ～わりに · ～わりに vs ～にしては (cont.)

Both take the same clause, and both are grammatical. The give-away is the direction of the verdict: `にしては` sets a low bar and reports it cleared, so a disappointed `にしては` sentence sounds strange, while `わりに` is neutral about which way the imbalance falls. And only `わりに` can attach to a bare adjective: ✅<code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いわりに</code>, ❌<code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いにしては</code>.

---

## ～わりに · ～わりに vs ～のに

> **Rule of thumb** - `わりに` weighs two things and finds the ratio off. `のに` says two facts should not go together at all, and adds the speaker's dissatisfaction. `わりに` needs a scale; `のに` does not.

この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>厚<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>い**わりに**、<ruby>軽<rt>かる</rt></ruby>い。<br>*This dictionary is light for its thickness.*

<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った**のに**、<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>開<rt>ひら</rt></ruby>いていない。<br>*I bought a dictionary and haven't opened it once.*

Thickness and weight are two points on scales, so they can be out of proportion - `わりに`. Buying a dictionary and never opening it is not a matter of proportion at all; it is a plain contradiction, with the speaker's chagrin attached, which is `のに`.

---

## ～わりに · ～わりに (proportion) vs わりと (adverb)

> **Rule of thumb** - if there is something to compare against, it is the proportion `わりに` and it needs `の` or a clause in front. If there is nothing to compare against, it is the adverb, and it means "fairly."

この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>は**わりと**<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>でした。<br>*That question was fairly straightforward.*

この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>た<ruby>目<rt>め</rt></ruby>の**わりに**<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>でした。<br>*That question was easier than it looked.*

The first tells you only that it was not hard. The second measures the difficulty against the intimidating look of it. In speech `わりと` is the commoner form of the adverb; `わりに` on its own can be either word, and the presence or absence of a comparison decides.

---

## ～わりに · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>わりに<ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>く<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>える → ✅ <ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>**の**わりに<ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>く<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>える<br>*`わり` is a noun, so a noun in front of it links with `の` - exactly like <code><ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>のせいで</code> or <code><ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>割合<rt>わりあい</rt></ruby></code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かわりに<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い → ✅ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**な**わりに<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い<br>*な-adjectives link with `な`. い-adjectives, by contrast, attach in their plain form with nothing added.*

- ❌ よく<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>たわりに、<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>になった → ✅ よく<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>た**だけあって**、<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>になった<br>*Sleeping well and feeling well are in proportion, so there is no mismatch for `わりに` to report.*

## ～わりに · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい____、<ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>はあまりない。

{reveal}
<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい**わりに**、<ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>はあまりない。

{reveal}
Size and strength are two scales that ought to move together, and the sentence reports that they do not. い-adjectives attach in their plain form, so nothing goes between <code><ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい</code> and `わりに`.

## ～わりに · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: この<ruby>旅館<rt>りょかん</rt></ruby>は（<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>のわりに・<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>にしては）<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>が<ruby>立派<rt>りっぱ</rt></ruby>だ。

{reveal}
この<ruby>旅館<rt>りょかん</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>のわりに**<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>が<ruby>立派<rt>りっぱ</rt></ruby>だ。

{reveal}
"Price" here is a general scale rather than a definite figure, and `にしては` needs something definite to measure against. Pin the figure down - この<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>にしては or <ruby>一万円<rt>いちまんえん</rt></ruby>にしては - and `にしては` becomes available again.

## ～わりに · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "For a small company, they pay surprisingly well."

{reveal}
<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さい<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>の**わりに**、<ruby>給料<rt>きゅうりょう</rt></ruby>がいい。

{reveal}
A noun phrase links with `の`. You could also say <ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>規模<rt>きぼ</rt></ruby>のわりに, and <code><ruby>意外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>に</code> before `いい` would carry the "surprisingly" more explicitly.

## ～わりに · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>わりに、<ruby>観光客<rt>かんこうきゃく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>ない。

{reveal}
✅ この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>**な**わりに、<ruby>観光客<rt>かんこうきゃく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>ない。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby></code> is a な-adjective, and `わりに` behaves like a noun, so the link is `な`. Compare the noun pattern, which links with `の`: <ruby>有名<rt>ゆうめい</rt></ruby>さのわりに would be the noun version of the same idea.

## ～わりに · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>をかけたわりに、いい<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なかった。 (b) <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>をかけたのに、いい<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なかった。

{reveal}
(a) is an assessment of the ratio: a lot of time went in, not much came out, and the speaker is stating the imbalance more than complaining about it. It would sit comfortably in a written review of a project.

{reveal}
(b) is a complaint. `のに` says the time invested should have guaranteed a result, and the speaker feels let down - frustration first, arithmetic second. Both are correct Japanese, and the choice tells the listener how much you mind.
