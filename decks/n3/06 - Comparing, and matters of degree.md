# ～につれて

**Meaning** · as one thing gradually progresses, another gradually changes with it

`～につれて` ties two slow changes together and says they move as a pair. Neither side is a single event: something is *in the process of* increasing, advancing, ageing, warming, and something else shifts along the way. English usually says this with "as", and Japanese wants both halves to be movement along a scale.

---

## ～につれて · Formation

The clause before `につれて` is always **non-past**, however clearly the sentence is set in the past. Tense is carried by the final verb only.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (change of state) | V(dictionary) + につれて | <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える → <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えるにつれて |
| Verb, formal writing | V(dictionary) + につれ | <ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>むにつれ |
| Noun naming a process | Noun + につれて | <ruby>成長<rt>せいちょう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>成長<rt>せいちょう</rt></ruby>につれて |
| Noun with no change in it | Noun + が + verb + につれて | <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がたつにつれて |

---

## ～につれて · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - only nouns that already contain the idea of change work directly: <code><ruby>成長<rt>せいちょう</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>変化<rt>へんか</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>発展<rt>はってん</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>回復<rt>かいふく</rt></ruby></code>. A plain noun needs a verb first - ❌<code><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>につれて</code> → ✅<code><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がたつにつれて</code>.

---

## ～につれて · Examples

<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がたつ**につれて**、<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>くなった。<br>*As time went on, the wind picked up.*

<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る**につれて**、<ruby>空気<rt>くうき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>冷<rt>つめ</rt></ruby>たくなった。<br>*As we climbed the mountain, the air turned colder.*

<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>短<rt>みじか</rt></ruby>くなる**につれて**、<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby><ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きるのがつらくなる。<br>*As the days get shorter, getting up in the morning gets harder.*

<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む**につれて**、スタンドの<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなっていった。<br>*As the match went on, the crowd got louder and louder.*

---

## ～につれて · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの<ruby>成長<rt>せいちょう</rt></ruby>**につれて**、<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>のサイズが<ruby>毎年<rt>まいとし</rt></ruby><ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わる。<br>*As a child grows, their shoe size changes every year.*

<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>重<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>ねる**につれて**、<ruby>指<rt>ゆび</rt></ruby>が<ruby>自然<rt>しぜん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>くようになってきた。<br>*As I put in more practice, my fingers started moving on their own.*

<ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む**につれて**、<ruby>駅前<rt>えきまえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>景色<rt>けしき</rt></ruby>はすっかり<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わってしまった。<br>*As the construction went ahead, the area in front of the station changed completely.*

<ruby>人口<rt>じんこう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>減<rt>へ</rt></ruby>る**につれ**、<ruby>村<rt>むら</rt></ruby>の<ruby>行事<rt>ぎょうじ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つずつなくなっていった。<br>*As the population fell, the village's annual events disappeared one by one.*

---

## ～につれて · Nuance & register

`～につれて` is neutral in politeness - it sits inside the sentence, so the formality of the page is set by the ending (<code><ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わる</code> / <code><ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わります</code>), not by the pattern. What it *does* carry is a mildly written flavour. It is at home in explanations, reports, narration and the sort of thing a newsreader says; in relaxed conversation people often reach for `～と、だんだん…` instead: <ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>になると、だんだん<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなる.

Clipping it to `につれ` raises the register another notch. That form belongs to written Japanese - newspapers, official notices, essays - and sounds stiff spoken aloud.

---

## ～につれて · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two habits go with the pattern. The second clause is normally a **description of change**, so it very often ends in `～くなる`, `～ていく` or `～てくる`. And because the sentence is reporting an observed process, the second clause cannot be a request, a command, an invitation or a statement of intention. "As it gets colder, please close the window" is not a `につれて` sentence; it is a `たら` or `と` sentence.

Finally, the speaker is standing outside watching. `～につれて` describes; it does not persuade. If you want the emotional weight of "the more, the more", the correlative `～ば～ほど` is the pattern with the pressure in it.

---

## ～につれて · ～につれて vs ～に従って

> **Rule of thumb** - in the parallel-change sense the two are close to interchangeable. `に従って` additionally has a life `につれて` does not have at all: **following** rules, instructions and arrows.

Where both work, `につれて` describes a change that simply happens, while `に従って` leans a little towards one thing being led by the other, and is the stiffer of the two:

<ruby>北<rt>きた</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む**につれて**、<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>くなった。<br>*The further north we went, the deeper the snow got.*

But only `に従って` can take an instruction as its object:

<ruby>係員<rt>かかりいん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>指示<rt>しじ</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>てください。<br>*Please go outside following the attendant's instructions.*

---

## ～につれて · ～につれて vs ～に従って (cont.)

❌<code><ruby>指示<rt>しじ</rt></ruby>につれて</code> is simply not Japanese. If you can replace the English "as" with "in accordance with", you need `に従って`.

---

## ～につれて · ～につれて vs ～とともに

> **Rule of thumb** - `につれて` needs both sides to be **gradual**. `とともに` only needs them to be **simultaneous**, so it also covers sudden events and plain accompaniment.

<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>が<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>づく**につれて**、<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>の<ruby>色<rt>いろ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わってきた。<br>*As spring drew near, the colour of the mountains began to change.*

Swap in `とともに` there and nothing breaks. But `とともに` also does this, and `につれて` cannot:

ベルが<ruby>鳴<rt>な</rt></ruby>る**とともに**、<ruby>生徒<rt>せいと</rt></ruby>たちが<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*The moment the bell rang, the students broke into a run.*

The bell is instantaneous - there is no scale to travel along - so ❌<code><ruby>鳴<rt>な</rt></ruby>るにつれて</code> is wrong. `とともに` is also the one that means "together with (a person)": <ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>とともに<ruby>暮<rt>く</rt></ruby>らす.

---

## ～につれて · ～につれて vs ～ば～ほど

> **Rule of thumb** - `につれて` reports one actual process unfolding. `～ば～ほど` states a general proportion, true whenever you test it, and it can be hypothetical.

<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>する**につれて**、<ruby>発音<rt>はつおん</rt></ruby>がよくなってきた。<br>*As I practised, my pronunciation improved.*

That is a report on your own past few months. The correlative version makes a claim about the world instead:

<ruby>発音<rt>はつおん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>すれ**ば**する**ほど**よくなる。<br>*The more you practise, the better your pronunciation gets.*

---

## ～につれて · ～につれて vs ～ば～ほど (cont.)

The second one can be said by someone who has never practised in their life. It also allows advice and predictions after it, which `につれて` does not.

---

## ～につれて · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>す**につれて**、<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>くなった。 → ✅ <ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>す**と**、<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>くなった。<br>*Flipping a switch is instantaneous. `につれて` needs a change that takes time and passes through middle stages.*

- ❌ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**たったにつれて** → ✅ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**たつにつれて**<br>*The clause before `につれて` is always non-past. The past tense goes on the end of the sentence.*

- ❌ <ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>くなる**につれて**、<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>をたくさん<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでください。 → ✅ <ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>くなる**と**、<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>をたくさん<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでください。<br>*A request cannot follow `につれて`. The second clause has to describe a change, not tell the listener to do something.*

---

## ～につれて · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>**につれて**、<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>みが<ruby>消<rt>き</rt></ruby>えた。 → ✅ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がたつ**につれて**、<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>みが<ruby>消<rt>き</rt></ruby>えていった。<br>*<code><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby></code> is not itself a change. Add the verb that supplies one.*

---

## ～につれて · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>を____につれて、<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちに<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いたくなる。

{reveal}
<ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る**につれて、<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちに<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いたくなる。

{reveal}
The verb before `につれて` stays in the dictionary form, and <code><ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る</code> is the standard collocation for ageing, which is exactly the kind of slow, stage-by-stage change this pattern wants.

---

## ～につれて · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>（につれて・に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って）<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いたら、<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>た。

{reveal}
<ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いたら、<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>た。

{reveal}
A map is something you obey, not a process that unfolds. `につれて` only links two gradual changes, so it cannot take a set of directions as its object.

---

## ～につれて · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "As the meeting went on, everyone got tired."

{reveal}
<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む**につれて**、みんな<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れてきた。

{reveal}
Both halves move by degrees, which is what licenses `につれて`, and the ending `～てきた` is the natural way to show the tiredness accumulating rather than arriving all at once.

---

## ～につれて · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>くにつれて、<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>づく**につれて**、<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>くなった。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>く</code> happens in an instant, and so does <code><ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> - neither side offers a scale. Replacing them with <code><ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>づく</code> and <code><ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>くなる</code> gives two gradual changes to link.

---

## ～につれて · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>人口<rt>じんこう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える**につれて**、ごみの<ruby>量<rt>りょう</rt></ruby>も<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えた。 (b) <ruby>人口<rt>じんこう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えれ**ば**<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える**ほど**、ごみの<ruby>量<rt>りょう</rt></ruby>も<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える。

{reveal}
(a) is a report about a particular place over a particular stretch of time: the population went up, and the rubbish went up alongside it. It is history.

{reveal}
(b) is a general law with no dates attached - for any town, more people means more rubbish. It would fit in an argument or a warning, and unlike (a) it can be said about a population increase that has not happened yet.

# ～に従って

**Meaning** · (1) in accordance with ～, following ～; (2) as ～ progresses, ～ changes with it

<code>～に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って</code> is the て-form of the verb <code><ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>う</code>, "to follow, to obey", and both of its meanings come straight out of that. Follow a set of instructions and you get sense (1). Let one change follow another and you get sense (2), where it is close to `～につれて`. Written as a single sentence-opening word, したがって is a third thing entirely: "therefore".

---

## ～に従って · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun: rule, order, model | Noun + に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って | <ruby>説明書<rt>せつめいしょ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>説明書<rt>せつめいしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って |
| Noun naming a process | Noun + に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って | <ruby>時代<rt>じだい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>変化<rt>へんか</rt></ruby> → <ruby>時代<rt>じだい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>変化<rt>へんか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って |
| Verb (change of state) | V(dictionary) + に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って | <ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>むに<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って |
| Formal writing | ～に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>規則<rt>きそく</rt></ruby> → <ruby>規則<rt>きそく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>い |
| Modifying a noun | ～に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>った + Noun | <ruby>指示<rt>しじ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>指示<rt>しじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>った<ruby>行動<rt>こうどう</rt></ruby> |
| As an ordinary verb | Noun + に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>う | ルールに<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>う |

---

## ～に従って · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - <code><ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>う</code> is an う-verb (<ruby>五段<rt>ごだん</rt></ruby> / godan / Group 1), so its て-form is <code><ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って</code> with a small つ. ❌<code><ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>て</code> and ❌<code><ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>いて</code> are both wrong. As with `～につれて`, the clause before it stays **non-past**.

---

## ～に従って · Examples

<ruby>矢印<rt>やじるし</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>んでください。<br>*Please go on, following the arrows.*

ルール**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**ゲームをしましょう。<br>*Let's play the game by the rules.*

<ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**、<ruby>塩分<rt>えんぶん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>減<rt>へ</rt></ruby>らしています。<br>*I'm cutting down on salt as the doctor told me to.*

<ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**、<ruby>夜<rt>よ</rt></ruby><ruby>更<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>かしがつらくなってきた。<br>*As I get older, staying up late has started to take its toll.*

---

## ～に従って · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>技術<rt>ぎじゅつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**、<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>道具<rt>どうぐ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>かけなくなった。<br>*As technology moved on, the old tools stopped being a common sight.*

<ruby>地元<rt>じもと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>習慣<rt>しゅうかん</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**、<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>脱<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>いで<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がった。<br>*Following the local custom, I took off my shoes and stepped up into the room.*

<ruby>法律<rt>ほうりつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>改正<rt>かいせい</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>い**、<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>手続<rt>てつづ</rt></ruby>きが<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わります。<br>*In accordance with the revision of the law, the procedure changes from next month.*

マニュアル**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**<ruby>操作<rt>そうさ</rt></ruby>したはずなのに、<ruby>機械<rt>きかい</rt></ruby>はまったく<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>かなかった。<br>*I'm sure I operated it according to the manual, but the machine wouldn't move at all.*

---

## ～に従って · Nuance & register

This is a formal expression in both of its senses. In sense (1) it carries the weight of the verb it comes from: obeying, complying, submitting. Told to a friend that you cooked <code>レシピに<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って</code>, it sounds oddly solemn - conversation prefers `レシピどおりに` or <code>レシピを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ながら</code>. Where it belongs is signage, safety announcements, contracts, court reporting and workplace instructions.

Trimming to <code>に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>い</code> is the written-only variant, one register above the て-form and common in notices: <code><ruby>規則<rt>きそく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>い<ruby>処理<rt>しょり</rt></ruby>いたします</code>.

---

## ～に従って · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note who is doing the following. <code>に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って</code> puts the subject in the position of complying with something with authority behind it - a rule, an order, a doctor, a custom. Using it about a mere suggestion overstates the case, and using it about a person can sound like obedience rather than agreement: <code><ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って</code> is "doing as the manager directs", not "along with the manager".

In sense (2), the parallel-change use, it is drier and more analytical than `～につれて`, and turns up mostly in writing that is explaining a trend. It also has a faint implication that the second change is *driven* by the first rather than merely accompanying it.

---

## ～に従って · Nuance & register (cont.)

> **Note** - sentence-initially, したがって means "therefore". It is one of the most formal connectives in the language, at home in academic papers and legal writing: <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>接近<rt>せっきん</rt></ruby>している。したがって、<ruby>明日<rt>あす</rt></ruby>の<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>とする。 In speech you would say だから or ですから.

---

## ～に従って · ～に従って vs ～につれて

> **Rule of thumb** - for two changes moving together, both work and `に従って` is the stiffer choice. For following instructions, only `に従って` is possible.

<ruby>南<rt>みなみ</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>下<rt>くだ</rt></ruby>る**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**、<ruby>畑<rt>はたけ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>くなっていく。<br>*As you go further south, the fields get wider.*

Replace `に従って` with `につれて` there and almost nothing changes; the sentence just sounds a shade less like a geography textbook. Now the half `につれて` cannot reach:

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>のアドバイス**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**、<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>してみました。<br>*I rewrote it following my teacher's advice.*

---

## ～に従って · ～に従って vs ～につれて (cont.)

There is no gradual change here at all - just compliance - so ❌`アドバイスにつれて` is impossible. Test it in English: if "as" can become "in accordance with", it is `に従って`.

---

## ～に従って · ～に従って vs ～とおりに・～どおりに

> **Rule of thumb** - `どおりに` means the result **matches the model exactly**. `に従って` means you **took the instruction as your authority**. Everyday speech prefers `どおりに`.

<ruby>説明書<rt>せつめいしょ</rt></ruby>の**とおりに**<ruby>組<rt>く</rt></ruby>み<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>てたら、ねじが<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>余<rt>あま</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I assembled it exactly as the instructions said, and had one screw left over.*

<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方針<rt>ほうしん</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**、<ruby>報告<rt>ほうこく</rt></ruby><ruby>書<rt>しょ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>形式<rt>けいしき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>えました。<br>*We changed the format of the report in line with company policy.*

---

## ～に従って · ～に従って vs ～とおりに・～どおりに (cont.)

A policy is an authority you obey; a diagram is a model you copy. Note also the shape of the word: after a noun with `の` it is `とおりに` (<code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>ったとおりに</code>), but glued straight onto a noun it becomes `どおりに` - <code><ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>どおりに</code>, <code><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>どおりに</code>.

---

## に従って inside a sentence vs したがって at the head of one

> **Rule of thumb** - if the word is attached to a noun or verb, it means "following". If it is standing alone at the start of a sentence with a comma after it, it means "therefore".

<ruby>前例<rt>ぜんれい</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**<ruby>判断<rt>はんだん</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*We made the decision following precedent.*

<ruby>前例<rt>ぜんれい</rt></ruby>がない。**したがって**、<ruby>慎重<rt>しんちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>判断<rt>はんだん</rt></ruby>する<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある。<br>*There is no precedent. Therefore, the decision must be made carefully.*

Same characters, unrelated jobs. The connective is normally written in kana in modern Japanese, which helps, but not reliably - and hearing it aloud, only the position in the sentence tells you which one it is.

---

## ～に従って · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>説明書<rt>せつめいしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>**て** → ✅ <ruby>説明書<rt>せつめいしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>**って**<br>*<code><ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>う</code> is an う-verb, so its て-form doubles the consonant: <code><ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>指示<rt>しじ</rt></ruby>**につれて**<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>いてください。 → ✅ <ruby>指示<rt>しじ</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>いてください。<br>*Instructions are obeyed, not gradually undergone. `につれて` has no "in accordance with" meaning at all.*

- ❌ <ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>**ったに**<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って → ✅ <ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>**るに**<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って<br>*In the parallel-change sense the verb before it stays in the dictionary form, whatever the tense of the sentence.*

---

## ～に従って · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。**<ruby>従<rt>したが</rt></ruby>って**<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>です。（in casual speech） → ✅ <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。**だから**<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>です。<br>*Not ungrammatical, but したがって in a chat sounds like reading out a legal notice. Keep it for writing.*

---

# ～ば～ほど

**Meaning** · the more ～, the more ～ - two quantities that rise and fall together

`～ば～ほど` links a cause and a result and says they are **proportional**: turn the first one up and the second one goes up with it. The same word appears twice, once in the `ば` form and once in the plain form, which is what makes the pattern instantly recognisable. Unlike `～につれて`, it is not a report on something that happened - it is a claim about how things work.

---

## ～ば～ほど · Formation

The first copy of the word goes into the conditional `ば` form; the second copy goes into the form that can sit in front of `ほど`, which is the plain form for verbs and い-adjectives and the `な` form for な-adjectives.

---

## ～ば～ほど · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| う-verb | ば-form + V(dictionary) + ほど | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めば<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>むほど |
| る-verb | ば-form + V(dictionary) + ほど | <ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>える → <ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えれば<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えるほど |
| する | すれば + する + ほど | <ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>すれば<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>するほど |
| い-adjective | ～ければ + ～い + ほど | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ければ<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いほど |
| な-adjective | ～なら + ～な + ほど | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなら<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなほど |
| な-adjective (formal) | ～であれば + あるほど | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby> → <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>であればあるほど |
| Noun | ～であれば + あるほど | プロ → プロであればあるほど |
| Negative | ～なければ + ～ないほど | <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない → <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らなければ<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないほど |
| Shortened | ～ほど（the ば half dropped） | <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>いほどいい |

---

## ～ば～ほど · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - う-verb / る-verb are the labels this book uses for what other books call <ruby>五段<rt>ごだん</rt></ruby> / godan / Group 1 and <ruby>一段<rt>いちだん</rt></ruby> / ichidan / Group 2. For な-adjectives, both <code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなら<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなほど</code> and <code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かであればあるほど</code> are correct; the second is the written, formal one, and it is the only option after a noun.

---

## ～ば～ほど · Examples

この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>め**ば**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む**ほど**おもしろくなる。<br>*The more you read this book, the more interesting it gets.*

<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しけれ**ば**<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい**ほど**おいしい。<br>*The fresher the vegetables, the better they taste.*

<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えれ**ば**<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>える**ほど**、<ruby>答<rt>こた</rt></ruby>えが<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からなくなった。<br>*The more I thought about it, the less I understood the answer.*

<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>なら<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>な**ほど**いいです。<br>*The simpler the explanation, the better.*

---

## ～ば～ほど · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>けれ**ば**<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>い**ほど**<ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い。<br>*The bigger the room, the colder it is in winter.*

<ruby>道具<rt>どうぐ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>単純<rt>たんじゅん</rt></ruby>であれ**ば**ある**ほど**<ruby>壊<rt>こわ</rt></ruby>れにくい。<br>*The simpler a tool is, the less likely it is to break.*

<ruby>締<rt>し</rt></ruby>め<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>りが<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>づけ**ば**<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>づく**ほど**、なぜか<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>の<ruby>掃除<rt>そうじ</rt></ruby>がしたくなる。<br>*The closer the deadline gets, the more I somehow want to clean my room.*

<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>すのは、<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>をすれ**ば**する**ほど**<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いて<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せるようになりますよ。<br>*When it comes to speaking in public, the more you prepare, the more calmly you'll be able to talk.*

---

## ～ば～ほど · Nuance & register

The pattern itself is register-neutral. Politeness lives on the final verb, so <code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ければ<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いほどいい</code> and <code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ければ<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いほどいいです</code> are the same grammar at different levels, and both are perfectly ordinary in conversation.

Because it states a proportion rather than an event, it is the natural shape for advice, opinions, slogans and rules of thumb - <code><ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>ければ<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>いほどいい</code> is something you will hear said about applications, hospital visits and train tickets alike. It is equally at home in a report describing a correlation.

---

## ～ば～ほど · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two useful habits. First, the result half is very often an evaluation: `いい`, <code><ruby>楽<rt>らく</rt></ruby>だ</code>, <code><ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>だ</code>, <code><ruby>安心<rt>あんしん</rt></ruby>する</code>. Second, the result is frequently a *deterioration* despite the "more … more" English - <code><ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えれば<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えるほど <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からなくなる</code> is one of the most common sentences built on this pattern, and English speakers reaching for "the more … the less" should notice that Japanese puts the "less" in the verb, not in the pattern.

One restriction worth remembering: the two halves must be **the same word**. This is not a general "if … then" - it is one scale echoing itself. If the cause and the result are different things, you want a plain `ば`, `と` or `たら` sentence.

---

## ～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs ～につれて

> **Rule of thumb** - `につれて` narrates a change that actually took place. `ば～ほど` states a rule that holds every time, including in situations that have not happened.

<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>づく**につれて**、<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えてきた。<br>*As summer approached, more and more flowers appeared in front of the shop.*

That is one shop, one summer. The correlative version is a general truth and can be said in the middle of winter:

<ruby>気温<rt>きおん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がれ**ば**<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がる**ほど**、アイスがよく<ruby>売<rt>う</rt></ruby>れる。<br>*The higher the temperature, the better ice cream sells.*

---

## ～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs ～につれて (cont.)

Practical consequence: only `ば～ほど` combines comfortably with advice, guesses and conditions - <code><ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>めば<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む ほど<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くなりますよ</code>. Try that with `につれて` and it sounds like you are describing prices you have already watched fall.

---

## ～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs the shortened ～ほど

> **Rule of thumb** - the `ば` half can be dropped when the word is an adjective and the result is a simple evaluation. Dropping it is shorter and slightly more written; keeping it adds emphasis.

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るい**ほど**いい。<br>*The brighter the room, the better.*

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るけれ**ば**<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るい**ほど**いい。<br>*The brighter the room, the better it is - really, as bright as you can get it.*

---

## ～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs the shortened ～ほど (cont.)

The short version reads like a specification; the full version sounds like someone insisting. With verbs the short form is much rarer, and ❌<code><ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>するほど<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>になる</code> can be understood but sounds clipped - say the verb twice.

---

## ～ば～ほど · ～ば～ほど vs どんどん・ますます

> **Rule of thumb** - `ば～ほど` states a **relationship between two scales**. `どんどん` and `ますます` are adverbs that simply say a single thing keeps increasing, with no second quantity attached.

<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っている<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が**どんどん**<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えてきた。<br>*The number of people waiting kept growing.*

Nothing is being proportioned there - it is one line on a graph going up. Compare:

<ruby>人気<rt>にんき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>れ**ば**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>る**ほど**、<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>りにくくなる。<br>*The more popular it gets, the harder it becomes to get a reservation.*

Two scales, tied together. The two often appear in the same sentence, and that is fine: <code><ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>すればするほどどんどん<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>になる</code>.

---

## ～ば～ほど · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む**ば**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む**ほど** → ✅ <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>め**ば**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む**ほど**<br>*The first copy of the verb has to be the `ば` form. Only the second one stays in the dictionary form.*

- ❌ <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>**いければ**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い**ほど** → ✅ <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>**ければ**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い**ほど**<br>*An い-adjective drops its final `い` before `ければ`. The `い` survives only on the second copy, in front of `ほど`.*

- ❌ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べれ**ば**<ruby>太<rt>ふと</rt></ruby>る**ほど** → ✅ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べれ**ば**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる**ほど**<ruby>太<rt>ふと</rt></ruby>る<br>*The word before `ば` and the word before `ほど` must be the same one. The consequence goes at the end of the sentence.*

- ❌ <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>なら<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>**ならほど** → ✅ <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>なら<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>**なほど**<br>*What sits in front of `ほど` is the noun-modifying form, so a な-adjective needs its `な`. The formal alternative is <code><ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>であればあるほど</code>.*

---

## ～ば～ほど · Practice 1

**1.** この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>____<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぐほど<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>えやすい。

{reveal}
<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>**げば**<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぐほど<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>えやすい。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぐ</code> is an う-verb, so the `ば` form replaces the final `ぐ` with `げば`. The second copy is left in the dictionary form because `ほど` attaches to a noun-modifying form.

## ～ば～ほど · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもは<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなる（につれて・ばなるほど）、<ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>と<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>さなくなる。

{reveal}
<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもは<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなる**につれて**、<ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>と<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>さなくなる。

{reveal}
The slot in the sentence already contains a full dictionary-form verb, so only `につれて` can attach there. To use the correlative you would have to rebuild the whole thing as <code><ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなればなるほど</code>, with the verb appearing twice.

## ～ば～ほど · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The more expensive the wine, the more carefully everyone drinks it."

{reveal}
ワインは<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>けれ**ば**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い**ほど**、みんな<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い</code> supplies both halves - `ければ` first, plain `い` second - and the consequence, everyone's carefulness, comes last. Note that Japanese needs no comparative form on either adjective.

# ～ほど

**Meaning** · to the extent that ～; so much that ～ - and, with numbers, "about"

`ほど` measures. Its main job at N3 is to state *how much* by describing what that amount is enough to cause: you were tired **to the point of not being able to stand**, the room was quiet **to the point that it was frightening**. Attached to a number it does something simpler and softer - it turns the number into an approximation, "about".

---

## ～ほど · Formation

The measuring clause comes **before** `ほど`, and the thing being measured comes after. That is the reverse of the English order, and it is the single most important fact on this page.

---

## ～ほど · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (plain) | V(plain) + ほど | <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>けない → <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>けないほど<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れた |
| い-adjective | ～い + ほど | <ruby>恥<rt>は</rt></ruby>ずかしい → <ruby>恥<rt>は</rt></ruby>ずかしいほど<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>くなった |
| な-adjective | ～な + ほど | <ruby>不思議<rt>ふしぎ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>不思議<rt>ふしぎ</rt></ruby>なほどよく<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れた |
| Noun used as a yardstick | Noun + ほど | <ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby> → <ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>ほどある |
| Number / quantity | Number + ほど（≒ぐらい） | <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby> → <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む |
| Modifying a noun | ～ほど + の + Noun | <ruby>涙<rt>なみだ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>るほどの<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby> |
| Proportional | ～ば～ほど | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ければ<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いほど |
| Negative comparison | A ほど B ない | <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くない |

---

## ～ほど · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - English says "so X that Y"; Japanese says "Y ほど X". Build the sentence by writing the vivid consequence first, then `ほど`, then the plain statement it is decorating.

---

## ～ほど · Examples

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**ほど**かかります。<br>*It takes about ten minutes to the station.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>**ほど**ある。<br>*I've got a mountain of homework today.*

<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ない**ほど**<ruby>叫<rt>さけ</rt></ruby>んでしまった。<br>*I yelled so much I lost my voice.*

<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じられない**ほど**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かな<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>だった。<br>*It was an unbelievably quiet morning.*

---

## ～ほど · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>合格<rt>ごうかく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らせを<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いて、<ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>きたくなる**ほど**うれしかった。<br>*When I heard I'd passed, I was so happy I could have cried.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>っていられない**ほど**<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>い。<br>*The wind today is strong enough to knock you off your feet.*

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>時計<rt>とけい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たくなる**ほど**<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>かった。<br>*The manager's speech was long enough to have you checking your watch.*

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一週間<rt>いっしゅうかん</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>ですので、<ruby>打<rt>う</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わせは<ruby>再来週<rt>さらいしゅう</rt></ruby>にしていただけませんか。<br>*I'll be away on business for about a week, so could we make the meeting the week after next?*

---

## ～ほど · Nuance & register

The degree use is neutral and works in any register, though it is a shade more written and a shade more emphatic than `ぐらい`. With numbers it is the more formal of the pair: a shop assistant says <code><ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>ほどおまちください</code>, a friend says <code><ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>ぐらい<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>って</code>.

Because the pattern works by naming a consequence, it invites exaggeration, and spoken Japanese takes the invitation. <code><ruby>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>ぬほど<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>い</code>, <code><ruby>嫌<rt>いや</rt></ruby>になるほど<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いた</code> and <code><ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>ほどある</code> are set phrases you will hear constantly. They are casual and strong: <code><ruby>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>ぬほど</code> is fine among friends and out of place in a meeting, where <code><ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じられないほど</code> or plain `とても` is safer.

---

## ～ほど · Nuance & register (cont.)

The consequence clause does not have to be real. Nobody who says <code><ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>きたくなるほどうれしい</code> is claiming to have cried; the clause is a measuring stick, not a report. This is why it so often appears in the negative potential - <code><ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>けないほど</code>, <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ていられないほど</code> - describing what the degree *prevented*.

One caution: `ほど` measures a **large** degree. Japanese has a separate job of marking a small or minimal one - "at least this much", "even just a coffee" - and that belongs to `ぐらい`. Reaching for `ほど` there produces a sentence native speakers will not follow.

---

## ～ほど · ～ほど vs ～くらい・ぐらい

> **Rule of thumb** - for a **high** degree the two overlap, with `ほど` the stronger and more formal. For a **low** or minimum degree only `ぐらい` works.

Overlapping:

<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>くなる**ほど**<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>き<ruby>回<rt>まわ</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I walked around until my feet hurt.*

Swap in `ぐらい` and you get the same meaning in a lighter, chattier voice. Now the place they part company:

コーヒー**ぐらい**おごりますよ。<br>*Let me at least buy you a coffee.*

---

## ～ほど · ～ほど vs ～くらい・ぐらい (cont.)

Here `ぐらい` says "this much and no more, it's a trivial amount", and ❌`コーヒーほど` is impossible. The same goes for <code><ruby>挨拶<rt>あいさつ</rt></ruby>ぐらいしなさい</code> - "the least you could do is say hello."

---

## ～ほど · ～ほど vs ～すぎて・～て（result）

> **Rule of thumb** - same facts, opposite order. `ほど` puts the consequence **first** and the plain statement last; a `て` or `すぎて` clause puts the cause first and the consequence last.

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ない**ほど**<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れていた。<br>*Yesterday I was so tired I couldn't even speak.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れすぎて、<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なかった。<br>*Yesterday I was too tired, and couldn't even speak.*

The difference is where the emphasis lands. The `ほど` version is a statement about **how tired you were**, with the silence used as a ruler. The `すぎて` version is a narrative: tiredness happened, and then this followed. Learners translating "so … that" word by word reliably produce the second when they wanted the first.

---

## ～ほど · ～ほど for degree vs ～ほど～ない for comparison

> **Rule of thumb** - look at the main predicate. If it is **negative** and the thing before `ほど` is a rival being measured against, you are reading a comparison, not a degree.

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れない**ほど**<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>かった。<br>*Yesterday was so hot I couldn't sleep.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>くない。<br>*Today isn't as hot as yesterday.*

---

## ～ほど · ～ほど for degree vs ～ほど～ない for comparison (cont.)

In the first, <code><ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れない</code> is inside the measuring clause and the sentence itself is positive. In the second, the negative is on the main adjective and <code><ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby></code> is the standard being beaten. When both readings are formally available the negative predicate decides it, which is why <code><ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない</code> means "not as high as a mountain", not "mountainously un-high".

---

## ～ほど · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れた**ほど**<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>けなかった。 → ✅ <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>けない**ほど**<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れた。<br>*The clause before `ほど` is the measuring stick, so the *consequence* goes there. English word order pushes learners into writing this one backwards.*

- ❌ お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでいきませんか。 → ✅ お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでいきませんか。<br>*"At least a cup of tea" is a *minimum*, and `ほど` cannot mark a minimum. That is `ぐらい` territory.*

- ❌ <ruby>涙<rt>なみだ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>るほど**な**<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>涙<rt>なみだ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>るほど**の**<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby><br>*`ほど` behaves like a noun, so it links to a following noun with `の`.*

---

## ～ほど · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>不思議<rt>ふしぎ</rt></ruby>**い**ほど<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ。 → ✅ <ruby>不思議<rt>ふしぎ</rt></ruby>**な**ほど<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ。<br>*A な-adjective keeps its `な` in front of `ほど`, exactly as it would in front of any other noun.*

---

## ～ほど · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The room was so cold that I couldn't take my coat off."

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいほど<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>もなかった。

# ～ほど～ない

**Meaning** · not as ～ as ～ - the standard side takes ほど, and it is the *bigger* one

This is how Japanese says "A isn't as X as B". The frame is `A は B ほど X ない`, and the trap is which noun goes where: `ほど` marks **B**, the one that *is* more X. English puts the winner last ("Osaka isn't as crowded as Tokyo"); Japanese puts it in the middle, and the sentence must end in a negative.

---

## ～ほど～ない · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (the standard) | Noun + ほど + ～ない | <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby> → <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない |
| Verb (plain) | V(plain) + ほど + ～ない | <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す → <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>すほど<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>ではない |
| An expectation | V(た) + ほど + ～ない | <ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>った → <ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>ったほど<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない |
| い-adjective predicate | ～くない | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くない |
| な-adjective predicate | ～ではない | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か → <ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かではない |
| Noun predicate | ～ではない | <ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>前回<rt>ぜんかい</rt></ruby>ほどの<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>ではない |
| "nothing is as ～ as" | Nほど + ～ + Nはない | これ → これほどうれしいことはない |
| Colloquial variant | ほど → くらい | <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>くらい<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くない |

---

## ～ほど～ない · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - whatever ranks **higher** takes `ほど`. Say the English out loud: "not as X as **B**" - B is the one that gets the particle, and everything after it must be negative.

---

## ～ほど～ない · Examples

<ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>く**ない**。<br>*This winter isn't as cold as last year's.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>が<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>じゃ**ない**。<br>*I'm not as good a cook as my older sister.*

テストは<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>った**ほど**<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しく**なかった**。<br>*The test wasn't as hard as I'd expected.*

この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>駅前<rt>えきまえ</rt></ruby>**ほど**にぎやかでは**ない**が、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>には<ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>らない。<br>*This area isn't as lively as the area by the station, but you can get everything you need.*

---

## ～ほど～ない · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>口<rt>くち</rt></ruby>で<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う**ほど**<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>では**ありません**でした。<br>*It wasn't as easy as it sounds.*

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>のきれいな<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たことが**ない**。<br>*I've never met anyone whose handwriting is as beautiful as his.*

<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えること**ほど**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>になることは**ない**。<br>*There's nothing that teaches you as much as teaching someone else.*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>職場<rt>しょくば</rt></ruby>は<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>残業<rt>ざんぎょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>く**ない**ので、<ruby>夕<rt>ゆう</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べられるようになりました。<br>*The new workplace doesn't have as much overtime as my old company, so I've been able to start eating dinner at home.*

---

## ～ほど～ない · Nuance & register

Register-neutral. The pattern is as ordinary in a chat as in a report, and the politeness is set by the ending - <code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くない</code> / <code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くないです</code> / <code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くありません</code>.

What it does carry is a quiet **concession**. Saying <code><ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くない</code> grants that last year set a high bar and places this year below it, without denying that it is cold. That makes it a polite way to decline a comparison you are being pushed into: <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>先輩<rt>せんぱい</rt></ruby>ほどできません</code> is modest, where `できません` alone would just be flat.

---

## ～ほど～ない · Nuance & register (cont.)

`くらい` / `ぐらい` can replace `ほど` here in casual speech, but the two are not equally safe. `ぐらい` also has its own "at least, merely" meaning, so <code><ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんぐらい<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>せない</code> can be heard two ways. `ほど` is unambiguous, and it is what you want in writing.

The pattern's second life is as a **superlative in disguise**: `N ほど … は ない`, "there is nothing as … as N". It sounds more emphatic and more personal than the plain `いちばん` superlative, and it is the frame of a great many proverbs and strong opinions - <code><ruby>健康<rt>けんこう</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>なものはない</code>.

---

## ～ほど～ない · AはBほど～ない vs AよりBのほうが～

> **Rule of thumb** - `ほど` attaches to the **greater** item and needs a negative. `より` attaches to the **lesser** item and needs a positive. Get the two mixed up and you say the opposite of what you meant.

The same fact, from both ends:

バスは<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**ない**。<br>*The bus isn't as fast as the train.*

バス**より**<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>の**ほうが**<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>い。<br>*The train is faster than the bus.*

---

## ～ほど～ない · AはBほど～ない vs AよりBのほうが～ (cont.)

Notice that <code><ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby></code>, the faster one, carries `ほど` in the first sentence and follows `より` in the second. Learners who translate word by word tend to produce ❌<code><ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>はバスほど<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>くない</code>, which claims that trains are the slow ones.

There is also a form restriction. Japanese does not like `より` in front of a negative predicate: ❌<code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>より<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない</code> is rejected by most textbooks and sounds wrong to most speakers. If your English has "not as … as" in it, use `ほど`.

---

## ～ほど～ない · ～ほど～ない vs あまり／そんなに～ない

> **Rule of thumb** - `ほど～ない` needs a **named standard**. `あまり～ない` and `そんなに～ない` measure against a vague expectation instead.

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は**あまり**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>く**ない**。<br>*This place isn't very expensive.*

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>駅前<rt>えきまえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>く**ない**。<br>*This place isn't as expensive as the one by the station.*

---

## ～ほど～ない · ～ほど～ない vs あまり／そんなに～ない (cont.)

The first sets an absolute, if fuzzy, level; the second says nothing at all about whether the place is cheap - only that another shop is worse. This is why <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>ったほど</code> is so common: it names your own expectation as the standard, which is the most useful yardstick a speaker has.

---

## ～ほど～ない · Nほど～はない vs the いちばん superlative

> **Rule of thumb** - `いちばん` ranks inside a stated group and is neutral. `N ほど～はない` claims **nothing anywhere** beats N, and it is an opinion with feeling behind it.

<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>で<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>**ほど**おいしいもの**はない**。<br>*There's nothing as good as water drunk on a mountain in summer.*

<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>で<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>が**いちばん**おいしいです。<br>*Of all drinks, water tastes the best.*

---

## ～ほど～ない · Nほど～はない vs the いちばん superlative (cont.)

The second is a survey result; the first is a declaration. Note the shape of the `ほど` version: the noun being praised comes first, and the sentence ends with a negated `ない` plus a general noun such as `もの`, `こと`, <code><ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby></code> or <code><ruby>所<rt>ところ</rt></ruby></code>.

---

## ～ほど～ない · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>富士山<rt>ふじさん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>高尾山<rt>たかおさん</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない。 → ✅ <ruby>高尾山<rt>たかおさん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>富士山<rt>ふじさん</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない。<br>*`ほど` goes on the taller mountain. Written the first way, the sentence says Fuji is the smaller one.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>**より**<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない。<br>*`より` does not sit comfortably in front of a negative. "Not as … as" is a `ほど` sentence.*

- ❌ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いです。 → ✅ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>くないです。<br>*This `ほど` only exists inside a negative sentence. With a positive predicate the sentence has no meaning.*

- ❌ <ruby>健康<rt>けんこう</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>なものが**ある**。 → ✅ <ruby>健康<rt>けんこう</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>なもの**はない**。<br>*The superlative frame ends in `はない`. Its whole force comes from denying that anything else measures up.*

---

## ～ほど～ない · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>____<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない。

{reveal}
<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>**ほど**<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない。

{reveal}
The older brother is the taller of the two, so he is the standard and takes `ほど`. The sentence then has to close in the negative, which is what turns it into "not as tall as".

---

## ～ほど～ない · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>（ほど・より）<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くない。 - is this sentence true to what you mean if you think Hokkaido is colder?

{reveal}
Neither version says what you mean. With `ほど`, the sentence reads "Hokkaido's winter isn't as cold as Tokyo's", which is the wrong way round; `より` cannot take a negative predicate at all. To say Hokkaido is colder, flip it: **<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くない**.

## ～ほど～ない · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "My new apartment isn't as quiet as the old one."

{reveal}
<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいアパートは<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>のアパート**ほど**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かでは**ない**。

{reveal}
The old flat is the quieter one, so it is the standard and takes `ほど`. Because <code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か</code> is a な-adjective, the negative is `ではない` (casually `じゃない`), not <code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かくない</code>.

## ～ほど～ない · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: この<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>ほどおもしろい<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>があります。

{reveal}
✅ この<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>**ほど**おもしろい<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>は**ありません**。

{reveal}
The `Nほど…はない` frame is a superlative built out of a denial, so it cannot end positively. As written, the original says something like "there is a film as interesting as this one", which is the opposite of the intended praise.

## ～ほど～ない · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>った**ほど**<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>じゃ**ない**。 (b) この<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は**あまり**<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>じゃ**ない**。

{reveal}
(a) measures the job against your own earlier expectation. It admits you were braced for something worse, and it therefore says the job *is* demanding - just less so than you feared.

{reveal}
(b) has no standard in it at all. It simply rates the job low on an absolute scale: not much trouble. Someone who had never thought about the job before could say (b), but only someone with a prior expectation can say (a).

# ～に比べて

**Meaning** · compared with ～; against the standard of ～

<code>～に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて</code> sets a backdrop. It names the thing you are measuring against, then leaves the rest of the sentence free to say whatever you like about the other side - bigger, cheaper, quieter, twenty per cent down. Unlike `より`, which has to sit next to a comparative predicate, <code>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて</code> hands you a whole clause to work in.

---

## ～に比べて · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて | <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて |
| Noun (mutual comparison) | Noun + と<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて | <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby> → <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>と<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて |
| Clause | V(plain) + の + に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて | <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>くのに<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて |
| Noun clause | ～のに<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いのに<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて |
| Formal writing | ～に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べ | <ruby>昨年<rt>さくねん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>昨年<rt>さくねん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べ |
| Softer, spoken | ～に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べると／～に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べたら | <ruby>都会<rt>とかい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>都会<rt>とかい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べると |
| As an ordinary verb | A と B を<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べる | <ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>と<ruby>質<rt>しつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べる |

---

## ～に比べて · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the particle is `に` or `と`, never `を`. `を` belongs to the plain verb <code><ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べる</code> and marks the *pair* being compared: <code><ruby>二<rt>ふた</rt></ruby>つのプランを<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べる</code>.

---

## ～に比べて · Examples

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて**、<ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>ない。<br>*Compared with last year, there's been little rain this year.*

<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて**、<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>はよくしゃべる。<br>*Compared with his older brother, the younger one is quite the talker.*

<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>のモデル**に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて**、<ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>がずいぶん<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになりました。<br>*Compared with the previous model, it's got a lot quieter.*

<ruby>都会<rt>とかい</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べると**、この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れがゆっくりしている。<br>*Compared with the city, time moves slowly in this town.*

---

## ～に比べて · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くの**に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて**、<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めない。<br>*Compared with going by train, you can't predict how long the car will take.*

<ruby>十年前<rt>じゅうねんまえ</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べ**、<ruby>市内<rt>しない</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人口<rt>じんこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>二割<rt>にわり</rt></ruby><ruby>減<rt>へ</rt></ruby>っている。<br>*Compared with ten years ago, the population of the city is down twenty per cent.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて**、<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby><ruby>代<rt>だい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>倍<rt>ばい</rt></ruby>になって<ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>いた。<br>*Compared with last summer, the electricity bill has doubled - I was shocked.*

<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>時期<rt>じき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>った<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>たち**に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて**、<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>だけ<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遅<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>い<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>がして<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいます。<br>*Compared with the others who started at the same time, I feel like I'm the only slow one, and it's getting me down.*

---

## ～に比べて · Nuance & register

Neutral, with a slight lean towards the explanatory: news bulletins, sales reports, weather summaries and any sentence with a percentage in it. It is perfectly normal in conversation too, especially in the <code>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べると</code> and <code>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べたら</code> shapes, which soften it and sound more like personal impression than analysis.

Clipping to <code>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べ</code> is written-only, one notch up in formality, and extremely common in newspapers: <code><ruby>前年<rt>ぜんねん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べ<ruby>三<rt>さん</rt></ruby>パーセント<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えた</code>.

---

## ～に比べて · Nuance & register (cont.)

`に` and `と` are close to interchangeable, with a tendency: `に` frames one thing as the fixed reference point you are working from, while `と` puts the two side by side as equals. <code><ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて</code> takes last year as the baseline; <code><ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>と<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて</code> sounds more like holding two people up against each other, and it is the one that can carry an edge of "why are you comparing us?"

One phrase worth having ready: <code>～に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べれば</code> means "compared with that, this is nothing" - <code><ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しさに<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べれば、 <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>楽<rt>らく</rt></ruby>なものだ</code>. It is a standard way of putting a complaint in perspective, your own or someone else's.

---

## ～に比べて · ～に比べて vs ～より

> **Rule of thumb** - `より` is glued to a comparative statement and needs a gradable predicate. `に比べて` sets up a frame and then lets you say anything at all.

Where a single adjective is doing the work, both are fine and `より` is lighter:

<ruby>地下鉄<rt>ちかてつ</rt></ruby>はバス**より**<ruby>速<rt>はや</rt></ruby>い。<br>*The subway is faster than the bus.*

But `に比べて` can head a whole clause, and even a clause with no comparative in it:

バス**に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて**、<ruby>地下鉄<rt>ちかてつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めるので<ruby>安心<rt>あんしん</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*Compared with the bus, the subway runs to time, so it's less stressful.*

---

## ～に比べて · ～に比べて vs ～より (cont.)

❌<code>バスより<ruby>安心<rt>あんしん</rt></ruby>だ</code> strains, because <code><ruby>安心<rt>あんしん</rt></ruby>だ</code> is not being measured on a scale - the sentence is describing a situation, not ranking two numbers. A second practical difference: `に比べて` normally opens the sentence, which makes it the natural choice when the comparison is the topic of a paragraph.

---

## ～に比べて · ～に比べて vs ～に対して

> **Rule of thumb** - `に比べて` puts two things on **one scale**. `に対して` sets them in **opposition**, "whereas", with no shared measure required.

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて**、<ruby>新入生<rt>しんにゅうせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>数<rt>かず</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えた。<br>*Compared with last year, the number of new students has gone up.*

Numbers, one axis. Now two facts that share no axis at all:

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>が<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>で<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>ぶのが<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きなの**に<ruby>対<rt>たい</rt></ruby>して**、<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一日中<rt>いちにちじゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいる。<br>*Where my older sister loves being outdoors, my younger sister reads all day.*

---

## ～に比べて · ～に比べて vs ～に対して (cont.)

Nothing is more or less than anything here; the two halves are simply different. Substituting `に比べて` would suggest reading and playing outside are two settings of one dial, which is not what the speaker means.

---

## ～に比べて · ～に比べて vs ～と違って

> **Rule of thumb** - `に比べて` is a difference of **degree**. `と違って` is a difference of **kind**: A does this, B does not, and there is no "more" about it.

<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて**、こちらは<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い。<br>*Compared with the shops in Tokyo, things here are cheap.*

<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>**と<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>って**、この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>日曜<rt>にちよう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みだ。<br>*Unlike the shops in Tokyo, this one closes on Sundays.*

Closing on Sunday is not a lesser amount of anything, so it takes <code>と<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>って</code>. A quick test: if you could add "by twenty per cent" to the English, you want <code>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて</code>.

---

## ～に比べて · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて、<ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>い。 → ✅ <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて、<ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>い。<br>*The standard is marked with `に` or `と`. `を` would make last year the object being examined, which needs a second object to pair it with.*

- ❌ <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く**に**<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>楽<rt>らく</rt></ruby>だ。 → ✅ <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>く**のに**<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>楽<rt>らく</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*A verb has to be nominalised with `の` before <code>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて</code> can attach to it.*

- ❌ <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて、バス**より**<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い。 → ✅ <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて、バス**は**<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い。<br>*One comparison, one marker. Once <code>に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて</code> has named the standard, `より` is redundant and the sentence reads as if it had two of them.*

---

## ～に比べて · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて、<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>はめがねをかけていない。 → ✅ <ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>**と<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>って**、<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>はめがねをかけていない。<br>*Wearing glasses is not a matter of degree, so there is nothing to compare. That is a <code>と<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>って</code> sentence.*

---

## ～に比べて · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>先月<rt>せんげつ</rt></ruby>____<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて、<ruby>今月<rt>こんげつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>残業<rt>ざんぎょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>ない。

{reveal}
<ruby>先月<rt>せんげつ</rt></ruby>**に**<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて、<ruby>今月<rt>こんげつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>残業<rt>ざんぎょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>ない。

{reveal}

## ～に比べて · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Compared with last week, there are far fewer people in the park."

{reveal}
<ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>比<rt>くら</rt></ruby>べて**、<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>がずいぶん<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>ない。

{reveal}
The comparison heads the sentence, exactly as "compared with" does in English, and the rest is a plain statement - no `より` and no comparative form needed anywhere else in the sentence.

# ～一方だ

**Meaning** · keeps going in one direction; more and more ～, with no sign of turning back

<code>～<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> reports a **trend**. Something is moving along a scale - rising, falling, worsening, spreading - and the speaker's point is that it has been moving that way steadily and shows no sign of stopping. It is the sentence-final shape of the newspaper headline "costs continue to climb".

---

## ～一方だ · Formation

Only verbs that contain a direction of change can take it. What goes in front is always the dictionary form; tense and politeness are carried by the `だ` at the end.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb of change | V(dictionary) + <ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ | <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える → <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ |
| Polite | ～<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>です | <ruby>減<rt>へ</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>減<rt>へ</rt></ruby>る<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>です |
| Past | ～<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だった | <ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がる → <ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がる<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だった |
| い-adjective (via なる) | ～くなる + <ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ | <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しくなる<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ |
| な-adjective / Noun (via なる) | ～になる + <ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ | <ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby>になる<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ |
| Continuing the sentence | ～<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>で、… | <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>が<ruby>足<rt>た</rt></ruby>りない |
| Modifying a noun | ～<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>の + Noun | <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby> |

---

## ～一方だ · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - if the verb cannot be pictured as an arrow on a graph, it cannot take <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code>. Adjectives and nouns get in only by first becoming a change verb with `なる`.

---

## ～一方だ · Examples

<ruby>物価<rt>ぶっか</rt></ruby>は<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がる**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ**。<br>*Prices just keep going up.*

この<ruby>村<rt>むら</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人口<rt>じんこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>減<rt>へ</rt></ruby>る**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>です**。<br>*The population of this village keeps falling.*

<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っているだけでは、<ruby>状況<rt>じょうきょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>くなる**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ**。<br>*If we just wait, the situation will only get worse.*

スマートフォンのおかげで、<ruby>生活<rt>せいかつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>になる**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ**。<br>*Thanks to smartphones, life keeps getting more convenient.*

---

## ～一方だ · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>づき、<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>くなる**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だった**。<br>*As the typhoon drew closer, the rain just kept getting heavier.*

<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby><ruby>暮<rt>ぐ</rt></ruby>らしを<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めてから、コンビニに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く<ruby>回数<rt>かいすう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ**。<br>*Since I started living alone, the number of trips I make to the convenience store has done nothing but go up.*

<ruby>医療<rt>いりょう</rt></ruby><ruby>費<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>世代<rt>せだい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>負担<rt>ふたん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>くなっている。<br>*Medical costs keep on rising, and the burden on the younger generation is growing heavier.*

---

## ～一方だ · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby><ruby>開<rt>ひら</rt></ruby>いても<ruby>結論<rt>けつろん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なくて、メンバーの<ruby>不満<rt>ふまん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなる**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>でした**。<br>*However many meetings we held, no conclusion came out of them, and the members' frustration only grew.*

---

## ～一方だ · Nuance & register

This is a written-leaning pattern. It belongs to news copy, reports, essays and the kind of speech that summarises a situation; in relaxed conversation the same idea comes out as <code>どんどん<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えてる</code> or <code>だんだん<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>くなってる</code>. Saying <code><ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> to a friend is not wrong, but it has a faintly editorial ring - which can be the joke.

The tone is **observational**. The speaker is standing back and reporting a curve, not reacting to it, and that is why the pattern sits so easily next to numbers and statistics. It is often used for unwelcome trends, simply because unwelcome trends are what people write about, but there is nothing negative in the grammar itself: <code><ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>になる<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> and <code><ruby>技術<rt>ぎじゅつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> are perfectly ordinary.

---

## ～一方だ · Nuance & register (cont.)

One structural point. The subject of <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> is normally a situation or a quantity rather than a person choosing to do something. A trend is not a decision, so <code><ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>る<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> does not work as "I keep running every day" - what can rise is <code><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>る<ruby>距離<rt>きょり</rt></ruby></code>, not the act itself.

---

## ～一方だ · ～一方だ vs ～ばかりだ

> **Rule of thumb** - both mean "only goes one way". <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> is neutral and can describe a welcome trend; `ばかりだ` is almost always about something getting worse, and it sounds more emotional.

<ruby>輸出<rt>ゆしゅつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>びる**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ**。<br>*Exports keep growing.*

<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>不安<rt>ふあん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなる**ばかりだった**。<br>*While I waited for a reply, my anxiety did nothing but grow.*

---

## ～一方だ · ～一方だ vs ～ばかりだ (cont.)

Swapping them is not ungrammatical but does change the colour. <code><ruby>輸出<rt>ゆしゅつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>びるばかりだ</code> makes growth sound like a runaway problem, and <code><ruby>不安<rt>ふあん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなる<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby> だった</code> makes your own anxiety sound like a line on a chart. Choose by whether you are reporting or complaining.

---

## ～一方だ · ～一方だ vs ～ていく・～てくる

> **Rule of thumb** - `～ていく` and `～てくる` simply track a change over time. <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> adds the claim that it has gone **only** that way and is still going.

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの<ruby>数<rt>かず</rt></ruby>が<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>なくなっ**ていく**。<br>*The number of children is decreasing.*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの<ruby>数<rt>かず</rt></ruby>は<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>なくなる**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ**。<br>*The number of children just keeps decreasing.*

---

## ～一方だ · ～一方だ vs ～ていく・～てくる (cont.)

The first is a neutral description that leaves room for the line to level off; the second insists it has not and will not. `～ていく` is also the everyday spoken form and works with any verb at all, while <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> demands a verb of change and a register to match.

---

## ～一方だ · ～一方だ vs ～一方（で） - "on the other hand"

> **Rule of thumb** - at the **end** of a sentence, <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> is a trend. Between two clauses that describe **different** things, <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>（で）</code> means "meanwhile, on the other hand".

<ruby>田舎<rt>いなか</rt></ruby>の<ruby>土地<rt>とち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くなる**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ**。<br>*Land in the countryside just keeps getting cheaper.*

<ruby>都心<rt>としん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>家賃<rt>やちん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がっている。**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>地方<rt>ちほう</rt></ruby>では<ruby>空<rt>あ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>家<rt>や</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えている。<br>*Rents in the city centre are rising. Meanwhile, empty houses are increasing in rural areas.*

---

## ～一方だ · ～一方だ vs ～一方（で） - "on the other hand" (cont.)

The two are told apart by position and by what surrounds them. A sentence-final <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> after a dictionary-form verb of change is the trend reading; a clause-initial <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby></code>, or <code>～<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>で</code> joining two clauses about *different* subjects, is the contrast reading. The tricky middle case - <code><ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>人手<rt>ひとで</rt></ruby>が<ruby>足<rt>た</rt></ruby>りない</code> - is the trend pattern in its て-form, and you can tell because both halves are about the same rising thing.

---

## ～一方だ · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>人口<rt>じんこう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>ない**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ。 → ✅ <ruby>人口<rt>じんこう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>減<rt>へ</rt></ruby>る**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*An adjective describes a state, and a state is not a trend. You need a verb with movement in it, or an adjective converted with `なる`.*

- ❌ <ruby>生活<rt>せいかつ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>苦<rt>くる</rt></ruby>しい**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ <ruby>生活<rt>せいかつ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>苦<rt>くる</rt></ruby>しくなる**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>です。<br>*Same problem in polite clothing. `～くなる` is the bridge from an い-adjective to a change verb.*

- ❌ <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>から<ruby>売上<rt>うりあげ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えた**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ。 → ✅ <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>から<ruby>売上<rt>うりあげ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*The verb stays in the dictionary form. If you need the past, put it on the end: <code><ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だった</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>る**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ。 → ✅ <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>る**<ruby>距離<rt>きょり</rt></ruby>が<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>びる**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*A habit you have chosen is not a trend that is happening to you. The subject has to be something that can rise or fall on its own.*

---

# ～ばかりだ

**Meaning** · things only go one way, and it is the wrong way - "it just keeps getting ～"

Put a verb of change in front of `ばかりだ` and you get a one-directional slide with a complaint attached. The pain only gets sharper, the misunderstanding only grows, the field only gets more overgrown. Where <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> reports a trend from a distance, `ばかりだ` is the voice of someone watching it happen and not liking it.

---

## ～ばかりだ · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb of change | V(dictionary) + ばかりだ | <ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>くなる → <ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>くなるばかりだ |
| Polite | ～ばかりです | <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える → <ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えるばかりです |
| Past | ～ばかりだった | <ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>くなる → <ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>くなるばかりだった |
| Continuing the sentence | ～ばかりで、… | <ruby>荒<rt>あ</rt></ruby>れる → <ruby>荒<rt>あ</rt></ruby>れるばかりで、<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない |
| い-adjective (via なる) | ～くなる + ばかりだ | ひどい → ひどくなるばかりだ |
| な-adjective / Noun (via なる) | ～になる + ばかりだ | <ruby>不安<rt>ふあん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>不安<rt>ふあん</rt></ruby>になるばかりだ |
| "all that is left" | あとは + V(dictionary) + ばかりだ | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ → あとは<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つばかりだ |

---

## ～ばかりだ · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the verb in front stays in the **dictionary form**. Swap it for the た-form and you have the completely different N4 pattern `～たばかりだ`, "have just done": <code><ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたばかりだ</code> means "I've only just arrived".

---

## ～ばかりだ · Examples

<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>みは<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>くなる**ばかりだ**。<br>*The pain just keeps getting worse.*

<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わなければ、<ruby>誤解<rt>ごかい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなる**ばかりです**。<br>*If you say nothing, the misunderstanding will only grow.*

<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>っても、<ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>溝<rt>みぞ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>深<rt>ふか</rt></ruby>くなる**ばかりだった**。<br>*However much they talked, the gap between them only got deeper.*

<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでも、せきはひどくなる**ばかりだった**。<br>*Even with the medicine, my cough did nothing but get worse.*

---

## ～ばかりだ · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>畑<rt>はたけ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>荒<rt>あ</rt></ruby>れる**ばかりで**、<ruby>手入<rt>てい</rt></ruby>れをする<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>がいない。<br>*The fields just get more overgrown, and there's no one to look after them.*

<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>は<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりました。あとは<ruby>本番<rt>ほんばん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ**ばかりです**。<br>*The preparations are done. All that's left now is to wait for the real thing.*

<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>減<rt>へ</rt></ruby>った<ruby>商店街<rt>しょうてんがい</rt></ruby>では、シャッターの<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>まった<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える**ばかりだ**。<br>*In a shopping street that has lost its people, the number of shuttered shops does nothing but grow.*

<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>積<rt>つ</rt></ruby>んだので、あとは<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby><ruby>出発<rt>しゅっぱつ</rt></ruby>する**ばかりです**。<br>*The luggage is all in the car, so all that remains is to set off tomorrow morning.*

---

## ～ばかりだ · Nuance & register

`ばかりだ` in this sense is more literary than <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code>. It is at home in narration, in a written complaint, in a doctor's notes and in the sentence a novel uses to end a paragraph; in ordinary chat people say `どんどんひどくなってる`. Using it aloud gives what you say a slightly grave, resigned colour - which is often exactly the point.

It carries an **evaluation**, and the evaluation is negative. That is the clearest difference from <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code>, which is happy to report good news. Native speakers find <code><ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>になるばかりだ</code> odd for a recovering patient, because the pattern is reaching for the wrong emotional register; the trend version of that sentence is <code><ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>になる<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code>.

---

## ～ばかりだ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two form notes. The casual contraction `ばっかり` belongs to the "nothing but" `ばかり` you met at N4 - <code>マンガばっかり<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでる</code> - and is rarely heard in this trend use, where the tone is too serious for it. And the `あとは～ばかりだ` frame, "everything is done except this", is a set expression with a faintly formal, tidy feel; the everyday equivalent is `あとは～だけだ`.

---

## ～ばかりだ · ～ばかりだ vs ～一方だ

> **Rule of thumb** - same one-way movement, different speaker. <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> reports; `ばかりだ` complains. Only <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> can be used about a change you welcome.

<ruby>技術<rt>ぎじゅつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>む**<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ**。<br>*Technology keeps advancing.*

<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たされて、いらいらが<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える**ばかりだった**。<br>*Being kept waiting, I only got more and more irritated.*

---

## ～ばかりだ · ～ばかりだ vs ～一方だ (cont.)

You could put `ばかりだ` on the first sentence, but it would turn progress into a threat. Going the other way, <code>いらいらが<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>える<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だった</code> sounds like a report on someone else's mood. Register differs too: <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> is the newspaper, `ばかりだ` is the diary.

---

## ～ばかりだ · 変化の動詞 vs 動作の動詞 in front of ばかりだ

> **Rule of thumb** - the verb decides the meaning. A verb of **change** gives "keeps getting worse". A verb of **action** gives "does nothing but ～".

<ruby>状況<rt>じょうきょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>くなる**ばかりだ**。<br>*The situation only gets worse.*

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>文句<rt>もんく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う**ばかりで**、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ってくれない。<br>*All he does is complain - he doesn't help with anything.*

---

## ～ばかりだ · 変化の動詞 vs 動作の動詞 in front of ばかりだ (cont.)

<code><ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>くなる</code> moves along a scale, so the first is a trend. <code><ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う</code> is something a person chooses to do, so the second is about exclusivity: complaining and nothing else. With an ongoing action the more common shape is `～てばかりいる` - <code><ruby>文句<rt>もんく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってばかりいる</code> - and the two are close, with `ばかりで` leaning towards "…and therefore nothing else gets done".

---

## ～ばかりだ · V(dictionary)ばかりだ vs V(た)ばかりだ

> **Rule of thumb** - dictionary form before `ばかり` = a change that keeps going. た-form before `ばかり` = something that finished a moment ago.

<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>くなる**ばかりだ**。<br>*It's only getting darker outside.*

<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた**ばかりで**、まだおなかがいっぱいだ。<br>*I've only just eaten lunch, so I'm still full.*

One string, two grammars, and the only visible difference is the form of the verb. Read the verb ending before you decide which one you are looking at - this is one of the most reliable ways to misread an N3 sentence.

---

## ～ばかりだ · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い**ばかりだ**。（meaning "it keeps getting colder"） → ✅ <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなる**ばかりだ**。<br>*The pattern needs a verb with movement in it. An い-adjective must first become `～くなる`.*

- ❌ <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>良<rt>よ</rt></ruby>くなるばかりだ**。 → ✅ <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>良<rt>よ</rt></ruby>くなる<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ**。<br>*Not a form error but a tone error: `ばかりだ` frames the change as unwelcome, so using it about a recovery sounds contradictory.*

- ❌ あとは<ruby>出発<rt>しゅっぱつ</rt></ruby>**した**ばかりだ。 → ✅ あとは<ruby>出発<rt>しゅっぱつ</rt></ruby>**する**ばかりだ。<br>*In the "all that's left" frame the verb is still in the future, so it takes the dictionary form. With the た-form the sentence flips to "we've only just left".*

---

## ～ばかりだ · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>不満<rt>ふまん</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなったばかりだった**。 → ✅ <ruby>不満<rt>ふまん</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きくなるばかりだった**。<br>*Tense lives on `だ` at the end. The verb in front of `ばかり` stays in the dictionary form however far in the past the events are.*

---

## ～ばかりだ · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>放<rt>ほう</rt></ruby>っておくと、<ruby>状況<rt>じょうきょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>く____ばかりだ。

{reveal}
<ruby>状況<rt>じょうきょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>く**なる**ばかりだ。

{reveal}
`ばかりだ` in this sense attaches to a dictionary-form verb of change, and `なる` is what turns the い-adjective <code><ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>い</code> into one. <code><ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>いばかりだ</code> has no movement in it and cannot express a trend.

## ～ばかりだ · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>輸出<rt>ゆしゅつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>びる（ばかりだ・<ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ）。

Growing exports are good news, and `ばかりだ` colours its trend as unwelcome. The neutral, report-like <code><ruby>一方<rt>いっぽう</rt></ruby>だ</code> is what a newspaper would print here.

## ～ばかりだ · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>はゲームをする**ばかりで**、<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しない。 (b) <ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>成績<rt>せいせき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がる**ばかりだ**。

{reveal}
(a) is about exclusivity. `する` is an action he chooses, so `ばかり` says that action fills the whole picture and crowds everything else out.

{reveal}
(b) is about direction. <code><ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がる</code> is a movement along a scale, so `ばかり` says the marks have gone one way only, with the speaker's disapproval attached. Same word, and the verb type is what tells them apart.
