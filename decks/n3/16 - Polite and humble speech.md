# 敬語

**Meaning** · the honorific system - three separate mechanisms for showing where you stand in relation to the person you are talking to and the person you are talking about

<ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> is not one politeness dial. It is three tools with different jobs: <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> raises the other person, <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> lowers you, and <ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> keeps the sentence itself polite. You choose the first two by asking *whose action it is*; the third you simply keep on.

---

## 敬語 · Formation

The individual frames belong to their own pages; the table below is the map. Read "Attaches to" as "the form the frame is built on".

---

## 敬語 · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - ask one question: **whose action is it?** Their action takes <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>. Your action takes <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>. Both sit on top of <ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>, which never goes away in a polite conversation.

> **Note** - the line between "us" and "them" moves. Inside your company your manager is above you and takes <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>. On the phone to a client, your manager is on your side of the line and takes <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>: <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>はただ<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>外出<rt>がいしゅつ</rt></ruby>しております. This is the single biggest structural difference from European politeness systems, and the mistake that most marks out a beginner.

---

## 敬語 · Examples

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>はもう**お<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りになりました**か。<br>*Has the teacher already gone home?*

<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>まで**ご<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>します**。<br>*I'll show you to the reception desk.*

<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>拝見<rt>はいけん</rt></ruby>しました**。ありがとうございます。<br>*I've read the documents. Thank you.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>に、そちらへ**<ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>います**。<br>*I'll call on you at three tomorrow.*

---

## 敬語 · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>ございません、<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>はただ<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>外<rt>はず</rt></ruby>しております**。<br>*I'm sorry - Tanaka is away from his desk at the moment.*

<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>が**おっしゃった**<ruby>方針<rt>ほうしん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>沿<rt>そ</rt></ruby>って<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>めております。<br>*We are proceeding along the lines the president set out.*

お<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいところ<ruby>恐<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>りますが、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しお<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>を**いただけますでしょうか**。<br>*I'm sorry to trouble you when you're busy, but might I have a moment of your time?*

---

## 敬語 · Nuance & register

<ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> is not simply "very polite Japanese". It is a way of drawing relationships, and the relationships it draws are situational rather than fixed: seniority at work, customer and shop, host and guest, first meeting versus tenth. The same two people may use it in the morning meeting and drop it entirely at dinner.

Two practical points. First, **volume matters less than direction**. A sentence built with the wrong branch - honorific verbs for your own actions - is far more jarring than a plain ます sentence would have been. When you are unsure, staying in <ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> and speaking clearly is safe; guessing at <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> and pointing it at yourself is not.

---

## 敬語 · Nuance & register (cont.)

Second, **more is not better**. Stacking two honorific mechanisms on one verb produces <ruby>二重<rt>にじゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> - おっしゃられる, お<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みになられる - which is widely regarded as incorrect, though you will certainly hear it. So-called manual keigo (よろしかったでしょうか, ～のほう) has the same over-polished quality and is criticised for the same reason.

Regionally and generationally there is variation: Kansai speech uses ～はる as a light honorific, and older speakers tend to use fuller forms than younger ones. Written business Japanese is the most conservative register of all, which is why email templates are worth collecting.

---

## 敬語 · 尊敬語 vs 謙譲語

> **Rule of thumb** - the branch is chosen by the **actor**, not by who you are speaking to. Their action, <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>; your action, <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>.

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>になりました**。<br>*The department head looked at the documents.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>拝見<rt>はいけん</rt></ruby>しました**。<br>*I looked at the documents.*

Both sentences are addressed to the same superior and both are polite. Swapping the verbs - ❌<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>がご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>になりました - claims honour for yourself, and it is the error native listeners notice fastest.

---

## 敬語 · お○○になる vs お○○する

> **Rule of thumb** - one frame, two endings, opposite directions. `になる` raises the actor; `する` lowers the actor.

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が**お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちになっています**。<br>*The teacher is waiting for you.*

<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>で**お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちします**。<br>*I'll wait outside.*

The trap is that they look almost identical. A useful check: if the subject is you, the ending must be `する` or `いたす`. There is no situation in which ❌お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちになります describes your own waiting.

---

## 敬語 · 丁寧語 vs 尊敬語

> **Rule of thumb** - `ます` is politeness to the **listener**. It says nothing at all about the person in the sentence.

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*The section chief is coming at three.*

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>に**いらっしゃいます**。<br>*The section chief will be arriving at three.*

The first sentence is polite Japanese and perfectly grammatical; spoken to a visitor about your own section chief it is exactly right. Spoken to a junior colleague about the chief, the second is the expected form. Politeness and honour are separate axes, and `ます` only moves one of them.

---

## 敬語 · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>がおっしゃられました。 → ✅ <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>が**おっしゃいました**。<br>*おっしゃる is already honorific; adding the honorific passive `られる` makes it <ruby>二重<rt>にじゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby>. One mechanism per verb.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>いらっしゃいます。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>**<ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>います**。<br>*いらっしゃる raises its subject, so pointing it at yourself is the one thing <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> never allows.*

- ❌（<ruby>取引先<rt>とりひきさき</rt></ruby>への<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>で）<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>はいらっしゃいません。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>は**おりません**。<br>*To someone outside the company, your own manager belongs to your side and takes humble forms - and the honorific `さん` and title drop away too.*

---

## 敬語 · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ どうぞ<ruby>拝見<rt>はいけん</rt></ruby>してください。 → ✅ どうぞ**ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>ください**。<br>*<ruby>拝見<rt>はいけん</rt></ruby>する is humble, so inviting someone to do it lowers them. The honorific counterpart is ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>になる.*

---

## 敬語 · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>はコーヒーを____か。（<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>）

{reveal}
<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>はコーヒーを**<ruby>召<rt>め</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がります**か。

{reveal}
The action belongs to the teacher, so it takes an honorific form, and <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む is one of the verbs with its own replacement word rather than a frame. お<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みになりますか is also correct, if a little stiffer.

---

## 敬語 · Practice 2

**2.** Choose the correct form: （<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が・<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>が）お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちします。

{reveal}
**<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が**お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちします。

{reveal}
The frame `お～する` is humble, so its subject has to be you or someone on your side. With <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby> as subject you would need お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちになります instead.

---

## 敬語 · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese, speaking to a client on the phone about your colleague <ruby>鈴木<rt>すずき</rt></ruby>: "Suzuki is out at the moment."

{reveal}
<ruby>鈴木<rt>すずき</rt></ruby>はただ<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>**<ruby>外出<rt>がいしゅつ</rt></ruby>しております**。

{reveal}
Your colleague is inside your circle and the client is outside it, so Suzuki takes humble forms and loses the `さん`. おります is the humble counterpart of います, and `ておる` is its て-form partner.

---

## 敬語 · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>がお<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しになられました。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>が**お<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しになりました**。

{reveal}
<code>お<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しになる</code> is already an honorific frame, and `られる` adds a second one on top. Doubling the mechanism is the classic <ruby>二重<rt>にじゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> error.

---

## 敬語 · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby><ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます。 (b) <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby><ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>いらっしゃいます。

{reveal}
Both are polite to the listener, because both end in `ます`. Only (b) also raises Professor Yamada himself. Sentence (a) is what you would say to someone outside the school if the professor were a colleague of yours; said to a student about their own teacher, it sounds oddly flat, as though you did not think the professor merited the honorific.

# ～させてもらう・～ていただく

**Meaning** · do something with your kind permission; have something done for me - humble framing built out of the receiving verbs

Two related frames live on this page. `～ていただく` is the humble `～てもらう`: **you** do something, and I receive the favour. `～させていただく` adds the causative in front of it: **I** do something, and what I receive is your permission to do it. Getting them the right way round is the whole lesson.

---

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · Formation

Build the causative, put it in the て-form, then attach もらう (plain) or いただく (humble). Every causative verb is a る-verb, so the て-form step never varies.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | あ-stem + せていただく | <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>わせていただきます |
| Verb (る-verb) | drop る + させていただく | <ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める → <ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めさせていただきます |
| Verb (する) | させていただく | <ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>させていただきます |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>させていただく | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>させていただきます |
| Casual equivalent | causative て-form + もらう | <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>らせてもらうね |
| Someone else's action | て-form + いただく | <ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>える → <ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えていただきました |
| Asking permission | causative て-form + いただけませんか | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ませていただけませんか |

---

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - count the actors. `させていただく`: I act, you permit. `ていただく`: you act, I benefit. If the verb describes something you are doing, the causative has to be in there.

> **Note** - う-verbs take `せる`, not `させる`. ❌<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>まさせていただきます is the error known as さ<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>言葉<rt>ことば</rt></ruby>, and it is common enough among native speakers to be a running complaint in style guides. The correct form is <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ませていただきます. る-verbs and する genuinely do take `させる`, which is where the confusion comes from.

---

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · Examples

コピーを**<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>らせていただきます**。<br>*I'll take a copy, if I may.*

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>由来<rt>ゆらい</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えていただきました**。<br>*The teacher was kind enough to explain where the character comes from.*

<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby><ruby>司会<rt>しかい</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>務<rt>つと</rt></ruby>めさせていただきます**、<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>と<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>します。<br>*I'm Tanaka, and I'll be chairing today's session.*

<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>ありませんが、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>から**<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ませていただけませんか**。<br>*I'm sorry to ask, but might I take tomorrow afternoon off?*

---

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · Examples (cont.)

こちらのお<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>、<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>らせていただいても**よろしいでしょうか。<br>*Would it be all right if I took a photograph of this room?*

<ruby>貴重<rt>きちょう</rt></ruby>なご<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>かせていただき**、ありがとうございました。<br>*Thank you for sharing your valuable thoughts with me.*

<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>で<ruby>恐縮<rt>きょうしゅく</rt></ruby>ですが、<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>欠席<rt>けっせき</rt></ruby>させていただきます**。<br>*I'm sorry for the short notice, but I'll be absent from next week's meeting.*

---

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · Nuance & register

`させていただく` is business Japanese's workhorse. It appears in announcements, presentations, shop signs and apologetic emails, and its effect is to present your own action as something granted rather than simply done. That is why it so often accompanies bad news: <ruby>閉店<rt>へいてん</rt></ruby>させていただきます on a shop door softens a closure by implying the customers' permission.

Note that a sentence ending in させていただきます is usually **not** a request. It announces. The genuine request forms are させていただけませんか, させていただけますでしょうか and させてください, and the difference matters: if you need an answer, use one of those.

---

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · Nuance & register (cont.)

The plain `させてもらう` is the same grammar without the humility, and it is ordinary casual speech: <ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>らせてもらうね, "I'm going to head off, if that's all right." It can also carry a faint note of asserting your right, which is why it sometimes sounds slightly assertive between equals.

Overuse is a real phenomenon. When nobody has granted anything - ❌<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>させていただいております for a job you were simply assigned - the form is criticised as inflation, and plain いたします or しております is better Japanese. The test is whether a permission-giver exists in the situation. In a presentation the audience has given you the floor, so <ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>させていただきます is well founded; in a self-introduction, nobody gave you permission to have your own name.

---

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · ～させていただく vs ～させてください

> **Rule of thumb** - させてください **asks**. させていただきます **announces**, on the assumption that permission is already there.

<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え**させてください**。<br>*Please let me think about it a little.*

<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>は<ruby>後日<rt>ごじつ</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>**させていただきます**。<br>*We'll be in touch with the result at a later date.*

Both are polite. させてください is a direct request and needs a yes; it can be a touch too direct toward a customer, which is why business speech leans on させていただきます and on the interrogative させていただけますでしょうか.

---

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · ～させていただく vs ～ていただく

> **Rule of thumb** - whose hands are moving? Mine: させていただく. Yours: ていただく.

<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>らせていただきます**。<br>*I'll send you the documents.*

<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>っていただけますか**。<br>*Could you send me the documents?*

One causative apart, and the two sentences point in opposite directions. Mixing them up is the most consequential mistake on this page: you can easily promise to do something when you meant to ask for it.

---

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · ～させていただく vs ～いたします

> **Rule of thumb** - `いたします` is plain humility. Add させていただく only when there is somebody whose permission is genuinely in play.

ご<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>**いたします**。<br>*Allow me to show you the way.*

<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>代<rt>か</rt></ruby>わりに<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>**させていただきます**。<br>*I'll be standing in and handling this today.*

The second sentence works because the listener is being asked to accept a substitute. Where no such acceptance is involved, いたします is shorter, older and better regarded.

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>まさせていただきます。 → ✅ **<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ませていただきます**。<br>*<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む is an う-verb, so the causative is <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ませる with `せ` alone. The extra `さ` is the さ<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>言葉<rt>ことば</rt></ruby> error.*

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>させていただきました。（meaning the teacher explained）→ ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>していただきました**。<br>*With the causative in place, the sentence says *you* did the explaining after the teacher allowed it. Drop the causative when the other person is the one acting.*

- ❌ お<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>かせていただかせていただきます。 → ✅ お<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>かせていただきます**。<br>*One layer of humility per verb. Stacking the frame on itself is <ruby>二重<rt>にじゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby>, not extra politeness.*

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>と<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>させていただきます。 → ✅ <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>と**<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>します**。<br>*Nobody grants you permission to be called by your own name, so there is nothing for the frame to receive. This is the textbook case of overuse.*

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>体調<rt>たいちょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>いので、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>____ませんか。

{reveal}
<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>らせていただけ**ませんか。

{reveal}
<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る is an う-verb, so the causative is <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>らせる, and the potential of いただく in the negative question form is what turns the sentence into a polite request rather than an announcement.

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · Practice 2

**2.** Choose the correct form: <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>推薦状<rt>すいせんじょう</rt></ruby>を（<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>かせていただきました・<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いていただきました）。

{reveal}
<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>推薦状<rt>すいせんじょう</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いていただきました**。

{reveal}
The manager is the one who wrote the letter, so the plain て-form plus いただく is correct. The causative version would mean the speaker wrote it after being given permission.

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "May I use this room?"

{reveal}
この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>わせていただけますか**。 （or <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>わせていただいてもよろしいでしょうか。）

{reveal}
<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>う is an う-verb, giving <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>わせる - not ❌<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>わさせる, which is the さ<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>言葉<rt>ことば</rt></ruby> error - and the potential form of いただく turns the humble frame into an actual question.

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>まさせていただきます。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、**<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ませていただきます**。

{reveal}
<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む is an う-verb and takes `せる`, not `させる`. Only る-verbs, する and <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る take the `させ` form; the mistaken extra `さ` here is さ<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>言葉<rt>ことば</rt></ruby>.

## ～させてもらう・～ていただく · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ませていただきます。 (b) <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ませていただけませんか。

{reveal}
Sentence (a) is an announcement dressed in humble clothing: the speaker has decided to take the day off and is informing you courteously. Sentence (b) actually asks, and leaves the decision with the listener. Say (a) to a manager who has already approved the leave; say (b) when you still need approval, or you will sound as though you are helping yourself to it.
