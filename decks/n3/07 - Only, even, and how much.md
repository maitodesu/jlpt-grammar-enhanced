# ～だけ - as much as

**Meaning** · as much as ～, as many as ～ - up to the limit of what is possible, wanted, or available

This is the same `だけ` you already know as "only", pointed the other way. Instead of shrinking the amount ("only three"), it names a **ceiling and then fills it**: as much as you like, as fast as I can, everything there was. The word is identical; what changes it is the kind of word in front of it.

---

## ～だけ - as much as · Formation

The extent reading appears when `だけ` follows a word that names a **capacity, a desire, or a stock** - a potential form, `～たい`, `ある`, or <code><ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな</code>. Very often the same verb is repeated on both sides of it.

---

## ～だけ - as much as · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Potential verb | Vれる／Vられる + だけ | <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>える → <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>えるだけ<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>う |
| ～たい | Vたい + だけ | <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>たい → <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>たいだけ<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>る |
| ある | ある + だけ | ある → あるだけ<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>う |
| な-adjective <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き | <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな + だけ | <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きだ → <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きなだけ<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>る |
| できる (fixed adverb) | できるだけ + adverb／clause | できるだけ<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く |
| ほしい | ほしい + だけ | <ruby>欲<rt>ほ</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>欲<rt>ほ</rt></ruby>しいだけ<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていく |
| Amount, as a modifier | Noun + だけの + Noun | これ → これだけの<ruby>人数<rt>にんずう</rt></ruby> |

---

## ～だけ - as much as · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - repeat the verb (<code><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>えるだけ<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>う</code>) and the "as much as" reading is unmistakable. Native speakers reach for that doubling precisely because it removes the ambiguity with "only".

> **Note** - `が` and `を` behave as they do with ordinary `だけ`: they are dropped. <code><ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きなだけ<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってください</code>, not ❌<code><ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きなだけを<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってください</code>. Other particles survive: <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>したいだけ<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>したあとで.

---

## ～だけ - as much as · Examples

<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな**だけ**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてください。<br>*Eat as much as you like.*

できる**だけ**<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>をします。<br>*I'll reply as soon as I can.*

ある**だけ**<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby>かばんに<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れた。<br>*I put every last one I had into my bag.*

<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>りたい**だけ**<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>ったら、<ruby>頭<rt>あたま</rt></ruby>がすっきりした。<br>*After I'd slept as much as I wanted, my head felt clear.*

---

## ～だけ - as much as · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>文句<rt>もんく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いたい**だけ**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>って、<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>はさっさと<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>戻<rt>もど</rt></ruby>った。<br>*My little brother complained to his heart's content and then marched back to his room.*

<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>れる**だけ**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ったが、<ruby>終電<rt>しゅうでん</rt></ruby>には<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わなかった。<br>*I ran as hard as I could, but I still missed the last train.*

これ**だけの**<ruby>人数<rt>にんずう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>めるのは、<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>ではなかったでしょう。<br>*Getting this many people together can't have been easy.*

<ruby>欲<rt>ほ</rt></ruby>しい**だけ**<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていっていいと<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>われたが、そんなに<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>る<ruby>袋<rt>ふくろ</rt></ruby>がなかった。<br>*They told me to take as much as I wanted, but I didn't have a bag big enough.*

---

## ～だけ - as much as · Nuance & register

The pattern itself is register-neutral: <code>できるだけ<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く</code> is at home in a business email and in a text message alike. What varies is the flavour the surrounding words give it.

<code><ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きなだけ</code> and `Vたいだけ` are **generous or indulgent** when you say them to someone else - "help yourself" - and slightly **self-critical or exasperated** when you say them about someone: <code><ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いたいだけ<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う</code> carries a clear "and never mind the consequences." Tone of voice does the rest.

`できるだけ` is the workhorse. It attaches to an adverb (<code>できるだけ<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く</code>, <code>できるだけ<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>く</code>) or to a whole clause (<code>できるだけ<ruby>参加<rt>さんか</rt></ruby>するようにします</code>) and appears constantly in polite requests and commitments. Its stiffer written cousin is <code>できる<ruby>限<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>り</code>, and in formal writing you also meet <code><ruby>可能<rt>かのう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>限<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>り</code>.

---

## ～だけ - as much as · Nuance & register (cont.)

`あるだけ` and <code><ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>っているだけ</code> imply the stock is now **exhausted**, which is why they so often pair with <code><ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby></code>. If you do not mean "and that emptied it", say `たくさん` instead.

One structural point worth noticing: because `だけ` here measures an amount, `これだけ`・`それだけ`・`あれだけ` mean "this much" rather than "only this" - <code>あれだけ<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>したのに</code>, "after all those warnings." Beginners read those as "only" and get the sentence backwards.

---

## ～だけ - as much as · ～だけ "as much as" vs ～だけ "only"

> **Rule of thumb** - look at what sits **in front** of `だけ`. A fixed thing (a number, a noun, a plain dictionary verb) gives "only". A capacity or a wish (potential form, `～たい`, `ある`, <code><ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな</code>) gives "as much as".

<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つ**だけ**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I bought only one.*

<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>える**だけ**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I bought as many as I could.*

---

## ～だけ - as much as · ～だけ "as much as" vs ～だけ "only" (cont.)

The second sentence does not say the amount was small - it may have been a trolley-load. It says the amount ran up against a limit.

The trap is the plain dictionary form, which stays on the "only" side:

<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る**だけ**にします。<br>*I'll just look. (only looking, not buying)*

Put that verb into its potential form and the meaning flips completely: <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>られるだけ<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ておこう</code>, "let's see as much of it as we can."

---

## ～だけ - as much as · できるだけ vs なるべく

> **Rule of thumb** - `できるだけ` aims at the **maximum**: as much as is possible. `なるべく` aims at a **preference**: if it can be managed. `なるべく` is the gentler of the two in a request.

なるべく<ruby>現金<rt>げんきん</rt></ruby>でお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします。<br>*Cash if at all possible, please.*

できるだけ<ruby>現金<rt>げんきん</rt></ruby>でお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします。<br>*Please pay in cash as far as you possibly can.*

Asking a colleague to hurry, `なるべく` leaves them room; `できるだけ` tells them to push. Neither is impolite, but a string of `できるだけ` requests starts to sound demanding, and `なるべく` never does.

---

## ～だけ - as much as · ～だけ vs ～かぎり

> **Rule of thumb** - they overlap on ability and stock (<code>できるだけ／できる<ruby>限<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>り</code>), and `かぎり` is the more formal and more emphatic of the two. But `かぎり` also works on **knowledge and range**, where `だけ` cannot follow at all.

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている**<ruby>限<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>り**、その<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>はまだ<ruby>営業<rt>えいぎょう</rt></ruby>しています。<br>*As far as I know, that shop is still open.*

❌<code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っているだけ</code> cannot mean "as far as I know" - it means "only knowing (and not acting)". The same goes for <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>わたす<ruby>限<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>り</code> "as far as the eye can see" and <code><ruby>命<rt>いのち</rt></ruby>のある<ruby>限<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>り</code> "as long as I live". Keep `だけ` for countable amounts and effort, and let `かぎり` handle scope.

---

## ～だけ - as much as · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きだけ<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってください。 → ✅ <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き**な**だけ<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*<code><ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き</code> is a な-adjective, so it needs `な` before `だけ` - the same rule as ordinary `だけ`.*

- ❌ <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きなだけ**を**<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってください。 → ✅ <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きなだけ<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*`を` and `が` drop after `だけ`. Leaving them in sounds like a translation.*

- ❌ <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>るだけ<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>った。（meaning "I ran as far as I could"） → ✅ <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>**れる**だけ<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>った。<br>*A plain dictionary form gives the "only" reading. The extent meaning needs the potential form, the `～たい` form, or `ある`.*

---

## ～だけ - as much as · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ できるだけ**に**<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ります。 → ✅ できるだけ<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*`できるだけ` is already adverbial. Nothing goes between it and the word it modifies.*

- ❌ <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きなほど<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてください。 → ✅ <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな**だけ**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてください。<br>*`ほど` measures a degree by comparison, not a helping. An invitation to take as much as you want is always `だけ`.*

# ～きり

**Meaning** · only; just that much - and, after a past-tense verb, "and that was the last of it"

`きり` limits, like `だけ` does, but it leaves a mark that `だけ` does not: a sense of **finality**. <code><ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>きり</code> is not merely two people, it is two people and nobody else was let in. <code><ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけたきり</code> is not merely "went out", it is "went out, and whatever should have happened next never did."

---

## ～きり · Formation

`きり` follows a noun, a counter, or a **past-tense verb**. Note the asymmetry: as a limiter it takes nouns; as "and that was the last of it" it takes `～た`, never the dictionary form.

---

## ～きり · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (people, things) | Noun + きり | <ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby> → <ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>きり |
| Counter (number of times) | Counter + きり | <ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby> → <ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>きり |
| Demonstrative | これ／それ／あれ + きり | それ → それきり |
| Noun + きり + で | Noun + きりで | <ruby>一回<rt>いっかい</rt></ruby>きりで<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる |
| Verb, past | Vた + きり + (usually a negative) | <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた → <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけたきり<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>らない |
| Verb, past, as a state | Vた + きり + だ／の + Noun | <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>た → <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>たきりの<ruby>生活<rt>せいかつ</rt></ruby> |
| Emphatic, colloquial | っきり | <ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>っきり／これっきり |
| Reinforced with しか | Noun + きりしか + negative | <ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>きりしかない |

---

## ～きり · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - after `Vた`, the second clause is nearly always **negative**. `Vたきり` exists in order to say that the expected sequel did not arrive: went out → did not come back, borrowed → did not return it, met once → have not met since.

> **Note** - <code><ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>たきり</code> has hardened into an ordinary noun meaning "bedridden": <code><ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>たきりになる</code>, <code><ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>たきりの<ruby>老人<rt>ろうじん</rt></ruby></code>. The same freezing happened to `つきっきり`, "staying right beside someone the whole time."

---

## ～きり · Examples

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>**きり**で<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*Yesterday we talked just the two of us.*

<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ったのは<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>**きり**です。<br>*I've met her exactly once.*

<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby><ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた**きり**、まだ<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってきません。<br>*My brother went out this morning and still isn't back.*

<ruby>貸<rt>か</rt></ruby>した<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は、それ**きり**<ruby>返<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってこなかった。<br>*That book I lent out never came back.*

---

## ～きり · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>には<ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>**きり**しか<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っていなかった。<br>*There was only a thousand yen in my wallet.*

<ruby>祖父<rt>そふ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>た**きり**の<ruby>生活<rt>せいかつ</rt></ruby>をしています。<br>*My grandfather has been bedridden for three years.*

<ruby>入学式<rt>にゅうがくしき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>挨拶<rt>あいさつ</rt></ruby>をした**きり**、<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じクラスの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>とはほとんど<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>していません。<br>*Apart from saying hello at the entrance ceremony, I've hardly spoken to anyone in my class.*

<ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>った**きり**、<ruby>夕<rt>ゆう</rt></ruby>ごはんの<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>になっても<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>てきませんでした。<br>*My son shut himself in his room and didn't come out even when dinner time came.*

---

## ～きり · Nuance & register

`きり` is **spoken** Japanese first. It is perfectly usable in polite speech - <code><ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>きりです</code>, <code><ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけたきりです</code> - but formal writing prefers `だけ`, `のみ`, or an explicit <code>それ<ruby>以来<rt>いらい</rt></ruby></code> for the "since then" idea.

The doubled form `っきり` is a straight intensifier and a clear register marker: <code><ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>っきり</code>, `これっきり`, `それっきり`. It is warm and casual with friends and slightly childish or blunt in a work setting. In particular, `これっきりにして` ("that's the last time - stop it") is a telling-off, not a neutral request.

---

## ～きり · Nuance & register (cont.)

The limiting `きり` on nouns has narrowed over the last century. In modern Japanese it is alive mainly in set combinations - <code><ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>きり</code>, <code><ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>きり</code>, <code><ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>きり</code>, <code><ruby>一回<rt>いっかい</rt></ruby>きり</code>, `これきり`, `それきり` - and it sounds dated or regional in freer combinations. <code><ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>きりしかない</code> is still heard, and it is colloquial and a touch old-fashioned; if in doubt, plain <code><ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>しかない</code> is what most speakers say today.

Emotionally, `きり` leans toward **shortage and loss**. <code><ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>きり</code> of a chance means "one shot only, so don't waste it". <code><ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>きり</code> can be romantic or claustrophobic depending on the story, but it is never neutral - it always says that the rest of the world has been shut out.

---

## ～きり · きり vs だけ

> **Rule of thumb** - `だけ` draws a boundary and reports it. `きり` draws the same boundary and adds "and nothing further" - that is either not enough, or the end of the matter.

<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ったのは<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>**だけ**です。<br>*I've met them once.*

<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ったのは<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>**きり**です。<br>*I've met them just the once - and that was that.*

The `だけ` sentence is a plain count; you could follow it with "so we're meeting again next week". The `きり` sentence closes the door, so a follow-up meeting would sound like a contradiction.

---

## ～きり · きり vs だけ (cont.)

`だけ` is also far more flexible. It attaches to verbs in the dictionary form, to adjectives, and to desires (<code><ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きなだけ</code>); `きり` does none of these. Conversely, `だけ` cannot do the `Vたきり` job at all: ❌<code><ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけただけ<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってこない</code> is simply broken.

---

## ～きり · ～たきり vs ～たまま

> **Rule of thumb** - `～たまま` describes a **state left unchanged**. `～たきり` describes a **sequel that never came**. `まま` looks at the thing; `きり` looks at what should have happened next and didn't.

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け**たまま**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけてしまった。<br>*I went out and left the window open.*

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け**たきり**、<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めていない。<br>*I opened the window and haven't closed it once since.*

Both are true of the same window, but they are aimed differently. The `まま` sentence says how the window is; the `きり` sentence complains about the closing that never happened. That is why a `きり` clause pulls a negative in behind it, while a `まま` clause happily takes a positive one.

---

## ～きり · きり vs しか～ない

> **Rule of thumb** - `しか～ない` **requires** a negative predicate and states a shortfall. `きり` takes a positive predicate and states a limit. They can be stacked (`きりしか～ない`) for emphasis, but neither one replaces the other.

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>には<ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>**しか**い**ません**でした。<br>*There were only two people in the room.*

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>で<ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>**きり**で<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*We talked in the room, just the two of us.*

---

## ～きり · きり vs しか～ない (cont.)

The `しか` version counts heads and finds them few. The `きり` version is about privacy, and it needs a positive verb - ❌<code><ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>きりで<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しませんでした</code> would have to mean "we didn't talk alone", which is not what the pattern is for.

---

## ～きり · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かける**きり**、<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってこない。 → ✅ <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけ**た**きり、<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってこない。<br>*The "and that was the last of it" reading is built on the past form. A dictionary form before `きり` does not work.*

- ❌ <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たきり、<ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってきた。 → ✅ <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たきり、<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってこない。<br>*`Vたきり` announces that the sequel is missing, so a second clause reporting that it did happen contradicts the pattern. Use `～てから` for a plain sequence.*

- ❌ <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな**きり**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてください。 → ✅ <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな**だけ**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてください。<br>*`きり` cannot follow an adjective or express a generous amount. It limits; it does not invite.*

---

## ～きり · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>きり**しか**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しました。 → ✅ <ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>きり**で**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*`しか` demands a negative verb. If the verb is positive, `きり` stands on its own, usually followed by `で`.*

---

# ～さえ

**Meaning** · even ～ (of all things); and, with `ば`, "if only ～ - that alone is enough"

`さえ` picks the **most extreme member** of a scale and says the statement holds even there. If even a child knows it, everyone knows it; if she cannot even drink water, she certainly cannot eat. The same word has a second life in `さえ～ば`, which turns that extreme into a **minimum**: one condition, and nothing else, is all that is required.

---

## ～さえ · Formation

`さえ` is a focus particle. It sits where the noun's own particle would sit, and `が` and `を` are pushed out.

---

## ～さえ · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (subject or object) | Noun + さえ（が・を drop） | <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby> → <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>さえ<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>めない |
| Noun + other particle | に／で／から／と + さえ | <ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>に → <ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>にさえ<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>さない |
| Emphatic variant | Noun + でさえ | <ruby>専門家<rt>せんもんか</rt></ruby> → <ruby>専門家<rt>せんもんか</rt></ruby>でさえ |
| Verb | Vて + さえ | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てさえいない |
| Minimum condition, noun | Noun + さえ + ～ば | <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>さえあれば |
| Minimum condition, verb | Vます-stem + さえすれば | <ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>す → <ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>しさえすれば |
| Minimum condition, い-adjective | ～く + さえあれば | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くさえあれば |
| Minimum condition, な-adjective / noun | ～で + さえあれば | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>でさえあれば |

---

## ～さえ · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `さえ` on its own means "even". `さえ` with a `ば` clause behind it means "if only". The `ば` is what switches the meaning, so look for it before you translate.

> **Note** - the conditional half of `さえ～ば` is normally `ば`, or `なら` after a noun. Pairing `さえ` with `たら` is much rarer and many speakers avoid it, so keep to <code><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>さえあれば</code> rather than <code><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>さえあったら</code> until you have heard otherwise.

---

## ～さえ · Examples

この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**さえ**<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている。<br>*Even children know this kanji.*

<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しくて、ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>**さえ**ありません。<br>*I'm so busy I don't even have time to eat.*

<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くて、<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>**さえ**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>めなかった。<br>*My fever was so high I couldn't even drink water.*

パスワード**さえ**<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かれば、すぐログインできます。<br>*As long as you know the password, you can log in straight away.*

---

## ～さえ · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き**さえすれば**、<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>みは<ruby>完了<rt>かんりょう</rt></ruby>です。<br>*Just write your name and the application is done.*

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>すことを、まだ<ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>に**さえ**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>していない。<br>*I haven't told even my parents that I'm moving.*

それは<ruby>専門家<rt>せんもんか</rt></ruby>で**さえ**<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>できない<ruby>現象<rt>げんしょう</rt></ruby>なのだそうだ。<br>*Apparently it's a phenomenon that not even specialists can explain.*

<ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>**さえ**よければ、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>どおり<ruby>行<rt>おこな</rt></ruby>われます。<br>*Provided the weather holds, tomorrow's match will go ahead as scheduled.*

---

## ～さえ · Nuance & register

`さえ` is more **emphatic and more written** than the everyday `も`. In relaxed conversation people say <code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもも<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている</code>; `さえ` raises the volume and is common in complaints, in news writing, and in argument. It is not stiff, though - nobody will blink at it in speech, and `これさえあれば` is thoroughly colloquial.

`でさえ` is `さえ` with the noun's `で` kept in place, and it is the usual choice with **people presented as authorities or as extreme cases**: <code><ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>でさえ<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>える</code>. The plain <code><ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>さえ<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>える</code> is also correct; `でさえ` is a little heavier and a little more formal.

---

## ～さえ · Nuance & register (cont.)

`すら` is the literary sibling. It means the same as this `さえ` and appears in essays, editorials and formal speeches - <code><ruby>挨拶<rt>あいさつ</rt></ruby>すらしない</code>. It sounds bookish in casual conversation, and it has no `すら～ば` conditional use, so it is not a drop-in replacement.

Emotionally, the "even" `さえ` almost always leans **negative**. It turns up with negative verbs and with complaints far more often than with happy news, because its job is to show that a floor has been breached: not even water, not even a greeting, not even the specialists. `さえ～ば`, by contrast, is optimistic and often relieved - "one thing, and we're fine."

---

## ～さえ · さえ vs も

> **Rule of thumb** - both mark an unexpected member, but `も` is the neutral, everyday word and `さえ` is the **scale-topping** one. `さえ` says "this is the last case you would expect, and even it holds." `も` can also mean plain "also"; `さえ` never can.

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**も**<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しめる<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>です。<br>*It's a film children can enjoy too.*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**さえ**<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しめる<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>です。<br>*It's a film that even children can enjoy.*

---

## ～さえ · さえ vs も (cont.)

The first adds children to the audience. The second uses children as the hardest case and implies that adults are covered a fortiori. Swap them the wrong way round and you get the classic error: ❌<code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>さえ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きたいです</code> for "I want to go too" - that says "even I, of all people, want to go."

---

## ～さえ · さえ vs まで

> **Rule of thumb** - `さえ` marks a **low bar** that was not cleared, or a hardest case that still holds. `まで` marks a range that was **extended further than expected** - "went so far as to."

<ruby>挨拶<rt>あいさつ</rt></ruby>**さえ**してくれなかった。<br>*He didn't even say hello.*

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に**まで**<ruby>文句<rt>もんく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>った。<br>*He went so far as to complain to the department head.*

The `さえ` sentence points downward: greeting is the minimum, and the minimum was missing. The `まで` sentence points upward: complaining to colleagues would be normal, and he pushed past that. Where the sentence is about something falling short, `さえ` is nearly always the one you want.

---

## ～さえ · さえ～ば vs plain ～ば

> **Rule of thumb** - plain `ば` states **a** condition. `さえ～ば` states **the only** condition: satisfy this one thing and nothing else matters.

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>らなければ、<ruby>出発<rt>しゅっぱつ</rt></ruby>します。<br>*If it doesn't rain, we'll set off.*

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>**さえ**<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>らなければ、<ruby>出発<rt>しゅっぱつ</rt></ruby>します。<br>*As long as it doesn't rain, we'll set off.*

---

## ～さえ · さえ～ば vs plain ～ば (cont.)

The second sentence dismisses everything else - wind, cold, a late start - as irrelevant. That is why `さえ～ば` is the natural pattern for reassurance (<code><ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>さえ<ruby>丈夫<rt>じょうぶ</rt></ruby>なら、<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>とかなる</code>) and for regret when the condition was not met (<code>もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>さえすれば、<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ったのに</code>).

Note the verb form: with a verb, the pattern splits the verb open - <code><ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>しさえすれば</code>, <code><ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いさえすれば</code> - putting `さえ` after the ます-stem and letting `する` carry the conditional.

---

## ～さえ · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>**を**さえ<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>めない。 → ✅ <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>さえ<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>めない。<br>*`が` and `を` are replaced by `さえ`, not stacked with it. `に`, `で`, `と` and `から` are kept: <code><ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>にさえ</code>.*

- ❌ ボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>す**さえ**すれば<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>きます。 → ✅ ボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>**し**さえすれば<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*With a verb, `さえ` follows the ます-stem, and `する` takes the conditional ending.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**さえ**<ruby>参加<rt>さんか</rt></ruby>したいです。（meaning "I'd like to join too"） → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**も**<ruby>参加<rt>さんか</rt></ruby>したいです。<br>*`さえ` is not "too". Using it here says you are the least likely person to want to come.*

- ❌ <ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>**さえ**いない。 → ✅ <ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>**て**さえいない。<br>*Attached to a verb in this sense, `さえ` follows the て-form.*

---

## ～さえ · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>さえあったら、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いたいです。 → ✅ お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>さえあれば、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います。<br>*The `さえ～ば` frame wants `ば` (or `なら` after a noun), and its main clause states a result rather than a wish.*

---

# ～こそ

**Meaning** · this one and no other; precisely this - emphasis on the word in front of it

`こそ` has no English word behind it. It does not add information and it does not change what the sentence claims; it takes one element and **presses on it**, so that "this year I'll do it" becomes "*this* year I'll do it". The most useful way to learn it is by what it **replaces**: `こそ` pushes `は` and `が` out of the sentence and stands in their place, carrying their grammatical job plus an insistence they do not have.

---

## ～こそ · Formation

`こそ` goes straight after the element it emphasises. What that element is matters less than what particle it was wearing before.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun that would take が | Noun + こそ（が drops） | <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby> → <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>こそ<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>うべきだ |
| Noun that would take は | Noun + こそ（は drops） | これ → これこそ<ruby>本物<rt>ほんもの</rt></ruby>だ |
| Noun + another particle | に／で／と／から + こそ | あなたに → あなたにこそ<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>したい |
| Time or occasion | <ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>／<ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby> + こそ | <ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby> → <ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>こそ |
| Reason clause | ～から + こそ | <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きだから → <ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きだからこそ |
| Verb | ～て + こそ | <ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける → <ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けてこそ |
| Fixed reply | こちらこそ | ありがとう → こちらこそ |

---

## ～こそ · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `こそ` never stacks with `が`. ❌`これがこそ` does not exist. With `は` the picture is softer: `は` normally drops, but <code><ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>こそは</code> and `これこそは` are heard when a speaker wants to lean on the point even harder.

> **Note** - `～てこそ` is a different shape with a related job: it says the result holds **only by way of** the action. <code><ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>でやってこそ<ruby>意味<rt>いみ</rt></ruby>がある</code>, "it only means something if you do it yourself." Its second clause is positive; for the negative version Japanese uses `～なければ`.

---

## ～こそ · Examples

<ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>**こそ**<ruby>勝<rt>か</rt></ruby>ちたい。<br>*This time I really want to win.*

「ありがとうございました。」「いいえ、こちら**こそ**。」<br>*"Thank you very much." "No, thank *you*."*

これ**こそ**<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>がずっと<ruby>探<rt>さが</rt></ruby>していた<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>です。<br>*This is exactly the dictionary I've been looking for all this time.*

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>**こそ**<ruby>行動<rt>こうどう</rt></ruby>すべきときだ。<br>*Now is precisely the time to act.*

---

## ～こそ · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>失敗<rt>しっぱい</rt></ruby>した<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>に**こそ**、<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてみたい。<br>*It's the people who failed that I'd most like to hear from.*

<ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>だから**こそ**、うるさいことを<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>うのです。<br>*Parents nag precisely because they're worried.*

<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しずつ<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**てこそ**、<ruby>力<rt>ちから</rt></ruby>がつくのだと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。<br>*I think it's only by keeping at it a little every day that you build any strength.*

<ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby>**こそ**<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>そろって<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こうと<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しています。<br>*We keep saying that next year, for sure, we'll all get away together as a family.*

---

## ～こそ · Nuance & register

`こそ` is **emphatic and slightly formal** in free use. Speeches, editorials, slogans and advertising love it - <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>こそ</code>, `これこそ`, <code><ruby>本物<rt>ほんもの</rt></ruby>こそ</code> - and in ordinary conversation too much of it sounds declamatory, as if you were addressing a room rather than a friend.

That said, several `こそ` phrases are completely everyday and you should simply learn them whole:

---

## ～こそ · Nuance & register (cont.)

- `こちらこそ` - the standard reply that turns thanks or an apology back<br>*on the speaker. It is polite, warm and used at every register.*
- <code><ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>こそ</code> - "this time for real", after previous attempts<br>*failed.*
- `だからこそ` - "that is exactly why", used to push back on someone who<br>*drew the opposite conclusion.*
- <code><ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>こそ</code> - the particle of New Year's resolutions.

---

## ～こそ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Because `こそ` insists, it very often arrives in the middle of a **disagreement or a correction**. It implies that some other candidate was in play and is being set aside: <code><ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>こそ</code> implies the earlier attempts, `これこそ` implies the things that were not it. If nothing is being set aside, `こそ` sounds strange and a plain `が` or `は` is what you want.

One thing `こそ` does **not** do is change the truth of the sentence. <code><ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>です</code> and <code><ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんこそ<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>にふさわしい</code> both leave Yamada in the job; only the speaker's investment differs. Learners who translate `こそ` as "only" or "must" end up asserting more than the Japanese does.

---

## ～こそ · こそ vs が

> **Rule of thumb** - `が` **identifies**: it fills the empty slot in "which one?". `こそ` **insists**: it argues for one candidate against others already in the listener's mind. Answering a question takes `が`; arguing takes `こそ`.

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**が**<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>です。<br>*I'm the one in charge.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>に**こそ**<ruby>責任<rt>せきにん</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*The responsibility is mine, and mine above all.*

---

## ～こそ · こそ vs が (cont.)

Asked <code>どなたが<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>ですか</code>, you answer with `が`; replying <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>こそ<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>です</code> would sound like a protest against someone else's claim to the role. The reverse holds too: in a heated exchange, `あなたこそ` - "*you're* the one who…" - cannot be replaced by `が` without losing the whole point.

---

## ～こそ · こそ vs は

> **Rule of thumb** - `は` sets its noun quietly against alternatives. `こそ` sets it loudly against alternatives **and rules them out**. `こそ` occupies the same slot, so you never write both.

<ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>禁煙<rt>きんえん</rt></ruby>します。<br>*This year I'm going to quit smoking.*

<ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>**こそ**<ruby>禁煙<rt>きんえん</rt></ruby>します。<br>*This year I'm really going to quit smoking.*

Both sentences make the same promise. The `は` version compares this year with other years neutrally; the `こそ` version admits that previous attempts failed and stakes a claim on this one. That admission is why `こそ` fits resolutions, rematches and second chances so well - and why it sounds odd on a first attempt at anything.

---

## ～こそ · ～からこそ vs ～から

> **Rule of thumb** - `から` gives a reason. `からこそ` gives **the real reason**, usually correcting a wrong one the listener has assumed.

<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がない**から**、<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>えません。<br>*I can't help, because I don't have time.*

<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>な<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちだ**からこそ**、はっきり<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>うんです。<br>*It's precisely because you're a good friend of mine that I'm being blunt.*

The second sentence answers an unspoken "if we're friends, why are you being so harsh?" That defensive shape is typical: `からこそ` almost always appears where the listener might have expected the opposite conclusion. Note also that a `からこそ` sentence usually closes with `のだ`／`んです`, because it is explaining rather than reporting.

---

## ～こそ · Common mistakes

- ❌ これ**が**こそ<ruby>本物<rt>ほんもの</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ これこそ<ruby>本物<rt>ほんもの</rt></ruby>です。<br>*`こそ` takes the place of `が`; the two never appear together. `に`, `で`, `と` and `から` do survive in front of it.*

- ❌ どれ**こそ**あなたの<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>ですか。 → ✅ どれ**が**あなたの<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>ですか。<br>*A question word is an unknown, and `こそ` exists to insist on a known candidate. Question words take `が`.*

- ❌ <ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>する**こそ**、<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>になる。 → ✅ <ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>し**てこそ**、<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>になる。<br>*With a verb, `こそ` follows the て-form. A dictionary form plus `こそ` is not a pattern.*

- ❌ 「どなたが<ruby>司会<rt>しかい</rt></ruby>ですか。」「<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**こそ**です。」 → ✅ 「<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>**が**<ruby>司会<rt>しかい</rt></ruby>です。」<br>*Filling in an unknown is the job of `が`. `こそ` in an answer sounds like you are contradicting someone.*

# 数量 + は - at least

**Meaning** · at least this much; this much as a minimum (reading: すうりょう + は)

Put `は` on a number and the number stops being a measurement and becomes a **floor**. <code><ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>かかります</code> reports a journey time; <code><ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>はかかります</code> says "reckon on thirty minutes - it will not be less, and it may well be more." It is the same contrastive `は` you already know, doing arithmetic.

---

## 数量 + は - at least · Formation

Any expression of amount can take this `は` - a number plus counter, a money amount, a period of time, or a vague quantity word. The predicate is normally **positive**.

---

## 数量 + は - at least · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Number + counter | Quantity + は + predicate | <ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>はかかる |
| Money | Amount + は + する | <ruby>五千円<rt>ごせんえん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>五千円<rt>ごせんえん</rt></ruby>はする |
| Number of people | Counter + は + いる／<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る | <ruby>十人<rt>じゅうにん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>十人<rt>じゅうにん</rt></ruby>はいる |
| Object of a verb | Quantity + は + Vた（を drops） | <ruby>三回<rt>さんかい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>三回<rt>さんかい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んだ |
| Vague quantity | <ruby>半分<rt>はんぶん</rt></ruby>／ある<ruby>程度<rt>ていど</rt></ruby> + は | <ruby>半分<rt>はんぶん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>半分<rt>はんぶん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わった |
| With an approximator | Quantity + くらい／ぐらい + は | <ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>ぐらいはかかる |
| With an estimate marker | Quantity + は + だろう／はずだ／<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>だ | <ruby>二日<rt>ふつか</rt></ruby>はかかるだろう |
| Negative predicate - **different meaning** | Quantity + は + negative | <ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えていない |

---

## 数量 + は - at least · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the `は` goes on the **quantity**, not on the noun. <code><ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>を<ruby>二本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>っておこう</code>, not ❌<code><ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>は<ruby>二本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>っておこう</code>, which would be contrasting water with something else instead of setting a minimum.

> **Note** - an approximator and this `は` can stack, and the order is fixed: `ぐらいは`, never ❌`はぐらい`. The combination softens the floor to "about that much at the very least."

---

## 数量 + は - at least · Examples

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**は**かかります。<br>*It takes a good thirty minutes to the station.*

この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>なら<ruby>五千円<rt>ごせんえん</rt></ruby>**は**するでしょう。<br>*A dictionary like this must cost five thousand yen at least.*

あの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>にはいつも<ruby>十人<rt>じゅうにん</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>並<rt>なら</rt></ruby>んでいる。<br>*There are always at least ten people queuing outside that shop.*

その<ruby>小説<rt>しょうせつ</rt></ruby>なら<ruby>三回<rt>さんかい</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みました。<br>*I've read that novel at least three times.*

---

## 数量 + は - at least · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しの<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>には<ruby>一週間<rt>いっしゅうかん</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>です。<br>*You'll need a week at the very least to get ready for the move.*

<ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>影響<rt>えいきょう</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>くまでに<ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby>**は**かかりそうです。<br>*Because of the typhoon it looks like it'll be three hours or more before the trains run.*

<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合格<rt>ごうかく</rt></ruby>したいなら、<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>二時間<rt>にじかん</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>したほうがいいですよ。<br>*If you want to pass the exam you should be studying two hours a day minimum.*

<ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>には<ruby>百人<rt>ひゃくにん</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まっていたと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いますが、<ruby>正確<rt>せいかく</rt></ruby>な<ruby>人数<rt>にんずう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません。<br>*I'd say a hundred people had gathered at the venue, though I don't know the exact number.*

---

## 数量 + は - at least · Nuance & register

This is a plain contrastive `は` and it is register-neutral: it appears in casual speech, in polite speech and in writing without changing shape. In conversation the **quantity takes the stress** - `さんじゅっぷんは かかるよ` - and that stress is doing much of the work, so listen for it.

What the pattern conveys is **confidence about the low end**. The speaker is not claiming precision; they are claiming that the true figure will not fall below the one given. That is why it pairs so naturally with hedges: `だろう`, `でしょう`, <code>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code>, `はずだ`, `そうです`. Combining a floor with a hedge is not a contradiction - "at least this much, probably more, I can't say exactly."

---

## 数量 + は - at least · Nuance & register (cont.)

The pattern is common in three everyday situations: estimating **time and cost** (<code><ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>はかかる</code>, <code><ruby>一万円<rt>いちまんえん</rt></ruby>はする</code>), asserting **effort you have already put in** (<code><ruby>三回<rt>さんかい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>した</code> - "I checked it three times, thank you very much"), and setting a **requirement** for someone else (<code><ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだほうがいい</code>). The second of these often carries a defensive edge, because the contrastive `は` invites the comparison "and that's more than you're giving me credit for."

Beside it stands the adverb <code><ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>なくとも</code>, which says "at least" explicitly. It is a shade more formal and more logical in tone, it does not replace the `は`, and the two frequently appear together in the same sentence.

---

## 数量 + は - at least · 数量 + は vs 数量 + も

> **Rule of thumb** - `は` sets a **floor**: at least this much. `も` expresses **surprise at the size**: as much as this. `は` is sober; `も` is impressed or appalled.

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>二時間<rt>にじかん</rt></ruby>**は**かかります。<br>*The meeting will take two hours at least.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>二時間<rt>にじかん</rt></ruby>**も**かかりました。<br>*The meeting dragged on for two whole hours.*

---

## 数量 + は - at least · 数量 + は vs 数量 + も (cont.)

The first is planning; the second is a complaint. Note the tense that comes with them: `は` looks forward to an amount not yet known, so it sits with non-past and with hedges, while `も` reacts to an amount already established, so it sits comfortably in the past. Using `も` for a forecast - ❌<code><ruby>二時間<rt>にじかん</rt></ruby>もかかります</code> as a neutral estimate - makes you sound outraged by your own schedule.

---

## 数量 + は - at least · 数量 + は vs 少なくとも

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>なくとも</code> is an adverb and states the minimum in words; the `は` marks it grammatically. Use `は` in speech, add <code><ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>なくとも</code> when you want the floor spelled out.

<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>なくとも<ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たされた。<br>*I was kept waiting at least half an hour.*

<code><ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>なくとも</code> can also modify things that are not quantities at all - <code><ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>なくとも<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>くらいはしてほしい</code>, "I'd at least like to be told" - where the number-plus-`は` pattern has nothing to attach to. In return, `は` is the one that works when no adverb is wanted, which in ordinary conversation is most of the time.

---

## 数量 + は with a positive predicate vs with a negative one

> **Rule of thumb** - the same shape does two opposite jobs. **Positive** predicate: at least this much. **Negative** predicate: **not all** of this much - partial negation.

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>百<rt>ひゃく</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えた。<br>*I've learned a hundred kanji at least.*

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えていない。<br>*I haven't learned all the kanji. (I have learned some)*

---

## 数量 + は with a positive predicate vs with a negative one (cont.)

This is not two grammars; it is one `は` marking a contrast, with the negative scoping over the quantity rather than over the verb. Still, the practical effect is that a careless negative reverses your meaning. If you want to say "it takes at least thirty minutes", the verb must be positive: <code><ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>はかかります</code>. <code><ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>はかかりません</code> tells the listener the opposite - it will not take a full thirty minutes.

---

## 数量 + は - at least · Common mistakes

# くらい / ぐらい

**Meaning** · about, roughly; to the extent that ～; and - dismissively - "no more than that"

`くらい` names a **degree**. Attached to a number it makes the number approximate ("about thirty minutes"); attached to a clause it measures one thing against another ("so tired I couldn't stand"); attached to a small task it belittles it ("it's only washing up"). Those three uses look unrelated in English, but they are one idea: this is the level we are talking about.

---

## くらい / ぐらい · Formation

`くらい` and `ぐらい` are the same word. Both attach the same way, and nothing about the attachment decides which of the two you say.

---

| Noun | Noun + くらい | <ruby>挨拶<rt>あいさつ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>挨拶<rt>あいさつ</rt></ruby>くらい |

## くらい / ぐらい · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `くらい` measures **how much**, so it goes with spans, amounts and degrees. It does not name a point on the calendar or the clock; that is `ごろ`. <code><ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby>ぐらい</code> but <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>ごろ</code>.

> **Note** - `～くらいなら` is a related pattern worth recognising: it rejects one option in favour of another. <code><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つくらいなら、<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いて<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る</code> - "if it comes to waiting, I'd rather walk home." The first clause names the level the speaker refuses to sink to.

---

## くらい / ぐらい · Nuance & register

`くらい` and `ぐらい` mean exactly the same thing and both are correct everywhere. The tendency - and it is only a tendency - is that `ぐらい` is a little more frequent in speech and after the demonstratives (`これぐらい`, `どれぐらい`), while `くらい` turns up slightly more often in careful writing. No listener will notice which you chose, so pick one and be consistent within a sentence.

The **approximate** use is completely neutral and extremely common; Japanese conversation prefers a hedged number to a bare one, and a bare <code><ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>かかります</code> can sound oddly exact where <code><ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>ぐらい</code> is expected.

---

## くらい / ぐらい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The **belittling** use is the one to handle with care. Applied to your own effort it is modest and generous: <code>それくらい、<ruby>喜<rt>よろこ</rt></ruby>んでやります</code> - "that's nothing, happy to." Applied to someone **else's** effort it says their contribution is trivial, and in a request it says the bar you are setting is embarrassingly low: <code><ruby>挨拶<rt>あいさつ</rt></ruby>くらいしなさい</code> is something a parent says to a child. Aimed upward at a manager or a customer it is rude, so keep it for peers, family and yourself.

There is also a superlative frame worth recognising: `～くらい～はない`, "nothing is as ～ as this." <code><ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>くらいつらいものはない</code> - "there's nothing as grim as summer exams." It behaves the same way as the more formal `～ほど～はない`.

---

## くらい / ぐらい · くらい vs ごろ

> **Rule of thumb** - `ごろ` marks a **point in time**; `くらい` marks a **span or an amount**. If the word answers "when?", use `ごろ`. If it answers "how long?" or "how many?", use `くらい`.

<ruby>七時<rt>しちじ</rt></ruby>**ごろ**<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*I leave the house around seven.*

<ruby>七時間<rt>しちじかん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ました。<br>*I slept for about seven hours.*

---

## くらい / ぐらい · くらい vs ごろ (cont.)

The counter tells you which is which: <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby></code> is a clock time and takes `ごろ`; <code><ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby></code> is a duration and takes `ぐらい`. Dates and days work like clock times - <code><ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>水曜日<rt>すいようび</rt></ruby>ごろ</code>. Money, distance, weight and headcount are all amounts, so they can only take `くらい`: ❌<code><ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>ごろ</code> is not Japanese.

> **Note** - you will hear <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>ぐらいに</code> in casual speech, and it is not an error, but `ごろ` is the standard choice for a clock time and the safe one in writing.

---

## くらい / ぐらい · くらい vs ほど

> **Rule of thumb** - for approximation they overlap, with `ほど` sounding more formal. For degree, `ほど` points **up** the scale and `くらい` can point **down** it. Only `くらい` can belittle.

<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>**ほど**お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちいただけますか。<br>*Could I ask you to wait for about an hour?*

<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ってよ。<br>*Come on, wait an hour, would you?*

Those two sentences carry the same number and completely different manners: `ほど` belongs to the polite register and shows up constantly in service Japanese, while the second sentence is a friend being pushy.

---

## くらい / ぐらい · くらい vs ほど (cont.)

The split is sharper with degrees. <code><ruby>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>ぬほど<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れた</code> and <code><ruby>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>ぬくらい<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れた</code> are both "dead tired", but move to the bottom of the scale and `ほど` stops working: <code>お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>くらい<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください</code> ("you could at least offer tea") cannot become ❌<code>お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください</code>. Remember also that `ほど` owns two frames `くらい` does not share: `～ば～ほど` and comparative `～ほど～ない`.

---

## くらい / ぐらい · Belittling くらい vs だけ

> **Rule of thumb** - `だけ` states a limit neutrally. `くらい` states the same limit and adds a judgement: that little is not worth making a fuss about.

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>五<rt>いつ</rt></ruby>つ**だけ**<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えました。<br>*I learned just five kanji.*

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>五<rt>いつ</rt></ruby>つ**ぐらい**<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えてください。<br>*Learn five kanji at least, would you.*

---

## くらい / ぐらい · Belittling くらい vs だけ (cont.)

`だけ` reports; `くらい` evaluates. That is why the second sentence works as a complaint and the first does not, and why swapping them in a request changes what you are doing socially. <code><ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>だけ<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかった</code> counts the absentees; <code><ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>ぐらい<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいい</code> waves them away as unimportant.

---

## くらい / ぐらい · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby>**ごろ**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しました。 → ✅ <ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*`ごろ` needs a point in time. A duration counter such as <code><ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby></code> can only take `くらい`／`ぐらい`.*

- ❌ <ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>くらい<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しかった。 → ✅ <ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>**な**くらい<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しかった。<br>*A な-adjective keeps its `な` before `くらい`, exactly as it does before `だけ` and `ので`.*

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**してください。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、ご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>をお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いできますか。<br>*Not a grammar error but a social one. Belittling `くらい` aimed at a superior is openly rude; save it for yourself and for people you are close to.*

- ❌ <ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>**ごろ**の<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**の<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby><br>*Money is an amount, never a point in time.*

---

## くらい / ぐらい · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しい**ぐらい**です。（meaning "it's really hard"） → ✅ この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>解<rt>と</rt></ruby>けない**ぐらい**<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しいです。<br>*`くらい` needs something to measure the degree **against**. On its own after an adjective it produces the opposite, dismissive reading: "it's merely hard."*

# ～あまり

**Meaning** · out of too much ～; so ～ that … - an excess of feeling or effort that produces an unintended result

`～あまり` names a **cause that went too far**. Worry, nerves, delight, haste or overwork build past a reasonable level, and the second half of the sentence reports what that excess brought about - usually something the person did not intend. English reaches for "so worried that…" or "in his haste…"; Japanese puts the excess in a noun-like phrase and hangs the consequence off it.

---

## ～あまり · Formation

The excess comes first, `あまり` closes it, and the result follows after a comma. Almost anything before `あまり` has to name a **feeling, a state or a degree**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Emotion noun | Noun + の + あまり | <ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>のあまり |
| Degree noun in ～さ | ～さ + の + あまり | <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しさ → <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しさのあまり |
| Verb, non-past | Vる + あまり | <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぐ → <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぐあまり |
| Verb, past | Vた + あまり | <ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby>した → <ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby>したあまり |
| Fronted degree | あまりの + Noun／～さ + に | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>さ → あまりの<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>さに |
| Result clause | ～あまり、（unintended outcome） | <ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>きのあまり、<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なかった |

---

## ～あまり · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the second clause reports something **beyond the speaker's control**: could not sleep, dropped it, forgot, burst into tears, missed the deadline. If the outcome was planned and ordinary, the sentence wants `ので` or `から`, not `あまり`.

> **Note** - `あまりの～に` is the same idea rearranged, with the degree word turned into a `さ`-noun: <code>あまりの<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>さに、<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>られなかった</code>. Learn both shapes; news writing uses them about equally.

---

## ～あまり · Examples

<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>の**あまり**、<ruby>昨夜<rt>さくや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れませんでした。<br>*I was so worried I couldn't sleep last night.*

<ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby>の**あまり**、<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なかった。<br>*I was so nervous my voice wouldn't come out.*

<ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>きの**あまり**、<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>に<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていたコップを<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>としてしまった。<br>*I was so startled that I dropped the glass I was holding.*

<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぐ**あまり**、<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れてきてしまった。<br>*In my hurry I went and left my wallet at home.*

---

## ～あまり · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>喜<rt>よろこ</rt></ruby>びの**あまり**、<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>はその<ruby>場<rt>ば</rt></ruby>で<ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*Overcome with joy, she burst into tears on the spot.*

<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しさの**あまり**、<ruby>友人<rt>ゆうじん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>誕生日<rt>たんじょうび</rt></ruby>をすっかり<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れていました。<br>*Things were so hectic that I completely forgot my friend's birthday.*

<ruby>完璧<rt>かんぺき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>求<rt>もと</rt></ruby>める**あまり**、<ruby>締<rt>し</rt></ruby>め<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>りに<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わなくなることがある。<br>*Chasing perfection can go so far that you end up missing the deadline.*

<ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>成績<rt>せいせき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>にする**あまり**、<ruby>母親<rt>ははおや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>毎晩<rt>まいばん</rt></ruby><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>小言<rt>こごと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>繰<rt>く</rt></ruby>り<ruby>返<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>すようになった。<br>*So preoccupied did she become with her son's grades that his mother started repeating the same scolding every night.*

---

## ～あまり · Nuance & register

This is a **written** pattern. It belongs to novels, newspaper reports, formal speeches and essays; in ordinary conversation people say <code><ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>で<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れなかった</code> or <code><ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>しすぎて<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れなかった</code>. Using <code><ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>のあまり</code> with a friend is not wrong, but it sounds as if you are narrating your own life.

The nouns that fit in front of it form a fairly closed set - emotions and pressures: <code><ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>き</code>, <code><ruby>喜<rt>よろこ</rt></ruby>び</code>, <code><ruby>悲<rt>かな</rt></ruby>しみ</code>, <code><ruby>怒<rt>いか</rt></ruby>り</code>, <code><ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れ</code>, <code><ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しさ</code>, <code><ruby>恥<rt>は</rt></ruby>ずかしさ</code>. A neutral noun does not work: ❌<code><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>のあまり</code> is not Japanese, because rain is not a degree of anything.

---

## ～あまり · Nuance & register (cont.)

The tone is nearly always **regretful or cautionary**. Even with a positive emotion, the result is an overreaction: joy that ends in tears, enthusiasm that ends in a missed deadline. If the result is simply good news, the sentence needs a different connector - <code><ruby>喜<rt>よろこ</rt></ruby>びのあまり<ruby>合格<rt>ごうかく</rt></ruby>した</code> is incoherent, because passing an exam is not something excess joy causes.

Grammatically, the phrase behaves like an adverbial and does not take `は` or `が`. It sits at the head of the sentence, or right after the subject: <code><ruby>母親<rt>ははおや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>のあまり…</code>.

---

## ～あまり · ～あまり vs ～ので

> **Rule of thumb** - `ので` gives a plain reason. `あまり` gives a reason that was **excessive**, and its result is correspondingly abnormal.

<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>だった**ので**、<ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*I was worried, so I rang several times.*

<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>の**あまり**、<ruby>夜中<rt>よなか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby>も<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>してしまった。<br>*I was so worried that I ended up ringing over and over in the middle of the night.*

The `ので` version reports reasonable behaviour. The `あまり` version admits the behaviour got out of hand - which is why `てしまう` is such a frequent companion. Swap them and you either lose the excess (`ので`) or accidentally confess to one (`あまり`).

---

## ～あまり · ～あまり vs ～すぎて

> **Rule of thumb** - `すぎて` is the everyday word for "too much" and attaches to any verb or adjective. `あまり` is literary and restricted to degrees of feeling and effort.

<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**すぎて**、お<ruby>腹<rt>なか</rt></ruby>が<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>くなった。<br>*I ate too much and got a stomach ache.*

<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え**すぎる****あまり**、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>められなくなった。<br>*He thought about it so hard that he could no longer decide anything.*

---

## ～あまり · ～あまり vs ～すぎて (cont.)

Notice that the second sentence uses both: `すぎる` supplies the excess and `あまり` frames it as the cause of the paralysis. That stacking is common and perfectly natural. What you cannot do is put an everyday physical action straight in front of `あまり`: ❌<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べるあまり</code> asks the reader to treat eating as an emotion.

---

## ～あまり · ～あまり vs あまり～ない

> **Rule of thumb** - same word, opposite structures. **Something + `の`／verb + あまり** at the *head of a clause* means "so much that". Bare **`あまり` in front of a negative** means "not very". The `の` and the negative are the tells.

<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れの**あまり**、<ruby>食欲<rt>しょくよく</rt></ruby>がなくなった。<br>*I was so exhausted that I lost my appetite.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は**あまり**<ruby>食欲<rt>しょくよく</rt></ruby>がない。<br>*I don't have much appetite today.*

---

## ～あまり · ～あまり vs あまり～ない (cont.)

The first `あまり` follows its noun; the second precedes the whole predicate and demands a negative. In speech the two are also stressed differently, and the "not very" one has the casual variants `あんまり` and `あんま`, which the `～あまり` pattern never takes: ❌<code><ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れのあんまり</code>.

---

## ～あまり · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>あまり、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>した。 → ✅ <ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>**の**あまり、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*A noun needs `の` to join `あまり`. Only verbs attach directly.*

- ❌ <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>し**い**あまり、<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れました。 → ✅ <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>し**さ**のあまり、<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れました。<br>*An い-adjective has to become a `さ`-noun first. The pattern wants a degree, and `～さ` is what turns an adjective into one.*

- ❌ <ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しさのあまり、<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>どおり<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>を<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>めた。 → ✅ <ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しさのあまり、<ruby>大声<rt>おおごえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げてしまった。<br>*The result clause has to be something the excess pushed the person into. An orderly, planned outcome contradicts the pattern.*

---

## ～あまり · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**あまりの**なかった。 → ✅ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**あまり**なかった。<br>*The "not very" adverb takes no `の` and no following noun. If you find yourself writing `のあまり` in a negative sentence, you have mixed the two patterns.*

- ❌ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>のあまり、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になった。 → ✅ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>のため、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になった。<br>*`あまり` needs a degree of feeling or effort, not an ordinary event. For a plain cause in written Japanese, use `のため`.*

# あまりに

**Meaning** · too ～; excessively ～ - so far past the normal level that something follows from it

`あまりに` is a degree adverb, like `とても`, but with a verdict attached. `とても` says the level is high; `あまりに` says the level has gone **past where it should be**, and the sentence usually goes on to say what that cost. Too expensive to buy, too sudden to answer, too detailed for anyone to remember.

---

## あまりに · Formation

`あまりに` stands **in front of** the word whose degree it is measuring. It never follows it.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective | あまりに + い-adjective | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い → あまりに<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い |
| な-adjective | あまりに + な-adjective | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ → あまりに<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ |
| な-adjective + noun | あまりに + ～な + Noun | <ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby> → あまりに<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby> |
| Verb | あまりに + Verb | <ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>く → あまりに<ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>いて |
| Emphatic | あまりにも + … | あまりにも<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い |
| With a result | あまりに～て／～ので、… | あまりに<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>くて、<ruby>飽<rt>あ</rt></ruby>きた |
| Degree turned into a noun | あまりの + ～さ／Noun + に | <ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>さ → あまりの<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>さに |

---

## あまりに · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `あまりに` is an **adverb**, so it takes no `の` and no `な` of its own. If you want a `の` after it, the following word must become a noun: あまり**に**<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くて／あまり**の**<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>**さ**に.

> **Note** - a bare `あんまりだ` is a fixed colloquial complaint meaning "that's not fair", used of someone's behaviour: `それはあんまりじゃない?`. It comes from this same word but works as a `な`-adjective predicate, not as an adverb.

---

## あまりに · Examples

<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が**あまりに**<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くて、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>えませんでした。<br>*It was so expensive that I couldn't buy it.*

**あまりに**<ruby>突然<rt>とつぜん</rt></ruby>のことで、<ruby>言葉<rt>ことば</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なかった。<br>*It was all so sudden that I couldn't find any words.*

<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>は**あまりにも**<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くて、<ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>はやめました。<br>*It was far too cold outside, so we gave up on the walk.*

<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>が**あまりに**うますぎるので、かえって<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>になった。<br>*The offer sounded too good, which in fact made me suspicious.*

---

## あまりに · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>は**あまりに**<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>く、<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>くなってしまった。<br>*His explanation went on far too long and I got sleepy halfway through.*

**あまりの**<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>さに、<ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>るのをあきらめた。<br>*There were so many people that I gave up on getting into the venue.*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>規則<rt>きそく</rt></ruby>は**あまりにも**<ruby>細<rt>こま</rt></ruby>かくて、<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えられる<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>はいないでしょう。<br>*The new rules are so fiddly that nobody is going to remember all of them.*

**あまりに**<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>なお<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らせだったので、<ruby>参加<rt>さんか</rt></ruby>できない<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>も<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>かったようです。<br>*The announcement came at such short notice that a lot of people apparently couldn't attend.*

---

## あまりに · Nuance & register

`あまりに` is **emphatic and a little formal**. In casual conversation the usual words are `すごく`, `めっちゃ` and the `～すぎる` ending; `あまりに` sounds deliberate, and it is at home in writing, in complaints made carefully, and in speech where you want the excess to register.

`あまりにも` adds another notch of emphasis and is, if anything, more common than the bare form in modern speech. There is no difference in meaning; `も` simply presses harder.

---

## あまりに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The word carries an **evaluation**, and that evaluation is usually negative: too expensive, too sudden, too strict. It can front a positive adjective, but even then the sentence is reporting a reaction that overwhelmed the speaker - <code>あまりに<ruby>美<rt>うつく</rt></ruby>しくて、<ruby>言葉<rt>ことば</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なかった</code>. What it cannot do is state a plain, contented high degree. For "the cake was really delicious", say `とても`; `あまりにおいしかった` without a consequence sounds unfinished, as though a "so I…" clause had been cut off.

That expectation of a consequence is the practical point. Most `あまりに` sentences have a second half - `～て`, `～ので`, `～ため` - or end in an exclamation. When you write one, ask yourself what followed from the excess; if nothing did, you probably wanted `とても`.

---

## あまりに · あまりに vs とても

> **Rule of thumb** - `とても` measures. `あまりに` judges. `とても` says "very"; `あまりに` says "more than it should be".

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は**とても**<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>いですね。<br>*This room is really spacious, isn't it.*

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は**あまりに**<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>くて、<ruby>掃除<rt>そうじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>です。<br>*This room is so big that cleaning it is a chore.*

---

## あまりに · あまりに vs とても (cont.)

Both sentences agree about the size of the room; only the second one has a problem with it. That is why `とても` fits compliments and `あまりに` fits complaints, warnings and excuses. Note too that `とても` has a second life in front of a negative (<code>とても<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じられない</code>, "I simply can't believe it") which `あまりに` does not share.

---

## あまりに · あまりに vs ～すぎる

> **Rule of thumb** - `～すぎる` is a **verb ending** attached to the word itself; `あまりに` is a separate **adverb** placed before it. They mean much the same thing, and Japanese happily uses both at once.

この<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>き**すぎ**ます。<br>*These clothes are too big.*

この<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>は**あまりに**<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>き**すぎ**ます。<br>*These clothes are far too big.*

---

## あまりに · あまりに vs ～すぎる (cont.)

`すぎる` is the everyday choice and the one to reach for in conversation. `あまりに` is what you add when you want to underline the excess or when the sentence is written. One structural difference matters: `すぎる` conjugates and can end a sentence on its own, while `あまりに` cannot - ❌<code>この<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>はあまりにです</code> is not a sentence.

---

## あまりに · あまりに vs ～あまり

> **Rule of thumb** - `あまりに` **precedes** the word it modifies and is an adverb. `～あまり` **follows** a noun (with `の`) or a verb and introduces a clause. Word order is the whole test.

**あまりに**<ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby>して、<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>が<ruby>震<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>えた。<br>*I was so nervous my hands shook.*

<ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby>の**あまり**、<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>が<ruby>震<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>えた。<br>*Out of sheer nerves, my hands shook.*

---

## あまりに · あまりに vs ～あまり (cont.)

The two sentences mean nearly the same thing, which is why the pair is so easily muddled. The difference is in what each one attaches to: `あまりに` needs a verb or adjective **after** it, while `あまり` needs a noun or verb **before** it. Get the order backwards - ❌<code><ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby>にあまり</code>, ❌<code>あまりの<ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby>して</code> - and neither pattern survives.

---

## あまりに · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いあまりに、<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なかった。 → ✅ **あまりに**<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くて、<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なかった。<br>*`あまりに` is an adverb and comes first. Putting it after the adjective produces neither pattern.*

- ❌ あまり**の**<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くて<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れなかった。 → ✅ あまり**に**<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くて<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れなかった。／✅ あまり**の**<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>**さ**に<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れなかった。<br>*`の` has to be followed by a noun. Either keep the adjective and use `に`, or turn the adjective into a `さ`-noun and use `の`.*

- ❌ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**あまりに**ありません。（meaning "I don't have much time"） → ✅ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**あまり**ありません。<br>*The "not very" adverb is the bare `あまり`. Adding `に` switches you to the "too much" word, which does not belong in front of a plain negative.*

---

## あまりに · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ あまりに<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>かなかった。 → ✅ あまりに<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**な**<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>かなかった。<br>*`あまりに` changes nothing about how the な-adjective attaches to its noun.*

- ❌ ケーキがあまりにおいしかったです。（as a simple compliment） → ✅ ケーキが**とても**おいしかったです。<br>*`あまりに` expects a consequence. With nothing following, a listener waits for the rest of the sentence.*

---

# ～ことか

**Meaning** · how ～! - an exclamation shaped like a question, for a feeling too big to state plainly

`～ことか` ends a sentence that looks like a question and expects no answer. The speaker is not asking how worried they were; they are saying the amount cannot be put into words. English does the same trick with "how many times have I told you!" - a question mark would be wrong there too.

---

## ～ことか · Formation

The pattern needs two halves: a **degree word** near the front, and `ことか` at the end. Without the degree word the sentence collapses into an ordinary question.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb, plain | Vる／Vた／Vている + ことか | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っていた → どれほど<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っていたことか |
| い-adjective | ～い + ことか | <ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しい → どんなに<ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しいことか |
| な-adjective | ～な + ことか | <ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>だ → どんなに<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>なことか |
| Noun predicate | ～な + ことか | <ruby>幸<rt>しあわ</rt></ruby>せ → なんと<ruby>幸<rt>しあわ</rt></ruby>せなことか |
| Degree words used | どんなに／どれほど／どれだけ／<ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby>／<ruby>何回<rt>なんかい</rt></ruby>／なんと | <ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby><ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったことか |
| Softer variants | ～ことだろう／～ことでしょう | どんなに<ruby>喜<rt>よろこ</rt></ruby>ぶことでしょう |

---

## ～ことか · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the clause before `ことか` takes the **plain form**, however polite the rest of the passage is. Politeness is expressed by switching to `ことでしょう`, not by inserting `です` or `ます`.

> **Note** - `ことだろう` and `ことでしょう` are the same exclamation with a softer edge, and they point outward at someone else's feelings: <code>ご<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>はどんなに<ruby>安心<rt>あんしん</rt></ruby>されたことでしょう</code>. Use `ことか` for your own feelings and `ことでしょう` when imagining another person's.

---

## ～ことか · Examples

どんなに<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>した**ことか**。<br>*You have no idea how worried I was.*

この<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>をどれほど<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っていた**ことか**。<br>*How long I've waited for this day.*

<ruby>何度<rt>なんど</rt></ruby><ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じことを<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>した**ことか**。<br>*How many times have I told you the same thing!*

<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で<ruby>暮<rt>く</rt></ruby>らすのがどんなに<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>な**ことか**、やってみて<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かった。<br>*Only when I tried it did I realise how hard living alone is.*

---

## ～ことか · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>合格<rt>ごうかく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らせを<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いて、<ruby>両親<rt>りょうしん</rt></ruby>はどんなに<ruby>喜<rt>よろこ</rt></ruby>んだ**ことか**。<br>*When they heard I'd passed, how delighted my parents must have been.*

<ruby>留学中<rt>りゅうがくちゅう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>からの<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>にどれだけ<ruby>励<rt>はげ</rt></ruby>まされた**ことか**。<br>*While I was studying abroad, those letters from home kept me going more than I can say.*

あのとき<ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>けてもらえなかったら、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>ごろどうなっていた**ことか**。<br>*If nobody had helped me then, goodness knows where I'd be now.*

<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>える<ruby>景色<rt>けしき</rt></ruby>の、なんと<ruby>美<rt>うつく</rt></ruby>しい**ことか**。<br>*How beautiful the view from the mountaintop is.*

---

## ～ことか · Nuance & register

`ことか` is **emotive and literary**. It lives in essays, memoirs, letters, speeches, drama and song lyrics; in everyday conversation it sounds heightened, and younger speakers use it sparingly. That is not a reason to avoid it - it is a reason to notice what you are doing when you say it. Dropping a <code>どんなに<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>したことか</code> into a conversation makes the moment weightier, which is sometimes exactly right.

The everyday equivalents are worth having side by side:

---

## ～ことか · Nuance & register (cont.)

- <code><ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>したよ</code> - plain and warm.
- <code>どれだけ<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>したと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>ってるの</code> - colloquial, and the<br>*usual spoken way of scolding someone with the same content.*
- <code>どんなに<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>したことか</code> - the written, weightier version.

The emotion is usually one of two kinds: **long-suffering** (worry, waiting, repeated warnings ignored) or **overflowing gratitude and relief**. Both are retrospective, which is why the verb before `ことか` is so often in the past or the `～ている` form.

---

## ～ことか · Nuance & register (cont.)

Punctuation is a small giveaway. Because the sentence is not a real question, it is normally written with a full stop rather than a question mark, and it is said with falling intonation. If you catch yourself waiting for an answer, you have misread it.

---

## ～ことか · ～ことか vs ～だろう

> **Rule of thumb** - `だろう` **guesses**. `ことか` **exclaims**. `だろう` is uncertain about a fact; `ことか` is certain about a feeling and only uncertain about how to measure it.

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いた**だろう**。<br>*He's probably arrived by now.*

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>到着<rt>とうちゃく</rt></ruby>をどれほど<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>った**ことか**。<br>*How long we waited for him to arrive.*

---

## ～ことか · ～ことか vs ～だろう (cont.)

The halfway house is `ことだろう`, which keeps the exclamation and adds a guess about someone else's inner state: <code>お<ruby>母<rt>かあ</rt></ruby>さんはどんなに<ruby>悲<rt>かな</rt></ruby>しんだことだろう</code> - "how his mother must have grieved." Note that a plain conjecture cannot take `ことか`: ❌<code><ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ることか</code> is not Japanese, because there is no emotion being measured.

---

## ～ことか · ～ことか vs ～ものか

> **Rule of thumb** - both are rhetorical questions, and they do opposite things. `ことか` says "**so** much!". `ものか` says "**not** a chance!" - it is a flat refusal or denial.

どんなに<ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>している**ことか**。<br>*I'm more grateful than I can say.*

あんな<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>二度<rt>にど</rt></ruby>と<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**ものか**。<br>*As if I'd ever go back to that place.*

`ものか` attaches to the plain non-past and needs no degree word; its casual spoken form is `もんか`, and it is distinctly rough. Mixing the two up reverses your meaning entirely, so check which of "how much!" and "no way!" you mean before you choose.

---

## ～ことか · ～ことか vs なんて

> **Rule of thumb** - same feeling, different register and different position. `なんて` fronts the exclamation and is ordinary spoken Japanese; `ことか` closes the sentence and is written.

**なんて**きれいな<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>なんだろう。<br>*What a beautiful sea.*

なんときれいな<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>である**ことか**。<br>*How beautiful this sea is.*

---

## ～ことか · ～ことか vs なんて (cont.)

The first is what you would actually say standing on the shore. The second belongs in a travel essay about standing on the shore. Note also that `なんて` has a second, unrelated use meaning "things like ～" (<code><ruby>運動<rt>うんどう</rt></ruby>なんて<ruby>嫌<rt>きら</rt></ruby>いだ</code>), which `ことか` never has.

---

## ～ことか · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>したことか。 → ✅ **どんなに**<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>したことか。<br>*Without a degree word there is nothing for the exclamation to measure, and the sentence reads as a broken question.*

- ❌ どんなに<ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しかった**です**ことか。 → ✅ どんなに<ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しかったことか。／✅ どんなに<ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しかったことでしょう。<br>*The clause before `ことか` stays plain. For a polite register, switch the ending to `ことでしょう`.*

- ❌ どんなに<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>**だ**ことか。 → ✅ どんなに<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>**な**ことか。<br>*A な-adjective keeps its `な` in front of `こと`, as it does before any noun.*

---

## ～ことか · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいことか。（meaning "next week will probably be busy"） → ✅ <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいだろう。<br>*`ことか` is not a conjecture ending. Use it only where a strong feeling is being exclaimed over.*

- ❌ 「どんなに<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>したことか。」「<ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>ぐらいです。」 → the question is rhetorical; it takes no answer.<br>*Treat it as a statement with an exclamation behind it, not as something to reply to.*

---

# ～ことに

**Meaning** · to my surprise / delight / regret - the speaker's reaction, stated before the news itself

`～ことに` puts your feeling **first** and the fact **second**. <code><ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>いたことに、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>っていた</code> - "to my astonishment, he had already gone home." The listener is told how to receive the news before they hear it, which is exactly what English does with "sadly," "remarkably," "to my relief."

---

## ～ことに · Formation

The slot in front of `ことに` takes a word of **evaluation or emotion**, in the form it would use to modify a noun. The clause after the comma states a fact.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| い-adjective | ～い + ことに | <ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しいことに |
| な-adjective | ～な + ことに | <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>なことに |
| Verb of reaction, past | Vた + ことに | <ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>いたことに |
| With a topic marker | ～ことに + は | <ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>った → <ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>ったことには |
| Usual い-adjectives | <ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しい／<ruby>悲<rt>かな</rt></ruby>しい／ありがたい／<ruby>面白<rt>おもしろ</rt></ruby>い／おかしい | ありがたいことに |
| Usual な-adjectives | <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>／<ruby>不思議<rt>ふしぎ</rt></ruby>／<ruby>幸<rt>さいわ</rt></ruby>い／<ruby>意外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby> | <ruby>幸<rt>さいわ</rt></ruby>いなことに |
| Usual verbs | <ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>いた／<ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>った／がっかりした | がっかりしたことに |

---

## ～ことに · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the second clause must be a **statement of fact**. A request, an invitation or a command cannot follow: ❌<code><ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しいことに、<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう</code>. The pattern comments on news; it does not introduce a proposal.

> **Note** - the reaction belongs to the **speaker** (or, in a story, to the narrator or the viewpoint character). You cannot use it to report someone else's feeling about their own news; for that, Japanese says <code><ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>いたようで、…</code>.

---

## ～ことに · Examples

<ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>いた**ことに**、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>っていた。<br>*To my surprise, he had already gone home.*

## ～ことに · Nuance & register

The pattern leans **written**. It is a staple of essays, reports, travel writing and news features, where it does the work of an English sentence adverb. In speech it is perfectly usable and does not sound stiff, but conversation more often reaches for the plain adverbs <code><ruby>幸<rt>さいわ</rt></ruby>い</code>, `あいにく`, <code><ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>ながら</code>, or simply for `びっくりした!` followed by the news.

Two combinations have drifted into everyday speech on their own: <code><ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>ったことに</code>, which introduces a problem you are still standing in, and `ありがたいことに`, which is a modest, slightly formal way of acknowledging help. Both are safe at work.

---

## ～ことに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Adding `は` - <code><ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>ったことには</code> - makes the phrase heavier and more literary. It is the same pattern with the contrastive `は` doing its usual job of setting this circumstance against the rest, and you will meet it far more often in print than in conversation.

Because the emotion is stated up front, the pattern has a mild **framing** effect: it tells the listener how to take what follows, which is why journalists like it and why it can sound editorial. If you want the facts to speak for themselves, leave it out.

---

## ～ことに · ～ことに vs ～ことにする / ～ことになる

> **Rule of thumb** - look at what `こと` is followed by. `ことに、` with a comma and a following clause is a comment. `ことにする` is a decision and `ことになる` is an arrangement - both of them **verbs**, both attached to a plain-form verb rather than to an emotion word.

<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>で<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>く**ことになりました**。<br>*It's been decided that I'll be working in Osaka from next month.*

<ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しい**ことに**、<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>で<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>けることになりました。<br>*Happily, it's been settled that I can work in Osaka from next month.*

---

## ～ことに · ～ことに vs ～ことにする / ～ことになる (cont.)

The second sentence contains both, which is the clearest way to see the difference: <code><ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しいことに</code> is the speaker's reaction, and `ことになりました` is the arrangement being reacted to. An emotion word can never take `ことにする`: ❌<code><ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しいことにしました</code> would have to mean "I decided on happiness."

---

## ～ことに · ～ことに vs 幸い・あいにく・残念ながら

> **Rule of thumb** - the adverbs are shorter, more spoken, and fixed in form. The `ことに` frame is open: any evaluation word can go into it, including ones that have no adverb of their own.

**あいにく**、<ruby>担当者<rt>たんとうしゃ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>外<rt>はず</rt></ruby>しております。<br>*Unfortunately the person in charge is away from their desk.*

**<ruby>情<rt>なさ</rt></ruby>けないことに**、<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>をまた<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れた。<br>*Pathetically, I've left my umbrella on the train again.*

---

## ～ことに · ～ことに vs 幸い・あいにく・残念ながら (cont.)

There is no single adverb for "pathetically" in this sense, and that is the frame's value: it manufactures one on demand. Where a ready-made adverb exists, prefer it in speech - <code>あいにく<ruby>品切<rt>しなぎ</rt></ruby>れです</code> is what a shop assistant says, not <code><ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>なことに<ruby>品切<rt>しなぎ</rt></ruby>れです</code>.

---

## ～ことに · ～ことに vs ～て

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>いて</code> makes the surprise a **cause** of what follows. <code><ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>いたことに</code> makes it a **comment** on what follows. The て-form joins two events; `ことに` joins a feeling to a fact.

<ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>い**て**、<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げてしまった。<br>*I was so startled that I cried out.*

<ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>いた**ことに**、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いたままだった。<br>*To my surprise, the window had been left open.*

In the first sentence the surprise causes the shout; in the second it causes nothing at all - it merely tells you how the open window struck the speaker. That is why the て-form sentence needs an action after it, while the `ことに` sentence needs a state of affairs.

## ～ことに · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>ことに、<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>られませんでした。 → ✅ <ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>**な**ことに、<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>られませんでした。<br>*A な-adjective keeps its `な` before `こと`. The same goes for <code><ruby>不思議<rt>ふしぎ</rt></ruby>な</code> and <code><ruby>幸<rt>さいわ</rt></ruby>いな</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>く**ことに**、<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>半分<rt>はんぶん</rt></ruby>になっていた。 → ✅ <ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>い**た**ことに、<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>半分<rt>はんぶん</rt></ruby>になっていた。<br>*With a verb of reaction the form is the past. The reaction has already happened by the time you report it.*

- ❌ <ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しいことに、<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか。 → ✅ <ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しいことに、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てくれるそうです。<br>*The clause after the comma has to be a fact. Invitations, requests and commands cannot follow.*

---

## ～ことに · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しいことに、<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合格<rt>ごうかく</rt></ruby>しました。 → ✅ <ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しいことに、<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合格<rt>ごうかく</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*The feeling is the speaker's, so the phrase should not be tucked in behind another person's topic where it looks like theirs.*

- ❌ <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む<ruby>嬉<rt>うれ</rt></ruby>しいことにしました。 → ✅ <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む**ことにしました**。<br>*`ことにする` is a separate pattern that attaches to a plain verb and means "decide to". Nothing emotional goes in front of it.*

## ～ことに · Practice 1

**1.** ____ことに、<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>交番<rt>こうばん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>けられていました。

{reveal}
**ありがたい**（<ruby>幸<rt>さいわ</rt></ruby>いな）ことに、<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>交番<rt>こうばん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>けられていました。

The news is good, so the slot takes a word of relief or gratitude. Any evaluation word fits the frame as long as it is in its noun-modifying form - `ありがたい` unchanged, <code><ruby>幸<rt>さいわ</rt></ruby>い</code> with `な`.

{reveal}

## ～ことに · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>っ**たことに**、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まっている。 (b) <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まっていて、**<ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>っている**。

{reveal}
(a) frames the news: before you hear about the trains, you are told that this is a problem. It reads as narration or as a report to a colleague, and it keeps a little distance from the speaker's own state.

{reveal}
(b) puts the same content the other way round - the stopped trains are the cause and the speaker's difficulty is the result. It is plainer, more conversational, and more directly about how the speaker is doing right now.
