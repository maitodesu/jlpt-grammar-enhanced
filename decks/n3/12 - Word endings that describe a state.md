# ～っぽい

**Meaning** · has the quality of X; comes across as X-ish

`～っぽい` says that something *gives off* the character of the word it attaches to, without necessarily being it. Attached to a verb stem it names a personal disposition - the kind of person who keeps doing that thing. Both uses lean mildly critical: `～っぽい` is what you reach for when a thing is X in a way you would rather it were not.

---

## ～っぽい · Formation

Whatever it attaches to, the result is an **い-adjective** and conjugates like one: <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>っぽい → <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>っぽくない → <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>っぽかった → <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>っぽくて. There is no `だ` after it.

---

## ～っぽい · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + っぽい | <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby> → <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>っぽい |
| Noun (person type) | Noun + っぽい | <ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby> → <ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>っぽい |
| い-adjective (stem, drop い) | Stem + っぽい | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>っぽい |
| Colour word (stem) | Stem + っぽい | <ruby>白<rt>しろ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>白<rt>しろ</rt></ruby>っぽい |
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + っぽい | <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れます → <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れっぽい |
| Result before a noun | っぽい + Noun | <ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>っぽい<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby> |
| Result as an adverb | っぽく + Verb | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>っぽく<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>える |
| **Casual speech only** - plain form + っぽい | Verb/Noun/Adj plain + っぽい | <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るっぽい（"looks like it'll rain"） |

---

## ～っぽい · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - this is **not** a suffix you can bolt onto any noun you like. The everyday stock is short and worth learning as vocabulary: <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>っぽい・<ruby>油<rt>あぶら</rt></ruby>っぽい・<ruby>湿<rt>しめ</rt></ruby>っぽい・ほこりっぽい・<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>っぽい・<ruby>荒<rt>あら</rt></ruby>っぽい・<ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>っぽい・<ruby>色<rt>いろ</rt></ruby>っぽい・<ruby>白<rt>しろ</rt></ruby>っぽい, and from verbs <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れっぽい・<ruby>飽<rt>あ</rt></ruby>きっぽい・<ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>りっぽい・<ruby>惚<rt>ほ</rt></ruby>れっぽい. The last row of the table is a separate, very productive **slang** pattern that young speakers use for "seems like"; keep it out of writing and out of exams.

---

## ～っぽい · Examples

このスープはちょっと**<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>っぽい**。<br>*This soup is a bit watery.*

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れっぽくて**、いつも<ruby>鍵<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>をさがしている。<br>*My little brother is forgetful and is always hunting for his keys.*

そんな**<ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>っぽい**ことを<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>うのはやめなさい。<br>*Stop saying such childish things.*

<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>年<rt>とし</rt></ruby>をとってから、<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>より**<ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>りっぽく**なった。<br>*My father has got quicker-tempered as he's aged.*

---

## ～っぽい · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>霧<rt>きり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>こうに**<ruby>白<rt>しろ</rt></ruby>っぽい**<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby>がぼんやり<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えた。<br>*A whitish building showed faintly through the fog.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>揚<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げ<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>油<rt>あぶら</rt></ruby>っぽくて**、<ruby>半分<rt>はんぶん</rt></ruby>しか<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べられなかった。<br>*Today's fried food was so greasy I could only get through half of it.*

<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>のわりに**<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>っぽく**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えるので、お<ruby>祝<rt>いわ</rt></ruby>いには<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>かないと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う。<br>*For the price it looks cheap and nasty, so I don't think it works as a celebration gift.*

<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>飽<rt>あ</rt></ruby>きっぽい**ので、<ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた<ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>い<ruby>事<rt>ごと</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>三<rt>みっ</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>目<rt>め</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*My sister loses interest quickly - the class she started this year is already her third.*

---

## ～っぽい · Nuance & register

The default flavour is **mildly negative**. <ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>っぽい is not "sweetly childlike", it is "immature"; <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>っぽい is not "affordable", it is "cheap-looking". If you want the approving version of "properly X", that is a different suffix (`らしい`), and mixing them up is how learners accidentally insult people.

Two families escape the negativity. **Colour** words - <ruby>白<rt>しろ</rt></ruby>っぽい, <ruby>黒<rt>くろ</rt></ruby>っぽい, <ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>っぽい - are purely descriptive "-ish", the polite way to hedge a colour you cannot name exactly. And <ruby>色<rt>いろ</rt></ruby>っぽい ("alluring") is a compliment, though a fairly forward one.

---

## ～っぽい · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral-to-casual. `～っぽい` in its ordinary sense appears in novels and newspapers without comment. The **slang** extension does not: attaching っぽい to a whole plain-form clause - <ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>っぽい, <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くっぽい, <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったっぽい - is conversational shorthand for `みたい` that spread through younger speech and still reads as loose. It is worth being able to understand; it is not worth writing in an email to a client.

Because the result is an い-adjective, everything an い-adjective does is available: <ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>っぽくないです, <ruby>油<rt>あぶら</rt></ruby>っぽかったら<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>してください, <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れっぽくなる. Learners who mentally file っぽい as "a noun ending" produce ❌<code><ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>っぽいだ</code>, which is the same error as ❌<code><ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いだ</code>.

---

## ～っぽい · ～っぽい vs ～らしい

> **Rule of thumb** - `らしい` says the thing has the qualities it *ought* to have and approves; `っぽい` says it has the surface flavour of something it is *not supposed* to be, and disapproves.

The two attach to the same nouns and mean nearly opposite things about the speaker's attitude:

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは**<ruby>男<rt>おとこ</rt></ruby>らしい**<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>だと、みんなが<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う。<br>*Everyone says Tanaka is a manly sort of person.*

<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>男<rt>おとこ</rt></ruby>っぽい**とよく<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>われるが、<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>にしていない。<br>*People often tell me I talk in a blokey way, but it doesn't bother me.*

---

## ～っぽい · ～っぽい vs ～らしい (cont.)

The clearest test is to apply both to the *same* subject. A child described as <ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>らしい is behaving exactly as a child should - lively, honest, unaffected. An adult described as <ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>っぽい is behaving as a child would, and should not be.

<ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby>なのに**<ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>っぽい**ところが、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>欠点<rt>けってん</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*His weakness is that he's childish for a grown man.*

Note that `らしい` has a second, unrelated job - hearsay, "apparently" - attached to a whole clause. `っぽい` never carries hearsay, only quality.

---

## ～っぽい · ～っぽい vs ～みたい / ～のよう

> **Rule of thumb** - `みたい` compares one thing to *another thing*; `っぽい` reports a quality the thing has *in itself*.

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**みたい**だ。<br>*He's like a teacher. (he resembles one - maybe he isn't one)*

<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>が<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>**っぽい**ね。<br>*You've got a rather teacherly way of talking.*

---

## ～っぽい · ～っぽい vs ～みたい / ～のよう (cont.)

`みたい` makes a comparison you could spell out ("like a teacher does"). `っぽい` picks out an attribute and hangs it on the subject directly, which is why it works on materials and textures where a comparison would be strange: <code><ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>っぽいスープ</code> is a soup that *is* thin, not a soup that resembles water. ❌<code><ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>みたいなスープ</code> would be an exaggeration about a completely different thing.

The one place they overlap is the slang use, where <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>っぽい and <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>みたい both mean "looks like rain" - and there `みたい` is the safe choice in any register above chatting with friends.

---

## ～っぽい · ～っぽい vs ～やすい

> **Rule of thumb** - verb-stem + `っぽい` describes a **person's standing disposition**; verb-stem + `やすい` describes how **easy the action is**, for anyone.

`やすい` was covered at N4 as "easy to do", and it does double duty as "prone to happen". That second sense is what collides with `っぽい`:

<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れっぽい**ので、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>メモを<ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>している。<br>*My mother is forgetful, so I hand her a shopping list.*

このパスワードは<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>すぎて**<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れやすい**。<br>*This password is so long it's easy to forget.*

---

## ～っぽい · ～っぽい vs ～やすい (cont.)

The first is a claim about my mother. The second is a claim about the password - anybody would forget it. Swap them and both sentences go wrong: a password does not have a personality, and ❌<code><ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>は<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れやすい</code> sounds like my mother is an object that is easily mislaid.

Only a handful of verbs take っぽい at all （<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れる・<ruby>飽<rt>あ</rt></ruby>きる・<ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>る・<ruby>惚<rt>ほ</rt></ruby>れる）, and every one of them names a temperament. `やすい` attaches to almost any verb.

---

## ～っぽい · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れる**っぽい**（meaning "forgetful"） → ✅ <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れ**っぽい**<br>*With verbs, `っぽい` takes the ます-stem. <code><ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れるっぽい</code> is not ungrammatical, but it is the slang "seems like they'll forget" - a different sentence.*

- ❌ この<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>っぽい**だ**。 → ✅ この<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>っぽい。<br>*The result is an い-adjective. No `だ`, and the polite form is <code><ruby>子供<rt>こども</rt></ruby>っぽいです</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>白<rt>しろ</rt></ruby>い**っぽい**<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>白<rt>しろ</rt></ruby>**っぽい**<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby><br>*With an い-adjective, the final `い` drops before っぽい - the same trim you make before `さ` or `すぎる`.*

---

## ～っぽい · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ お<ruby>父<rt>とう</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>男<rt>おとこ</rt></ruby>っぽくて<ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby>しています。 → ✅ お<ruby>父<rt>とう</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>男<rt>おとこ</rt></ruby>らしくて<ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby>しています。<br>*Praise wants `らしい`. `っぽい` would read as "he's a bit of a lad", which is not what you meant to say about someone's father.*

- ❌ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>はほこり**っぽいだらけ**だ。 → ✅ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>はほこり**っぽい**。<br>*Two different suffixes cannot stack. Pick one: っぽい for the stuffy feel of the air, だらけ for visible dust lying on everything.*

---

## ～っぽい · Practice 1

**1.** このみそ<ruby>汁<rt>しる</rt></ruby>、お<ruby>湯<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れすぎて<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>____なってしまった。

{reveal}
<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>**っぽく**なってしまった。

{reveal}
`っぽい` builds an い-adjective, so before `なる` it takes the adverbial form `っぽく` - exactly like <code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くなる</code>. The sentence complains that the soup has gone thin and watery.

---

## ～っぽい · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>は（<ruby>飽<rt>あ</rt></ruby>きっぽい・<ruby>飽<rt>あ</rt></ruby>きやすい）ので、どの<ruby>楽器<rt>がっき</rt></ruby>も<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>く<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>かない。

{reveal}
<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>飽<rt>あ</rt></ruby>きっぽい**ので、どの<ruby>楽器<rt>がっき</rt></ruby>も<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>く<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>かない。

{reveal}
The sentence is about my sister's temperament, and a standing personal disposition is `っぽい` territory. <code><ruby>飽<rt>あ</rt></ruby>きやすい</code> would describe something that *is easily got bored of* - a property of the instrument, not of the player.

---

## ～っぽい · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The room smelled dusty, so I opened the window."

{reveal}
<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>がほこり**っぽかった**ので、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けた。

{reveal}
`ほこりっぽい` is the standing expression for air that feels dusty and stale. The past tense is the ordinary い-adjective past, `っぽかった`. `ほこりだらけ` would be a different complaint - dust you can see lying on the furniture.

---

## ～っぽい · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>新入生<rt>しんにゅうせい</rt></ruby>のスピーチは<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>で、とても<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>っぽくてよかった。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>新入生<rt>しんにゅうせい</rt></ruby>のスピーチは<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>で、とても<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>らしくてよかった。

{reveal}
The speaker is praising the speech, and `っぽい` cannot carry praise - <code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>っぽい</code> would suggest the speech was amateurish. When the point is that something has the qualities it should have, use `らしい`.

---

## ～っぽい · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby>**っぽい**。 (b) <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>大人<rt>おとな</rt></ruby>**らしい**。

{reveal}
(a) says he gives off a grown-up air without actually being one - the natural reading is that he is young, and that his manner, clothes or voice are more adult than his age. It is often said of teenagers, and can be admiring or faintly uneasy.

{reveal}
(b) says he is an adult and behaves as an adult properly should: responsible, measured, the way the role is supposed to be filled. It is straightforwardly approving, and it presupposes that he really is one.

# ～ぎみ

**Meaning** · showing a slight touch of; a bit on the ～ side

`～ぎみ` (written <ruby>気味<rt>きみ</rt></ruby> when it stands alone, voiced to ぎみ as a suffix) reports that something is **a little way** toward a state, right now. Not fully there - just far enough that you can see it. It is the word for a cold you have not properly caught yet and a train that is running four minutes behind.

---

## ～ぎみ · Formation

The result is a **noun**. It predicates with だ/です, modifies a following noun with `の`, and takes `な` before `ので` and `のに` exactly as any noun does.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + ぎみ | <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>ぎみ |
| Noun (する-verb noun) | Noun + ぎみ | <ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby>ぎみ |
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + ぎみ | <ruby>太<rt>ふと</rt></ruby>ります → <ruby>太<rt>ふと</rt></ruby>りぎみ |
| Predicate | ～ぎみ + だ / です | <ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れぎみです |
| Before a noun | ～ぎみ + の + Noun | <ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れぎみの<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby> |
| Before ので・のに | ～ぎみ + な + ので | <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れぎみなので |
| As an adverb | ～ぎみ + に + Verb | <ruby>焦<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>りぎみに<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す |

---

## ～ぎみ · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the reading is ぎみ, with rendaku. The unvoiced <ruby>気味<rt>きみ</rt></ruby> is a separate word meaning "sensation", and it survives in <code><ruby>気味<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>い</code> - "creepy, unsettling" - which has nothing to do with this suffix. Say きみわるい for that and ぎみ for this.

---

## ～ぎみ · Examples

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>はちょっと<ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>**ぎみ**です。<br>*I've got a bit of a cold today.*

<ruby>最近<rt>さいきん</rt></ruby>、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>太<rt>ふと</rt></ruby>り**ぎみ**だ。<br>*I've put on a little weight lately.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>より<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れ**ぎみ**で<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>んでいる。<br>*The meeting is running slightly behind schedule.*

<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れ**ぎみ**の<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby>をしていたので、<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>るように<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>った。<br>*He looked a bit worn out, so I told him to go home early.*

---

## ～ぎみ · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby>**ぎみ**の<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>新人<rt>しんじん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>自己紹介<rt>じこしょうかい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた。<br>*In a slightly nervous voice, the new employee began her self-introduction.*

このところ<ruby>売<rt>う</rt></ruby>り<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げが<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がり**ぎみ**なので、<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>広告<rt>こうこく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>やします。<br>*Sales have been trending a little down lately, so we'll step up the advertising from next month.*

<ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>受験<rt>じゅけん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>づいてから<ruby>寝不足<rt>ねぶそく</rt></ruby>**ぎみ**で、<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>なかなか<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きられない。<br>*Since the entrance exams got close my son has been a bit short of sleep and struggles to get up.*

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>焦<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>り**ぎみ**に<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>していたが、<ruby>結局<rt>けっきょく</rt></ruby><ruby>結論<rt>けつろん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なかった。<br>*The manager spoke with a hint of impatience, but in the end nothing was decided.*

---

## ～ぎみ · Nuance & register

`～ぎみ` almost always points **away from normal in an unwelcome direction**. Health that is slipping, a schedule that is slipping, a number that is slipping. You will not hear ❌<code><ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>ぎみ</code> or ❌<code><ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>ぎみ</code>: there is no such thing as being slightly too well.

It is also about **degree, not frequency**. <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>ぎみ does not mean "I often catch colds"; it means "right now, a small amount of cold". That is why it pairs so naturally with <ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し and ちょっと, which reinforce rather than contradict it: <ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れぎみです.

---

## ～ぎみ · Nuance & register (cont.)

The register is neutral and it is entirely at home in business Japanese: <ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がりぎみ, <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れぎみ and <ruby>伸<rt>の</rt></ruby>びぎみ are ordinary vocabulary in a sales report. In writing it is normally spelled with the kanji <ruby>気味<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>; the reading of the suffix is still ぎみ.

One social use worth knowing: `～ぎみ` softens. Telling a colleague <ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れぎみですね is far gentler than <ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れていますね, because it presents the observation as slight and provisional rather than as a verdict on how they look. The same hedging instinct makes <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>ぎみなので<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みます a polite way to call in sick without claiming to be seriously ill.

---

## ～ぎみ · ～ぎみ vs ～がち

> **Rule of thumb** - `ぎみ` is **how much**, `がち` is **how often**. `ぎみ` says one state is slightly present now; `がち` says an event keeps recurring.

<ruby>今朝<rt>けさ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れ**ぎみ**だった。<br>*This morning's train was running a bit late.*

この<ruby>路線<rt>ろせん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れ**がち**だ。<br>*Trains on this line tend to run late.*

---

## ～ぎみ · ～ぎみ vs ～がち (cont.)

The first sentence is about one morning and a small number of minutes. The second is about a pattern across many mornings and says nothing about how late. Use the wrong one and you make a claim you did not intend: telling your boss <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れがちです about a single delayed delivery accuses the whole operation of being unreliable.

They also behave differently in a sentence. `ぎみ` builds a noun and takes `の` before a following noun; `がち` builds a な-adjective and takes `な`: <ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れぎみ**の**<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby> but <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>がち**な**<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども.

---

## ～ぎみ · ～ぎみ vs ～っぽい

> **Rule of thumb** - `ぎみ` says a *little of the real thing is present*; `っぽい` says it *feels like* the thing, whether or not it is.

The pair that shows this best is a cold, because both are said constantly:

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>から<ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>**ぎみ**で、<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでいる。<br>*I've had a touch of a cold since yesterday, so I'm taking medicine.*

<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>となく<ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>**っぽい**から、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>る。<br>*I feel like I might be coming down with something, so I'm going to bed early.*

---

## ～ぎみ · ～ぎみ vs ～っぽい (cont.)

`ぎみ` states a mild fact: there is a cold, in small quantity. `っぽい` states an impression: something about how I feel resembles a cold. Which is why <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>っぽい is what you say before you are sure, and <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>ぎみ once you are.

Grammatically they part company completely. `っぽい` produces an い-adjective - <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>っぽかった, <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>っぽくない - while `ぎみ` produces a noun: <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>ぎみだった, <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>ぎみではない.

---

## ～ぎみ · ～ぎみ vs ちょっと・すこし

> **Rule of thumb** - ちょっと turns *any* predicate down a notch. `ぎみ` only attaches to a fixed kind of word, and it carries a judgement that ちょっと does not.

For a plain matter of degree, an adverb is the natural choice, and `ぎみ` is simply not available:

このコーヒーは**ちょっと**<ruby>苦<rt>にが</rt></ruby>い。<br>*This coffee is a little bitter.*

❌<code><ruby>苦<rt>にが</rt></ruby>いぎみ</code> is impossible - `ぎみ` does not attach to い-adjectives at all. Nor does it attach to happy states: ❌`うれしいぎみ`, ❌<code><ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しみぎみ</code>.

Where both are possible, `ぎみ` adds the sense of a **deviation from where things should be**:

---

## ～ぎみ · ～ぎみ vs ちょっと・すこし (cont.)

<ruby>体重<rt>たいじゅう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>え**ぎみ**なので、<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>は<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べないようにしている。<br>*My weight has been creeping up, so I'm trying not to eat at night.*

<code><ruby>体重<rt>たいじゅう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えた</code> is a neutral measurement. <code><ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えぎみ</code> is a measurement plus a frown. And the two combine happily: <ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>えぎみ.

---

## ～ぎみ · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>太<rt>ふと</rt></ruby>る**ぎみ** → ✅ <ruby>太<rt>ふと</rt></ruby>り**ぎみ**<br>*With verbs, `ぎみ` attaches to the ます-stem, never the dictionary form.*

- ❌ <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>ぎみ**の**です。 → ✅ <ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>ぎみ**です**。<br>*`の` goes between ～ぎみ and a following **noun** （<code><ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>ぎみの<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby></code>）, not before です.*

- ❌ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>**ぎみ**です。 → ✅ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>です。<br>*`ぎみ` marks a slide toward something unwanted. There is no polite way to be a little bit too healthy.*

- ❌ <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>み**ぎみ**だ。（meaning "he's often absent"） → ✅ <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>み**がち**だ。<br>*Repeated absences are frequency, and frequency is `がち`.*

---

## ～ぎみ · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ かぜきみです。 → ✅ かぜぎみです。<br>*The suffix voices. きみ unvoiced belongs to <code><ruby>気味<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>い</code>, an unrelated expression.*

## ～ぎみ · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>あまり<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ていないので、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れ____です。

{reveal}
<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れ**ぎみ**です。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れる</code> takes its ます-stem <code><ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れ</code> before ぎみ, and the result is a noun, so です attaches directly. The sentence claims mild tiredness right now, not a habit of being tired.

---

## ～ぎみ · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: この<ruby>時計<rt>とけい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し（<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>みぎみ・<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>みがち）なので、<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じすぎないほうがいい。

{reveal}
この<ruby>時計<rt>とけい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し**<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>みぎみ**なので…

{reveal}
The clock is permanently a small amount fast - that is degree, so `ぎみ`. `がち` would need repeated separate events, which does not fit a clock that simply runs slightly ahead. Note the `な` before ので, which is the noun pattern.

## ～ぎみ · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The numbers have been a little low this month, so let's meet on Friday."

{reveal}
<ruby>今月<rt>こんげつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>数字<rt>すうじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がり**ぎみ**なので、<ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>に<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まりましょう。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がりぎみ</code> is standard business phrasing for a mild downward trend, and it is softer than <code><ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がっています</code> - which is exactly why people reach for it in meetings.

---

## ～ぎみ · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>最近<rt>さいきん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>曇<rt>くも</rt></ruby>りぎみで、<ruby>洗濯物<rt>せんたくもの</rt></ruby>が<ruby>乾<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>かない<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い。

{reveal}

{reveal}
The clue is <code><ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い</code> - the sentence is counting days, so it is about how often, not how much. Cloudiness recurring across many days is <code><ruby>曇<rt>くも</rt></ruby>りがち</code>, a fixed and very common combination.

## ～ぎみ · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby>**ぎみ**だった。 (b) <ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby>していた。

{reveal}
(a) reports a small, visible amount of nerves - her voice a little tight, her hands a little busy - and it hedges. The speaker is describing an impression from the outside and leaving room to be wrong.

{reveal}

# ～がち

**Meaning** · has a habit of ～ing; keeps ending up that way

`～がち` says that something happens **often, and shouldn't**. It is not a measurement of degree and not a statement about one occasion - it is a complaint about a pattern, whether the pattern belongs to a person, a machine or the weather.

---

## ～がち · Formation

The result is a **な-adjective**: `がちだ` as a predicate, `がちな` before a noun, `がちに` before a verb.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + がち | <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れます → <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れがち |
| Verb (ます-stem of a する-verb) | Noun + し + がち | <ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>します → <ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>しがち |
| Noun (short fixed list) | Noun + がち | <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby> → <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>がち |
| Predicate | ～がち + だ / です | <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れがちです |
| Before a noun | ～がち + な + Noun | <ruby>見落<rt>みお</rt></ruby>としがちな<ruby>点<rt>てん</rt></ruby> |
| Before a verb | ～がち + に + Verb | <ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>がちに<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す |
| Common set phrase | ～がち + になる | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みがちになる |

---

## ～がち · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - with verbs, `がち` is broadly productive. With **nouns** it is not: the usable list is essentially <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>がち, <ruby>曇<rt>くも</rt></ruby>りがち, <ruby>留守<rt>るす</rt></ruby>がち, <ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>がち, <ruby>伏<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>目<rt>め</rt></ruby>がち, and the lexicalised ありがち ("all too common"). Inventing new noun combinations - ❌ <code><ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>がち</code>, ❌<code><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>がち</code> - does not work.

---

## ～がち · Examples

<ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>運動不足<rt>うんどうぶそく</rt></ruby>に**なりがち**だ。<br>*In winter you end up short of exercise.*

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さいころ**<ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>がち**でした。<br>*My little brother was ill a lot when he was small.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>曇<rt>くも</rt></ruby>りがち**で、<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ったシーツがなかなか<ruby>乾<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>かない。<br>*It's cloudy on and off today, and the sheets I washed just won't dry.*

<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいと、<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを**<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>きがち**になる。<br>*When you're busy you get into the habit of skipping breakfast.*

---

## ～がち · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>がちに**<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>挙<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げて、<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>した。<br>*He raised his hand hesitantly and asked his question in a small voice.*

メールの<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>後回<rt>あとまわ</rt></ruby>しにすると、そのまま**<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れがち**だ。<br>*If you put off replying to an email, you tend to forget about it altogether.*

<ruby>説明書<rt>せつめいしょ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>細<rt>こま</rt></ruby>かい<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>見落<rt>みお</rt></ruby>としがちな**ところなので、<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>して<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでください。<br>*The small print in the manual is the sort of thing people miss, so read it aloud.*

<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby><ruby>暮<rt>ぐ</rt></ruby>らしを<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めてから、<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>足<rt>た</rt></ruby>りなく**なりがち**で、<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>反省<rt>はんせい</rt></ruby>している。<br>*Since I started living alone I keep ending up short on vegetables, and I'm not proud of it.*

---

## ～がち · Nuance & register

The judgement built into `がち` is the thing to remember. Every ordinary use points at something the speaker would rather did not keep happening: illness, absence, oversight, delay, cloud. Turn it on a good outcome and the sentence collapses - ❌<code><ruby>成功<rt>せいこう</rt></ruby>しがち</code>, ❌<code><ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>になりがち</code> - not because the grammar breaks but because the word carries a frown that has nowhere to land.

The frown can be aimed at yourself, and often is: <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>も <ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>しがちなので<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>をつけます is a standard, slightly rueful thing to say. It can also be aimed at people in general, which is where the pattern earns its keep in writing - ～と<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えがちだが, "we tend to think that, but", is a staple opening move in essays and newspaper columns.

---

## ～がち · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral. `がち` appears in conversation, in business email and in print without shifting tone. Two combinations have drifted into fixed vocabulary and no longer feel like grammar at all: <ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>がち ("diffident, hanging back") and ありがち ("the sort of thing that happens all the time" - usually said of a predictable plot or a familiar mistake).

Finally, `がち` needs **repetition**. It cannot describe one event. Reporting a single late train as <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れがちだった is wrong in the same way that saying "the train tends to be late" about one specific morning is wrong in English.

---

## ～がち · ～がち vs ～やすい

> **Rule of thumb** - `やすい` is about **how easily** something can happen, and can be good or bad. `がち` is about **how often** it does happen, and is always bad.

N4 taught `やすい` as "easy to do". Its second sense, "liable to", is what overlaps here - and the two still split cleanly:

この<ruby>皿<rt>さら</rt></ruby>は<ruby>薄<rt>うす</rt></ruby>いので**<ruby>割<rt>わ</rt></ruby>れやすい**。<br>*These plates are thin, so they break easily.*

<ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>にまかせると、<ruby>皿<rt>さら</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>割<rt>わ</rt></ruby>りがち**だ。<br>*If I leave it to my son, he's forever breaking plates.*

---

## ～がち · ～がち vs ～やすい (cont.)

The first is a property of the plates that would hold even if nobody ever broke one. The second counts actual breakages and blames somebody for them. This is also why `やすい` is happy in praise - <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>いやすい, <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりやすい - and `がち` never is.

They also build different words: `やすい` makes an い-adjective （<ruby>割<rt>わ</rt></ruby>れやすかった, <ruby>割<rt>わ</rt></ruby>れやすくない）, `がち` a な-adjective （<ruby>割<rt>わ</rt></ruby>りがちだった, <ruby>割<rt>わ</rt></ruby>りがちな<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>）.

---

## ～がち · ～がち vs ～ぎみ

> **Rule of thumb** - `がち` counts occasions; `ぎみ` measures amount. If you could answer with "three times a week", it is `がち`. If you could answer with "just a little", it is `ぎみ`.

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>から<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みがち**だ。<br>*He's been off work a lot since last year.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し**<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れぎみ**なので、<ruby>残業<rt>ざんぎょう</rt></ruby>はやめておく。<br>*I'm a bit worn out today, so I'll skip the overtime.*

---

## ～がち · ～がち vs ～ぎみ (cont.)

Some verbs take both, and then the difference is unmistakable. <ruby>太<rt>ふと</rt></ruby>りぎみ means I am currently a couple of kilos heavier than I should be; <ruby>太<rt>ふと</rt></ruby>りがち means I am the kind of person who puts weight on again and again. Only one of those is a comment about a body shape, and only one is a comment about a life.

---

## ～がち · ～がち vs ことが多い

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>ことが<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い</code> reports frequency as a neutral fact. `がち` reports the same frequency and disapproves of it.

<ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>る**ことが<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い**。<br>*It often snows in the mountains in winter.*

That sentence is a piece of information. Nothing is wrong with the snow. Swap in `がち` and you have implied that the snow is a nuisance - fine if you are a driver, odd in a geography textbook.

---

## ～がち · ～がち vs ことが多い (cont.)

The neutral phrase is therefore the safe default for objective writing, and it takes a whole clause rather than a stem, so it works with verbs `がち` cannot reach: <code><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ることが<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い</code> is fine, while ❌<code><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>がちだ</code> is not Japanese.

---

## ～がち · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れる**がち** → ✅ <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れ**がち**<br>*`がち` takes the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.*

- ❌ <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>がち**<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども** → ✅ <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>がち**な**<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども<br>*The result is a な-adjective, so it needs `な` before a noun.*

- ❌ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れ**がち**だった。（about one morning） → ✅ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れていた。<br>*`がち` requires a repeated pattern. One late train is just a late train.*

- ❌ <ruby>最近<rt>さいきん</rt></ruby>よく<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れて、<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>になり**がち**です。 → ✅ <ruby>最近<rt>さいきん</rt></ruby>よく<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れて、<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>になってきました。<br>*`がち` only fits tendencies the speaker regrets. Nobody regrets getting healthier.*

---

## ～がち · Common mistakes (cont.)

# ～だらけ - covered all over

**Meaning** · with X all over it; caked in X

`～だらけ` attaches to a noun and says that the stuff named is **all over the surface** of something, in an amount nobody wanted. Mud on a coat, dust on a shelf, scratches on a table. The word is a complaint before it is a description.

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · Formation

`だらけ` attaches to a **bare noun** - no particle in front of it - and the result is itself a **noun**. It predicates with だ/です, modifies a following noun with `の`, and pairs constantly with なる.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + だらけ | <ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>だらけ |
| Predicate | ～だらけ + だ / です | <ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>がほこりだらけだ |
| Before a noun | ～だらけ + の + Noun | <ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>だらけの<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby> |
| Becoming that way | ～だらけ + に + なる | <ruby>砂<rt>すな</rt></ruby>だらけになる |
| Negation | ～だらけ + では ない | <ruby>傷<rt>きず</rt></ruby>だらけではない |

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - `だらけ` never attaches to a verb or an adjective, and never takes `な` before a noun. It is also **not** freely productive in the way it looks: the noun has to name something you would rather were not there. <ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>・<ruby>砂<rt>すな</rt></ruby>・<ruby>血<rt>ち</rt></ruby>・ほこり・<ruby>傷<rt>きず</rt></ruby>・<ruby>毛<rt>け</rt></ruby>・しわ are all natural; ❌<code><ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>だらけ</code> and ❌<code><ruby>光<rt>ひかり</rt></ruby>だらけ</code> are not, unless you are being pointedly sarcastic.

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · Examples

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>だらけ**になった。<br>*The kids' clothes ended up covered in mud.*

<ruby>棚<rt>たな</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>が**ほこりだらけ**だ。<br>*The top of the shelf is thick with dust.*

<ruby>転<rt>ころ</rt></ruby>んで、<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>血<rt>ち</rt></ruby>だらけ**になってしまった。<br>*I fell over and my hands ended up covered in blood.*

<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がったら、<ruby>体中<rt>からだじゅう</rt></ruby>**<ruby>砂<rt>すな</rt></ruby>だらけ**だった。<br>*When I came out of the sea I was covered in sand from head to foot.*

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>猫<rt>ねこ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>抱<rt>だ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げたら、コートが**<ruby>毛<rt>け</rt></ruby>だらけ**になった。<br>*I picked the cat up and my coat ended up covered in fur.*

<ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったばかりの<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>わくまで<ruby>白<rt>しろ</rt></ruby>い**<ruby>粉<rt>こな</rt></ruby>だらけ**だった。<br>*The room where the work had just finished was covered in white dust, right up to the window frames.*

<ruby>祖父<rt>そふ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>傷<rt>きず</rt></ruby>だらけ**で、<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>く<ruby>畑<rt>はたけ</rt></ruby><ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>をしてきたことがすぐに<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かった。<br>*My grandfather's hands were covered in scars - you could tell at once he'd worked the fields for years.*

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>を<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ってきたらしく、<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>鞄<rt>かばん</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>だらけ**のまま<ruby>玄関<rt>げんかん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>っていた。<br>*He must have run through the rain - my brother was standing in the entryway with his shoes and bag still caked in mud.*

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · Nuance & register

`だらけ` is **evaluative**. Even when the fact reported is neutral, the word supplies the reaction: dismay, disgust, or a weary "look at the state of this". A parent says <ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>だらけ about a child's trousers with the sigh already in it.

It is also **hyperbolic**. <ruby>傷<rt>きず</rt></ruby>だらけ does not mean every square centimetre is scarred; it means enough of them that the scars are what you notice. Nobody counts.

Register is ordinary spoken and written Japanese. It is not slangy, but it is blunt, so in a formal report or a polite complaint to a company you would soften it - <ruby>汚<rt>よご</rt></ruby>れがひどい<ruby>状態<rt>じょうたい</rt></ruby>でした rather than <ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>だらけでした. Between friends and inside a family it is completely standard.

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · Nuance & register (cont.)

Note the shape of the sentence: what is covered is the **subject**, and what covers it goes in front of だらけ with nothing between. <ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>**が**ほこりだらけだ - the desk is the topic of the complaint, the dust is the incriminating substance. Learners often reverse this and produce ❌<code>ほこりが<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>だらけだ</code>, which says the dust is covered in desks.

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · ～だらけ vs ～まみれ

> **Rule of thumb** - `まみれ` is for a **liquid or a fine powder smeared over a surface**. `だらけ` is for **many separate things scattered all over**, and it also reaches abstract nouns, which `まみれ` cannot.

Where the substance is genuinely a coating, both work, and `まみれ` is the more vivid, slightly more literary of the two:

<ruby>工場<rt>こうじょう</rt></ruby>から<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>てきた<ruby>作業員<rt>さぎょういん</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby>まで**<ruby>油<rt>あぶら</rt></ruby>まみれ**だった。<br>*The worker who came out of the factory was covered in oil right up to his face.*

<ruby>台所<rt>だいどころ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>床<rt>ゆか</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>油<rt>あぶら</rt></ruby>だらけ**で、<ruby>滑<rt>すべ</rt></ruby>りそうだった。<br>*The kitchen floor was greasy all over and felt like it would be slippery.*

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · ～だらけ vs ～まみれ (cont.)

The line shows itself at the edges. Sweat forms a film, so <ruby>汗<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>まみれ is normal and ❌<code><ruby>汗<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>だらけ</code> is odd. Scars are countable marks, so <ruby>傷<rt>きず</rt></ruby>だらけ is normal and ❌<code><ruby>傷<rt>きず</rt></ruby>まみれ</code> is odd. Dust, mud and blood sit in the middle and take either - with <ruby>血<rt>ち</rt></ruby>まみれ sounding like a crime scene and <ruby>血<rt>ち</rt></ruby>だらけ sounding like a kitchen accident.

`まみれ` extends metaphorically to only a very small set of fixed expressions （<ruby>借金<rt>しゃっきん</rt></ruby>まみれ, <ruby>罪<rt>つみ</rt></ruby>にまみれる）, always with the image of being smeared. It does not take scattered abstract defects: ❌<code><ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>いまみれ</code>, ❌<code><ruby>穴<rt>あな</rt></ruby>まみれ</code>.

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · ～だらけ vs ～っぽい

> **Rule of thumb** - `だらけ` says the stuff is **physically there, all over it**. `っぽい` says the thing merely **has the quality** of the stuff.

The clearest pair is dust, because both are everyday complaints about a room:

<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>っていない<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は**ほこりだらけ**で、まず<ruby>掃除<rt>そうじ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた。<br>*The unused room was thick with dust, so we started with the cleaning.*

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めきっていたので、<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が**ほこりっぽくて**<ruby>息苦<rt>いきぐる</rt></ruby>しい。<br>*We'd kept the windows shut, so the room feels dusty and stuffy.*

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · ～だらけ vs ～っぽい (cont.)

The first is about what is lying on the surfaces; you could photograph it. The second is about how the air feels; you could not. Grammatically they are unrelated too - `だらけ` builds a noun, `っぽい` an い-adjective - so the endings never look alike: ほこりだらけだった against ほこりっぽかった.

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · ～だらけ vs ～がついている

> **Rule of thumb** - `～がついている` reports that some of the stuff is on there. `だらけ` reports that it is *everywhere* on there. The difference is amount, and with it, tone.

シャツに<ruby>醤油<rt>しょうゆ</rt></ruby>が**ついている**よ。<br>*You've got soy sauce on your shirt.*

<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>のあと、ユニフォームは**<ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>だらけ**だった。<br>*After the match the uniform was caked in mud.*

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · ～だらけ vs ～がついている (cont.)

The first is a helpful heads-up about one spot. The second is a verdict on the whole garment. This is why `ついている` is the polite way to tell a stranger about a stain and `だらけ` is not: pointing at someone's jacket and saying <ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>だらけですよ makes it sound as though they had rolled in a field.

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>**が**だらけ → ✅ <ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>だらけ<br>*Nothing goes between the noun and `だらけ` - no `が`, no `で`, no `の`.*

- ❌ <ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>だらけ**な**<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>泥<rt>どろ</rt></ruby>だらけ**の**<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby><br>*The result is a noun, not a な-adjective, so it links with `の`.*

- ❌ <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>汚<rt>よご</rt></ruby>れている**だらけ**だ。 → ✅ <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>がごみだらけだ。<br>*`だらけ` attaches only to nouns. A verb or an adjective in front of it is not possible.*

- ❌ <ruby>汗<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>**だらけ**になって<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っていた → ✅ <ruby>汗<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>**まみれ**になって<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っていた<br>*Sweat is a film over the skin, not a scatter of separate marks, so it takes `まみれ`. <code><ruby>汗<rt>あせ</rt></ruby>びっしょり</code> is the everyday alternative.*

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>庭<rt>にわ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>**だらけ**できれいだ。 → ✅ <ruby>庭<rt>にわ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>でいっぱいできれいだ。<br>*`だらけ` cannot be used approvingly. Saying it about flowers turns a pretty garden into an infestation.*

---

## ～だらけ - covered all over · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>転<rt>ころ</rt></ruby>んで、ズボンが<ruby>砂<rt>すな</rt></ruby>____になってしまった。

# ～だらけ - full of

**Meaning** · riddled with X; nothing but X wherever you look

This is the same suffix used of things you can **count** rather than a substance smeared over a surface: mistakes in an essay, holes in a jumper, tourists in a street, debts on a balance sheet. The tone is unchanged - `だらけ` never reports abundance neutrally. It reports abundance and objects to it.

---

## ～だらけ - full of · Formation

Identical to the physical sense: a **bare noun** in front, and a **noun** as the result.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Countable noun | Noun + だらけ | <ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>いだらけ |
| Abstract noun | Noun + だらけ | <ruby>借金<rt>しゃっきん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>借金<rt>しゃっきん</rt></ruby>だらけ |
| Verb-derived noun (ます-stem) | ます-stem + だらけ | <ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>えます → <ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>いだらけ |
| Predicate | ～だらけ + だ / です | この<ruby>計画<rt>けいかく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>だらけだ |
| Before a noun | ～だらけ + の + Noun | <ruby>穴<rt>あな</rt></ruby>だらけの<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby> |
| Becoming that way | ～だらけ + に + なる | <ruby>雑草<rt>ざっそう</rt></ruby>だらけになる |

---

## ～だらけ - full of · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the noun still has to name something unwelcome. That includes people when you resent them being there - <ruby>観光客<rt>かんこうきゃく</rt></ruby>だらけ, <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもだらけ - but not things you are glad of. ❌<code>いい<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えだらけ</code> is not a compliment; it is not anything.

---

## ～だらけ - full of · Examples

この<ruby>作文<rt>さくぶん</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>いだらけ**だ。<br>*This essay is full of mistakes.*

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が**ごみだらけ**で、<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちを<ruby>呼<rt>よ</rt></ruby>べない。<br>*My room is full of rubbish, so I can't have friends over.*

<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>観光客<rt>かんこうきゃく</rt></ruby>だらけ**だった。<br>*Kyoto in summer was heaving with tourists.*

<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>借金<rt>しゃっきん</rt></ruby>だらけ**で、<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>できずにいるらしい。<br>*My brother is up to his neck in debt and apparently can't even bring himself to talk to the family.*

---

## ～だらけ - full of · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>いセーターが**<ruby>穴<rt>あな</rt></ruby>だらけ**になっていたので、とうとう<ruby>捨<rt>す</rt></ruby>てた。<br>*The old sweater had got so full of holes that I finally threw it out.*

<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりやすかったが、<ruby>計画<rt>けいかく</rt></ruby><ruby>自体<rt>じたい</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>だらけ**だと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う。<br>*The explanation was clear, but the plan itself strikes me as riddled with problems.*

<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりに<ruby>山小屋<rt>やまごや</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったら、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>の<ruby>内側<rt>うちがわ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>虫<rt>むし</rt></ruby>だらけ**で、<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>る<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>になれなかった。<br>*When we got to the mountain hut at the end of summer the inside of the windows was crawling with insects, and I couldn't face going in.*

<ruby>一年<rt>いちねん</rt></ruby><ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れていない<ruby>庭<rt>にわ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>雑草<rt>ざっそう</rt></ruby>だらけ**で、どこから<ruby>片<rt>かた</rt></ruby>づければいいのか<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からない。<br>*The garden hasn't been touched for a year and is choked with weeds; I've no idea where to start.*

---

## ～だらけ - full of · Nuance & register

This sense of `だらけ` is a **verdict**. Calling a document <ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>いだらけ is not the same as saying it contains errors; it is saying the document is not worth reading in its present state. Aimed at a colleague's work it is genuinely rude, and Japanese office language has softer replacements - <ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>いが<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>いようです, <ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>すところがあります. Aimed at your own work it is a normal, slightly theatrical form of apology.

---

## ～だらけ - full of · Nuance & register (cont.)

Like the physical sense it is **hyperbolic**: <ruby>穴<rt>あな</rt></ruby>だらけ needs three or four holes, not fifty. And it is **saturating**, not gradable, so it does not take degree adverbs. ❌<code>とても<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>いだらけ</code> is impossible for the same reason "very full of holes" is odd in English - the phrase already means "as many as matters".

One extension worth knowing because it looks like an exception: `まみれ`, which otherwise refuses abstract nouns, does take <ruby>借金<rt>しゃっきん</rt></ruby> - <ruby>借金<rt>しゃっきん</rt></ruby>まみれ is as common as <ruby>借金<rt>しゃっきん</rt></ruby>だらけ. The image there is of debt smeared over a person like grease, which is why that one combination works and ❌<code><ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>いまみれ</code> does not.

---

## ～だらけ - full of · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register: everyday spoken and written Japanese, blunt but not vulgar. Headlines and opinion columns use it freely; contracts and formal reports do not.

---

## ～だらけ - full of · ～だらけ vs ～ばかり

> **Rule of thumb** - `ばかり` says **nothing but X, to the exclusion of other things**. `だらけ` says **X everywhere you look**, and adds a complaint.

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>肉<rt>にく</rt></ruby>**ばかり**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べて、<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>をつけない。<br>*My brother eats nothing but meat and won't touch the vegetables.*

<ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>が<ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>い<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>**だらけ**で、<ruby>捨<rt>す</rt></ruby>てるところから<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた。<br>*The fridge was full of old food, so I started by throwing things out.*

---

## ～だらけ - full of · ～だらけ vs ～ばかり (cont.)

`ばかり` sets up a contrast with what is *absent*, and it is neutral about whether that is good. You can say <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>ばかり<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいる about a studious child admiringly. `だらけ` has no such flexibility, and it never comments on absence - only on how much of the offending thing is present.

They also fit differently into a sentence. `ばかり` follows verbs, particles and quantities as well as nouns（<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てばかりいる, <ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>にばかり）; `だらけ` follows a bare noun and nothing else.

---

## ～だらけ - full of · ～だらけ vs ～でいっぱい

> **Rule of thumb** - `でいっぱい` reports a container being **full**, with no judgement, and works for good things. `だらけ` reports a **mess**.

<ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>開演<rt>かいえん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>**でいっぱい**だった。<br>*The hall was packed with people well before the performance began.*

<ruby>駅前<rt>えきまえ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>**だらけ**で、<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>めなかった。<br>*The area in front of the station was jammed with people and I couldn't get through.*

Both describe crowds; only the second is annoyed about it. That is why `でいっぱい` is the one that can carry positive nouns - <ruby>幸<rt>しあわ</rt></ruby>せでいっぱい, <ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>でいっぱいの<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby> - while `だらけ` cannot go near them.

Note the particle: `でいっぱい` takes `で` before it, `だらけ` takes nothing.

---

## ～だらけ - full of · ～だらけ vs ～が多い

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い</code> is a gradable, neutral measurement. `だらけ` is an ungradable verdict. Anywhere you need to be polite or precise, use <code><ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い</code>.

この<ruby>報告書<rt>ほうこくしょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>数字<rt>すうじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>いが**<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い**ようです。<br>*There seem to be rather a lot of numerical errors in this report.*

<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>返<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>したら、<ruby>一<rt>いち</rt></ruby><ruby>枚目<rt>まいめ</rt></ruby>から**<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>いだらけ**だった。<br>*When I read it back myself, it was riddled with mistakes from page one.*

---

## ～だらけ - full of · ～だらけ vs ～が多い (cont.)

The first sentence can be handed to the person who wrote the report. The second cannot - but it is exactly right for talking about your own draft. The grammatical consequence of the difference is that <code><ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い</code> takes degree words （<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い, とても<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>い）and `だらけ` refuses them.

---

## ～だらけ - full of · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>い**が**だらけの<ruby>作文<rt>さくぶん</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>いだらけの<ruby>作文<rt>さくぶん</rt></ruby><br>*No particle before `だらけ`, and `の` after it when a noun follows.*

- ❌ この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**だらけ**だ。 → ✅ この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>はとても<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ。<br>*`だらけ` needs a countable or concrete noun. Adjectives and states cannot go in front of it.*

- ❌ <ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>が**とても**<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>だらけだ。 → ✅ <ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>だらけだ。<br>*The suffix already means "saturated", so it takes no degree adverb.*

- ❌ <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>だらけで、<ruby>気持<rt>きも</rt></ruby>ちがいい。 → ✅ <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>でいっぱいで、<ruby>気持<rt>きも</rt></ruby>ちがいい。<br>*Praise wants `でいっぱい`. `だらけ` would make the flowers sound like a problem the council should deal with.*

---

## ～だらけ - full of · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>のレポートは<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>いだらけですね。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>のレポート、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>すところがあるようです。<br>*Grammatically fine, socially not. Save `だらけ` for your own work, or for things rather than people.*

---

## ～だらけ - full of · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby><ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>っていない<ruby>物置<rt>ものおき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けたら、<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>はくもの<ruby>巣<rt>す</rt></ruby>____だった。

{reveal}
<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>はくもの<ruby>巣<rt>す</rt></ruby>**だらけ**だった。

{reveal}
Cobwebs are countable things scattered everywhere, and they are unwelcome - both conditions `だらけ` needs. It attaches straight to the noun and takes だった for the past, because the whole phrase is a noun.

---

## ～だらけ - full of · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: この<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>は（<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>だらけ・<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>でいっぱい）で、このままでは<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>せない。

{reveal}
`でいっぱい` describes a container being full and is neutral, so it fits badly with an abstract defect like <code><ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby></code>. `だらけ` supplies exactly the disapproval the second half of the sentence assumes.

{reveal}

{reveal}
<code><ruby>穴<rt>あな</rt></ruby>だらけ</code> works for a physical sweater and, as here, for gaps in an argument - one of the few abstract nouns that keeps the concrete image. Since the speaker is criticising their own work, the bluntness is fine.

## ～だらけ - full of · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもだらけがいて、とてもにぎやかだった。

{reveal}
`だらけ` already contains "there are lots of them", so it cannot be followed by `がいる`. It behaves as a noun, so it joins the next clause with `で`. Note that with にぎやか the sentence reads as good-humoured exaggeration rather than a real complaint - that is about as positive as `だらけ` ever gets.

# ～まま

**Meaning** · in the same state, unchanged; while still ～

`まま` freezes a state. Something reached a condition, nobody changed it, and whatever happens next happens **with that condition still in place**. Shoes still on, light still burning, town still exactly as it was ten years ago.

---

## ～まま · Formation

`まま` is a formal noun. What comes in front of it must be in a form that can modify a noun, which is why verbs use the **た-form** and not the dictionary form.

---

## ～まま · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (past) | た-form + まま | <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>つ → <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ったまま |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + まま | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べない → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べないまま |
| Noun | Noun + の + まま | <ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby> → <ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>のまま |
| い-adjective | Plain form + まま | <ruby>熱<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>熱<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いまま |
| な-adjective | Stem + な + まま | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなまま |
| Demonstrative | この / その / あの + まま | そのまま |
| As a predicate | ～まま + だ / です | <ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>のままです |
| Deliberately keeping it so | ～まま + に + する / しておく | そのままにしておく |

---

## ～まま · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the た-form here is **not** past tense. <code><ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ったまま</code> does not mean "having stood in the past"; it means the standing-up has happened and the resulting posture is still current. This is why only verbs that leave a visible result work well: <ruby>着<rt>き</rt></ruby>たまま, つけたまま, <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けたまま. Verbs that leave nothing behind - ❌<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたまま<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す</code> for "talk while eating" - produce nonsense.

---

## ～まま · Examples

<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>をはいた**まま**<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がらないでください。<br>*Please don't come up with your shoes on.*

<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>をつけた**まま**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てしまった。<br>*I fell asleep with the light on.*

この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>の**まま**だ。<br>*This town is exactly as it was ten years ago.*

<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わない**まま**、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>て<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った。<br>*He left the room without ever saying anything.*

---

## ～まま · Examples (cont.)

スープは<ruby>熱<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>い**まま**<ruby>運<rt>はこ</rt></ruby>んでください。<br>*Please carry the soup while it's still hot.*

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けた**まま**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけてしまい、<ruby>一日中<rt>いちにちじゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>になっていた。<br>*I went out having left the window open, and it bothered me all day.*

<ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>りた<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>まない**まま**<ruby>返<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>すことになりそうだ。<br>*It looks as though I'll end up returning the book I borrowed without having read it.*

<ruby>祖母<rt>そぼ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>亡<rt>な</rt></ruby>くなったときの**まま**にしてあり、<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>のめがねも<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>かしていない。<br>*My grandmother's room has been left just as it was when she died - even the glasses on her desk haven't been moved.*

---

## ～まま · Nuance & register

`まま` itself is completely neutral and register-free. It appears in children's speech, in polite requests, in legal notices（<code>そのままお<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちください</code>） and in literary description without changing colour.

What supplies the colour is the **combination**. When the frozen state is one that ought to have been undone, the sentence sounds careless: <ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>をはいたまま, <ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>をつけたまま, <ruby>化粧<rt>けしょう</rt></ruby>をしたまま<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>る. When the frozen state is one being deliberately preserved, it sounds respectful or careful: <ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>のまま, <ruby>熱<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いまま. The suffix is only holding the state still; the judgement comes from what the state is.

---

## ～まま · Nuance & register (cont.)

Three connected forms are worth separating. Bare `まま` and `ままで` are largely interchangeable in the adverbial slot - <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ったまま<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる and <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ったままで<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる are both ordinary, with `で` a shade more explicit. `ままに` is different: it is literary, and it also heads a small family of fixed expressions about following someone else's lead - <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>われるままに<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>にサインした, "I signed the papers just as I was told to."

Finally, そのまま is so common that it has become a word in its own right: "as is, leave it, don't touch it". `そのままでいいです` ("it's fine like that") is one of the most useful short sentences in the language.

---

## ～まま · ～まま vs ～ながら

> **Rule of thumb** - `ながら` joins **two actions** happening at once. `まま` holds **one state** in place while an action happens. If both halves are things you *do*, you want `ながら`.

<ruby>音楽<rt>おんがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>き**ながら**<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>をしている。<br>*He's doing his homework while listening to music.*

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいて、<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>った**まま**<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んだ。<br>*The train was crowded, so I read standing up.*

---

## ～まま · ～まま vs ～ながら (cont.)

Listening and doing homework are two ongoing activities, so `ながら`. Standing is not an activity you keep performing - you stand up once and then you are standing - so it takes `まま`. That is why ❌<code><ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ちながら<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む</code> sounds wrong to a native ear, and why ❌<code><ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いたまま<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>をする</code> does too.

A second, purely grammatical difference: `ながら` needs the **same subject** for both halves, while `まま` does not care - <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けたまま<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した is fine.

---

## ～まま · ～まま vs ～っぱなし

> **Rule of thumb** - `っぱなし` is `まま` **with blame attached**, and it only attaches to verb stems. `まま` is neutral and attaches to nouns and adjectives too.

テレビをつけた**まま**、<ruby>台所<rt>だいどころ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>をしていた。<br>*I was doing the washing-up in the kitchen with the TV left on.*

テレビを**つけっぱなし**にして<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけるのはやめてほしい。<br>*I wish you'd stop going out and leaving the TV on.*

The first simply reports the arrangement; the TV may well have been on for a reason. The second is a complaint about carelessness, and `っぱなし` is what carries it.

---

## ～まま · ～まま vs ～っぱなし (cont.)

There is one thing `っぱなし` can do that `まま` cannot: describe an action going on **without a break**. <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ちっぱなしだった means "I was on my feet the whole time", which is about duration, not about a posture held during some other action. <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ったまま cannot mean that.

---

## ～まま · ～ないまま vs ～ないで

> **Rule of thumb** - `ないで` says you did B **instead of** A. `ないまま` says the un-done state of A **persisted** - usually longer than it should have.

<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**ないで**<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>た。<br>*I left the house without eating breakfast.*

<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**ないまま**、<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>になってしまった。<br>*I got all the way to evening without having eaten anything since morning.*

The first is about one moment: at the point of leaving, breakfast had not happened. The second stretches the emptiness across a whole day and invites you to notice how long it went on. Where a stretch of time is involved - an unread book, an unanswered letter, an unresolved argument - `ないまま` is the natural choice and carries a faint regret that `ないで` does not.

---

## ～まま · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>つ**まま**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ → ✅ <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>った**まま**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ<br>*Verbs take the た-form. The dictionary form before `まま` is not possible.*

- ❌ <ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>**まま**の<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>**のまま**の<ruby>建物<rt>たてもの</rt></ruby><br>*A noun needs `の` to reach `まま`, exactly as it does to reach any other noun.*

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**まま**だった → ✅ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**なまま**だった<br>*A な-adjective keeps its `な`.*

- ❌ ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた**まま**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>さないでください。（meaning "don't talk while eating"） → ✅ ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**ながら**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>さないでください。<br>*Eating is an ongoing action, not a state left behind, so it takes `ながら`. The た-form version would mean "don't talk in the state of having eaten", which is not a thing.*

## ～まま · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けたまま**する** → ✅ <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けたまま**にする**<br>*To say you are deliberately leaving something in a state, the pattern is `～ままにする` or `～ままにしておく`.*

# ～っぱなし

**Meaning** · left in that state and never put right; kept going without a break

`～っぱなし` comes from <ruby>放<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す, "to let go", and it keeps the word's flavour. Something was set going or set open and then **abandoned** - the tap still running, the drawer still hanging out. A second use stretches an action across a whole span of time with no pause: on your feet the entire journey, losing every match for three years.

---

## ～っぱなし · Formation

`っぱなし` attaches to the **ます-stem** of a verb and produces a **noun**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + っぱなし | <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>します → <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しっぱなし |
| る-verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + っぱなし | <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けます → <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けっぱなし |
| Predicate | ～っぱなし + だ / です | <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けっぱなしだ |
| Before a noun | ～っぱなし + の + Noun | つけっぱなしのテレビ |
| Deliberate / accusing | ～っぱなし + に + する | <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しっぱなしにする |
| Ending up that way | ～っぱなし + に + なる | つけっぱなしになる |

---

## ～っぱなし · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - two families of verb take this suffix and they give the two senses. **Transitive** verbs that put something into a state give the "left that way" reading: つけっぱなし・<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けっぱなし・<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しっぱなし・<ruby>脱<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ぎっぱなし・<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>きっぱなし. **Intransitive or durative** verbs give the "without a break" reading: <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ちっぱなし・<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>りっぱなし・<ruby>鳴<rt>な</rt></ruby>りっぱなし・<ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けっぱなし・<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>われっぱなし. Verbs outside those two groups do not take it at all - ❌<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べっぱなし</code>, ❌<code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>りっぱなし</code>.

---

## ～っぱなし · Examples

テレビを**つけっぱなし**にして<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てしまった。<br>*I fell asleep with the TV left on.*

<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しっぱなし**にしないでね。<br>*Don't leave the tap running.*

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けっぱなし**だったので、<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>がすっかり<ruby>冷<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>えていた。<br>*The window had been left open, so the room had gone completely cold.*

<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>から<ruby>名古屋<rt>なごや</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>空<rt>あ</rt></ruby>かず、**<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ちっぱなし**だった。<br>*No seat came free from Tokyo to Nagoya, so I was on my feet the whole way.*

---

## ～っぱなし · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>鳴<rt>な</rt></ruby>りっぱなし**で、<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>がまったく<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>まなかった。<br>*The phone rang nonstop from morning yesterday and I got nothing done.*

<ruby>上着<rt>うわぎ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>脱<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ぎっぱなし**にしておくと、<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>に<ruby>必<rt>かなら</rt></ruby>ずしかられる。<br>*If I leave my jacket where I took it off, my mother tells me off without fail.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>では<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しっぱなし**で、こちらは<ruby>一言<rt>ひとこと</rt></ruby>も<ruby>意見<rt>いけん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>えなかった。<br>*The manager talked for the whole meeting and I couldn't get a single word in.*

<ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>の<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>こそ<ruby>勝<rt>か</rt></ruby>ちたい。ここ<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>、あのチームには**<ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けっぱなし**なのだ。<br>*I really want to win this one. We've lost to that team every single time for three years.*

---

## ～っぱなし · Nuance & register

The first sense is **never neutral**. `っぱなし` is the word you use when you are annoyed, or admitting fault. Saying <ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>がつけっぱなしだ is not reporting that a light is on; it is saying somebody left it on and should not have. Parents, flatmates and office managers use it constantly, and it is one of the standard building blocks of Japanese nagging.

The second sense - "without a break" - trades blame for **endurance**. <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ちっぱなし, <ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>きっぱなし, <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>りっぱなし are complaints about being worn out. <ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けっぱなし and <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>われっぱなし add a note of being on the receiving end and doing nothing about it, which is why <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>われっぱなしではいられない - "I can't just take this lying down" - is a fixed expression.

---

## ～っぱなし · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is casual to neutral. It is perfectly normal at work between colleagues, but in a written report or a formal apology you would step up to something flatter: <ruby>電源<rt>でんげん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>り<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れておりました rather than つけっぱなしでした. The kanji spelling <code>～っ<ruby>放<rt>ぱな</rt></ruby>し</code> exists but is rare; kana is the norm.

Watch the small tsu. <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す gives <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しっぱなし, not ❌<code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>すっぱなし</code>; the っ belongs to the suffix and the stem stays short.

---

## ～っぱなし · ～っぱなし vs ～てある

> **Rule of thumb** - `てある` says somebody did it **on purpose and left it that way for a reason**. `っぱなし` says somebody did it and **couldn't be bothered to undo it**. Same physical scene, opposite verdicts.

<ruby>空気<rt>くうき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れかえるため、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け**てある**。<br>*The window has been left open to air the room out.*

<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>も<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めないので、<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け**っぱなし**だ。<br>*Nobody ever shuts it, so the window just stays open.*

Both sentences describe a window that is open because a person opened it. `てある` supplies a purpose - and can be followed by one, as here. `っぱなし` supplies negligence, and it cannot take a purpose clause without sounding sarcastic.

---

## ～っぱなし · ～っぱなし vs ～てある (cont.)

This is the single most useful thing to know about the pattern, because the English "left open" covers both and gives you no clue which to choose.

---

## ～っぱなし · ～っぱなし vs ～たまま

> **Rule of thumb** - `たまま` is the **neutral** version. `っぱなし` is the same state with a complaint attached, and it is limited to verb stems.

<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>をはいた**まま**<ruby>畳<rt>たたみ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がってはいけない。<br>*You mustn't step onto the tatami with your shoes on.*

<ruby>玄関<rt>げんかん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>脱<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ぎっぱなし**で、<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>踏<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>場<rt>ば</rt></ruby>もない。<br>*Shoes have been kicked off and left all over the entryway - there's nowhere to stand.*

`まま` will also attach to nouns and adjectives（<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>のまま, <ruby>熱<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いまま）, which `っぱなし` never does.

---

## ～っぱなし · ～っぱなし vs ～たまま (cont.)

And only `っぱなし` has the "without a break" sense. <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ったまま<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる means eating in a standing posture; <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ちっぱなしだった means being on your feet for hours. Substituting one for the other changes the sentence completely.

---

## ～っぱなし · ～っぱなし vs ～ておく

> **Rule of thumb** - `ておく` is leaving something as it is **because that is the useful thing to do**. `っぱなし` is leaving it as it is because nobody thought about it.

<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>もまた<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>うので、パソコンはつけ**ておきます**。<br>*I'll leave the computer on, since I'll be using it again this afternoon.*

<ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>も**つけっぱなし**にしていたら、<ruby>電気代<rt>でんきだい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>なことになる。<br>*Leave it on for three days and the electricity bill will be horrifying.*

Notice that both can appear with the same verb in the same room. The choice is entirely about how the speaker wants the action judged - which makes `っぱなし` a useful thing to *avoid* when you are talking about your own deliberate decisions.

---

## ～っぱなし · Common mistakes

- ❌ つける**っぱなし** → ✅ つけ**っぱなし**<br>*The suffix takes the ます-stem. Dictionary form in front of it is not possible.*

- ❌ <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>った**っぱなし** → ✅ <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち**っぱなし**<br>*The た-form belongs to `まま`, not to `っぱなし`.*

- ❌ <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けっぱなし**な**<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けっぱなし**の**<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby><br>*The result is a noun, so a following noun is linked with `の`.*

- ❌ お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>さんのために、<ruby>入口<rt>いりぐち</rt></ruby>のドアを<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け**っぱなし**にしておきました。 → ✅ お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>さんのために、<ruby>入口<rt>いりぐち</rt></ruby>のドアを<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け**ておきました**。<br>*You did it on purpose and for a good reason, so `ておく`. `っぱなし` would tell your boss you were careless.*

---

## ～っぱなし · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**っぱなし**で<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた → ✅ <ruby>食器<rt>しょっき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**っぱなし**にして<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた<br>*Eating leaves no object in a state, so the verb does not take the suffix. Name the thing that was actually left - the dishes.*

# ～てばかりいる

**Meaning** · do nothing but ～; be forever ～ing

`～てばかりいる` says a person spends their time on **one activity to the exclusion of everything else** - and that the speaker thinks they should be doing something different. It is a complaint dressed as a description of a schedule.

---

## ～てばかりいる · Formation

`ばかり` slots between the て-form and `いる`. The whole thing is an ordinary る-verb, so it conjugates exactly like `いる`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | て-form + ばかり + いる | <ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>ぶ → <ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>んでばかりいる |
| Polite | て-form + ばかり + います | <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てばかりいます |
| Past | て-form + ばかり + いた | <ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>いてばかりいた |
| Joining a clause | て-form + ばかり + いて / いても | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てばかりいても |
| Without いる | て-form + ばかり + だ | <ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>ってばかりだ |
| Noun + する verb | Noun + ばかり + している | <ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>ばかりしている |
| Casual contraction | て-form + ばっかり(いる) | <ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>んでばっかり |

---

## ～てばかりいる · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - `～てばかりいる` and `～てばかりだ` are close, and both are common. The version with `いる` presents the behaviour as going on right now or over a stretch of time; the version with `だ` is flatter, closer to a verdict on the person. Do not mix them into ❌`～てばかりする`, which is not a form.

---

## ～てばかりいる · Examples

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>んでばかりいる**。<br>*My little brother does nothing but play.*

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>文句<rt>もんく</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってばかりいて**、<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>では<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>もしない。<br>*He does nothing but complain and never does anything himself.*

**<ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>いてばかりいて**も<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わらないよ。<br>*Crying about it isn't going to change anything.*

<ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>はスマホを**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てばかりいて**、<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しかけても<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>をしない。<br>*My son is forever staring at his phone and doesn't answer when I speak to him.*

---

## ～てばかりいる · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みの<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てばかりいる**と、かえって<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れが<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>れない。<br>*If you do nothing but sleep on your days off, you actually end up less rested.*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>職場<rt>しょくば</rt></ruby>では**<ruby>失敗<rt>しっぱい</rt></ruby>してばかりで**、<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでいる。<br>*At my new job I do nothing but make mistakes, and I'm down about it every day.*

<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>のことを**<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してばかりいて**も<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>めないので、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいことを<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めたい。<br>*Dwelling on the past won't get me anywhere, so I want to start something new.*

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>反対<rt>はんたい</rt></ruby>してばかりいた**が、<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つも<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さなかった。<br>*The manager did nothing but object throughout the meeting, yet didn't offer a single idea of his own.*

---

## ～てばかりいる · Nuance & register

The pattern is **almost always critical**. Even when the activity is one you would normally praise, `てばかりいる` turns it into an imbalance: <ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>してばかりいる is not "such a hard worker", it is "he never comes out of his room" - worry, not admiration. If you want the approving version, use a plain progressive: よく<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しています.

Aimed at yourself it becomes self-reproach, and this is extremely common: <ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>ってばかりですみません, <ruby>迷惑<rt>めいわく</rt></ruby>をかけてばかりで. In that use it functions almost as an apology formula.

The casual contraction is `ばっかり`, and in fast speech it shortens further to `ばっか`: <ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>んでばっかりいないで<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ってよ. That is friends and family register; keep `ばかり` for anything else.

---

## ～てばかりいる · Nuance & register (cont.)

One genuine ambiguity to be aware of. `ばかり` has a second life after the **た-form** meaning "just did" - <ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたばかりです, "I've only just arrived". The two are different patterns with nothing in common but the word, and the て-form versus た-form is what tells them apart. Confusing them produces sentences that are grammatical and mean the wrong thing, which is the worst kind of error.

Register is otherwise neutral: `てばかりいる` is at home in speech, in fiction and in a newspaper column about people who spend too long online.

---

## ～てばかりいる · ～てばかりいる vs Noun + ばかり

> **Rule of thumb** - after a **noun**, `ばかり` restricts *what* you do it to. After a **て-form**, `ばかり` restricts *what you do* - the whole activity.

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>肉<rt>にく</rt></ruby>**ばかり**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べて、<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>す。<br>*My brother eats nothing but meat and leaves the vegetables.*

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>から**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてばかりいる**。<br>*My brother has done nothing but eat since this morning.*

---

## ～てばかりいる · ～てばかりいる vs Noun + ばかり (cont.)

In the first, eating is fine; the problem is the menu. In the second, eating itself is the problem - he should be doing something else with his day. The noun version is also neutral enough to be used approvingly（<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>ばかり<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べている can be a compliment）, which the て-form version essentially never is.

---

## ～てばかりいる · ～てばかりいる vs ～ている

> **Rule of thumb** - `ている` reports what is happening. `てばかりいる` reports the same thing plus "and nothing else, and I object".

<ruby>娘<rt>むすめ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>でゲームを**している**。<br>*My daughter is playing a game in her room.*

<ruby>娘<rt>むすめ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>でゲームを**してばかりいる**。<br>*My daughter does nothing but play games in her room.*

---

## ～てばかりいる · ～てばかりいる vs ～ている (cont.)

`ている` covers this moment or a neutral habit. `てばかりいる` needs a **stretch of repeated behaviour**, so it cannot describe a single instance: pointing at someone right now and saying <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てばかりいます about one glance is wrong the way "he's forever looking" would be wrong about one look.

---

## ～てばかりいる · ～てばかりいる vs ～がち

> **Rule of thumb** - `がち` says it happens **often**, leaving room for other things. `てばかりいる` says it is **all** that happens.

<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>時期<rt>じき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>外食<rt>がいしょく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>くなり**がち**だ。<br>*When things get busy I tend to eat out more.*

<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby><ruby>暮<rt>ぐ</rt></ruby>らしを<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めてから、<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてばかりいる**。<br>*Since I started living alone I've done nothing but eat out.*

---

## ～てばかりいる · ～てばかりいる vs ～がち (cont.)

Both disapprove, but they measure different things - frequency against exclusivity. They also attach differently: `がち` takes the ます-stem and builds a な-adjective, while `てばかりいる` takes the て-form and stays a verb. That means only `てばかりいる` can carry tense and aspect of its own: <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてばかりいた, <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてばかりいるだろう.

---

## ～てばかりいる · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>ぶ**ばかりいる** → ✅ <ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>ん**でばかりいる**<br>*`ばかり` in this pattern follows the **て-form**, not the dictionary form.*

- ❌ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてばかり**する** → ✅ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてばかり**いる**<br>*The verb at the end is `いる`. `している` belongs to the noun pattern <code><ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>ばかりしている</code>.*

- ❌ お<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**てばかりいます**。（meaning "I've just eaten lunch"） → ✅ お<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**たばかりです**。<br>*"Just did" is `た-form + ばかり`. The て-form version says you have been eating all day.*

- ❌ <ruby>娘<rt>むすめ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>してばかりいて、えらいですね。 → ✅ <ruby>娘<rt>むすめ</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby>よく<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>していて、えらいですね。<br>*The pattern carries disapproval, so it cannot be used to praise someone.*

---

## ～てばかりいる · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はこちらを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てばかりいます。（about one moment） → ✅ <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はこちらを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ています。<br>*`てばかりいる` needs behaviour repeated over time; it cannot report a single instant.*

# ～かけ / ～かける

**Meaning** · started and not finished; on the point of ～ing

`～かけ` marks an action that got under way and then stopped short of its end. With an action you chose to start, that means **half-done**: a half-eaten sandwich, a half-written email. With a change that happens to you or to the world, it means **almost happened**: nearly fell, nearly forgot, sun starting to set.

---

## ～かけ / ～かける · Formation

The suffix has a **noun** form, `かけ`, and a **verb** form, `かける` (a る-verb, from <ruby>掛<rt>か</rt></ruby>ける).

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) → noun | ます-stem + かけ | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みます → <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みかけ |
| Before a noun | ～かけ + の + Noun | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みかけの<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby> |
| Predicate | ～かけ + だ / です | この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みかけだ |
| Verb (ます-stem) → verb | ます-stem + かける | <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>います → <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いかける |
| Past - began and stopped | ～かけた | <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いかけた |
| Progressive - is on the verge | ～かけている | <ruby>沈<rt>しず</rt></ruby>みかけている |
| Before a noun (verb form) | ～かけた + Noun | <ruby>消<rt>き</rt></ruby>えかけた<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby> |

---

## ～かけ / ～かける · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the verb has to name something with an **endpoint** to fall short of. <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む, <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる, <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>く, <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う, <ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ちる, <ruby>消<rt>き</rt></ruby>える, <ruby>沈<rt>しず</rt></ruby>む all qualify. States and open-ended activities do not: ❌<code><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>みかけ</code>, ❌<code><ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>びかけ</code>, ❌<code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>りかけ</code>. Note also that ます-stem + かける has a separate, purely lexical life in words like <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しかける ("speak *to* someone") and <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かける ("go out") - those are vocabulary, not this pattern.

---

## ～かけ / ～かける · Examples

<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**かけ**のパンがテーブルに<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>いてある。<br>*There's a half-eaten piece of bread sitting on the table.*

<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み**かけ**の<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby><ruby>冊<rt>さつ</rt></ruby>もたまっている。<br>*I've got several books piled up that I started and never finished.*

<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>い**かけて**、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>黙<rt>だま</rt></ruby>ってしまった。<br>*He started to say something and then suddenly went quiet.*

<ruby>階段<rt>かいだん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>転<rt>ころ</rt></ruby>び**かけた**が、<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>すりをつかんで<ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>かった。<br>*I nearly fell on the stairs but grabbed the handrail and was fine.*

---

## ～かけ / ～かける · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き**かけ**のメールを<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ってしまい、あわてて<ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I accidentally sent a half-written email and hurried to apologise.*

<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>沈<rt>しず</rt></ruby>み**かけている**ので、そろそろ<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>下<rt>お</rt></ruby>りましょう。<br>*The sun is starting to go down, so let's head off the mountain.*

<ruby>祖父<rt>そふ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>声<rt>こえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れ**かけていた**ことに<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>づいて、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>悲<rt>かな</rt></ruby>しくなった。<br>*I realised I'd nearly forgotten what my grandfather's voice sounded like, and it made me sad.*

やり**かけ**の<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>いたまま<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってしまったので、<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby><ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>片<rt>かた</rt></ruby>づけます。<br>*I went home leaving unfinished work on my desk, so I'll deal with it first thing in the morning.*

---

## ～かけ / ～かける · Nuance & register

The noun form is a plain, everyday word - you can point at a plate and say <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べかけ with no more ceremony than saying "leftovers". It does carry a faint sense of **someone else's**: a <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みかけのコーヒー is a cup you would rather not drink from, which is why offering one is slightly awkward.

The verb form is where the two senses separate cleanly, and the split follows the verb. With a **volitional** verb, `かけた` means the speaker began and was interrupted: <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いかけた, <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がりかけた. With a **non-volitional** change, it means the process was under way and did not complete - or is still under way: <ruby>消<rt>き</rt></ruby>えかけた<ruby>火<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>, <ruby>倒<rt>たお</rt></ruby>れかけた<ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>, <ruby>沈<rt>しず</rt></ruby>みかけている<ruby>船<rt>ふね</rt></ruby>. Fiction leans on the second heavily.

---

## ～かけ / ～かける · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral throughout. Nothing about `かけ` is casual or formal; it turns up in conversation, in instructions and in literature alike.

One shape worth memorising because it is so useful: <code><ruby>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>にかける</code> → <ruby>死<rt>し</rt></ruby>にかけた, "came close to dying". It is the standard way to say a near-miss with something serious, and it is much more common than any construction with "almost".

---

## ～かけ / ～かける · ～かけ vs ～ている途中

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby></code> means you are **in the middle of it right now**. `かけ` means you **stopped** partway and are not doing it at the moment.

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいる**<ruby>途中<rt>とちゅう</rt></ruby>**なので、あとで<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>してもいいですか。<br>*I'm in the middle of reading it, so can we talk later?*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った<ruby>小説<rt>しょうせつ</rt></ruby>が、まだ**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みかけ**のままだ。<br>*The novel I bought last year is still sitting there half-read.*

The first sentence has a person actively engaged. The second has a book on a shelf and nobody reading it. This is why `かけ` so often appears with `まま` and `そのまま` - the state has been abandoned and left standing.

---

## ～かけ / ～かける · ～かける vs ～そうだ

> **Rule of thumb** - `そうだ` predicts from appearances: it **looks like** it will happen. `かける` reports that the process **actually began**.

<ruby>棚<rt>たな</rt></ruby>の<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ち**そう**だから、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>奥<rt>おく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れて。<br>*The books on the shelf look like they're going to fall - push them back a bit.*

<ruby>棚<rt>たな</rt></ruby>の<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ち**かけた**ので、あわてて<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*The books started to fall, so I grabbed at them.*

Nothing has moved in the first sentence; it is a judgement about a risk. In the second, the books were on their way down. Learners who reach for `かける` when they mean "looks like" end up reporting events that never occurred.

---

## ～かけ / ～かける · ～かけ vs ～たばかり

> **Rule of thumb** - opposite ends of the same action. `かけ` = **begun, not finished**. `たばかり` = **finished a moment ago**.

そのケーキ、まだ**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べかけ**だから<ruby>捨<rt>す</rt></ruby>てないで。<br>*Don't throw that cake out - I haven't finished it.*

ケーキなら、さっき**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたばかり**です。<br>*If you mean cake, I've only just had some.*

The forms make the difference visible: `かけ` sits on the ます-stem, `ばかり` in this sense sits on the た-form. If you can see a た, the action is over.

---

## ～かけ / ～かける · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む**かけ**の<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み**かけ**の<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby><br>*`かけ` attaches to the ます-stem.*

- ❌ <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みかけ**な**<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みかけ**の**<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby><br>*The noun form links to a following noun with `の`.*

- ❌ もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しで<ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>に**あいかけだった**。 → ✅ もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しで<ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>に**あいかけた**。<br>*The bare noun `かけ` is for objects left unfinished. The "on the verge" sense needs the verb `かける`, conjugated.*

- ❌ <ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんは<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**かけ**です。（meaning "I've just eaten"） → ✅ <ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんは<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**たばかり**です。<br>*<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べかけ</code> means the plate is still half full.*

---

## ～かけ / ～かける · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>みかけている**。 → ✅ <ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいる。<br>*<code><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>む</code> has no endpoint to stop short of, so it does not take `かける` at all.*

---

# ～たて

**Meaning** · freshly ～ed; only just ～ed

`～たて` says a thing came out of some process **moments ago and is still in that condition**. Bread out of the oven, rice out of the cooker, paint still wet, a graduate still blinking at the world. The freshness is presented as a quality of the thing, and it is a quality that will fade.

---

## ～たて · Formation

`たて` attaches to the **ます-stem** and produces a **noun**.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + たて | <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きます → <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きたて |
| する-verb | Noun + し + たて | <ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>します → <ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>したて |
| Before a noun | ～たて + の + Noun | <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きたてのパン |
| Predicate | ～たて + だ / です | ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>炊<rt>た</rt></ruby>きたてです |
| Joining a clause | ～たて + で | <ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>いたてで、まだ<ruby>下手<rt>へた</rt></ruby>だ |

---

## ～たて · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - this is the most **lexically restricted** suffix in the group. Two small families use it and nothing else does. Things coming out of a **process**: <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きたて・<ruby>揚<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げたて・<ruby>炊<rt>た</rt></ruby>きたて・<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れたて・<ruby>塗<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>りたて・できたて・とれたて・<ruby>生<rt>う</rt></ruby>まれたて. People who have just **entered a status**: <ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>いたて・<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えたて・<ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>したて・<ruby>入社<rt>にゅうしゃ</rt></ruby>したて・なりたて. Outside those, it simply is not said: ❌<code><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いたて</code>, ❌<code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みたて</code>, ❌<code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たて</code>. (The existing words <ruby>見立<rt>みた</rt></ruby>て, <ruby>仕立<rt>した</rt></ruby>て and <ruby>組<rt>く</rt></ruby>み<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>て are separate vocabulary, not this suffix.)

---

## ～たて · Examples

**<ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きたて**のパンはやっぱりおいしい。<br>*Freshly baked bread really is delicious.*

ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>炊<rt>た</rt></ruby>きたて**が<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*Rice is best straight out of the cooker.*

ペンキ**<ruby>塗<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>りたて**なので、さわらないでください。<br>*The paint is still wet, so please don't touch.*

<ruby>市場<rt>いちば</rt></ruby>で**とれたて**の<ruby>魚<rt>さかな</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>って<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I bought some freshly caught fish at the market and took it home.*

---

## ～たて · Examples (cont.)

**<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れたて**のコーヒーのにおいで<ruby>目<rt>め</rt></ruby>が<ruby>覚<rt>さ</rt></ruby>めた。<br>*The smell of freshly brewed coffee woke me up.*

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby><ruby>免許<rt>めんきょ</rt></ruby>を**とりたて**で、まだ<ruby>高速<rt>こうそく</rt></ruby><ruby>道路<rt>どうろ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>怖<rt>こわ</rt></ruby>いらしい。<br>*He's only just got his driving licence and is apparently still nervous of the motorway.*

**<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えたて**の<ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby>を<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>おうとして、かえって<ruby>変<rt>へん</rt></ruby>な<ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>になってしまった。<br>*I tried to use the keigo I'd just learned and ended up with odder Japanese than before.*

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>揚<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げたて**のコロッケを<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つずつ<ruby>紙<rt>かみ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>包<rt>つつ</rt></ruby>んでくれるので、<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>でつい<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べてしまう。<br>*This shop wraps each freshly fried croquette in paper for you, so I always end up eating one on the way home.*

---

## ～たて · Nuance & register

`たて` is a **warm, appetising** word, and it is the only suffix in this group that is reliably positive. Shops and menus lean on it - <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きたてパン, できたてのお<ruby>弁当<rt>べんとう</rt></ruby> - because it promises the best version of the thing. The two combinations <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きたて and できたて are so entrenched that they function as ordinary nouns.

The freshness has a **short shelf life** built in, and this is what makes it different from "new". Bread is <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きたて for an hour, not a week. A person is <ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>したて for a few months. If the interval you mean is long, the word is wrong.

---

## ～たて · Nuance & register (cont.)

Applied to people, the tone is affectionate but a little indulgent: <ruby>新入社員<rt>しんにゅうしゃいん</rt></ruby>なりたて and <ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>いたて both carry "and it shows". It is not an insult, but it is not a compliment on competence either.

There is no register restriction - <ruby>塗<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>りたて appears on public signs, <ruby>揚<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げたて on menus, <ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えたて in casual conversation. Some combinations take the kanji <ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>て in writing（<ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>き<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>て）, but kana is far more usual.

---

## ～たて · ～たて vs ～たばかり

> **Rule of thumb** - `たばかり` works with **any verb** and measures time since an event. `たて` works with a **short fixed list** and describes the thing as being in a fresh condition.

この<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>っ**たばかり**だ。<br>*I only bought this car last week.*

<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby><ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>には、**<ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きたて**のパンが<ruby>並<rt>なら</rt></ruby>んでいた。<br>*At six in the morning the shop had freshly baked bread lined up.*

---

## ～たて · ～たて vs ～たばかり (cont.)

Where both are available, they say slightly different things. <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>いたばかりのパン tells you when the baking happened; <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きたてのパン tells you what the bread is like. The second is what you want on a sign, and the first is what you want in a sentence about your morning.

The safe rule for production: if the combination is not one of the fixed set, use `たばかり`. ❌<code><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いたての<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby></code> is not Japanese; <code><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ったばかりの<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby></code> is.

---

## ～たて · ～たて vs 新しい

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい</code> means not old. `たて` means straight out of the process that made it, with the heat still in it.

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいパンを<ruby>二<rt>ふた</rt></ruby>つください。<br>*Two of the fresh loaves, please.*

<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>すぎに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くと、もう**<ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きたて**は<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>っていない。<br>*If you go after midday, there's nothing left that's just out of the oven.*

---

## ～たて · ～たて vs 新しい (cont.)

<code><ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい</code> covers anything baked today, and it would still be true tomorrow morning relative to last week's loaf. `たて` is a claim about minutes. This is also why <code><ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい</code> can describe things that were never made - <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>の<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby> - while `たて` needs a process and an agent.

---

## ～たて · ～たて vs ～かけ

> **Rule of thumb** - the two ends of one action. `かけ` = begun and **abandoned partway**; `たて` = **just completed**.

<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み**かけ**のお<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>が、まだテーブルに<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>っていた。<br>*There was a half-drunk cup of tea still on the table.*

<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れ**たて**のお<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>を、<ruby>来客<rt>らいきゃく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。<br>*I served the visitors tea I'd just made.*

---

## ～たて · ～たて vs ～かけ (cont.)

Structurally they are twins: both sit on the ます-stem, both produce a noun, both take `の` before a following noun. The difference is entirely one of where in the action they point - and, as a result, of mood. A <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みかけ cup is a small domestic mess; an <ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れたて cup is hospitality.

---

## ～たて · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>く**たて**のパン → ✅ <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>き**たて**のパン<br>*`たて` attaches to the ます-stem, not the dictionary form.*

- ❌ <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きたて**な**パン → ✅ <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>きたて**の**パン<br>*The result is a noun and links with `の`.*

- ❌ <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い**たて**のパソコン → ✅ <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>っ**たばかり**のパソコン<br>*Buying is not a process that leaves a thing freshly made, and <code><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いたて</code> is not used. The productive pattern is `たばかり`.*

- ❌ <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby><ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>したてなので、まだ<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れていません。 → ✅ <ruby>先月<rt>せんげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>したてなので、まだ<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れていません。<br>*The freshness must be genuinely recent. A year is too long for `たて`; use <code><ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby><ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>したばかり</code> if you really mean a year ago.*

---

## ～たて · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ この<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>いたてで<ruby>古<rt>ふる</rt></ruby>いです。 → ✅ この<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>は<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>いたてでいいにおいがします。<br>*`たて` is a positive word about a fresh state. Pairing it with a negative assessment of the same thing pulls the sentence in two directions.*

---

# ～きる

**Meaning** · do all of it, right to the end; be completely in a state

`～きる` (from <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>る, "to cut") takes a verb and adds **completion with nothing left over**. Not merely finishing, but getting through the whole quantity: the last page, the last spoonful, the last yen. A second use pushes a state to its limit - utterly exhausted, thoroughly cold, perfectly obvious.

---

## ～きる · Formation

`きる` attaches to the **ます-stem** and produces a verb. Because it is <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>る, it conjugates as an **う-verb** (a Group 1 / <ruby>五段<rt>ごだん</rt></ruby> verb), despite ending in -る.

---

## ～きる · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + きる | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきる |
| する-verb | Noun + し + きる | <ruby>整理<rt>せいり</rt></ruby>します → <ruby>整理<rt>せいり</rt></ruby>しきる |
| Past | ～きった | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みきった |
| て-form | ～きって | <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>いきって |
| Negative (う-verb!) | ～きらない | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきらない |
| Potential | ～きれる / ～きれない | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきれない |
| State pushed to its limit | ～きった + Noun | <ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れきった<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby> |

---

## ～きる · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the completion sense needs a **quantity or a course** to run to the end of: a plateful, a book, a savings account, a race. The intensifying sense needs a verb of **change of state**: <ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れる, <ruby>冷<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>える, <ruby>弱<rt>よわ</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>澄<rt>す</rt></ruby>む, <ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>る. Verbs with neither - ❌<code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>きる</code> in the sense "look completely", ❌<code><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>みきる</code> - do not take it.

---

## ～きる · Examples

<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きなカレーを<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきった**。<br>*I finished off a huge plate of curry by myself.*

<ruby>今月<rt>こんげつ</rt></ruby>のお<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>を、もう**<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>いきって**しまった。<br>*I've already spent all of this month's money.*

<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>い<ruby>小説<rt>しょうせつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みきった**。<br>*I got through a long novel in three days.*

<ruby>最後<rt>さいご</rt></ruby>まで**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>りきった**<ruby>選手<rt>せんしゅ</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きな<ruby>拍手<rt>はくしゅ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>られた。<br>*The runners who made it all the way to the finish got a big round of applause.*

---

## ～きる · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しの<ruby>片<rt>かた</rt></ruby>づけで**<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れきって**、<ruby>夕<rt>ゆう</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べずに<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>た。<br>*I was completely worn out from unpacking after the move and went to bed without dinner.*

そんなことは**<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりきった**<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>だから、わざわざ<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わなくていい。<br>*That's blindingly obvious, so there's no need to spell it out.*

<ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>、**<ruby>冷<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>えきった**<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>でストーブをつけるのは、なかなかつらい。<br>*On winter mornings, lighting the heater in a room that's gone stone cold is pretty grim.*

<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby><ruby>分<rt>ぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>一<rt>いっ</rt></ruby><ruby>週間<rt>しゅうかん</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>整理<rt>せいり</rt></ruby>しきる**のは、さすがに<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>だと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。<br>*Getting three years of documents completely sorted in one week honestly isn't going to happen.*

---

## ～きる · Nuance & register

The completion sense carries a note of **achievement or effort**. <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みきった is not a neutral "I finished reading"; it says the book was long and you got to the end of it. This is why the pattern is common in sports reporting and in talking about anything demanding - <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>りきる, やりきる, <ruby>戦<rt>たたか</rt></ruby>いきる.

The intensifying sense produces a small set of very common `～きった` adjectives. <ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れきった, <ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>りきった, <ruby>冷<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>えきった, <ruby>澄<rt>す</rt></ruby>みきった<ruby>空<rt>そら</rt></ruby>, <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりきったこと. These feel a shade literary before a noun and are entirely ordinary in speech as predicates （もう<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れきっています）.

---

## ～きる · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral. Several combinations have hardened into everyday vocabulary and no longer feel like grammar: <ruby>売<rt>う</rt></ruby>り<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>れる ("sell out"), <ruby>締<rt>し</rt></ruby>め<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>り ("deadline"), <ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>って ("taking the plunge"). You already know those as words; this page is about the productive pattern behind them.

The conjugation trap is worth stating plainly. <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>る **is an う-verb**, so the negative is <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきらない, not ❌<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきない</code>, and the polite past is <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきりました. Learners who see the -る and treat it as a る-verb get every form wrong at once.

---

## ～きる · ～きる vs ～てしまう

> **Rule of thumb** - `てしまう` is about the action being **over and irreversible**, often with regret. `きる` is about the **quantity being exhausted**, often with satisfaction.

<ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>のケーキを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**てしまった**。<br>*I've gone and eaten the cake that was in the fridge.*

<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きなケーキだったが、みんなで<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**きった**。<br>*It was a big cake, but between us we finished the lot.*

---

## ～きる · ～きる vs ～てしまう (cont.)

The first invites "…and I shouldn't have". The second invites "…and we're rather pleased". They stack, too, and the combination is very common: <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>いきってしまった - used it all up, and now there is none left.

---

## ～きる · ～きる vs ～終わる

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> means the **activity stopped** because you reached the end of it. `きる` means the **whole amount was got through**.

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>やっとレポートを<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わった**。<br>*I finally finished writing the report yesterday.*

<ruby>三<rt>さん</rt></ruby><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>かけて、<ruby>残<rt>のこ</rt></ruby>っていた<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby>やり**きった**。<br>*I spent three hours on it and got through every one of the remaining questions.*

---

## ～きる · ～きる vs ～終わる (cont.)

<code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> is the neutral, always-available choice and attaches to almost any verb. `きる` adds difficulty and completeness, and it needs something countable to be complete *of*. That is why <code><ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> is unremarkable while <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>ききる sounds like the report nearly defeated you.

---

## ～きる · ～きる vs ～抜く

> **Rule of thumb** - both mean "to the very end". `きる` stresses that **nothing was left**; <code><ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>く</code> stresses that you **kept going despite hardship**.

<ruby>苦<rt>くる</rt></ruby>しかったが、<ruby>最後<rt>さいご</rt></ruby>まで**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>りきった**。<br>*It hurt, but I made it all the way to the end.*

どんなに<ruby>反対<rt>はんたい</rt></ruby>されても、<ruby>最後<rt>さいご</rt></ruby>までやり**<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>く**つもりだ。<br>*However much people object, I intend to see it through to the end.*

---

## ～きる · ～きる vs ～抜く (cont.)

With a race, either works and they shade differently: <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>りきる is about covering the whole distance, <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>り<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>く about enduring it. With a quantity - money, food, paper - only `きる` fits, because there is nothing to endure. <code><ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>く</code> belongs to a later level, but the pair is worth seeing now so that `きる` does not get stretched to cover perseverance.

---

## ～きる · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる**きる** → ✅ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**きる**<br>*`きる` attaches to the ます-stem.*

- ❌ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**きない** → ✅ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**きらない**<br>*<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>る is an う-verb, so its negative is きらない. The same goes for きります, きって, きった.*

- ❌ <ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>をなくし**きった**。 → ✅ <ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>をなくし**てしまった**。<br>*A wallet is one object, not a quantity to work through, so there is nothing for `きる` to exhaust. Regret over a single completed action is `てしまう`.*

- ❌ この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>三十年<rt>さんじゅうねん</rt></ruby><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>み**きった**。 → ✅ この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>三十年<rt>さんじゅうねん</rt></ruby><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいる。<br>*<code><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>む</code> has no endpoint, so it takes neither sense of `きる`.*

---

## ～きる · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れきる<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れきった<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby><br>*In the intensifying sense, the state has already been reached, so the form before a noun is the past `きった`.*

# ～きれない

**Meaning** · there is too much of it to get through; more than one can manage

`～きれない` is the negative potential of <ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>る in its "do it all" sense. It does not say you are incapable of the action - it says the **amount defeats you**. You can eat; there is simply more food than you can eat. You can count; there are simply more stars than you can count.

---

## ～きれない · Formation

`きれない` attaches to the **ます-stem**. It is already a negative potential, so it never takes a second potential ending.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + きれない | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきれない |
| する-verb | Noun + し + きれない | <ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>します → <ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>しきれない |
| Polite | ～きれません | <ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えきれません |
| Past | ～きれなかった | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みきれなかった |
| Before a noun (with ほど/くらい) | ～きれない + ほど / くらい + の | <ruby>数<rt>かぞ</rt></ruby>えきれないほどの<ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby> |
| Affirmative counterpart | ～きれる | <ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で<ruby>運<rt>はこ</rt></ruby>びきれる |
| Literary negative て-form | ～きれず(に) | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちきれずに<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた |

---

## ～きれない · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the affirmative `きれる` exists but is far less common than the negative. When you mean "I managed to finish it", the ordinary <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきった</code> is what people actually say; <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきれた</code> is reserved for contexts where the *capacity* is the point（<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきれる<ruby>量<rt>りょう</rt></ruby>です）. The pattern is lopsided, and it is worth learning it as a negative first.

---

## ～きれない · Examples

<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>すぎて、**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきれない**。<br>*There's too much food - I can't finish it.*

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>くて、<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>では**<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えきれない**。<br>*There are so many kanji that I can't learn them all in one day.*

<ruby>空<rt>そら</rt></ruby>には**<ruby>数<rt>かぞ</rt></ruby>えきれない**ほどの<ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>光<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>っていた。<br>*There were countless stars shining in the sky.*

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>楽<rt>たの</rt></ruby>しみで、**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちきれない**。<br>*I'm so looking forward to next week's trip that I can hardly wait.*

---

## ～きれない · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>くて<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>では**<ruby>運<rt>はこ</rt></ruby>びきれない**ので、<ruby>二回<rt>にかい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>けます。<br>*There's too much to carry in one trip, so I'll split it into two.*

お<ruby>世話<rt>せわ</rt></ruby>になった<ruby>方々<rt>かたがた</rt></ruby>への<ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>言葉<rt>ことば</rt></ruby>では**<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えきれません**。<br>*I can't put into words how grateful I am to everyone who looked after me.*

<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>すぎて<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>までに**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みきれなかった**ので、<ruby>要点<rt>ようてん</rt></ruby>だけまとめてもらった。<br>*There was too much material to get through before the meeting, so I had someone summarise the key points.*

---

## ～きれない · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>では**<ruby>抱<rt>かか</rt></ruby>えきれない**<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>を、<ruby>何年<rt>なんねん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わずにいたそうだ。<br>*Apparently he kept a problem too big for one person to handle to himself for years, without telling anyone.*

---

## ～きれない · Nuance & register

The core meaning is **capacity overrun**, and it is the amount that overruns, not your skill. That distinction does real work in a sentence: <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みきれない means the book was too long, while <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めない would mean you cannot read it at all - bad eyesight, wrong language, handwriting you cannot decipher. Learners who use `きれない` for lack of ability end up claiming that a two-line note is too long for them.

`きれない` is register-neutral and it turns up constantly in one particular shape: `～きれないほど`, "so many/much that you couldn't ～". <ruby>数<rt>かぞ</rt></ruby>えきれないほど is the standard Japanese for "countless", and <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきれないほどの<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby> is what you say about a table that groans.

---

## ～きれない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two idiomatic uses do not follow the "too much" template, and both are worth knowing. <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちきれない is eager anticipation - the waiting exceeds what you can bear - and it is a warm, positive expression. <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じきれない and <ruby>割<rt>わ</rt></ruby>りきれない (in its emotional sense) describe a feeling you cannot fully settle into; they are more literary.

In writing you will also meet the て-form `きれず(に)`: <ruby>我慢<rt>がまん</rt></ruby>しきれずに<ruby>笑<rt>わら</rt></ruby>ってしまった. That is standard written Japanese and sounds slightly formal in speech.

---

## ～きれない · ～きれない vs the plain potential negative

> **Rule of thumb** - `られない` denies the **ability**. `きれない` grants the ability and denies that it is **enough for the quantity**.

<ruby>辛<rt>から</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>が<ruby>苦手<rt>にがて</rt></ruby>なので、このカレーは**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べられない**。<br>*I'm no good with spicy food, so I can't eat this curry.*

おいしいけれど<ruby>量<rt>りょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>くて、このカレーは**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきれない**。<br>*It's delicious, but there's so much of it that I can't finish this curry.*

In the first sentence the speaker cannot eat one spoonful. In the second they have been eating happily and have simply run out of room. Notice that `きれない` presupposes the action is under way or possible - which is why it fits so naturally with a half-empty plate.

---

## ～きれない · ～きれない vs ～すぎる

> **Rule of thumb** - `すぎる` describes **the thing**: there is too much of it. `きれない` describes **the person**: they cannot get to the end of it.

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>すぎる**。<br>*There's far too much homework today.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>までに**やりきれない**。<br>*I won't get through today's homework by tonight.*

---

## ～きれない · ～きれない vs ～すぎる (cont.)

They are two views of the same problem and they combine constantly - the commonest frame in the whole pattern is `～すぎて～きれない`: <ruby>多<rt>おお</rt></ruby>すぎて<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきれない. Using both is not redundant; the first names the cause and the second the consequence.

---

## ～きれない · ～きれない vs ～きった

> **Rule of thumb** - these are the two halves of one pattern. `きった` = got through the whole lot. `きれない` = could not.

<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きなピザだったが、<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきった**。<br>*It was a big pizza, but the three of us polished it off.*

<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きなピザで、<ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>では**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきれなかった**。<br>*It was a big pizza, and the two of us couldn't finish it.*

---

## ～きれない · ～きれない vs ～きった (cont.)

The asymmetry to remember is that the affirmative side normally uses the **plain** form <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきる / <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきった</code>, not the potential <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきれる</code>, while the negative side almost always uses the **potential** <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきれない</code> rather than the plain <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べきらない</code>. Both plain negatives exist, but they sound deliberate - "doesn't finish it" rather than "can't finish it".

---

## ～きれない · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる**きれない** → ✅ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**きれない**<br>*The suffix goes on the ます-stem.*

- ❌ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**きれられない** → ✅ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**きれない**<br>*`きれない` is already the potential negative. Adding another potential ending doubles it.*

- ❌ この<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さくて**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みきれない**。 → ✅ この<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さくて**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めない**。<br>*Small print is a problem of ability, not of quantity. `きれない` needs an amount to fall short of.*

- ❌ <ruby>数<rt>かぞ</rt></ruby>えきれない**の**<ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>数<rt>かぞ</rt></ruby>えきれない**ほどの**<ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby><br>*`きれない` is a negative い-adjective form, so it modifies a noun directly（<ruby>数<rt>かぞ</rt></ruby>えきれない<ruby>星<rt>ほし</rt></ruby>）or through `ほどの`. It never takes a bare `の`.*

---

## ～きれない · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>のライブが**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちきれる**! → ✅ <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>のライブが**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちきれない**!<br>*The excited "I can't wait!" is the negative. The affirmative would mean you are perfectly capable of waiting, which rather kills the mood.*

---

# ～がたい

**Meaning** · hard to bring oneself to ～; almost impossible to ～

`～がたい` (from <ruby>難<rt>かた</rt></ruby>い, voiced to がたい as a suffix) marks a difficulty that lives in the **mind, not in the hands**. Something is hard to believe, hard to accept, hard to part with, hard to put into words. It is not about a task being physically awkward - for that Japanese has a different suffix entirely.

---

## ～がたい · Formation

`がたい` attaches to the **ます-stem** and produces an **い-adjective**: <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じがたい → <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じがたかった → <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じがたく → no `だ`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + がたい | <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じます → <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じがたい |
| する-verb | Noun + し + がたい | <ruby>理解<rt>りかい</rt></ruby>します → <ruby>理解<rt>りかい</rt></ruby>しがたい |
| Before a noun | ～がたい + Noun | <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れがたい<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby> |
| Past | ～がたかった | <ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>け<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れがたかった |
| Polite | ～がたいです | <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じがたいです |
| Adverbial | ～がたく | <ruby>捨<rt>す</rt></ruby>てがたく<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う |

---

## ～がたい · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - this suffix is **not productive**. It attaches to a short list of verbs of thinking, feeling, saying and judging: <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じる・<ruby>理解<rt>りかい</rt></ruby>する・<ruby>想像<rt>そうぞう</rt></ruby>する・<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れる・<ruby>捨<rt>す</rt></ruby>てる・<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う・<ruby>認<rt>みと</rt></ruby>める・<ruby>近寄<rt>ちかよ</rt></ruby>る・<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>かす・<ruby>得<rt>え</rt></ruby>る・<ruby>耐<rt>た</rt></ruby>える. Everyday physical verbs do not take it: ❌<code><ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けがたい</code>, ❌<code><ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>きがたい</code>, ❌<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べがたい</code>. If you are unsure whether a combination exists, it almost certainly does not - use `にくい`.

---

## ～がたい · Examples

あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じがたい**。<br>*His story is hard to believe.*

<ruby>一年間<rt>いちねんかん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れがたい**<ruby>経験<rt>けいけん</rt></ruby>になった。<br>*The year abroad turned into an experience I'll never forget.*

どちらの<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>捨<rt>す</rt></ruby>てがたい**ので、もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えさせてください。<br>*Both proposals have something going for them, so let me think a bit longer.*

なぜ<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>があんな<ruby>嘘<rt>うそ</rt></ruby>をついたのか、どうしても**<ruby>理解<rt>りかい</rt></ruby>しがたい**。<br>*I really cannot understand why he told a lie like that.*

---

## ～がたい · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し**<ruby>近寄<rt>ちかよ</rt></ruby>りがたい**<ruby>雰囲気<rt>ふんいき</rt></ruby>があるが、<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>してみると<ruby>優<rt>やさ</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>だ。<br>*The manager comes across as a bit unapproachable, but he's kind once you talk to him.*

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>の<ruby>段階<rt>だんかい</rt></ruby>では、この<ruby>計画<rt>けいかく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>成功<rt>せいこう</rt></ruby>したとは**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いがたい**。<br>*At this stage it would be hard to call the project a success.*

<ruby>信頼<rt>しんらい</rt></ruby>できる<ruby>同僚<rt>どうりょう</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>得<rt>え</rt></ruby>がたい**<ruby>存在<rt>そんざい</rt></ruby>だと、<ruby>転職<rt>てんしょく</rt></ruby>して<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めて<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>づいた。<br>*It took changing jobs for me to see how rare a colleague you can trust really is.*

<ruby>数字<rt>すうじ</rt></ruby>がこれだけそろっているのだから、この<ruby>結論<rt>けつろん</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>かしがたい**だろう。<br>*With this many figures lined up, this conclusion is going to be hard to shift.*

---

## ～がたい · Nuance & register

`がたい` is **noticeably formal**. It belongs to written Japanese, prepared speech, news copy and careful argument. In everyday conversation the same idea comes out as <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じられない, ちょっとわからない, <ruby>捨<rt>す</rt></ruby>てるのはもったいない. Saying <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じがたいですね in a chat with a friend is not wrong, but it lands like "that strains credulity" in English - correct, and a little theatrical.

The kind of difficulty it names is **psychological or principled**. A conclusion that is <ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>かしがたい is not heavy; it is well supported. A person who is <ruby>近寄<rt>ちかよ</rt></ruby>りがたい is not physically distant; they are intimidating. Note that this is a comment on how they come across and not necessarily a criticism - <ruby>近寄<rt>ちかよ</rt></ruby>りがたい often shades into "commands respect".

---

## ～がたい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The suffix is also useful for **hedged negatives**, and this is where business Japanese likes it. <ruby>成功<rt>せいこう</rt></ruby>したとは<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いがたい means "I would not go so far as to call it a success" - a way of saying it failed without saying it failed. <ruby>賛成<rt>さんせい</rt></ruby>しがたい softens a refusal in the same way.

Two lexical footnotes. ありがたい ("grateful, welcome") comes from this suffix historically but is now simply a word, and it is not built the same way. And the reading voices: がたい, not かたい. <ruby>固<rt>かた</rt></ruby>い and <ruby>硬<rt>かた</rt></ruby>い are unrelated adjectives about hardness.

---

## ～がたい · ～がたい vs ～にくい

> **Rule of thumb** - `にくい` is **practical** difficulty and attaches to almost any verb. `がたい` is **psychological or moral** difficulty and attaches to a short fixed list. When in doubt, `にくい` is nearly always the right answer.

この<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さすぎて**<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>きにくい**。<br>*These shoes are too small and hard to walk in.*

<ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>のことは<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>には**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いがたい**。<br>*It's hard to bring myself to tell my family about the accident.*

---

## ～がたい · ～がたい vs ～にくい (cont.)

Where a verb takes both, the difference is exactly this. <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いにくい is awkward to pronounce or awkward to raise - a practical obstacle. <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いがたい is something you cannot in conscience assert. Likewise <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じにくい ("hard to credit, on the evidence") is milder than <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じがたい ("beyond belief").

Register follows the same line: `にくい` is at home everywhere, `がたい` is formal. Learners producing ❌<code><ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>いがたいアプリ</code> for "an app that's hard to use" are picking the wrong suffix twice over - wrong kind of difficulty and wrong level of formality.

---

## ～がたい · ～がたい vs ～ようがない

> **Rule of thumb** - `がたい` says it is hard and you would rather not. `ようがない` says there is **no method at all**, so the question of willingness never arises.

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>い<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じがたい**が、<ruby>証拠<rt>しょうこ</rt></ruby>もない。<br>*His excuse is hard to believe, though there's no evidence either way.*

<ruby>連絡先<rt>れんらくさき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないので、<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らせ**ようがない**。<br>*I don't have his contact details, so there's no way to let him know.*

The first speaker *could* believe the excuse and is declining to. The second speaker would happily make the call and cannot. That is why `がたい` combines naturally with `が` and けれど - it admits the other side - while `ようがない` tends to close a topic down.

---

## ～がたい · ～がたい vs ～られない

> **Rule of thumb** - `られない` reports that you **cannot**. `がたい` reports that it is **hard to**, leaving the possibility open.

そんな<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>、とても**<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じられない**。<br>*I simply can't believe a story like that.*

<ruby>報告<rt>ほうこく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>内容<rt>ないよう</rt></ruby>には、いくつか**<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じがたい**<ruby>点<rt>てん</rt></ruby>がある。<br>*There are several points in the report that are hard to credit.*

The first is an emotional reaction, spoken, and it slams the door. The second is an assessment, written, and it leaves room for the report to turn out right after all. Choosing `がたい` where `られない` was wanted makes you sound as though you are drafting a memo in the middle of an argument; choosing `られない` in a formal document sounds hot-headed.

---

## ～がたい · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じる**がたい** → ✅ <ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じ**がたい**<br>*`がたい` attaches to the ます-stem.*

- ❌ <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れがたい**だ** → ✅ <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れがたい<br>*The result is an い-adjective. The polite form is <code><ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れがたいです</code>.*

- ❌ この<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>くて**<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>りがたい**。 → ✅ この<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>は<ruby>狭<rt>せま</rt></ruby>くて**<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>りにくい**。<br>*Physical awkwardness is `にくい`. `がたい` is for difficulty of the mind.*

- ❌ しんじ**かたい** → ✅ しんじ**がたい**<br>*The suffix voices. Unvoiced かたい belongs to <ruby>固<rt>かた</rt></ruby>い and <ruby>硬<rt>かた</rt></ruby>い, which mean "hard" in the physical sense.*

---

## ～がたい · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みがたい**。 → ✅ この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みにくい**。<br>*A character that is hard to make out is a practical problem, and <code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みがたい</code> is not a combination Japanese uses.*

---

## ～がたい · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>三十年<rt>さんじゅうねん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ると、あの<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れ____<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>だ。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れがたい</code> is one of the fixed combinations, and as an い-adjective it sits directly in front of the noun with no `の` and no `な`. It is warmer and more literary than <code><ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れられない</code>.

---

## ～がたい · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: この<ruby>説明書<rt>せつめいしょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さくて（<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みにくい・<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みがたい）。

Small print is a practical obstacle, and practical obstacles take `にくい`. <code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みがたい</code> is not used at all - the verb <code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む</code> is outside the small set that takes `がたい`.

## ～がたい · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "At this point it would be hard to say the plan has gone well."

{reveal}

## ～がたい · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>態度<rt>たいど</rt></ruby>は<ruby>理解<rt>りかい</rt></ruby>しがたいだと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。

# ～ようがない / ～ようもない

**Meaning** · there is no way of doing it; no method exists

`よう` here is <ruby>様<rt>よう</rt></ruby>, "manner, way of doing". `～ようがない` therefore says that the **means are missing**: not that you lack the skill, not that you are unwilling, but that there is nothing available to do it with. No address, so no way to send it. No pieces left, so no way to mend it.

---

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · Formation

`ようがない` attaches to the **ます-stem** and the result is an **い-adjective**, because it ends in `ない`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) | ます-stem + ようがない | <ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>します → <ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>しようがない |
| する-verb | Noun + し + ようがない | <ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>します → <ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>しようがない |
| Emphatic variant | ます-stem + ようもない | <ruby>想像<rt>そうぞう</rt></ruby>しようもない |
| Polite | ～ようがありません | <ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>しようがありません |
| Past | ～ようがなかった | <ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>けようがなかった |
| て-form / joining | ～ようがなく(て) | <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きようがなくて<ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>った |
| Before a noun | ～ようがない + Noun | <ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>しようがない<ruby>時計<rt>とけい</rt></ruby> |

---

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - `ようもない` is the same pattern with `も` in place of `が`, and the `も` adds "not even". It is a shade stronger and a shade more literary; everything else is identical. Do not confuse either with the **volitional** <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こう</code>, <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べよう</code> - those come from a different base and cannot take this ending.

---

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · Examples

<ruby>住所<rt>じゅうしょ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からないので、<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>り**ようがない**。<br>*I don't know the address, so there's no way to send it.*

こんなに<ruby>壊<rt>こわ</rt></ruby>れていては、<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>し**ようがない**。<br>*Broken like this, there's no fixing it.*

<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby><ruby>番号<rt>ばんごう</rt></ruby>も<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないから、<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>し**ようがない**。<br>*I don't even know his phone number, so there's no way to get in touch.*

<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きく<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてあるので、<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>え**ようがない**。<br>*The name is written in big letters, so there's no way you can get it wrong.*

---

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>本人<rt>ほんにん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>してくれない<ruby>以上<rt>いじょう</rt></ruby>、こちらからは<ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>け**ようがない**。<br>*As long as he won't talk to us, there's nothing we can do to help.*

<ruby>材料<rt>ざいりょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>もないのだから、<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>り**ようがない**じゃないか。<br>*There aren't any ingredients, so how exactly am I supposed to make anything?*

あのときの<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>気持<rt>きも</rt></ruby>ちは、<ruby>想像<rt>そうぞう</rt></ruby>し**ようもない**。<br>*I cannot even begin to imagine how she felt at the time.*

データがすべて<ruby>消<rt>き</rt></ruby>えてしまったので、<ruby>報告書<rt>ほうこくしょ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き**ようがなく**、<ruby>締<rt>し</rt></ruby>め<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>りを<ruby>延<rt>の</rt></ruby>ばしてもらった。<br>*All the data was wiped, so there was simply no way to write the report and I had the deadline pushed back.*

---

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · Nuance & register

The feeling is **resignation without excuse-making**. Because the sentence claims that no method exists, it removes blame from everybody: <ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>しようがない is not "I failed to fix it" but "it was not fixable". That makes the pattern useful for declining responsibility gracefully, and it also makes it a strong claim - if a method *did* exist, the sentence is simply untrue.

There is a mirror-image use that surprises learners: `ようがない` on a verb of error or failure produces a **guarantee**. <ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>えようがない means "you couldn't get it wrong if you tried"; <ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けようがない, "there's no way we lose". The logic is the same - no method available - but the effect is confident rather than defeated.

---

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register is neutral, leaning slightly written for `ようもない`. The one thing that is *not* neutral is the contraction. <ruby>仕方<rt>しかた</rt></ruby>がない has a spoken cousin しょうがない, and しょうがない is a contraction of しようがない - so the two patterns share a form. In practice, しょうがない has drifted away into a fixed phrase meaning "oh well, nothing to be done", and it is quite casual; if you mean specifically "there is no way to do it", say しようがない with the full vowel.

Note also that しょうがない<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> means "hopeless case, incorrigible" - an affectionate insult, not a comment on methods.

---

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · ～ようがない vs the potential negative

> **Rule of thumb** - `られない` says **I** can't. `ようがない` says **nobody** can, because there is nothing to do it with.

<ruby>道具<rt>どうぐ</rt></ruby>がないので、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>には**<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>せない**。<br>*I haven't got the tools, so I can't fix it.*

<ruby>部品<rt>ぶひん</rt></ruby>がもう<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>られていないので、**<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>しようがない**。<br>*The parts aren't made any more, so it simply can't be repaired.*

---

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · ～ようがない vs the potential negative (cont.)

In the first sentence, a repair shop could still do it. In the second, nobody can. This is why `ようがない` is the right choice when you want to close off the possibility entirely - and the wrong choice when the obstacle is personal. ❌<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しくて<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きようがない is a common learner error; being busy is your circumstance, not an absence of method, so it should be <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しくて<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>けない.

---

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · ～ようがない vs ～がたい

> **Rule of thumb** - `がたい` is **hard**; `ようがない` is **impossible**. `がたい` concedes that the thing could be done and says you can hardly bring yourself to; `ようがない` says the question does not arise.

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>理解<rt>りかい</rt></ruby>しがたい**が、<ruby>一応<rt>いちおう</rt></ruby><ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いておこう。<br>*His explanation is hard to make sense of, but let's hear him out anyway.*

<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一<rt>いち</rt></ruby><ruby>枚<rt>まい</rt></ruby>もなくては、**<ruby>理解<rt>りかい</rt></ruby>しようがない**。<br>*Without a single document to go on, there's no way to understand it.*

The give-away is what can follow. `がたい` sits comfortably before `が` or けれど, because there is another side. `ようがない` normally ends the matter.

---

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · ～ようがない vs 仕方がない

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>仕方<rt>しかた</rt></ruby>がない</code> is about **accepting a situation**: it can't be helped. `ようがない` is about **an action having no method**: there's no way to do it.

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まってしまったのだから、**<ruby>仕方<rt>しかた</rt></ruby>がない**。<br>*The trains have stopped, so there's nothing for it.*

<ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>も<ruby>電波<rt>でんぱ</rt></ruby>もないので、<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べ**ようがない**。<br>*With no map and no signal, there's no way to look up the route.*

---

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · ～ようがない vs 仕方がない (cont.)

<code><ruby>仕方<rt>しかた</rt></ruby>がない</code> stands alone as a comment on the whole state of affairs and attaches to nothing. `ようがない` must attach to a verb stem and names a specific action that cannot be carried out. They often appear in the same breath: <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べようがないのだから、<ruby>仕方<rt>しかた</rt></ruby>がない.

---

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す**ようがない** → ✅ <ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>し**ようがない**<br>*The suffix takes the ます-stem.*

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こう**がない** → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ようがない**<br>*The volitional <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こう</code> is a different form entirely. `よう` here is the noun <ruby>様<rt>よう</rt></ruby>, and it follows the stem.*

- ❌ <ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>する**ようがない** → ✅ <ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>し**ようがない**<br>*With a する-verb, the stem is `し`.*

- ❌ <ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>しようがない**だ** → ✅ <ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>しようがない<br>*It ends in `ない`, so it is an い-adjective and takes no `だ`. Polite is <code><ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>しようがありません</code>.*

---

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>くて<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>し**ようがない**。 → ✅ <ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>くて<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>**できない**。<br>*Being sleepy is a personal state, not a missing method. `ようがない` needs the means themselves to be absent.*

---

{reveal}

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · Practice 2

The shop's disappearance removes the means, not the speaker's ability - exactly the distinction the pattern draws. <code><ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いようがない</code> sits on the ます-stem of <code><ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う</code>.

## ～ようがない / ～ようもない · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この<ruby>時計<rt>とけい</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>せない**。 (b) この<ruby>時計<rt>とけい</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>しようがない**。

# ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる

**Meaning** · four suffixes that bolt a direction, a partner, a second attempt or a habit onto a verb

Japanese builds a great deal of its vocabulary by welding a second verb onto the ます-stem of a first. These four are the ones an N3 learner meets constantly: <code>～<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む</code> sends the action **inward or deep**, <code>～<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う</code> makes it **mutual**, <code>～<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す</code> makes it a **second go**, and <code>～<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れる</code> says you have **done it often enough to be comfortable**.

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Formation

All four attach to the **ます-stem** and produce an ordinary verb. <ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む, <ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う and <ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す are **う-verbs**; <ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れる is a **る-verb**.

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (ます-stem) - inward | ます-stem + <ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む | <ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>びます → <ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>び<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む |
| Verb (ます-stem) - deeply, at length | ます-stem + <ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む | <ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えます → <ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む |
| Verb (ます-stem) - mutually | ます-stem + <ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う | <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>します → <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う |
| Verb (ます-stem) - do over | ます-stem + <ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す | <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きます → <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す |
| Verb (ます-stem) - get used to doing | ます-stem + <ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れる | <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>います → <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れる |

| Noun instead of a stem | Noun + に + <ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れる | <ruby>生活<rt>せいかつ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れる |

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - these are **word-formation**, not free grammar. Each combination is a separate item of vocabulary, and only some combinations exist: <ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む is a word, ❌<code><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>り<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む</code> in the sense "run inward" mostly is not. <ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む is the least predictable of the four - <ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む means both "use for years" and "embezzle" - so treat new combinations as vocabulary to look up rather than forms to generate. <ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す and <ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う are the two you can most safely build yourself.

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Examples

この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>えたので、**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>します**。<br>*I got this kanji wrong, so I'll write it again.*

クラスのみんなで**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った**。<br>*The whole class talked it over together.*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもが<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>たまりに**<ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>び<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んだ**。<br>*The child jumped into the puddle.*

この<ruby>道具<rt>どうぐ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby><ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>っているので、すっかり**<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れて**いる。<br>*I've been using this tool for ten years, so it's completely second nature by now.*

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いて、しばらく**<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んで**いた。<br>*The manager heard the question and sat there thinking hard for a while.*

<ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>をやめるかどうか、もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>**<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>して**みることにした。<br>*I decided to give the question of quitting the exchange programme another think.*

<ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>ったときに**<ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>け<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>える**<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちが<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>くにいるのは、ありがたいことだ。<br>*It's a blessing to have friends nearby who can help each other out when things go wrong.*

<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>満員<rt>まんいん</rt></ruby><ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>にもだんだん**<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れて**きて、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>では<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>める。<br>*I've gradually got used to Tokyo's packed trains, and these days I can even read on them.*

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>昔<rt>むかし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>友人<rt>ゆうじん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>い、<ruby>喫茶店<rt>きっさてん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>二<rt>に</rt></ruby><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んで**しまった。<br>*I ran into an old friend at the station and ended up talking for two solid hours in a coffee shop.*

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Nuance & register

All four are ordinary, register-neutral vocabulary. Nothing about them is formal or casual; what varies is how freely you can invent new combinations.

<code>～<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す</code> is the most productive and the most useful for a learner. Almost any deliberate action can be redone: <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す, <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す, <ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す, <ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>り<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す, やり<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す. It always implies the first attempt was unsatisfactory, which is why it does not work for pleasant repetition - you do not <ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>き<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す a song you love, you listen again.

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Nuance & register (cont.)

<code>～<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う</code> requires **two or more parties acting on each other**. That is a real constraint: <ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>け<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う needs both sides helping, and if only one person helps, it is plain <ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>ける. This use produces a warm, cooperative tone, which is why <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>い is the word for a proper discussion rather than an argument.

<code>～<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む</code> splits into a physical direction（<ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>び<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む, <ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>り<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む, <ruby>流<rt>なが</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む）and an intensity or duration sense（<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む, <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む, <ruby>黙<rt>だま</rt></ruby>り<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む, <ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>え<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む）. The second group tends to suggest being absorbed to the point of losing track of the outside world.

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Nuance & register (cont.)

<code>～<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れる</code> almost always appears as **～<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れている** or **～<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れてくる**, because it names a state you have arrived at rather than an event. And keep it apart from its noun-taking twin: <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>生活<rt>せいかつ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れる takes a noun and `に`; <ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>で<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れる takes a stem.

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · ～直す vs もう一度～する

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby></code> is neutral repetition. <code>～<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す</code> says the first attempt was **wrong or inadequate** and this is the corrected version.

<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きな<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>なので、**もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たい。<br>*It's a film I love, so I'd like to watch it again.*

<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>抜<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>けていたので、<ruby>申込書<rt>もうしこみしょ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>した**。<br>*The name was missing, so I filled the application form in again.*

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · ～直す vs もう一度～する (cont.)

Swap them and the meanings shift audibly. <ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby><ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す would mean watching it again to check something, or - in another sense - coming to think better of it. もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>く is simply writing a second copy, with no implication that the first was faulty.

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · ～合う vs 一緒に～する

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>～<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う</code> means the action goes **between** the participants. <code><ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に</code> means they do it **side by side**, usually to something or someone else.

<ruby>三人<rt>さんにん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>運<rt>はこ</rt></ruby>んだ。<br>*The three of us carried the luggage together.*

<ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>っている<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>を、みんなで**<ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>け<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>おう**。<br>*Let's all help each other out when we're in trouble.*

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · ～合う vs 一緒に～する (cont.)

The luggage is a third party; nobody is carrying anybody. That is why ❌<code><ruby>運<rt>はこ</rt></ruby>び<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う</code> is odd. With <ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>ける, the sentence only takes <ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う if the helping flows in both directions - <ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>ける would mean two people helping a third.

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · ～込む vs ～出す

> **Rule of thumb** - as directional suffixes they are opposites: <code><ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む</code> points **in**, <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> points **out**. Note that <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> also has the unrelated "suddenly begin" sense you met at N4.

かばんから<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した**。<br>*I took my wallet out of my bag.*

ポケットに<ruby>切符<rt>きっぷ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んだ**。<br>*I shoved the ticket into my pocket.*

The same verb takes both and the pair is worth memorising: <ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>び<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す ("dash out, leap out") against <ruby>飛<rt>と</rt></ruby>び<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む ("dive in").

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · ～込む vs ～出す (cont.)

The trap is that <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> has a second job - marking the sudden start of an action, as in <ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す - and that job has nothing to do with direction. Context tells them apart: <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す is "break into a run", not "run outwards".

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す** → ✅ <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き**<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す**<br>*All four attach to the ます-stem.*

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>し**<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った**。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>した。<br>*<code>～<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う</code> needs the action to run both ways. Consulting a teacher goes one way, so the plain verb is correct.*

- ❌ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>生活<rt>せいかつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>み**<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れた**。 → ✅ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>生活<rt>せいかつ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れた。<br>*With a **noun**, use <code>に<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れる</code>. The stem form needs a verb in front of it: <code>この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>み<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れた</code>.*

- ❌ この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>をもう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>**<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>もう**。 → ✅ この<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>をもう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>**<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>そう**。<br>*<code><ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む</code> is to brood, absorbed and silent. To rethink something is <code><ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す</code>.*

---

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ このパソコンは**<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れる**。 → ✅ このパソコンは**<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>慣<rt>な</rt></ruby>れている**。<br>*The suffix names a state you have reached, so the ている form is what you almost always want.*

## ～込む・～合う・～直す・～慣れる · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>黙<rt>だま</rt></ruby>って<ruby>下<rt>した</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ていた。 (b) <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>黙<rt>だま</rt></ruby>り<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んで**しまった。

{reveal}
(a) simply describes what he was doing: not speaking, eyes down. It is a neutral piece of stage direction and says nothing about why.

{reveal}
(b) says he shut down into silence and stayed there. The <code><ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>む</code> supplies both depth and duration - he has gone somewhere inward - and `しまった` adds that this was not what anyone wanted. It is a much heavier silence than (a).
