# ～ないで

**Meaning** · without doing something - and, in speech, a bare "don't"

`～ないで` is the **negative て-form of a verb**. Like the ordinary て-form it hooks one clause onto the next, but it hooks on a *non-event*: the thing that did not happen while the main action went ahead. You met it at N5 inside `～ないでください`; that `で` was never decoration, and the form stands on its own.

---

## ～ないで · Formation

Build the ない-form, then add `で`. Nothing else changes.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ない-form + で | <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぐ → <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>がないで |
| Verb (る-verb) | ない-form + で | <ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>める → <ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めないで |
| Verb (する) | しないで | <ruby>運動<rt>うんどう</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>運動<rt>うんどう</rt></ruby>しないで |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないで | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないで |
| Written / formal equivalent | ない-form − ない + ずに | <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぐ → <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>がずに |
| With a following ending | ないで + ください／ほしい／くれる | <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>がないでください |
| い-adjective | not available - use くなくて | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い → ❌<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くないで |
| な-adjective / Noun | not available - use でなくて | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ → ❌<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かじゃないで |

---

## ～ないで · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `ないで` is **verb-only**. If the negated word is an adjective or a noun, the joining form is `なくて`, and no `で` version exists.

> **Note** - the same string does three jobs, and context separates them cleanly. (1) *Without doing*: <ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ないで<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>いた. (2) *Instead of doing*: <ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しないでゲームをした. (3) A **negative request**, with `ください` or with nothing at all: <ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けないでください／<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けないで.

---

## ～ないで · Examples

けさは**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べないで**<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>た。<br>*I left the house this morning without eating.*

<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>わないで**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*I came home without buying anything.*

<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>脱<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>がないで**<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*Please come in without taking your shoes off.*

<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>わないで**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでみましょう。<br>*Let's try reading it without using a dictionary.*

---

## ～ないで · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>はどこにも**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないで**、<ruby>一日中<rt>いちにちじゅう</rt></ruby>ゲームをしていた。<br>*On Sunday I didn't go anywhere - I played games all day.*

お<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>い、この<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>はまだ**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わないで**ね。<br>*Please - don't tell anyone about this yet.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れていたので、<ruby>晩<rt>ばん</rt></ruby>ごはんも**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>らないで**そのまま<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てしまいました。<br>*I was so tired yesterday that I fell asleep without even making dinner.*

<ruby>説明書<rt>せつめいしょ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>まないで**<ruby>組<rt>く</rt></ruby>み<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>てたら、ねじが<ruby>二本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby><ruby>余<rt>あま</rt></ruby>ってしまった。<br>*I assembled it without reading the instructions and ended up with two screws left over.*

---

## ～ないで · Nuance & register

The joining `ないで` is register-neutral. It appears in casual speech and in ordinary polite writing alike, and the politeness of the sentence is decided at the end, by the main verb: <code><ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>たないで<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた</code> and <code><ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>たないで<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけました</code> differ only there.

The **request** use is different, and it is the one to watch. Bare `ないで` with nothing after it - <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ないで, <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないで - is casual and emotionally direct. It is what you say to a friend, a partner, a child or a pet, and it can sound pleading rather than commanding. `ね` softens it further (<code><ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れないでね</code>); `よ` presses. Said to a stranger or a superior it is far too blunt, and you want `ないでください` at minimum, or `ないでいただけますか` if you are genuinely asking a favour.

---

## ～ないで · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two neighbouring endings live on the same stem and are worth having ready: `～ないでほしい`「I'd rather you didn't」, which reports a wish instead of issuing an order, and `～ないでくれる？／～ないでくれない？`「could you not…?」, casual but softer than the bare form because it is a question.

In writing - reports, instructions, signage, anything with a formal voice - the joining `ないで` is routinely replaced by `ずに`. That swap is not optional in a stiff register; <code><ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>しないで<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りました</code> in a written apology reads as spoken language.

---

## ～ないで · ～ないで vs ～ずに

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different register. `ないで` is spoken and neutral; `ずに` is written and formal. Only `ずに` has an irregular form: する becomes **せずに**, never ❌しずに.

`ずに` is a survival from classical Japanese, and it attaches to the same negative stem: <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>かない → <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>かずに, <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べない → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べずに, <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ずに.

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わずに**<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>て<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った。<br>*He left the room without saying a word.*

<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>を**せずに**ゆっくり<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んでください。<br>*Please rest properly and don't push yourself.*

---

## ～ないで · ～ないで vs ～ずに (cont.)

Two limits on `ずに`. It cannot carry a request: there is no ❌<code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ずにください</code> - requests are `ないで` territory. And the *instead of* reading is weaker with `ずに`; for that sense `ないで` is the natural choice in speech.

You will also meet bare `ず` with no `に`, joining clauses in written Japanese: <code><ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>せず、<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めた</code>. Treat it as the same word wearing its most formal clothes.

---

## ～ないで · ～ないで vs ～なくて

> **Rule of thumb** - `ないで` tells you *how* the next thing happened; `なくて` tells you *why* it happened.

<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べないで**<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った。<br>*I went to work without eating breakfast.*

<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べなくて**、おなかがすいた。<br>*I didn't eat breakfast, so I got hungry.*

---

## ～ないで · ～ないで vs ～なくて (cont.)

The first sentence is about the manner of going; the second gives the cause of the hunger. The practical consequence is that `なくて` cannot introduce a request or a command - ❌<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べなくてください</code> is not Japanese - and that a `ないで` clause must be followed by something the same subject deliberately did. This distinction has its own page at this level; it repays the time.

---

## ～ないで · ～ないで vs ～ないように

> **Rule of thumb** - `ないで` is *without doing it*; `ないように` is *so that it does not happen*. One reports, the other aims.

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めないで**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた。<br>*I went out without closing the window.*

<ruby>風邪<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>を**ひかないように**、<ruby>上着<rt>うわぎ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>って<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*I'll take a jacket so I don't catch a cold.*

---

## ～ないで · ～ないで vs ～ないように (cont.)

The difference matters most in requests. <code><ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れないでください</code> is a flat "please don't be late"; <code><ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れないようにしてください</code> is "please make sure you're not late", which frames the same content as advice and lands far more gently. `ように` also survives being said upward, where the bare negative request does not.

---

## ～ないで · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ない**て** → ✅ <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ない**で**<br>*The negative て-form is `ないで`. There is no form ❌`ないて` anywhere in the language.*

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**じゃないで**<ruby>集中<rt>しゅうちゅう</rt></ruby>できない。 → ✅ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**じゃなくて**<ruby>集中<rt>しゅうちゅう</rt></ruby>できない。<br>*`ないで` attaches only to verbs. Adjectives and nouns join with `なくて` / `でなくて`.*

- ❌ <ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>を**しずに**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた。 → ✅ <ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>を**せずに**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた。<br>*する is irregular before `ずに`: the stem is `せ`, not `し`.*

- ❌ <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**なくて**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた。 → ✅ <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**ないで**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた。<br>*Going out is not *caused* by not eating. When the negative describes the manner of the main action, it must be `ないで`.*

---

## ～ないで · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、そこに<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>ら**ないで**。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、そちらには<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>ら**ないでください**。<br>*Bare `ないで` is intimate and blunt. Upward it needs `ください`, or better, a question form.*

---

## ～ないで · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>た____<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけたので、ぬれてしまった。

{reveal}
<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>た**ないで**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけたので、ぬれてしまった。

{reveal}
Not taking an umbrella is the *manner* in which the going-out happened, so the joining form is `ないで`. Writing <code><ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>たなくて</code> would claim that going out was caused by the lack of an umbrella.

---

## ～ないで · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>（しないで・しなくて）<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ってしまいました。

{reveal}
<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>**しないで**<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ってしまいました。

{reveal}
The sending is the main event and the not-checking describes how it was done - carelessly, in one motion. `しなくて` would need a consequence after it, such as <code><ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>しなくて、あとで<ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>った</code>.

---

## ～ないで · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese, in a formal written register: "He signed the contract without reading it."

{reveal}
<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>契約書<rt>けいやくしょ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>まずに**サインした。

{reveal}
`ずに` is the written counterpart of `ないで`, and a sentence about contracts is exactly where it belongs. <code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>まないで</code> is not wrong, but it sounds spoken.

---

## ～ないで · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>をしずにねてしまった。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>を**せずに**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てしまった。

{reveal}
The `ず` ending takes an irregular stem from する - `せ`. Every other verb uses its ordinary ない-stem, which is why this one is so easy to get wrong.

---

## ～ないで · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>さ**ないで**。 (b) <ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>さ**ないでください**。

{reveal}
(a) is bare and intimate. With no ending after it, it reads as an immediate, emotional "don't!" - the kind of thing said to a family member or a close friend, and depending on the tone it can sound closer to pleading than to ordering.

{reveal}
(b) adds the standard polite request ending. It is neutral, usable with a colleague or a stranger, and it sounds like an instruction rather than a reaction. Between the two sit <code><ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>さないでね</code> (softer) and <code><ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>さないでくれる？</code> (casual but framed as a question).

# ～ないで vs ～なくて

**Meaning** · the two negative て-forms - *without doing* versus *because … not*

Japanese has one positive て-form and **two** negative ones. `～ないで` joins a non-event to an action and says *how* that action happened. `～なくて` joins a non-event to a consequence and says *why* it happened. They are not stylistic variants, and swapping them usually produces a sentence that means nothing.

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Formation

Both start from the ない-form. The split is in the ending, and in what the word being negated is.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb → *without doing* | ない-form + で | <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わないで |
| Verb → *because not* | ない-form − い + くて | <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わなくて |
| Verb (する) | しないで／しなくて | <ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>しないで／しなくて |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないで／<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくて | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないで／<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくて |
| い-adjective | ～くない → ～くなくて (no `ないで`) | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くなくて |
| な-adjective | じゃない → じゃなくて (no `ないで`) | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>じゃなくて |
| Noun | じゃない → じゃなくて (no `ないで`) | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>じゃなくて |
| Written equivalent of `ないで` | ない-form − ない + ずに | <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わずに |

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `なくて` can negate **anything**; `ないで` only ever negates a **verb**. So if the word before the blank is an adjective or a noun, the answer is `なくて` before you have thought about meaning at all.

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Examples

<ruby>上着<rt>うわぎ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>着<rt>き</rt></ruby>ないで**<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>た。<br>*I went outside without putting a jacket on.*

<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくて**、<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>です。<br>*No reply has come, and I'm worried.*

<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>たないで**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っていてください。<br>*Please wait without leaving your seat.*

<ruby>意味<rt>いみ</rt></ruby>が**わからなくて**、<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*I didn't understand what it meant, so I asked the teacher.*

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べないで**<ruby>肉<rt>にく</rt></ruby>ばかり<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる。<br>*My son leaves the vegetables and eats nothing but meat.*

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>くなくて**、ベッドが<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>りませんでした。<br>*The room wasn't big enough, so the bed wouldn't fit.*

<ruby>原因<rt>げんいん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>**じゃなくて**、<ruby>工事<rt>こうじ</rt></ruby>でした。<br>*The cause wasn't the rain - it was the roadworks.*

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>かなくて**、<ruby>面接<rt>めんせつ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れてしまいました。<br>*The trains weren't running, and I ended up half an hour late for my interview.*

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>にも**<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>しないで**<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>辞<rt>や</rt></ruby>めたので、<ruby>親<rt>おや</rt></ruby>にひどく<ruby>怒<rt>おこ</rt></ruby>られました。<br>*I quit my job without talking to anyone about it, and my parents were furious.*

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Nuance & register

Neither form is marked for politeness. Both sit in the middle of the sentence, and the ending decides the register.

What `なくて` *is* marked for is **control**. Causal `なくて` is at its most natural when the thing that failed to happen was outside the speaker's hands - the bus, the weather, an email, your own memory or ability: <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>つからなくて</code>, <code><ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わなくて</code>, <code><ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくて</code>, <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>せなくて</code>. When the non-action was a choice you made, native speakers reach for `なかったので／なかったから` instead: <code><ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しなかったので、<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ちた</code> rather than ❌<code><ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しなくて、<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ちた</code>.

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Nuance & register (cont.)

The other half of that restriction is what comes **after** `なくて`. It wants a state, a feeling or an involuntary result - <ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>った, <ruby>寂<rt>さび</rt></ruby>しい, <ruby>安心<rt>あんしん</rt></ruby>した, よかった, <ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>だった. It does not want a deliberate next action, and it flatly cannot take a request, an invitation or an intention. That single fact resolves most of the exam questions on this pair.

Two frozen expressions are worth memorising whole, because you will use them constantly and both take `なくて`: <code><ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>られなくてすみません</code>「sorry I couldn't come」 and <code><ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きい<ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>にならなくてよかった</code>「thank goodness it wasn't a serious accident」.

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, `ないで` has a written twin, `ずに`, and `なくて` does not. In a formal report <code><ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>せずに</code> replaces <code><ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>しないで</code>, while a causal `なくて` in the same document would normally be rewritten as `ないため` - <code><ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>かないため、<ruby>再送<rt>さいそう</rt></ruby>いたします</code>.

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Manner vs cause

> **Rule of thumb** - ask what the second clause *is*. An action performed → `ないで`. A result suffered → `なくて`.

めがねを**かけないで**<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>した。<br>*I drove without wearing my glasses.*

めがねが**なくて**<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>できなかった。<br>*I didn't have my glasses, so I couldn't drive.*

In the first, the driving happened and the not-wearing describes the state I was in while doing it - one event, described two ways. In the second there are two facts in a chain: no glasses, therefore no driving. English blurs this because both come out as "without" or "didn't … so"; Japanese does not.

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Manner vs cause (cont.)

A useful test: if you can insert 「そのまま」 or 「その<ruby>状態<rt>じょうたい</rt></ruby>で」 before the main verb and the sentence still works, you want `ないで`.

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · What can follow each

> **Rule of thumb** - requests, commands, invitations and intentions can only follow `ないで`. `なくて` is followed by things that happen *to* you.

<ruby>砂糖<rt>さとう</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れないで**ください。<br>*Please don't put sugar in it.*

<ruby>砂糖<rt>さとう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>っていなくて**、<ruby>安心<rt>あんしん</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*There was no sugar in it, which was a relief.*

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · What can follow each (cont.)

There is no ❌<code><ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れなくてください</code>, no ❌<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなくて、<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ろう</code>, no ❌<code><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たなくて、<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めます</code>. If the sentence ends in ください, ましょう, つもり, たい or an imperative, the negative in front of it is `ないで`.

The mirror of that rule: an emotion word after the negative almost forces `なくて`. <ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れなくて<ruby>辛<rt>つら</rt></ruby>かった, <ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>えなくて<ruby>残念<rt>ざんねん</rt></ruby>です, <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>らなくてよかった.

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Adjectives and nouns: only なくて exists

> **Rule of thumb** - `ないで` is verb-only. Adjectives and nouns have exactly one negative て-form, and it is `なくて` / `でなくて`.

この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**じゃなくて**、<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>できません。<br>*This room isn't quiet, so I can't study.*

<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>**くなくて**、<ruby>味<rt>あじ</rt></ruby>もよかったです。<br>*It wasn't expensive, and it tasted good too.*

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Adjectives and nouns: only なくて exists (cont.)

Note the second sentence: with adjectives, `なくて` is not always causal. It can simply be the *and* of a list - "not A, and B" - exactly as the positive て-form links two descriptions. The same neutral linking shows up in corrections, where `じゃなくて` means "not A but B": <code><ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>じゃなくて、あさってです</code>.

For nouns the formal written variant is `ではなくて`, and in the stiffest registers `ではなく` with no `て` at all: <code><ruby>対象<rt>たいしょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>ではなく、<ruby>社会人<rt>しゃかいじん</rt></ruby>です</code>.

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Common mistakes

- ❌ ここに<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>か**なくて**ください。 → ✅ ここに<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>か**ないで**ください。<br>*A request can only follow `ないで`. This is the single most common error with the pair, and it survives well past N4 if it is not fixed early.*

- ❌ <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>る**くないで**、<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めない。 → ✅ <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>る**くなくて**、<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めない。<br>*<code><ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>るい</code> is an adjective, and adjectives have no `ないで` form at all.*

- ❌ <ruby>鍵<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>つから**ないで**、<ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>りました。 → ✅ <ruby>鍵<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>つから**なくて**、<ruby>困<rt>こま</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*Being stuck is a result, not an action performed while not finding the key. Causal chain → `なくて`.*

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>し**なくて**、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けた。 → ✅ <ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>し**なかったので**、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>負<rt>ま</rt></ruby>けた。<br>*The non-action was a choice. Causal `なくて` prefers things you could not help; for deliberate causes use `なかったので／なかったから`.*

- ❌ <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**なくて**<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。 → ✅ <ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**ないで**<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*Going to work is a deliberate act, and skipping breakfast is the manner of doing it, not its cause.*

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>くて、エアコンを<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>さ____<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てしまった。

{reveal}
エアコンを<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>さ**ないで**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てしまった。

{reveal}
Sleeping is something the speaker did, and leaving the air conditioner on describes the state they were in while doing it. That is manner, so `ないで`.

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 2

**2.** バスが<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>（ないで・なくて）、<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちました。

{reveal}
バスが<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>**なくて**、<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちました。

{reveal}
The waiting is a consequence the speaker suffered, and the bus not coming was outside their control - the textbook profile for causal `なくて`.

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Please don't decide anything without asking me."

{reveal}
<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>か**ないで**<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めないでください。

{reveal}
Two negatives, both `ないで`, and for different reasons: the first is *without asking* (manner), the second is the negative request. Neither slot can take `なくて`.

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くないで、あまり<ruby>人気<rt>にんき</rt></ruby>がない。

{reveal}
✅ この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>**くなくて**、あまり<ruby>人気<rt>にんき</rt></ruby>がない。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い</code> is an い-adjective. Only verbs form `ないで`; every other word class negates and joins with `なくて`, built from the `くない` form.

---

## ～ないで vs ～なくて · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>し**ないで**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りました。 (b) <ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>が**なくて**<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>しました。

{reveal}
(a) is about the speaker's own behaviour: they went home, and the going home was done without getting in touch first. The focus is on the manner of one action, and it carries a faint sense of something skipped that should have been done.

{reveal}
(b) is about what happened to the speaker as a result of someone else's silence. There are two linked facts - no contact, therefore worry - and the second half is a feeling rather than a deliberate act, which is exactly the environment causal `なくて` wants.

# ～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to

**Meaning** · you have to do it; there is no option of not doing it

This is the same double-negative machine you met at N5 in `～なくてはいけない`: *if you don't do it, that won't do*. What changes here is the conditional in front. `～なければ` is the **ば-conditional of ない**, and it is the tidier, more formal of the two ways in. `なければならない` is the wording of rules, contracts and careful written Japanese.

---

## ～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Formation

`ない` is an い-adjective, so it conditionalises exactly like one: <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>ければ, therefore ない → なければ. Build the ない-form, drop the final `い`, add `ければ`.

---

## ～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ない-form − い + ければ | <ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>わなければならない |
| Verb (る-verb) | ない-form − い + ければ | <ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける → <ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けなければならない |
| Verb (する) | しなければならない | <ruby>提出<rt>ていしゅつ</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>提出<rt>ていしゅつ</rt></ruby>しなければならない |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なければならない | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なければならない |
| い-adjective | ～くない → ～くなければ | <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しくなければならない |
| な-adjective | drop だ + でなければ | <ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>でなければならない |
| Noun | noun + でなければ | <ruby>本人<rt>ほんにん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>本人<rt>ほんにん</rt></ruby>でなければならない |
| Polite | いけません／なりません | <ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>わなければいけません |
| Past | いけなかった／ならなかった | <ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>わなければなりませんでした |

---

## ～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Casual contraction | なければ → なきゃ | <ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>わなきゃ |

> **Rule of thumb** - the ending `ならない` is a **verb**, so its polite form is `なりません`, never ❌`ならないです`. Same for `いけない` → `いけません`.

> **Note** - `なければ` on its own is just an ordinary conditional and has nothing to do with obligation: <code><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がなければ、また<ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>でいいですよ</code> 「if you haven't got time, another day is fine」. The obligation only appears when `いけない` or `ならない` closes the sentence.

---

## ～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Examples

もう**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なければなりません**。<br>*I have to leave now.*

<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>を**しなければいけない**。<br>*I have to do my homework.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>も<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なければなりません**か。<br>*Do I have to come in tomorrow as well?*

<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>みは<ruby>本人<rt>ほんにん</rt></ruby>**でなければなりません**。<br>*The application must be made by the person themselves.*

---

## ～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>健康<rt>けんこう</rt></ruby>のために、もっと<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べなければいけない**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っている。<br>*I know I ought to eat more vegetables for my health.*

<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>められた<ruby>場所<rt>ばしょ</rt></ruby>に**とめなければならない**。<br>*Bicycles must be parked in the designated area.*

<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>すので、<ruby>今週中<rt>こんしゅうちゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めなければいけません**。<br>*I'm moving next month, so I have to settle on a new flat this week.*

<ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>づいているので、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>に**しなければならない**かもしれません。<br>*A typhoon is on the way, so we may have to call tomorrow's match off.*

---

## Nuance & register

There is only one meaning in this family, and five common ways to say it. Put them on a single line and the choice becomes mechanical:

1. **～なければならない** - the most formal. Laws, contracts, manuals, academic writing, official announcements. Impersonal: it states a requirement that holds for anyone, not a chore facing one person. 2. **～なければいけません** - formal-polite speech and ordinary business writing. Still stiff, but it can be about you. 3. **～なくてはいけません** - the everyday polite form (this is the N5 branch). What you say to a colleague or in a classroom. 4. **～ないといけない** - the everyday **spoken** form, and the most frequent of all in conversation. 5. **～なきゃ／～なくちゃ** - casual, contracted, usually with the ending dropped entirely. What people actually say to themselves and to friends.

---

## Nuance & register (cont.)

Two axes are hiding inside that list. **`なければ` versus `なくては`** is a matter of tidiness: `なければ` is the written-feeling conditional, `なくては` the more conversational one. **`ならない` versus `いけない`** is a matter of who the requirement belongs to: `ならない` states a standing rule with no addressee, while `いけない` points at a situation and often at a person.

Because `なければならない` is impersonal, it is a **safe** thing to say upward. You cannot comfortably tell your boss <code><ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>しなければいけませんよ</code>, but you can report the rule - <code><ruby>規則<rt>きそく</rt></ruby>では<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>のサインが<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>です</code> - and let the rule do the pushing.

Do not use it about a mild preference. `なければならない` is heavy; if what you mean is "you'd be better off", the pattern is `～たほうがいい`, and reaching for the obligation form instead sounds alarming.

---

## Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, the polite negative-past forms come out longer than learners expect: <code><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たなければなりませんでした</code>「I had to wait」. There is no shortcut, and in speech people simply say <code><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たなきゃいけなかった</code>.

---

## ～なければ vs ～なくては

> **Rule of thumb** - identical meaning. `なければ` is the neater, more written conditional; `なくては` is a shade more conversational.

<ruby>会費<rt>かいひ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby>までに**<ruby>納<rt>おさ</rt></ruby>めなければなりません**。<br>*Membership fees must be paid by March.*

<ruby>会費<rt>かいひ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby>までに**<ruby>納<rt>おさ</rt></ruby>めなくてはいけませんね**。<br>*We've got to get the membership fee in by March, haven't we.*

The requirement is the same; the voice is not. The first could be printed on a form. The second is two people discussing the form.

---

## ～なければ vs ～なくては (cont.)

One structural difference worth knowing: `なくては` contracts to `なくちゃ` and `なければ` contracts to `なきゃ`, and the contractions do not cross. There is no ❌`なくきゃ`.

---

## いけない vs ならない

> **Rule of thumb** - `ならない` for a rule that stands on its own; `いけない` for something a particular person has to do about a particular situation.

<ruby>受験生<rt>じゅけんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>入室<rt>にゅうしつ</rt></ruby>し**なければならない**。<br>*Candidates must be seated by nine o'clock.*

あ、<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>か**なければいけない**んだった。<br>*Oh - I have to be there by nine.*

`ならない` is faceless, which is why it dominates in writing and why it sounds odd when someone uses it about their own small errands. `いけない` is the workhorse of spoken obligation above the casual line. In a `～んです` explanation, in a question, in an apology, `いけない` is almost always the one.

---

## ～なければならない vs ～たほうがいい

> **Rule of thumb** - obligation versus advice. `なければならない` says there is no alternative; `たほうがいい` says one alternative is better.

<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>は<ruby>食後<rt>しょくご</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まなければなりません**。<br>*The medicine must be taken after meals.*

<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>は<ruby>食後<rt>しょくご</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだほうがいい**ですよ。<br>*You're better off taking the medicine after meals.*

The first is the pharmacist reading the instruction; the second is a friend passing on a tip. Learners routinely over-use the obligation form because English "you have to" is so mild - but Japanese `なければならない` is not mild, and using it for a suggestion makes a recommendation sound like a regulation.

---

## Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**なければ**なりません。 → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>か**なければ**なりません。<br>*The pattern builds on the ない-form, never on the dictionary form: <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない</code> → <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなければ</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**だなければ**いけません。 → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**でなければ**いけません。<br>*Nouns and な-adjectives negate through `で(は)ない`, so the conditional is `でなければ`.*

- ❌ <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>がなければ**ならないです**。 → ✅ <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>がなければ**なりません**。<br>*`ならない` is the negative of the verb `なる`. Verbs do not take `です` to become polite.*

- ❌ <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>る**いなければ**いけません。 → ✅ <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>明<rt>あか</rt></ruby>る**くなければ**いけません。<br>*い-adjectives negate to `くない` first, and only then take `ければ`.*

---

## Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なければならな**くないです**。（intending "you don't have to come"）→ ✅ <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>**なくてもいいです**。<br>*You cannot negate the obligation form to release someone from it. The opposite of `なければならない` is a different pattern: `なくてもいい`.*

---

## ～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Practice 1

**1.** パスポートの<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>六<rt>ろく</rt></ruby>か<ruby>月<rt>げつ</rt></ruby><ruby>以内<rt>いない</rt></ruby>のもの____なりません。

{reveal}
<ruby>六<rt>ろく</rt></ruby>か<ruby>月<rt>げつ</rt></ruby><ruby>以内<rt>いない</rt></ruby>のもの**でなければ**なりません。

{reveal}
The word being required is a noun (もの), so the negative conditional is `でなければ`. `ならない` fits because this is a printed regulation rather than one person's errand.

---

## ～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなってきたから、そろそろコートを（<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さなければなりません・<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>したほうがいいです）ね。

{reveal}
そろそろコートを**<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>したほうがいい**ですね。

{reveal}
Nothing is forcing the speaker to dig the coat out - it is simply a good idea. `なければなりません` would turn a remark about the weather into a rule, which is much heavier than the English "you should" that prompted it.

---

## ～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese, as a line in a company handbook: "Employees must report any accident on the same day."

{reveal}
<ruby>社員<rt>しゃいん</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>事故<rt>じこ</rt></ruby>があった<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>、<ruby>当日<rt>とうじつ</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>報告<rt>ほうこく</rt></ruby>し**なければならない**。

{reveal}
A handbook is exactly where `なければならない` belongs: plain form, no addressee, a requirement stated as holding for a whole class of people. Using `なきゃいけない` here would be jarringly conversational.

---

## ～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>までにこの<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらせなきゃなりません。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>までにこの<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらせ**なければなりません**。 （casual: <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらせ**なきゃ**。）

{reveal}
`なきゃ` is the contracted, casual form of `なければ`, and gluing the formal tail `なりません` onto it mixes two registers inside one word. Pick one level and stay there.

---

## ～なければいけない・～なければならない - must; have to · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ま**なければならない**。 (b) <ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ま**なければいけない**。

{reveal}
(a) reads as the requirement itself, quoted. It has no addressee and no speaker feeling in it, so it fits a notice, a set of guidelines, or someone reciting what the rules say.

{reveal}
(b) attaches the same requirement to a situation and usually to a person: the speaker, or whoever they are talking to, has this to deal with. Add `ね` or `んです` and it becomes unmistakably conversational - which you cannot really do to (a).

# ～ないといけない

**Meaning** · have to; must - the version people actually say out loud

Third conditional, same machine. `～なくては` and `～なければ` build obligation out of *if you don't … that won't do*; `～ないと` does the same with the `と` conditional. What makes it worth a page of its own is frequency: in conversation this form outnumbers the textbook ones, and it has a trick the others do not - it can stop in the middle and still be a complete sentence.

---

## ～ないといけない · Formation

Take the ない-form and add `と`. Then close with `いけない`, `だめだ`, or nothing at all.

---

## ～ないといけない · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ない-form + といけない | <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す → <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さないといけない |
| Verb (る-verb) | ない-form + といけない | <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べる → <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べないといけない |
| Verb (する) | しないといけない | <ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>しないといけない |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないといけない | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないといけない |
| い-adjective | ～くないと + いけない | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くないといけない |
| な-adjective / Noun | ～でないと + いけない | <ruby>会員<rt>かいいん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>会員<rt>かいいん</rt></ruby>でないといけない |
| Polite | いけません | <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さないといけません |
| Past | いけなかった／いけませんでした | <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さないといけませんでした |
| Blunter casual | といけない → とだめ | <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さないとだめ |

---

## ～ないといけない · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Ending dropped | ～ないと (＋ ね／な) | <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さないと。／<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さないとね。 |

> **Rule of thumb** - the verb in front of `と` must be **negative**. <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないといけない</code> is "I have to go"; <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くといけない</code> is a different sentence altogether - "it would be bad if I went."

---

## ～ないといけない · Examples

そろそろ**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>らないといけない**。<br>*I have to head home soon.*

<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないといけません**。<br>*I have to go to the bank.*

あ、ごみを**<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さないと**。<br>*Oh - I need to put the rubbish out.*

<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さくなったので、<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいのを**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>わないといけない**。<br>*My shoes have got too small, so I have to buy new ones.*

---

## ～ないといけない · Examples (cont.)

このプールは<ruby>会員<rt>かいいん</rt></ruby>**でないといけない**んですか。<br>*Do you have to be a member to use this pool?*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもが<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>したので、<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>迎<rt>むか</rt></ruby>えに**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないといけなくなりました**。<br>*My child has come down with a fever, so now I have to go and collect them early.*

<ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>までにこの<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わら**ないといけない**んです。<br>*The thing is, I have to finish this book by the weekend.*

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に、ホテルもチケットも<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>し**ないといけません**。<br>*Before next week's business trip I have to book the hotel and the tickets myself.*

---

## ～ないといけない · Nuance & register

`～ないといけない` sits in the middle of the obligation ladder and leans casual. In plain form it is what friends, family and colleagues use with each other all day. In `いけません` it is perfectly usable at work, though it still sounds like speech rather than writing - a report or a notice would say `なければならない`.

The ending falls off constantly. <code><ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>がないと</code>, <code><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>わないとね</code>, <code>そろそろ<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ないとな</code> are not fragments; the trailing `と` carries the whole meaning, and adding `いけない` would sound oddly complete. Learners consistently under-produce this and it is one of the quickest wins in sounding natural. `ね` invites agreement, `な` is a masculine-leaning mutter to oneself.

---

## ～ないといけない · Nuance & register (cont.)

`～ないとだめ` swaps a softer ending for a blunter one. `だめ` is flat and physical - "no good" - so <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さないとだめだよ</code> presses harder than <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さないといけないよ</code>. Said to a child or a close friend it is normal; said to a colleague it can sting.

Two things to file away. First, `ならない` almost never follows `と`: you will occasionally hear `～ないとならない`, but it is uncommon and `いけない` is the safe choice. Second, in Kansai the whole family is replaced by `～なあかん` (<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなあかん</code>, `せなあかん`), which you will hear constantly in the west and in comedy, and which no textbook prepares you for.

---

## ～ないといけない · Nuance & register (cont.)

As with every obligation form, aiming it at someone above you is strong. Reporting your own obligation is always safe; telling your boss <code><ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>しないといけませんよ</code> is not.

---

## ～ないといけない · ～ないといけない vs ～なければならない

> **Rule of thumb** - same requirement, different room. `ないと` belongs in the kitchen; `なければならない` belongs on the noticeboard.

<ruby>申込書<rt>もうしこみしょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>窓口<rt>まどぐち</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>提出<rt>ていしゅつ</rt></ruby>しなければならない**。<br>*Application forms must be submitted at the counter.*

<ruby>申込書<rt>もうしこみしょ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>窓口<rt>まどぐち</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さないといけないんだって**。<br>*Apparently you have to hand the form in at the counter.*

The information is identical. The first is impersonal and could be printed; the second is one person relaying it to another, complete with `んだ` and hearsay `って`. Mixing them up is not a grammar error, it is a costume error - and it is just as noticeable.

---

## ～ないといけない · ～ないと, and stopping there

> **Rule of thumb** - a bare `～ないと` at the end of an utterance is a finished sentence, not a broken one. The unspoken `いけない` is understood.

やばい、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る。**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>らないと**。<br>*Uh-oh, the train's coming. We've got to run.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>だから、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ないとね**。<br>*I've got an exam tomorrow, so I'd better get some sleep.*

This works because Japanese lets a conditional trail off when the consequence is obvious, and "that would be bad" is always obvious. The same trick is available to `なきゃ` and `なくちゃ`, which is why the casual end of this family so often sounds unfinished to a learner's ear. It is not.

---

## Obligation ～ないと vs the plain conditional ～ないと

> **Rule of thumb** - if a consequence follows, `と` is just "if". Obligation only appears when the consequence is `いけない`/`だめ`, or is left unsaid.

<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ないと**、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わないよ。<br>*If we don't leave soon we won't make the train.*

<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ないと**。<br>*We've got to get going.*

Same five words, two different sentences. In the first, `と` behaves exactly as it does in any conditional - the second half spells out what goes wrong. In the second there is no second half, so the listener supplies the standard one and the whole thing reads as obligation.

---

## Obligation ～ないと vs the plain conditional ～ないと (cont.)

This is also why `ないと` cannot be used for a rule stated in the abstract. A conditional needs a situation; a regulation with no situation attached wants `なければならない`.

---

## ～ないといけない · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きるといけない**。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きないといけない**。<br>*The obligation lives in the negative. <code><ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きるといけない</code> means "it would be a problem if I got up", which is the opposite of the intended sentence.*

- ❌ <ruby>三人以上<rt>さんにんいじょう</rt></ruby>**だといけません**。（intending "there must be three or more people"）→ ✅ <ruby>三人以上<rt>さんにんいじょう</rt></ruby>**でないといけません**。<br>*Nouns negate with `でない` before `と`. `だと` is the affirmative conditional and flips the meaning.*

---

## ～ないといけない · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たないといけない**でした**。 → ✅ <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たないといけません**でした**。／<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たないといけなかった。<br>*`いけない` is a negative predicate in its own right; make it past by conjugating it (`いけなかった`) or polite-past (`いけませんでした`), not by hanging `でした` on the plain form.*

- ❌ <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>がないと**ならないです**。 → ✅ <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>がないと**いけません**。／<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>が**なければなりません**。<br>*`ならない` is at home after `なければ`/`なくては`, not after `と`, and it never takes `です`.*

- ❌（in a written report）<ruby>至急<rt>しきゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>対応<rt>たいおう</rt></ruby>し**ないといけない**。 → ✅ <ruby>至急<rt>しきゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>対応<rt>たいおう</rt></ruby>し**なければならない**。<br>*Not ungrammatical, just spoken. In documents the `と` branch of the family reads as if someone forgot they were writing.*

---

## ～ないといけない · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>る。だから<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>き____いけない。

{reveal}
<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>き**ないと**いけない。

{reveal}
The obligation form needs the negative in front of `と`: <code><ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きない</code> + `と` + `いけない`. The literal logic is "if I don't get up at five, that's no good."

---

## ～ないといけない · Practice 2

**2.** （<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>らないと・<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ると）<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わないよ。

{reveal}
**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>らないと**<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わないよ。

{reveal}
Here `と` is doing ordinary conditional work, and the two halves have to line up: not running leads to not making it. <code><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ると<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わない</code> would claim that running is what makes you late.

## ～ないといけない · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese, casually, as something you would mutter on remembering it: "Ah, I have to answer that email."

{reveal}
あ、メールの<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>、**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>かないと**。

{reveal}
The trailing `ないと` with the ending left off is the natural shape for remembering a task out loud. <code><ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>かないといけない</code> is fine too, but the fuller form sounds like reporting the obligation rather than reacting to it.

## ～ないといけない · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: パスポートを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>せるといけませんか。

{reveal}
✅ パスポートを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>せ**ないといけません**か。

{reveal}
Without the negative, the sentence asks whether showing your passport would cause a problem. The obligation reading requires <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>せない</code> before `と`.

## ～ないといけない · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>け**ないといけないよ**。 (b) <ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>け**ないとだめだよ**。

{reveal}
(a) states the requirement and lets `よ` add a nudge. It is the ordinary spoken way to point something out, and it can be said sympathetically - the speaker may well be on the listener's side about the chore.

{reveal}
(b) replaces the neutral ending with `だめ`, which is flat and unarguable. It is what a parent says to a child or one close friend says to another, and it carries disapproval that (a) does not. To a colleague it would sound like being told off.

# ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ

**Meaning** · gotta - the same obligation, squeezed down for speech

These are not new grammar. `なくちゃ` is `なくては` with the syllables worn off, and `なきゃ` is `なければ` with the same treatment. What they add is register: they are what obligation sounds like in an actual conversation, and they usually appear with the `いけない`/`ならない` tail chopped off entirely. Learners are blindsided by `なきゃ` because no textbook sentence ever contains it and every real one does.

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Formation

Japanese contracts `ては` to `ちゃ` everywhere (<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ては → <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ちゃ), and `ければ` to `きゃ`. Apply that to the two obligation conditionals and you get these.

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ない-form → なくちゃ／なきゃ | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなくちゃ／<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなきゃ |
| Verb (る-verb) | ない-form → なくちゃ／なきゃ | <ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きる → <ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きなくちゃ／<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きなきゃ |
| Verb (する) | しなくちゃ／しなきゃ | <ruby>洗濯<rt>せんたく</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>洗濯<rt>せんたく</rt></ruby>しなきゃ |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくちゃ／<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なきゃ | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なきゃ |
| い-adjective | ～くなくちゃ／～くなきゃ | <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しくなきゃ |
| な-adjective / Noun | ～じゃなくちゃ／～じゃなきゃ | <ruby>無料<rt>むりょう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>無料<rt>むりょう</rt></ruby>じゃなきゃ |
| With the tail kept | ＋ いけない／だめ | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなきゃいけない／<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなきゃだめ |
| With the tail dropped | nothing after it | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなきゃ。／<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなくちゃ。 |
| Emphatic variant | なくちゃ → なくっちゃ | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなくっちゃ |

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the two contractions come from different parents and do not mix. `なくては` → **なくちゃ**; `なければ` → **なきゃ**. There is no ❌`なくきゃ` and no ❌`なけちゃ`.

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Examples

もう**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>なきゃ**。<br>*I've got to go to bed.*

あ、<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby><ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>さ**なきゃ**。<br>*Oh - I need to turn the light off.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>いから、そろそろ**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>らなくちゃ**。<br>*I've got an early start, so I should be heading home.*

<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらせなきゃいけない**んだ。<br>*The thing is, I've got to get the homework finished today.*

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>空<rt>から</rt></ruby>っぽだ。<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなきゃ**。<br>*The fridge is empty. I need to go shopping.*

<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>って**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなくちゃだめだよ**。<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>から<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>だって。<br>*You've got to take an umbrella - apparently it's raining from the afternoon.*

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちの<ruby>結婚式<rt>けっこんしき</rt></ruby>だから、<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>わなきゃ**いけないなあ。<br>*My friend's wedding is next week, so I'm going to have to buy something new to wear.*

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>がこんなに<ruby>散<rt>ち</rt></ruby>らかっているのに、**<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けなくちゃ**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>うだけで<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>もしていない。<br>*The room is in this state and all I do is think I ought to tidy it, without ever actually starting.*

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Nuance & register

Casual. That is the whole point, and it is also the whole restriction. `なくちゃ` and `なきゃ` belong in speech among friends, family and close colleagues, and in writing only where writing imitates speech - messages, chat, blogs, dialogue in manga and novels. They do not appear in reports, emails to clients, exam answers or announcements.

They are **not** sloppy or uneducated, and they are not gender-marked: adults of every age and both sexes use them constantly. What they are is *unguarded*. Saying <code><ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>らなきゃ</code> lets the listener see you making a decision, which is exactly right with a friend and wrong in front of a customer.

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Nuance & register (cont.)

`なきゃ` is the more frequent of the two in everyday Tokyo speech. `なくちゃ` is a touch lighter and rounder, and it turns up often when someone is talking to a child or being deliberately breezy; a few speakers hear it as slightly cuter than `なきゃ`, but nobody would call it a different level of politeness. The stretched `なくっちゃ` adds emphasis - <code>これは<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>なくっちゃ</code>「this I've *got* to see」.

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Nuance & register (cont.)

The tail is normally dropped. When it is kept, it stays in plain form: `なきゃいけない`, `なきゃだめ`, and in very casual Tokyo speech `なきゃなんない` (from `ならない`). Attaching a polite tail is the classic learner error - you cannot half-contract a sentence, and ❌<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなきゃいけません</code> mixes two registers inside one predicate. If you need politeness, expand the whole thing back out to `なければなりません`.

One more thing that catches people out in listening: because the ending is missing, these forms often end an utterance on a rising or trailing intonation and sound unfinished. <code>あ、<ruby>洗濯<rt>せんたく</rt></ruby>しなきゃ…</code> is a complete thought. Do not wait for the rest of it.

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · ～なきゃ vs ～なくちゃ

> **Rule of thumb** - the same word twice over. Choose by ear, not by meaning; `なきゃ` is the more common default.

そろそろ**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なきゃ**。<br>*I'd better get going.*

そろそろ**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>なくちゃ**。<br>*I'd better get going.*

There is no difference of meaning, obligation strength or politeness to report, and a page that invented one would be lying. The only observable tendencies are that `なきゃ` is heard more often overall, `なくちゃ` sounds slightly softer and is common in speech directed at children, and only `なくちゃ` has the emphatic stretched form `なくっちゃ`.

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · ～なきゃ vs ～ないと

> **Rule of thumb** - both are casual spoken obligation. `ないと` is neutral and usable slightly further up the politeness scale; `なきゃ` is more informal and more self-directed.

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ら**ないと**いけませんね。<br>*We'll need to put the meeting materials together, won't we.*

うわ、<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby><ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ら**なきゃ**。<br>*Ugh - I've got to make the handouts.*

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · ～なきゃ vs ～ないと (cont.)

`ないと` survives being made polite: <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないといけません</code> is fine at work. `なきゃ` does not. And `なきゃ` leans towards things the speaker is telling *themselves*, which is why it so often follows あ or うわ and precedes nothing at all.

---

## ～なきゃ as obligation vs ～なきゃ as plain "unless"

> **Rule of thumb** - `なきゃ` is just contracted `なければ`. If a consequence follows it, it is an ordinary conditional and has nothing to do with obligation.

<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>なきゃ**わからないよ。<br>*You won't know unless you look for yourself.*

<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>なきゃ**。<br>*I've got to go and look for myself.*

The first is "if you don't look, you won't understand" - the `いけない` is not implied and never was. The second has nothing after it, so the listener fills in the missing "that won't do" and hears an obligation.

---

## ～なきゃ as obligation vs ～なきゃ as plain "unless" (cont.)

The same holds for `なくちゃ`, and it explains a shape you will meet often: <code><ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くなきゃ<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>わない</code>「I won't buy it unless it's cheap」, <code><ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>じゃなきゃだめだ</code>「it has to be you」.

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>か**なきゃです**。 → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>か**なきゃ**。／<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>か**なければなりません**。<br>*There is no polite version of a contraction. `です` cannot be bolted onto `なきゃ`; if you need politeness, expand the form.*

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**なきゃ** → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>か**なきゃ**<br>*The contraction still starts from the ない-form. Build <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない</code> first, then squeeze.*

- ❌ <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>が**なくきゃ** → ✅ <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>が**なくちゃ**／<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>が**なきゃ**<br>*The two contractions have different parents and cannot be blended. `なくては`→`なくちゃ`, `なければ`→`なきゃ`.*

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>会員<rt>かいいん</rt></ruby>**だなきゃ**だめです。 → ✅ <ruby>会員<rt>かいいん</rt></ruby>**じゃなきゃ**だめだよ。<br>*Nouns negate to `じゃない`, so the contracted conditional is `じゃなきゃ`. Note the ending changed too - `だめです` after `じゃなきゃ` is a register mismatch.*

- ❌（in an email to a client）<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ら**なきゃ**いけません。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ら**なければなりません**。<br>*Contractions do not belong in written business Japanese, however polite the tail you attach to them.*

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 1

**1.** やばい、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>っちゃう。<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ら____。

{reveal}
<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ら**なきゃ**。（also fine: <ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>ら**なくちゃ**。）

{reveal}
The situation is urgent, spoken and self-directed, which is the natural home of the contracted forms with the ending dropped. Anything longer would sound like the speaker had time to think.

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に（<ruby>報告<rt>ほうこく</rt></ruby>しなきゃいけません・<ruby>報告<rt>ほうこく</rt></ruby>しなければなりません）。

{reveal}
<ruby>報告<rt>ほうこく</rt></ruby>し**なければなりません**。

{reveal}
`なきゃ` is a casual contraction and `いけません` is a polite ending; putting them in one predicate mixes registers inside a single word. Pick one level - fully contracted for speech among friends, fully expanded when you are being polite.

---

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese, as something you say to yourself on remembering it: "Oh no, I have to return the library books today."

{reveal}
あ、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby><ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby><ruby>返<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>さ**なきゃ**。

{reveal}
Muttered self-obligation drops the `いけない` and drops the particles too. The interjection at the front is part of the pattern: these forms almost always follow a moment of realisation.

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: この<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>、<ruby>絶対<rt>ぜったい</rt></ruby><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>るなきゃ。

{reveal}
✅ この<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>、<ruby>絶対<rt>ぜったい</rt></ruby><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>**なきゃ**。（emphatic: <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>**なくっちゃ**。）

{reveal}
`なきゃ` replaces `なければ`, which is built on the ない-form - <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ない</code> → <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>なければ</code> → <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>なきゃ</code>. The dictionary form never appears in front of it.

## ～なくちゃ・～なきゃ · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>しなきゃ**<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>がなくなるよ。 (b) <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>しなきゃ**。

{reveal}
(a) is not an obligation sentence at all. `なきゃ` here is simply contracted `なければ` working as an ordinary conditional, and the second half states the consequence: book early, or the seats will be gone.

{reveal}
(b) leaves the consequence out. Because the missing half is understood to be "that won't do", the whole utterance is heard as the speaker realising they have to act. The contraction is identical in both; what decides the reading is whether anything follows it.

# ～なくてもいい

**Meaning** · you don't have to; it's fine not to

`～なくてもいい` is the exact negative counterpart of `～てもいい`. Same shape, same `も` of concession, same verdict `いい` - only the thing being conceded is now a *non*-action: "even if you don't do it, that's fine." It does not tell anyone to stop. It removes a requirement and leaves the choice open.

---

## ～なくてもいい · Formation

Negate first, swap the final `い` of `ない` for `くても`, then finish with `いい`.

---

## ～なくてもいい · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ない-form − い + くてもいい | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ → <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たなくてもいい |
| Verb (る-verb) | ない-form − い + くてもいい | <ruby>着<rt>き</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>着<rt>き</rt></ruby>なくてもいい |
| Verb (する) | しなくてもいい | <ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>しなくてもいい |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいい | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいい |
| い-adjective | ～くない → ～くなくてもいい | <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しくなくてもいい |
| な-adjective | drop だ + でなくてもいい | <ruby>完璧<rt>かんぺき</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>完璧<rt>かんぺき</rt></ruby>でなくてもいい |
| Noun | noun + でなくてもいい | スーツ → スーツでなくてもいい |
| Polite | いい → いいです | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たなくてもいいです |
| Asking | ～なくてもいいですか | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たなくてもいいですか |

---

## ～なくてもいい · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Softer / politer | いい → かまいません, <ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>です | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たなくてもかまいません |
| Casual (も dropped) | ～なくていい | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たなくていいよ |

> **Rule of thumb** - the particle carries the whole meaning. `～なくて**は**いけない` = must; `～なくて**も**いい` = needn't. Everything else in the two patterns is shared scaffolding.

---

## ～なくてもいい · Examples

<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>脱<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>がなくてもいいです**。<br>*You don't have to take your shoes off.*

<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>が**なくてもいい**よ。<br>*There's no need to rush.*

<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>かなくてもいいです**か。<br>*Is it all right if I don't write back?*

<ruby>服装<rt>ふくそう</rt></ruby>はスーツ**でなくてもいい**そうです。<br>*I hear it doesn't have to be a suit.*

---

## ～なくてもいい · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>広<rt>ひろ</rt></ruby>くなくてもいい**けど、<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>いほうがいい。<br>*The flat doesn't have to be big, but I'd rather it were near a station.*

そんなに**<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>しなくてもいい**んじゃない？<br>*Don't you think you're worrying more than you need to?*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったので、<ruby>残業<rt>ざんぎょう</rt></ruby>は**しなくてもいい**ことになりました。<br>*Work finished early today, so it turns out we don't have to stay late.*

レポートは<ruby>手書<rt>てが</rt></ruby>きでも<ruby>印刷<rt>いんさつ</rt></ruby>でもよくて、<ruby>表紙<rt>ひょうし</rt></ruby>は**つけなくてもかまいません**。<br>*The report may be handwritten or printed, and you needn't add a cover page.*

---

## ～なくてもいい · Nuance & register

The pattern itself is neutral; `いいです` makes it polite in form. What is not neutral is the **social act**. Saying "you don't have to" means you are in a position to lift the requirement, and that is a position of authority. Between equals it is generous. Aimed upward it is presumptuous: telling your manager <code><ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいいですよ</code> sounds like you are giving them permission to skip work.

Upward, the same content goes through different wording: <code>ご<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>なさらないでください</code>, <code>お<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>になさらないでください</code>, <code>そのままで<ruby>結構<rt>けっこう</rt></ruby>です</code>. Note that <code><ruby>結構<rt>けっこう</rt></ruby>です</code> is genuinely ambiguous - it can mean "that's fine as it is" or "no thank you" - and tone and context are the only things separating them.

---

## ～なくてもいい · Nuance & register (cont.)

In speech the `も` disappears constantly: <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなくていいよ</code>, <code><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>たなくていいから</code>. This is not a different pattern and carries no change in meaning; it is simply what fast Japanese does to the phrase. Learners who have only seen `なくてもいい` in a textbook often fail to recognise `なくていい` in conversation. The more emphatic casual variant is `なくたっていい` (<code><ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>らなくたっていいよ</code>).

Three neighbours worth knowing, in rising order of formality: <code>なくても<ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby></code> (warm, spoken), `なくてもかまいません`「it doesn't matter if you don't」(neutral, business), <code>なくても<ruby>結構<rt>けっこう</rt></ruby>です</code>「it is not required」(formal, institutional). `かまわない` is the one to reach for when you want to sound like you truly do not mind.

---

## ～なくてもいい · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, the pattern is not only about other people. `～なくてもいい` about yourself reports relief or a decision: <code><ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きなくてもいいから、<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>よう</code>.

---

## ～なくてもいい · ～なくてもいい vs ～てもいい

> **Rule of thumb** - `～てもいい` opens a door: you *may* do it. `～なくてもいい` closes a duty: you *needn't* do it. Neither one pushes you in either direction.

<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>ってもいい**ですか。<br>*Is it okay if I take photos?*

<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>らなくてもいい**ですか。<br>*Is it okay if I don't take photos?*

The first asks whether the action is permitted; the second asks whether the action is required. In a museum you would ask the first; on a job where photographing is part of your task, you would ask the second.

---

## ～なくてもいい · ～なくてもいい vs ～てもいい (cont.)

They pair up into a neat square with the obligation forms. `てもいい` may, `てはいけない` must not, `なくてはいけない` must, `なくてもいい` needn't. Permission and prohibition are built on the て-form; obligation and exemption on the negative stem.

---

## ～なくてもいい · ～なくてもいい vs ～ないでください

> **Rule of thumb** - "you don't have to" is not "please don't". `なくてもいい` leaves the action available; `ないでください` takes it away.

<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>わ**なくてもいいですよ**。<br>*You don't have to help.*

<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>わ**ないでください**。<br>*Please don't help.*

---

## ～なくてもいい · ～なくてもいい vs ～ないでください (cont.)

English blurs these - "you don't have to help" is often *meant* as a polite refusal - but Japanese does not. The first sentence releases someone who was offering; the second turns them away, and would be startling from a colleague. When you want to decline an offer kindly, the natural wording is <code><ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>です、ありがとうございます</code>, not either of these.

---

## ～なくてもいい vs ～なくてもかまいません・～なくても大丈夫

> **Rule of thumb** - same exemption, three temperatures. <code><ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby></code> is warm, `いい` is neutral, `かまいません` is businesslike.

<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>に<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby>**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べなくても<ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>だよ**。<br>*Honestly, you don't have to finish it all.*

<ruby>当日<rt>とうじつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>印鑑<rt>いんかん</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちにならなくてもかまいません**。<br>*You do not need to bring a personal seal on the day.*

---

## ～なくてもいい vs ～なくてもかまいません・～なくても大丈夫 (cont.)

<code><ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby></code> says *don't worry about it* and belongs in speech between people who know each other. `かまいません` says *it makes no difference to us* and belongs in counters, forms and customer service. Plain `いい` sits between them and is never wrong, which is why it is the form to learn first.

---

## ～なくてもいい · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>わ**なくてはいい**です。 → ✅ <ruby>払<rt>はら</rt></ruby>わ**なくても**いいです。<br>*`なくては` pairs with `いけない`; `なくても` pairs with `いい`. Mixing them produces a pattern that does not exist.*

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**だなくても**いいです。 → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**でなくても**いいです。<br>*Nouns and な-adjectives negate through `で(は)ない`, so the join is `でなくても`.*

- ❌ <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい**じゃなくても**いい。 → ✅ <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>し**くなくても**いい。<br>*<code><ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい</code> is an い-adjective, so its negative is `くない` and the join is `くなくても`.*

---

## ～なくてもいい · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌（meaning "you don't have to come tomorrow"）<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>**ないでください**。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>**なくてもいいですよ**。<br>*Straight from English. The `ないでください` version instructs the person to stay away, which is a completely different message.*

- ❌ <ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>出席<rt>しゅっせき</rt></ruby>し**なくてもいいですよ**。 → ✅ <ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>、ご<ruby>都合<rt>つごう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>ければ、ご<ruby>欠席<rt>けっせき</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>ございません。<br>*Releasing someone from a duty implies you had the authority to impose it. Upward, state the situation instead of granting an exemption.*

---

## ～なくてもいい · Practice 1

**1.** このレストランは<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>し____いいですよ。いつも<ruby>空<rt>す</rt></ruby>いていますから。

{reveal}
<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>し**なくても**いいですよ。

{reveal}
The restaurant is always empty, so booking is not required - the exemption reading. Note the particle: `なくても` with `いい`, never `なくては`.

---

## ～なくてもいい · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>のとき、ネクタイを（しなくてもいいです・しないでください）。

{reveal}
Either is grammatical, but they say different things. **しなくてもいいです** means a tie is optional; **しないでください** instructs people not to wear one. For a normal dress-code announcement the first is what is meant.

---

## ～なくてもいい · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese, politely: "You don't need to bring anything."

{reveal}
<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>って<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいいですよ**。 （more formal: <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も**お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちいただかなくてもかまいません**。）

{reveal}
The `よ` matters: without it the sentence can read as a flat instruction, whereas telling a guest not to trouble themselves should sound warm. <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る</code> takes the irregular negative stem <code><ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby></code>.

---

## ～なくてもいい · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>のパーティーは<ruby>正式<rt>せいしき</rt></ruby>な<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>だなくてもいいです。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>のパーティーは<ruby>正式<rt>せいしき</rt></ruby>な<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>**でなくてもいいです**。

{reveal}
`だ` never survives in front of a negative join. A noun negates as <code><ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>ではない</code>, and the exemption form built on that is <code><ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>でなくてもいい</code>.

---

## ～なくてもいい · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ま**なくてもいいです**。 (b) <ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ま**なくてはいけません**。

{reveal}
(a) removes a requirement: reading all of it is optional, and the listener may read as much or as little as they like. One particle - `も` - is doing that work.

{reveal}
(b) imposes one: all of it has to be read, and there is no choice in the matter. The `は` closes off the alternative that `も` opens. Because the two sentences differ by a single kana in the middle of a long predicate, they are easy to mishear and worth saying aloud a few times.

# ～必要がある

**Meaning** · there is a need to do it; it is necessary to do it

Read <ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>. <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> is a noun meaning *necessity*, and `ある` is the ordinary verb of existence - so the pattern literally says *a need to do X exists*. That is why it feels cooler than the obligation forms: it does not push anybody, it states a fact about the situation.

---

## ～必要がある · Formation

The verb in front stays in the **dictionary form**. Nouns join with `の`.

---

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|

| Verb (する) | dictionary form + <ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある | <ruby>登録<rt>とうろく</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>登録<rt>とうろく</rt></ruby>する<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある |

| Polite | <ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります／ありません | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります |
| Past | <ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があった／なかった | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>はなかった |
| Question | <ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がありますか | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がありますか |
| Formal negative | <ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>はございません | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>はございません |
| い-adjective / な-adjective | not available | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ → ❌<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かな<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある |

---

## ～必要がある · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the negative flips the particle. Affirmative <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>が**ある**</code>, negative <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>は**ない**</code>. The `は` is doing what `は` always does with a negative: marking the thing being denied. <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がない</code> is possible, but `は` is the everyday choice.

> **Note** - the negation goes on `ある`, never on the verb in front. "You don't need to go" is <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>はない</code>, not ❌<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある</code>, which would mean something like "there is a need for you not to go."

## ～必要がある · Examples

## ～必要がある · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby><ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>った<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>なので、<ruby>数字<rt>すうじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいものに<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>す**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります**。<br>*These materials are from last year, so the figures need updating.*

<ruby>会員<rt>かいいん</rt></ruby>カードをお<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちの<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>住所<rt>じゅうしょ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>はございません**。<br>*Members do not need to write their address at reception.*

---

## ～必要がある · Nuance & register

<code>～<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある</code> is the **objective** member of the family. It describes a requirement as a property of the situation, with nobody's authority behind it, which makes it the default in manuals, instructions, application guidance, business email and academic writing. If you have ever read a Japanese form, you have read this pattern.

That neutrality has a practical use: it lets you tell someone what has to happen without telling *them* what to do. <code>ご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>いただく<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がございます</code> is still direct, but <code><ruby>三月<rt>さんがつ</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>提出<rt>ていしゅつ</rt></ruby>する<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります</code> said about a shared deadline points at the deadline, not at the listener.

---

## ～必要がある · Nuance & register (cont.)

The flip side is that it sounds stiff about small personal errands. Nobody says <code><ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>をする<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある</code> to a friend - that is <code><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないと</code> territory. Using the formal pattern for a trip to the supermarket sounds like reading from a report.

The negative is the half you will use most, and it is unusually warm for such a formal pattern. <code><ruby>謝<rt>あやま</rt></ruby>る<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>はないよ</code>「you don't need to apologise」 and <code><ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>にする<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>はありません</code>「there's no need to worry about it」 are everyday reassurances. In casual speech `が` drops out altogether: <code>そんなに<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぐ<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ない</code>.

---

## ～必要がある · Nuance & register (cont.)

At the formal end, `ある` is replaced by its humble-polite equivalents: <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がございます</code> and <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>はございません</code>, which is what shop staff and official notices use.

---

## ～必要がある · ～必要がある vs ～なければならない

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある</code> reports that a need exists; `なければならない` says the alternative is unacceptable. The first describes, the second compels.

この<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>は<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります**。<br>*This medicine needs to be taken every day.*

この<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>は<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby>**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まなければなりません**。<br>*You must take this medicine every day.*

---

## ～必要がある · ～必要がある vs ～なければならない (cont.)

Both are correct and a doctor might say either. The first sounds like an explanation of how the treatment works; the second sounds like an instruction being issued to you. When the requirement comes from a rule with force behind it - a law, a contract, a school regulation - `なければならない` is the stronger fit. When it comes from the nature of the task, <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある</code> is.

There is also a grammatical difference worth noticing: `なければならない` attaches to a *negative* stem, while <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある</code> attaches to a plain dictionary form. That makes the second much easier to build under time pressure.

---

## ～必要がある · ～必要はない vs ～なくてもいい

> **Rule of thumb** - `なくてもいい` grants an exemption; <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>はない</code> denies that a requirement ever existed.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくてもいいですよ**。<br>*You don't have to come in tomorrow.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>はありません**。<br>*There is no need for you to come in tomorrow.*

The first is a person releasing another person, which is why it needs `よ` or a smile to sound kind. The second is a statement about the schedule, and because it does not position the speaker as the one granting permission, it is often the safer thing to say upward or to a stranger.

---

## ～必要がある · ～必要はない vs ～なくてもいい (cont.)

In their negative-reassurance use they come very close: <code><ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>しなくてもいい</code> and <code><ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>する<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>はない</code> are near-synonyms, the second slightly firmer, as if arguing the worry away rather than waving it off.

---

## ～必要がある · ～必要がある vs ～が必要だ

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>する<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある</code> needs an **action**; <code>が<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>だ</code> needs a **thing**.

ここで<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>脱<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ぐ<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります**。<br>*You need to take your shoes off here.*

ここでは<ruby>上<rt>うわ</rt></ruby>ばきが**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>です**。<br>*You need indoor shoes here.*

---

## ～必要がある · ～必要がある vs ～が必要だ (cont.)

The two are often interchangeable in translation and never in grammar. A verb before <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> requires the dictionary form and the `が…ある` frame; a noun before it takes `が` and the plain な-adjective predicate <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>だ／です</code>. A noun can also be forced into the `ある` frame with `の` - <code><ruby>修理<rt>しゅうり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある</code> - which sounds distinctly more formal than <code><ruby>修理<rt>しゅうり</rt></ruby>が<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>だ</code>.

---

## ～必要がある · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ます**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります。 → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*The verb in front of <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> is plain and non-past. `ます` cannot appear inside the sentence; politeness goes on `あります` at the end.*

# 必要

**Meaning** · necessary; needed - the word itself, not the pattern

Read <ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>. It is a noun that behaves as a な-adjective: something <code>が<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>だ</code>「is needed」, and a <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>な</code> thing is a required one. The verb pattern <code>～<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある</code> is built out of this word, but the word does plenty of work on its own, and getting its particles right is what stops learners writing English with Japanese vocabulary in it.

---

## 必要 · Formation

The thing that is needed takes `が`, not `を` - <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> is not a verb and has no object.

---

## 必要 · Nuance & register

<code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> is a Sino-Japanese word, and like most of them it leans formal and slightly abstract. It is completely ordinary in polite speech, business email, notices and writing. In casual conversation, though, Japanese usually reaches for the plain verb `いる` instead: a friend asks <code><ruby>袋<rt>ふくろ</rt></ruby>いる？</code>, not <code><ruby>袋<rt>ふくろ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ですか</code>. At a convenience store counter you will hear both, because staff speech is formal.

The negatives are worth separating. <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ない</code> is short, everyday and can sound abrupt - <code>そんなの<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ない</code> is close to "we don't need that rubbish." <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ありません</code> is the neutral polite version and the one to use with strangers. <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ではありません</code> is stiffer again and belongs in writing. As a refusal at a shop counter, the standard phrase is not any of these but <code><ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>です</code> or <code><ruby>結構<rt>けっこう</rt></ruby>です</code>.

---

## 必要 · Nuance & register (cont.)

<code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>とする</code> is a genuine verb and a register marker: it appears in journalism, official documents and formal description - <code><ruby>支援<rt>しえん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>としている<ruby>地域<rt>ちいき</rt></ruby></code>「areas that require assistance」. Using it in conversation would sound like reading a press release.

One more thing to notice: <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> describes what a *purpose* demands. It carries no warmth. That is why it is the right word for documents, tools, time and money, and the wrong word for people - see the contrast below.

---

## 必要 · 必要 vs いる

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different word class and different register. `いる` is a verb and casual; <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> is a な-adjective and formal.

レシート、**いる**？<br>*D'you want the receipt?*

レシートは**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ですか**。<br>*Would you like a receipt?*

---

## 必要 · 必要 vs いる (cont.)

Both take their object with `が` (or with no particle at all in speech), which is the one thing they share structurally. `いる` - written <ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>る, though usually left in kana - is a う-verb: `いります`, `いらない`, `いった`. Beware that its past and て-forms collide with <code><ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>る</code>, <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く</code> and the existence verb `いる`, which is why it is far more common in the non-past.

`いる` is also limited to things. For an *action* you need <code>～<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある</code> or one of the obligation forms: there is no ❌<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くがいる</code>.

---

## 必要 · 必要 vs 大切・大事

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> = required for a purpose. <code><ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby></code>／<code><ruby>大事<rt>だいじ</rt></ruby></code> = important, precious, valued. Do not use <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> about people.

<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>はとても**<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>です**。<br>*My family means a great deal to me.*

この<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>には<ruby>集中力<rt>しゅうちゅうりょく</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>です**。<br>*This job requires concentration.*

---

## 必要 · 必要 vs 大切・大事 (cont.)

Swap them and both sentences go wrong in interesting ways. <code><ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>です</code> sounds like you are shopping for one - the word treats its object as a resource. And <code><ruby>集中力<rt>しゅうちゅうりょく</rt></ruby>が<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>です</code> is fine but says something milder: concentration *matters*, rather than the job being impossible without it.

<code><ruby>大事<rt>だいじ</rt></ruby></code> is the more conversational of the two importance words; <code><ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby></code> is a shade warmer and more careful. Both are な-adjectives, like <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> itself.

---

## 必要 · 必要だ vs 必要がある

> **Rule of thumb** - a **noun** is needed → <code>が<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>だ</code>. An **action** is needed → <code>～する<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある</code>.

<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>です**。<br>*A reservation is required.*

<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>する**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります**。<br>*You need to make a reservation.*

The information is the same and the framing is not. The first names a thing the situation demands, and would suit a sign on a door. The second describes something the listener has to do, and suits an explanation.

---

## 必要 · 必要だ vs 必要がある (cont.)

A noun can be pushed into the `ある` frame with `の` (<code><ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります</code>), which sounds noticeably more bureaucratic. A verb, on the other hand, cannot use the `だ` frame at all - there is no ❌<code><ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>するが<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>です</code>.

---

## 必要 · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>**の**<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>**な**<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby><br>*<code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> is a な-adjective when it modifies a noun. `の` looks tempting because the word is also a noun, but it is not how the phrase is built.*

- ❌ パスポート**を**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ パスポート**が**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>です。<br>*There is no verb here, so there is no object. The needed thing is the subject and takes `が`.*

- ❌ この<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>**くない**。 → ✅ この<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>**じゃない**。<br>*Only い-adjectives negate with `くない`. <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> takes `じゃない`, `ではない`, or bare `ない`.*

- ❌ <ruby>病院<rt>びょういん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**が**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ <ruby>病院<rt>びょういん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります**。<br>*A verb cannot be marked with `が`. To say an action is needed, switch to the <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>がある</code> frame.*

---

## 必要 · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌（to a friend, about a plastic bag）<ruby>袋<rt>ふくろ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ですか。 → ✅ <ruby>袋<rt>ふくろ</rt></ruby>いる？<br>*Grammatical and oddly formal. Among friends the everyday word for needing a thing is `いる`.*

## 必要 · Practice 1

**1.** この<ruby>手続<rt>てつづ</rt></ruby>きに____な<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>を<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>って<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。

{reveal}
この<ruby>手続<rt>てつづ</rt></ruby>きに**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>**な<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>を<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>って<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ました。

{reveal}
Modifying a noun, <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> behaves as a な-adjective, so the connector is `な`. The `に` marks the purpose the things are needed *for*.

## 必要 · Practice 2

**2.** ペン、（いる・<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>でいらっしゃいますか）？ - said to a close friend.

{reveal}
ペン、**いる**？

{reveal}
Between friends the plain verb is what people use; the honorific version is not merely over-polite, it is comic. <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> itself is not rude here, but it still sounds like a form rather than a question about a pen.

## 必要 · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese, as a line on a website: "A credit card is not required."

{reveal}
クレジットカードは**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ありません**。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ありません</code> is the standard polite negative and the usual wording on notices. <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ない</code> would be too blunt for a public-facing page, and <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ではございません</code> would be stiffer than most sites go.

## 必要 · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きるが<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>です。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きる**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります**。

{reveal}
`が` can only mark a noun. When what is needed is an action, the dictionary form attaches straight to <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> and the sentence closes with `ある`.

## 必要 · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>には<ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>だ**。 (b) この<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>にとって<ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby>な<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>だ**。

{reveal}
(a) says the company cannot function without you. <code><ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby></code> frames a person as something the organisation requires, so the compliment is real but cool - it is about what you produce. In a film this is the line a boss says to stop someone resigning.

{reveal}
(b) says you are valued. <code><ruby>大切<rt>たいせつ</rt></ruby></code> is about worth rather than utility, and it is the word you would use about a person if you wanted the sentence to sound warm. Both are natural Japanese; only one of them is affectionate.
