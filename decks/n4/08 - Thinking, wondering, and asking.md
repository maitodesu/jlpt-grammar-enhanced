# ～と思う

**Meaning** · I think (that) … - <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code> is "think", and `と` is the string that ties a whole sentence into a parcel for it to carry

`と` is a **quotation marker**. It takes an entire sentence, wraps it up, and hands it to a verb of thinking or saying. <code>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code> is therefore not "think about" - it reports a complete thought, word for word, as your opinion. The parcel is always tied in **plain form**, no matter how polite the sentence around it is.

---

## ～と思う · Formation

`と` follows the plain form of the quoted clause. Nouns and な-adjectives keep their `だ`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>ると<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う |
| Verb (past) | た-form + と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う | <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わった → <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う | <ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わない → <ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わないと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う |
| い-adjective | plain form + と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う |
| な-adjective | + だ + と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う | <ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby> → <ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>だと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う |
| Noun | + だ + と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う | <ruby>冗談<rt>じょうだん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>冗談<rt>じょうだん</rt></ruby>だと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う |
| Volitional - "I'm thinking of…" | よう-form + と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>もう → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>もうと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う |
| Somebody else's opinion | plain form + と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っている | <ruby>正<rt>ただ</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>正<rt>ただ</rt></ruby>しいと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っている |

---

## ～と思う · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - nothing polite goes in front of `と`. ❌<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code> → ✅<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code>. The politeness of the whole sentence is carried by the **final** verb and nowhere else.

> **Note** - the `だ` that disappears before `でしょう` stays before `と`. It is <code><ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>でしょう</code> but <code><ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>だと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code>.

---

## ～と思う · Examples

あの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>まっている**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う**。<br>*I think that shop has already closed.*

この<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんのだ**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います**。<br>*I think this umbrella is Yamada's.*

<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>はそんなに<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しくない**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う**よ。<br>*I don't think the test will be all that hard.*

<ruby>今年<rt>ことし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>より<ruby>暖<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かい**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いませんか**。<br>*Don't you think this winter is milder than last year's?*

---

## ～と思う · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>通<rt>かよ</rt></ruby>おう**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っています**。<br>*I'm thinking of cycling to work from next month.*

<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>が<ruby>世界<rt>せかい</rt></ruby>で<ruby>一番<rt>いちばん</rt></ruby>おいしい**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っている**。<br>*My sister is convinced her own cooking is the best in the world.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います**が、<ruby>念<rt>ねん</rt></ruby>のため<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>しておきます。<br>*I think the meeting finishes at three, but I'll check to be safe.*

<ruby>正直<rt>しょうじき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>うと、あの<ruby>計画<rt>けいかく</rt></ruby>はうまくいかない**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います**。<br>*To be honest, I don't think that plan is going to work.*

---

## ～と思う · Nuance & register

<code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code> is the plain form and <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code> the polite one; everything before `と` is untouched by that choice. This is the single most useful thing to know about the pattern, and the single most common place learners slip.

Beyond grammar, <code>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code> is a **softener**. A bare assertion - その<ruby>方法<rt>ほうほう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>です - commits you to a fact. Adding <code>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code> reframes it as one person's reading of the situation, which is why Japanese workplace speech is full of it. Overusing it is a real thing too: answering a question about your own department with <code><ruby>多分<rt>たぶん</rt></ruby>そうだと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code> can sound as if you have not bothered to find out.

---

## ～と思う · Nuance & register (cont.)

In casual speech you will hear `って` in place of `と` - <ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>だって<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>った - and the whole clause often ends in `んだけど` to leave the door open: <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>でもいいと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>うんだけど。 At the other end, formal writing prefers the passive <code>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>われます</code> or <code>と<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えられます</code>, which removes the "I" altogether.

One limit worth stating plainly: the plain-form rule is about **indirect** quotation. If you put actual quotation marks round someone's words you are reproducing what came out of their mouth, so anything can appear inside: 「<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みます」と<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っていました。 That is a direct quote, and it is not an exception to the rule - it is a different operation.

---

## ～と思う · ～と思う vs ～と思っている

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code> is a thought formed **now**, at the moment of speaking. <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っている</code> is a view somebody **holds** - and it is the only option for a flat statement about a third person.

その<ruby>色<rt>いろ</rt></ruby>のほうが<ruby>似合<rt>にあ</rt></ruby>う**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う**。<br>*I think that colour suits you better.*

<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>をまだ<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもだ**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っている**。<br>*My father still thinks of me as a child.*

---

## ～と思う · ～と思う vs ～と思っている (cont.)

You cannot see into someone else's head, so a bare ❌<code><ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>は…と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code> claims access you do not have. The `ている` turns it into an observation about a state you can infer from outside. About yourself both work: <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>もそう<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っています</code> just adds that you have thought so for a while.

---

## ～と思う · ～と思う vs ～でしょう

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code> labels the claim as **mine**. `でしょう` estimates how **likely** the claim is. One is about the speaker, the other about the world.

<ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がる**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います**。<br>*I think vegetable prices will go up next year.*

<ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がる**でしょう**。<br>*Vegetable prices will probably go up next year.*

---

## ～と思う · ～と思う vs ～でしょう (cont.)

The second is what a news presenter says; the first is what you say at dinner. Because <code>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code> keeps the responsibility on you, it is usually the safer choice when you might be wrong, and the wrong choice when you are supposed to be the authority.

---

## ～と思う · ～ないと思う vs ～と思わない

> **Rule of thumb** - English negates "think"; Japanese negates the **thought**. "I don't think it will rain" is <code><ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>らないと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code>, not <code><ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ると<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>わない</code>.

<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>はその<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います**。<br>*I don't think she knows about it.*

<code>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>わない</code> is not ungrammatical - it is simply much stronger. It rejects the proposition rather than doubting it, so it lands as disagreement:

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>はそうは**<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いません**。<br>*I don't see it that way.*

---

## ～と思う · ～ないと思う vs ～と思わない (cont.)

Note the `は` on `そう`, which is typical: negating <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code> almost always comes with contrastive `は`, and that is part of why it feels like pushing back.

---

## ～と思う · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>りますと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います**。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ると<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います**。<br>*Nothing in ます-form may sit in front of `と`. Politeness belongs to the last verb.*

- ❌ この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う**。 → ✅ この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>だと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う**。<br>*な-adjectives and nouns keep `だ` before `と`. Only い-adjectives and verbs attach bare.*

- ❌ <ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>はその<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>がつまらないと**<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います**。 → ✅ <ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>はその<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>がつまらないと**<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っています**。<br>*A plain statement about what someone else thinks needs `ている` - or a hedge such as <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っているようです</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**あると<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いません**。 → ✅ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**ないと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います**。<br>*Put the negative inside the quoted thought. The outer negative is reserved for disagreeing.*

---

## ～と思う · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>____<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>**だと**<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby></code> is a noun, and a noun predicate keeps its `だ` in front of the quotative `と`. Dropping it is only correct before `でしょう`.

## ～と思う · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>は<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>正<rt>ただ</rt></ruby>しい（と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います・と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っています）。

{reveal}
<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>は<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>正<rt>ただ</rt></ruby>しい**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っています**。

{reveal}
The subject is a third person, so a bare <code>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code> would claim direct knowledge of her mind. `ている` presents the belief as something you observe her holding.

---

## ～と思う · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "I don't think he will come today."

{reveal}
<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>**<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います**。

{reveal}
The negative goes inside the quoted thought, not on <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code>. <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>ると<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いません</code> would read as flatly rejecting the idea rather than doubting it.

---

## ～と思う · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>親切<rt>しんせつ</rt></ruby>ですと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
✅ あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>親切<rt>しんせつ</rt></ruby>だと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います**。

{reveal}
Two things go wrong at once: `です` cannot appear before `と`, and the な-adjective still needs its plain copula `だ`. The polite ending survives on <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code>.

---

## ～と思う · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) この<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>失敗<rt>しっぱい</rt></ruby>すると<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。 (b) この<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>失敗<rt>しっぱい</rt></ruby>するでしょう。

{reveal}
(a) marks the prediction as your own reading and keeps the responsibility with you - appropriate in a meeting where you are offering an opinion someone may argue with.

{reveal}
(b) states it as a likely outcome, the way a forecast or an analysis does. It sounds more detached and more authoritative, which is fine from an expert and presumptuous from a newcomer.

# って

**Meaning** · the spoken shortcut - "they said…", "you know…", "the thing called…"

`って` is what `と` (and often `という`, and sometimes `は`) becomes in relaxed speech. It quotes, it reports hearsay, it introduces a name, and it puts a topic on the table with a little more energy than `は` does. One syllable, several jobs - but they all come from the same place: `って` **presents a chunk of language** and asks the listener to take it as a unit.

---

## って · Formation

Like `と`, `って` follows the **plain form**. Nouns and な-adjectives keep `だ` when `って` is quoting; they drop it when `って` is marking a topic.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb - quoting | plain form + って（+ <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う／<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>く／<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う） | <ruby>辞<rt>や</rt></ruby>める → <ruby>辞<rt>や</rt></ruby>めるって<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってた |
| Noun / な-adjective - quoting | + だ + って | <ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby> → <ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>だって<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いた |
| Sentence-final - hearsay | plain form + って（falling tone） | <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないって |
| Direct quote | 「…」+ って | 「まだだ」 → 「まだだ」って<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>われた |
| Naming - replaces `という` | Noun + って + Noun | <ruby>林<rt>はやし</rt></ruby> → <ruby>林<rt>はやし</rt></ruby>さんって<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby> |
| Topic - replaces `は` / `というのは` | Noun + って（no だ） | <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> → <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby>って<ruby>難<rt>むずか</rt></ruby>しい |

---

## って · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `って` inherits the `と` rule exactly: the clause in front of it is **plain**, however polite you are being. ❌<code><ruby>晴<rt>は</rt></ruby>れますって</code> → ✅<code><ruby>晴<rt>は</rt></ruby>れるって</code>. The one exception is a direct quote in 「」, where you are copying someone's actual words.

---

## って · Examples

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない**って**。<br>*Tanaka says he's not coming tomorrow.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>だ**って**。<br>*Apparently the meeting's off.*

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>**って**<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えるのが<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>だね。<br>*Kanji really are a slog to memorise, aren't they.*

さっき<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>はない**って**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってたよ。<br>*The teacher said a moment ago that there's no homework today.*

---

## って · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>林<rt>はやし</rt></ruby>さん**って**<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>から<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>がありましたよ。<br>*There was a phone call from someone called Hayashi.*

「もう<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りたい」**って**<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby>をしているよ。<br>*You've got an "I want to go home already" look on your face.*

<ruby>妹<rt>いもうと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>貸<rt>か</rt></ruby>した<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>、まだ<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでない**って**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>うんだけど、もう<ruby>一<rt>いっ</rt></ruby>か<ruby>月<rt>げつ</rt></ruby><ruby>経<rt>た</rt></ruby>つよ。<br>*My sister says she still hasn't read the book I lent her - and it's been a month.*

<ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby><ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>する**って**<ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>？いつ<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めたの？<br>*Is it true you're studying abroad next year? When did you decide?*

---

## って · Nuance & register

`って` is **spoken casual and nothing else**. It belongs in conversation with friends, family and people you are on plain-form terms with. It does not belong in an email to a client, a report, an essay, or a sentence you are otherwise ending in `ます`. The neutral replacements are `と` for quoting and `そうです`／ `とのことです` for hearsay.

Intonation does a lot of work. Falling `って` at the end of a sentence is hearsay - <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>になるって。 Rising `って`? is a request for confirmation, close to "wait, did you say…?" - <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みだって？ Both are the same particle.

---

## って · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two extensions you will hear constantly and will not find in a textbook: `っていうか` at the head of a sentence, which retracts what you just said and restarts ("or rather…"), and `ってば`, which repeats something impatiently - だから<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>うってば。 The second is distinctly childish or intimate; it is not a neutral emphasis marker.

There is a semi-polite hearsay form, `～んですって`, which keeps a `です` on the end: となりの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めるんですって。 It is polite in form but chatty in feel, and in modern Tokyo speech it leans female. `～だってです` is not a form at all.

---

## って · って vs と

> **Rule of thumb** - same grammar, different register: `って` is `と` in a T-shirt. But `って` also covers jobs `と` cannot do, namely naming and topic-marking.

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>出席<rt>しゅっせき</rt></ruby>できない**と**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っていました。<br>*The section chief said he can't attend.*

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>出席<rt>しゅっせき</rt></ruby>できない**って**。<br>*The boss says he can't make it.*

---

## って · って vs と (cont.)

Both report the same fact. The first is what you write in a message to the team; the second is what you say over your shoulder to the colleague next to you. Where the two part company is self-introduction and any fixed formula: <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>と<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>します is the set phrase, and `って` there is simply wrong.

---

## って · topic って vs は

> **Rule of thumb** - `は` sets a topic quietly. `って` sets a topic and signals that you are about to say something **about the category itself**, usually with a reaction attached.

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>**は**<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*Summers in Japan are hot.*

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>**って**<ruby>暑<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>いね。<br>*Japanese summers are hot, aren't they.*

---

## って · topic って vs は (cont.)

The `って` version reads as a comment brought up fresh, and it pairs naturally with `ね`, `よ` and evaluative adjectives. It is close to <code><ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>というのは</code>, which is exactly what it is a contraction of - and that is why it can also mean "what is…?": 「バイト」**って**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>？

---

## って · hearsay って vs ～そうだ

> **Rule of thumb** - identical content, opposite registers. `って` is what you say; `そうです` is what you write.

<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さん、<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>す**って**。<br>*Yamada's moving house next month, apparently.*

<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>す**そうです**。<br>*I hear that Yamada is moving house next month.*

---

## って · hearsay って vs ～そうだ (cont.)

One practical difference: `って` easily carries a named source in front of it - <ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>が<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなるって<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってた - while `そうだ` usually leaves the source vague or marks it with `によると`. If you want to say who told you, `って` is the more natural spoken frame.

---

## って · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>**って<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>します**。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>**と<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>します**。<br>*Self-introduction is a fixed humble formula. `って` cannot be dropped into a phrase that is formal by definition.*

- ❌ <ruby>天気予報<rt>てんきよほう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>晴<rt>は</rt></ruby>れますって**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってた。 → ✅ …**<ruby>晴<rt>は</rt></ruby>れるって**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってた。<br>*An indirect quote takes the plain form, exactly as with `と`. `ます` only survives inside 「」.*

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>**だってです**。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>**だそうです**。<br>*You cannot make `って` polite by bolting `です` on. Switch to `そうです` or `とのことです`.*

- ❌ (in a written report) <ruby>調査<rt>ちょうさ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>結果<rt>けっか</rt></ruby>、<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>はなかった**って**。 → ✅ …<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>はなかった**とのことです**。<br>*`って` is a spoken form. In writing it reads as though the report were a text message.*

---

# かどうか

**Meaning** · whether or not - packs a yes/no question inside a bigger sentence

`かどうか` takes a question that could be answered *yes* or *no*, ties it into a single lump, and hands that lump to a verb like <code><ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かる</code>, <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない</code> or <code><ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べる</code>. It is literally "か（yes?）どう（how?）か" - is it so, or how is it? The English that maps onto it is **whether**, and mapping it onto **if** instead is where nearly all the trouble starts.

---

## かどうか · Formation

`か` attaches to plain forms. Nouns and な-adjectives **lose** their `だ` - the opposite of what happens before the quotative `と`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + かどうか | <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>くかどうか |
| Verb (past) | た-form + かどうか | <ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>った → <ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ったかどうか |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + かどうか | <ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>らない → <ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>らないかどうか |
| い-adjective | plain form + かどうか | <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいかどうか |
| な-adjective | drop だ + かどうか | <ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>かどうか |
| Noun | drop だ + かどうか | <ruby>本物<rt>ほんもの</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>本物<rt>ほんもの</rt></ruby>かどうか |
| Both options spelled out | ～か～ないか | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くか<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないか |

---

## かどうか · Formation (cont.)

Typical main verbs after it: <code><ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かる</code>, <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らない</code>, <code><ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>く</code>, <code><ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べる</code>, <code><ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめる</code>, <code><ruby>迷<rt>まよ</rt></ruby>う</code>, <code><ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えていない</code>, <code><ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>める</code>.

> **Rule of thumb** - nothing polite and no `だ` in front of `か`. ❌<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますかどうか</code>, ❌<code><ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>だかどうか</code> → ✅<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くかどうか</code>, ✅<code><ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>かどうか</code>.

> **Note** - the particle that would follow the lump (`を`, `が`) is normally left out: <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>いたかどうか<ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめます. `は` does appear, and adds contrast: <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>いたかどうかは、まだ<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません。

---

## かどうか · Examples

これでいい**かどうか**、<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てください。<br>*Please have a look and tell me whether this is all right.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>る**かどうか**<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません。<br>*I don't know whether it will snow tomorrow.*

この<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>が<ruby>安全<rt>あんぜん</rt></ruby>**かどうか**、<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べたほうがいいよ。<br>*You should check first whether this water is safe.*

<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>かった**かどうか**は、<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>に<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かります。<br>*Whether I passed or not will be clear next Friday.*

---

## かどうか · Examples (cont.)

パーティーに<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>る**かどうか**、まだ<ruby>迷<rt>まよ</rt></ruby>っているんです。<br>*I still can't make up my mind whether to go to the party.*

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>のことを<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っている**かどうか**、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>には<ruby>判断<rt>はんだん</rt></ruby>できません。<br>*I'm in no position to judge whether he's telling the truth.*

サイズが<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う**かどうか**、<ruby>試着<rt>しちゃく</rt></ruby>してから<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めようと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。<br>*I'll try it on before deciding, to see whether the size works.*

<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>みが<ruby>締<rt>し</rt></ruby>め<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>りに<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>った**かどうか**、<ruby>事務所<rt>じむしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>してみます。<br>*I'll check with the office whether my application made the deadline.*

---

## かどうか · Nuance & register

`かどうか` is register-neutral. It appears unchanged in a chat with friends, in a lecture and in a contract; as always, the politeness of the sentence lives on the final verb. It is also one of the few N4 patterns with no casual contraction of its own - although in relaxed speech people often drop `どうか` entirely and leave a bare `か`: <ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>うか<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かんない。 That is shorter, not ruder.

The one thing `かどうか` cannot do is finish a sentence. It is a lump that some other verb has to pick up, so <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るかどうか。</code> on its own is a fragment. If you want to end there, you need the wondering forms instead - <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るかな</code>, <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るでしょうか</code>.

---

## かどうか · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two fixed expressions are worth having ready. <code>かどうかは<ruby>別<rt>べつ</rt></ruby>として</code> ("setting aside whether…") concedes a point before moving on, and <code>かどうか<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりませんが</code> is the standard hedge for offering something you are not sure will help: お<ruby>役<rt>やく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>立<rt>た</rt></ruby>つかどうか<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりませんが、こちらの<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>をどうぞ。

---

## かどうか · かどうか vs か

> **Rule of thumb** - if the embedded question contains a **question word** （<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>, どこ, だれ, いつ, どう, どちら）, use bare `か`. `かどうか` is only for questions whose answer is *yes* or *no*.

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>を<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>した**か**<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えていません。<br>*I don't remember what I ordered yesterday.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby><ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>した**かどうか**<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えていません。<br>*I don't remember whether I ordered yesterday.*

---

## かどうか · かどうか vs か (cont.)

The first asks about the *contents* of the order, the second about whether the ordering happened at all. Sticking `どうか` onto the first - ❌<code><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>を<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>したかどうか</code> - is ungrammatical, because <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby> has already supplied the question and `どうか` has nothing left to ask.

Bare `か` is also available for yes/no questions, especially in speech: <ruby>空<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いているか<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てきます。 `かどうか` simply spells the alternative out and is the clearer choice in writing.

---

## かどうか · かどうか vs ～たら／～ば

> **Rule of thumb** - English "if" is two unrelated words. "If" you could replace with **whether** → `かどうか`. "If" meaning **on condition that** → `たら`／`ば`／`と`.

<ruby>木村<rt>きむら</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>参加<rt>さんか</rt></ruby>する**かどうか**、<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてみます。<br>*I'll ask whether Kimura is taking part.*

<ruby>木村<rt>きむら</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>参加<rt>さんか</rt></ruby>し**たら**、<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>増<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>やします。<br>*If Kimura takes part, I'll add one more seat.*

---

## かどうか · かどうか vs ～たら／～ば (cont.)

These are not stylistic variants - they say different things. The first is about the speaker's ignorance; the second is about a consequence. "I don't know if he's coming" is ❌<code><ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たら<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません</code> and ✅<code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るかどうか<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません</code>. This is the most common single error with the pattern, and it comes straight out of English.

---

## かどうか · かどうか vs ～か～ないか

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, more deliberate delivery. And when the two options are not *yes* and *no* but two **different things**, only the spelled-out version works.

<ruby>出席<rt>しゅっせき</rt></ruby>する**かどうか**、<ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>までにお<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>ください。<br>*Please reply by Friday as to whether you'll attend.*

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**かバスで<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くか**、まだ<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めていません。<br>*I haven't decided yet whether to go by train or by bus.*

The second could not be rewritten with `かどうか`: there is no *no* to negate, just two alternatives. Keep `かどうか` for the yes/no case and lay both options out when they are genuinely two options.

---

## かどうか · Common mistakes

- ❌ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだかどうか**<ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめます。 → ✅ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かかどうか**<ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめます。<br>*Nouns and な-adjectives drop `だ` before `か`. It is `だ` before `と`, no `だ` before `か` - the two quotative-ish particles behave in opposite ways.*

- ❌ <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんが**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ますかどうか**<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>りません。 → ✅ <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんが**<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るかどうか**<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>りません。<br>*The embedded clause is plain. `ます` belongs to the end of the sentence, not to the middle of it.*

- ❌ <ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>いたら**<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません。 → ✅ <ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>いたかどうか**<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません。<br>*"I don't know if the parcel arrived" is *whether*, not a condition. `たら` here would mean "once it arrives, I won't understand", which is nonsense.*

---

## かどうか · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ **どこで<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>えるかどうか**<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてください。 → ✅ **どこで<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>えるか**<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてください。<br>*With a question word present, `どうか` has nothing to do. Drop it.*

---

## かどうか · Practice 1

**1.** この<ruby>情報<rt>じょうほう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>正確<rt>せいかく</rt></ruby>____<ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめる<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります。

{reveal}
この<ruby>情報<rt>じょうほう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>正確<rt>せいかく</rt></ruby>かどうか**<ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめる<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>があります。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>正確<rt>せいかく</rt></ruby></code> is a な-adjective, so its `だ` disappears before `か`. Writing ❌<code><ruby>正確<rt>せいかく</rt></ruby>だかどうか</code> is the single most frequent form error here.

---

## かどうか · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>する（か・かどうか）<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いておきます。

{reveal}
<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>する**か**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いておきます。

{reveal}
The clause already contains the question word <ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>, so the question has been asked and `どうか` would be asking a second, different one. `かどうか` is reserved for yes/no.

---

## かどうか · Practice 3

{reveal}

{reveal}
"Whether" is the giveaway for `かどうか`. Note that a conditional （❌<code><ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ったら<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません</code>）would say something quite different and quite strange.

## かどうか · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べましたかどうか<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えていません。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>ごはんを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたかどうか**<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えていません。

An embedded question takes the plain form, so <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べました</code> becomes <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた</code>. The sentence stays polite thanks to <code><ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えていません</code> at the end.

# かもしれない

**Meaning** · might; maybe - the possibility is open, and the speaker refuses to say more than that

Take the pieces apart and the word explains itself: `か` (is it so?) + `も` (even) + <code><ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>れない</code> (cannot be known) - "it cannot even be known whether it is so." `かもしれない` does **not** say something is likely. It says the door is open, and leaves the door on the other side open too. That is the whole of its meaning, and it is why it sits at the bottom of the confidence ladder.

---

## かもしれない · Formation

Attaches to plain forms. Nouns and な-adjectives drop `だ`.

---

## かもしれない · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|

## かもしれない · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - tense lives **inside**, politeness lives **outside**. "It might have been wrong" is <code><ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>っていたかもしれません</code>, never ❌<code><ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>っているかもしれませんでした</code>.

---

## かもしれない · Examples

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れる**かもしれない**。<br>*The train might be late.*

あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんの<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>さん**かもしれません**。<br>*That might be Tanaka's younger brother.*

かぎを<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れてきた**かもしれない**。<br>*I may have left my keys at home.*

<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>ったより<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い**かもしれない**から、<ruby>上着<rt>うわぎ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>っていこう。<br>*It might be colder than we expect up the mountain, so let's take jackets.*

---

## かもしれない · Examples (cont.)

もしかしたら、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>勘違<rt>かんちが</rt></ruby>いだった**かもしれません**。<br>*It's possible I got the wrong end of the stick.*

この<ruby>方法<rt>ほうほう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>面倒<rt>めんどう</rt></ruby>**かもしれない**けど、<ruby>確実<rt>かくじつ</rt></ruby>だよ。<br>*This way might be a bit of a faff, but it's reliable.*

<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>がないので、メールが<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>いていない**かもしれません**ね。<br>*There's been no reply, so perhaps the email never arrived.*

<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>いにくいのですが、この<ruby>数字<rt>すうじ</rt></ruby>はどこかで<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>っている**かもしれません**。<br>*This is awkward to raise, but there may be an error somewhere in these figures.*

---

## かもしれない · Nuance & register

Plain `かもしれない`, polite `かもしれません`. In speech the tail is very often chopped off to `かも` - そうかも。／<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>けないかも。 - and `かも` on its own is a complete reply, roughly "could be". `かもね` softens it further and `かもよ` warns. None of these short forms belong in writing; `かもしれない` itself is perfectly at home in an essay.

---

## かもしれない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Its social use is at least as important as its logical one. Because `かもしれません` commits to nothing, it is the standard tool for **raising something delicate**: a risk nobody has mentioned, an error in a superior's work, a disagreement you do not want to turn into a confrontation. <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>足<rt>た</rt></ruby>りなかったかもしれません</code> takes the blame without quite admitting it, which is exactly why it is said so often.

The flip side is that overusing it sounds evasive. If your manager asks when your own work will be finished, <code><ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わるかもしれません</code> reads as "I haven't really thought about it". About your own actions and your own knowledge, commit: <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わると<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code>.

---

## かもしれない · Nuance & register (cont.)

`もしかしたら` and `ひょっとすると` at the head of the sentence flag the guess in advance and push it further towards "just possibly". They pair with `かもしれない` almost automatically, and a sentence that opens with `もしかしたら` and then ends flatly in `です` sounds unfinished.

---

## かもしれない · かもしれない vs でしょう・だろう

> **Rule of thumb** - `でしょう` is an **estimate**: the speaker thinks it probably *is* so. `かもしれない` is an **admission**: the speaker will only say it *could* be so, and that the opposite could be too.

<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>は<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>くなる**でしょう**。<br>*The wind will probably get stronger this afternoon.*

<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby>は<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>くなる**かもしれません**。<br>*The wind might get stronger this afternoon.*

---

## かもしれない · かもしれない vs でしょう・だろう (cont.)

The forecaster says the first; the person deciding whether to hang the washing out says the second. There is a neat test: you can chain `かもしれない` with its own negation - <ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>くなるかもしれないし、ならないかもしれない - and it sounds entirely normal, because both branches were open all along. Doing the same with `でしょう` sounds like someone contradicting themselves.

---

## かもしれない · かもしれない vs はずだ

> **Rule of thumb** - the difference is **what justifies the claim**. `はずだ` rests on a reason you could name if challenged. `かもしれない` rests on nothing at all, and needs nothing.

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いている**はずです**。<ruby>七時<rt>しちじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>ると<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>っていましたから。<br>*The section chief should already be there - he said he'd take the seven o'clock train.*

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いている**かもしれません**。<br>*The section chief might already be there.*

Ask どうして？ after the first and you owe an answer; the timetable is the grounds. Ask it after the second and "no particular reason, it's just possible" is a complete reply. This is also why `はずです` can be **wrong** in a way `かもしれません` cannot: if he is not there, your reasoning has failed, and Japanese has a form for exactly that disappointment - `はずだったのに`.

---

## かもしれない · かもしれない vs ～と思う

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う</code> puts an opinion on the record. `かもしれない` declines to have one.

こちらの<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>のほうが<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>いやすい**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います**。<br>*I think this dictionary is easier to use.*

こちらの<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>のほうが<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>いやすい**かもしれません**。<br>*This dictionary might be easier to use.*

Both are polite; only the second leaves the listener free to disagree without contradicting you, which is why it is the gentler way to suggest a change to someone senior. The cost is authority: if you are the person who is supposed to know, `かもしれません` shifts the decision back onto the listener.

---

## かもしれない · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だかもしれません**。 → ✅ <ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>かもしれません**。<br>*Nouns and な-adjectives lose `だ` in front of `か`. Compare <code><ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code>, where the `だ` stays.*

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますかもしれません**。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くかもしれません**。<br>*The clause in front is plain. The politeness is already carried by `かもしれません`.*

- ❌ さっきの<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>っているかもしれませんでした**。 → ✅ さっきの<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>っていたかもしれません**。<br>*Past tense goes on the embedded clause, not on `かもしれない`. There is no ❌`かもしれませんでした`.*

---

## かもしれない · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ 「レポートはいつ<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>せますか。」「<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>せる**かもしれません**。」 → ✅ 「<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>せる**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います**。」<br>*Not a grammar error, a register one. About your own work, `かもしれません` sounds like you have not looked at the calendar.*

# はずだ

**Meaning** · it should be; it must be; it's supposed to be - an expectation you could justify if asked

`はず` is a noun meaning something like *what follows*. Attach a clause to it and you are saying: given what I know, this is how things ought to line up. The key word is **given**. `はずだ` always rests on grounds - a timetable, a promise, a rule, a fact you were told - and if somebody asks どうして？ you are expected to produce them.

---

## はずだ · Formation

`はず` is a noun, so the clause in front takes the forms a noun-modifying clause takes: な-adjectives get `な`, nouns get `の`.

---

## はずだ · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + はずだ | <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>くはずだ |
| Verb (past) | た-form + はずだ | <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>た → <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たはずだ |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + はずだ | <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないはずだ |
| い-adjective | plain form + はずだ | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いはずだ |
| な-adjective | + な + はずだ | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>なはずだ |
| Noun | + の + はずだ | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>み → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みのはずだ |
| Polite | はずです | <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>くはずです |
| Expectation that failed | はずだった（のに） | <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>くはずだった |

---

## はずだ · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `な` for な-adjectives, `の` for nouns. ❌<code><ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>はずだ</code>, ❌<code><ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みはずだ</code>. Compare `かもしれない`, which takes both bare - the two patterns pull in opposite directions and learners routinely swap them.

---

## はずだ · Examples

<ruby>銀行<rt>ぎんこう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いている**はず**です。<br>*The bank should be open until three.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>木曜日<rt>もくようび</rt></ruby>だから、あの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>み**のはず**だ。<br>*It's Thursday, so that shop should be shut.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby><ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りましたから、もう<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>いている**はず**です。<br>*I posted it yesterday, so it should have arrived by now.*

この<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>のほうが<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>い**はず**なんだけど、あまり<ruby>自信<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>がない。<br>*This road should be the shorter one, though I'm not too sure.*

---

## はずだ · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>な**はず**です。10<ruby>年<rt>ねん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>していますから。<br>*He should be a good driver - he's driven every day for ten years.*

おかしいな。ここに<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>いた**はず**なのに、<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>つからない。<br>*That's odd. I'm sure I put it here, and yet I can't find it.*

あの<ruby>二人<rt>ふたり</rt></ruby>は<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>高校<rt>こうこう</rt></ruby>だから、<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>り<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>い**のはず**ですよ。<br>*Those two went to the same school, so they should know each other.*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいカードは<ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>く**はずだった**のに、まだ<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>もありません。<br>*My new card was supposed to arrive last week, and I haven't even had word.*

---

## はずだ · Nuance & register

`はずだ` is plain, `はずです` polite, and the pattern is otherwise register-neutral: it turns up in conversation, in business email and in written explanation without changing shape. In casual speech people say `はずだよ`, `はずでしょ`, and - dropping the copula entirely - <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るはず、<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るはず。 as a mutter of reassurance.

The past form `はずだった` is worth learning as a unit. It does not mean "it should have been" in the sense of blame; it means the expectation existed and reality did not cooperate. It nearly always comes with `のに` or `が`, and it carries audible disappointment.

There is also a small idiom that catches learners off guard: `はずだ` after a discovery means "**no wonder**". You notice something, work out why, and say so.

---

## はずだ · Nuance & register (cont.)

どうりで<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い**はず**だ。<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いている。<br>*No wonder it's cold - the window's open.*

Here the speaker is not predicting anything; they are confirming, after the fact, that the world lines up. It is the same `はず` seen from the other end.

Finally, a limit: `はずだ` is poor at describing **your own intentions**, because you do not need to deduce what you have decided. <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くはずです</code> sounds as though your movements are arranged by someone else. That said, when they genuinely are - a shift roster, a schedule you have been handed - `はず` about yourself is fine: <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みのはずなんですが、<ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめてみます。

---

## はずだ · はずだ vs べきだ

> **Rule of thumb** - English "should" is two words. "I expect it is so" → `はずだ`. "It is the right thing to do" → `べきだ`.

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>はその<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>をもう<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っている**はず**です。<br>*He should already know about it. (I expect he does)*

<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>にその<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らせる**べき**です。<br>*We should tell him about it soon. (it's the right thing to do)*

---

## はずだ · はずだ vs べきだ (cont.)

The test is substitution. If "should" can become "is supposed to" or "I expect", it is `はずだ`, and the sentence is a prediction. If it can become "ought to", it is `べきだ`, and the sentence is a judgement. Telling a friend ❌<code><ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>るはずです</code> does not advise them to go to bed; it predicts that they will.

---

## はずだ · はずだ vs かもしれない・でしょう

> **Rule of thumb** - a ladder of confidence, `かもしれない` → `でしょう` → `はずだ` - but `はずだ` is not merely the top rung. It is a **deduction from stated grounds**, where the other two are estimates.

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>**のはず**です。<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>からだと<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*The director should be in a meeting - I was told it started at three.*

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>**でしょう**。<br>*The director is probably in a meeting.*

---

## はずだ · はずだ vs かもしれない・でしょう (cont.)

Strip the second sentence of the first one and `はずです` starts to feel unsupported; `でしょう` never needs support. The practical consequence is that `はずだ` can be *refuted*: if the meeting was cancelled, your premise was wrong and you say <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>のはずだったんですが. A `でしょう` that misses was only ever a guess.

---

## はずだ · はずだ vs つもり・予定

> **Rule of thumb** - `つもり` is what **I** have decided, <code><ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby></code> is what the **diary** says, `はず` is what **I work out** about someone or something else.

<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>す**つもり**です。<br>*I'm planning to move next month.*

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>す**はず**です。<br>*Tanaka should be moving next month, as far as I know.*

---

## はずだ · はずだ vs つもり・予定 (cont.)

Swap them and the meaning goes badly wrong: `つもり` about a third person claims to know their intentions, and `はず` about your own decision hands your life over to somebody else's timetable. <code><ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>です</code> sits between them - it reports an arrangement without saying whose idea it was, which is why it is the safest thing to say at work.

---

## はずだ · Common mistakes

- ❌ この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>はず**です。 → ✅ この<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>なはず**です。<br>*`はず` is a noun, so a な-adjective in front of it needs its `な`.*

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>祝日<rt>しゅくじつ</rt></ruby>はず**ですよ。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>祝日<rt>しゅくじつ</rt></ruby>のはず**ですよ。<br>*A noun links to `はず` with `の`. Dropping it is the same error one step along.*

- ❌ <ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>いから、たばこを**やめるはず**です。 → ✅ <ruby>体<rt>からだ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>いから、たばこを**やめるべき**です。<br>*This is advice, not prediction. English "should" hides the difference; Japanese does not.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby><ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>けるはず**です。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby><ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>けるつもり**です。<br>*Your own intention is not something you deduce. `はず` here sounds as if someone else had signed you up.*

# はずがない

**Meaning** · there's no way; it cannot possibly be - the negative twin of はずだ, and much stronger than it looks

`はず` is the noun "what follows from what I know". `はずがない` says there is **no such noun to be had**: nothing in what I know leaves room for this. It is not "probably not". It is a flat refusal to entertain the idea, and like `はずだ` it normally comes with a reason attached.

---

## はずがない · Formation

Identical to `はずだ`: `はず` is a noun, so な-adjectives take `な` and nouns take `の`. Only the ending changes.

---

## はずがない · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + はずがない | <ruby>勝<rt>か</rt></ruby>つ → <ruby>勝<rt>か</rt></ruby>つはずがない |
| Verb (past) | た-form + はずがない | <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っていた → <ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っていたはずがない |
| Verb (potential) | potential form + はずがない | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>める → <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>めるはずがない |
| い-adjective | plain form + はずがない | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いはずがない |
| な-adjective | + な + はずがない | <ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>なはずがない |
| Noun | + の + はずがない | <ruby>偶然<rt>ぐうぜん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>偶然<rt>ぐうぜん</rt></ruby>のはずがない |
| Polite | はずがありません／はずはありません | <ruby>勝<rt>か</rt></ruby>つはずがありません |
| Casual | はずない | <ruby>勝<rt>か</rt></ruby>つはずないよ |
| With contrastive `は` | はずはない | <ruby>勝<rt>か</rt></ruby>つはずはない |

---

## はずがない · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the negative goes on `ない`, not on the verb. <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るはずがない</code> = "there's no way he's coming." <code><ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないはずがない</code> = "there's no way he *isn't* coming" - a double negative, and almost never what a learner means.

---

## はずがない · Examples

こんな<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る**はずがない**。<br>*There's no way Tanaka is coming at this hour.*

その<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>**のはずがありません**。<br>*That story can't possibly be true.*

まだ<ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>っていないのだから、<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かる**はずがない**よ。<br>*They haven't been taught it yet, so of course they can't be expected to understand.*

<ruby>一週間<rt>いっしゅうかん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby><ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えられる**はずがない**でしょう。<br>*There's no way anyone could memorise the lot in a week.*

---

## はずがない · Examples (cont.)

あんなに<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>したのだから、<ruby>失敗<rt>しっぱい</rt></ruby>する**はずがありません**。<br>*She's practised that hard - there's no chance of her slipping up.*

<ruby>鍵<rt>かぎ</rt></ruby>をかけて<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たのだから、<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>かが<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>れた**はずはない**。<br>*I locked up when I left, so nobody can have got in.*

<ruby>先月<rt>せんげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ったばかりの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>がそんな<ruby>事情<rt>じじょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>っていた**はずがありません**。<br>*Someone who only joined last month couldn't possibly have known about that.*

<ruby>毎晩<rt>まいばん</rt></ruby>コーヒーを<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでいたら、よく<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れる**はずがない**よ。<br>*If you drink coffee every night, no wonder you can't sleep properly.*

---

## はずがない · Nuance & register

`はずがない` is emphatic. On the scale that runs <code><ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないかもしれません</code> → <code><ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないでしょう</code> → <code><ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないはずです</code> → <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るはずがありません</code>, it sits at the far end: the last of those does not estimate a low probability, it rules the possibility out.

That force gives it a temperature. `はずがない` is often said with feeling - indignation, loyalty, disbelief - and <code>あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>犯人<rt>はんにん</rt></ruby>のはずがありません</code> is a defence of someone, not a calculation. Used against another person's claim it is combative: telling your manager `そんなことがあるはずがありません` is close to "that's simply not the case", and in a workplace you would usually reach for <code>ないと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いますが</code> instead.

---

## はずがない · Nuance & register (cont.)

Register: `はずがない` is the neutral form and works in speech and writing. `はずがありません` is its polite version. In casual speech the `が` drops - そんなはずないよ - which is a very common short reply. `はずはない` swaps `が` for contrastive `は` and narrows the denial to this one point: whatever else may be true, *that* cannot be.

---

## はずがない · はずがない vs ないはずだ

> **Rule of thumb** - `はずがない` denies the **possibility**. `ないはずだ` expects the **negative outcome**. The first says the idea is out of the question; the second says you would be surprised.

<ruby>木村<rt>きむら</rt></ruby>さんがこの<ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る**はずがない**。<br>*There's no way Kimura would come to this.*

<ruby>木村<rt>きむら</rt></ruby>さんはこの<ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>には<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない**はずだ**。<br>*I don't think Kimura is coming to this one.*

Both end up predicting his absence, but only the first would make you argue with someone who claimed to have seen him. `ないはずだ` is an ordinary expectation with ordinary grounds - a message he sent, a scheduling clash - and it can be quietly wrong. `はずがない` cannot be quietly wrong; if he walks in, something you believed was false.

---

## はずがない · はずがない vs わけがない

> **Rule of thumb** - very close in meaning. `はずがない` reasons from grounds; `わけがない` tends to be blunter and more emotional, and belongs more to speech.

この<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>本物<rt>ほんもの</rt></ruby>の**はずがありません**。<br>*At this price it can't be genuine.*

あんな<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>、<ruby>信<rt>しん</rt></ruby>じる**わけがない**でしょう。<br>*As if anyone would believe a story like that.*

Swapping them rarely produces something wrong, but the flavour shifts: `わけがない` carries a shrug or a snort with it, and is at home in an argument. `はずがない` keeps the shape of an inference even when it is said with heat, which is why it is the safer of the two in writing.

---

## はずがない · はずがない vs ～ことができない

> **Rule of thumb** - English "can't" is two things. "It is impossible that…" → `はずがない`. "is unable to…" → the potential form or `ことができない`.

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>はまだ<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐことができません。<br>*My little brother can't swim yet.*

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>であそこまで<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>いだ**はずがありません**。<br>*There's no way my brother swam out that far on his own.*

---

## はずがない · はずがない vs ～ことができない (cont.)

The first is about his ability, the second about your belief. A sentence like ❌<code><ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐはずがありません</code>, meant as "he can't swim", comes out as "there is no possibility of him swimming", which sounds like you are denying an accusation.

---

## はずがない · Common mistakes

- ❌ そんな<ruby>作業<rt>さぎょう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>はずがない**。 → ✅ そんな<ruby>作業<rt>さぎょう</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>簡単<rt>かんたん</rt></ruby>なはずがない**。<br>*`はず` is a noun; a な-adjective in front of it keeps its `な`.*

- ❌ これが<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>はずがありません**。 → ✅ これが<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>字<rt>じ</rt></ruby>のはずがありません**。<br>*A noun links with `の`. The `の` is easy to lose when there is already another `の` earlier in the sentence.*

- ❌ <ruby>祖母<rt>そぼ</rt></ruby>はパソコンを**<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>うはずがありません**。（meaning: she isn't able to）→ ✅ <ruby>祖母<rt>そぼ</rt></ruby>はパソコンを**<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>えません**。<br>*Inability is the potential form. `はずがない` is about what you refuse to believe, not about what someone is capable of.*

---

## はずがない · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れないはずがない**。 → ✅ <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れるはずがない**。<br>*Two negatives cancel. The first sentence means "there's no way he'd *fail* to forget", which is the opposite of the intended defence.*

# かな

**Meaning** · I wonder - a question aimed at yourself, said out loud

`かな` is the question particle `か` plus the soft `な` you tack onto a thought you are having rather than an enquiry you are making. Grammatically the sentence is a question; socially it is not, because nobody has been asked. That gap is what makes `かな` so useful: it lets you put a question into the room without putting anyone on the spot.

---

## かな · Formation

Plain forms only. Nouns and な-adjectives drop `だ`.

---

## かな · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + かな | <ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う → <ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>うかな |
| Verb (past) | た-form + かな | <ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>いた → どこに<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>いたかな |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + かな | <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない → <ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないかな |
| い-adjective | plain form + かな | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いかな |
| な-adjective | drop だ + かな | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだ → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かかな |
| Noun | drop だ + かな | <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>かな |
| With a question word | どこ・<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>・いつ… + かな | いつ<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るかな |
| Drawn out | かなあ | <ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>かなあ |
| Hoping | ～ないかな（あ） | <ruby>晴<rt>は</rt></ruby>れないかなあ |

---

## かな · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Softening an opinion | ～かなと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>う | <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>すぎるかなと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います |

> **Rule of thumb** - `かな` never follows `です`／`ます`, and never follows `だ`. ❌<code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだかな</code> → ✅<code><ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かかな</code>. The polite way to wonder aloud is `でしょうか`.

---

## かな · Examples

<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う**かな**。<br>*Will we make it in time, I wonder.*

この<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>、<ruby>似合<rt>にあ</rt></ruby>う**かな**。<br>*Does this outfit suit me?*

あれ、かぎはどこに<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>いた**かな**。<br>*Hmm - where did I put the keys?*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>、<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になる**かな**。<br>*I wonder if tomorrow's match will be rained off.*

---

## かな · Examples (cont.)

ちょっと<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ってもらえない**かな**。<br>*Any chance you could give me a hand?*

<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>くバスが<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ない**かなあ**。<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>くなってきた。<br>*I wish the bus would hurry up. My feet are starting to ache.*

この<ruby>色<rt>いろ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>派手<rt>はで</rt></ruby>すぎる**かな**。<ruby>地味<rt>じみ</rt></ruby>なほうがいいかもしれない。<br>*Is this colour too loud? A plainer one might be better.*

<ruby>上司<rt>じょうし</rt></ruby>に<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>したほうがいい**かな**と<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>ったんですが、どう<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いますか。<br>*I was wondering whether I ought to raise it with my boss - what do you reckon?*

---

## かな · Nuance & register

`かな` is casual. It belongs with plain-form speech and with people you are relaxed around; the polite way to wonder is `でしょうか`, and to a customer or a superior that is what you should reach for. (In easy-going polite speech you will also hear `ですかね` and `ますかね` - a blend of the two registers that works among colleagues but is too loose for a formal setting.)

Its usefulness comes from the fact that it is *addressed to nobody*. Because you have not asked, the listener is free to answer or not, and that makes `かな` the natural way to float a request - <code><ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby><ruby>貸<rt>か</rt></ruby>してもらえないかな</code> - or to dissent gently: <code>それはちょっと<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>うかな</code> is a good deal easier to hear than <code><ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>います</code>. The same trick works inside polite speech through <code>かなと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います</code>, which is common even in careful ます-style conversation.

---

## かな · Nuance & register (cont.)

`かなあ`, with the vowel stretched, is more wistful and more felt. On its own - どうかなあ。 - it can be a complete, non-committal answer meaning something like "hmm, I'm not sure about that."

On who says it: `かな` is unmarked and used by speakers of every age and gender. Its close relative `かしら` leans strongly female in modern speech, and `かね` leans older and somewhat male. If you want one wondering form that never sounds odd coming out of your mouth, `かな` is it.

---

## かな · かな vs か

> **Rule of thumb** - `か` asks the **listener**. `かな` asks the **air**. Only one of them obliges anybody to answer.

<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さんは<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます**か**。<br>*Is Yamada coming?*

<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>さん、<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る**かな**。<br>*I wonder whether Yamada's coming.*

---

## かな · かな vs か (cont.)

There is a second reason to prefer `かな` in casual speech: a plain-form question ending in bare `か` - <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るか。 - sounds abrupt and rather rough, more like a demand than a question. Casual questions are normally marked by rising intonation alone （<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る？）or softened with `かな`. Bare `か` after a plain form is not a neutral casual question.

---

## かな · かな vs かしら

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different speaker profile. `かな` is the unmarked choice; `かしら` leans distinctly female and slightly formal or old-fashioned.

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>、いる**かな**。<br>*Will I need an umbrella today?*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>、いる**かしら**。<br>*Will I need an umbrella today?*

The sentences mean the same thing. What differs is what the listener infers about the speaker - and these are tendencies, not rules: plenty of women use `かな` as their default, and `かしら` is far more common in novels and drama than on the street.

---

## かな · ～かな vs ～ないかな

> **Rule of thumb** - put a negative in front of `かな` and wondering turns into **hoping**.

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>、やむ**かな**。<br>*I wonder if the rain will stop.*

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>、やまない**かなあ**。<br>*I do wish the rain would stop.*

Literally the second asks "won't it stop?", but what it expresses is a wish. The stretched `かなあ` and a falling, sighing delivery are the usual signals. Context still matters: <code><ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないかな</code> can also be a straight negative wondering - "I wonder if he isn't coming" - so listen for the tone.

---

## かな · Common mistakes

- ❌ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>、**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだかな**。 → ✅ この<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>、**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かかな**。<br>*な-adjectives and nouns drop `だ` in front of `か`, just as they do before `かどうか` and `かもしれない`.*

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>かな**。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>でしょうか**。<br>*`かな` is a casual, self-directed form. Pointed at a teacher it sounds as though you are talking past them.*

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、これ、**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てもらえないかな**。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、これ、**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ていただけないでしょうか**。<br>*The indirect-request use of `かな` works downward and sideways, not upward.*

- ❌ (in an email to a client) ご<ruby>都合<rt>つごう</rt></ruby>はいかが**かな**。 → ✅ ご<ruby>都合<rt>つごう</rt></ruby>はいかが**でしょうか**。<br>*`かな` has no place in writing that is not a message to a friend.*

# かしら

**Meaning** · I wonder - the same wondering particle as かな, in a distinctly softer and more feminine key

`かしら` is a worn-down <code>か<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らぬ</code> - "I don't know whether…". Like `かな` it turns a question inward, so that it hangs in the air rather than landing on anybody. What separates the two is not meaning but **who is heard saying it**: in modern standard Japanese `かしら` leans strongly female, and carries a slightly refined, slightly old-fashioned air with it.

---

## かしら · Formation

Plain forms only, exactly as with `かな`. Nouns and な-adjectives drop `だ`.

---

## かしら · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + かしら | <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>くかしら |
| Verb (past) | た-form + かしら | <ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めた → <ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めたかしら |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + かしら | <ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>らない → <ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>らないかしら |
| い-adjective | plain form + かしら | <ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>いかしら |
| な-adjective | drop だ + かしら | <ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>かしら |
| Noun | drop だ + かしら | どちら → どちらかしら |
| With a question word | どこ・どなた・いつ… + かしら | どなたかしら |
| Softened request | ～てくださらないかしら | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てくださらないかしら |
| With `ね` | かしらね | いらっしゃるかしらね |

---

## かしら · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `かしら` sounds genteel but is **not polite**. It attaches to plain forms, never to `です`／`ます`, and it does not belong in business speech or writing. The polite way to wonder aloud is still `でしょうか`.

---

## かしら · Examples

あの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>、どなた**かしら**。<br>*Who is that, I wonder.*

この<ruby>花<rt>はな</rt></ruby>、また<ruby>咲<rt>さ</rt></ruby>く**かしら**。<br>*Will these flowers bloom again, do you think?*

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めてきた**かしら**。<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>になってきたわ。<br>*Did I shut the window? I'm starting to worry.*

<ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>はちゃんと<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べている**かしら**。<br>*I do wonder whether my son is eating properly.*

---

## かしら · Examples (cont.)

すみません、この<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>、<ruby>空<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いている**かしら**。<br>*Excuse me - is this seat free?*

どちらの<ruby>色<rt>いろ</rt></ruby>がいい**かしら**。<ruby>迷<rt>まよ</rt></ruby>ってしまうわ。<br>*Which colour would be better? I can't make up my mind.*

ちょっと<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>貸<rt>か</rt></ruby>してくださらない**かしら**。<br>*Would you be so kind as to give me a hand?*

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まりには、あの<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>もいらっしゃる**かしら**ね。<br>*I wonder whether she'll be at next week's gathering too.*

---

## かしら · Nuance & register

Two separate things are going on, and keeping them apart saves a lot of confusion.

**Register.** `かしら` is casual. It sits on plain forms and belongs to relaxed speech, so despite its genteel ring it cannot be used where `です`／`ます` is required. What makes it *sound* refined is the company it keeps: it pairs naturally with polite vocabulary - どなた, いらっしゃる, くださる, ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>になる - and that combination produces the soft, well-mannered effect people associate with it.

---

## かしら · Nuance & register (cont.)

**Speaker profile.** In present-day standard Japanese `かしら` is heard mainly from women, and more from older speakers than younger ones; many younger women use `かな` as their default. In fiction and dubbing it is a stock signal for an elegant, mature or old-fashioned female character. These are tendencies, not rules - men did use it freely in earlier periods, and it survives in some regional and literary usage - but a learner who adopts `かしら` as a neutral "I wonder" will be heard as marked. If you want a wondering particle that raises no eyebrows, use `かな`.

`かしら` also does the two extra jobs `かな` does. With a negative it becomes a wish or a hint - <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないかしら - and in the form `～てくださらないかしら` it is an unusually soft request. Note the softness has a ceiling: it is gentle among equals, and still too informal to aim at a client.

---

## かしら · かしら vs かな

> **Rule of thumb** - identical grammar, identical meaning. The difference is entirely in who is expected to say it: `かな` is unmarked, `かしら` leans female and a touch formal.

この<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>すぎる**かな**。<br>*Is this parcel too heavy, I wonder.*

この<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>すぎる**かしら**。<br>*Is this parcel too heavy, I wonder.*

Nothing in the content changes. `かな` would be unremarkable from anyone; `かしら` sketches a speaker. Because the marking is one-directional - `かな` is safe for everybody, `かしら` is not - the practical advice is to **recognise** `かしら` fluently and **produce** `かな`, unless you are deliberately matching the speech style of the people around you.

---

## かしら · かしら vs でしょうか

> **Rule of thumb** - `かしら` is *soft*; `でしょうか` is *polite*. Softness is not politeness, and only one of them is safe upward.

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>からだった**かしら**。<br>*What time was the meeting again?*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>からだった**でしょうか**。<br>*Might I ask what time the meeting was again?*

The first is fine muttered to a colleague you know well and wrong addressed to a director. `でしょうか` keeps you in ます／です style while still avoiding a blunt question, which is why it is the workhorse of polite enquiry.

---

## かしら · ～かしら vs ～ないかしら

> **Rule of thumb** - as with `かな`, adding a negative turns the wondering into **hoping** or into a **hint**.

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さん、<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>がつく**かしら**。<br>*I wonder whether Tanaka will notice.*

<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>か<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>がついてくれない**かしら**。<br>*I do wish somebody would notice.*

The second is not really a question about anyone's likelihood of noticing; it is a wish, and said in front of the right person it is a request that never had to be made. This indirectness is the main reason `かしら` survives in polite female speech: it asks for things without asking.

---

## かしら · Common mistakes

- ❌ この<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>十分<rt>じゅうぶん</rt></ruby>だかしら**。 → ✅ この<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>十分<rt>じゅうぶん</rt></ruby>かしら**。<br>*な-adjectives and nouns lose `だ` before `か`, in `かしら` exactly as in `かな` and `かどうか`.*

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ますかしら**。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るかしら**。／**いらっしゃるでしょうか**。<br>*`かしら` attaches to plain forms. Adding `ます` in front does not make it polite; it just breaks it.*

- ❌ (in an email to a client) ご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>いただける**かしら**。 → ✅ ご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>いただけない**でしょうか**。<br>*However genteel it sounds, `かしら` is casual speech and reads as startlingly informal in business writing.*

---

## かしら · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ A learner using `かしら` as their everyday "I wonder" in mixed company. → ✅ `かな`.<br>*Not an error of grammar but of self-presentation. `かしら` tells the listener something about you that you may not have meant to say.*

---

# かい

**Meaning** · turns a plain-form sentence into a yes/no question - friendly, familiar, and noticeably old-fashioned

`かい` is the question particle `か` with the edges rounded off. A plain-form sentence ending in bare `か` sounds curt, almost like an interrogation; add the `い` and the same question comes out warm - the tone of a parent asking a child how school went. That warmth is the whole point of the particle, and it comes with a speaker profile attached.

---

## かい · Formation

Plain forms only. Nouns and な-adjectives drop `だ`.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + かい | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くかい |
| Verb (past) | た-form + かい | <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わった → <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったかい |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + かい | <ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>らない → <ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>らないかい |
| い-adjective | plain form + かい | <ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>いかい |
| な-adjective | drop だ + かい | <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>かい |
| Noun | drop だ + かい | <ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>本当<rt>ほんとう</rt></ruby>かい |
| Explanatory | ～のかい／～んかい | <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たのかい |
| Question-word counterpart | ～んだい | どこへ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くんだい |

---

## かい · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `かい` is for **yes/no questions only**. With a question word in the sentence you need `だい` instead: ❌<code>どこへ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くかい</code> → ✅<code>どこへ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くんだい</code>. The pair `かい`／`だい` splits the work that polite `ですか` does on its own.

---

## かい · Examples

<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>**かい**。<br>*How are you keeping?*

もう<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わった**かい**。<br>*Have you finished your homework yet?*

お<ruby>茶<rt>ちゃ</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む**かい**。<br>*Fancy a cup of tea?*

<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>でここまで<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たの**かい**。<br>*Did you come all this way on your own?*

---

## かい · Examples (cont.)

その<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>重<rt>おも</rt></ruby>くない**かい**。<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>とうか。<br>*Isn't that bag heavy? Let me take it.*

そう**かい**。それは<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>だったね。<br>*Is that right. That must have been rough.*

<ruby>久<rt>ひさ</rt></ruby>しぶりだね。<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>のほうは<ruby>順調<rt>じゅんちょう</rt></ruby>**かい**。<br>*Long time no see. Is work going all right?*

まだ<ruby>諦<rt>あきら</rt></ruby>めていない**かい**。それなら、もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>やってみなさい。<br>*So you haven't given up? In that case, have another go.*

---

## かい · Nuance & register

`かい` is plain-style and cannot be made polite: there is no ❌`かいです`, and `ますかい` is not a form of standard Tokyo Japanese. If you need politeness, the question is `ですか`／`ますか`.

Its social shape matters more than its grammar. In modern standard Japanese `かい` leans **male and older**, and it leans **downward or sideways** - a parent to a child, a grandparent to a grandchild, a teacher to a pupil, a senior colleague being kind to a junior. Younger speakers use it rarely in earnest; where they do, it is often a deliberate impression of that older voice. Translated fiction and dubbing are full of it for exactly this reason: it is the standard way to make a character sound avuncular. As always these are tendencies rather than rules, but they are strong enough that a learner using `かい` with a superior will sound not merely informal but oddly patronising.

---

## かい · Nuance & register (cont.)

The default casual yes/no question in ordinary modern speech is not `かい` at all - it is the plain form said with a rise: <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く？／<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた？ `かい` is the marked, warmer, older-sounding alternative to that.

One expression worth knowing on its own: `そうかい`. Said with interest it means "oh really?"; said flat it means "oh, is that so" and sounds dismissive, even sulky. The words are identical, so listen to the delivery.

---

## かい · かい vs だい

> **Rule of thumb** - `かい` answers *yes* or *no*. `だい` answers a **question word**. They are a matched pair and cannot be swapped.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>も<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る**かい**。<br>*Are you coming tomorrow as well?*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る**んだい**。<br>*What time are you coming tomorrow?*

---

## かい · かい vs だい (cont.)

`だい` normally follows the explanatory `の`／`ん`, which is why you see `んだい` far more often than bare `だい` after a verb; after a noun it stands alone - <ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>だい。 Both particles carry the same familiar, male-leaning, somewhat dated flavour, so they turn up together in the mouth of the same character.

---

## かい · かい vs か

> **Rule of thumb** - bare `か` on a plain form is **blunt**; `かい` is the same question made **kind**.

<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かった**か**。<br>*Got that?*

<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かった**かい**。<br>*Did that make sense?*

The first is the tone of an instructor or a superior checking compliance, and it can land as a challenge. The second checks the same fact with none of the pressure. This is worth remembering in reverse too: bare plain-form `か` is not a neutral casual question, and a learner reaching for informality often produces something much sharper than intended.

---

## かい · かい vs rising intonation

> **Rule of thumb** - the everyday casual question is the plain form with a rise. `かい` adds warmth **and** a generation.

これ、<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる？<br>*Are you going to eat this?*

これ、<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる**かい**。<br>*Would you like this?*

Both are casual and both are friendly. The difference is that the first is unmarked in any age group, while the second sketches an older speaker addressing someone younger. If you are a learner in your twenties talking to friends, the rise is what you want; keep `かい` for recognising, and for the moments when you are consciously playing the older role.

---

## かい · Common mistakes

- ❌ **どこで<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ったかい**。 → ✅ **どこで<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ったんだい**。<br>*A question word rules `かい` out. `かい` is strictly yes/no; `だい` handles the rest.*

- ❌ <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>だかい**。 → ✅ <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>かい**。<br>*な-adjectives and nouns lose `だ` in front of `か`, as they do before every other `か`-based ending.*

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、もう**お<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りかい**。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、もう**お<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りですか**。<br>*`かい` is friendly, but it is friendly *downward*. Aimed upward it manages to be informal and condescending at the same time.*

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ますかい**。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るかい**。／**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ますか**。<br>*`かい` attaches to plain forms. There is no polite version of it - you switch particles instead.*

---

{reveal}
<ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れていない**かい**。

{reveal}
A yes/no question in plain style, asked kindly and downward - the exact situation `かい` exists for. Bare <code><ruby>疲<rt>つか</rt></ruby>れていないか</code> would sound like a sergeant checking on a recruit.

**2.** Choose: <ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>は（どこへ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったかい・どこへ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったんだい）。

{reveal}
<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>はどこへ**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったんだい**。

{reveal}
The sentence contains どこ, so it is not a yes/no question and `かい` cannot be used. `だい` is the question-word member of the pair, and it normally follows the explanatory `ん`.

# じゃないか

**Meaning** · isn't it? - an assertion wearing the clothes of a negative question

`じゃないか` is not really a question. It states something and dares the listener to disagree: *look, it's raining* / *I did tell you* / *hey, that's rather good*. The negative-question shape is what makes it feel shared rather than imposed - you are inviting the other person to notice what you have noticed. Which of its several flavours you get depends almost entirely on tone and situation.

---

## じゃないか · Formation

Attaches to plain forms. Nouns and な-adjectives drop `だ`.

---

## じゃないか · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain form + じゃないか | <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>った → <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったじゃないか |
| い-adjective | plain form + じゃないか | いい → いいじゃないか |
| な-adjective | drop だ + じゃないか | <ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>じゃないか |
| Noun | drop だ + じゃないか | <ruby>嘘<rt>うそ</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>嘘<rt>うそ</rt></ruby>じゃないか |
| Polite | じゃないですか | <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったじゃないですか |
| Formal spoken | ではありませんか | <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったではありませんか |
| Written / rhetorical | ではないか | <ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったではないか |
| Very casual | じゃん | いいじゃん |
| Softer, rising | じゃない？ | <ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>じゃない？ |

---

## じゃないか · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Floating a suspicion | ～んじゃないか | <ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>っているんじゃないか |

> **Rule of thumb** - no `だ` in front. ❌<code><ruby>嘘<rt>うそ</rt></ruby>だじゃないか</code>. The `じゃ` is already the contracted `では` of the copula, so the copula cannot appear twice.

---

## じゃないか · Examples

ほら、やっぱり<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ってきた**じゃないか**。<br>*See - it's started raining after all.*

いい**じゃないか**。<ruby>一杯<rt>いっぱい</rt></ruby>くらい。<br>*Oh, come on. One drink won't hurt.*

あれ、<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>、ここにあった**じゃない**。<br>*Oh - the wallet was here all along.*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れるなって<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>った**じゃないか**。<br>*I told you yesterday not to be late, didn't I.*

---

## じゃないか · Examples (cont.)

なかなか<ruby>上手<rt>じょうず</rt></ruby>**じゃないか**。いつ<ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>ったんだ？<br>*Hey, that's not bad at all. When did you learn?*

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きな<ruby>本屋<rt>ほんや</rt></ruby>があった**じゃないですか**。あそこが<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>まるそうです。<br>*You know that big bookshop in front of the station? Apparently it's closing.*

そんなに<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>がなくてもいい**じゃないですか**。まだ<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>はありますよ。<br>*There's really no need to rush, is there. We've still got time.*

この<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>だと、<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>混乱<rt>こんらん</rt></ruby>するん**じゃないでしょうか**。<br>*Wouldn't this explanation leave the reader confused?*

---

## じゃないか · Nuance & register

The forms make a ladder, and the rungs are far apart.

`じゃないか` with the `か` said out loud is blunt and, in casual speech, leans male. `じゃない` with a rise - <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いじゃない？ - is the wider-used everyday version and sounds softer. `じゃん` is very casual and youthful. Going the other way, `じゃないですか` is the ordinary polite form, `ではありませんか` is formal and a little theatrical, and `ではないか` belongs to written argument, where it works as a rhetorical "is it not the case that…?" rather than as a question anybody answers.

Three things it does, all recognisable in context:

---

## じゃないか · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **Pointing out** - you have spotted something the listener has not.<br>*ここに<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてあるじゃないですか。*
- **Reproach or reminder** - you said this before and were ignored.<br>*だから<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>をつけろと<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったじゃないか。*
- **Discovery** - pleased surprise. <ruby>意外<rt>いがい</rt></ruby>とおいしいじゃないか。

---

## じゃないか · Nuance & register (cont.)

A warning about the polite form. `じゃないですか` is often used to open a topic by presenting something as shared knowledge - <code><ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>、<ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>があったじゃないですか</code>. That is fine when the listener genuinely does know. Used about things they could not possibly know - <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>、<ruby>甘<rt>あま</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>が<ruby>苦手<rt>にがて</rt></ruby>じゃないですか</code> - it is widely criticised in Japan as pushy, because it assigns the listener knowledge they never had. Say `んですが` instead.

---

## じゃないか · じゃないか vs plain じゃない

> **Rule of thumb** - same syllables, two different sentences. Falling `じゃない` is a plain **negative**. Rising `じゃない`, and anything with `か` attached, is an **assertion seeking agreement**.

これは<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のかばん**じゃない**。<br>*This isn't my bag.*

これ、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のかばん**じゃない**？<br>*Hang on - this is my bag, isn't it?*

---

## じゃないか · じゃないか vs plain じゃない (cont.)

Adding `か` removes the ambiguity entirely: <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>のかばんじゃないか</code> can only be the second reading. In writing, where intonation is unavailable, the question mark or the `か` is doing all the work, so leave it in.

---

## じゃないか · じゃないか vs でしょう

> **Rule of thumb** - both fish for agreement. `でしょう` assumes the listener **already knows** and asks them to confirm. `じゃないか` asserts and **presses**, often implying they had overlooked it.

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby><ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>した**でしょう**？<br>*I mentioned it yesterday, remember?*

<ruby>昨日<rt>きのう</rt></ruby><ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>した**じゃないか**。<br>*I did tell you yesterday!*

---

## じゃないか · じゃないか vs でしょう (cont.)

The first is a reminder; the second is close to an accusation. That extra push is why `じゃないか` is risky upward: telling a manager <code><ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>っているじゃないですか</code> is not "isn't this wrong?" but "this is wrong and you missed it."

---

## じゃないか · じゃないか vs ～んじゃないか

> **Rule of thumb** - bare `じゃないか` asserts something you are **sure** of. `んじゃないか` floats a **suspicion** you are not sure of at all.

この<ruby>数字<rt>すうじ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>っている**じゃないか**。<br>*This figure is wrong - look.*

この<ruby>数字<rt>すうじ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>っている**んじゃないか**。<br>*I've a feeling this figure might be wrong.*

The `ん` turns the whole thing tentative, and the polite `んじゃないでしょうか` is one of the most useful phrases in a Japanese workplace: it raises a doubt without accusing anyone of anything. When you want to question a superior's work, this is the form to reach for.

---

## じゃないか · Common mistakes

- ❌ この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだじゃないですか**。 → ✅ この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かじゃないですか**。<br>*`じゃ` is already the copula. な-adjectives and nouns attach bare.*

- ❌ もう**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりましたじゃないですか**。 → ✅ もう**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったじゃないですか**。<br>*The clause in front is plain. `です` on the end carries all the politeness the sentence needs.*

- ❌ <ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>計算<rt>けいさん</rt></ruby>、**<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>うじゃないですか**。 → ✅ <ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>計算<rt>けいさん</rt></ruby>、**<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>うんじゃないでしょうか**。<br>*Polite in form, confrontational in force. Adding `ん` and `でしょう` converts an accusation into a query.*

---

## じゃないか · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>、<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>出身<rt>しゅっしん</rt></ruby>**じゃないですか**。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>、<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>出身<rt>しゅっしん</rt></ruby>**なんですが**。<br>*You cannot present your own biography as something the listener already knows. This misuse is a well-known irritant.*

---

## じゃないか · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないじゃないですか。 (b) <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないんじゃないでしょうか。
