# お○○になる

**Meaning** · the general-purpose honorific frame - describes *someone else's* action while raising them

Japanese politeness runs on three separate systems. <ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> - です／ます - makes your *speech* polite to whoever is listening. <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> raises the **person you are talking about**. <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> lowers **you**. `お○○になる` is the workhorse of the second group: take almost any verb, and it becomes a respectful description of what your customer, your teacher or your boss did.

---

## お○○になる · Formation

Take the ます-stem, put `お` in front and `になる` behind. `になる` then conjugates as an ordinary う-verb (<ruby>五段<rt>ごだん</rt></ruby>, Group 1).

---

## お○○になる · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | お + ます-stem + になる | <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きます → お<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きになる |
| Verb (る-verb) | お + ます-stem + になる | <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べます → お<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べになる |
| する-verb, Chinese-derived root | ご + noun + になる | <ruby>利用<rt>りよう</rt></ruby>する → ご<ruby>利用<rt>りよう</rt></ruby>になる |
| する-verb, native root | お + noun + になる | <ruby>問<rt>と</rt></ruby>い<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わせする → お<ruby>問<rt>と</rt></ruby>い<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わせになる |
| Polite / past | お + stem + になります／になりました | お<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>いになりました |
| Verb with its own honorific | frame **not** used | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → <ruby>召<rt>め</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がる |
| One-mora ます-stem | frame **not** used | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る → ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>になる、<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>る → お<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みになる |

---

## お○○になる · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - if the ます-stem is only one mora long （<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>、<ruby>着<rt>き</rt></ruby>、<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>、<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>、し）, the frame will not carry it. Those verbs all have a dedicated honorific word instead: ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>になる、お<ruby>召<rt>め</rt></ruby>しになる、お<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みになる、いらっしゃる、なさる.

---

## お○○になる · Examples

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>に**お<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りになります**か。<br>*What time will you be leaving, sensei?*

この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>をもう**お<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みになりました**か。<br>*Have you read this book already?*

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>すぎに**お<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけになりました**。<br>*The department head went out in the early afternoon.*

お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>が**お<ruby>選<rt>えら</rt></ruby>びになった**のは、こちらの<ruby>色<rt>いろ</rt></ruby>でよろしいでしょうか。<br>*Is this the colour you chose, madam?*

---

## お○○になる · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>が**お<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めになった**<ruby>方法<rt>ほうほう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>進<rt>すす</rt></ruby>めます。<br>*We'll go ahead with the method the section chief settled on.*

エレベーターを**ご<ruby>利用<rt>りよう</rt></ruby>になる**<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>右<rt>みぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>通路<rt>つうろ</rt></ruby>へお<ruby>回<rt>まわ</rt></ruby>りください。<br>*Those using the lift, please go round to the right-hand corridor.*

<ruby>皆<rt>みな</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>が**お<ruby>集<rt>あつ</rt></ruby>まりになる**<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>午後<rt>ごご</rt></ruby><ruby>二時<rt>にじ</rt></ruby>でよろしいでしょうか。<br>*Would two o'clock suit as the time for everyone to gather?*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>計画<rt>けいかく</rt></ruby>について、<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>はどう**お<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えになっている**のでしょうか。<br>*I wonder what the president makes of the new plan.*

---

## お○○になる · Nuance & register

This is business and service language. In a shop, a hospital, a hotel, a <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby><ruby>室<rt>しつ</rt></ruby> or a classroom it is completely ordinary; among friends it is not. Saying お<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べになりますか to a classmate is not "extra polite", it is strange - it puts a counter between the two of you. Overusing <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> socially is a real mistake, not a safe default.

Three things follow from the fact that `お○○になる` is <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>:

- **Never about yourself.** ❌<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>がお<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りになりました is the<br>*single commonest <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> error learners make. You cannot raise your own head. Your own actions take <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> - お<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りしました*

- or plain ます.

---

## お○○になる · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **Never doubled.** ❌お<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みになられる stacks the frame on top of the<br>*`られる` honorific. That is <ruby>二重<rt>にじゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby>, and it sounds overcooked rather than extra respectful. One layer is enough.*
- **Not for your own side.** Speaking to a customer about a colleague, even your<br>*own boss, you drop the honorific: <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>はただいま<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>をはずしております - never お<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>をはずしになっています.*

On the politeness ladder, `お○○になる` sits above `れる／られる` and below the set honorific verbs. Treat that as a tendency rather than a measurement: in most offices お<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きになる and <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>かれる are simply two acceptable choices, and which one a person reaches for depends on the workplace and on how far above them the other person stands.

---

## お○○になる · お○○になる vs ～れる／～られる

> **Rule of thumb** - both are honorific. `られる` is the light, everyday office form; `お○○になる` is heavier, more deferential, and unambiguous.

<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>はもう**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>られました**。<br>*The president has already left for the day.*

<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>はもう**お<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りになりました**。<br>*The president has already left for the day.*

---

## お○○になる · お○○になる vs ～れる／～られる (cont.)

The second is the more respectful of the two, and it is what you would say to an outside visitor; the first is what one colleague says to another. The practical advantage of `お○○になる` is that it cannot be misread. <code><ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>られる</code> is also the passive and the potential, so <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>られました can be read two ways, while <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>がお<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しになりました cannot.

---

## お○○になる · お○○になる vs お○○する

> **Rule of thumb** - same stem, opposite direction. `になる` raises the other person; `する` lowers you.

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちになります**。<br>*The teacher is going to carry the bags himself.*

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちします**。<br>*I'll carry your bags for you, sensei.*

One says the teacher does the carrying; the other offers to do it for him. Swapping them reverses who is being honoured, which is why the pair is worth drilling until it is automatic.

---

## お○○になる · お○○になる vs the set honorific verbs

> **Rule of thumb** - if a verb owns a dedicated honorific word, the frame is closed to it. Learn that short list; use the frame for everything else.

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>はもうお<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>召<rt>め</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がりました**か。<br>*Has sensei had lunch yet?*

<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>はもう**ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>になりました**か。<br>*Have you had a look at the documents yet?*

---

## お○○になる · お○○になる vs the set honorific verbs (cont.)

<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる</code>, <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る</code>, <code><ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う</code>, `する`, <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く</code>, <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る</code>, `いる` and <code><ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>る</code> all belong to that closed set. Note that <code>ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>になる</code> ends in `になる` but is not built from a ます-stem - <code>ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby></code> is a noun, and the word has to be memorised whole.

---

## お○○になる · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りになりました**。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>りしました**。<br>*Honorific language points away from the speaker. Describing your own work with `お○○になる` is the error a Japanese listener notices fastest.*

- ❌ <ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>になりました**か。 → ✅ <ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>を**ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>になりました**か。<br>*<code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る</code> has a one-mora stem and its own honorific verb. The frame does not build on it.*

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が**お<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しになられました**。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が**お<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しになりました**。<br>*Two honorific layers on one verb is <ruby>二重<rt>にじゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby>. Pick one.*

---

## お○○になる · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>が**お<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>になりました**。 → ✅ <ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>が**ご<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>になりました**。<br>*A Chinese-derived する-verb root takes `ご`: ご<ruby>利用<rt>りよう</rt></ruby>、 ご<ruby>出発<rt>しゅっぱつ</rt></ruby>、ご<ruby>記入<rt>きにゅう</rt></ruby>.*

- ❌ ねえ、この<ruby>漫画<rt>まんが</rt></ruby>**お<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みになった**? → ✅ ねえ、この<ruby>漫画<rt>まんが</rt></ruby><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んだ?<br>*Grammatical, wrong for the situation. Full <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> aimed at a friend reads as sarcasm or as sudden distance.*

---

## お○○になる · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>新聞<rt>しんぶん</rt></ruby>を____<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>____になります。

{reveal}
<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>新聞<rt>しんぶん</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みになります**。

{reveal}
The frame is `お` + ます-stem + `になる`. The stem of <code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みます</code> is <code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み</code>, so `お` goes in front of it and `になります` behind.

---

## お○○になる · Practice 2

**2.** Choose: <ruby>係<rt>かかり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>が（ご<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>になります・ご<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>します）。

{reveal}
<ruby>係<rt>かかり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>が**ご<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>します**。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>係<rt>かかり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby></code> is a member of your own side, so the sentence has to lower rather than raise. `ご○○になる` would honour your own staff in front of a customer, which is exactly backwards.

---

## お○○になる · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "What time will the president be arriving?"

{reveal}
<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>に**お<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きになります**か。

{reveal}
<code><ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きます</code> gives the stem <code><ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>き</code>, hence お<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きになります. ご<ruby>到着<rt>とうちゃく</rt></ruby>になりますか works too, since <ruby>到着<rt>とうちゃく</rt></ruby> is a Chinese-derived root and takes `ご`.

---

## お○○になる · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>十時<rt>じゅうじ</rt></ruby>にお<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しになります。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>十時<rt>じゅうじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>ります。

{reveal}
Two faults in one clause. The verb describes you, so it needs <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>, not <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>. And <code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る</code> is outside the frame anyway: the honorific for someone else coming is お<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しになる or いらっしゃる, while the humble word for your own coming is <ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>る.

---

## お○○になる · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>はいつ<ruby>出発<rt>しゅっぱつ</rt></ruby>されますか。 (b) <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>はいつご<ruby>出発<rt>しゅっぱつ</rt></ruby>になりますか。

{reveal}
Both are honorific and both are correct. (a) uses the `される` form, which is light and quick - the register of a colleague, or of a routine business email.

{reveal}
(b) is the heavier frame, and it is what a hotel clerk or a receptionist would say to a guest. It sounds more careful and more deliberate. The gap is one of degree, not of grammar: choosing (a) with a customer would not offend anyone, it would only sound a little brisk.

# お○○ください

**Meaning** · "please do ○○" - an honorific request, the form used to customers, guests and audiences

`お○○ください` asks the listener to do something while raising them. It is the <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> counterpart of `～てください`: same job, but the listener is honoured rather than simply addressed politely. Every announcement you hear on a Japanese train, in a lift and in a shop is built out of this frame.

---

## お○○ください · Formation

`ください` is the imperative of `くださる`, the honorific of `くれる`. Put it after `お` + the ます-stem.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | お + ます-stem + ください | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちます → お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちください |
| Verb (る-verb) | お + ます-stem + ください | <ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べます → お<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べください |
| する-verb, Chinese-derived root | ご + noun + ください | <ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>する → ご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>ください |
| する-verb, native root | お + noun + ください | <ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>みする → お<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>みください |
| Set honorific verb | fixed form | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る → ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>ください |
| Extra-polite service form | + ませ | お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちくださいませ |
| Negative ("please don't") | frame **not** used | ご<ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>ください／～ないでください |

---

## お○○ください · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - one-mora stems are shut out here for the same reason as in `お○○になる`: ❌お<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ください、❌お<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ください do not exist. Use ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>ください and お<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しください.

---

## お○○ください · Examples

こちらで**お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちください**。<br>*Please wait here.*

どうぞ**おかけください**。<br>*Please have a seat.*

この<ruby>線<rt>せん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>内側<rt>うちがわ</rt></ruby>まで**お<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がりください**。<br>*Please step back behind this line.*

<ruby>温<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かいうちに**お<ruby>召<rt>め</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がりください**。<br>*Please eat it while it's still warm.*

---

## お○○ください · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>足元<rt>あしもと</rt></ruby>に**ご<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>ください**。<br>*Please mind your step.*

<ruby>順番<rt>じゅんばん</rt></ruby>にご<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>しますので、こちらに**お<ruby>並<rt>なら</rt></ruby>びください**。<br>*We'll show you through in turn, so please queue here.*

ご<ruby>不明<rt>ふめい</rt></ruby>な<ruby>点<rt>てん</rt></ruby>がございましたら、いつでも**お<ruby>問<rt>と</rt></ruby>い<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わせください**。<br>*If anything is unclear, please get in touch at any time.*

<ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>にお<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>りの<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>でこちらの<ruby>用紙<rt>ようし</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しください**。<br>*When you enter the hall, please hand this form in at reception.*

---

## お○○ください · Nuance & register

This frame is service and announcement language, and it carries a faint flavour of *instruction*. A station guard, a shop assistant, a nurse calling a patient and a website's help page all use it. Between friends it is bizarre - you do not tell a classmate ❌ちょっとお<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちください unless you are joking.

The instructional flavour matters when you want a personal favour. `ください` is still an imperative underneath, so a bare お<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きください to your boss can land like a directive from below. For asking a superior for something, shift to a question form:

---

## お○○ください · Nuance & register (cont.)

- お<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きいただけますか - "could you write it?"
- お<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きいただけませんでしょうか - the softest, and standard in email.
- <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いていただけると<ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>かります - "it would help me if you would."

`ませ` on the end（お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちくださいませ、いらっしゃいませ）belongs to shop and hotel speech specifically. It is warm and formal at once, and it is heard far more from staff than from anyone else.

---

## お○○ください · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two words about お<ruby>召<rt>め</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がりください and お<ruby>求<rt>もと</rt></ruby>めください. Strictly, <ruby>召<rt>め</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がる is already honorific, so adding `お` makes a double layer; usage guides nonetheless list these as established by custom, and you will hear them constantly. Treat them as fixed phrases rather than as models for building new ones.

One more tendency worth knowing: many speakers avoid お<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>りください in favour of おかけください, because お<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>り on its own is what you say to a dog. Plenty of hospitals and offices say お<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>りください anyway, so it is a preference rather than a rule - but おかけください is never the wrong choice.

---

## お○○ください · お○○ください vs ～てください

> **Rule of thumb** - `～てください` is polite; `お○○ください` is polite **and** honorific. The first addresses the listener courteously, the second raises them.

ここに<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>いてください**。<br>*Please write your name here.*

こちらにお<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きください**。<br>*Please write your name here.*

---

## お○○ください · お○○ください vs ～てください (cont.)

The first is what a teacher says to a class, or a friend to a friend. The second is what a bank clerk says to a customer - notice how `ここ` becomes `こちら` and <ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby> takes its prefix. <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> rarely arrives alone; the whole sentence shifts register together.

---

## お○○ください · お○○ください vs お○○いただけますか

> **Rule of thumb** - `ください` tells; `いただけますか` asks. When the favour is for *your* benefit, ask.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>までに**ご<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>ください**。<br>*Please reply by tomorrow.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>までに**ご<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>いただけますでしょうか**。<br>*Might I ask you to reply by tomorrow?*

---

## お○○ください · お○○ください vs お○○いただけますか (cont.)

Both are polite. The first states what needs to happen and suits a notice or a form; the second leaves the decision with the listener and suits a request to a client or a superior. `いただく` is <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>, so the second sentence lowers you at the same time as it raises them - which is why it feels so much softer.

---

## お○○ください · お○○ください vs ご遠慮ください

> **Rule of thumb** - the frame has no negative. To ask someone *not* to do something, use a different verb: ご<ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>ください or お<ruby>控<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>えください.

<ruby>車内<rt>しゃない</rt></ruby>でのお<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>は**ご<ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>ください**。<br>*Please refrain from making phone calls on board.*

<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>撮影<rt>さつえい</rt></ruby>は**お<ruby>控<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>えください**。<br>*Please refrain from taking photographs.*

---

## お○○ください · お○○ください vs ご遠慮ください (cont.)

There is no ❌お<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しにならないでください in ordinary use. Japanese public notices prefer to name a polite verb of refraining rather than to negate the request - it reads as an appeal to the listener's discretion instead of a ban.

---

## お○○ください · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に**お<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ください**。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に**お<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しください**。<br>*<code><ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る</code> has no place in the frame. - ❌ こちらのボタンを**ご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>してください**。 → ✅ こちらのボタンを**ご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>ください**。<br>*`ご○○する` is the *humble* frame, so <code>ご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>してください</code> tells the listener to humble themselves. Drop the `して`.*

- ❌ メニューを**お<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ください**。 → ✅ メニューを**ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>ください**。<br>*One-mora stem, dedicated honorific verb. The same goes for お<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べください → お<ruby>召<rt>め</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がりください.*

---

## お○○ください · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>に**おサインください**。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>に**ご<ruby>署名<rt>しょめい</rt></ruby>いただけますでしょうか**。<br>*Two problems: loanwords do not take the prefix, and a direct `ください` to your own boss for your own paperwork is too flat. # いらっしゃる

**Meaning** · the honorific verb covering いる, <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く and <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る all at once

`いらっしゃる` is <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>: it raises the person it describes, so it is used about your teacher, your client, your guest - never about yourself. Three ordinary verbs collapse into it, and Japanese speakers do not find that ambiguous, because the situation always says which one is meant.

---

## いらっしゃる · Formation

| Plain verb | Honorific | Example |
|---|---|---|
| いる | いらっしゃる | <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>にいらっしゃいます |
| <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く | いらっしゃる | どちらへいらっしゃいますか |
| <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る | いらっしゃる | よくいらっしゃいました |
| ～ている | ～ていらっしゃる | <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っていらっしゃいます |
| Noun + です (about a person) | ～でいらっしゃる | <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>でいらっしゃいます |

It is a う-verb, but its ます-form is irregular - the `り` you expect becomes `い`. Four verbs share this quirk, and they are all <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby>:

---

## いらっしゃる · Formation (cont.)

| Dictionary | ます-form | Never |
|---|---|---|
| いらっしゃる | いらっしゃいます | ❌いらっしゃります |
| おっしゃる | おっしゃいます | ❌おっしゃります |
| なさる | なさいます | ❌なさります |
| くださる | くださいます | ❌くださります |

---

## いらっしゃる · Formation (cont.)

| Other forms | Shape |
|---|---|
| Negative polite | いらっしゃいません |
| Plain negative | いらっしゃらない |
| て-form | いらっしゃって（contracted: いらして） |
| Request | いらしてください／お<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しください |
| Greeting form | いらっしゃい／いらっしゃいませ |

---

## いらっしゃる · Examples

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>教室<rt>きょうしつ</rt></ruby>に**いらっしゃいます**か。<br>*Is the teacher in the classroom?*

どちらへ**いらっしゃいます**か。<br>*Where are you off to?*

<ruby>遠<rt>とお</rt></ruby>いところ、よく**いらっしゃいました**。<br>*Thank you for coming all this way.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>会<rt>かい</rt></ruby>には<ruby>何名<rt>なんめい</rt></ruby>ほど**いらっしゃいます**か。<br>*Roughly how many people will be coming to tomorrow's gathering?*

---

## いらっしゃる · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、<ruby>会議室<rt>かいぎしつ</rt></ruby>には**いらっしゃいません**。<br>*The department head isn't in the meeting room at the moment.*

<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby><ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>から**いらっしゃる**そうです。<br>*I hear Professor Yamada is coming up from Osaka next week.*

お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>ほどから<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っていらっしゃいます**。<br>*The customer has been waiting at reception for a little while now.*

<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>ですが、<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>で**いらっしゃいます**か。<br>*Excuse me - would you be Mr Tanaka?*

---

## いらっしゃる · Nuance & register

`いらっしゃる` is the first <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> verb most learners can actually use, because it turns up everywhere: shop doorways, phone calls, introductions, reception desks. It belongs to business, service and formal social speech. Saying it about a friend - ❌ゆうべ<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちが<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>にいらっしゃった - is not extra-polite, it is odd, because you are honouring someone who is on your own level.

Some texture:

---

## いらっしゃる · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **いらっしゃいませ** is not an invitation to come. It is a shop and restaurant<br>*greeting, roughly "welcome", and it expects no reply at all.*
- **いらっしゃい** on its own is warm and informal - a host to a guest, an adult to<br>*a child, a market stall to passers-by.*
- **いらして／いらしてください** is the contracted て-form. It is softer and a<br>*little lighter than いらっしゃって, and it leans towards polite conversational speech rather than announcements.*
- **お<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しになる, おいでになる, お<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えになる** are alternatives for<br>*the "come" sense, each a shade more formal than plain いらっしゃる.*

---

## いらっしゃる · Nuance & register (cont.)

The trap that catches every English speaker is the **in-group rule**. Japanese politeness is not about rank in the abstract; it is about which side of a line the person stands on. Inside your company, <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby> outranks you and takes <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>. The moment you speak to somebody *outside* the company - a client, a supplier, a caller - your whole company becomes your in-group, and your boss is lowered along with you:

- To a colleague: <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>はもうお<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りになりました。
- To a client: <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>は<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby><ruby>外出<rt>がいしゅつ</rt></ruby>しております。

---

## いらっしゃる · Nuance & register (cont.)

No title, no `さん`, and `おります` rather than `いらっしゃいます`. It feels disloyal to an English speaker; in Japanese it is exactly the loyal thing to do, because lowering your own side is how you raise the customer.

---

## いらっしゃる · いらっしゃる vs おる

> **Rule of thumb** - `いらっしゃる` for people you are raising; `おる` for yourself and for anyone on your own side of the line.

<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三階<rt>さんがい</rt></ruby>に**いらっしゃいます**。<br>*The president is on the third floor.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>どもは<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>も<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>に**おります**。<br>*We'll be at the shop tomorrow as well.*

---

## いらっしゃる · いらっしゃる vs おる (cont.)

The same phone call can need both, one clause apart. An outside caller asking <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんはいらっしゃいますか is right - from where they stand, <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby> is an outsider and deserves the honorific. Your reply, <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>はただいま<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>をはずしております, is also right, because from where *you* stand he is family.

---

## いらっしゃる · いらっしゃる vs 参る・伺う

> **Rule of thumb** - `いらっしゃる` is them going or coming; <code><ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>る</code> and <code><ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>う</code> are you going or coming.

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>に**いらっしゃいます**。<br>*Sensei will be arriving at three.*

<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>研究室<rt>けんきゅうしつ</rt></ruby>へ**<ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>います**。<br>*I'll come to your office at three.*

<code><ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>う</code> is used when the place you are going belongs to somebody you respect; <code><ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>る</code> is the plainer humble word and works even when nobody in particular is being honoured - <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>ります on a platform announcement is about a train, not a person.

---

## いらっしゃる · いらっしゃる vs おいでになる・お見えになる

> **Rule of thumb** - all three are honorific. `いらっしゃる` is the default; `おいでになる` is more formal; <code>お<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えになる</code> only ever means "come".

<ruby>会長<rt>かいちょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>に**おいでになりました**。<br>*The chairman has arrived at the venue.*

お<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>が**お<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えになりました**。<br>*Your appointment has arrived.*

---

## いらっしゃる · いらっしゃる vs おいでになる・お見えになる (cont.)

Because `いらっしゃる` covers three meanings, the alternatives are sometimes chosen simply for clarity: お<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えになりました can only be an arrival, so nobody has to work out whether the person came, went or is present.

---

## いらっしゃる · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>**いらっしゃります**。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>**いらっしゃいます**。<br>*The `り` becomes `い` in the ます-form. The same applies to おっしゃいます, なさいます and くださいます.*

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に**いらっしゃいます**。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>ります**。<br>*<ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> about yourself is the classic error. You cannot raise your own head - use the humble verb.*

- ❌ （to a client）<ruby>弊社<rt>へいしゃ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>**いらっしゃいません**。 → ✅ <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>佐藤<rt>さとう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby>**おりません**。<br>*The in-group rule. Talking to an outsider, your own president is lowered like everyone else on your side - and loses the `さん`.*

---

## いらっしゃる · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ こちらの<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>は**いらっしゃいます**か。 → ✅ こちらの<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>は**ございます**か。<br>*`いらっしゃる` honours a person. Objects take `ある` and its polite form `ございます`; there is nobody there to raise.*

(b) is the same word without the `ませ`, and dropping `ませ` drops the formality. It is what a host says opening the door to a guest, or a parent to a child's friend: friendly and personal. A market trader shouting いらっしゃい, いらっしゃい is using it as a call to passers-by, which is friendlier still.

# なさる

**Meaning** · the honorific of する - "to do", said about someone you are raising

`なさる` is <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>. Because `する` combines with hundreds of nouns, `なさる` gives you an honorific for every one of them at a stroke: <ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>なさる、<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>なさる、ご<ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>なさる. It is also the verb behind the two most useful service questions in Japanese, どうなさいますか and いかがなさいましたか.

---

## なさる · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| する | なさる | する → なさる |
| Noun + する | Noun + なさる | <ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>なさる |
| Noun + する, with prefix | ご／お + Noun + なさる | <ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>する → ご<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>なさる |
| ～ている | ～なさっている | <ruby>研究<rt>けんきゅう</rt></ruby>している → <ruby>研究<rt>けんきゅう</rt></ruby>なさっている |
| Choosing between options | Noun + に + なさる | コーヒーにする → コーヒーになさる |

Like `いらっしゃる`, it is a う-verb with an irregular ます-form:

---

## なさる · Formation (cont.)

| Form | Shape |
|---|---|
| Polite | なさいます（❌なさります） |
| Past polite | なさいました |
| Negative | なさらない／なさいません |
| て-form | なさって |
| Negative request | ～なさらないでください |
| Softened command | ます-stem + なさい（<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べなさい） |

---

## なさる · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - `～なさい` is built from this verb but does **not** honour anyone. It is a gentle order aimed *downward*: a parent to a child, a teacher to a class, a test paper to a candidate. おやすみなさい and ごめんなさい are fossilised leftovers of the same form.

---

## なさる · Examples

どう**なさいました**か。<br>*What seems to be the trouble?*

コーヒーと<ruby>紅茶<rt>こうちゃ</rt></ruby>、どちらに**なさいます**か。<br>*Coffee or tea - which would you like?*

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>を**なさいます**。<br>*Sensei goes for a walk every morning.*

どうぞご<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>**なさらないで**ください。<br>*Please don't worry about it.*

---

## なさる · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>にゴルフを**なさる**そうです。<br>*I gather the president plays golf at weekends.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>集合<rt>しゅうごう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>に**なさいます**か。<br>*What time would you like to meet tomorrow?*

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>長年<rt>ながねん</rt></ruby>**<ruby>研究<rt>けんきゅう</rt></ruby>なさっている**テーマについて<ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>いたいと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っております。<br>*I'd like to ask you about the subject you have been researching for so many years.*

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>はこの<ruby>件<rt>けん</rt></ruby>をどのように<ruby>処理<rt>しょり</rt></ruby>**なさる**おつもりでしょうか。<br>*How does the department head intend to handle this matter?*

---

## なさる · Nuance & register

`なさる` is squarely business, service and formal-social language. Shop staff, receptionists, doctors, flight attendants and hotel clerks use it constantly, mostly in the fixed openers どうなさいますか（"what would you like to do?"）, いかがなさいますか（the more formal version）and どうなさいましたか（"what's wrong?"）. To a friend, ❌<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>をなさっているんですか would be comic.

Because it is <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> it obeys the same two hard limits as the rest of the system. It never describes your own action - that is `いたす` - and it never describes your own side when you are speaking to an outsider. On the phone to a customer, ❌<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>が<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>なさいます honours your own colleague; the sentence you want is <ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>が<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>いたします.

---

## なさる · Nuance & register (cont.)

`される` is the lighter alternative: <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>はどうされますか is perfectly polite and rather more everyday than <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>はどうなさいますか. As a tendency, `なさる` is felt as warmer and more attentive, and it is heard more often from service staff and in careful formal speech; `される` is the workhorse of ordinary office Japanese. Neither is wrong in the other's setting.

Do not stack layers. ❌おなさりになる and ❌なさられる are <ruby>二重<rt>にじゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby>; `なさる` on its own is already the honorific.

---

## なさる · なさる vs いたす

> **Rule of thumb** - `なさる` for what *they* do; `いたす` for what *you* do. Same verb, opposite directions.

お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>はどちらに**なさいます**か。<br>*Which will you have, madam?*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>どもがご<ruby>用意<rt>ようい</rt></ruby>**いたします**。<br>*We'll get it ready for you.*

This pair is the cleanest illustration of the whole three-way system. Both sentences are polite to the customer; the first gets there by lifting her, the second by lowering the speaker. Using `なさる` about yourself does not merely sound odd - it claims a status you have just given to somebody else.

---

## なさる · なさる vs される

> **Rule of thumb** - both are honorific. `される` is lighter and unmarked; `なさる` is more attentive and more distinctly <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby>.

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>はいつ<ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>**されます**か。<br>*When are you going on the business trip, <ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>?*

<ruby>会長<rt>かいちょう</rt></ruby>はいつ<ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>**なさいます**か。<br>*When will the chairman be going on his trip?*

---

## なさる · なさる vs される (cont.)

There is one practical reason to reach for `なさる`: `される` is also the passive. <ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>されました can mean "you kindly took care" or "I got told off", and <ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>なさいました can only mean the first.

---

## なさる · なさる vs ～なさい

> **Rule of thumb** - `なさる` looks up; `～なさい` looks down. They come from the same root and point in opposite social directions.

<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に**しなさい**。<br>*Do your homework first.*

<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>はもう**なさいました**か。<br>*Have you done your homework yet? (said respectfully)*

---

## なさる · なさる vs ～なさい (cont.)

`～なさい` is softer than the bare imperative しろ, which is why parents and teachers like it - but softness is not respect. Aimed upward it is an insult, so ❌<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>、こちらに<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きなさい must be <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>、こちらにお<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>きください.

---

## なさる · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出発<rt>しゅっぱつ</rt></ruby>**なさりますか**。 → ✅ <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出発<rt>しゅっぱつ</rt></ruby>**なさいますか**。<br>*The `り` turns into `い` before ます. Same irregularity as いらっしゃいます and くださいます.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>がご<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>**なさいます**。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>がご<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>**いたします**。<br>*<ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> pointed at yourself. Your own actions take <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>.*

- ❌ お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>、どう**しましたか**。 → ✅ お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>、どう**なさいましたか**。<br>*Grammatically fine, socially thin. `しましたか` is only <ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> - polite speech with no honorific in it - and a customer expects to be raised, not merely addressed politely.*

---

## なさる · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>**なさられました**。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>**なさいました**。<br>*`なさる` plus `られる` is two honorifics on one verb. One is the ceiling.*

# 敬語の動詞

**Meaning** · the closed set of verbs that have their own honorific and humble replacements

A dozen or so of the commonest Japanese verbs do not use the お○○になる／お○○する frames at all. They have **separate words** instead - one that raises the person you are talking about, and one that lowers you. Learn them as pairs and most of <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> is done.

---

## 敬語の動詞 · Formation

These verbs are not built; they are swapped in. The plain verb disappears and a different word takes its place.

| Verb with a set humble | plain verb → <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> word | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>拝見<rt>はいけん</rt></ruby>する |

| Noun + する (humble) | ご／お + noun + する・いたす | <ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>する → ご<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>いたす |

## 敬語の動詞 · Nuance & register

Hold the three-way system in mind and none of this is guesswork. <ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>（です・ます）makes your speech polite to the listener. <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> raises the subject of the verb. <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> lowers the subject of the verb, which raises whoever the action is aimed at. Choosing a form is therefore a question about *who is doing the verb*, not about how polite you feel.

Two refinements worth having early:

---

## 敬語の動詞 · Nuance & register (cont.)

**The humble column has two halves.** <ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>う, <ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げる, <ruby>拝見<rt>はいけん</rt></ruby>する and お<ruby>目<rt>め</rt></ruby>にかかる need a specific person on the receiving end who deserves the respect. <ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>す and おる do not - they simply lower and formalise, which is why <ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>ります works on a platform announcement even though a train respects nobody. That second group is sometimes labelled <ruby>丁重<rt>ていちょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>, and it is the group you use for your own side in front of an outsider.

---

## 敬語の動詞 · Nuance & register (cont.)

**In-group before rank.** English speakers expect politeness to track seniority. Japanese tracks the boundary of your group first. Talking to a client, your own <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby> is lowered exactly like you: not ❌<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>がおっしゃっていました but <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中村<rt>なかむら</rt></ruby>が<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>しておりました - no `さん`, humble verb, and the title placed before the surname rather than after it. Inside the office, with only colleagues listening, おっしゃいました is right again. Nothing about the boss changed; the audience did.

Some smaller points:

---

## 敬語の動詞 · Nuance & register (cont.)

- `いただく` does double duty - humble "eat/drink" and humble "receive". The<br>*context separates them.*
- <ruby>存<rt>ぞん</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げる is used about **people**<br>*（お<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>存<rt>ぞん</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げております）, and <ruby>存<rt>ぞん</rt></ruby>じる about **facts**.*
- <ruby>承<rt>うけたまわ</rt></ruby>る is heavy formal business language - order-taking, phone<br>*messages - and is rare in speech otherwise.*
- お<ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>いします is strictly a double layer, but it is so established that<br>*usage guides let it stand. Do not extend the pattern to new verbs.*

And the honest register note: this is the language of work and of service. It is not a politer version of everyday Japanese that you can switch on at will. Using it with friends puts a formal wall between you, and Japanese speakers read that wall as coldness or as mockery.

---

## 敬語の動詞 · 召し上がる vs いただく

> **Rule of thumb** - <ruby>召<rt>め</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がる is *them* eating; いただく is *you* eating. Both mean "eat", and they are never interchangeable.

<ruby>冷<rt>さ</rt></ruby>めないうちに**<ruby>召<rt>め</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がって**ください。<br>*Do please eat before it gets cold.*

おいしいお<ruby>菓子<rt>かし</rt></ruby>を**いただきました**。<br>*I had some lovely cakes.*

---

## 敬語の動詞 · 召し上がる vs いただく (cont.)

The commonest single <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> error learners make is turning the honorific on themselves, and this is where it happens most: ❌<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>召<rt>め</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がりました. You have just told the room you consider yourself the honoured guest. The word for your own eating is いただきました - the same word as in the いただきます said before a meal.

---

## 敬語の動詞 · おっしゃる vs 申す・申し上げる

> **Rule of thumb** - おっしゃる is their speaking; <ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>す is yours. <ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げる adds a person you are speaking *to* and deserves.

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か**おっしゃいました**か。<br>*Did sensei say anything?*

はじめまして、<ruby>鈴木<rt>すずき</rt></ruby>と**<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>します**。<br>*How do you do - my name is Suzuki.*

Introducing yourself with ❌<ruby>鈴木<rt>すずき</rt></ruby>とおっしゃいます would mean "I am called Suzuki, the honourable gentleman". The reverse error is just as common: asking a customer ❌<ruby>何<rt>なん</rt></ruby>と<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>しますか lowers the customer.

---

## 敬語の動詞 · 伺う vs 参る

> **Rule of thumb** - both are humble "go/come". <code><ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>う</code> requires a person at the destination whom you respect; <code><ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>る</code> does not.

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>のお<ruby>宅<rt>たく</rt></ruby>へ**<ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>います**。<br>*I'll visit your home next week, sensei.*

<ruby>三番<rt>さんばん</rt></ruby><ruby>線<rt>せん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>ります**。<br>*A train is arriving at platform three.*

---

## 敬語の動詞 · 伺う vs 参る (cont.)

Announcements, weather reports and formal self-description use <ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>る freely; you cannot substitute <ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>う into any of them, because there is nobody to honour. Note too that <ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>う has a second job - humble "ask" - so ちょっと<ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>いますが is "may I ask you something?"

---

## 敬語の動詞 · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>になりました**。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>拝見<rt>はいけん</rt></ruby>しました**。<br>*<ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> about yourself. Looking at somebody's documents is your own action, so it takes the humble word.*

- ❌ （to a customer）<ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>さんが**そうおっしゃいました**。 → ✅ <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>佐藤<rt>さとう</rt></ruby>が**そう<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>しておりました**。<br>*The in-group rule. Your own president is lowered in front of an outsider, referred to by bare surname, with the title in front.*

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>のお<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>きになりました**。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>のお<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>いました**。<br>*Same error with a different verb. When *you* are the listener, the humble verb is <ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>う.*

---

## 敬語の動詞 · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ その<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>のお<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>存<rt>ぞん</rt></ruby>じております**。 → ✅ その<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>のお<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>存<rt>ぞん</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げております**。<br>*Knowing *a person* takes <ruby>存<rt>ぞん</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げる; <ruby>存<rt>ぞん</rt></ruby>じる on its own is for facts and things.*

- ❌ ねえ、もう**<ruby>召<rt>め</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がった**? → ✅ ねえ、もう<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた?<br>*Correct grammar, wrong world. Full <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> between friends reads as a joke at the listener's expense.*

# お○○する

**Meaning** · the general-purpose humble frame - describes *your own* action while lowering you

`お○○する` is the mirror image of `お○○になる`. Same prefix, same ます-stem, but `する` on the end instead of `になる`, and the whole thing points the other way: it lowers the speaker, and by lowering the speaker it raises the person the action is done for. This is <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>, and it is the half of <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> that learners most often leave out.

---

## お○○する · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | お + ます-stem + する | <ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちます → お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちする |
| Verb (る-verb) | お + ます-stem + する | <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>けます → お<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>けする |
| する-verb, Chinese-derived root | ご + noun + する | <ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>する → ご<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>する |
| する-verb, native root | お + noun + する | <ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>いする → お<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>いする |
| More humble | お／ご + stem + いたす | お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちいたします |
| Most formal (written) | お／ご + stem + <ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げる | ご<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby><ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げます |
| Ongoing / standing state | お／ご + stem + しております | お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちしております |
| Verb with a set humble word | frame **not** used | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>拝見<rt>はいけん</rt></ruby>する |

---

## お○○する · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the frame needs a **recipient**: somebody the action is done to, for, or in front of, whom you respect. お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちします works because you carry it *for them*. ❌お<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>します and ❌お<ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>します do not, because nobody is on the receiving end.

---

## お○○する · Examples

お<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちします**。<br>*Let me take your bag.*

この<ruby>傘<rt>かさ</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>貸<rt>か</rt></ruby>しします**。<br>*I'll lend you this umbrella.*

<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>を**ご<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>します**。<br>*I'll explain how it works.*

ロビーで**お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちしております**。<br>*I'll be waiting in the lobby.*

---

## お○○する · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>までに**お<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>けします**。<br>*I'll get the documents to you by tomorrow.*

<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し**お<ruby>尋<rt>たず</rt></ruby>ねしたい**ことがあるのですが、よろしいでしょうか。<br>*There's something I'd like to ask you, if I may.*

<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>会場<rt>かいじょう</rt></ruby>まで、<ruby>係<rt>かかり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>が**ご<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>します**。<br>*A member of staff will show you from reception through to the hall.*

<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいサービスにつきましては、あらためて<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りします**ので、ご<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>いただければ<ruby>幸<rt>さいわ</rt></ruby>いです。<br>*I'll send you the material on the new service separately, so I'd be grateful if you would take a look.*

---

## お○○する · Nuance & register

Everything here belongs to work, service and formal dealings with strangers. In a shop, on a client call, in a business email it is the normal way to describe what you are about to do; between friends it is not, and お<ruby>貸<rt>か</rt></ruby>ししようか to a classmate sounds like you are role-playing a hotel clerk.

The ladder inside the frame is worth knowing:

- **お<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りします** - standard humble. Fine for almost everything.
- **お<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りいたします** - `いたす` is itself the humble `する`, so this is<br>*one notch lower and one notch more formal. Common in business speech.*
- **お<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>り<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げます** - ceremonial and written. Notices,<br>*formal letters, wedding invitations.*

---

## お○○する · Nuance & register (cont.)

Treat that as a gradient rather than three fixed rungs: most speakers move between the first two without thinking about it, and the third is rare enough that using it in conversation would draw attention.

`しております` is the humble `～ている`, and it is everywhere in service Japanese because so much of that language describes standing states rather than single acts - お<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちしております, ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>をお<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちしております, お<ruby>問<rt>と</rt></ruby>い<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わせをお<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>けしております.

---

## お○○する · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two limits to keep straight. First, the frame never describes somebody else's action: ❌<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>がお<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しします turns your teacher into your servant. Second, an action with no human on the other end will not take it, which is why "I'll go home early today" cannot be ❌<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>くお<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りします - say <ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>します or <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>らせていただきます instead.

---

## お○○する · お○○する vs お○○になる

> **Rule of thumb** - identical up to the last word. `する` = my action, lowered. `になる` = their action, raised.

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>住所<rt>じゅうしょ</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らせします**。<br>*I'll let sensei know my new address.*

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>住所<rt>じゅうしょ</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らせになりました**。<br>*Sensei let us know his new address.*

Get the pair backwards and you say the opposite of what you mean. It is worth practising them as a drill: pick a verb, build both, and say out loud who is doing which.

---

## お○○する · お○○する vs ～させていただく

> **Rule of thumb** - `お○○する` says "I'll do it for you"; `～させていただく` says "I'll take the liberty, with your permission".

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>を**ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>します**。<br>*I'll let you know next week's schedule.*

<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>めに**<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>めさせていただきます**。<br>*We'll be closing early today.*

`させていただく` frames the action as something you are permitted to do, so it fits things that affect the listener without being *for* them - closing early, taking leave, declining. It has spread so far in recent business Japanese that it is regularly criticised as overused; where the action really is a service you are performing, `お○○する` is the cleaner choice.

---

## お○○する · お○○する vs plain ます

> **Rule of thumb** - `ます` is <ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> only: polite speech, no humility. `お○○する` adds the humility.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ります**。<br>*I'll send the material tomorrow.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りします**。<br>*I'll send the material to you tomorrow.*

Both are polite; neither is rude. The first is what you write to a colleague, the second to a customer. This is the distinction to keep hold of when people say <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> is "polite Japanese": です／ます politeness and <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> humility are separate dials, and you can turn one without the other.

---

## お○○する · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>内容<rt>ないよう</rt></ruby>を**ご<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>します**。（to a colleague）→ ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>内容<rt>ないよう</rt></ruby>を**ご<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>になります**。<br>*The frame lowers whoever is doing the verb. Applied to your department head in front of colleagues, it demotes him.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が**お<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>します**。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が**ご<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>します**。<br>*<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby> is a Chinese-derived root, so the prefix is `ご`. Compare ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>します, ご<ruby>報告<rt>ほうこく</rt></ruby>します, ご<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>します.*

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>論文<rt>ろんぶん</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>しました**。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>論文<rt>ろんぶん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>拝見<rt>はいけん</rt></ruby>しました**。<br>*One-mora stems are shut out of the frame, and <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る has its own humble verb.*

---

## お○○する · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>用事<rt>ようじ</rt></ruby>があるので、<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**お<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>りします**。 → ✅ <ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>用事<rt>ようじ</rt></ruby>があるので、**お<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>します**。<br>*Going home is not done *for* anybody, so there is no recipient for the humility to reach. Worse, お<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>り is strongly associated with the honorific, so the sentence sounds as if you are honouring yourself.*

---

## お○○する · Practice 3

# いたす

**Meaning** · the humble of する - "to do", said about your own action

`いたす` is <ruby>謙譲<rt>けんじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>. It replaces `する` when the person doing the verb is you, or anyone on your side of the group boundary, and by lowering that person it raises whoever you are speaking to. It is the direct opposite number of `なさる`, and between them the two cover every `する` verb in the language.

---

## いたす · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| する | いたす | する → いたす |
| Noun + する | Noun + いたす | <ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>いたす |
| Noun + する, with prefix | ご／お + Noun + いたす | <ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>する → ご<ruby>案内<rt>あんない</rt></ruby>いたす |
| Verb (humble frame) | お + ます-stem + いたす | <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>けます → お<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>けいたす |
| ～ている | いたしております | <ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>している → <ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>いたしております |

Unlike `なさる` and `いらっしゃる`, `いたす` is a completely regular う-verb:

---

## いたす · Formation (cont.)

| Form | Shape |
|---|---|
| Polite | いたします |
| Past polite | いたしました |
| Negative polite | いたしません |
| Plain negative | いたさない |
| て-form | いたして／いたしまして |

> **Note** - in practice you will almost never meet the plain form in speech. `いたす` lives in the ます-form; a plain いたす turns up mainly in written notices and in fixed compounds.

---

## いたす · Examples

その<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が**いたします**。<br>*I'll take care of that job.*

<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>**いたします**。<br>*Excuse me. / I'll be on my way.*

どう**いたしまして**。<br>*Not at all - you're very welcome.*

<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>は<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>どもが**いたします**ので、ご<ruby>安心<rt>あんしん</rt></ruby>ください。<br>*We'll handle the preparations, so please don't worry.*

---

## いたす · Examples (cont.)

ただいま<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>が<ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>**いたします**。<br>*Someone from the relevant team will check that right now.*

<ruby>承知<rt>しょうち</rt></ruby>**いたしました**。すぐに<ruby>手配<rt>てはい</rt></ruby>いたします。<br>*Understood. I'll arrange it straight away.*

<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>貴重<rt>きちょう</rt></ruby>なお<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>をいただき、<ruby>感謝<rt>かんしゃ</rt></ruby>**いたします**。<br>*Thank you for giving us your valuable time today.*

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>打<rt>う</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>わせにつきましては、あらためてメールでご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>**いたします**。<br>*As for next week's meeting, I'll be in touch again by email.*

---

## いたす · Nuance & register

`いたします` is one of the most-used words in Japanese working life, largely because it is welded into a handful of phrases you will say every day:

- **よろしくお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いいたします** - closes almost every business email.
- **<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>いたします** - entering a room, leaving a room, ending a call.
- **<ruby>承知<rt>しょうち</rt></ruby>いたしました** - "understood", to a customer or a superior.
- **どういたしまして** - a frozen phrase built on this verb, used to wave away<br>*thanks.*

---

## いたす · Nuance & register (cont.)

There is a useful register ordering for acknowledging an instruction, though it is a tendency rather than a rule: かしこまりました is the most deferential and belongs to service staff, <ruby>承知<rt>しょうち</rt></ruby>いたしました is the business standard, わかりました is neutral, and <ruby>了解<rt>りょうかい</rt></ruby>しました is felt by many people to be too casual to send upward. Plenty of workplaces use <ruby>了解<rt>りょうかい</rt></ruby>です internally without anyone minding; to a client it is worth avoiding.

---

## いたす · Nuance & register (cont.)

`いたす` also has a slightly different flavour depending on what it is attached to. In ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>いたします it is aimed at a specific person you are serving. In <ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>いたします or <ruby>開店<rt>かいてん</rt></ruby><ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>変更<rt>へんこう</rt></ruby>いたしました it simply makes the statement formal and self-effacing, with nobody in particular being honoured - the same job that <ruby>参<rt>まい</rt></ruby>る and <ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>す do. Both uses are humble; only the second works in an announcement.

Finally, the limit that defines the word: `いたす` never describes somebody else's action when you are honouring them. ❌<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>いたしました lowers your teacher. The exception is your own in-group in front of an outsider, where lowering is exactly right: <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中村<rt>なかむら</rt></ruby>がご<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>いたします.

---

## いたす · いたす vs なさる

> **Rule of thumb** - `いたす` for what I do, `なさる` for what you or they do. One verb, two directions.

お<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>はどちらに**なさいます**か。<br>*Which table would you like?*

それでは、<ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>ほどで**お<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちいたします**。<br>*In that case, I'll bring it to you in about thirty minutes.*

One restaurant exchange, two verbs. Everything the customer does takes `なさる`; everything the staff do takes `いたす`. A single conversation swings between them constantly, and the swing follows the **subject of each verb**, not the overall tone of the conversation.

---

## いたす · いたす vs する

> **Rule of thumb** - `します` is polite speech. `いたします` is polite speech **plus** humility. They are not two levels of the same dial.

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>**します**。<br>*I'll phone tomorrow morning.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>の<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>、お<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>**いたします**。<br>*I'll telephone you tomorrow morning.*

The first is perfectly polite and entirely normal to a colleague or a friend's parent. The second lowers you in front of the listener and belongs to a customer, a client or a professor. Reaching for `いたします` with a friend does not make you sound respectful; it makes you sound like a call centre.

---

## いたす · お○○いたす vs お○○する

> **Rule of thumb** - both are humble. `いたす` is one notch more formal, because the humble frame and the humble verb are working together.

<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>しします**。<br>*I'll hand you the material.*

<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を**お<ruby>渡<rt>わた</rt></ruby>しいたします**。<br>*I'll hand you the material.*

---

## いたす · お○○いたす vs お○○する (cont.)

Strictly this doubles up, but it is long-established and nobody objects to it - unlike ❌お<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みになられる on the honorific side, which does draw comment. Use `お○○します` in ordinary business speech and `お○○いたします` when you are being formal: a first meeting, a customer, a public announcement.

---

## いたす · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>**いたしました**。 → ✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>**なさいました**。<br>*A humble verb applied to the person you mean to honour. It says your teacher demeaned himself in order to explain.*

- ❌ お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>、どう**いたしますか**。 → ✅ お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>、どう**なさいますか**。<br>*The customer is doing the deciding, so the verb must raise. どういたしましょうか is fine, because there the speaker is offering to act.*

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出席<rt>しゅっせき</rt></ruby>**いたしますか**。 → ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出席<rt>しゅっせき</rt></ruby>**なさいますか**。<br>*Inside the company your department head is above you and takes <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>. Only in front of an outsider does he get lowered.*

---

# ございます

**Meaning** · the very polite form of ある - "there is", "we have"

`ございます` does the same work as `あります`, one register higher. It is <ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>: it lifts the *tone of your speech*, not the subject of the sentence. That is why you can use it about the listener's questions and about your own shop's stock without contradicting yourself - nobody is being raised or lowered, only the language.

---

## ございます · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ある | ございます | <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がある → <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がございます |
| ない | ございません | <ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>がない → <ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>がございません |
| Past | ございました／ございませんでした | <ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>がございました |
| Conditional | ございましたら | ご<ruby>希望<rt>きぼう</rt></ruby>がございましたら |
| い-adjective + ございます | sound change on the く-form | ありがたい → ありがとうございます |
| Noun + です | でございます | <ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>でございます |

The い-adjective pattern is worth seeing once, because three of the commonest greetings in the language come out of it:

---

## ございます · Formation (cont.)

| Adjective | く-form | Sound change | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| ありがたい | ありがたく | ～あく → ～おう | ありがとうございます |
| おめでたい | おめでたく | ～あく → ～おう | おめでとうございます |
| <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>い | <ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く | ～あく → ～おう | お<ruby>早<rt>はよ</rt></ruby>うございます |
| うれしい | うれしく | ～しく → ～しゅう | うれしゅうございます |

> **Note** - the plain form ござる has dropped out of modern speech; you will meet it only in samurai films. Treat ございます as a word without a dictionary form, and note that it shares the irregular ます-form of いらっしゃる and なさる: ❌ござります does not exist.

---

## ございます · Examples

お<ruby>手洗<rt>てあら</rt></ruby>いは<ruby>二階<rt>にかい</rt></ruby>に**ございます**。<br>*The washrooms are on the second floor.*

---

## ございます · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>会員<rt>かいいん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>には<ruby>特別<rt>とくべつ</rt></ruby>な<ruby>割引<rt>わりびき</rt></ruby>が**ございます**。<br>*There is a special discount for members.*

<ruby>恐<rt>おそ</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>りますが、<ruby>喫煙<rt>きつえん</rt></ruby>できるお<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>は**ございません**。<br>*I'm sorry, but we have no seats where smoking is allowed.*

<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>かご<ruby>要望<rt>ようぼう</rt></ruby>が**ございましたら**、フロントまでお<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>付<rt>つ</rt></ruby>けください。<br>*If there is anything you need, please let the front desk know.*

<ruby>車内<rt>しゃない</rt></ruby>にお<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れ<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>の**ございません**よう、ご<ruby>注意<rt>ちゅうい</rt></ruby>ください。<br>*Please take care not to leave anything behind on the train.*

---

## ございます · Nuance & register

This is service language above all. Shops, hotels, restaurants, station announcements, formal speeches and business correspondence run on `ございます`; a conversation between friends does not. Saying ❌<ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>にビールがございます to a flatmate is not politer, it is theatrical.

Because it is <ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby> and not <ruby>尊敬<rt>そんけい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>, `ございます` never honours anybody by itself. That has one practical consequence worth knowing: strictly, asking about something the listener possesses calls for the honorific おありです - ご<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>はおありでしょうか. In real service Japanese, ご<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>はございませんか is what you will hear almost every time, and it is not treated as an error. Use whichever you like; recognise both.

---

## ございます · Nuance & register (cont.)

Three details:

---

## ございます · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **ありがとうございます／ありがとうございました.** The present form thanks for<br>*something still going on or just now done; the past form closes it off. A shop says ありがとうございました as you leave with the bag.*
- **<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>ございません** is the standard business apology. A<br>*minority of usage guides object to it on the grounds that <ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>ない is a single adjective that should not be split, and prefer <ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>なく<ruby>存<rt>ぞん</rt></ruby>じます. In practice the objection has lost; you will hear ございません everywhere, and using it will not mark you.*
- **The い-adjective form is dated.** よろしゅうございます, うれしゅうございます<br>*and <ruby>大<rt>おお</rt></ruby>きゅうございます are grammatical, but they belong to older, very formal or self-consciously refined speech. The frozen greetings survive; the productive pattern has largely gone.*

---

## ございます · ございます vs あります

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different altitude. `あります` is polite; `ございます` is polite and formal, and it announces that you are in a service or ceremonial situation.

<ruby>地下<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>駐車場<rt>ちゅうしゃじょう</rt></ruby>が**あります**。<br>*There's a car park in the basement.*

<ruby>地下<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>駐車場<rt>ちゅうしゃじょう</rt></ruby>が**ございます**。<br>*There is a car park in the basement.*

Nothing about the car park has changed. The second sentence tells you the speaker is behind a counter. Reach for `あります` in ordinary conversation, in class and with colleagues, and switch to `ございます` when you are serving somebody or speaking formally to a group.

---

## ございます · ございます vs いらっしゃいます

> **Rule of thumb** - `ございます` is for things and facts; `いらっしゃいます` is for people you are honouring.

<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>案内図<rt>あんないず</rt></ruby>が**ございます**。<br>*There is a floor plan at reception.*

<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>係<rt>かかり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>が**いらっしゃいます**。<br>*There is a member of staff at reception.*

---

## ございます · ございます vs いらっしゃいます (cont.)

The mistake goes one way: learners reach for `ございます` about a person because it feels like the most polite word available. It is not more polite about a person - it is wrong, because it treats them as an object. Note that your **own** side does take a non-honorific verb, but the word is おります, not ございます: <ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>が<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>におります.

---

## ございます · ございます vs でございます

> **Rule of thumb** - `ございます` replaces `あります`; `でございます` replaces `です`. Existence versus identity.

<ruby>三階<rt>さんがい</rt></ruby>に<ruby>喫茶店<rt>きっさてん</rt></ruby>が**ございます**。<br>*There is a coffee shop on the third floor.*

こちらが<ruby>喫茶店<rt>きっさてん</rt></ruby>**でございます**。<br>*This is the coffee shop.*

---

## ございます · ございます vs でございます (cont.)

The `で` is the giveaway. If the English is "there is a…" or "we have a…", you want the bare form; if it is "X is Y", you want `でございます`. Mixing them produces ❌こちらが<ruby>喫茶店<rt>きっさてん</rt></ruby>ございます, which is simply ungrammatical.

---

## ございます · Common mistakes

- ❌ ご<ruby>用件<rt>ようけん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か**ござりますか**。 → ✅ ご<ruby>用件<rt>ようけん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か**ございますか**。<br>*The `り` becomes `い` before ます - the same irregularity as いらっしゃいます, おっしゃいます, なさいます and くださいます.*

- ❌ お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>二階<rt>にかい</rt></ruby>に**ございます**。 → ✅ お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>二階<rt>にかい</rt></ruby>に**いらっしゃいます**。<br>*A person is not a thing. `ございます` about a customer sounds as though they are stock on a shelf.*

- ❌ ねえ、<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>しゴム**ございます**? → ✅ ねえ、<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>しゴム**ある**?<br>*A register error rather than a grammar one, and the kind that makes people laugh. Service Japanese aimed at a classmate is comic.*

- ❌ **ありがとうでございます**。 → ✅ **ありがとうございます**。<br>*`ございます` follows the adjective form directly. The `で` belongs only after a noun.*

# でございます

**Meaning** · the formal copula - "is", at the register of a hotel desk or a station announcement

`でございます` replaces `です`. Like `ございます`, it is <ruby>丁寧<rt>ていねい</rt></ruby><ruby>語<rt>ご</rt></ruby>: it raises the formality of the sentence without honouring anybody in it. That is why you can say <ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>でございます about yourself, and why you must **not** say it about the person you are talking to.

---

## でございます · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + でございます | <ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>です → <ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>でございます |
| な-adjective | な-adj + でございます | <ruby>結構<rt>けっこう</rt></ruby>です → <ruby>結構<rt>けっこう</rt></ruby>でございます |
| Negative | ではございません | ちがいます → ではございません |
| Past | でございました | <ruby>以上<rt>いじょう</rt></ruby>でございました |
| Tentative | でございましょう | そのとおりでございましょう |
| い-adjective | frame **not** used | よろしいです → よろしゅうございます |
| About a person you honour | でいらっしゃいます | <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>でいらっしゃいます |

---

## でございます · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `でございます` for things, places, prices, facts, and for **yourself**. `でいらっしゃいます` for the listener and for anyone else you are raising.

---

## でございます · Examples

こちらが<ruby>会議室<rt>かいぎしつ</rt></ruby>**でございます**。<br>*This is the meeting room.*

はい、<ruby>山田<rt>やまだ</rt></ruby>**でございます**。<br>*Yes, Yamada speaking.*

お<ruby>会計<rt>かいけい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三千<rt>さんぜん</rt></ruby><ruby>円<rt>えん</rt></ruby>**でございます**。<br>*That comes to three thousand yen.*

<ruby>次<rt>つぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>終点<rt>しゅうてん</rt></ruby>、<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>**でございます**。<br>*The next stop is the last one, Tokyo.*

---

## でございます · Examples (cont.)

こちらのお<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>はすべて<ruby>禁煙<rt>きんえん</rt></ruby>**でございます**。<br>*All of the seats in this section are non-smoking.*

<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>ございませんが、その<ruby>商品<rt>しょうひん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>当店<rt>とうてん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>り<ruby>扱<rt>あつか</rt></ruby>い**ではございません**。<br>*I'm sorry, but that isn't a product we carry.*

<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>者<rt>もの</rt></ruby>は<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby><ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>み**でございます**ので、<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>あらためてご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>いたします。<br>*The person handling this is off today, so we'll be in touch again tomorrow.*

<ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby>はお<ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいところお<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>しいただき、ありがとうございます。<ruby>司会<rt>しかい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>務<rt>つと</rt></ruby>めます<ruby>佐藤<rt>さとう</rt></ruby>**でございます**。<br>*Thank you for coming today despite your busy schedules. I'm Sato, and I'll be chairing.*

---

## でございます · Nuance & register

Announcements, counters, telephones and formal speeches. In a shop or a hotel it is the default copula and nobody notices it; in a conversation with a friend it would be as odd as answering the door in a uniform. Between those extremes, a business meeting will use it for set pieces - introductions, figures, conclusions - and drop back to `です` for ordinary discussion.

The single most useful thing to keep straight is that `でございます` does not honour. Saying ❌<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>でございますか to a visitor is a real error, not merely a stiff one: it equates the guest with a price or a room number. The honorific copula is `でいらっしゃいます`, and it is used the same way for descriptions: お<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>でいらっしゃいますか, <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>奥様<rt>おくさま</rt></ruby>でいらっしゃいます.

Some notes on the edges:

---

## でございます · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **Negatives.** ではございません is the formal negative. In quick speech people<br>*reduce it to じゃございません, which is a mixed register and is best avoided in writing.*
- **い-adjectives.** There is no ❌よろしいでございますか. The forms that exist are<br>*よろしゅうございますか, which sounds old-fashioned, and よろしいでしょうか, which is what you should actually say.*
- **Placement.** `でございます` closes the sentence. Inside a sentence you use the<br>*て-form でございまして, which is heavy and almost entirely a written or speech-making form.*
- **Do not stack it on <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> you have already used.**<br>*ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>になるお<ruby>品<rt>しな</rt></ruby>でございます is fine, but ❌ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>になられるお<ruby>品<rt>しな</rt></ruby>でございます piles two honorific layers under the formal copula.*

---

## でございます · でございます vs です

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different height. `です` is polite; `でございます` is polite and formal, and it signals a service or ceremonial setting.

これは<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>**です**。<br>*This is last year's material.*

こちらが<ruby>昨年度<rt>さくねんど</rt></ruby>の<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>**でございます**。<br>*This is last year's material.*

---

## でございます · でございます vs です (cont.)

Notice how much moves with the copula. `これ` becomes `こちら`, <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby> becomes <ruby>昨年度<rt>さくねんど</rt></ruby>. Register is carried by the whole sentence, and a lone `でございます` on top of casual vocabulary sounds like a joke.

---

## でございます · でございます vs でいらっしゃいます

> **Rule of thumb** - `でございます` about yourself and about things; `でいらっしゃいます` about the person you are honouring. Getting this backwards is the classic <ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby> error.

<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>ですが、ご<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>**でいらっしゃいます**か。<br>*Excuse me - do you have a reservation with us?*

<ruby>受付<rt>うけつけ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>木村<rt>きむら</rt></ruby>**でございます**。<br>*I'm Kimura from reception.*

---

## でございます · でございます vs でいらっしゃいます (cont.)

The pair shows the whole three-way system in two lines. The first raises the guest; the second simply formalises a statement about the speaker. Swap them and you have honoured yourself while treating the guest as an object - which is why ❌<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>でいらっしゃいます is such a jarring sentence.

---

## でございます · でございます vs ～になります

> **Rule of thumb** - `になる` means "becomes". For "is", the formal copula is `でございます`.

こちらがお<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>み<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>のメニュー**でございます**。<br>*Here is the drinks menu.*

<ruby>合計<rt>ごうけい</rt></ruby>で<ruby>二千<rt>にせん</rt></ruby><ruby>円<rt>えん</rt></ruby>**でございます**。<br>*That's two thousand yen altogether.*

---

## でございます · でございます vs ～になります (cont.)

You will hear こちらメニューになります and <ruby>二千<rt>にせん</rt></ruby><ruby>円<rt>えん</rt></ruby>になります constantly in Japanese shops. It is common enough to have its own nickname - マニュアル<ruby>敬語<rt>けいご</rt></ruby>, "manual keigo" - and a large number of speakers consider it wrong, on the grounds that nothing is turning into anything. `になります` is correct when there really is a change （<ruby>四月<rt>しがつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>料金<rt>りょうきん</rt></ruby>になります）. For a plain identification, `でございます` is the safe choice.

---

## でございます · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>**でございます**か。 → ✅ <ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby><ruby>様<rt>さま</rt></ruby>**でいらっしゃいます**か。<br>*`でございます` does not raise anybody. Asked of a guest, it puts them in the same grammatical box as the room key.*

- ❌ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>鈴木<rt>すずき</rt></ruby>**でいらっしゃいます**。 → ✅ <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>鈴木<rt>すずき</rt></ruby>**でございます**。<br>*The mirror error, and the one learners make when they decide `でいらっしゃいます` is simply "the politest version".*

- ❌ お<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>はこちらで**よろしいでございますか**。 → ✅ お<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>はこちらで**よろしいでしょうか**。<br>*An い-adjective cannot take `でございます`. Either use でしょうか, or the old-fashioned よろしゅうございますか.*

---

## でございます · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ こちら、お<ruby>釣<rt>つ</rt></ruby>り**になります**。 → ✅ こちら、お<ruby>釣<rt>つ</rt></ruby>り**でございます**。<br>*The change is nothing to do with becoming. Very widely heard, and very widely disliked.*
