# ～とき

**Meaning** · when; at the time that

`とき` is the ordinary noun for "time", pressed into service as a joint between two clauses: *at the time when X, Y*. What lifts it above vocabulary is the tense inside the clause. Japanese sets it by looking at the **main verb**, not at the present moment - so <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くとき</code> and <code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったとき</code> can both sit in a sentence about last year and mean quite different things.

---

## ～とき · Formation

`とき` is a noun, so anything that can modify a noun can modify it - and it takes that modifier in the **plain** form.

---

## ～とき · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (dictionary) | dictionary form + とき | <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かける → <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけるとき |
| Verb (た-form) | た-form + とき | <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけた → <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけたとき |
| Verb (ない-form) | ない-form + とき | わからない → わからないとき |
| Verb (ている) | ている + とき | <ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>している → <ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>しているとき |
| い-adjective | plain い + とき | <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいとき |
| な-adjective | な + とき | <ruby>暇<rt>ひま</rt></ruby>だ → <ruby>暇<rt>ひま</rt></ruby>なとき |
| Noun | Noun + **の** + とき | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>のとき |
| Noun (past state) | Noun + だった + とき | <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもだったとき |
| Particles after it | とき + は／に／も | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いときは、<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いときに |

---

## ～とき · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| In kanji | とき = <ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby> | <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの<ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby> |
| Politeness | on the **final** verb only | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るとき<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い**ます** |

> **Rule of thumb** - the verb in front of `とき` is plain. ❌<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ますとき is not how ordinary Japanese sounds; politeness lives on the last verb of the sentence and nowhere else.

---

## ～とき · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the `に` is optional. Adding it pins the clause to a definite point (<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ったときに<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました); leaving it off lets the clause read as a general circumstance (<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>いとき、<ruby>温<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かいものが<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたくなる). With `は` on the end - `ときは` - the clause becomes the topic, which is how you state a rule or a piece of advice.

---

## ～とき · Examples

<ruby>暇<rt>ひま</rt></ruby>な**とき**、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>をしますか。<br>*What do you do when you have free time?*

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの**とき**、ピアノを<ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>っていました。<br>*I used to take piano lessons when I was little.*

<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>い**とき**は、<ruby>冷<rt>つめ</rt></ruby>たい<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>で<ruby>顔<rt>かお</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>います。<br>*When I'm sleepy I wash my face with cold water.*

<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>がわからない**とき**は、<ruby>駅員<rt>えきいん</rt></ruby>さんに<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてください。<br>*When you can't work out the way, ask a member of station staff.*

---

## ～とき · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>る**とき**、かぎをかけるのを<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れてしまいました。<br>*I forgot to lock up as I was leaving the house.*

<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いた**とき**、もう<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>の<ruby>十時<rt>じゅうじ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>過<rt>す</rt></ruby>ぎていました。<br>*By the time I got to Osaka it was already gone ten at night.*

<ruby>運転<rt>うんてん</rt></ruby>している**とき**は、<ruby>携帯<rt>けいたい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ないでください。<ruby>危<rt>あぶ</rt></ruby>ないですから。<br>*Please don't look at your phone while you're driving - it's dangerous.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>長<rt>なが</rt></ruby>くなりそうな**とき**は、<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べておいたほうがいいですよ。<br>*When it looks like the meeting is going to run long, you're better off eating lunch first.*

---

## ～とき · Nuance & register

`とき` is register-neutral and appears in every kind of Japanese, from a text message to a contract. The politeness of the sentence is decided entirely by the final verb, so <code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どものとき、よく<ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>いた</code> and <code><ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どものとき、よく<ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>きました</code> differ only at the end.

One thing that *is* worth copying from careful writing: when `とき` is doing this grammatical job, Japanese usually writes it **in kana**. The kanji <code><ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby></code> is kept for cases where it means time as a real thing - <code><ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>経<rt>た</rt></ruby>つ</code>, <code><ruby>時<rt>とき</rt></ruby>には</code>. Both spellings are read the same and neither is wrong; kana simply looks more natural on the page.

---

## ～とき · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two step-ups you will meet as the register rises. <code><ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に</code> is the formal twin of `とき` and is everywhere in announcements and manuals: <code>ご<ruby>利用<rt>りよう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>際<rt>さい</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>係員<rt>かかりいん</rt></ruby>にお<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ください</code>. And <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code> ("in the case that") takes over when the sentence is about a **contingency** rather than a time: <code><ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は、エレベーターを<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>わないでください</code>. Using `とき` there is not wrong, but `場合` is what the sign says.

Going the other way, casual speech shortens nothing about `とき` itself - it just drops the `に` and often the comma, and adds `さ` or `ね`: <code><ruby>高校<rt>こうこう</rt></ruby>のときさ、よくここで<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>んだよね</code>.

---

## ～とき · 行くとき vs 行ったとき

> **Rule of thumb** - the tense before `とき` is measured against the **main clause**, not against now. Dictionary form: at the moment of the main action, the とき-event has **not finished**. た-form: it **has**.

This is the single most tested thing about `とき`, and English gives you no help, because English says "when" for both.

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**とき**、スーツケースを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*I bought a suitcase for my trip to Japan. (bought before I got there)*

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った**とき**、スーツケースを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*I bought a suitcase when I was in Japan. (bought after I arrived)*

---

## ～とき · 行くとき vs 行ったとき (cont.)

Both sentences are about a finished trip, and both end in `ました`. Only the form in front of `とき` tells you which side of the arrival the shopping happened on.

The same logic makes some pairs obviously right and obviously wrong:

お<ruby>風呂<rt>ふろ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>る**とき**、<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>脱<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ぎます。<br>*You take your clothes off when you get into the bath.*

You are not yet in the bath when you undress, so it has to be <code><ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>るとき</code>. ❌<code><ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ったとき<ruby>服<rt>ふく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>脱<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>ぐ</code> describes someone undressing in the water.

---

## ～とき · ～とき vs ～たら

> **Rule of thumb** - `とき` names a **time**; `たら` sets a **condition**. If you could replace the English with "at the time when", use `とき`. If it is really "once" or "if", use `たら`.

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>い**たら**、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Give me a ring once you get to the station.*

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いた**とき**、あの<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>はもう<ruby>閉<rt>し</rt></ruby>まっていました。<br>*When I got to the station, that shop had already closed.*

`たら` looks forward and triggers something; `とき` looks at a point on the timeline and reports what was true there. That is why an instruction about the future almost always takes `たら`, and why a narrated memory almost always takes `とき`.

---

## ～とき · ～とき vs ～たら (cont.)

`とき` also reaches places `たら` cannot go without extra machinery: it attaches straight to a noun with `の` - <code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>のとき</code> - where `たら` needs <code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>だったら</code>, and that already sounds hypothetical ("if you were a student").

---

## ～とき · ～ているとき vs ～ながら

> **Rule of thumb** - `ながら` needs **one person doing two things at once**, and the main action is the one at the end. `ているとき` has no such restriction: the two clauses can have different subjects entirely.

テレビを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>**ながら**ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます。<br>*I eat while watching TV.*

<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>がごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**ているとき**に、<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>が<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってきました。<br>*My older sister got home while I was eating.*

---

## ～とき · ～ているとき vs ～ながら (cont.)

Swap them and the second sentence breaks: ❌<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べながら<ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>が<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってきた</code> would claim your sister was eating and coming home simultaneously. When the two events belong to different people, `ているとき` is the only one of the two that works.

---

## ～とき · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**とき**、よく<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しました。 → ✅ <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ども**の**とき、よく<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*`とき` is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs `の`, exactly as in <code><ruby>日本語<rt>にほんご</rt></ruby>の<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby></code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**とき** → ✅ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**な**とき<br>*な-adjectives keep their `な` before any noun, and `とき` is a noun.*

- ❌ <ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>り**ますとき**、<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>します。 → ✅ <ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る**とき**、<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>します。<br>*The clause before `とき` is plain. Politeness goes on the final verb only.*

---

## ～とき · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ ガイドブックは<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>っ**たとき**、<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>本屋<rt>ほんや</rt></ruby>で<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。 → ✅ <ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**とき**、<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>本屋<rt>ほんや</rt></ruby>で<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>いました。<br>*You bought it before you set off, so the trip had not happened yet: dictionary form. The た-form would put the Tokyo bookshop after your arrival in Kyoto.*

---

## ～とき · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>大学生<rt>だいがくせい</rt></ruby>____とき、<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>しました。

{reveal}
<ruby>大学生<rt>だいがくせい</rt></ruby>**の**とき、<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>しました。

{reveal}
`とき` is a noun, and a noun modifying another noun needs `の` between them. Only verbs and adjectives attach to `とき` directly.

---

## ～とき · Practice 2

**2.** ドアを（<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ける・<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けた）とき、このかぎを<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>ってください。

{reveal}
ドアを**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ける**とき、このかぎを<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>ってください。

{reveal}
The key is what makes the opening possible, so at the moment you use it the door is still shut - the とき-event has not finished. That is the dictionary form. <code><ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けたとき</code> would have you fiddling with the key after the door was already open.

---

## ～とき · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "When I was a high school student, I worked part-time at a convenience store."

{reveal}
<ruby>高校生<rt>こうこうせい</rt></ruby>の**とき**、コンビニでアルバイトをしていました。

{reveal}
A noun takes `の` before `とき`. The main verb carries both the tense and the politeness, and `ていました` is the natural choice because the job lasted a stretch of time rather than happening once.

---

## ～とき · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいですとき、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>しないでください。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい**とき**、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>しないでください。

{reveal}
An い-adjective goes in front of `とき` in its plain form. `です` is a sentence ending and cannot sit in the middle of a clause that modifies a noun.

---

## ～とき · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>るとき、<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>しました。 (b) <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たとき、となりの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いました。

{reveal}
(a) uses the dictionary form because the leaving was not finished yet: you were still inside, on your way out, when you hit the light switch. (b) uses the た-form because the leaving *was* finished: you were already through the door when you ran into the neighbour.

{reveal}
Swapping them produces nonsense in both directions - turning off the lights from the street, and bumping into the neighbour in your own hallway.

# ～後で

**Meaning** · after something; afterwards, later on

N5 taught this word in kana, as `～たあとで`. Here it is in kanji, plus the two jobs the kana page left alone: `後で` standing on its own as the adverb *later*, and the four different readings the character takes once it appears in writing.

---

## ～後で · Formation

The clause in front of `後で` is in the **た-form**, whatever the tense of the sentence as a whole. A noun joins with `の`.

---

## ～後で · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | た-form + <ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で |
| Noun | Noun + **の** + <ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で | <ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>**の**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で |
| On its own (adverb) | <ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で + clause | <ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>します |
| Demonstrative | その<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で／この<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby> | その<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めましょう |
| Without で | た-form + <ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby> | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>た<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>、<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えます |
| With に instead | た-form + <ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>に | <ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>の<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>発表<rt>はっぴょう</rt></ruby>します |
| Amount of time | Quantity + <ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>（に） | <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>に |
| Sino-Japanese compound | Noun + <ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby> | <ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>、<ruby>使用<rt>しよう</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby> |
| Formal register | <ruby>後<rt>のち</rt></ruby>ほど | <ruby>後<rt>のち</rt></ruby>ほどご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>いたします |

---

## ～後で · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Politeness | on the **final** verb only | <ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いた<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>し**ます** |
| ❌ | ❌ dictionary form + <ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で | ❌<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で |

> **Rule of thumb** - one character, four readings, and they are not interchangeable. <code><ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby></code> is *after* in time, <code><ruby>後<rt>うし</rt></ruby>ろ</code> is *behind* in space, <code><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby></code> is the suffix on Sino-Japanese words and on amounts of time, and <code><ruby>後<rt>のち</rt></ruby></code> is the formal, written one. Reading <code><ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby></code> as あと marks you out instantly.

---

## ～後で · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - `で` is optional and drops freely, and the whole phrase can take `は` or `も`: <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>くなる</code>. What you cannot do is put a verb in the dictionary form in front of it - that slot belongs to <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code>.

---

## ～後で · Examples

**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**メールしますね。<br>*I'll email you later.*

<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>は<ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでください。<br>*Please take the medicine after meals.*

<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**、ジムで<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぐつもりです。<br>*I'm planning to swim at the gym after work.*

<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらせた**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**なら、ゲームをしてもいいよ。<br>*You can play games once you've finished your homework.*

---

## ～後で · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**、チームのみんなでラーメンを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った。<br>*After the match the whole team went out for ramen.*

<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>した**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>住所<rt>じゅうしょ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わったことを<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>伝<rt>つた</rt></ruby>えました。<br>*After I moved, I let the company know my address had changed.*

<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いた**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>がある<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>を<ruby>挙<rt>あ</rt></ruby>げてください。<br>*If you have a question after hearing the explanation, please raise your hand.*

<ruby>三十分<rt>さんじゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**<ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>出発<rt>しゅっぱつ</rt></ruby>しますので、それまでに<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>をお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いします。<br>*We leave in thirty minutes, so please be ready by then.*

---

## ～後で · Nuance & register

As a clause connector `後で` is plain and neutral. As a **standalone adverb** it carries something extra, and that is worth knowing: `後で` is deliberately vague. It means "at some point after now, probably today", and it names no time at all.

That vagueness is friendly among equals - <code>じゃあ、また<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>でね</code> is how you end a conversation you expect to resume - and unhelpful upward. Told <code><ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>でやります</code>, a manager has learned nothing; <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>します</code> is what they wanted. If you need the vagueness *and* the politeness, the business word is <code><ruby>後<rt>のち</rt></ruby>ほど</code>: <code><ruby>後<rt>のち</rt></ruby>ほど<ruby>担当<rt>たんとう</rt></ruby>よりご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>いたします</code>. It is one register step above `後で` and never sounds casual.

---

## ～後で · Nuance & register (cont.)

Spelling is a genuine choice here. In the grammatical use, careful writing often prefers kana - <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたあとで</code> - because the kanji pulls the reader towards the "behind" meaning. Inside a compound the kanji is compulsory and the reading switches: <code><ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>開店<rt>かいてん</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>二年<rt>にねん</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby></code>.

One last spoken habit: <code><ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>にする</code> means "leave it for later" - <code>その<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>にしよう</code>. It is not the same construction as `後で`, but it lives in the same corner of the language.

---

## ～後で · ～た後で vs ～てから

> **Rule of thumb** - `てから` says the second thing does not happen **until** the first is done. `後で` only says it happens later. When the order is a requirement, use `てから`; when it is just the order things came in, `後で` is enough.

お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>を<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れ**てから**、ボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Put the money in first, then press the button.*

Here `てから` is doing real work: pressing the button before paying achieves nothing. Swap in <code><ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れた<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で</code> and the instruction goes soft - it now merely reports a sequence.

<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**、<ruby>喫茶店<rt>きっさてん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>した。<br>*After the film we chatted for a bit in a coffee shop.*

---

## ～後で · ～た後で vs ～てから (cont.)

Nothing about the film enabled the chat. This is plain sequence, so `後で` fits and `てから` would sound oddly purposeful.

`てから` has one job `後で` cannot take on at all: measuring elapsed time from a starting point. <code><ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てから<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>になります</code> - "it's been three years since I came to Japan." ❌<code><ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby>になります</code> is not Japanese. Going the other way, `後で` attaches straight to a noun with `の`, which `てから` cannot do: <code><ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で</code> has no `てから` equivalent short of adding a verb.

---

## ～後で · 後で vs 後に vs ～後に

> **Rule of thumb** - `で` is the everyday "afterwards". `に` locates a **point**: use it when you are placing something in an order, and you must use the <code><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby></code> reading with a measured quantity of time.

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんの**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>発表<rt>はっぴょう</rt></ruby>します。<br>*I'm presenting after Tanaka.*

<ruby>五分<rt>ごふん</rt></ruby>**<ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>**にまたかけ<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>します。<br>*I'll call back in five minutes.*

---

## ～後で · 後で vs 後に vs ～後に (cont.)

A quantity of time never takes `後で`: ❌<code><ruby>五分<rt>ごふん</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で</code> is simply wrong, both in the particle and in the reading. In the ordinary "X happened, then Y" use, `後で` and `後に` overlap heavily and `後で` is the safer spoken default; `後に` leans written, and it is what you want when the sentence is about a slot in a sequence rather than about a time.

There is also <code><ruby>後<rt>のち</rt></ruby>に</code>, read のち, which means "later in life" or "subsequently" and belongs to biography and history: <code><ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>後<rt>のち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>市長<rt>しちょう</rt></ruby>になった</code>. `後で` cannot carry that sense.

---

## ～後で · 後で vs 今度・そのうち

> **Rule of thumb** - `後で` is later **today**. <code><ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby></code> is **next time**, on some other occasion. `そのうち` is **eventually**, with no occasion in mind at all.

English collapses all three into "later", and picking the wrong one changes what you have promised.

その<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ておきます。<br>*I'll look over that document later on. (today, before I go home)*

**<ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>**いっしょに<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう。<br>*Let's go for a drink sometime.*

---

## ～後で · 後で vs 今度・そのうち (cont.)

That second sentence is an invitation, not an appointment, and everyone hears it that way. If you say <code><ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby><ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>します</code> to someone waiting for a call in the next hour, they will assume you mean next week. `そのうち` is vaguer still - <code>そのうち<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けます</code> is close to admitting it may never happen.

---

## ～後で · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>歯<rt>は</rt></ruby>をみがきます。 → ✅ <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**た**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で<ruby>歯<rt>は</rt></ruby>をみがきます。<br>*`後で` takes the た-form. The dictionary form in that slot belongs to <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>しました。 → ✅ <ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>**の**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*`後` is a noun, so a noun in front of it needs `の`. (The compound <code><ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby></code> exists and needs no `の` - but then it is read ご and belongs to written style.)*

- ❌ <ruby>二時間<rt>にじかん</rt></ruby>**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>戻<rt>もど</rt></ruby>ります。 → ✅ <ruby>二時間<rt>にじかん</rt></ruby>**<ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>戻<rt>もど</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*With a measured amount of time the reading is ご and the particle is `に`.*

---

## ～後で · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ すぐ<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わるので、**<ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby>**<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>します。 → ✅ すぐ<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わるので、**<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>します。<br>*<code><ruby>今度<rt>こんど</rt></ruby></code> pushes the call to another occasion. For "later today", it has to be `後で`.*

{reveal}
The verb in front of `後で` is always in the た-form, even though the sentence describes a habit and ends in the present `ます`. The た marks the shower as complete relative to the beer, not relative to now.

You would hear (b) from a call centre or a receptionist, and it would sound stiff between friends.

# ～までに

**Meaning** · by - at any point up to that limit, but not after it

N5 gave you `まで`, "until", which measures a stretch of time. Adding `に` turns the stretch into an **edge**. `までに` names the last moment at which something may happen, and says nothing about when inside the window it actually does. It is the particle of deadlines.

---

## ～までに · Formation

`までに` follows a time expression, or a clause in the **dictionary form** that names the moment the window closes.

---

## ～までに · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Time noun | Noun + までに | <ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>までに |
| Day or date | Noun + までに | <ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby> → <ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>までに |
| Event noun | Noun + までに | <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>までに |
| Verb | **dictionary form** + までに | <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かける → <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけるまでに |
| Demonstrative | それ／いつ + までに | それまでに、いつまでに |
| Emphasis | までに + は | <ruby>八時<rt>はちじ</rt></ruby>までには<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きます |
| Very often with | までに + ～ておく | <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>っておく、<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けておく |
| ❌ | ❌ た-form + までに | ❌<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたまでに |
| ❌ | ❌ ない-form + までに | ❌<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>ないまでに |

---

## ～までに · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the main verb of a `までに` sentence has to be something that **finishes in one go**: <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す, <ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>く, <ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>める, <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらせる, <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る. If the verb describes something that goes *on* - <ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つ, <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>る, <ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>く - you want plain `まで`, not `までに`.

> **Note** - `までには` adds "at the latest, and I mean it": <code><ruby>七時<rt>しちじ</rt></ruby>までには<ruby>戻<rt>もど</rt></ruby>ります</code> promises seven as the outer bound, whereas <code><ruby>七時<rt>しちじ</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>戻<rt>もど</rt></ruby>ります</code> merely states the deadline. The `は` is what turns a fact into a commitment.

---

## ～までに · Examples

<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>**までに**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ってきてね。<br>*Be back by five, all right?*

レポートは<ruby>金曜日<rt>きんようび</rt></ruby>**までに**<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Please hand the report in by Friday.*

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る**までに**、きっぷを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>っておきます。<br>*I'll buy the ticket before the train gets here.*

<ruby>夏<rt>なつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる**までに**、<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>は<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きたいなあ。<br>*I'd like to get to the sea at least once before the summer's over.*

---

## ～までに · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>月曜日<rt>げつようび</rt></ruby>**までに**お<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>をいただけますか。<br>*Could I have your reply by Monday next week?*

お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る**までに**、<ruby>会議室<rt>かいぎしつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けておきましょう。<br>*Let's get the meeting room tidied up before the client arrives.*

<ruby>三十歳<rt>さんじゅっさい</rt></ruby>になる**までに**、<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>持<rt>も</rt></ruby>ちたいと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*I'm hoping to have my own shop by the time I turn thirty.*

<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>みは<ruby>今月<rt>こんげつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりまで<ruby>受<rt>う</rt></ruby>け<ruby>付<rt>つ</rt></ruby>けていますが、できれば<ruby>今週<rt>こんしゅう</rt></ruby>**までに**<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>めてほしいそうです。<br>*Applications are open to the end of the month, but apparently they'd like a decision by this week if possible.*

---

## ～までに · Nuance & register

`までに` is register-neutral and lives in the parts of life that have deadlines: homework, forms, trains, work. Nothing about it is polite or blunt in itself - the verb at the end does that. <code><ruby>金曜<rt>きんよう</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>せ</code> is an order, <code><ruby>金曜<rt>きんよう</rt></ruby>までにご<ruby>提出<rt>ていしゅつ</rt></ruby>ください</code> is a notice, and the `までに` is identical in both.

What it *does* carry is pressure, because it draws a line. Softening usually happens around it rather than to it. `できれば` ("if possible"), <code><ruby>念<rt>ねん</rt></ruby>のため</code> ("just in case") and turning the request into a question - <code><ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>までにいただけますか</code> - all take the edge off without moving the deadline. Dropping `に` does not soften anything; it changes the meaning.

---

## ～までに · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two things you will hear rather than read. First, in speech the whole phrase is often reduced to the bare time: <code>じゃ、<ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>ね</code>. Second, the normal way to *ask* about a deadline keeps the `に` in place and adds a verb - <code>いつまでに<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>せばいいですか</code>, <code>いつまでにお<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>すればよろしいでしょうか</code>. Asking `いつまでですか` without the `に` is a different question: it asks how long something lasts, not when it is due.

---

## ～までに · までに vs まで

> **Rule of thumb** - `まで` fills the whole span; `までに` picks one moment inside it. Ask whether the verb can go on for hours. If it can, `まで`. If it happens and is over, `までに`.

This is the whole of the point, and the two are not interchangeable in either direction.

<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>**まで**ここで<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*I'll be waiting here until three.*

Waiting occupies every minute up to three o'clock, so `まで`. There is no single instant at which the waiting happens.

<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>**までに**ここへ<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てください。<br>*Please get here by three.*

---

## ～までに · までに vs まで (cont.)

Arriving takes an instant, and any instant before three will do. Say ❌<code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てください</code> and you have asked someone to keep arriving continuously until three.

The mirror error is just as common. ❌<code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>までに<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っていてください</code> asks the listener to complete the act of waiting before three - which is not a thing waiting can do.

---

## ～までに · までに vs ～前に

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> reports **order**; `までに` imposes a **limit**. Both often fit the same sentence, but only `までに` implies that being late is a failure.

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みました。<br>*I read the material before the meeting.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>**までに**<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでおいてください。<br>*Please have read the material before the meeting.*

The first simply tells you which came first. The second sets a deadline and implies a consequence for missing it, which is why notices, homework instructions and workplace requests reach for `までに`.

---

## ～までに · までに vs ～前に (cont.)

Two mechanical differences. <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> attaches to a noun with `の` - <code><ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> - while `までに` attaches to the bare noun: <code><ruby>食事<rt>しょくじ</rt></ruby>までに</code>. And <code><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> can follow a duration to mean "ago" - <code><ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に</code> - which `までに` can never do.

---

## ～までに · までに vs ～中に

> **Rule of thumb** - `までに` draws a line and lets you act any time **before** it. <code>～<ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> names a period and asks you to act **inside** it.

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>**<ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>をします。<br>*I'll get back to you before the day is out.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>**までに**<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>をします。<br>*I'll get back to you by tomorrow.*

---

## ～までに · までに vs ～中に (cont.)

<code><ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> is said *on* the day in question and treats today as a container. <code><ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>までに</code> can be said days in advance and treats tomorrow as a wall. When the two overlap - <code><ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>までに</code> versus <code><ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> - the first is what a form says to you in advance, the second is what a colleague says to you at nine in the morning.

> **Note** - watch the reading of `中`. After a span you can live through it is じゅう: <code><ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に</code>, <code><ruby>今週<rt>こんしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に</code>, <code><ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby></code>. After a word naming an activity or a portion of the day it is ちゅう: <code><ruby>午前<rt>ごぜん</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby>に</code>, <code><ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>ちゅう</rt></ruby></code>. Same character, and no rule will save you at the moment you have to say it aloud.

---

## ～までに · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>**まで**<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください。 → ✅ <ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>**までに**<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Handing something in is momentary, so it needs the deadline particle. Without `に` you have asked for continuous handing-in until five.*

- ❌ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>がやむ**までに**、ここで<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んでいましょう。 → ✅ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>がやむ**まで**、ここで<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んでいましょう。<br>*Resting goes on throughout, so the span particle is the right one.*

- ❌ <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>い**たまでに** → ✅ <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>く**までに**<br>*A clause before `までに` is in the dictionary form. It names the moment the window shuts, and that moment has not arrived yet.*

---

## ～までに · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>み**までに**<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>します。 → ✅ <ruby>夏休<rt>なつやす</rt></ruby>み**まで**<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>します。<br>*<code><ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby></code> spreads the action over the whole period, which is exactly what `までに` forbids.*

---

## ～までに · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>____<ruby>空港<rt>くうこう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>かなければなりません。

{reveal}
<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>**までに**<ruby>空港<rt>くうこう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>かなければなりません。

{reveal}
Arriving is a one-off event, and nine o'clock is the limit it must fall inside. <code><ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>く</code> would ask you to go on arriving all morning.

---

## ～までに · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>がやむ（まで・までに）、この<ruby>喫茶店<rt>きっさてん</rt></ruby>にいましょう。

{reveal}
<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>がやむ**まで**、この<ruby>喫茶店<rt>きっさてん</rt></ruby>にいましょう。

{reveal}
Staying somewhere occupies the whole stretch of time, so it takes the span particle. `までに` would need a verb that finishes in a moment - for instance <code><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>がやむまでに<ruby>注文<rt>ちゅうもん</rt></ruby>しましょう</code>.

## ～までに · Practice 3

{reveal}

{reveal}

{reveal}

# ～ごろ

**Meaning** · around, about - attached to a **point** in time and nothing else

`ごろ` blurs a moment on the clock or the calendar: <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>ごろ</code>, <code><ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>ごろ</code>. It is not a general-purpose "approximately". The moment you are approximating an *amount* - three hours, five people, a thousand yen - `ごろ` is the wrong word and `ぐらい` is the right one.

---

## ～ごろ · Formation

`ごろ` is a suffix. It goes straight onto the time word, with nothing in between.

---

## ～ごろ · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Clock time | Time + ごろ | <ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>ごろ |
| Date, month, year | Noun + ごろ | <ruby>五月<rt>ごがつ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>五月<rt>ごがつ</rt></ruby>ごろ |
| Part of the day | Noun + ごろ | <ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby> → <ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby>ごろ |
| Question word | <ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>／いつ + ごろ | <ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>ごろ、いつごろ |
| With に | ごろ（に） - the に is optional | <ruby>七時<rt>しちじ</rt></ruby>ごろ（に）<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きます |
| With other particles | ごろから、ごろまで、ごろには | <ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>ごろから |
| As an independent noun | ころ | <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どものころ、あのころ |
| In kanji | <ruby>頃<rt>ころ</rt></ruby>／<ruby>頃<rt>ごろ</rt></ruby> | <ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>頃<rt>ころ</rt></ruby> |
| ❌ Length of time | ❌<ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby>ごろ | ✅<ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby>ぐらい |

---

## ～ごろ · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ Number or amount | ❌<ruby>五人<rt>ごにん</rt></ruby>ごろ | ✅<ruby>五人<rt>ごにん</rt></ruby>ぐらい |

> **Rule of thumb** - ask which question the phrase answers. If it answers `いつ` ("when?"), use `ごろ`. If it answers `どのくらい` ("how much? how long?"), use `ぐらい`. <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby></code> is a when; <code><ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby></code> is a how-long, and one character is the whole difference.

---

## ～ごろ · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the `に` sits *after* `ごろ`, never before it: <code><ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>ごろに</code>, ❌<code><ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>にごろ</code>. And once `ごろ` is there, `に` is optional - it is dropped more often than not, because an approximate time is already loose.

---

## ～ごろ · Examples

<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>七時<rt>しちじ</rt></ruby>**ごろ**<ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*I get up around seven every morning.*

<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>**ごろ**から<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>るそうですよ。<br>*Apparently it's going to rain from around midday.*

<ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>**ごろ**そちらに<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>きますか。<br>*Roughly what time will you get there?*

<ruby>桜<rt>さくら</rt></ruby>は<ruby>四月<rt>しがつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>め**ごろ**に<ruby>咲<rt>さ</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*The cherry blossom comes out around the beginning of April.*

---

## ～ごろ · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの**ころ**、この<ruby>川<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>でよく<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>いだものだ。<br>*When I was small I used to swim in this river all the time.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>**ごろ**は、まだ<ruby>大阪<rt>おおさか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいました。<br>*This time last year I was still living in Osaka.*

<ruby>夕方<rt>ゆうがた</rt></ruby>**ごろ**から<ruby>風<rt>かぜ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>強<rt>つよ</rt></ruby>くなるので、<ruby>洗濯物<rt>せんたくもの</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>めに<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>り<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んでください。<br>*The wind picks up from around evening, so bring the washing in early.*

<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby>**ごろ**に<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>す<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>なので、それまでに<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>をまとめないといけません。<br>*I'm due to move around the middle of next month, so I have to get my things packed before then.*

---

## ～ごろ · Nuance & register

`ごろ` is register-neutral and extremely common in speech. It does, however, do something socially useful: it declines to pin anyone down. Saying <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>ごろ<ruby>伺<rt>うかが</rt></ruby>います</code> promises an afternoon visit without promising the minute, which is why it turns up in arrangements between friends and in service Japanese alike. The flip side is that a business appointment that genuinely starts at three is announced as <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>から</code> - putting `ごろ` on a fixed start time reads as vagueness where precision was expected.

---

## ～ごろ · Nuance & register (cont.)

The `ご`/`こ` alternation is not two words. It is one word that voices when it is stuck straight onto a time expression: <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>ごろ</code>, <code><ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>ごろ</code>, <code><ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>ごろ</code>, <code><ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>ごろ</code>. Left standing on its own - after `の`, after an adjective, after この／その／あの - it stays unvoiced: <code><ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>のころ</code>, <code><ruby>若<rt>わか</rt></ruby>いころ</code>, `あのころ`. Both are written <code><ruby>頃<rt>ころ</rt></ruby></code> when written in kanji, though modern text usually leaves them in kana.

<code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>ごろ</code> deserves a line of its own, because it has an idiomatic second life. Literally "around now", it is also the word for *too late*: <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>ごろ<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ても、もう<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったよ</code> - "turning up now, are you? It's over." Tone carries the difference, and the sting is real.

---

## ～ごろ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Finally, `ごろ` has quietly built a small family of vocabulary meaning "the right time for": <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ごろ</code> (best time to see it), <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べごろ</code> (perfectly ripe), <code><ruby>手<rt>て</rt></ruby>ごろ</code> (a reasonable price or size). <code><ruby>桜<rt>さくら</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>ごろです</code> is what the sign in the park says.

---

## ～ごろ · ごろ vs ぐらい・くらい

> **Rule of thumb** - `ごろ` approximates a **point**; `ぐらい` approximates an **amount**. Clock times, dates and eras take `ごろ`. Durations, counts, prices, distances and sizes take `ぐらい`.

<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>**ごろ**<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*I'll leave the office at about three.*

<ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**かかりました。<br>*It took about three hours.*

---

## ～ごろ · ごろ vs ぐらい・くらい (cont.)

The pair differ by one character, and that character decides everything: `三時` is a place on the clock, `三時間` is a length. Everything countable behaves like the second one - <code><ruby>五人<rt>ごにん</rt></ruby>ぐらい</code>, <code><ruby>千円<rt>せんえん</rt></ruby>ぐらい</code>, <code><ruby>十<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>キロぐらい</code> - and none of them will take `ごろ`.

> **Note** - you will hear <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>ぐらいに</code> in casual speech, and speakers do not treat it as an error any more. `ごろ` is still the standard choice for a clock time and the one an exam expects. What is unambiguously clumsy is stacking them: <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>ごろぐらい</code> says the same thing twice.

---

## ～ごろ · ごろ vs 約・だいたい

> **Rule of thumb** - `ごろ` is a **suffix** and goes after the time. <code><ruby>約<rt>やく</rt></ruby></code> is a **prefix** on a number and belongs to written and formal Japanese. `だいたい` is an ordinary **adverb** and floats freely.

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>約<rt>やく</rt></ruby>**<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby><ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れています。<br>*The train is running about ten minutes late.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は**だいたい**<ruby>五時<rt>ごじ</rt></ruby>**ごろ**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わります。<br>*The meeting will finish around five-ish.*

---

## ～ごろ · ごろ vs 約・だいたい (cont.)

<code><ruby>約<rt>やく</rt></ruby></code> attaches to a quantity, so it patterns with `ぐらい`, not with `ごろ`; you see it in timetables, reports and announcements, and it sounds stiff in conversation. `だいたい` is happy in front of almost anything and can sit in the same sentence as `ごろ`, as the second example shows - the two are not in competition. In written notices you will also meet <code><ruby>前後<rt>ぜんご</rt></ruby></code>: <code><ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby><ruby>前後<rt>ぜんご</rt></ruby>に<ruby>到着<rt>とうちゃく</rt></ruby>の<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby></code>, "scheduled to arrive around three", which is `ごろ` in a suit.

---

## ～ごろ · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>二時間<rt>にじかん</rt></ruby>**ごろ**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちました。 → ✅ <ruby>二時間<rt>にじかん</rt></ruby>**ぐらい**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちました。<br>*<code><ruby>二時間<rt>にじかん</rt></ruby></code> is a length of time, not a point on the clock. Lengths take `ぐらい`.*

- ❌ <ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>**にごろ**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ります。 → ✅ <ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>**ごろに**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*`ごろ` bonds to the time word first; any particle comes after it. The `に` is optional, but it can only stand in that order.*

- ❌ <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの**ごろ**は<ruby>野球<rt>やきゅう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きでした。 → ✅ <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの**ころ**は<ruby>野球<rt>やきゅう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きでした。<br>*After `の` the word is standing on its own as a noun, so it does not voice.*

- ❌ <ruby>十時<rt>じゅうじ</rt></ruby>**ごろぐらいに**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ます。 → ✅ <ruby>十時<rt>じゅうじ</rt></ruby>**ごろ**<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*Both words mean "about". Using them together is not wrong so much as sloppy - pick one.*

Eight o'clock is a point on the clock, and the sentence is guessing at *when*, so `ごろ` is the suffix that fits. Nothing here is being measured, so `ぐらい` has no job to do.

# ～おきに

**Meaning** · at intervals of - leaving a gap of X between one occurrence and the next

`おきに` describes something that repeats with a fixed **gap** in between. The word to hold on to is *gap*, not *every*, because that single idea explains why <code><ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>おきに</code> means "every ten minutes" while <code><ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>おきに</code> means "every **other** day". English translates them differently; Japanese is saying the same thing both times.

---

## ～おきに · Formation

`おきに` is a suffix and needs a **number** in front of it. It attaches straight to a quantity - of time, of distance, or of countable things.

---

## ～おきに · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Minutes, hours | Quantity + おきに | <ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>おきに |
| Days, weeks, months, years | Quantity + おきに | <ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby> → <ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>おきに |
| Distance | Quantity + おきに | <ruby>五<rt>ご</rt></ruby>メートル → <ruby>五<rt>ご</rt></ruby>メートルおきに |
| Countable things | Counter + おきに | <ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つ → <ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つおきに |
| People, seats, rows | Counter + おきに | <ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>おきに、<ruby>一列<rt>いちれつ</rt></ruby>おきに |
| Before a noun | Quantity + おき + **の** | <ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>おきの<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby> |
| Vague quantity | <ruby>数<rt>すう</rt></ruby>～ + おきに | <ruby>数分<rt>すうふん</rt></ruby>おきに、<ruby>数<rt>すう</rt></ruby>メートルおきに |
| ❌ Bare unit | ❌<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>おきに | ✅<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>おきに |
| ❌ Before a noun with な | ❌<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>おきな | ✅<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>おきの |

---

## ～おきに · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `おきに` measures the **space between** two occurrences, never the length of the cycle. <code><ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>おきに</code> leaves one whole day empty: Monday, Wednesday, Friday.

> **Note** - why do the English translations diverge? Because of what the unit is. With days, weeks and years, and with seats and people, the unit is a **container** the event sits inside, so a gap of one means one whole container left empty: <code><ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つおきに<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>る</code> is one seat used, one seat free. With minutes, hours and metres, the event takes no measurable time or space of its own, so a gap of ten minutes simply puts the events ten minutes apart. One definition, two arithmetics.

## ～おきに · Examples

バスは<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**おきに**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*The buses come every ten minutes.*

この<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>は<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>**おきに**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んでください。<br>*Take this medicine every other day.*

---

## ～おきに · Nuance & register

`おきに` is neutral and faintly precise-sounding - it belongs to timetables, instructions on a packet, and descriptions of routines. Nothing about it is formal or casual, and it does not change shape in polite speech.

It does have one real-world problem, and you should know about it rather than be caught by it: with days, weeks and years, **speakers themselves disagree** about what `おきに` means. By the rule above, <code><ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>おきに</code> leaves three days empty and so happens once every four days - but a large minority of native speakers use it to mean once every three days. Everyone agrees about <code><ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>おき</code> ("every other day"); past that, confidence drops.

---

## ～おきに · Nuance & register (cont.)

The practical consequence is that when it genuinely matters, Japanese avoids the word. A pharmacist writes <code><ruby>二日<rt>ふつか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>一回<rt>いっかい</rt></ruby></code>, a timetable writes <code><ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby><ruby>間隔<rt>かんかく</rt></ruby></code>, and a coach says <code><ruby>中<rt>なか</rt></ruby><ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>あけて</code>. If you are giving instructions someone has to follow, say how many times per period and skip `おきに` entirely. If you are merely describing a rhythm, `おきに` is exactly right.

Before a noun, use `の`: <code><ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>おきの<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>おきのアナウンス</code>. It is a noun-like word, not a な-adjective.

---

## ～おきに · おきに vs ごとに

> **Rule of thumb** - `ごとに` names the **cycle**; `おきに` names the **gap**. So <code><ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>ごとに</code> is day 1, day 4, day 7 - and <code><ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>おきに</code> is day 1, day 5, day 9.

<ruby>二日<rt>ふつか</rt></ruby>**ごとに**<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>をやります。<br>*I water them every two days.*

<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>**おきに**<ruby>水<rt>みず</rt></ruby>をやります。<br>*I water them every other day.*

Those two sentences describe **the same schedule**, which is the neatest way to feel the difference: a cycle of two days is a gap of one. Whenever you are unsure, convert to `ごとに` in your head and count.

---

## ～おきに · おきに vs ごとに (cont.)

With small units of time the two land in the same place - <code><ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>ごとに</code> and <code><ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>おきに</code> both describe buses ten minutes apart - which is why the distinction seems to vanish and then reappear the moment you switch to days.

`ごとに` also does something `おきに` cannot: it attaches to ordinary nouns and to verbs, meaning "each". <code><ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>ごとに<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>え<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>が<ruby>違<rt>ちが</rt></ruby>う</code> ("everyone thinks differently"), <code><ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>うごとに<ruby>背<rt>せ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くなる</code> ("he's taller every time I see him"). `おきに` needs a number and always will.

---

## ～おきに · おきに vs 毎～

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt></ruby></code> means **every single one, no gap**. `おきに` exists precisely to describe the gaps that <code><ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt></ruby></code> denies.

<ruby>毎週<rt>まいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>にテニスをします。<br>*I play tennis every Sunday.*

<ruby>一週間<rt>いっしゅうかん</rt></ruby>**おきに**テニスをします。<br>*I play tennis every other week.*

---

## ～おきに · おきに vs 毎～ (cont.)

The mechanical difference is just as important: <code><ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt></ruby></code> is a prefix on a bare unit - <code><ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>毎週<rt>まいしゅう</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>毎月<rt>まいつき</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>毎年<rt>まいとし</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>毎回<rt>まいかい</rt></ruby></code> - and it will not take a number. ❌<code><ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt></ruby><ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby></code> does not exist; the Japanese for "every three days" has to be <code><ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>ごとに</code> or <code><ruby>二日<rt>ふつか</rt></ruby>おきに</code>.

---

## ～おきに · おきに vs ～に一回

> **Rule of thumb** - <code>～に<ruby>一回<rt>いっかい</rt></ruby></code> counts occurrences inside a period and is never ambiguous. When you need to be understood exactly, use it instead of `おきに`.

<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>**おきに**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*I go running every other day.*

<ruby>二日<rt>ふつか</rt></ruby>**に<ruby>一回<rt>いっかい</rt></ruby>**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*I go running once every two days.*

Again the same schedule, said two ways. The second phrasing has no gap-versus-cycle trap in it at all, which is why hospitals, schools and instruction manuals prefer it. `おきに` is more compact and sounds more like a rhythm you live by; `に一回` is what you write when someone will be counting.

---

## ～おきに · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt></ruby><ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby><ruby>掃除<rt>そうじ</rt></ruby>します。 → ✅ <ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>**ごとに**<ruby>掃除<rt>そうじ</rt></ruby>します。<br>*<code><ruby>毎<rt>まい</rt></ruby></code> cannot take a number. Use `ごとに` for a cycle, or <code><ruby>二日<rt>ふつか</rt></ruby>おきに</code> for the same schedule expressed as a gap.*

- ❌ <ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby>**おきに**<ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>っています。（meaning "every day"） → ✅ <ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>学校<rt>がっこう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*`おきに` always inserts a gap. If nothing is skipped, the word you want is <code><ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby></code>.*

- ❌ **おきに**<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby><ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます。 → ✅ <ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>**おきに**<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*`おきに` is a suffix. The quantity always comes first.*

# ～始める

**Meaning** · start doing something; begin to

<code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> welded onto the stem of another verb marks the moment an action gets under way. It is the plain, unremarkable "start": no surprise, no drama, and equally usable for something you planned, something you decided on the spot, and a slow change in the weather.

---

## ～始める · Formation

Attach <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> to the **ます-stem**. The result is a single compound verb, and it conjugates as an ordinary る-verb.

---

## ～始める · Nuance & register

Compound verbs of this shape are register-neutral. <code><ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> is as ordinary in a text message as in a report, and the politeness of the sentence is decided at the very end - <code><ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた</code> versus <code><ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めました</code>.

What makes <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> the workhorse is that it has no attitude. It does not say the start was sudden, welcome, overdue or surprising; it just puts a mark on the timeline. That neutrality is also why it can appear in forms its rival <code>～<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> cannot: you can request one (<code><ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めてください</code>), propose one (<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めましょう</code>), or plan one (<code><ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>通<rt>かよ</rt></ruby>い<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めます</code>).

---

## ～始める · Nuance & register (cont.)

With する-nouns there are two routes and they are not quite the same. <code><ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> treats the studying as an activity that has now begun; <code><ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> treats it as a thing you have taken up. For events with a fixed shape - a meeting, a lesson, a ceremony - only the second works: you say <code><ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めます</code>, never ❌<code><ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めます</code>.

In speech you will also hear the whole idea carried by <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた</code> on its own - <code>もう<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた?</code> - where context supplies the verb. That is ellipsis, not a different pattern.

---

## ～始める · ～始める vs ～出す

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> is a **neutral** start; <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> is a **sudden, unplanned** one. If you chose to do it, or you are asking someone else to, it has to be <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code>.

<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>水泳<rt>すいえい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>い**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>め**ます。<br>*I'm starting swimming lessons next month.*

<ruby>後<rt>うし</rt></ruby>ろの<ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>り**<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**た。<br>*The dog behind us suddenly took off running.*

The first is a decision announced in advance, and ❌<code><ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>します</code> would be strange - you cannot schedule a surprise. The second is an event nobody controlled, which is exactly <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code>'s territory.

---

## ～始める · ～始める vs ～出す (cont.)

The clearest test is the form of the verb. Requests, invitations and volitionals are closed to <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code>: ❌<code><ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください</code>, ❌<code><ruby>歌<rt>うた</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>そう</code>. Their <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> versions are unremarkable.

---

## ～始める · ～始める vs 始まる

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> needs somebody doing the starting; <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まる</code> is the thing starting by itself. The pair splits exactly as <code><ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ける</code>／<code><ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>く</code> does.

<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>にミーティングを**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>め**ます。<br>*We'll start the meeting at nine. (someone is starting it)*

ミーティングは<ruby>九時<rt>くじ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まり**ます。<br>*The meeting starts at nine. (that's the schedule)*

---

## ～始める · ～始める vs 始まる (cont.)

Note the particles: `を` with <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code>, `が`／`は` with <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まる</code>. The compound suffix built on the first of these is the only one that exists - there is **no** ❌<code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まる</code>. If you want to say a process got under way on its own, either use <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> anyway (<code><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた</code>) or reach for <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code>.

---

## ～始める · ～始める vs ～てくる

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> points at the **starting line**; `～てくる` traces a change **moving towards now**. Both fit gradual change, and they emphasise different halves of it.

<ruby>葉<rt>は</rt></ruby>が<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>くなり**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>め**ました。<br>*The leaves have started turning red.*

<ruby>葉<rt>は</rt></ruby>が<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>くなって**きました**。<br>*The leaves have been turning red.*

---

## ～始める · ～始める vs ～てくる (cont.)

The first says the process has a beginning and we have just passed it. The second says the change has been accumulating and here is where it has got to. In everyday speech about weather and seasons, `～てくる` is the more common of the two, and the two happily combine: <code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなり<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めてきた</code> is heard, though it is doing the job twice over.

---

## ～始める · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める** → ✅ <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める**<br>*The suffix attaches to the ます-stem, never to the dictionary form: <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みます → <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み + <ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める.*

- ❌ <ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まり**ました。 → ✅ <ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>め**ました。<br>*<code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>まる</code> is intransitive and cannot take the `を` object. The compound suffix is always <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>から<ruby>運動<rt>うんどう</rt></ruby>**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>め**ます。 → ✅ <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>から<ruby>運動<rt>うんどう</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めます。／<ruby>運動<rt>うんどう</rt></ruby>**し**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めます。<br>*A する-noun needs either `を` in front of the independent verb, or `し` to make the compound. It cannot sit bare against <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code>.*

---

## ～始める · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>め**ました。 → ✅ <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>く**なり**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めました。<br>*Adjectives have no ます-stem. Turn the adjective into the verb of change first - <code><ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなる</code> - and attach the suffix to that.*

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) みんなが<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>き<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた。 (b) みんなが<ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した。

# ～出す

**Meaning** · suddenly start doing; burst out doing

<code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> on the stem of another verb says the action **broke out**. Where <code>～<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> merely marks a beginning, <code>～<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> adds abruptness: nobody saw it coming, nobody arranged it, and often nobody could have stopped it.

---

## ～出す · Formation

Attach <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> to the **ます-stem**. The compound is a う-verb, so it conjugates like <code><ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>す</code>.

---

## ～出す · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> removes the plan. Because of that, it cannot appear in any form that assumes control: no ください, no ましょう, no volitional, and no announcement of what you intend to do next week.

---

## ～出す · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - like <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code>, this suffix needs a verb with some duration. Something that is over in an instant has no onset to mark: ❌<code><ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>消<rt>き</rt></ruby>え<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した</code>. Say <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>消<rt>き</rt></ruby>えた</code> instead, and keep <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> for things that go on once they have started - rain, crying, running, arguing.

---

## ～出す · Examples

<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>ちゃんが<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**た。<br>*The baby suddenly burst into tears.*

<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>たら、<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**ていた。<br>*I looked outside and it had started snowing.*

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>がおもしろくて、クラスのみんなが**<ruby>笑<rt>わら</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**た。<br>*The teacher's story was so funny the whole class burst out laughing.*

バスが**<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**てから、かばんを<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れたことに<ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>がついた。<br>*It was only after the bus pulled away that I realised I'd left my bag behind.*

---

## ～出す · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>も<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>わずに**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**たので、みんな<ruby>驚<rt>おどろ</rt></ruby>きました。<br>*She broke into a run without a word, and everyone was taken aback.*

<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだった<ruby>教室<rt>きょうしつ</rt></ruby>が、ベルの<ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>で<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>騒<rt>さわ</rt></ruby>ぎ<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**た。<br>*The classroom had been silent, and at the bell it erupted all at once.*

<ruby>夜中<rt>よなか</rt></ruby>に<ruby>隣<rt>となり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>鳴<rt>な</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**て、<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>れませんでした。<br>*The dog next door started barking in the night and I couldn't sleep till morning.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりそうになったころ、<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>反対<rt>はんたい</rt></ruby>だと**<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**て、また<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>かかりました。<br>*Just as the meeting was winding down, Tanaka suddenly came out against it, and it took another hour.*

---

## ～出す · Nuance & register

The suffix is register-neutral - it is as at home in a novel as in a complaint to a friend - but it is never *emotionally* neutral. Something about the event was unexpected, and the speaker is registering that. This is why <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> collects the same adverbs over and over: <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に</code>, `いきなり`, <code><ruby>突然<rt>とつぜん</rt></ruby></code>.

The loss of control has one consequence learners rarely anticipate: used about your own deliberate action, <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> sounds as if you surprised yourself. <code><ruby>気<rt>き</rt></ruby>がついたら<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>していた</code> ("I looked up and I was already writing") is a good sentence; ❌ <code><ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>から<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>します</code> is not, because you cannot schedule your own outburst.

---

## ～出す · Nuance & register (cont.)

<code><ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> deserves separate mention. It means "to come out with", "to be the one who raises it", and it carries a whiff of blame: <code><ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>したのは<ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>だよ</code> - "you're the one who brought it up." That is the suffix doing exactly its usual job, applied to speech.

Finally, be aware that a handful of very common verbs ending in <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> are fully lexicalised and have nothing to do with suddenness: <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> (remember), <code><ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> (take out), <code><ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> (draw out), <code><ruby>貸<rt>か</rt></ruby>し<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> (lend out), <code><ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>つけ<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> (track down). You learn these as words, not as this pattern.

---

## ～出す · ～出す vs ～始める

> **Rule of thumb** - same slot, different attitude. <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> is available in every form of the verb; <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> is available only where nobody is in charge.

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>から<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>のジョギングを**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>め**ます。<br>*I'm taking up jogging in the mornings from next week.*

<ruby>信号<rt>しんごう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>青<rt>あお</rt></ruby>になって、<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>一斉<rt>いっせい</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**た。<br>*The lights went green and the cars all pulled away together.*

The first announces a plan; <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> cannot go there. The second describes something that simply happened, and <code><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>り<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた</code> would drain the moment of its snap.

---

## ～出す · ～出す vs ～始める (cont.)

When the same verb takes either suffix, the difference is a threshold. <code><ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>き<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めた</code> reports that crying commenced; <code><ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>き<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>した</code> shows you the face crumpling.

---

## ～出す · ～出す vs 急に・いきなり

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> is an **adverb** that can modify any event. <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> is a **suffix** that can only mark a beginning. They stack, and neither replaces the other.

<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>消<rt>き</rt></ruby>えた。<br>*The lights suddenly went out.*

<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**た。<br>*It suddenly started to rain.*

---

## ～出す · ～出す vs 急に・いきなり (cont.)

Going out is instantaneous, so there is no onset for <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> to mark and only the adverb is possible. Raining lasts, so both work - and the second sentence uses both at once, which is completely normal and not redundant: the adverb says it was sudden, the suffix says this was the *start* of something that then went on.

---

## ～出す · 出す "suddenly begin" vs 出す "outwards"

> **Rule of thumb** - if the first verb is about **handling or moving something**, <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> is probably the literal "out". If it is about an **activity that can start**, it is the aspect marker.

ポケットからかぎを**<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**た。<br>*I took the key out of my pocket.*

<ruby>犬<rt>いぬ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>庭<rt>にわ</rt></ruby>から**<ruby>逃<rt>に</rt></ruby>げ<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**た。<br>*The dog got out of the garden and ran off.*

Nothing in the first sentence is sudden - the key came *out*, and that is all the suffix is contributing. The second sits on the border and carries a little of both: escaping is outward motion, and it is also something that happened without warning.

---

## ～出す · 出す "suddenly begin" vs 出す "outwards" (cont.)

The one to watch is <code><ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>い<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code>. It means *remember*, not "suddenly begin to think" - the memory comes out, it does not start. Treat it as a single word.

---

## ～出す · Common mistakes

- ❌ みなさん、そろそろ<ruby>歌<rt>うた</rt></ruby>い**<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>し**てください。 → ✅ みなさん、そろそろ<ruby>歌<rt>うた</rt></ruby>い**<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>め**てください。<br>*A request assumes the listener is in control, which is precisely what <code><ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code> denies.*

# 急に

**Meaning** · suddenly, all at once - and, of an arrangement, at short notice

<code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> marks a change that arrived with no warning. Its second job is quieter and just as common: applied to plans and appointments it means *unexpectedly, at the last minute*, which is why it opens so many apologies.

---

## 急に · Usage

<code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> is the adverbial form of the な-adjective <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>だ</code>. That matters in practice, because in front of a noun it has to change shape: <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>用事<rt>ようじ</rt></ruby></code> ("something urgent that has come up"), <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby></code> ("short notice"), <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>坂<rt>さか</rt></ruby></code> ("a steep hill" - the same word, applied to a slope). Only the `に` form modifies a verb.

Its position in the sentence is free; most often it sits immediately before the verb, or at the head of the clause when it colours the whole event. It clusters around three kinds of predicate:

- **change** - <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>くなる</code>,<br>*<code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かになる</code>, <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>変<rt>か</rt></ruby>わる</code>*
- **starting and stopping** - <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す</code>,<br>*<code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まる</code>, <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>消<rt>き</rt></ruby>える</code>*
- **arrangements** - <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>決<rt>き</rt></ruby>まる</code>,<br>*<code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む</code>, <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>呼<rt>よ</rt></ruby>ばれる</code>*

---

## 急に · Usage (cont.)

It does not take degree adverbs. ❌<code>とても<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> is not said - suddenness is not a quantity you can have more of.

The word to keep separate is <code><ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いで</code>, the て-form of the verb <code><ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぐ</code>. Same kanji, different reading, opposite kind of meaning: <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> is about the event surprising you, <code><ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いで</code> is about you hurrying.

---

## 急に · Examples

<ruby>空<rt>そら</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>くなった。<br>*The sky went dark all of a sudden.*

<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まって、びっくりしました。<br>*The train stopped suddenly and gave me a fright.*

**<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>用事<rt>ようじ</rt></ruby>ができたので、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>約束<rt>やくそく</rt></ruby>はキャンセルさせてください。<br>*Something's come up out of the blue, so please let me cancel today's plans.*

<ruby>先月<rt>せんげつ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>元気<rt>げんき</rt></ruby>だった<ruby>祖父<rt>そふ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>入院<rt>にゅういん</rt></ruby>することになって、<ruby>家族<rt>かぞく</rt></ruby>みんなで<ruby>病院<rt>びょういん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>通<rt>かよ</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*My grandfather was fine until last month and has suddenly ended up in hospital, so the whole family has been going in to see him.*

---

## 急に · Nuance & register

<code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> is register-neutral and extremely frequent. It is equally at home in a weather report and in a text to a friend, and it carries no politeness of its own.

Its most useful social function is as the opening of an apology. When you have to change something at the last minute, Japanese says so first and explains afterwards: <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>にすみません</code> among friends, <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>で<ruby>申<rt>もう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>訳<rt>わけ</rt></ruby>ありませんが</code> at work, <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>なお<ruby>願<rt>ねが</rt></ruby>いで<ruby>恐縮<rt>きょうしゅく</rt></ruby>ですが</code> in a formal email. In all three the word is doing the same thing: acknowledging that the other person has had no time to prepare. Leaving it out makes the same request sound inconsiderate.

---

## 急に · Nuance & register (cont.)

Watch the shape when a noun follows. <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>だ</code> is a な-adjective, so it is <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>変更<rt>へんこう</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby></code>. The related noun <code><ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぎ</code> exists too and behaves differently: <code><ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぎの<ruby>用事<rt>ようじ</rt></ruby></code> joins with `の`, and it means "a job that must be done fast" rather than "a job that came up unexpectedly".

---

## 急に · 急に vs 突然・いきなり

> **Rule of thumb** - all three mean "suddenly". <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> is the everyday default; <code><ruby>突然<rt>とつぜん</rt></ruby></code> is stronger and more written; `いきなり` complains that there was no lead-up at all.

**<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*A meeting's been put in my diary at short notice.*

**<ruby>突然<rt>とつぜん</rt></ruby>**、<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>の<ruby>電気<rt>でんき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>消<rt>き</rt></ruby>えた。<br>*All at once the lights in the room went out.*

**いきなり**<ruby>大声<rt>おおごえ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しかけられて、こわかった。<br>*Someone started shouting at me out of nowhere, and it was frightening.*

---

## 急に · 急に vs 突然・いきなり (cont.)

<code><ruby>突然<rt>とつぜん</rt></ruby></code> is dramatic. It suits accidents, disappearances and news, and it reads as slightly literary in ordinary chat. `いきなり` is the most colloquial and focuses on the missing preliminaries - no greeting, no warning, no run-up - which is why it so often carries irritation.

<code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> is the only one of the three that comfortably covers scheduling. <code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みます</code> is the normal way to say you are taking unplanned leave; ❌<code><ruby>突然<rt>とつぜん</rt></ruby><ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みます</code> would sound as though you had vanished.

---

## 急に · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てください。（meaning "come quickly"） → ✅ <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いで<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てください。／すぐに<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てください。<br>*<code><ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に</code> describes an event that surprises people. Telling someone to hurry needs <code><ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>いで</code> or `すぐに`.*

- ❌ <ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>用事<rt>ようじ</rt></ruby>があるので、<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>します。 → ✅ <ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>用事<rt>ようじ</rt></ruby>があるので、<ruby>失礼<rt>しつれい</rt></ruby>します。<br>*In front of a noun the な-adjective needs its `な`. The `に` form can only modify a verb.*

- ❌ バスがとても<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まった。 → ✅ バスが<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まった。<br>*Suddenness has no degrees, so `とても` has nothing to intensify. If you want the event to sound more violent, change the verb, not the adverb.*

# ～続ける

**Meaning** · keep doing; go on doing without stopping

<code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code> welded onto a verb stem is the mirror image of <code>～<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code>. Where that one marks the moment something starts, this one claims the stretch after it: the action ran on, and it did not break off. Very often the sentence is also saying that this took some doing.

---

## ～続ける · Formation

Attach <code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code> to the **ます-stem**. The compound is a る-verb.

---

> **Note** - the suffix covers two shapes of continuation, and both are normal. What it cannot do is attach to something over in an instant - ❌<code><ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code> of one arrival has nothing to stretch.

---

<ruby>外<rt>そと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>暗<rt>くら</rt></ruby>くなっても、<ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>かって**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**ていた。<br>*Even after it got dark outside, my brother stayed at his desk writing.*

<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がり<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**ているので、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>が<ruby>大変<rt>たいへん</rt></ruby>です。<br>*Vegetable prices just keep going up, so shopping is hard work.*

<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なくても、<ruby>彼女<rt>かのじょ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>毎週<rt>まいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちに<ruby>手紙<rt>てがみ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>り<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**ました。<br>*Even though no reply ever came, she went on sending her friend a letter every week.*

---

## ～続ける · Nuance & register

The suffix is register-neutral, but it is rarely emotionally flat. Because it insists that something *did not stop*, it tends to pick up either admiration or exasperation from the surrounding sentence. <code><ruby>夢<rt>ゆめ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>追<rt>お</rt></ruby>い<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code> is a compliment; <code><ruby>文句<rt>もんく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>い<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code> is not. That is context, not grammar - but it is why the word is so common in speeches and so common in complaints.

The form you will use most is <code>～<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けている</code>, which is how Japanese says "have been doing X for N years": <code><ruby>二十年<rt>にじゅうねん</rt></ruby><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>み<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けています</code>. The plain past <code>～<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けた</code> closes the stretch off, so it belongs to stories about finished periods.

---

## ～続ける · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two neighbours worth knowing. <code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けて</code> works as a bare adverb meaning "in a row" or "carry on": <code><ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けて<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>った</code>, <code>どうぞ<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けてください</code>. And with a する-noun, spoken Japanese often prefers the independent verb - <code><ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code> rather than <code><ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>し<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code> - especially when the activity has a name and a shape.

---

## ～続ける · ～続ける vs ～続く

> **Rule of thumb** - if a person is keeping it going, <code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code>. If it simply goes on, <code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>く</code>. The particles follow: `を` with the first, `が` with the second.

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>三日<rt>みっか</rt></ruby>も**<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>い**ている。<br>*It's been raining for three days straight.*

<ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>夜中<rt>よなか</rt></ruby>まで**ゲームをし<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**ていた。<br>*My son carried on playing games until the middle of the night.*

With weather and other things nobody controls, both suffixes are heard, and <code><ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>く</code> is the more usual - it is what a forecast says. With a human agent only <code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code> is possible; there is no ❌<code><ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>し<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>く</code>.

---

## ～続ける · ～続ける vs ～続く (cont.)

The independent verbs split the same way, and this is the pair to memorise:

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**ましょう。<br>*Let's carry on with the meeting.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>まで**<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>き**ました。<br>*The meeting went on until six.*

---

## ～続ける · ～続ける vs ～ている

> **Rule of thumb** - `ている` says the action is **under way**; <code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code> says it **has not stopped**. The first is a snapshot, the second is a claim about the whole stretch.

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、レポートを<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>い**ています**。<br>*I'm writing the report right now.*

<ruby>朝<rt>あさ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じレポートを**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**ています。<br>*I've been writing the same report since this morning.*

---

## ～続ける · ～続ける vs ～ている (cont.)

Both are true of the same moment, and only the second complains. Note also what `ている` can do that this suffix cannot: it covers habits (<code><ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っています</code>) and resulting states (<code><ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>しています</code>). ❌<code><ruby>結婚<rt>けっこん</rt></ruby>し<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けています</code> is not a way to say you are married - it would mean you keep getting married.

---

## ～続ける · ～続ける vs ずっと

> **Rule of thumb** - `ずっと` is an **adverb** you drop into any sentence; <code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code> is a **suffix** that changes the verb. They stack happily, and `ずっと` reaches places the suffix cannot.

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どものころから**ずっと**サッカーが<ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>きです。<br>*I've loved football ever since I was a child.*

<code><ruby>好<rt>す</rt></ruby>き</code> is not a verb, so there is no stem for <code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code> to attach to; `ずっと` has no such problem and works with adjectives, nouns and states alike. It also does duty as "a long way back" - <code>ずっと<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いました</code> - which has nothing to do with continuing at all.

---

## ～続ける · ～続ける vs ずっと (cont.)

Where both are available, `ずっと` reports the span while <code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code> insists on the unbrokenness, and putting them together is completely normal: <code>ずっと<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ち<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けていました</code>.

---

## ～続ける · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む**<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける** → ✅ <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み**<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける**<br>*The suffix takes the ます-stem, never the dictionary form.*

- ❌ <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>きます。 → ✅ <ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**ます。／<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>き**ます。<br>*<code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>く</code> is intransitive and cannot take a `を` object. Pick the verb that matches the particle.*

- ❌ <ruby>夜中<rt>よなか</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>**<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**ました。 → ✅ <ruby>夜中<rt>よなか</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>**し<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**ました。<br>*A する-noun needs the `し` to make the compound - or use the independent verb with `を`: <code><ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けました</code>.*

---

## ～続ける · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>み**<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**ます。（meaning "I've lived here ten years"） → ✅ この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby><ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>み**<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**ています。<br>*A stretch that reaches the present needs `ている`. The plain <code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けます</code> is a promise about the future.*

---

## ～続ける · Practice 1

{reveal}
<ruby>三時間<rt>さんじかん</rt></ruby><ruby>以上<rt>いじょう</rt></ruby><ruby>歩<rt>ある</rt></ruby>き**<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>け**て、<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>くなりました。

## ～続ける · Practice 2

**2.** すみません、<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>を（<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けて・<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>いて）ください。

{reveal}

{reveal}
For a stretch that runs up to the present, the suffix takes `ている`. The plain past <code><ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>え<ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>けました</code> would close the period off and imply she has stopped.

## ～続ける · Practice 4

# ～終わる

**Meaning** · finish doing something; get to the end of it

<code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> on a verb stem closes the bracket that <code>～<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> opened. It says the action ran its course and reached its natural end - the book was read to the last page, the meal was eaten, the letter was written and signed.

---

## ～終わる · Formation

Attach <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> to the **ます-stem**. The compound is a う-verb.

---

## ～終わる · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | ます-stem + <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みます → <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる |
| Verb (る-verb) | ます-stem + <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べます → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる |
| Verb (する) | し + <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる | <ruby>掃除<rt>そうじ</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>掃除<rt>そうじ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる |
| Conjugation | う-verb | <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わった、<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わって |
| Object particle | unchanged from the first verb | <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>**を**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる |
| Deliberate / written twin | ます-stem + <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>える | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>える |
| Independent verb | Noun + **が** + <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる | <ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる |

---

## ～終わる · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ Instantaneous verb | ❌<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>き<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる | ✅<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いた |
| ❌ Uncontrolled event | ❌<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる | ✅<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>がやむ |

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> wants a verb with a beginning, a middle and a finish line. Reading a book qualifies. Arriving does not: it has no middle. Raining does not either - it has no finish line, only a stop, and Japanese uses `やむ` for that.

---

## ～終わる · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - the compound looks intransitive but keeps the `を` of the first verb: <code><ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりました</code>. Nothing about the object changes when you bolt <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> on, even though the independent verb <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> would never take `を` on its own.

---

## ～終わる · Examples

ごはんを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**たら、お<ruby>皿<rt>さら</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>ってね。<br>*When you've finished eating, wash your plate.*

この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>はもう**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わり**ました。<br>*I've finished this book.*

<ruby>作文<rt>さくぶん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**た<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>から<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Hand your composition in as soon as you've finished writing it.*

<ruby>洗濯<rt>せんたく</rt></ruby>を**し<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**たら、ベランダに<ruby>干<rt>ほ</rt></ruby>しに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*Once I've finished the washing I'll go and hang it out on the balcony.*

---

## ～終わる · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>き<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**てから、ゆっくり<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えましょう。<br>*Let's hear the whole story out and then think it over properly.*

<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>最後<rt>さいご</rt></ruby>まで**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**たときには、もう<ruby>夜中<rt>よなか</rt></ruby>の<ruby>一時<rt>いちじ</rt></ruby>だった。<br>*By the time I'd watched the film to the end it was already one in the morning.*

<ruby>試験<rt>しけん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>問題<rt>もんだい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>全部<rt>ぜんぶ</rt></ruby>**<ruby>解<rt>と</rt></ruby>き<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**たのに、<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>くのを<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れてしまいました。<br>*I got through every question on the exam and then forgot to write my name.*

<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>け<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**たら、<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>くの<ruby>喫茶店<rt>きっさてん</rt></ruby>でコーヒーでも<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みませんか。<br>*When we've finished putting everything away, shall we get a coffee somewhere nearby?*

---

## ～終わる · Nuance & register

The suffix is register-neutral and enormously frequent, above all in the form <code>～<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったら</code>, which is how Japanese sequences the tasks of an ordinary day.

Its close relative <code>～<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>える</code> means the same thing with a different feel. <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>える</code> is genuinely transitive and sounds more deliberate and slightly more written: <code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>える</code> suggests you set out to get through the thing and did. <code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> just notes that the reading came to an end. In conversation <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> is far more common; in a report or an essay <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>える</code> often reads better.

---

## ～終わる · Nuance & register (cont.)

Around the independent verbs the picture is worth keeping straight. <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> is basically intransitive - <code><ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> - and its everyday transitive partners are <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらせる</code> ("get it finished") and <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>える</code> ("bring it to a close"). There is one place where <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> does take `を`, and you will hear it constantly: the fixed announcement <code>これで<ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>を<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わります</code>, <code><ruby>本日<rt>ほんじつ</rt></ruby>はこれで<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わります</code>. Careful writers prefer <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わりにします</code> or <code><ruby>終了<rt>しゅうりょう</rt></ruby>します</code>, but the `を` version is standard spoken Japanese in that formula.

---

## ～終わる · ～終わる vs ～終える

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different temperature. <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> is the neutral everyday form; <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>える</code> is transitive, deliberate, and at home in writing.

レポートを<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わり**ました。<br>*I've finished writing the report.*

レポートを<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>き**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>え**ました。<br>*I have completed the report.*

The second sounds like a person reporting an achievement, and it would be the better choice in an email to a client. Between friends it is faintly formal.

---

## ～終わる · ～終わる vs ～終える (cont.)

The one thing <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>える</code> cannot do is describe something ending by itself: ❌<code><ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>える</code> is impossible, because <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>える</code> always needs someone bringing the thing to an end.

---

## ～終わる · ～終わる vs ～てしまう

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> is about **reaching the end** of a process. `てしまう` is about **completion with an attitude** - either "the whole lot" or "and I wish I hadn't".

ケーキを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**てから、コーヒーを<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れた。<br>*I made coffee after I'd finished the cake.*

<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>でケーキを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**てしまっ**た。<br>*I went and ate the whole cake on my own.*

---

## ～終わる · ～終わる vs ～てしまう (cont.)

The first is neutral sequencing. The second reports a completed act with a feeling attached - pride, or more often regret. `てしまう` also attaches happily to verbs with no duration at all (<code><ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れてしまった</code>, <code><ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>としてしまった</code>), which is exactly where <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> is impossible.

---

## ～終わる · ～終わる vs 終わる・終わらせる

> **Rule of thumb** - as a **suffix** it rides on another verb's stem. As an **independent verb** it needs a noun and `が`; to say that *you* finished something, the verb is <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらせる</code> or <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>える</code>.

<ruby>授業<rt>じゅぎょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>三時<rt>さんじ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わり**ます。<br>*Class finishes at three.*

<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>にこの<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらせ**ます。<br>*I'll get this job finished before the day is out.*

---

## ～終わる · ～終わる vs 終わる・終わらせる (cont.)

The first has no agent - three o'clock arrives and the class is over. The second has you in it, pushing. That is why a manager says <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わらせてください</code> and never ❌<code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わってください</code> when they mean "get it done"; <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わってください</code> would be asking the task to end of its own accord.

---

## ～終わる · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる** → ✅ <ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>み**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる**<br>*The suffix goes on the ます-stem, like <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>める</code> and <code><ruby>続<rt>つづ</rt></ruby>ける</code>.*

- ❌ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>り**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**た。 → ✅ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>がやんだ。<br>*Rain does not complete anything; it stops. Uncontrolled processes with no finish line take `やむ`, <code><ruby>止<rt>と</rt></ruby>まる</code> or <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わる</code> as an independent verb.*

- ❌ <ruby>掃除<rt>そうじ</rt></ruby>**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わり**ました。 → ✅ <ruby>掃除<rt>そうじ</rt></ruby>**し<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わり**ました。／<ruby>掃除<rt>そうじ</rt></ruby>**が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わり**ました。<br>*A する-noun needs either `し` to build the compound, or `が` in front of the independent verb.*

- ❌ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>き**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わっ**た。 → ✅ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いた。<br>*Arriving takes no time, so there is no process to finish. The plain past says everything.*

---

# ～ているところ

**Meaning** · right in the middle of doing it - at this exact stage

`ところ` is the ordinary noun for "place". Put after `ている`, the place it names is on a **timeline** rather than a map: the point you have reached in an action that is under way. N5 gave you `ている` for "is doing"; `ているところ` narrows that to *this stage, right now, neither before nor after*.

---

## ～ているところ · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | ている + ところ | <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる → <ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べているところ |
| Verb (する) | している + ところ | <ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>しているところ |
| Plain sentence end | ているところだ | <ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>っているところだ |
| Polite sentence end | ているところです | <ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>っているところです |
| Past reference | ているところだった | <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ているところだった |
| Interrupted by something | ているところ + に／へ | <ruby>話<rt>はな</rt></ruby>しているところに<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た |
| Seen or caught | ているところ + を | <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ているところを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>られた |
| Typical companions | <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、ちょうど、ちょうど<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby> | <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいるところです |
| Casual contraction | てるとこ | <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってるとこ |

---

## ～ているところ · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ Habit | ❌<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っているところです | ✅<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っています |
| ❌ Resulting state | ❌<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いているところです | ✅<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いています |

> **Rule of thumb** - `ている` has three jobs: action in progress, resulting state, and habit. `ているところ` takes **only the first**. If you could not put <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby></code> or `ちょうど` in front of the sentence, `ところ` does not belong there.

---

## ～ているところ · Formation (cont.)

> **Note** - because `ところ` is really "place", the same string of characters can be a literal noun phrase: <code><ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>住<rt>す</rt></ruby>んでいるところ</code> is "where I live", not "the stage I am at in living". Context sorts this out easily. The aspect reading almost always ends the sentence with `だ`／`です`, or carries on with `に`, `へ` or `を`.

---

## ～ているところ · Examples

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、ごはんを**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>っているところ**です。<br>*I'm making dinner right now.*

ちょうど<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>を**やっているところ**だよ。<br>*I'm doing my homework as we speak.*

その<ruby>件<rt>けん</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>**<ruby>調<rt>しら</rt></ruby>べているところ**です。もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しお<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちください。<br>*We're looking into that at the moment - please bear with us a little longer.*

<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>を**かけているところ**に、お<ruby>客<rt>きゃく</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てしまった。<br>*A customer turned up right as I was in the middle of a phone call.*

---

## ～ているところ · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>がケーキを**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べているところ**を<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>に<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>られた。<br>*My little brother got caught by our mother in the act of eating the cake.*

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>けているところ**だったので、まだ<ruby>散<rt>ち</rt></ruby>らかっています。<br>*I was in the middle of tidying the room, so it's still a mess.*

さっき<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いて、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>タクシーを**<ruby>探<rt>さが</rt></ruby>しているところ**です。<br>*I got to the station a moment ago and I'm looking for a taxi now.*

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>計画<rt>けいかく</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>しているところ**だったので、<ruby>質問<rt>しつもん</rt></ruby>はあとにしました。<br>*The manager was in the middle of explaining the new plan, so I saved my question for later.*

---

## ～ているところ · Nuance & register

`ているところです` is the standard polite way to report **status**, and this is the single most useful thing to take from the page. On the phone, in an email, at a counter, <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>確認<rt>かくにん</rt></ruby>しているところです</code> ("I'm checking on that now") says far more than `まだです`: it tells the other person the work is actually under way, and it buys time without sounding evasive. Business Japanese leans on it heavily.

In casual speech the whole thing contracts. `ている` becomes `てる` and `ところ` becomes `とこ`: <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>てるとこ</code>, <code><ruby>向<rt>む</rt></ruby>かってるとこだよ</code>. The `だ` usually disappears too. This is extremely common between friends and completely out of place in writing.

---

## ～ているところ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two constructions grow out of the same phrase. With `に` or `へ`, `ところ` sets up an **interruption** - something arrives at the stage you were at: <code><ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけようとしているところに<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>鳴<rt>な</rt></ruby>った</code>. With `を`, it sets up being **caught**, usually by a verb of seeing or stopping: <code>サボっているところを<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>つかった</code>. Both are worth recognising even before you produce them.

---

## ～ているところ · ～ているところ vs ～ている

> **Rule of thumb** - `ている` is broad; `ているところ` is narrow. Adding `ところ` throws away the habit reading and the resulting-state reading and keeps only "in progress at this moment".

<ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>六時<rt>ろくじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っています。<br>*I run at six every morning.*

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っているところなので、あとでかけ<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>します。<br>*I'm out running at the moment, so I'll call you back.*

---

## ～ているところ · ～ているところ vs ～ている (cont.)

The first is a habit, and ❌<code><ruby>毎朝<rt>まいあさ</rt></ruby><ruby>走<rt>はし</rt></ruby>っているところです</code> is impossible - a habit is not a stage you are standing in. The same goes for states: <code><ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いています</code> ("the window is open") describes a result, and ❌<code><ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いているところです</code> would be describing a window in the middle of opening itself.

Where both are possible, `ところ` is the emphatic one. It answers "have you done it yet?" with "I am doing it *this second*", and it is much harder to hear as an excuse.

---

## ～ているところ · 食べるところ vs 食べているところ vs 食べたところ

> **Rule of thumb** - one noun, three stages. Dictionary form = **about to**. `ている` = **in the middle of**. た-form = **just finished**.

これから<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる**ところ**です。<br>*I'm just about to have lunch.*

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**ているところ**です。<br>*I'm having lunch right now.*

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**たところ**です。<br>*I've just finished lunch.*

---

## ～ているところ · 食べるところ vs 食べているところ vs 食べたところ (cont.)

Nothing changes but the form of the verb, and the three sentences point at three different minutes. Learn them as a set: the same `ところ` is doing the same job in all three, and only the tense of the verb moves you along the timeline.

---

## ～ているところ · ～ているところ vs ～ている間に

> **Rule of thumb** - `ているところ` describes **the subject's own stage**, and usually ends the sentence. <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code> opens a **window** during which something else happens - and the `に` decides whether the other event fills the window or falls inside it.

The `に` is the entire difference, and it is worth slowing down for:

<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>が<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ている**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>**、ずっとそばにいました。<br>*I stayed beside my mother the whole time she was asleep.*

<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>が<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ている**<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に**、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってきました。<br>*While my mother was asleep, I nipped out and did the shopping.*

---

## ～ているところ · ～ているところ vs ～ている間に (cont.)

Without `に`, the second clause must last as long as the window - hence `ずっと`, and hence verbs like <code><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っていた</code>, <code><ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでいた</code>. With `に`, the second clause is a one-off event that fits somewhere inside the window, and it is typically something finishable: going out, making a call, tidying up. Swap them and both sentences go wrong - ❌<code><ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ている<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った</code> claims you shopped for the entire nap.

`ているところ` is doing something different again. It is not a time clause at all; it is a statement about where the subject has got to:

<ruby>母<rt>はは</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>**ているところ**です。<br>*My mother is asleep just at the moment.*

---

## ～ているところ · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>運動<rt>うんどう</rt></ruby>しているところです。 → ✅ <ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby><ruby>運動<rt>うんどう</rt></ruby>しています。<br>*A habit has no "stage". `ところ` only works for something happening at the moment of speaking.*

- ❌ <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いているところです。 → ✅ <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>が<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*This `ている` is a resulting state, not an action in progress, and `ところ` cannot attach to it.*

- ❌ <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べるているところです。 → ✅ <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べているところです。<br>*The `ている` is built on the て-form, not the dictionary form. The dictionary form goes straight onto `ところ` and means "about to".*

- ❌ <ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>十年<rt>じゅうねん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>から<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いているところです。 → ✅ …<ruby>働<rt>はたら</rt></ruby>いています。<br>*Ten years is not a stage you are standing in. Long-running situations take plain `ている`.*

---

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>が<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>をしている<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>皿<rt>さら</rt></ruby>を<ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>っていました。 (b) <ruby>姉<rt>あね</rt></ruby>が<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby>をしている<ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>は<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きました。

## ～ているところ · Practice 5 — answer

(a) has no `に`, so the two activities are the same length: for the whole time your sister was cooking, you were at the sink. Both clauses describe stretches, and neither has a single point attached to it.

(b) has `に`, which turns the cooking into a **window** and puts a one-off event inside it. The shopping trip started and finished somewhere in the middle of the cooking; it did not last the whole time and nobody claims it did.

That one syllable is the whole distinction. A verb that goes on (<code><ruby>洗<rt>あら</rt></ruby>っていた</code>, <code><ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>っていた</code>) wants <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby></code>; a verb that finishes (<code><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った</code>, <code><ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>した</code>) wants <code><ruby>間<rt>あいだ</rt></ruby>に</code>.

# ～ところ

**Meaning** · about to do; on the point of doing

With a verb in the **dictionary form**, `ところ` names the stage just *before* the action: you have not started, and you are about to. The same construction in the past - `～ところだった` - becomes one of the most useful sentences in the language: "I very nearly did."

---

## ～ところ · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | **dictionary form** + ところだ | <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>るところだ |
| Polite | dictionary form + ところです | <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>るところです |
| Past - "nearly" | dictionary form + ところだった | <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れるところだった |
| Past polite | dictionary form + ところでした | <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れるところでした |
| More imminent still | ようとしている + ところ | <ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけようとしているところ |
| Interrupted | ところ + に／へ | <ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>るところに<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た |
| Typical companions | これから、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>から、ちょうど、もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しで | ちょうど<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めるところ |
| Casual contraction | とこ | <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>るとこ |
| ❌ ます-form | ❌<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きますところ | ✅<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くところ |

---

## ～ところ · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ Distant future | ❌<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くところです | ✅<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>です |

> **Rule of thumb** - the dictionary form in front of `ところ` means the action has **not begun**. Not "I am doing it", not "I have done it" - the foot is still in the air.

> **Note** - in this sense `ところ` normally ends the sentence with `だ`／`です`, or carries on with `に`／`へ` to introduce an interruption. It is not a free subordinate clause: to say "the phone rang as I was about to go to bed" you need <code><ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>るところに</code>, not a bare <code><ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>るところ</code> followed by a comma.

ちょうど<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>る**ところ**なので、あと<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby>くらいで<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>くと<ruby>思<rt>おも</rt></ruby>います。<br>*I'm walking out the door right now, so I should be there in about ten minutes.*

<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>かずに<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る**ところ**でしたが、<ruby>同僚<rt>どうりょう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてくれて<ruby>助<rt>たす</rt></ruby>かりました。<br>*I was about to go home without hearing the explanation, but a colleague filled me in - a lucky escape.*

## ～ところ · Nuance & register

In its present-tense use, `ところです` is a status report about the very near future, and it is the natural answer to <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>してる?</code> or <code>もう<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>た?</code>. In casual speech it contracts to `とこ` and drops the copula: <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>るとこ</code>. Both registers are extremely common; what you will not hear is this pattern in a formal written notice, where a schedule would be stated with times instead.

The past form is where the real value is. `～ところだった` means *it nearly happened and it didn't*, and it almost always carries relief. It collects a fixed set of adverbs - <code>もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しで</code>, <code><ruby>危<rt>あや</rt></ruby>うく</code>, `あぶなく` - and it is how Japanese says all of these:

---

## ～ところ · Nuance & register (cont.)

- <code><ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れるところでした</code> - "I almost forgot."
- <code><ruby>遅刻<rt>ちこく</rt></ruby>するところだった</code> - "I nearly ended up late."
- <code><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ってしまうところだった</code> - "I almost bought it."

Note that the verb stays in the **dictionary form** even though the sentence is about the past. The past lives in `だった`／`でした` at the end; the verb has to stay un-happened, because the whole point is that it never happened.

One thing this pattern does **not** do is announce plans. It has a horizon of a few minutes. For anything further out, the words are `つもり` and <code><ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby></code>.

---

## ～ところ · 出るところ vs 出たところ

> **Rule of thumb** - the form of the verb walks you across the event. Dictionary form: **not started**. た-form: **just finished**. The `ところ` is identical in both.

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>る**ところ**です。<br>*I'm just about to leave the house.*

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>、<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>た**ところ**です。<br>*I've just left the house.*

---

## ～ところ · 出るところ vs 出たところ (cont.)

Said into a phone, these two put you in completely different places: still indoors, or already on the street. The middle member of the set - <code><ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ているところ</code> - is built on `ている` and covers the stretch in between.

This is why the form in front of `ところ` deserves more care than any other choice on this page. The word never changes; the verb carries all the information.

---

## ～ところ · ～ところだ vs ～つもり・～予定

> **Rule of thumb** - `ところ` reaches a few **minutes** into the future. `つもり` is an **intention** and <code><ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby></code> is a **schedule**, and both stretch as far as you like.

<ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby>、<ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**つもり**です。<br>*I'm planning to go to Kyoto next month.*

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**ところ**です。<br>*I'm on my way to the station this minute.*

❌<code><ruby>来月<rt>らいげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くところです</code> is wrong, and audibly so: `ところ` claims you are on the very brink of the action, and you cannot be on the brink of something a month away.

---

## ～ところ · ～ところだ vs ～つもり・～予定 (cont.)

The reverse substitution is not an error so much as a loss. <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くつもりです</code> reports your intention and leaves open whether you have moved; <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くところです</code> says you are already on your feet.

---

## ～ところ · ～ところだった vs ～そうになった

> **Rule of thumb** - both mean "almost". `ところだった` is a whole **situation** you narrowly avoided; `そうになった` is a **physical event** that nearly happened to you.

もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しで<ruby>遅刻<rt>ちこく</rt></ruby>する**ところ**でした。<br>*I very nearly ended up late.*

<ruby>階段<rt>かいだん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>転<rt>ころ</rt></ruby>び**そうになり**ました。<br>*I almost fell on the stairs.*

---

## ～ところ · ～ところだった vs ～そうになった (cont.)

The mechanics differ too: `そうになる` attaches to the **ます-stem** (<code><ruby>転<rt>ころ</rt></ruby>び</code>, <code><ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>ち</code>, <code><ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>き</code>), while `ところ` takes the dictionary form. And their coverage differs. `そうになる` wants something on the verge of physically occurring - falling, dropping, crying, crashing. `ところだった` is happy with outcomes you avoided by *deciding differently*: <code><ruby>危<rt>あや</rt></ruby>うく<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いほうを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>うところだった</code>, "I almost bought the expensive one." `そうになる` cannot say that.

---

## ～ところ · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけ**ますところ**です。 → ✅ <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>かけ**る**ところです。<br>*`ところ` is a noun, and a noun takes the plain form in front of it. The politeness is already there in `です`.*

- ❌ <ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby>、アメリカに<ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>する**ところ**です。 → ✅ <ruby>来年<rt>らいねん</rt></ruby>、アメリカに<ruby>留学<rt>りゅうがく</rt></ruby>する**<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>**です。<br>*A year is far outside the range of this pattern. Distant plans take <code><ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby></code> or `つもり`.*

- ❌ もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しで<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れ**たところ**でした。 → ✅ もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しで<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れ**るところ**でした。<br>*The verb stays in the dictionary form: the forgetting never happened. Only `でした` moves into the past.*

---

## ～ところ · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**ているところ**だったので、<ruby>後<rt>あと</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。（meaning "I was about to eat"） → ✅ <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**るところ**だったので…<br>*`ている` puts you in the middle of the meal. If the food is untouched, the dictionary form is the one you want.*

# ～たところ

**Meaning** · have just done it - standing at the moment immediately after

With the verb in the **た-form**, `ところ` names the stage right after the action finished. The emphasis is on *right*: this is a claim about the clock, not about how fresh the event feels. Minutes ago, not last month.

## ～たところ · Nuance & register

`たところです` is the polite way to report that something is done *this instant*, and it is a workplace staple. Asked whether you have sent the file, <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ったところです</code> is better than <code><ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>りました</code> because it tells the other person to look now. In casual speech it shortens to `たとこ`: <code>ごめん、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きたとこ</code>.

The construction is **objective**. It is measured against the clock, and it will not stretch. That is its whole personality, and it is exactly what separates it from `たばかり`, which is measured against how the speaker feels. Because of that, `たところ` has no emotional colour of its own: it neither excuses nor boasts, it just reports where on the timeline you are standing.

---

## ～たところ · Nuance & register (cont.)

Two structural limits follow from the same fact. `たところ` cannot modify a noun - there is no ❌<code><ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ったところの<ruby>料理<rt>りょうり</rt></ruby></code> - because it describes a moment rather than a property of the thing. And it has no negative form: "I haven't eaten yet" is <code>まだ<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べていません</code>, never ❌<code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べなかったところです</code>.

For narration in the past, use `たところだった`: <code><ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ったとき、<ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>ったところだった</code>.

---

## ～たところ · ～たところ vs ～たばかり

> **Rule of thumb** - `たところ` measures with a **clock**; `たばかり` measures with a **feeling**. Both translate as "just", and only one of them will stretch to last year.

<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたところ**です。<br>*I've just arrived at the station.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby><ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たばかり**です。<br>*I only came to Japan last year.*

---

## ～たところ · ～たところ vs ～たばかり (cont.)

The second sentence is impossible with `ところ`: ❌<code><ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby><ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たところです</code>. A year is not a moment. `ばかり` survives it because the speaker is not reporting the clock - they are saying the arrival is still recent *as far as they are concerned*, which is why it works as an excuse for not knowing their way around.

The second difference is structural and easy to test. `ばかり` modifies nouns freely; `ところ` does not:

<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った**ばかりの**パソコンが<ruby>壊<rt>こわ</rt></ruby>れてしまった。<br>*The computer I'd only just bought has broken.*

---

## ～たところ · ～たところ vs ～たばかり (cont.)

❌<code><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ったところのパソコン</code> is not Japanese. If a noun follows, `ばかり` is your only option.

---

## ～たところ · ～たところ vs ～たあとで

> **Rule of thumb** - `たところ` is a **statement about now** and ends the sentence. `～たあとで` is a **connector** and always has another clause after it.

<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったところ**です。<br>*I've just finished work.*

<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わった**あとで**、<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*After work I'm going out for a drink.*

The first sentence is complete; the second would be unfinished without its second half. And they carry different information: `あとで` says nothing at all about how recent the event is, while `たところ` says nothing about what happens next.

---

## ～たところ · ～たところ vs ～たあとで (cont.)

When you do want to hang a following clause on `たところ`, it needs a particle - `に`, `へ` or `で` - and the meaning becomes "just as I had done X, Y happened": <code><ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>座<rt>すわ</rt></ruby>ったところに<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってきた</code>.

---

## ～たところ · ～たところだ vs ～たところ、…

> **Rule of thumb** - with `だ`／`です` it means **"just finished"**. Standing at a comma with no copula, it is a formal connective meaning **"when I did X, it turned out that Y"**.

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いたところ**です。<br>*I've just this minute asked the section chief.*

<ruby>課長<rt>かちょう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いたところ**、<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>になったそうです。<br>*I asked the section chief, and apparently the meeting has been cancelled.*

---

## ～たところ · ～たところだ vs ～たところ、… (cont.)

The connective version has three tells: no `だ`, a comma, and a second clause that reports something **discovered** as a result. It belongs to written and formal Japanese - reports, business email, news - and in speech people say <code><ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてみたら</code> instead. You need to recognise it so that a sentence like the second one does not read to you as "I have just asked."

---

## ～たところ · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>この<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ったところです。 → ✅ <ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby>この<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>った**ばかり**です。<br>*A year ago is not "just now" on any clock. Subjective recency is `ばかり`'s job.*

- ❌ これは<ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った**ところの**<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ これは<ruby>先週<rt>せんしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った**ばかりの**<ruby>自転車<rt>じてんしゃ</rt></ruby>です。<br>*`ところ` cannot modify a noun. When a noun follows, only `ばかり` works.*

- ❌ <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる**ところ**です。（meaning "I've just eaten"） → ✅ <ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**た**ところです。<br>*The dictionary form means you have not started. One character decides whether the food is gone.*

---

## ～たところ · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ まだ<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>こ</rt></ruby>なかったところです。 → ✅ まだ<ruby>返事<rt>へんじ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>ていません。<br>*There is no negative version of this pattern. "Not yet" is `まだ～ていません`.*

---

## ～たところ · Practice 1

**1.** 「もう<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べましたか。」「ええ、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ____ところです。」

{reveal}
ええ、<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**た**ところです。

{reveal}
The た-form places you immediately after the meal, which is what <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby></code> and the question both call for. <code><ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べるところです</code> would mean the food is still untouched, and would answer the question with a flat no.

---

## ～たところ · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>した（ところ・ばかり）です。

{reveal}
<ruby>三年<rt>さんねん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>を<ruby>卒業<rt>そつぎょう</rt></ruby>した**ばかり**です。

{reveal}
Three years cannot be squeezed into `ところ`, which is measured on the clock. `ばかり` reports how recent it feels to the speaker - here, that they still count as a new graduate.

---

## ～たところ · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "The train has just left, so the next one is in ten minutes."

{reveal}
<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby>**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たところ**なので、<ruby>次<rt>つぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>十分<rt>じゅっぷん</rt></ruby><ruby>後<rt>ご</rt></ruby>です。

{reveal}
The departure happened seconds ago and the sentence is about the clock, so `たところ` is exactly right. <code><ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たばかり</code> would also be understood but would sound like a complaint about your luck rather than a timetable fact.

---

## ～たところ · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: これは<ruby>先月<rt>せんげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>したところのテレビです。

{reveal}
✅ これは<ruby>先月<rt>せんげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>した**ばかりの**テレビです。

{reveal}
Two problems, one fix. `ところ` cannot sit in front of a noun, and last month is too far back for it anyway. `ばかり` handles both: it takes `の` before a noun and it stretches to cover anything the speaker still thinks of as recent.

## ～たところ · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たところです。 (b) <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たばかりです。

{reveal}
(a) means you have this moment arrived - you are at the airport, or you have just walked out of it, and the suitcase is still in your hand. It is a statement about the clock and it will not stretch past a few minutes.

{reveal}
(b) means you are new here. It might be a week, it might be six months; what it reports is that the arrival is still recent from where you stand. This is the one you use to explain why your Japanese is shaky or why you got lost, and (a) would be strange in that role.

# ～たばかり

**Meaning** · only just did it - recent as far as the speaker is concerned

`たばかり` also translates as "just", but it is measuring something quite different from `たところ`. `ところ` reads the clock. `ばかり` reads the **speaker**: it says the event is still recent *to them*, and that judgement stretches. Five minutes, five weeks, sometimes five years - as long as the speaker still feels new to it.

---

## ～たばかり · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | **た-form** + ばかりだ | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たばかりだ |
| Verb (voiced た-form) | だ-form + ばかりだ | <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んだばかりだ |
| Polite | た-form + ばかりです | <ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたばかりです |
| **Before a noun** | た-form + ばかり + **の** + Noun | <ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>いたばかりのパン |
| Giving a reason | たばかりなので／たばかりで | <ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ったばかりなので… |
| Past reference | たばかりだった | <ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めたばかりだった |
| Casual | ばっかり／ばっか | <ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たばっかり |
| Different sense: "only" | Noun + ばかり | ゲームばかりしている |
| Different sense: "nothing but" | て-form + ばかり + いる | <ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>んでばかりいる |

---

## ～たばかり · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ❌ Dictionary form | ❌<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>るばかりです | ✅<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たばかりです |

> **Rule of thumb** - recency here is **relative to the thing**. A bus that left five minutes ago, a job started last month and a building put up five years ago can all take `ばかり`, because "new" means something different for each. That flexibility is exactly what `たところ` refuses.

> **Note** - the noun-modifying form is the practical reason to learn this pattern. <code><ruby>生<rt>う</rt></ruby>まれたばかりの<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>ちゃん</code>, <code><ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>ったばかりの<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby></code>, <code><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ったばかりの<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby></code> - all completely ordinary, and none of them can be built with `ところ`. Do not drop the `の`.

---

## ～たばかり · Examples

さっき<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ごはんを<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べた**ばかり**です。<br>*I only had lunch a little while ago.*

この<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>った**ばかり**で、まだ<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>もわかりません。<br>*I've only just joined this company, so I don't know anything yet.*

<ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>いた**ばかり**のパンはやっぱりおいしいね。<br>*Bread straight out of the oven really is good, isn't it.*

<ruby>生<rt>う</rt></ruby>まれた**ばかり**の<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>ちゃんは、<ruby>一日<rt>いちにち</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>ています。<br>*A newborn baby sleeps all day long.*

---

## ～たばかり · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>習<rt>なら</rt></ruby>った**ばかり**の<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>を、もう<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れてしまいました。<br>*I've already forgotten the kanji I only just learned.*

<ruby>去年<rt>きょねん</rt></ruby><ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った**ばかり**のパソコンなのに、もう<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>かなくなった。<br>*I only bought this computer last year and it's already stopped working.*

この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>してきた**ばかり**なので、この<ruby>辺<rt>へん</rt></ruby>のことはまだよく<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らないんです。<br>*I've only just moved to this town, so I don't really know the area yet.*

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>た**ばかり**のころは、コンビニで<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>い<ruby>物<rt>もの</rt></ruby>をするだけでも<ruby>緊張<rt>きんちょう</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*When I'd only just arrived in Japan, even shopping in a convenience store made me nervous.*

---

## ～たばかり · Nuance & register

`たばかり` is register-neutral, and its most characteristic job is **explaining yourself**. `～たばかりなので` is how Japanese excuses inexperience, disorder or ignorance without apologising outright: <code><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めたばかりなので、まだ<ruby>下手<rt>へた</rt></ruby>です</code>, <code><ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>したばかりで、<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>片付<rt>かたづ</rt></ruby>いていません</code>. It buys the speaker some room, and because the recency is subjective, nobody argues with it.

In casual speech it lengthens rather than shortens: `ばっかり` and `ばっか` are both extremely common - <code><ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>起<rt>お</rt></ruby>きたばっかりだよ</code> - and both are firmly spoken-only.

---

## ～たばかり · Nuance & register (cont.)

Be aware that the same word `ばかり` has a much more frequent meaning, "only, nothing but": <code><ruby>肉<rt>にく</rt></ruby>ばかり<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる</code>, <code><ruby>文句<rt>もんく</rt></ruby>ばかり<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>う</code>, <code><ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>てばかりいる</code>. The two are told apart by what comes in front. A **た-form** gives you "just did"; a **noun** or a **て-form** gives you "nothing but". Getting this wrong produces a sentence that means the opposite of what you intended, so it is worth a moment's care.

---

## ～たばかり · ～たばかり vs ～たところ

> **Rule of thumb** - `ところ` is measured on the **clock**; `ばかり` is measured by the **speaker**. In the few minutes after an event both are available; outside that window only `ばかり` survives.

<ruby>入学<rt>にゅうがく</rt></ruby>した**ばかり**なので、<ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちがまだ<ruby>少<rt>すく</rt></ruby>ないです。<br>*I've only just started at the school, so I don't have many friends yet.*

<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>今<rt>いま</rt></ruby><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わった**ところ**です。<br>*The meeting finished just now.*

---

## ～たばかり · ～たばかり vs ～たところ (cont.)

Try swapping them. ❌<code><ruby>入学<rt>にゅうがく</rt></ruby>したところなので</code> would claim you enrolled minutes ago, which makes nonsense of the excuse. And <code><ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったばかりです</code>, while perfectly grammatical, shifts the emphasis from the timetable to how it feels - closer to "it's barely over" than to "it finished at 3.02".

The second difference is the one that decides most sentences in practice: only `ばかり` can sit in front of a noun. <code><ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>いたばかりの<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby></code> is normal; ❌<code><ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>いたところの<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby></code> does not exist.

---

## ～たばかり · ～たばかり vs ばかり ("only, nothing but")

> **Rule of thumb** - look left. **た-form** + ばかり = "just did". **Noun** + ばかり, or **て-form** + ばかり + いる = "nothing but".

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>は<ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しいゲームを<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>った**ばかり**だ。<br>*My little brother has only just bought the new game.*

<ruby>弟<rt>おとうと</rt></ruby>はゲーム**ばかり**している。<br>*My little brother does nothing but play games.*

---

## ～たばかり · ～たばかり vs ばかり ("only, nothing but") (cont.)

Same four syllables, and the two sentences do not even share a topic: the first is about when, the second about proportion. The `ている` version is the one to watch, because it looks so close to the pattern on this page: <code><ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>いてばかりいる</code> means "does nothing but cry", while <code><ruby>泣<rt>な</rt></ruby>いたばかりだ</code> would mean "has only just cried".

---

## ～たばかり · ～たばかり vs 最近

> **Rule of thumb** - <code><ruby>最近<rt>さいきん</rt></ruby></code> is an adverb about a **period**; `たばかり` attaches to a **single completed event**. They combine happily, but they are not substitutes.

**<ruby>最近<rt>さいきん</rt></ruby>**、よく<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きます。<br>*I've been going to the cinema a lot lately.*

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>先月<rt>せんげつ</rt></ruby><ruby>開<rt>ひら</rt></ruby>いた**ばかり**です。<br>*This shop only opened last month.*

---

## ～たばかり · ～たばかり vs 最近 (cont.)

<code><ruby>最近<rt>さいきん</rt></ruby></code> can cover a habit spread over weeks, which `ばかり` cannot do at all - there is no single event for it to attach to. Conversely `ばかり` pins the recency to one thing that happened, which is why it, and not <code><ruby>最近<rt>さいきん</rt></ruby></code>, can go in front of a noun.

Putting them together is completely normal and adds emphasis: <code><ruby>最近<rt>さいきん</rt></ruby><ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めたばかりの<ruby>趣味<rt>しゅみ</rt></ruby>です</code>, "it's a hobby I've only recently taken up."

---

## ～たばかり · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る**ばかり**です。 → ✅ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>**た**ばかりです。<br>*This pattern is built on the た-form. The dictionary form gives you an entirely different, much rarer construction.*

- ❌ <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ったばかり<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>せてください。 → ✅ <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ったばかり**の**<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>せてください。<br>*In front of a noun, `ばかり` needs `の`. Leaving it out is the single most common slip with this pattern.*

- ❌ <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>た**ばかり**います。 → ✅ <ruby>兄<rt>あに</rt></ruby>は<ruby>寝<rt>ね</rt></ruby>**て**ばかりいます。<br>*"Does nothing but sleep" is built on the て-form plus `いる`. With the た-form the sentence would mean he had only just fallen asleep.*

---

## ～たばかり · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>**て**ばかりです。（meaning "I've only just come to Japan"） → ✅ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>**た**ばかりです。<br>*The て-form pushes you into the "nothing but" meaning, which here would be something like "all I do is come to Japan".*

---

## ～たばかり · Practice 1

**1.** この<ruby>町<rt>まち</rt></ruby>に<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>して<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>____ばかりなので、まだ<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>がよくわかりません。

{reveal}
<ruby>引<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>っ<ruby>越<rt>こ</rt></ruby>して<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>**た**ばかりなので…

{reveal}
The pattern needs the た-form. Note what the sentence is doing: the move might have been a month ago, and `ばかり` is being used to excuse not knowing the streets yet - a job the clock-bound `たところ` could not do.

## ～たばかり · Practice 2

**2.** これは<ruby>五年<rt>ごねん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>建<rt>た</rt></ruby>った（ところ・ばかり）のビルです。

{reveal}
これは<ruby>五年<rt>ごねん</rt></ruby><ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>建<rt>た</rt></ruby>った**ばかり**のビルです。

{reveal}
Two reasons, and either one settles it. Five years is far outside `ところ`'s range, and `ところ` cannot modify a noun in the first place. For a building, five years old genuinely counts as new - which is exactly the kind of judgement `ばかり` is built to express.

## ～たばかり · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "These are cakes I've just baked - please help yourself."

{reveal}
<ruby>焼<rt>や</rt></ruby>いた**ばかりの**ケーキです。どうぞ<ruby>召<rt>め</rt></ruby>し<ruby>上<rt>あ</rt></ruby>がってください。

{reveal}
A noun follows, so the form must be `たばかりの` with the `の` in place. This is the everyday way to say "freshly made", and it is one of the commonest uses of the pattern.

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しないで、<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>んだばかりいます。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>息子<rt>むすこ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>勉強<rt>べんきょう</rt></ruby>しないで、<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>**んで**ばかりいます。

{reveal}
"Does nothing but play" is the *other* `ばかり`, and it is built on the て-form plus `いる`. With the た-form the sentence would say he had only just started playing, which is not a complaint at all.

## ～たばかり · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めたばかりです。 (b) <ruby>新<rt>あたら</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>を<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めたところです。

{reveal}
(a) says you are new to the job. It could be your third day or your third month; what the sentence claims is that you have not settled in yet, and it usually leads into an explanation - you are still learning, you cannot answer that question without asking someone.

{reveal}
(b) reads the clock. It says the starting happened moments ago, which for a job is odd unless you are describing this morning: "I've just this minute started." Native speakers would understand it as the literal instant, and would probably have expected (a).
