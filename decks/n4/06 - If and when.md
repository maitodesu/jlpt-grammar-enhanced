# と - conditional

**Meaning** · whenever A happens, B follows - automatically, every time

This is not the `と` you already know from N5, which joins nouns ("A and B") or marks a companion ("with"). This one joins two **clauses** and says the second follows from the first as a matter of course. Not a plan, not a choice: this is how the world works.

---

## と - conditional · Formation

The clause in front of `と` is always **plain non-past**, no matter when the sentence as a whole happens. There is no past-tense form here at all.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + と | <ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>す → <ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>すと |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + と | <ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>さない → <ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>さないと |
| い-adjective | plain form + と | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いと |
| い-adjective (negative) | くない + と | <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くない → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くないと |
| な-adjective | stem + **だ** + と | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだと |
| Noun | Noun + **だ** + と | <ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>冬<rt>ふゆ</rt></ruby>だと |
| Polite (announcements) | ます-form + と | <ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>します → <ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>しますと |

---

## と - conditional · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - nothing that expresses the **speaker's will** may stand in the result clause. No ～ましょう, no ～てください, no ～たい, no ～たほうがいい, no command, no invitation. `と` reports what *happens*, never what anyone decides to do.

> **Note** - the `だ` in front of `と` is not optional for nouns and な-adjectives. <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**だ**と is "if you're a student"; bare <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>と is "with a student" - the N5 particle, and a completely different sentence.

---

## と - conditional · Examples

ここを**<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>すと**、<ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*Press here and it makes a sound.*

<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>に**なると**、この<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>は<ruby>緑<rt>みどり</rt></ruby>になります。<br>*When spring comes, this mountain turns green.*

3に5を**<ruby>足<rt>た</rt></ruby>すと**、8になります。<br>*Three plus five is eight.*

<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ないと**、<ruby>電車<rt>でんしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いません。<br>*If we don't leave soon, we won't make the train.*

---

## と - conditional · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>父<rt>ちち</rt></ruby>は<ruby>酒<rt>さけ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>むと**、いつも<ruby>同<rt>おな</rt></ruby>じ<ruby>話<rt>はなし</rt></ruby>を<ruby>始<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めます。<br>*Whenever my father drinks, he starts telling the same story.*

この<ruby>角<rt>かど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>右<rt>みぎ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>曲<rt>ま</rt></ruby>がると**、<ruby>左側<rt>ひだりがわ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>花屋<rt>はなや</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*Turn right at this corner and there's a little flower shop on your left.*

<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ると**、<ruby>誰<rt>だれ</rt></ruby>もいませんでした。<br>*When I went into the room, there was nobody there.*

この<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>週末<rt>しゅうまつ</rt></ruby>に**なると**<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>込<rt>こ</rt></ruby>んで、<ruby>一時間<rt>いちじかん</rt></ruby>ぐらい<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>つことになります。<br>*This place suddenly fills up at weekends, and you end up waiting about an hour.*

---

## と - conditional · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けると**、<ruby>遠<rt>とお</rt></ruby>くで<ruby>祭<rt>まつ</rt></ruby>りの<ruby>太鼓<rt>たいこ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>音<rt>おと</rt></ruby>が<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>こえた。<br>*When I opened the window, I could hear festival drums somewhere far off.*

---

## と - conditional · Nuance & register

`と` is register-neutral. It sounds the same in speech and in writing, and the clause before it stays plain even in the politest sentence: <ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>すと<ruby>開<rt>ひら</rt></ruby>き**ます** - the politeness lives on the final verb only. The one polite variant worth recognising is `ますと`, which turns up in announcements, instruction manuals and careful business speech: こちらのボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>し**ますと**、<ruby>扉<rt>とびら</rt></ruby>が<ruby>開<rt>ひら</rt></ruby>きます。

Where `と` really lives is anywhere a fixed procedure is being described: recipes, machine instructions, road directions, arithmetic, seasons, and habits. If you can put "every time" in front of the English and it still makes sense, `と` is probably right.

Two things learners are blindsided by:

---

## と - conditional · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **`もし` and `と` do not go together.** `もし` announces a hypothesis; `と`<br>*announces a regularity. もし<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ると… is odd. Use たら or ば after `もし`.*
- **`～ないと` on its own means "I have to".** The consequence is simply left<br>*unsaid, and everybody fills it in: もう<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かないと。 - "I've got to go." これ、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby><ruby>中<rt>じゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>さないと。 - "I have to hand this in today." Extremely common in casual speech.*

Finally, `と` with a **past-tense main clause** is not a conditional at all. It is the narrative "upon doing X, …", used for something the speaker then discovered or witnessed. Novels and travel diaries are full of it, and it is the one place a `と` sentence describes a single occasion rather than a rule.

---

## と - conditional · と vs たら

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` says *every time, automatically*. `たら` says *on the occasion that this happens*. Only `たら` can be followed by a request, an invitation, or a plan.

This is the difference that gets sentences rejected, because the ban on will-expressing main clauses after `と` is absolute. Compare:

<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたら**、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Call me when you get to the station.*

❌<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>くと、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>してください is wrong, and not mildly so - it is asking the listener to do something as an automatic physical consequence of arriving, which is not a thing you can ask.

---

## と - conditional · と vs たら (cont.)

この<ruby>自動販売機<rt>じどうはんばいき</rt></ruby>は、お<ruby>金<rt>かね</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>入<rt>い</rt></ruby>れると**ランプがつきます。<br>*On this vending machine, a light comes on when you put money in.*

That is a property of the machine - invariable, no speaker in it. Switch to `たら` and it becomes a report of one particular time you fed it a coin.

---

## と - conditional · と vs ば

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` states a link the speaker treats as **actual and invariable**. `ば` puts a condition up as a **premise** and reasons from it.

Both handle general rules, so they overlap widely, and in many sentences either is fine. Where they part company is attitude. `と` has no speaker in it; `ば` is the form you use when you are weighing something up.

<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いと**、すぐ<ruby>売<rt>う</rt></ruby>り<ruby>切<rt>き</rt></ruby>れます。<br>*When something is cheap, it sells out straight away.*

<ruby>値段<rt>ねだん</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ければ**、<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います。<br>*If it's cheap, I'll buy it.*

---

## と - conditional · と vs ば (cont.)

The first is an observation about how this shop behaves. The second is my decision procedure - and a decision is exactly what `と` cannot host. Two more consequences of this: `ば` is the form that takes `もし` comfortably, and `ば` builds the regret pattern ～ばよかった, for which there is no ～とよかった.

---

## と - conditional · と vs なら

> **Rule of thumb** - `なら` picks up a **premise someone has just supplied**, usually the other person. `と` connects two events with nobody supplying anything.

`なら` is a reply; `と` is a description. This shows up most clearly in conversation, where `なら` can also put its main clause **earlier in time** than its condition - something `と` can never do.

「<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くんです。」「<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>**なら**、<ruby>六月<rt>ろくがつ</rt></ruby>がいいですよ。」<br>*"I'm going to Hokkaido." "Hokkaido? June's the time to go."*

❌<ruby>北海道<rt>ほっかいどう</rt></ruby>へ<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くと、<ruby>六月<rt>ろくがつ</rt></ruby>がいいですよ is impossible: going to Hokkaido does not cause June to be good, and the speaker is giving advice, not reporting a mechanism.

---

## と - conditional · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>くと、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>してください。 → ✅ <ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>い**たら**、<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*The clause after `と` is an automatic result, so no request, command, invitation or wish can stand there. This is the single most common `と` error.*

- ❌ <ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>がいいと、<ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>しましょう。 → ✅ <ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>が**よかったら**、<ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>しましょう。<br>*Same rule. `～ましょう` is a proposal - the speaker's will - and `と` has no room for it.*

- ❌ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ったと、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>る**と**／<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っ**たら**、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>です。<br>*Nothing past ever stands in front of `と`. The tense is carried by the main clause.*

---

## と - conditional · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ もし<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>があると、<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>います。 → ✅ もし<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**あれば**／**あったら**、<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>います。<br>*Two problems at once: `もし` flags a hypothesis, and <ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>います is an offer. `と` wants neither.*

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>と<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くなります。 → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**だ**と<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>くなります。<br>*Without `だ`, this is the N5 particle: "it gets cheaper with a student."*

---

## と - conditional · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>秋<rt>あき</rt></ruby>になる____、この<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>が<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>くなります。

{reveal}
<ruby>秋<rt>あき</rt></ruby>になる**と**、この<ruby>公園<rt>こうえん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>木<rt>き</rt></ruby>が<ruby>赤<rt>あか</rt></ruby>くなります。

{reveal}
Every autumn, without fail, with nobody deciding anything - that is the definition of a `と` sentence. Note the plain non-past form `なる` in front of it even though the main clause is polite.

---

## と - conditional · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に（<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>くと・<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたら）、メールをください。

{reveal}
<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたら**、メールをください。

{reveal}
The main clause is a request. `と` forbids requests outright because its result clause reports an automatic consequence, and you cannot ask someone to do something automatically. `たら` has no such restriction.

---

## と - conditional · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "When it gets cold, my grandmother's legs hurt."

{reveal}
<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>く**なると**、<ruby>祖母<rt>そぼ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>足<rt>あし</rt></ruby>が<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>くなります。

{reveal}
A recurring, involuntary consequence - nobody's choice, every time. `と` is the natural form. `たら` would push it towards one specific cold day.

---

## と - conditional · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>があると、<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**あったら**（or **あれば**）、<ruby>映画<rt>えいが</rt></ruby>を<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか。

{reveal}
`～ませんか` is an invitation, so the sentence ends with the speaker's will. `と` cannot carry that. Both `たら` and `ば` work here, because the condition is a state (`ある`) rather than an action.

---

## と - conditional · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けると、<ruby>雪<rt>ゆき</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っていた。 (b) <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けると、<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>が<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなる。

{reveal}
(a) has a **past** main clause, so it is not a conditional at all. It narrates one occasion: "I opened the window, and - snow." The speaker is reporting a discovery, and this is the standard way to write that in Japanese.

{reveal}
(b) has a **non-past** main clause, so it is the ordinary `と`: a general rule about this room. Open the window, and it gets cold - every time.

{reveal}
Same `と`, and the tense of the *main* clause is what decides which reading you get.

# ～ば

**Meaning** · if A, then B - A offered as the condition that would bring B about

`～ば` is the conditional of **reasoning**. It puts a premise on the table and follows it to its consequence, which is why it dominates written Japanese, instructions, arguments - and regrets about what you should have done.

---

## ～ば · Formation

Every ば-form ends in **-eba**. For verbs, change the final `-u` of the dictionary form to `-e` and add `ば`.

---

## ～ば · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (う-verb) | final -u → -e + ば | <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>けば |
| Verb (う-verb) | final -u → -e + ば | <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>ぐ → <ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>げば |
| Verb (る-verb) | る → れば | <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>れば |
| Verb (する) | する → すれば | <ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>する → <ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>すれば |
| Verb (<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る) | irregular | <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>る → <ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>れば |
| Verb (negative) | ない → なければ | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなければ |
| い-adjective | い → ければ | <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しければ |
| いい | irregular | いい → よければ |
| い-adjective (negative) | くない → くなければ | <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くない → <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>くなければ |

---

## ～ば · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| な-adjective | stem + なら(ば) | <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby> → <ruby>便利<rt>べんり</rt></ruby>なら(ば) |
| Noun | Noun + なら(ば) | <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>なら(ば) |
| Noun / な-adj (formal) | + であれば | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>であれば |

> **Note** - the bottom two rows are why `なら` looks like part of this table. It is: `なら(ば)` genuinely is the ば-form of the copula. But the standalone `なら` you meet as its own pattern does a different job, and the two should be kept apart in your head.

---

## ～ば · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - if the ば-clause is an **action verb with the same subject** as the main clause, you cannot end the sentence with a request, a command, an invitation or a statement of intention. Switch to `たら`. The restriction lifts when the ば-clause is a **state**: an adjective, `ある`／`いる`, a potential form, or a negative.

That single rule explains why <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からなければ<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてください is perfectly idiomatic while ❌<ruby>東京<rt>とうきょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>けば<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>してください is not.

---

## ～ば · Examples

**<ruby>急<rt>いそ</rt></ruby>げば**、<ruby>間<rt>ま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>合<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いますよ。<br>*If we hurry, we'll make it.*

<ruby>天気<rt>てんき</rt></ruby>が**よければ**、<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えます。<br>*If the weather's good, you can see the mountains.*

<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>が**あれば**、<ruby>手伝<rt>てつだ</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*If you have time, please give me a hand.*

もっと**<ruby>練習<rt>れんしゅう</rt></ruby>すれば**、うまくなりますよ。<br>*You'll get better if you practise more.*

---

## ～ば · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>説明書<rt>せつめいしょ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>まなければ**、<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>い<ruby>方<rt>かた</rt></ruby>が<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません。<br>*You won't know how to use it unless you read the manual.*

もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>れば**よかった。<br>*I should have left home a bit earlier.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>らなければ**、<ruby>川<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>でバーベキューをする<ruby>予定<rt>よてい</rt></ruby>です。<br>*If it doesn't rain tomorrow, we're planning to have a barbecue by the river.*

この<ruby>地図<rt>ちず</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>れば**、どこに<ruby>駐車場<rt>ちゅうしゃじょう</rt></ruby>があるかすぐ<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かります。<br>*One look at this map and you'll see straight away where the car parks are.*

---

## ～ば · Nuance & register

`ば` itself is neither polite nor casual, but it leans **written and considered**. Notices, manuals, instructions, mathematical and legal prose, and any sentence that is doing a piece of reasoning all reach for it. In relaxed conversation `たら` is more frequent - except in the fixed patterns below, which are everywhere in speech.

Patterns built on `ば` that you will meet constantly:

---

## ～ば · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **～ばいい** - "you just have to…", "it'd be good if…":<br>*この<ruby>紙<rt>かみ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>名前<rt>なまえ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>書<rt>か</rt></ruby>けばいいですか。*
- **～ばよかった** - regret about the road not taken:<br>*もっと<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>言<rt>い</rt></ruby>えばよかった。 There is no `～とよかった`.*
- **～ば～ほど** - "the more…, the more…":<br>*<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えれば<ruby>考<rt>かんが</rt></ruby>えるほど<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からなくなる。*
- **～なければならない／なければいけない** - the written sibling of the N5<br>*`～なくてはいけない`. Same meaning, stiffer feel.*

The casual contractions are the part that catches learners out in real conversation:

---

## ～ば · Nuance & register (cont.)

- `なければ` → **なきゃ**: <ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ら**なきゃ**。 - "I've got to go home."<br>*Very common, all ages, both genders.*
- `なければならない` → **なきゃ** on its own, with the rest swallowed.
- `～れば` → **～りゃ**: <ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>りゃ<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かる。 - blunt, older, and<br>*male-sounding. Recognise it; don't produce it.*

---

## ～ば · ～ば vs ～と

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` reports an **actual, invariable** link. `ば` floats a **premise** and reasons from it, which is why only `ば` can end in a wish, an offer or a request.

<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>になる**と**、この<ruby>池<rt>いけ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>氷<rt>こおり</rt></ruby>が<ruby>溶<rt>と</rt></ruby>けます。<br>*Every spring the ice on this pond melts.*

<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>に**なれば**、この<ruby>池<rt>いけ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>氷<rt>こおり</rt></ruby>も<ruby>溶<rt>と</rt></ruby>けるでしょう。<br>*Once spring comes, the ice on this pond should melt too.*

The `と` version is a fact about how the pond behaves. The `ば` version is the speaker working forward from a supposition about *this* year - note that it needs `でしょう`, because a prediction is a judgement, and `と` has no room for judgements.

---

## ～ば · ～ば vs ～と (cont.)

Two more `ば`-only territories: `もし` sits comfortably in front of it (もし<ruby>間違<rt>まちが</rt></ruby>っていれば<ruby>直<rt>なお</rt></ruby>してください), and counterfactual regret belongs to it alone (～ばよかった, ～ばよかったのに).

---

## ～ば · ～ば vs ～たら

> **Rule of thumb** - where `ば` is blocked, `たら` steps in: before requests and commands after an action verb, and for anything that actually happened once.

<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたら**、すぐ<ruby>報告<rt>ほうこく</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*Report in as soon as you get to the office.*

❌<ruby>会社<rt>かいしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>けば、すぐ<ruby>報告<rt>ほうこく</rt></ruby>してください is rejected: <ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>く is an action the listener performs, and the main clause is a command aimed at the same person. But make the condition a state and `ば` returns:

<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>から**なければ**、いつでも<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてください。<br>*If you don't understand, ask me any time.*

---

## ～ば · ～ば vs ～たら (cont.)

The other gap is the past. `たら` can narrate a one-off sequence that really happened - <ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだら<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がった - and `ば` cannot. A `ば` sentence ending in the past tense is read as counterfactual instead: <ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>けばよかった means "I should have asked", i.e. I didn't.

---

## ～ば · ～ば vs ～なら

> **Rule of thumb** - `ば` conditions on the event **happening**; `なら` conditions on a supposition **being true**. So `ば`'s main clause follows in time, while `なら`'s can come first.

<ruby>沖縄<rt>おきなわ</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>けば**、12<ruby>月<rt>がつ</rt></ruby>でも<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>げますよ。<br>*If you go to Okinawa, you can swim even in December.*

<ruby>沖縄<rt>おきなわ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**なら**、<ruby>飛行機<rt>ひこうき</rt></ruby>を<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>めに<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>したほうがいいですよ。<br>*If you're going to Okinawa, you should book your flight early.*

In the first, the swimming happens once you are there. In the second, the booking happens *before* you go - the trip is a premise the listener has just announced, and the speaker responds to it. `ば` cannot reverse the order like that.

---

## ～ば · ～ば vs ～なら (cont.)

The second difference is mechanical: `なら` attaches straight to a bare noun and picks it up as a topic - コーヒー**なら**<ruby>台所<rt>だいどころ</rt></ruby>にありますよ - a job no ば-form can do.

---

## ～ば · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>う**ば** → ✅ <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>**えば**<br>*The ば-form ends in **-eba**. For a う-verb the final vowel changes: ka-**u** → ka-**e**ba.*

- ❌ <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>**いければ** → ✅ <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>**ければ**<br>*The final `い` of an い-adjective is *replaced* by `ければ`, not kept in front of it.*

- ❌ **いければ**／**いいば** → ✅ **よければ**<br>*`いい` borrows every conjugated form from `よい`, here as elsewhere.*

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**ければ** → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**なら(ば)**／<ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**であれば**<br>*`ければ` belongs to い-adjectives only. Nouns and な-adjectives take `なら(ば)`, or `であれば` in formal writing.*

---

## ～ば · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>**れば**、<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ってください。 → ✅ <ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>っ**たら**、<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*Action verb plus a request aimed at the same person - the one configuration `ば` refuses.*

---

## ～ば · Practice 1

**1.** もっと<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ____、<ruby>健康<rt>けんこう</rt></ruby>になりますよ。

{reveal}
もっと<ruby>野菜<rt>やさい</rt></ruby>を<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べ**れば**、<ruby>健康<rt>けんこう</rt></ruby>になりますよ。

{reveal}
<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べる is a る-verb, so the `る` is replaced by `れば`. The sentence is a piece of reasoning - eat more vegetables and health follows - which is exactly what `ば` is for.

---

## ～ば · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>空港<rt>くうこう</rt></ruby>に（<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>けば・<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたら）、メールしてください。

{reveal}
<ruby>空港<rt>くうこう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>着<rt>つ</rt></ruby>いたら**、メールしてください。

{reveal}
The condition is an action the listener performs, and the main clause is a request to that same listener. `ば` is blocked in exactly that configuration. Had the condition been a state - <ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>があれば - `ば` would have been fine.

---

## ～ば · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "If it's cheap, I'll buy two."

{reveal}
<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>**ければ**、2つ<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
い-adjective: <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ければ. The main clause is the speaker's own decision, not a request to anyone, and the condition is a state - so `ば` is comfortable here even though the sentence expresses an intention.

---

## ～ば · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>いければ、<ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>ります。

{reveal}
✅ この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>**ければ**、<ruby>図書館<rt>としょかん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>借<rt>か</rt></ruby>ります。

{reveal}
い-adjectives lose their final `い` before `ければ`. Keeping it produces a form that does not exist. The same trap catches <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>ければ, <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>ければ, <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しければ.

## ～ば · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ければ、<ruby>涼<rt>すず</rt></ruby>しくなりますよ。 (b) <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けると、<ruby>涼<rt>すず</rt></ruby>しくなります。

{reveal}
(b) is a statement about how this room works: open the window and it cools down, every time. There is no speaker in it - it could be printed on a sign.

{reveal}
(a) presents opening the window as a **solution** the listener might adopt. The speaker is reasoning from a premise and offering the result, which is why the `よ` fits so naturally on the end. In practice you would say (a) to someone who has just complained about the heat, and (b) to someone asking how the room behaves.

# ～たら

**Meaning** · if / when / after - the all-purpose conditional

`～たら` is the one you reach for when you are not sure which conditional you want, and you will usually be right. It takes any kind of main clause, it handles both "if" and "when", and it is the everyday spoken default.

---

## ～たら · Formation

Take the **plain past** of anything at all and add `ら`. That is the entire rule, which is a large part of why `たら` is so easy to use.

---

## ～たら · Formation (cont.)

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | plain past + ら | <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだ → <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだら |
| Verb (る-verb) | plain past + ら | <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>ける → <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>けた → <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>けたら |
| Verb (negative) | なかった + ら | <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まない → <ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>まなかったら |
| い-adjective | かった + ら | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>かったら |
| い-adjective (negative) | くなかった + ら | <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くない → <ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>くなかったら |
| な-adjective | だった + ら | <ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby> → <ruby>不便<rt>ふべん</rt></ruby>だったら |
| Noun | だった + ら | <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>だったら |
| Polite (formal) | ました + ら | <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>きます → <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>きましたら |

---

## ～たら · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the past form in front of `ら` says nothing about **past time**. It marks the condition as *completed at the moment the main clause happens*. The tense of the sentence is carried entirely by the final verb.

`たら` does three jobs, and it is worth naming them separately:

1. **If** - a genuine condition: <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>だったら<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>です。 2. **When / after** - for something certain to happen, where English would not say "if": 20<ruby>歳<rt>さい</rt></ruby>になったら… 3. **Discovery** - with a **past** main clause, no conditional meaning survives at all. See the last two examples.

---

## ～たら · Examples

**<ruby>寒<rt>さむ</rt></ruby>かったら**、エアコンを<ruby>消<rt>け</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*If you're cold, turn the air conditioning off.*

<ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>**<ruby>晴<rt>は</rt></ruby>れたら**、<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>こう。<br>*If it's clear tomorrow, let's go to the sea.*

<ruby>仕事<rt>しごと</rt></ruby>が**<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったら**、<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>みに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きませんか。<br>*Shall we go for a drink after work?*

20<ruby>歳<rt>さい</rt></ruby>に**なったら**、<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で<ruby>暮<rt>く</rt></ruby>らしたいです。<br>*When I turn twenty, I want to live on my own.*

---

## ～たら · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>薬<rt>くすり</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだら**、<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がりました。<br>*I took the medicine and my fever went down.*

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けたら**、<ruby>小<rt>ちい</rt></ruby>さな<ruby>鳥<rt>とり</rt></ruby>が<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>ってきた。<br>*I opened the window and a little bird flew in.*

もし<ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>落<rt>お</rt></ruby>としたら**、すぐ<ruby>交番<rt>こうばん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>けてください。<br>*If you drop your wallet, take it straight to a police box.*

<ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby>の<ruby>会議<rt>かいぎ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>資料<rt>しりょう</rt></ruby>が**できたら**、<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>せてから、みんなに<ruby>送<rt>おく</rt></ruby>ってください。<br>*Once next week's meeting materials are ready, show them to the manager and then send them round.*

---

## ～たら · Examples (cont.)

もし<ruby>道<rt>みち</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>迷<rt>まよ</rt></ruby>ったら**、<ruby>遠慮<rt>えんりょ</rt></ruby>しないで<ruby>近<rt>ちか</rt></ruby>くの<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてみてください。<br>*If you get lost, don't hesitate - just ask someone nearby.*

---

## ～たら · Nuance & register

`たら` is the spoken workhorse. In ordinary conversation it outnumbers `と`, `ば` and `なら` put together; in formal writing it thins out and <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code> and `ば` take over. It is not casual in itself - the politeness sits on the final verb, and `ましたら` exists for genuinely formal contexts: <ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>きましたら、ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>します。

`もし` pairs with `たら` more naturally than with anything else. `もし` is optional and only reinforces the "this may not happen" reading; it never replaces the `たら`.

Two constructions built on `たら` that you meet immediately:

---

## ～たら · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **～たらいい／～たらいいですか** - "it'd be good if…", "what should I…".
- **～たらどうですか／～たら?** - a suggestion aimed at the listener. Both have<br>*their own pages; note only that the suggestion reading comes from `どう`, not from `たら`.*

The **discovery** use deserves its own paragraph, because it is the one that makes learners think they have misread the sentence. With a past-tense main clause, `たら` means "I did X, and then - as it turned out - Y". There is no condition anywhere in it. The Y part has to be something outside the speaker's control: a discovery, a surprise, an event that simply occurred. A *planned* next action is not allowed there, and needs `～てから` instead.

---

## ～たら · ～たら vs ～と

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` needs the link to be automatic and repeatable, and bans any main clause expressing the speaker's will. `たら` accepts single occasions and every kind of ending.

この<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>ける**と**、<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えます。<br>*Open this window and you can see the sea.*

<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け**たら**、<ruby>海<rt>うみ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>えました。<br>*I opened the window, and there was the sea.*

---

## ～たら · ～たら vs ～と (cont.)

The first is a permanent property of the window; the second is one morning. And where `と` would refuse outright - ❌<ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けると、<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>りましょう - `たら` is fine: <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けたら、<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>りましょう。

Note the shapes too: `と` takes the plain **non-past**, `たら` the plain **past**. If you find yourself writing a past form in front of `と`, you have picked the wrong one.

---

## ～たら · ～たら vs ～ば

> **Rule of thumb** - after an **action verb**, only `たら` can be followed by a request or a command. And only `たら` narrates something that actually happened once.

<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんが**<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たら**、<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてください。<br>*Let me know when Tanaka gets here.*

❌<ruby>田中<rt>たなか</rt></ruby>さんが<ruby>来<rt>く</rt></ruby>れば、<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてください is wrong. Both work, though, once the condition is a state rather than an action: <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からなかったら／<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からなければ、<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてください are equally good.

---

## ～たら · ～たら vs ～ば (cont.)

The second gap is the past. A `たら` clause with a past main clause reports a real sequence; the `ば` equivalent flips into counterfactual regret (<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>けばよかった = "I should have asked, and didn't"). If you want to say what happened, `ば` is not available.

---

## ～たら · ～たら vs ～なら

> **Rule of thumb** - a `たら` clause always happens **before** its main clause. A `なら` clause need not: `なら` takes up a supposition, and the main clause can be carried out first.

The clearest illustration is a road-safety slogan you see on posters all over Japan, which uses both in one breath:

**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだら**<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>るな、<ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>る**なら**<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>むな。<br>*If you've drunk, don't drive; if you're going to drive, don't drink.*

<code><ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>んだら</code> = the drinking is finished, and the driving would come after it. <code><ruby>乗<rt>の</rt></ruby>るなら</code> = driving is a plan you are entertaining, and the instruction applies *beforehand*. Swap the two and the slogan collapses.

---

## ～たら · ～たら vs ～なら (cont.)

The same split in a plain sentence:

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>っ**たら**、<ruby>温泉<rt>おんせん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>入<rt>はい</rt></ruby>りたい。<br>*When I get to Japan, I want to go to a hot spring.*

<ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**なら**、<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>がいちばんいいですよ。<br>*If you're going to Japan, spring is the best time.*

---

## ～たら · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**たら** → ✅ <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>っ**たら**<br>*`たら` is built on the **past** form, never the dictionary form. <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>った → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったら.*

- ❌ <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い**たら** → ✅ <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>**かったら**<br>*い-adjectives go through their past form first: <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>かった → <ruby>高<rt>たか</rt></ruby>かったら.*

- ❌ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったら、ビザを<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>ります。 → ✅ <ruby>日本<rt>にほん</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**なら**、ビザを<ruby>取<rt>と</rt></ruby>ります。<br>*A `たら` clause must come first in time, and you get the visa before you fly. For a plan you are reacting to, `なら`.*

---

## ～たら · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>けたら、<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>掃除<rt>そうじ</rt></ruby>しました。 → ✅ <ruby>窓<rt>まど</rt></ruby>を<ruby>開<rt>あ</rt></ruby>け**てから**、<ruby>部屋<rt>へや</rt></ruby>を<ruby>掃除<rt>そうじ</rt></ruby>しました。<br>*With a past main clause, `たら` means the speaker *discovered* something. A deliberate next action needs `～てから`.*

- ❌ このボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>したら、お<ruby>湯<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ます。 → ✅ このボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>す**と**、お<ruby>湯<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*Not ungrammatical, but wrong for a manual: `たら` frames it as one occasion, while a machine's fixed behaviour calls for `と`.*

---

## ～たら · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>____、もう<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>**かったら**、もう<ruby>一<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>つ<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>います。

{reveal}
An い-adjective forms `たら` through its past: <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>い → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>かった → <ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>かったら. ❌<ruby>安<rt>やす</rt></ruby>いたら does not exist.

---

## ～たら · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>を（<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ると・<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たら）、<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>るつもりです。

{reveal}
<ruby>大学<rt>だいがく</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>たら**、<ruby>国<rt>くに</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>るつもりです。

{reveal}
`つもり` states the speaker's intention, and `と` cannot host anything that expresses the speaker's will. `たら` has no such limit - and here it carries the "when", not "if": graduating is going to happen.

---

## ～たら · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "When the rain stops, let's go for a walk."

{reveal}
<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が**やんだら**、<ruby>散歩<rt>さんぽ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>きましょう。

{reveal}
`～ましょう` is a proposal, so `と` is out. The event is expected rather than doubtful, which is why English says "when" and Japanese still uses `たら` - the same form covers both.

---

## ～たら · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったら、ホテルを<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>しておきます。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>来週<rt>らいしゅう</rt></ruby><ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**なら**、ホテルを<ruby>予約<rt>よやく</rt></ruby>しておきます。

{reveal}
You book the hotel *before* leaving, and a `たら` clause is always the earlier event. `なら` is the one conditional that lets the main clause come first in time.

## ～たら · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>したら、<ruby>留守<rt>るす</rt></ruby>でした。 (b) <ruby>彼<rt>かれ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>したら、<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてください。

{reveal}
(a) has a **past** main clause, so there is no condition in it at all: "I phoned him, and he was out." The `たら` clause just sets the scene, and what follows is something the speaker found out.

{reveal}
(b) has a **request** as the main clause, so it is a genuine conditional: "if (or when) you phone him, let me know." Nothing has happened yet.

{reveal}
The `たら` is identical in both. The tense of the main clause decides whether you are reading a report or a condition - and this is the single most useful thing to know about `たら`.

# ～なら

**Meaning** · if that's the case - taking up something just said, and replying to it

`～なら` is the conditional that **answers**. It picks up a premise - nearly always one the other person has just handed you - and builds the speaker's judgement on top of it: advice, an offer, an opinion, a decision. Nothing about `なら` reports what will happen; it reports what the speaker thinks about it.

---

## ～なら · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun | Noun + なら (**no** だ) | <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby> → <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>なら |
| な-adjective | stem + なら (**no** だ) | <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か → <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かなら |
| い-adjective | plain form + なら | <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しいなら |
| Verb | plain non-past + なら | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くなら |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + なら | <ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>らない → <ruby>要<rt>い</rt></ruby>らないなら |
| Verb (past) | plain past + なら | <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わった → <ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わったなら |
| With の | plain form + のなら／んなら | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くのなら／<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>くんなら |
| Formal | + であれば／でしたら | <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby> → <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>であれば |

---

## ～なら · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - `だ` never appears in front of `なら`. ❌<ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>かだなら, ❌<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>だなら. Compare `と`, which **requires** it (<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>だと). Getting these two the wrong way round is the commonest form error at N4.

> **Note** - `だったら`, `でしたら` and `それなら` are the everyday cousins of this pattern. `だったら` is the casual "in that case"; `でしたら` is the polite version you hear across a counter: <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby>でしたら、お<ruby>席<rt>せき</rt></ruby>がございます。

---

## ～なら · Examples

ビール**なら**<ruby>冷蔵庫<rt>れいぞうこ</rt></ruby>にありますよ。<br>*Beer? There's some in the fridge.*

<ruby>土曜日<rt>どようび</rt></ruby>は<ruby>無理<rt>むり</rt></ruby>だけど、<ruby>日曜日<rt>にちようび</rt></ruby>**なら**<ruby>空<rt>あ</rt></ruby>いているよ。<br>*Saturday's no good, but Sunday I'm free.*

<ruby>頭<rt>あたま</rt></ruby>が<ruby>痛<rt>いた</rt></ruby>い**なら**、<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ったほうがいいですよ。<br>*If your head hurts, you should go home early.*

お<ruby>寿司<rt>すし</rt></ruby>**なら**、<ruby>駅前<rt>えきまえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>がおいしいですよ。<br>*For sushi, the place in front of the station is good.*

---

## ～なら · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>君<rt>きみ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない**なら**、<ruby>私<rt>わたし</rt></ruby>も<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない。<br>*If you're not going, I'm not going either.*

<ruby>宿題<rt>しゅくだい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>終<rt>お</rt></ruby>わった**なら**、<ruby>遊<rt>あそ</rt></ruby>びに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ってもいいよ。<br>*If you've finished your homework, you can go out and play.*

<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>る**なら**、いい<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>っておいたほうがいいですよ。<br>*If you're going to climb a mountain, you'd better buy proper boots first.*

<ruby>中古<rt>ちゅうこ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>車<rt>くるま</rt></ruby>を<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>う**なら**、<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>専門<rt>せんもん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>見<rt>み</rt></ruby>てもらったほうが<ruby>安心<rt>あんしん</rt></ruby>です。<br>*If you're buying a used car, it's safer to have a specialist look at it first.*

---

## ～なら · Nuance & register

`なら` is conversational glue, and its natural habitat is the second turn of a dialogue. Someone says something; you take their words and hand back a judgement. That is why so many `なら` sentences begin by repeating a noun the other person just used - ビール**なら**, <ruby>京都<rt>きょうと</rt></ruby>**なら**, その<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>**なら**. In that use it behaves almost like a topic marker, and it is the only conditional that can attach to a bare noun this way.

The main clause is always the speaker: `～たほうがいい`, `～てあげる`, `～てください`, `～つもりだ`, an opinion, an offer. It is never a natural consequence - that is `と`'s territory.

---

## ～なら · Nuance & register (cont.)

`のなら`（casual `んなら`）adds a shade of "given that you're telling me this", which can read as sympathetic or as slightly pointed depending on the tone: そんなに<ruby>嫌<rt>きら</rt></ruby>いなのなら、<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べなくていいよ。 Used that way, `なら` carries a real edge - you are throwing the listener's own premise back at them.

In writing, `なら` is replaced by `であれば` or by <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は</code>; the stiff `ならば` survives in formal prose (<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>ならば<ruby>再度<rt>さいど</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>ください). In speech, `ならば` sounds bookish.

---

## ～なら · ～なら vs ～たら

> **Rule of thumb** - with `たら`, the main clause comes **after** the condition in time. With `なら`, it can come **before**. And `たら` reports events, while `なら` responds to premises.

<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>く**なら**、カメラを<ruby>貸<rt>か</rt></ruby>してあげるよ。<br>*If you're going on a trip, I'll lend you my camera.*

<ruby>旅行<rt>りょこう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>っ**たら**、<ruby>写真<rt>しゃしん</rt></ruby>をたくさん<ruby>撮<rt>と</rt></ruby>りたい。<br>*When I go on the trip, I want to take lots of photos.*

The camera is lent **before** the trip - the trip is only a plan the listener has mentioned. The photos are taken **during** it. `たら` can never do what the first sentence does; that inversion is `なら`'s alone.

---

## ～なら · ～なら vs ～たら (cont.)

The second half of the difference is where the condition comes from. `たら` supposes something itself; `なら` accepts something already on the table. So `なら` is at home in replies, and slightly odd as the opening line of a conversation.

---

## ～なら · ～なら vs ～と

> **Rule of thumb** - `と` is a mechanism with no speaker in it. `なら` is nothing *but* the speaker, reacting to a premise.

このボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>す**と**、お<ruby>湯<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ます。<br>*Press this button and hot water comes out.*

❌このボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>すなら、お<ruby>湯<rt>ゆ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>出<rt>で</rt></ruby>ます is wrong: the water coming out is a fact about the machine, not the speaker's opinion about a supposition. Conversely:

このボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>す**なら**、<ruby>先<rt>さき</rt></ruby>に<ruby>説明書<rt>せつめいしょ</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んでください。<br>*If you're going to press this button, read the manual first.*

---

## ～なら · ～なら vs ～と (cont.)

Now there is a speaker with an instruction, and the instruction applies *before* the button is pressed - both hallmarks of `なら`, both impossible for `と`.

`なら` also cannot express a certain future "when": ❌<ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>になるなら, ❌<ruby>夜<rt>よる</rt></ruby>になるなら. Something guaranteed to occur is not a premise anyone needs to entertain; use `と` or `たら`.

---

## ～なら · ～なら vs ～ば

> **Rule of thumb** - `なら(ば)` is literally the ば-form of the copula, which is why they blur. Separate them by what the condition is *about*: `ば` conditions on an event **occurring**, `なら` on a supposition **being true**.

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>けば**、すぐ<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりますよ。<br>*Ask the manager and you'll know right away.*

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>く**なら**、<ruby>午前中<rt>ごぜんちゅう</rt></ruby>がいいですよ。<br>*If you're going to ask the manager, the morning is a good time.*

In the first, the asking produces the result. In the second, the asking is a plan the listener has announced, and the advice is about *how* to carry it out. Note again that the advice applies before the asking happens.

---

## ～なら · ～なら vs ～ば (cont.)

The other split is mechanical. `ば` needs a predicate to attach to; `なら` fastens straight onto a bare noun and lifts it out as a topic - その<ruby>辞書<rt>じしょ</rt></ruby>**なら**<ruby>机<rt>つくえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>上<rt>うえ</rt></ruby>にあります - and no ば-form can do that.

---

## ～なら · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**だなら** → ✅ <ruby>静<rt>しず</rt></ruby>か**なら**<br>*`だ` disappears before `なら`. The same for nouns: ❌<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>だなら → ✅ <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>なら／<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>だったら.*

- ❌ <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>る**なら**、シャワーを<ruby>浴<rt>あ</rt></ruby>びます。 → ✅ <ruby>家<rt>いえ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>っ**たら**、シャワーを<ruby>浴<rt>あ</rt></ruby>びます。<br>*A plain sequence of your own actions is not a premise you are responding to, and the shower comes after you get home. That is a `たら` sentence.*

- ❌ <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>になる**なら**、<ruby>暖<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かくなります。 → ✅ <ruby>春<rt>はる</rt></ruby>になる**と**、<ruby>暖<rt>あたた</rt></ruby>かくなります。<br>*Spring is not a supposition, and warming up is not the speaker's judgement. Natural consequences belong to `と`.*

---

## ～なら · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>る**なら**、<ruby>試合<rt>しあい</rt></ruby>は<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>です。 → ✅ <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>っ**たら**／<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>れ**ば**…<br>*If *you* are the one supposing the rain, use `たら` or `ば`. `降るなら` becomes correct the moment somebody else says it is going to rain and you reply to them.*

- ❌ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>**の**なら<ruby>割引<rt>わりびき</rt></ruby>があります。 → ✅ <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>なら<ruby>割引<rt>わりびき</rt></ruby>があります。<br>*A noun takes `なら` directly. The `の` belongs with <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code>, not with `なら`.*

The second speaker picks up the exact noun the first one used and replies to it with an offer. That is `なら` at its most typical - attached to a bare noun, no `だ`, and a main clause that is purely the speaker's own decision.

# 場合は

**Meaning** · in the event that X - one case picked out, with the rule that applies to it

<code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code> (read ばあい) is an ordinary noun meaning "case, occasion". Used as a conditional it sets out one possible situation among several and states what applies in it. It is the language of notices, manuals and rules, not of conversation.

---

## 場合は · Formation

Everything in front of <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code> is a noun-modifying clause - exactly the N5 pattern - because <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code> is just a noun.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | dictionary form + <ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は | <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れる → <ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れる<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は |
| Verb (past) | た-form + <ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は | <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れた → <ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れた<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は |
| Verb (negative) | ない-form + <ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は | <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>かない → <ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>かない<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は |
| い-adjective | plain form + <ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は | <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい → <ruby>忙<rt>いそが</rt></ruby>しい<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は |
| な-adjective | stem + **な** + <ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は | <ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby> → <ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>な<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は |
| Noun | Noun + **の** + <ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は | <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby> → <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は |

---

## 場合は · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - if you can put it in front of a noun, you can put it in front of <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code>, using exactly the same shape. That is why な-adjectives take `な` and nouns take `の` here, while `と` demands `だ` and `なら` demands nothing at all.

> **Note** - the `は` is the contrast particle doing its usual job: "in *this* case (as opposed to the others)". Drop it - <ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>、<ruby>中止<rt>ちゅうし</rt></ruby>します - and the sentence is flatter and slightly more written. `には` makes the contrast heavier still. On forms and in headings you often see the bare noun phrase alone: 「<ruby>団体<rt>だんたい</rt></ruby>の<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>」.

---

## 場合は · Examples

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は**、<ruby>体育館<rt>たいいくかん</rt></ruby>で<ruby>行<rt>おこな</rt></ruby>います。<br>*In the event of rain, it will be held in the gym.*

<ruby>遅<rt>おく</rt></ruby>れる**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は**、<ruby>必<rt>かなら</rt></ruby>ず<ruby>連絡<rt>れんらく</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*If you are going to be late, be sure to get in touch.*

<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がない**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は**、メールでも<ruby>大丈夫<rt>だいじょうぶ</rt></ruby>です。<br>*If you don't have time, email is fine too.*

パスワードを<ruby>忘<rt>わす</rt></ruby>れた**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は**、こちらのボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>してください。<br>*If you have forgotten your password, press this button.*

---

## 場合は · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>火事<rt>かじ</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は**、この<ruby>階段<rt>かいだん</rt></ruby>をお<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>いください。<br>*In case of fire, please use these stairs.*

<ruby>説明<rt>せつめい</rt></ruby>が<ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>な**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は**、いつでもお<ruby>呼<rt>よ</rt></ruby>びください。<br>*If you need an explanation, call me any time.*

<ruby>荷物<rt>にもつ</rt></ruby>が1<ruby>週間<rt>しゅうかん</rt></ruby><ruby>以内<rt>いない</rt></ruby>に<ruby>届<rt>とど</rt></ruby>かない**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は**、こちらの<ruby>番号<rt>ばんごう</rt></ruby>までお<ruby>電話<rt>でんわ</rt></ruby>ください。<br>*If your parcel has not arrived within a week, please call this number.*

キャンセルは<ruby>前日<rt>ぜんじつ</rt></ruby>まで<ruby>無料<rt>むりょう</rt></ruby>ですが、<ruby>当日<rt>とうじつ</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は**<ruby>料金<rt>りょうきん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>半分<rt>はんぶん</rt></ruby>をいただきます。<br>*Cancellation is free up to the day before; on the day itself we charge half the fee.*

---

## 場合は · Nuance & register

This is the **formal, written** conditional. Signs at stations, safety notices, insurance documents, school regulations, software error messages, business email - that is where <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は</code> lives. It has a bureaucratic steadiness to it: here is a contingency, here is the procedure.

Because <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code> is a noun, it behaves like one. It takes demonstratives (その<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は - "in that case"), it appears in fixed expressions (<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>によって - "depending on the case"; <ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>によっては - "in some cases"), and it can head a column on a form. None of the other conditionals can do any of that.

---

## 場合は · Nuance & register (cont.)

The flip side is that using it with a friend sounds like reading aloud from a manual. ❌お<ruby>腹<rt>なか</rt></ruby>がすいた<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>るよ is grammatical and comic; say お<ruby>腹<rt>なか</rt></ruby>がすいたら、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>か<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>るよ. A rough guide: if the sentence could plausibly be printed and laminated, use <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は</code>; otherwise use `たら`.

One more consequence of its formality: the main clause is almost always an instruction, a rule, or a consequence stated impersonally - `～てください`, `お～ください`, `～ことになっています`, `～ます`. You will rarely see it with a casual ending.

---

## 場合は · 場合は vs ときは

> **Rule of thumb** - `とき` locates an event **in time** and takes for granted that it happens. <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code> presents it as **one possibility among others**, and usually leads to an instruction.

This is the pair that matters, because both translate as "when".

<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの**ときは**、よく<ruby>川<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぎました。<br>*When I was a child, I often swam in the river.*

❌<ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は、よく<ruby>川<rt>かわ</rt></ruby>で<ruby>泳<rt>およ</rt></ruby>ぎました is not a sentence about the speaker's childhood at all - it says "in the case of children…", i.e. as opposed to adults. Childhood is a period of time, not a contingency.

---

## 場合は · 場合は vs ときは (cont.)

<ruby>地震<rt>じしん</rt></ruby>の**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は**、エレベーターを<ruby>使<rt>つか</rt></ruby>わないでください。<br>*In the event of an earthquake, do not use the lift.*

Here <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code> is right precisely because the earthquake may never come, and because the sentence sits in a list beside <ruby>火事<rt>かじ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は… and <ruby>停電<rt>ていでん</rt></ruby>の<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は….

---

## 場合は · 場合は vs ～たら

> **Rule of thumb** - same meaning, different room. `たら` is what you say; <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は</code> is what the sign says.

<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がらない**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は**、もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>来院<rt>らいいん</rt></ruby>ください。<br>*If your temperature does not come down, please come in again.*

<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>下<rt>さ</rt></ruby>がらな**かったら**、もう<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby><ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>てくださいね。<br>*If your fever doesn't go down, come back in again, all right?*

---

## 場合は · 場合は vs ～たら (cont.)

Identical content; the first is the printed instruction slip, the second is the doctor speaking. Note also that <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code> being a noun lets a notice enumerate cases in parallel, each on its own line - a structure `たら` handles clumsily.

---

## 場合は · 場合は vs ～と

> **Rule of thumb** - in a manual the two divide the labour: `と` says what the machine normally **does**; <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は</code> introduces the exception and tells you what **you** should do.

ボタンを<ruby>押<rt>お</rt></ruby>す**と**、ランプが<ruby>光<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>ります。<ruby>光<rt>ひか</rt></ruby>らない**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は**、<ruby>電池<rt>でんち</rt></ruby>を<ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめてください。<br>*Press the button and the lamp lights up. If it does not light up, check the batteries.*

The first half is an automatic consequence with no speaker in it, which is `と`'s whole job - and `と` cannot be followed by an instruction. The second half needs one, so it switches to <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は</code>. Almost every instruction manual in Japanese alternates between exactly these two.

---

## 場合は · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>、よく<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>りました。 → ✅ <ruby>子<rt>こ</rt></ruby>どもの**とき**、よく<ruby>山<rt>やま</rt></ruby>に<ruby>登<rt>のぼ</rt></ruby>りました。<br>*A stretch of past time is `とき`. <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code> would mean "as for children (as opposed to adults)".*

- ❌ <ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>**だ**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby> → ✅ <ruby>必要<rt>ひつよう</rt></ruby>**な**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby><br>*<code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code> is a noun, so a な-adjective in front of it takes `な` - the ordinary noun-modifying rule from N5.*

- ❌ <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は → ✅ <ruby>病気<rt>びょうき</rt></ruby>**の**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は<br>*A noun needs `の` to modify another noun. Compare `と`, which wants `だ`, and `なら`, which wants nothing.*

- ❌ <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちと<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は、いつも<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちます。 → ✅ <ruby>友<rt>とも</rt></ruby>だちと<ruby>会<rt>あ</rt></ruby>う**ときは**、いつも<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>で<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ちます。<br>*A regular habit is not a contingency. <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby></code> needs something that might or might not arise.*

{reveal}
<ruby>都合<rt>つごう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>い**<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>**は、<ruby>前<rt>まえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>日<rt>ひ</rt></ruby>までにお<ruby>知<rt>し</rt></ruby>らせください。

{reveal}
An い-adjective attaches in its plain form, with nothing in between. The polite `お～ください` ending confirms the register: this is a written notice, which is where <code><ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は</code> belongs.

## 場合は · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>学生<rt>がくせい</rt></ruby>の（<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は・ときは）、<ruby>料金<rt>りょうきん</rt></ruby>が<ruby>半額<rt>はんがく</rt></ruby>になります。

{reveal}

# ～たらどうですか

**Meaning** · why don't you…? - putting an action to the listener as a suggestion

Literally "if you did X, how would that be?" - the `たら` is the ordinary conditional and `どうですか` is the N5 "how about it?". Put together they make a suggestion aimed squarely at the other person, and that aim is what makes the pattern touchier than it looks.

---

## ～たらどうですか · Formation

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb | た-form + らどうですか | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだらどうですか |
| Verb (negative) | なかった + らどうですか | <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かない → <ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>かなかったらどうですか |
| Casual | た-form + ら? | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだら? |
| Softer | た-form + らどうでしょうか | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだらどうでしょうか |
| Politer | た-form + らいかがですか | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだらいかがですか |
| Politest (upward) | て-form + はいかがでしょうか | <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む → お<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みになってはいかがでしょうか |

---

## ～たらどうですか · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - the suggestion lives in `どう`, not in `たら`. Change `どう` and you change the sentence completely: `～たらどうですか` = "why don't you…?", but `～たらどうしますか` = "what will you do if…?" - a real question about a real condition.

> **Note** - inserting `～てみる` softens the whole thing, because it proposes a *trial* rather than a course of action: <ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてみたらどうですか lands much more gently than <ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いたらどうですか. This is the single easiest way to take the edge off.

---

## ～たらどうですか · Examples

もう<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し**<ruby>待<rt>ま</rt></ruby>ったらどうですか**。<br>*Why don't you wait a bit longer?*

<ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてみたらどう**?<br>*Why not try asking the teacher?*

<ruby>熱<rt>ねつ</rt></ruby>があるなら、<ruby>今日<rt>きょう</rt></ruby>は<ruby>早<rt>はや</rt></ruby>く**<ruby>帰<rt>かえ</rt></ruby>ったらどうですか**。<br>*If you've got a fever, why don't you go home early today?*

<ruby>時間<rt>じかん</rt></ruby>がないなら、タクシーで**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったらどうでしょうか**。<br>*If you're short of time, how about taking a taxi?*

---

## ～たらどうですか · Examples (cont.)

そんなに<ruby>心配<rt>しんぱい</rt></ruby>なら、<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>確<rt>たし</rt></ruby>かめたらどう**?<br>*If you're that worried, why don't you go and check it yourself?*

<ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、こちらの<ruby>案<rt>あん</rt></ruby>も<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>覧<rt>らん</rt></ruby>に**なってはいかがでしょうか**。<br>*Might I suggest you also take a look at this proposal?*

<ruby>毎日<rt>まいにち</rt></ruby>コンビニのお<ruby>弁当<rt>べんとう</rt></ruby>ばかりじゃなくて、たまには<ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>作<rt>つく</rt></ruby>ってみたらどうですか**。<br>*Instead of convenience-store bentos every single day, why not cook for yourself once in a while?*

<ruby>一人<rt>ひとり</rt></ruby>で<ruby>悩<rt>なや</rt></ruby>んでいないで、<ruby>一度<rt>いちど</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>両親<rt>りょうしん</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>相談<rt>そうだん</rt></ruby>してみたらいかがですか**。<br>*Rather than worrying about it alone, why not talk it over with your parents?*

---

## ～たらどうですか · Nuance & register

This is **not** a neutral pattern, and the polite `ですか` does not make it safe. `～たらどうですか` carries an unstated "you are not doing the obvious thing", which is fine from a friend and grating from anyone else. Repeated, it is nagging; delivered flatly, it is criticism.

The register scale, roughly:

---

## ～たらどうですか · Nuance & register (cont.)

- **<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだら?** - casual, friendly with rising intonation. With<br>**falling* intonation it is openly annoyed: <ruby>自分<rt>じぶん</rt></ruby>でやったら? = "then do it yourself."*
- **<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだらどうですか** - polite in form; use downward or sideways, not<br>*upward.*
- **<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだらどうでしょうか** - hedged, noticeably softer.
- **<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだらいかがですか** - polite vocabulary, safe with customers and<br>*acquaintances.*
- **お<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みになってはいかがでしょうか** - the form to use with a superior.<br>*Notice it abandons `たら` entirely for `～ては`.*

---

## ～たらどうですか · Nuance & register (cont.)

It is a spoken pattern. In writing you would use `～てはいかがでしょうか` or `～ことをおすすめします`.

Two traps worth keeping apart, both hinging on what follows `たら`:

- **～たらどうですか** = a suggestion.
- **～たらどうしますか** = "what will you do if…?" -<br>*<ruby>終電<rt>しゅうでん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>逃<rt>のが</rt></ruby>したらどうしますか。*
- **～たらどうなりますか** = "what happens if…?" - about consequences, not<br>*people.*

---

## ～たらどうですか · ～たらどうですか vs ～ほうがいい

> **Rule of thumb** - `～たらどうですか` **asks**; `～ほうがいい` **tells**.

`～ほうがいい` (N5) weighs one option against the alternative and comes down on one side, often with an unspoken warning attached. `～たらどうですか` floats an action and hands the decision back to the listener - formally, at least. It ends in a question, and an answer is expected.

<ruby>病院<rt>びょういん</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったらどうですか**。<br>*Why don't you go to the doctor?*

<ruby>病院<rt>びょういん</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったほうがいいですよ**。<br>*You'd better go to the doctor.*

---

## ～たらどうですか · ～たらどうですか vs ～ほうがいい (cont.)

The second is stronger and implies a consequence if you don't. The first is softer on the surface - but because it points at something the listener has visibly failed to do, it can end up sounding more pointed. Neither is safe to aim at a superior.

---

## ～たらどうですか · ～たらどうですか vs ～ませんか／～ましょう

> **Rule of thumb** - `～ませんか` and `～ましょう` include the speaker. `～たらどうですか` is for the listener **alone**.

<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べませんか**。<br>*Shall we have lunch together?*

<ruby>昼<rt>ひる</rt></ruby>ご<ruby>飯<rt>はん</rt></ruby>くらい**<ruby>食<rt>た</rt></ruby>べたらどうですか**。<br>*Why don't you at least eat some lunch?*

The second explicitly excludes the speaker: you are telling someone else to eat. Using `～たらどうですか` when you mean to come along is one of the more embarrassing N4 errors, because it turns an invitation into a criticism of the listener's habits.

---

## ～たらどうですか · ～たらどうですか vs ～たらいいですよ

> **Rule of thumb** - `～たらいいですよ` **answers** a request for advice. `～たらどうですか` **offers** advice nobody asked for.

「どこで<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>えますか。」「<ruby>駅前<rt>えきまえ</rt></ruby>の<ruby>店<rt>みせ</rt></ruby>で**<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ったらいいですよ**。」<br>*"Where can I buy one?" "You could buy one at the shop by the station."*

`～たらいい` states the good option as information - no question, no pressure. It is the natural reply to どうしたらいいですか. Swapping in <ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>ったらどうですか would be odd there, because the listener has already asked; suggesting it back at them implies they should have thought of it themselves.

---

## ～たらどうですか · Common mistakes

- ❌ <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む**たらどうですか** → ✅ <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>**んだら**どうですか<br>*`たら` is built on the past form: <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>む → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだ → <ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだら.*

- ❌ <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>し<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>んだらどうですか。 → ✅ <ruby>社長<rt>しゃちょう</rt></ruby>、<ruby>少<rt>すこ</rt></ruby>しお<ruby>休<rt>やす</rt></ruby>みに**なってはいかがでしょうか**。<br>*Grammatical, and rude upward. Telling a superior what they ought to be doing needs the `～てはいかがでしょうか` frame and an honorific verb.*

- ❌ <ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>にコンサートに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったらどうですか。（meaning "shall we go together?"）→ ✅ <ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>にコンサートに<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>き**ませんか**。<br>*`～たらどうですか` never includes the speaker.*

---

## ～たらどうですか · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ Reading <ruby>台風<rt>たいふう</rt></ruby>が<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たらどうしますか as a suggestion → it<br>*means "what will you do if a typhoon comes?" `どうですか` suggests; `どうしますか` asks.*

---

## ～たらどうですか · Practice 1

**1.** <ruby>眠<rt>ねむ</rt></ruby>そうですね。コーヒーでも<ruby>飲<rt>の</rt></ruby>____どうですか。

{reveal}

{reveal}

## ～たらどうですか · Practice 2

**2.** <ruby>明日<rt>あした</rt></ruby><ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>にテニスを（しませんか・したらどうですか）。

{reveal}

{reveal}
<ruby>一緒<rt>いっしょ</rt></ruby>に puts the speaker in the picture, and `～たらどうですか` is strictly about what the *listener* should do. With `したらどうですか` the sentence would mean "why don't you play tennis with someone tomorrow" - and would sound faintly critical of how they spend their time.

---

## ～たらどうですか · Practice 3

**3.** Translate into Japanese: "Why don't you try asking the station staff?"

{reveal}
<ruby>駅<rt>えき</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>に**<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いてみたらどうですか**。

{reveal}
`～てみる` inside the pattern turns it into "why not give it a try", which is the friendliest version available. Without it, <ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いたらどうですか sounds more like "you should have asked already".

## ～たらどうですか · Practice 4

**4.** Find and fix the error: <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>を<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>んだらどうですか。

{reveal}
✅ <ruby>先生<rt>せんせい</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>本<rt>ほん</rt></ruby>をお<ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>みに**なってはいかがでしょうか**。

{reveal}
The grammar of the original is fine; the problem is direction. Suggesting an action to a teacher, boss or customer needs `～てはいかがでしょうか` with an honorific verb. A slightly lighter option is <ruby>読<rt>よ</rt></ruby>まれてはいかがでしょうか.

## ～たらどうですか · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) <ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったらどうですか。 (b) <ruby>医者<rt>いしゃ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>行<rt>い</rt></ruby>ったほうがいいですよ。

{reveal}

{reveal}

{reveal}

# どうしたらいいですか

**Meaning** · what should I do? - asking someone to tell you the right course of action

This is a frame, not a single word: a **question word** + a **conditional** + `いい`. "If I did what, would it be good?" Swap the question word and the verb and you can build the whole family - どこに<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>せばいいですか, <ruby>何時<rt>なんじ</rt></ruby>に<ruby>来<rt>き</rt></ruby>たらいいですか - which is why it is worth learning as a shape rather than as a phrase.

---

## どうしたらいいですか · Formation

The `たら` and `ば` versions are interchangeable in almost every sentence.

| Attaches to | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Verb (たら) | た-form + らいいですか | <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す → <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>したらいいですか |
| Verb (ば) | ば-form + いいですか | <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>す → <ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>せばいいですか |
| する (fixed frame) | どう + したら／すれば + いいですか | する → どうしたらいいですか |
| Other question words | <ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>／どこ／いつ／どう + …たらいいですか | どこ → どこで<ruby>買<rt>か</rt></ruby>えばいいですか |
| Softened | …らいい**でしょうか** | どうしたらいいでしょうか |
| Embedded (no ですか) | …らいい**か** + <ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>からない | どうしたらいいか<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません |
| Formal / to a superior | どのように…らよろしいでしょうか | どのようにすればよろしいでしょうか |

---

## どうしたらいいですか · Formation (cont.)

> **Rule of thumb** - when the question is embedded inside a larger sentence, the `ですか` disappears and a bare `か` holds the question together: どうしたらいい**か**<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてください. Never ❌どうしたらいいですかを<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてください.

> **Note** - the `いい` here is an ordinary い-adjective, so it conjugates: どうしたらよかったんですか ("what should I have done?"), どうしたらいいのか<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません, どうしたらよろしいでしょうか (formal, `いい` → `よろしい`).

---

## どうしたらいいですか · Examples

<ruby>切符<rt>きっぷ</rt></ruby>をなくしました。**どうしたらいいですか**。<br>*I've lost my ticket. What should I do?*

この<ruby>書類<rt>しょるい</rt></ruby>はどこに**<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>したらいいですか**。<br>*Where should I hand in these documents?*

<ruby>靴<rt>くつ</rt></ruby>はここで**<ruby>脱<rt>ぬ</rt></ruby>げばいいですか**。<br>*Should I take my shoes off here?*

<ruby>漢字<rt>かんじ</rt></ruby>がなかなか<ruby>覚<rt>おぼ</rt></ruby>えられません。**どうすればいいですか**。<br>*I just can't seem to memorise kanji. What should I do?*

---

## どうしたらいいですか · Examples (cont.)

<ruby>次<rt>つぎ</rt></ruby>は**どうしたらいいか**、<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてください。<br>*Please tell me what I should do next.*

パソコンが<ruby>急<rt>きゅう</rt></ruby>に<ruby>動<rt>うご</rt></ruby>かなくなったんですが、**どうしたらいいでしょうか**。<br>*My computer's suddenly stopped working - what would you suggest I do?*

<ruby>初<rt>はじ</rt></ruby>めての<ruby>出張<rt>しゅっちょう</rt></ruby>で、<ruby>何<rt>なに</rt></ruby>を**<ruby>準備<rt>じゅんび</rt></ruby>したらいいか**<ruby>全<rt>まった</rt></ruby>く<ruby>分<rt>わ</rt></ruby>かりません。<br>*It's my first business trip and I have no idea what I should be getting ready.*

<ruby>保証書<rt>ほしょうしょ</rt></ruby>をなくしてしまった<ruby>場合<rt>ばあい</rt></ruby>は、**どうしたらよろしいでしょうか**。<br>*In the event that the warranty card has been lost, how should we proceed?*

---

## どうしたらいいですか · Nuance & register

`どうしたらいいですか` is the standard polite way to ask for guidance, and it is safe with strangers, shop staff, teachers and colleagues. It is neither stiff nor casual on its own; the register is set by the ending you attach.

- **どうしよう** - muttered to yourself. "Oh no, what do I do."
- **どうしたらいい?／どうすればいいの?** - casual, to a friend.
- **どうしたらいいですか** - the neutral polite default.
- **どうしたらいいでしょうか** - softer, and slightly more deferential; it<br>*presents the question as something you are wondering rather than demanding an answer to.*
- **どうしたらよろしいでしょうか／どのようにすればよろしいでしょうか** - business<br>*and customer-facing.*

---

## どうしたらいいですか · Nuance & register (cont.)

`たら` versus `ば` in this frame: they are close to interchangeable, and no native speaker will correct you either way. `どうすればいいですか` leans a touch more procedural and written - it fits "what is the correct procedure" - while `どうしたらいいですか` is marginally more common in speech and a little softer. With other question words the same holds: どこに<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>いたらいいですか and どこに<ruby>置<rt>お</rt></ruby>けばいいですか are both fine.

One keigo distinction worth knowing early: if you are offering to act *for* someone - a shop assistant, say - the humble `いかがいたしましょうか` ("how would you like me to…?") replaces this pattern entirely. `どうしたらいいですか` asks for instructions; `いかがいたしましょうか` offers service.

---

## どうしたらいいですか · どうしたらいいですか vs どうしますか

> **Rule of thumb** - `どうしたらいいですか` asks for **advice**; `どうしますか` asks about **intention**.

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ってきました。**どうしますか**。<br>*It's started raining. What do you want to do?*

<ruby>雨<rt>あめ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>降<rt>ふ</rt></ruby>ってきました。**どうしたらいいですか**。<br>*It's started raining. What should we do?*

The first hands the decision to the listener and expects them to state their own plan; a waiter or a travel agent would use it of you. The second admits you do not know the right answer and asks to be told. Asking a superior `どうしますか` when you meant "what should I do?" is a small but real workplace mistake - it sounds like you are asking about *their* plans.

---

## どうしたらいいですか · どうしたらいいですか vs どうしたんですか

> **Rule of thumb** - these look alike and share nothing. `どうしたんですか` = "what happened? / what's wrong?"

<ruby>顔色<rt>かおいろ</rt></ruby>が<ruby>悪<rt>わる</rt></ruby>いですね。**どうしたんですか**。<br>*You look pale. What's the matter?*

`どうしたんですか` is `どうした` (past of する) plus the N5 explanatory `んです` - it looks backwards at a situation. `どうしたらいいですか` looks forwards at an action. The same trap catches `どうしましたか`, which a doctor uses to mean "what seems to be the problem?" - never "what should I do?"

---

## どうしたらいいですか · どうしたらいいですか vs ～たほうがいいですか

> **Rule of thumb** - `どうしたらいいですか` is an **open** question. `～たほうがいいですか` names one action and asks yes or no.

この<ruby>紙<rt>かみ</rt></ruby>は<ruby>捨<rt>す</rt></ruby>て**たほうがいいですか**。<br>*Should I throw this paper away?*

この<ruby>紙<rt>かみ</rt></ruby>は**どうしたらいいですか**。<br>*What should I do with this paper?*

Use the first when you already have a candidate action in mind and want it confirmed; use the second when you have nothing. The answer you get back will often be `～たらいいですよ` or `～たらどうですか` - the mirror image of this pattern, and the reason the two sit next to each other in this book.

---

## どうしたらいいですか · Common mistakes

- ❌ どう**する**たらいいですか → ✅ どう**した**らいいですか<br>*`たら` is built on the plain past. する → した → したら.*

- ❌ どうしたらいい**ますか** → ✅ どうしたらいい**ですか**<br>*`いい` is an い-adjective, so it takes `です`, not `ます`.*

- ❌ <ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>をなくしました。**どうしましたか**。 → ✅ <ruby>財布<rt>さいふ</rt></ruby>をなくしました。**どうしたらいいですか**。<br>*`どうしましたか` asks what happened - which you have just said. It cannot mean "what should I do?"*

- ❌ どうすればいいですか**を**<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてください。 → ✅ どうすればいい**か**<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてください。<br>*An embedded question drops `ですか` and ends in bare `か`. It is not a noun and cannot take `を`.*

---

## どうしたらいいですか · Common mistakes (cont.)

- ❌ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>件<rt>けん</rt></ruby>はどうしますか。（meaning "what should I do?"）→ ✅ <ruby>部長<rt>ぶちょう</rt></ruby>、この<ruby>件<rt>けん</rt></ruby>はどうしたら**よろしいでしょうか**。<br>*Asking upward for instructions needs the advice frame, and `よろしいでしょうか` for the register.*

**1.** この<ruby>申込書<rt>もうしこみしょ</rt></ruby>は、どこに<ruby>出<rt>だ</rt></ruby>____いいですか。

{reveal}

{reveal}
Either conditional works in this frame. `出したら` is the past form plus `ら`; `出せば` is the ば-form. The question word `どこ` simply replaces `どう` - the shape of the sentence is unchanged.

**4.** Find and fix the error: どうすればいいですかを<ruby>教<rt>おし</rt></ruby>えてください。

{reveal}

## どうしたらいいですか · Practice 5

**5.** Explain the difference in nuance: (a) どうしたらいいですか。 (b) どうしますか。

{reveal}
(a) says "I don't know the right thing to do - please tell me." The speaker is asking to be given a course of action, and the answer is expected to be advice: <ruby>係<rt>かかり</rt></ruby>の<ruby>人<rt>ひと</rt></ruby>に<ruby>聞<rt>き</rt></ruby>いたらいいですよ.

{reveal}
(b) says "what are you going to do?" The decision belongs to the listener, and the answer will be a plan, not advice. Said to a customer it is a polite "what would you like to do?"; said upward to a boss when you meant (a), it lands as though you were querying their intentions rather than asking for instructions.
